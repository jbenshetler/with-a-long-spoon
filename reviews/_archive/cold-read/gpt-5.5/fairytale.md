## Reader reaction

This chapter hit me as a beautiful trap closing after I’d already let myself be swept up. I felt the swoon first, honestly: the snow, the lift, John’s ridiculous confident “my dear wife,” the black run where he is better than Randi and does not make a show of it, the champagne, the blue dress, the ball, the sleigh. It works on me because it works on Randi. I could feel how badly she wants the fairytale to be real, not abstractly but in her body: “she was grinning without deciding to,” and later, “this was the night the rest of it started.” For a long stretch I was carried by it.

John is attractive on the page in that polished, dangerous-to-resist way: socially effortless, physically capable, direct, rich, and competent. But by the hotel door that attractiveness starts to sour for me. He “had never asked her where the night was going.” The chapter is careful there: Randi likes not being asked, and I believe that in the moment; this does not read as violation. But it also exposes exactly why this kind of man cannot reach her. He assumes the shape of the story, and she enters it, but he never has to meet the person underneath. By the end he feels less like a villain than like the perfect answer to the wrong question.

Randi broke my heart here. Seeing her with her parents makes the practiced face feel less glamorous and more like armor that has grown into her skin. “Randi, relying on her sorority practice, returned the smile warmly and hugged him, eating her revulsion” is immediately awful. Her mother is even colder to me: “all edges and points against her,” exquisite and labeled and lit. The bracelet being kept out of the light feels like Randi trying to hide a shackle even from herself.

The erotic charge is present but deliberately not lush by the time the sex arrives. The kiss at midnight has heat because it is all command and surrender: “He kissed as he led, his mouth telling her what it wanted, and she gave it.” The hotel sex is not described in explicit detail, but the aftermath tells me enough: lipstick on his chest and lower, her body “loose with how good.” The sharp thing is that the pleasure is real and still empties instantly. That made the scene more devastating than if the sex had simply been bad. Her body can respond; her self does not arrive.

The biggest pull, for me, is the comparison between kinds of being led. John’s lead is easier than Pace’s, and Randi wants easier to mean better. But the chapter lets the truth come in after the body: Pace’s lead requires her attention, her participation, her being there. John makes her beautiful inside a story; Pace finds her. That line, “Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best,” lands hard because the chapter earned both halves of it.

Vee’s presence at the end made me melt and ache at once. The text message is such a small kindness: “i’m here if you want to talk. or not.” It feels exactly like Vee, plain and warm and not grabbing. Randi answering with a fairytale instead of the thing is painful because every word is “true” and still evasive. “Every word of it true. None of it the thing.” That may be the chapter’s emotional key for me.

The title, “Fairytale,” first feels literal: chalet, snow, ball, dress, sleigh, handsome man. By the end it is crueler and sadder. A fairytale can be a story everyone approves of, a life handed to her complete, and also a beautiful nothing. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, still feels like it is pointing at desire that enters through manners, invitations, plausible deniability, social forms. The series title *With a Long Spoon* keeps feeling like a warning about intimacy at a distance: wanting something dangerous, or someone dangerous, while pretending etiquette is enough protection. I don’t have a cover tagline retained in the prior state, so I can’t use one.

What I want next is Randi and Vee in the same room with this new knowledge vibrating under Randi’s face. My guess from here: Randi will try harder to keep the friendship exactly as it is, and that will make it less stable, not more. I dread Vee being made to “arrive at it on her own” inside yet another concealed frame, especially now that Randi knows exactly what she is withholding.
