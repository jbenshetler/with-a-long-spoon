## Reader reaction

This one hurt in a quieter, more ordinary way than the Pace confrontation. I felt Vee’s loneliness physically: the too-short childhood bed, the sheets to her chin, the lavender plugins, the “room that loved a girl who no longer lived inside her.” It made home feel tender and suffocating at once. Her parents’ house is not cruel; that is part of why it aches. It loves Vivienne, but Vee has outgrown the version of herself it can hold.

I feel fiercely tender toward Vee here, and also very caught in how her fear invents cruelty for her. Pace’s silence has left a vacuum, and she fills it with the worst possible picture: “some pretty girl from the dance hall,” blonde and tan, sitting in the place where Vee had once been fed and seen. That was the chapter’s sharpest turn for me, because the erotic memory of Pace cooking after sex is still warm, still charged, and then her fear steals it from her. “She could not keep her own night” landed hard.

Pace is not on the page in person, but my feeling about him chills through his absence. The same traits that were tender before — he “leaves you the room,” he is not glued to his phone, he does not chase or crowd — become painful when Vee needs pursuit, words, proof. I do not suddenly distrust him as a predator or a liar from this page, but I do feel the cost of his restraint. Whether it is care, avoidance, divided loyalty, or something else, Vee is suffering inside the space he leaves.

Randi is also absent but vivid, and I felt the pull of her strongly through Vee’s eyes. Her Instagram is “another country,” and Vee wants it even knowing “what had bought the dream.” That is messy and very human: envy does not wait for moral clarity. Randi’s text back is warm enough to matter — “miss your face most though, gorgeous” — but because I know from the prior state how much she is hiding, it also feels like another true small thing standing in for the larger truth. Still, Vee receives it as real warmth, and I do too.

The erotic charge here is mostly remembered and displaced, not present action. It peaks in Vee remembering Pace cooking after sex: the top sheet, wine light, garlic and pepper, her body “quiet,” the sheet slipping and “her not fixing it.” That memory still has heat because it is domestic and sexual at once. Then the heat curdles when the imagined blonde takes Vee’s place. The chapter is not explicit in the way a sex scene is, but it is charged by deprivation: wanting arms around her and immediately fearing whose body he might be holding instead.

I did not feel much authorial thumb, though the chapter is heavy with image and repetition. The line that most risked feeling pointed was “the room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed,” but it worked on me because the concrete details around it earned the abstraction.

The chapter title, “Old Acquaintances,” feels bitterly right after reading. It points to New Year’s, yes, but more to the old selves and old rooms that still “know” Vee while no longer knowing her. Her bedroom, old teammates, old family rhythms, old acceptable food, even old explanations of Pace all become acquaintances rather than home. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, still feels like it is leading me through doors that are opened beautifully but not innocently. The series title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps suggesting closeness handled with distance, desire approached carefully because it may burn or stain.

What I want next is for Pace to come after Vee with words, not just warmth. My dread, from here, is that he will offer care again and still not understand that care has become unbearable without a name. I also want the term to start because Vee’s certainty about Randi’s return feels like the only stable light in the chapter, and that makes me both relieved and afraid.

Characters named or mentioned: Vivienne as the old self her room knows; Vee’s mother; Vee’s father; Pace; Randi; Cassie; Sheri; Christine/the older lady at the bar/restaurant; a bartender; Randi’s father implied through the chalet; Vee’s old synchronized-swimming friends in photos.

Setting: Vee’s childhood bedroom in her parents’ house in Ohio on New Year’s Eve, near midnight. The room is cold at night because the furnace goes into savings mode. Vee lies in a twin bed her legs outgrew after middle school, under sheets pulled to her chin. The house is dark by ten.

Objects and room details: flannel sheets; fabric softener scent used by Vee’s mother; lavender plug-in air fresheners in every room; blankets; phone charging on a little nightstand; corkboard with ribbons pinned in bunting; round ribbons, first and second ribbons, gold-fringed best-in-show ribbon; synchronized-swimming trophies on a shelf, little gold girls frozen mid-lift with toes pointed; clarinet in its case in the closet since senior year; faces/photos on the board of girls Vee once thought were her whole life; Christmas tree; ornaments in the same places; wrapping at the kitchen table; tape; scissors; low news; casserole; potatoes; good meat; Wheel of Fortune downstairs; phone message field; old text messages from Pace; photographs on Vee’s phone; Randi’s social-media feed.

Stated times/dates/durations: New Year’s Eve. The year has forty minutes left at the opening. Vee’s mother works the hospital floor and sleeps hard when she can. Vee’s father is up at five for the shop. The house is down by ten. Vee notes six hours ahead for Randi in Gstaad, nearly six in the morning there. The phone reads 11:52. Midnight passes after poppers go off and a dog barks.

Vee’s physical/state details: lying cold in bed with knees a bit bent because her legs outgrew the bed; not tired enough to sleep; alone rather than watching the ball drop downstairs; feels the ceiling close over her; feels she has been breathing bigger air for months and come home to a place that no longer fits; reaches for phone repeatedly; holds thumb over message field; starts “are you—” to Pace and deletes it letter by letter; looks through pictures in the dark; falls asleep thinly, covers to her chin.

Vee’s parents: mother works the floor at the hospital, sleeps like the dead when she can, uses the familiar fabric softener, wraps gifts with Vee at the kitchen table, makes Christmas food as love, gets Wheel of Fortune puzzles early. Father is up at five for the shop and calls letters during Wheel of Fortune. Both ask repeatedly about Vee’s “young man,” when they will meet him, and whether it is serious.

Pace-related details: no message from Pace; old messages include checking Vee got back to the dorm and asking how her day had been. Vee thinks he likely does not live on his phone and may never see typing dots. She remembers he “leaves you the room,” once appreciated and now hated. She reviews the last night: the fight if it was a fight, her question “what am I to you,” his warm, level, wrong answer, silence neither broke, no coming-after, and nothing since across break. She wonders if it is over, if he thinks it is over, or if he thinks nothing happened. She remembers he cooked for Sheri at Thanksgiving because Sheri was alone. She imagines he may be cooking for someone else over Christmas.

Photos Vee views: photo of Vee and Pace on his couch, Vee’s arm out with the phone, both laughing at nothing, Vee’s face open in a way it never is in her parents’ house; Vee in the black keyhole shirt and borrowed boots before the dance went wrong; Cassie’s induction photo of Vee mid-stride in the long step, leg out through the slit to the hip, burgundy dress lit like it holds its own light, heel buckled by Randi. Vee sent Pace the whole induction photo. Vee sent her mother a waist-cropped version, cutting away the leg and what the dress did.

Randi feed/text details: Randi’s feed shows the top of a ski run, white snow, Randi in goggles and a grin; a chalet at evening with golden windows against blue snow; Randi dressed for dinner somewhere fancy, bare shoulders, white-gold bracelet, solitaire at her throat, black mountains in the glass behind her. Vee knows what bought the dream because she saw the Miranda Holdings name in the glass room and saw Randi blanch. Vee writes: “thinking about you 💛 hope it's beautiful out there. i'm here if you want to talk. or not.” Randi replies near midnight: “you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here — dad's got us in this insane chalet, mountains for days, i'm never coming home. miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋”

Vee’s imagined Randi: glamorous, wild, awake on New Year’s Eve in Gstaad; imagined at a white-tie crush where the whole room knows Randi’s name, or slipped away with a man out of stories; Vee imagines a devastatingly handsome billionaire and a wild weekend.

Vee’s Pace jealousy/fear: without a name for herself with Pace, she feels she has no standing to ask where he is or whose he might be. She imagines “some pretty girl from the dance hall,” built from spite: blonde, tan, fashion model body where Vee’s is a stripper’s, the exact opposite of herself. She imagines Pace wanting that girl once free of Vee. She tries to return to her best night with Pace: after sex, wrapped in top sheet at the table with wine she had not drunk earlier, watching him build fra diavolo from where they left it; oil, garlic and pepper blooming, tomatoes going in, kitchen smelling right, body quiet, evening having used her well, sheet slipping and Pace seeing and Vee not fixing it. The imagined blonde replaces Vee in that memory.

Christine/older-lady/bartender memory: Vee remembers the older lady at closing saying Pace does not take advantage, she would have heard, Pace does not bring dates, and it was not Pace the jealous people were jealous of.

End of chapter: at midnight, small poppers go off somewhere down the street and a dog barks. Vee lets herself imagine having Pace’s arms around her, then cannot keep from imagining whose body he may have them around instead. She thinks she will have Randi back the day term starts: a class, a face lighting up and calling her gorgeous. Pace offers no circled hour or sure room. She lies in the too-short bed, holding both Randi and Pace and setting neither down, thinks “and what about Pace,” and sleep pulls her under while the cold room keeps watch over “the girl who was gone.”
