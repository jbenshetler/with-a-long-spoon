## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel almost embarrassingly happy for Vee before it made me hot. The publicness of it — the sheer relief of Pace saying “Vee’s boyfriend” again and again — hit me harder than the explicit sex at first, because that word has been the empty place for so long. He spends it here without drama, without being cornered, without Vee begging for it: “Vee’s boyfriend,” “the same two words every time,” and Vee hearing it “every single time.” I felt the release in my own chest.

Pace is very attractive to me in this chapter, not just sexually but socially. I love that his listening turns out to include Meg’s boots, Kayla’s paint-off-a-wall quality, Cassie’s Bricklayers, Theo’s jazz, Danny’s lifting. It retroactively makes all those kitchen nights matter: Vee talking to his back, thinking maybe she was just running on, and then learning he has kept “her friends’ names on a shelf.” That is the kind of attention the book has made erotic from the start. And he doesn’t perform being impressive; he gives other people a first line and lets them become more themselves. “He set them up” is exactly right. I trusted him more here than I have in a while. Not because all the older questions are answered, but because this is the public, social version of his care. He does not just feed Vee in private. He makes room for her people.

Vee is radiant in this chapter. She is so easy to love when she is watching her worlds come into one room. I especially liked that she does not simplify her friends into an interchangeable cheering section. She knows a fancy dinner would make Cassie “be a good sport,” and she rejects that. That felt like real growth in her tenderness: she wants Pace to meet her people, but she does not want to stage it at someone’s expense. Her joy is also very physical — the word boyfriend lands “somewhere low,” and by the parking lot the whole evening has become arousal. That made complete sense to me. Recognition becomes desire.

Randi is the person who moved most sharply for me, because she is wonderful here and also the chapter lets something tremble underneath her. On the surface, she is perfect: first there, dressed exactly right, warm with Pace, generous to Vee, publicly toasting “my beautiful best friend.” I felt the pull of her. But her hand closing hard around Vee’s when Pace says “Vee’s boyfriend” complicated that moment. Vee reads it as fierce happiness, and maybe it is partly that, but I couldn’t help feeling the pressure in it. Then later, after the toast, Randi goes into “a glazed sort of quiet,” turning the glass “a slow quarter at a time.” Vee thinks she knows that quiet as being moved. I felt less sure. The text earned my unease there: not because Randi does anything bad on the page, but because the quiet is given so much charge and because we know, as readers, that Randi has a much more complicated inner life about Vee than Vee understands. That “small sentence” still standing at a window in Vee at the end feels like the chapter itself not letting me forget Randi’s face.

Cassie remains wonderful. “You can stay,” like “the results of an audit,” is perfect. It’s funny, spare, and deeply affectionate. I felt almost teary watching her talk to Pace about how she and Vee became roommates, because Cassie’s approval matters. Kayla is delightful noise. Theo and Danny both come alive briefly because Pace knows how to ask the right question. Meg also feels more real here through Pace’s ability to remember the geography of her stories.

Trust vs suspicion: I trust Pace more after this chapter in the specific area that hurt Vee most: public naming. He gives her the word without having to be forced. I still don’t know why he couldn’t say “I love you,” and this chapter does not solve that, but “boyfriend” is not nothing. It is a big thing. My suspicion is around Randi, but it is not a simple “she’s evil” suspicion. It’s more that her warmth is too layered to be safe. The chapter itself gives me that through the hard grip, the too-bright eyes, the glazed quiet, and Vee’s own fleeting jealousy when Pace makes Randi laugh. Also, Pace and Randi “get along,” and his line — “They share an interest. … You.” — is sweet to Vee, but to me it also sounds accidentally exact in a way Vee cannot yet hear.

The erotic charge works very well for me, especially because it grows out of social tenderness. The first peak is not the blowjob; it is “Vee’s boyfriend.” The second is the parking lot kiss, when Vee pushes his hands under her sweater because the whole evening has been moving toward her body. The truck sex is hot, but what really makes it work is continuity: the September mountain road, the January county road, the remembered long-coat drive, the coat lining’s “dark bloom,” the sense that their roads together are accumulating. The line “the truck ran on steady under his one wrist the whole time like the road itself was something he was doing to her” is very charged. It could have gone too risky or mechanical, but for me it stayed inside Vee’s wanting and Pace’s competence. The only slight slack for me is that the moving-truck hand sex strains realism/safety a bit — I believed Vee’s arousal, but part of me was aware of the road. The text knows the risk, though, because it echoes her previous unsafe drive, so it didn’t feel oblivious.

The chapter title, “Among Friends,” seemed straightforward at first: Pace among Vee’s friends, Vee’s worlds gathered together. By the end it has a slyer pressure. Everyone is “among friends,” but some friendships contain more than Vee has words for. Randi’s kiss is “only what they did,” and yet that phrase itself makes me look twice. The title makes the warmth real, but it also makes the hidden currents more intimate: the trap may not be strangers or enemies, but friends.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps feeling more and more like food, care, appetite, and caution all at once. This chapter’s buffet is the public version of Pace’s feeding: not pancakes at midnight, but brisket, cornbread, sweet tea, plates constantly rearranging people. The tagline — “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” — feels especially sharp here because Vee says yes to everything: the public word, the friends gathering, the parking lot, the truck. None of it feels coerced. That is exactly why I’m nervous. The pleasure is real; the information is still incomplete.

What I want next: I want the debrief with Randi, badly. I want to know how Randi tells this night back to Vee, and whether she lets the “boyfriend” word sit cleanly or turns it in her fingers like the glass. I also want Pace and Randi alone at some point, because they clearly “get along” and Vee is reading that through a beautiful innocence. I dread the moment when Vee realizes “among friends” did not mean everyone was equally known.
