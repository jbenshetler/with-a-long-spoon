## Reader reaction

This one melted me clean through. There's no sex on the page — nothing but a fashion show for one — and it's one of the most erotically charged chapters in the book, because everything is in the *not touching*. His hand coming "partway up and he put it away again, and the putting-away cost him something she could see" — that cost, visible, is the sexiest thing on the page. She has the power now: she puts him in the chair, she keeps him there with one flat palm, she sets the terms ("You have to pick which costume you like better"). And the reveal structure — dress first, then *heels and nothing* — is her own design, her own theatre, executed in the same house where the bet's architecture used to be his.

The line that undid me: "The other is for the world. This is just for me." That's the whole book's shame wound answered in one sentence. The induction audience got the dress; he gets what was under it. And the parallel nobody says aloud: the induction walk ("the long stride, the one she'd given the induction on purpose") is now *hers to re-perform for him*, the theatre reclaimed from the stage and brought home. "His face did what the room's murmur had done, except silently, and to her it was louder."

And the small devastations: she'd zipped the dress into its bag Wednesday, then on Friday "lifted the bag off the rail and laid it across the back seat" — brought no overnight bag, nothing she wasn't letting herself plan on, *and brought the dress*. Her body planning what her mind won't sign. His boots "too large by half," a child playing dress-up — then four inches taller into the long line. The heels have been *living in his bedroom since Friday* — she's already moved in, one pair of shoes at a time. "She did not cover one inch of it." The girl who flinched at the sticky evidence, gone.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing here earned unease from me. The dress-in-the-car is a half-secret, but it's a gift-secret, not a lie. My only background note stands: Randi buckled those heels in the boutique, and Randi doesn't know any of this is happening. The sentence "the dear black straps Randi had knelt to buckle" sits in the middle of the love scene like a stone in the honey. But this chapter, on its own terms, is pure warmth.

Title: "Made-Up" — costume, invention, playing somebody else on the one night nobody has to explain it. And, I think, its opposite: the second costume is no costume at all; made-up is what she takes *off*. Also faintly "made up" as in reconciled — which is where they are. It lands, and it recolors: Halloween as the night you get to be most yourself by claiming you're not. Next: I want Randi's side of this silence. My dread-guess from here: the closer this gets to "Move in. I mean it," the louder "That's not finished" gets.
