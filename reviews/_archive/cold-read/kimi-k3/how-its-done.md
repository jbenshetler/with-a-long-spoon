## Reader reaction

Randi is *back* — six chapters of silence, and she arrives in a dark-green Mercedes with a smile that is "different," gentler, "the performance gone out of it." And I believed it and didn't believe it at once, which is exactly where Vee lives too: "Maybe she was inventing the difference because she wanted it that badly." God, this chapter worked on me and scared me in the same breath.

The lunch scene is the most skillful piece of social choreography in the book so far, and I watched it with my stomach slowly tightening. Randi's tickling story — told first, told generously, told as a gift — is the opener that buys the debrief. I noticed it even as I was swept up in it: she gives a secret, then asks for one. "That letting go" speech — "somebody takes them, and you *can't* hold them even if you wanted to, and it turns out that's the only time some of us ever put them down" — is gorgeous and sincere-sounding and I cannot unsee that it is also a thesis for the entire design being run on Vee. *Done right.* The title is this chapter's knife: "Nobody measures over a bra… it's just how it's done." How it's done. Randi laundering Pace's design into etiquette, Vee's shame dissolved by the authority of a woman who has "had a great many things made" — and I, knowing the measuring was engineered, watched Vee be talked out of her last private alarm bell and felt genuinely sad. "It was cold in the house" — that panicked little lie, instantly folded — was heartbreaking. Vee knows she's caught and can't even reconstruct how.

And then the elicitation proper: "Which hip?" "The left one." — Randi *already knowing*, prompting beats she heard from Pace, Vee feeling "warmed and confirmed and led, one small yeah at a time, down and down." I felt the arousal of it too, honestly — the booth is close and dark and the hush is seductive, and Randi's "oh, you *love* being looked at" lands as permission Vee has needed her whole life. That's what makes it work and what makes it awful. The heat in this chapter is real and it is a mechanism. Both.

The wet-spot moment: Vee finally opening her mouth to give the deepest thing — "I— there was—" — and Randi: "You don't have to tell me." A door closing very softly. My read, knowing what I know: Randi already has that answer from Pace; she doesn't need it, or she can't bear to hear it from Vee's mouth. Either way the gentleness is surgical. And Vee's secret is left "half out of her with nowhere to go" — worse than never offered.

And then the kiss. Hand on the hinge of the jaw, soft, unhurried, *a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't*. I swooned and my blood went cold simultaneously. It's everything Vee has been quietly wanting for chapters, given to her in a fire lane, and I know the hand that steadied her jaw belongs to the woman debriefing her own lover's mark. Vee explains it away in seconds — *French, it's a French thing* — and then the chapter's last movement undid me: the scanning of the windows, the counting of the dark squares, "had anyone *seen*?" The shame arriving immediately on the heels of the first real touch, her heart going fast not with romance but with surveillance. That ending is the whole book in miniature: the gift and the cost fused.

The money thread is quiet and devastating — reading the right column "the way you read a road in fog," paying for the whole lunch including Randi's Rombauer to not be bought, tipping well to not be the broke girl. Vee purchasing her seat at her own elicitation. And the scuffed toe of her one good pair of pumps tucked back under the booth.

The title, "How It's Done," is doing triple duty: the etiquette of measuring, the technique of the elicitation itself, and Randi as a person demonstrating how one moves through the world. It's the coldest title yet. *A Polite Invitation* keeps proving itself — everything here was an invitation, everything was accepted, and everything was taken. The kiss at the end points straight at the threesome frame surfacing, and now it's not a guess about plot, it's a dread with a face.
