## Reader reaction

I was swept. Completely, shamelessly swept — and I know better, and I was swept anyway, which is exactly what this book keeps doing to me. The date is a masterpiece of warmth on the page: the text that "left it with me, like it could keep"; the lobby vigil where she sees his showered hair and razor-pinked jaw and the care "ran past anything the room could see"; "May I choose?" landing as relief rather than control; the hunger that "went down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line broke me a little. She stops watching herself eat — the mother-voice absent a second time now — and lets the thread of his sentence go "and nothing bad happened." The hand-holding that feels "less like something beginning than something resolving" is the swoon peak. And then the kiss negotiation: the rule out of her mouth, his perfect acceptance, and her feet refusing to move — "I'm making an exception." I ached. I wanted it.

And underneath, cold at my own post: the right-hand rule he keeps "without seeming to think about it," a practiced system; "May I choose?" as the chapter's title — her handing the choice across, him weighing it for her. I cannot unsee the designer in the tenderness. The drip he licks off his hand made her lose the thread — and the froyo drip she let happen was her unwatching herself; the book keeps rhyming them. Is this care, or the most beautifully engineered care ever built? The page lets both stand and so do I. My trust in him as a feeling man grew; my suspicion of the frame grew exactly in step.

Cassie in the opening is again the only clean warmth — "That's not a boy. That's a man," and she *doesn't tease her about this*. Christine knowing him, easing at him, is a small character-witness I filed carefully: people who know him are glad to see him.

The title gives the chapter its hinge — the choice she hands over and the exception she makes herself; both hers, both inside his architecture. Friction: almost none. Maybe the soft-edged back half ran slightly dreamy-long, but it earned it.

Next: the kiss happens off-page-break; I expect consummation soon, and I dread the Randi reveal detonating all of this.
