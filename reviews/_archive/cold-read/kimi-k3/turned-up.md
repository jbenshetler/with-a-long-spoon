## Reader reaction

This chapter is a joy — a debrief scene done as pure girl-talk, and after the intensity of the measuring it felt like exhaling. Cassie is a gift: the highlighter set down, the pillow thrown and re-thrown, "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?" — I laughed out loud. And Vee gets to *narrate herself*, to pick which details matter, which is where the interesting filtering happens. She tells Cassie the heat, the riser, the bra, the waiting, the knees — but I noticed she edits: the wet spot stays hers alone, and "I was grinding on him" arrives as comedy ("his *package*... definitely not gay") rather than as what I know it cost him. She presents his restraint as a puzzle of orientation rather than a wound of effort. That's not her lying; it's her still not having language for what she witnessed — or choosing not to hand it over.

The warm beat that undid me: "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*... Like he'd decided I was worth it, and he was glad. Glad it was me up there." And Cassie: "No. I think I have it." The hand on the knee, once. Cassie remains the only care in the book with no design behind it, and she also caught the shirt — "You've been sleeping in that shirt all week" — which Vee had *filed under weird things about him* rather than let herself solve. That filing habit, named on the page, is Vee's whole method of not-knowing. Cassie paying attention is the closest thing Vee has to a guardian, and I love her for it.

Also notable: who is *not* in this telling. No Randi. The one person who will want this debrief most is absent from the room, and Cassie — who doesn't know about Randi's role at all — is getting the truer version than Randi ever will. That sat quietly under the whole scene for me. No suspicion beyond what I already carry; the chapter earns only warmth.

The title, "Turned Up" — the heat, turned up hours before she arrived. The detail she filed and then understood. It also plays as *her*, turned up, lit, turned on — but the radiator is the literal referent and it's perfect. My pull forward: the dress gets finished, he calls, the induction waits — and somewhere Randi is owed a version of this afternoon.
