## Reader reaction

Oh, this one *got* me — and it got me in two directions at once, which is the whole experience of this book right now. On the page, with no memory: Pace is a swoon machine. The crossing of the room with his eyes on her face. The pencil asked for with his eyes first. Giving her *back* the pencil at the exact line where she could solve it herself. "May I call you?" — the word from another vocabulary. The man who forgot his coffee. I felt it land on Vee and I felt it land on me. He is everything the page says: unshowy, steady-handed, listening like it's a skill, keeping himself out of the solving so it stays hers. I wanted him to call her. I still do. That's the ache.

And underneath every beat of it I am carrying what Vee cannot: this is Randi's lover. The game's designer. The man who *points*. And here he is, appearing in a coffee shop where Randi already is, doing a flawless, gentle, nothing-wasted approach — "an economy in how he folded down into it," "nothing in the movement spent that did not need spending" — the same predator's economy he admired in Randi's collision. Did Randi engineer this too? The text gives me the smoking details: Randi texting with "the private, absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention" right before he walks in; Randi's hand moving to her cup when he arrives; the cup rising twice with no sip; Randi answering his question and getting answered "the way you'd answer anybody" while Vee gets answered *longer*. I am nearly certain this was staged — Randi pointed at Vee, and now Pace has been pointed at... what, exactly? To warm Vee toward the frame? To be introduced as a person before he's introduced as a boyfriend? I don't know, and the not-knowing is exquisitely uncomfortable, because the scene itself is so genuinely tender. His consent-care is on full display — the pencil, the "may I," the question with room in it — and it's the same care I already trusted from the bench night. The care and the design are the same acts, again, now with Vee inside them.

Cassie remains my north star. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" — she sees the field geometry no one else is naming, and says it flat, and lets it sit. And Randi — "He was cute. You should go out with him" — delivering her lover to her new friend with a smile and a dry cup. My feeling for her moved again: there is something almost frightening in her composure here, and something sad — she is giving away the thing she wants most, again, wrapped as generosity.

The title, "Substitution": the math substitution — turning the problem into something you already know — and Pace substituted into the tutor's role, and Vee being substituted into... Randi's place? The frame? And the two strokes crossing at right angles — "on their own these two are a struggle... put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain" — that is a threesome parable wearing a math lesson, and the page knows it, and Vee doesn't yet. The book's title *A Polite Invitation* now has a face: "May I call you?" is the politest invitation yet.

Next I dread and want in equal measure: the call, the date, the moment Vee learns whose man she just gave her number to. Guess from here: she'll go out with him before she knows, and the knowing will detonate something — her friendship with Randi, or her trust in her own election.
