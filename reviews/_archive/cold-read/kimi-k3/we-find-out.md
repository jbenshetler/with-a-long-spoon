## Reader reaction

This chapter wrecked me in the good way. I was braced for the design to come crashing through the door, and instead I got the warmest, sexiest, most *honest* scene in the book so far — and the strange thing is it's Pace, the man whose hands built the whole machine, being the person I trust most on the page right now.

The opening alone did me in: *she* texts first, "the nakedness of having asked," and then his face doing "the gladness he never quite managed to hide." And the play — she's never once landed anything on him, "a wall since the day she met him," and she watches the wall flinch. The stolen shorts waved like a flag, her discovering she's faster than all that mass, flashing him ("She had never in her life done such a thing"). It's joyful. It's *hers*.

Then the spank, and "There it is," and my pulse went with hers. The bet is such a neat piece of consent engineering — she can end it with a word ("admit you lost"), and instead she chooses to be found out. The finger finding her already wet, "her body had decided without her," the shame of the *plenty* of it — that's the exact shame this book keeps circling, and his response is the undoing of it: he looks at it "like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." I wanted to cry. The sex itself — no theatre, no counting, nothing like Randi's story, just quiet and slow and the kiss on the warm pink of her — is the hottest thing in the book precisely because it's the least performed. "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness."

And the aftercare. Juice. *Juice*, faintly ridiculous, held to her mouth before anything else. Her head in his lap, "wonderful, wonderful," and she sleeps "treasured" — the word this whole ledger has been measuring. Then the scrunchies. A whole form of them, no note, "that he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." I melted. Then the curry, toasted spices, more vegetables than she's used to seeing in one pot — he *feeds* her, elaborately.

My honest wariness, small but real: "Slow. That's part of the bet too" — it wasn't part of any bet he named, and the page knows it, and she consents anyway, too far down to argue rules. That's the design's texture inside even this room: the rules get written as he goes and she signs after. And the scrunchies, found "online and never in any store in town," a prepared plenty — prepared means thought-ahead, and thought-ahead is what he *does*. But I refuse to let that spoil what the page earned: this is consummation with her yes built into its bones, and she leaves it saying "I'm going to start bringing a bag" and meaning it.

The title — "We Find Out" — is the bet's phrase turned into the chapter's thesis: we find out what her body already decided, what she wants and won't say, and also (in the kitchen, at the end) that he can be ordinary and hers. "A Polite Invitation" keeps proving itself: every escalation arrives as an offer she could refuse. *With a Long Spoon* still hums under it — she's supping with someone whose plans she can't see — but tonight the spoon fed her curry and I am not sorry.

What I want next: Pace opening that photo in Berkeley is still pending, and now I also dread/want the moment Randi hears about this — because Vee's "no Sunday, no Randi to be told" told me exactly who's waiting to be told, and that telling is the design's food.
