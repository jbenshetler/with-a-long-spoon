## Reader reaction

The first time I've been given the inside of Randi's head, and it rearranges her — and it hurts. I came in braced (this is Pace's lover, the keeper of the creed, the "He's all mine") and instead I got a woman living her best possible evening with her face turned toward two people who aren't in the room.

**Randi.** My feeling for her *moved*, hard, in two directions at once and both of them earned. The sympathy: she performs breakfast for her father — invents safe boys because "the invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table" — and hugs him "eating her revulsion" on sorority fuel, and something about that word *revulsion* sat down in my chest and stayed there. The unease: she decides, coldly and completely, to keep Vee managed — "Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways... as long as the word kept its cover." That's love and manipulation in the same sentence, and the page lets both be true. The saddest line in the chapter might be "She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be." She's not a schemer. She's a fortress with a person inside it.

**John** is flawless on purpose, and I mean that as the page's purpose: skied better than her "and spent none of it proving it," cotillion-led her across marble, conjured a sleigh out of a full cab line, and in bed "never found her underneath." I have no suspicion of him as a *danger* — she chooses everything, and the page is careful that she chooses: "She did not need it. She took it anyway." My unease is existential, not plot: he is the acceptable world's absolute best offer, and the best is a good sandwich eaten fast.

**The swoon is real and then it's slaughtered beautifully.** The lift-line meet genuinely charmed me ("You're whispering"), the dance made me melt — "she gave it up... it was the easiest thing in the world" — and the midnight kiss with silver falling got me. I let it. And then the aftermath took it apart stitch by stitch: the lipstick ring she left on him "where she had taken him in her mouth and felt her own mouth do it from a long way off" — sex as dissociation — and "The eyeliner had held. Of course it had. Nothing had happened to her eyes." And *then* the chapter shows me where the real current runs: "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best. She wished it wasn't true," and the Vee paragraph — "Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of, and her whole body knew it." "Vee was her gorgeous" might be the hottest, saddest line I've read in this book, and nobody touches anybody in it.

**Vee appears only as a text and it broke my heart.** "thinking about you 💛 hope it's beautiful out there. i'm here if you want to talk. or not." — sent from her own Ohio wreckage, post-walkout, an open door with zero demand — and Randi answers it with wallpaper: "you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here... Every word of it true. None of it the thing." Then smuggles one true line under cover — "miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋" — and turns the phone facedown. Two people who could actually reach each other, both choosing cover on the same night. I know everything; they each know nothing. It's the cruelest cut the book has given me.

**Erotic charge:** inverted by design and it works. The sex is there (the mouth-print, the ring of her lipstick) but rendered as proof of absence — "her body had spent itself on him and she remained unmoved." The actual heat lives in the chairlift elbow, the goggles-up look she'd "have minded" from anyone else, the waltz, and — most of all — in the absent: Pace's light-pressure lead she has to be *listening* for, Vee across a Tuesday table. The chapter's thesis-as-sensation: John's firm lead needs no one home; Pace's asking "made her say it, out loud, every time" — and being made to surface is what being reached feels like.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** none of the dangerous kind for John. The one thing I keep turning over is the bracelet — white gold, "locked on her wrist," "never came off," kept out of the light while her mother's tennis bracelet plays in it, catching on her hand mid-spin. The page insists on it three times and explains it zero. I'm holding it open, not solving it. And "eating her revulsion" at her father's hug — against what Vee knows about this family, I hear a seam there, and the page gives me nothing more. Holding that open too.

**Friction:** very little. The parents are almost stock (hearty dad, icy exquisite mom) and the molecular-gastronomy parade flirts with luxury-catalog, but both pay off — the breakfast is the machinery that built her, and the illusion-food is the sandwich-line's setup. The one beat I didn't fully buy is John's worked-perfection ("some string she didn't catch him pulling") — but the chapter *knows* he's an exhibit, so it reads as design.

**Titles:** "Fairytale" is her own word, texted as cover, and the chapter is the fairytale run at full conviction — scripted in advance, "the year I met your father," told to a daughter she doesn't have — and found empty. It spoiled nothing; it recolored everything. *A Polite Invitation* got a new facet for me tonight: "Pardon, pardon. Do you mind? I'm with her" / "Play along" — the whole evening is a polite invitation into a shared fiction, accepted. And *With a Long Spoon* — my reading, from the page: lying there feeling her own mouth work "from a long way off" is about as long a spoon as a person can hold. No cover tagline survives in my memory, so the titles carry it alone, and right now they're carrying: everyone in this book is dining at a distance from something.

**Next:** I want — and dread — the homecoming. Randi flying back from Gstaad with "i'm never coming home" being the truest lie she told; Vee back from Ohio into Cassie's kitchen; the brunch where both covers meet at the same table. My guess from here: the pressure from Vee's walkout and Randi's hollow NYE both vent toward each other, and "let Vee arrive at it on her own" gets stress-tested fast. Also watching: does Pace call, does John recur (my guess: he was an exhibit, one perfect specimen, gone), and what the bracelet is.

The dissociation ("from a long way off") reading as the series title's long spoon — my own reading, from the page.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** after Vee's walkout, cutting across the world to Randi is a mirror, not a stall — the two chapters rhyme (she walked out of real warmth; Randi walked into fake warmth and stayed) and the rhyme is the momentum. I trust no one more or less than the page asks: John is harmless-empty; Randi is heartbreaking and complicit in the same breath — her love for Vee is the truest thing she has and she has built a policy out of never risking it; Pace is vindicated-in-part (his asking, his reach, by the measure of the woman who hides him) without being let off Vee's hook. Mood: tender toward both women, braced for the homecoming, and carrying that facedown phone like a stone.

### Chapter record

Champagne, very cold.

Her body had spent itself on him and she remained unmoved"; he fell asleep; nothing "developed" from the holding. She got up, saw her marks on him, remade her face ("even, exact"; "The smile still worked, she checked, and meant nothing"). Verdicts: "He was safe. She had put nothing on the table and had won exactly that"; "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best. She wished it wasn't true"; "Pace went straight to her... he would never once belong in the life she had built to keep her hidden"; Vee — "her whole body knew it, and she knew exactly what it was"; the scenario she cannot run is Vee's face while told; "Vee was the best friend she had ever had"; "Vee was her gorgeous"; decision — "she would not tell her... Final: "she turned the phone facedown... went back to the bed and lay down against him again, in the warmth she could borrow and could not make, and stayed."
