## Reader reaction

This one is barely a scene — one bunk, one night, one other person asleep through all of it — and it hollowed me out more than the fight did. The empty text field did it first: "There weren't any. There was nothing she could put in there that would come back as what she needed." I've watched her retire a sentence, drown a word, wear a forgiveness — but those were all things she did near him. This is the first time I see her alone with the arithmetic, and there's no move left. "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them" — I believe her completely, and it hurts that I do.

Then the shirt turn, which is the chapter's knife. The shirt has been the portable him all along — and now she's sharp enough to see through her own reliquary: "the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." All that scent, "every last thing he did," and "none of it told her what she was to him." I felt that in my stomach. And the detail that undid me most is the smallest: she puts it on over her own sleep shirt "for the hall... a girl too far gone in a boy" — a costume for Cassie's hypothetical eye — and knows it: "It hid what it was." Even her grief has staging, and she can see the staging now.

And then the new thing: "it was worse. It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." She has spent this whole book reaching for the repair — retire the sentence, grant the forgiveness, let the climax drown the word "as she let it." This night she just lies in it, bare in the cold, leaves the shirt "in a heap where a dropped thing lands," and stays. I can't tell if that's honesty, or penance, or exhaustion — the page holds it open and I'm holding it open — but it feels like the first un-repaired thing she's done, and it frightens me a little, in both directions at once.

My feelings about people: Vee — deeper sympathy than I've had yet, threaded with a sting I didn't expect, "a man with nothing grinding in him." That line is her 3 a.m. version of Pace and I know its unfairness from his chapters — the fork, the flickers, "I'm listening now" — but I can't disprove it either; maybe he does sleep easy, and the page lets her have the thought without correcting it. Pace himself, absent: no movement, just the ache of knowing his costs are invisible to her at exactly the hour she needs them visible. Cassie — asleep below, "slow and easy," somehow the loneliest presence in the chapter: the confidante six feet away, unreachable, and the room cold in the first place because of the two inches of window that are *hers*. I half-dread, half-hope for the morning scene (guess from here).

Erotic charge: present but transposed — his shirt straight onto bare skin, "the way she wore it when no one could see," breathing him off her own shoulder. It's the erotic with the object removed; longing as the only body in the room. It worked on me precisely because there was no one to work on her.

Friction: almost none. It's a sustained single note, and if anyone arrived impatient from the Chili's aftermath they might call it static — I didn't. It's an aria and it earned its length; the shirt-turn gives it an event.

The title **"Bare"**: literal nudity, yes, but more — the stripping of every layer of mediation. Costume-shirt, sleep-shirt, then nothing. Every proxy has now failed in sequence — the sentence, the word, the forgiveness costume, and tonight the shirt — and what's left is her, bare, in a cold she stops escaping. It didn't give anything away; it landed only at the end, retroactively. **A Polite Invitation** now reads almost cruelly against a night where the one invitation she needs cannot even be typed. **With a Long Spoon** still hums its warning, and tonight the spoon's length is literal: phone, cloth, memory — everything between her and him is a handle, and no handle reaches. (The cover tagline isn't in front of me this chapter, so I won't invent it.)

What I want next: morning, and whether Cassie wakes into the evidence or through it. Whether this bare honesty becomes a fact she carries or one more thing swept. And I'm bracing for the first "how are you" from him after this — because there is no text that answers it, either.
