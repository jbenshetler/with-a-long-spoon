## Reader reaction

This might be the sexiest chapter in the book so far and almost nobody touches anybody. The erotic engine is *the telling itself* — Vee laying the spanking story on the tablecloth piece by piece for Randi, "the laying-down was its own hand on her," her body keeping faith with every word in the full clean light. I was climbing right along with her, and when Randi got to "dress up, panties to the side, knees wide, doing a private dance," I understood exactly why Vee was on her feet before she'd decided to stand. The brunch *is* the sex scene. And then the bathroom: the hard blue light that "found everything and forgave none of it," and the moment Randi's face arrives behind her eyes and her hand just — stops. "Frustrated and cowed in the same breath." That hurt. Last chapter she stood uncovered in Pace's warm lamp with no denial left; this chapter, under one *imagined* look from Randi, she blots herself dry and holds her wrists under cold water like a fainting Victorian. The same body, two witnesses, opposite instructions. That's the chapter's whole knife, and I felt it go in.

Randi. God. "There's my girl," keeping the table, the caught hand, "You look well" — and then later, "You're my best friend in the whole world," and the kiss that stays a beat too long, draws off, comes back "with a little more in them." I swooned, plainly. I wanted Vee to have exactly that: to be picked out of everyone. And underneath the swoon, the sour note the page itself has been tuning for chapters: Randi *walked her* through every explicit beat — "Walk me," "How was it settled," "*Inside*?" — and Randi is Pace's lover, and "Some girls have all the luck" is a very strange thing to say to the woman describing your own man's hands, if she knows. Does she know? The page won't tell me, and I've learned to hold it open. The Radford story also landed strangely shaped — too perfectly calibrated to send Vee over the edge, right down to "perfectly willing to take care of myself," and then Vee fleeing to do precisely that, and then: "Sticky fingers?" The floor left the room for me too. The relief of the pastry-explanation, and then Vee standing there unable to tell "whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." *She sat down. She did not find out.* I found that restraint exquisite and maddening in the right way.

The one-bite beat quietly undid me: Randi takes one neat bite, says "Delicious," closes the subject, drinks her coffee black. Tastes everything, finishes nothing. And then the final image — the glaze on Vee's lower lip from "a pastry she had never once touched." The only way it got there is Randi's mouth. Vee gets her sweetness secondhand, possibly without even knowing what she's tasting. That's the whole chapter, maybe the whole book, in one thread of sugar. The title means exactly that: one bite is all Randi ever takes, and one bite — borrowed, indirect — is all Vee ever gets. *A Polite Invitation* keeps meaning more: "Sticky fingers?" is an invitation wearing deniable clothes, if it's anything. *With a Long Spoon* still reads like the proverb — and Randi's art is the long spoon itself, tasting everything at a distance. I don't have a retained tagline, so I won't riff on one.

Friction: essentially none. Public-brunch explicitness could have strained; it didn't — the chapter manages the voices, the glances, the dropped register. Pace of the scene earned its length because the scene's length *was* the arousal curve.

What I want next: I want to know whether Randi knew who "he" was. I want Vee's guardless face to someday learn that the woman she debriefs is the lover of the man she's describing. I want the Thursdays. Guesses from here, marked: Randi knew (guess); "Sticky fingers?" was aimed (guess, held gently); the Radford boy may be real and still have been deployed (guess).
