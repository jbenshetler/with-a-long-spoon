## Reader reaction

This is the first time I've been inside Pace's morning, on the page, and it warms him considerably — which is exactly the trouble. Riding with Sheri, he gets *past the name before he'd decided to be*, telling her about the redhead running her thumb down the walnut joint he spent a weekend on, pulling the math book off the shelf. "He heard himself going and didn't stop." And Sheri's "Listen to you" — with him genuinely not knowing what there is to listen to — is the tenderest, most disarming beat the book's given me for him. He's smitten and doesn't see it. After all the engineering I've been dreading, here he is unguarded, and I felt it.

But the chapter doesn't let me rest there. "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" / "Randi. That's not finished." Two women at once, and Sheri runs the arithmetic anybody could run: "If that's not already complicated, it will be." That's the vise inside the warmth — the reader knows how *not* separate these two threads are, how Randi is the closer delivering Vee to this same man. His silence at that line landed heavy.

Sheri's a good new voice — hard, funny, "hell of a backside on her, though," remarks on women like weather. I liked her instantly. Her account of Vee Thursday ("sat by herself, glaring at the floor... danced with nobody... left alone") is another angle on that bar, and it sits oddly next to the bartender's earlier correction about the chair and the jealousy — I can't tell yet if Sheri *is* that bartender or a separate friend who was also there. Holding that open.

"You want me to not like her?" / "No. She couldn't take your heat" — that exchange told me what Sheri's loyalty is worth ("everything, on the other side") and how casually lethal she can be. I trust her to be honest with him in a way nobody in the operation is.

Heat is nil; this is a talk-on-a-climb chapter and earns its short length. **Hills and Valleys** reads first as the literal road — climbs that take the breath, the long descents where they can talk — and then, on a beat, as the emotional terrain and maybe the shape of a graph that lies in stretches. What I want: to see whether this soft, smitten Pace survives contact with the machine he's running, tomorrow at his door. Guess from here: Sheri's "it will be" complicated is a promise the book means to keep.
