# Cold read — On Her Floor

## Reader reaction

This one swept me up and then quietly gutted me, and it did both with the same hand — which is exactly the thing I've been dreading and wanting from Randi all along.

Let me start with the swoon, because it's real and I'm not going to shortchange it. That kiss is *extraordinary*. It goes on and on, generous and unhurried, and the whole build to it — the held inch, "the held inch became its own long event," Randi's face at a distance Vee's never had it, "the perfume beneath it that Vee had been quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping the file" — that landed on me like a struck bell. I've watched Vee not-look at this wanting since the parking-lot goodbye, and here the page finally lets her look, and it's overwhelming. "a want so far past the size of its excuse that it frightened her even as she sank further into it." The heat is total: her body making "the same humiliating announcement it always made... and making it for Randi." I wanted it for her. I wanted the rest of it as badly as she did.

And then the trap closed, and it's the softest, cruelest thing in the book so far. "You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it." Randi folds the whole thing back into a bit for a boy named Harrison who exists purely to be performed at, hands Vee the night "neat and folded," and Vee — undone, ringing, slick, wide open — has no choice but to take the folded version, because it's "the only thing held out to her." That last paragraph is devastating: her body "had not been told," she wants to do it again "so badly her hands were not quite steady, and that there was no version of the night in which she could ask." This is precisely what I said I was waiting to see — what Randi does with a success that hurts. She engineers the wanting and then denies it a name. It's the exact grammar of "boyfriend, not love," now in Randi's mouth as *a show, not a kiss*.

Because make no mistake — this was **orchestrated**, and my suspicion is fully, coldly armed now. The borrowed skirt Randi "knew right where it was," a scrap she was so sure about, that she knew would climb on Vee's longer legs (Cassie called it flat: "That hem's not going to stay put"). The drinks that arrive unbidden — "a second margarita was in front of her; she hadn't seen Randi order it." The hands on Vee's hips widening her dancing "an arc Vee would never have given herself," lifting her arms and *holding* them so "there was nothing Vee could do about it, because Randi had her hands." Getting her exposed to a room, then leaning in — "Your panties are showing" — to light the shame, then immediately: "Let's put on a show." Every step of tonight was Randi walking Vee out past the edge of her own carefulness and taking what she wanted under cover of a joke.

But here's what keeps it two-valence and keeps me honest: I don't think it was *only* a performance for Randi either. "Randi came back from it a moment behind. She didn't speak — only looked at Vee, and drew a breath, and shook her head, blinking." That's the cracked architect from Among Friends, the one who overshot her toast to "year." She wanted this too. So it isn't clean using — it's Randi taking something she genuinely aches for and then refusing to let it cost her anything, refusing to let it be real out loud. That's worse, somehow, and more human.

The **bracelet** finally paid off, and beautifully. The plain heavy white-gold band that never comes off "is called Love." Locked on, no clasp, "Taking it off requires tools." "It's a real commitment." I felt the floor drop when I read that. In a book where the man won't *say* love and now the woman kisses Vee and calls it a show — the one thing actually named Love is a hard metal band welded shut on Randi's own wrist, worn *for someone*, that she can't remove. Whose? For Pace? I don't know, and the page won't say, and that silence is loud. Vee "tucked it away with the thousand glamorous things Randi knew that she didn't" — she has no idea she just touched the marker of the very thing being kept from her.

**Brooke** — I clocked her "kind after all" beat and I don't buy it, or rather I buy that it's calculated. Randi already told me: "She'll spend a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime." The Chambord in the trash-can punch is the whole woman. So when Brooke carries Vee's history "by heart" and pointedly *doesn't* mention the morning Vee came in wrecked — that's not mercy, that's the same expensive discretion. Though it did make me tender toward Vee, who "braced" for it and felt gratitude instead.

**Vee** — I love her and I ache for her. The thing that's new and lovely: for once she *let herself want to be looked at*. "in Randi's skirt the wanting wasn't hers to answer for" — she borrows Randi's permission along with the skirt, holds her own eyes in the mirror "instead of dropping them." That's growth, and it's also the exact hook Randi baits. The heels are back doing their quiet work — "These told the foot what to do," she stands "an inch taller and better made, held." Everything about tonight is Vee being *held* and *run* and loving it. And Pace is barely a thought — "He gets me Friday. He gets me Sunday. You get me Saturday night" — she splits herself between them without a tremor of seeing they might be the same story.

The one place I felt the author's hand at all was the hemline arithmetic running a beat long — "the arithmetic of it — how high was still a joke, how high stopped being one" — but honestly it earns its length, because the exposure *is* the mechanism, so I'll allow it.

**The title.** "On Her Floor" — I read it three ways and they stack. Literal: the dance floor, Randi's floor, her turf, her sorority, her party, her rules — Vee is playing an away game all night and doesn't know it. But it snapped back on me from the very first exchange: the "yes" that came out "fast, a yes with no floor under it." Vee has no floor under her — no footing, nothing to stand on when Randi asks — and tonight she's *on Randi's floor* in every sense, run around by the wrists with nowhere solid to put her feet. It's a beautiful, quiet title. It didn't give anything away going in; it recolored on the exit.

*A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — the invitation to the mixer *was* the polite one, "Wanna go to a party with me?" answered before the question finished. And the long spoon — you sup with the devil with a long spoon — feels closer than ever: whatever Randi is, Vee is supping right out of her hand tonight, no spoon at all.

What I want and dread: I dread that call — the debrief, *still* not on the page, now with this to report. I dread that this kiss gets reported to Pace, or was *for* Pace. I want to know who the Love bracelet is for. And I'm waiting, harder than ever, for Sheri, who feels like the only person in this world who might hand Vee a floor to stand on.
