## Reader reaction

So here it is — the machine in full daylight, narrated from inside the machinist, and it's the most seductive and most sickening chapter yet.

The opening bed scene is Pace's POV and it's genuinely, expertly hot — he reads the difference between a woman performing release and one "delivered to it," he times his entry to her undefended aftershock, and then, into "the warm disorder of her," he plants the question: *Would you like to taste another woman?* And she comes helplessly, "ahead of herself." I felt the erotic charge and the horror land in the same beat, which is the whole trick: he harvests an involuntary orgasm as *data*, as truth extracted before she can "make the wanting mean anything safer," and then rebrands the extraction as *gentleness* — "let it come out laughing, dressed as play, before she had to look it full in the face. She deserved that gentleness." He is planting a suggestion at her most porous and calling it respect for her freedom to choose. The book has never shown the engineering this nakedly while the engineer narrates himself as tender.

The pointing game on the quad is the same move stretched to a quarter-mile. He *lets* her believe the choosing is hers — "I point. You pick" — and watches her stop being "a good sport" and start "shopping." The awful, precise line: "He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could." Except he *is* choosing — he steers her past the mirrors and the matched pairs and the closed projects toward exactly one door.

And then Vee. My first sight of the jacket's protagonist, and she's rendered as the opposite of everything Randi's grammar approves — lush, uncurated, "nothing apologized for," athletic under the abundance, red hair sparking copper, cinnamon freckles, throat bare to the sky, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." I loved her instantly and feared for her instantly, because I'm watching her get *pointed at*. Randi's bare "Yes" — "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — is the realest thing she's said in the book. And the collision is a masterpiece of predation dressed as accident: Randi's dance-trained body engineers the bump so cleanly that *Vee* is the one who flies her hand to her mouth and apologizes. The hunted apologizing to the hunter.

What the page earned as suspicion, hard: Pace "had not chosen the spot in advance. He chose it now… for the angle it gave on the doors." And Vee turns out to already share a stats class with Randi. So either the universe is being romantic, or Pace knew who would come out of that dining hall and ran the whole "spontaneous" game to deliver Randi to a woman already inside her orbit. The page holds it ajar — his POV reads as genuine improvisation and genuine surprise — but the seams are there and I can't unsee them. Coupled with Brooke collecting his details last chapter, I feel a larger apparatus I can't yet map.

"Let's go feed you" closes it, and the feeding-as-opening motif snaps shut like a trap. I'm swept up and I'm uneasy, and both are earned — the chapter didn't manufacture the shadow, it *is* the shadow.

The title: "The Pointing Game" is literal on its face and, on one beat's reflection, the name of the engine — he points, she thinks she picks. *A Polite Invitation* now has a referent: the manufactured collision-and-apology is the polite social door pried open onto Vee, who will be *invited*. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil with a long spoon — reads now as Pace at his light pole, "far enough back to be nobody," keeping exactly that careful distance.

What I want next: Vee, from inside Vee. I dread how warm the invitation will be.
