## Reader reaction

This chapter swept me up and made me uneasy in exactly the proportions the book keeps training me for. On the surface it's the most swoon-forward scene yet: he texts and doesn't make her answer, and that single restraint eases "something behind her ribs" — the wait-rule Randi killed, now felt as tenderness. The dinner is a slow undoing. The three-word title, "May I Choose," lands as the pivot of the whole thing: she hands him the menu and "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." And then I feel the second meaning behind mine — *choose*, when I know he engineered the choosing already. She thinks she's the one making an exception at the end; I watched him hand her the whole room and let her walk into it.

The feeding motif came roaring back and it terrified me a little. She's ravenous, hasn't eaten since breakfast, and then: "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." The portioning voice — her mother's *that's enough* — goes silent. Last chapter I flagged the un-portioning as the harvest working, and here it's rendered as pure relief: "the most restful thing she had felt in as long as she could remember." She stops keeping up her end of the conversation, stops bracing for "the bill of a thing to come due," and lets him carry it. Every one of those is a defense going down, and the page frames each as a gift. That's the ache — I can't tell her the melting *is* the mechanism.

The right hand: "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." Pure economy-of-movement, his signature, "one more thing he knew that she didn't." I don't know what the rule *means* but the page made me notice it, so I'm holding it.

Erotic charge: real, and almost entirely in restraint and food and the single hand at the end. The bread, the smoke-dark dip "to fold you under," the brass cages built to hold what's too hot to hold — sensory and warm. The peak is the hand on the walk home: "less like something beginning than something resolving." I felt that. And the ending genuinely got me — the rule spoken, him not arguing, letting it close, and her feet not going. "I'm making an exception." I swooned and I dreaded, both.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no *new* seam from the text here — he's flawless, kind, unhurried, and Christine's genuine gladness even vouches for him. The suspicion I carry is entirely from prior knowledge, not manufactured by this page. That's the discomfort: nothing here is off, and that's what unsettles me.

Book/series titles: *A Polite Invitation* rings loud — this whole date is an invitation with all the room "left with her," and the politeness *is* the trap's texture. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — quiet on the page but I felt the sweetness she's eating with no spoon at all, both hands, no watching.
