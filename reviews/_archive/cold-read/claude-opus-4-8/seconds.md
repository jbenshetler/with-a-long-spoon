## Reader reaction

This one's quiet and it hurts. She built a sentence for weeks — an offering shaped *around* the words she isn't allowed to say first — and she finally set it down: "I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal." It's a beautiful line, and I felt her nerve in it, the poem-memorized calm of someone who has rehearsed courage. And then the small held silence, the two breaths she'd never heard him take — that landed on me as genuinely as anything; for one beat I thought *he's moved, he's about to meet her.*

And he doesn't. "I love how good we are together." "What you do — what your body does." The pivot from *you* to *what your body does* went cold on me even before she registered it. He answers her reach for love with an appraisal of the sex, of the *thing they have* — and I clocked immediately that "the two things had not been the same shape." She feeds him the most naked, non-nakedly-worded love she can, and he returns a compliment on the arrangement's performance. He held her, kissed her hair longer than usual — real warmth, I don't doubt the affection — but the words did not come back, and he steered her body into settling so the *wait* behind her breastbone went "into the body where small waits go." That's the ache: she buries it in real time, using the warmth itself as the burial.

The laundry-room coda is where it cut deepest. Away from the quilt, the sentence comes back and she can't finish the thought "because either end of it hurt." "Maybe he understood and chose to say it like that. Maybe it had gone past him entirely." She leaves it ajar — and I love that she leaves it ajar, because so do I; I genuinely can't tell whether he deflected with precision or simply didn't catch it, and the not-knowing is the whole point: "the one person who could resolve it was the one person she could not ask." And then the pile — "small flickers, moments across the fall when what she knew about him had had to do small repair work to keep functioning. This joined them. She did not look at the pile." That's the closest she's come to seeing it, and she still won't look.

No suspicion earned that wasn't already earned; this is the apparatus tightening by one exact turn. My trust in his tenderness held but got a colder draft: he *reliably* does not give her the thing when the giving would cost him the position of the one who says it first. The heat is nil here and correctly so — this is aftermath and interior, and it's the strongest interior chapter I've had.

**Title — "Seconds":** the second cup of coffee already set out for her; his *second* breath she'd never heard; the beat "longer than his stillness had been the moment before." But most of all, after "you fed me a meal" — she asked, obliquely, for *seconds*: for the sentence returned, a second helping of what she gave. She doesn't get them. The meal metaphor she built to reach him becomes the exact measure of what he withholds. On reread it's cruel and precise.

**Book / series titles:** *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — "he that sups with the devil needs a long spoon." Nothing new confirmed, but this chapter is the spoon: she reached across the distance and he kept it. The politeness of the deflection — warm, held, kissed — is exactly the "polite" that keeps the true thing at arm's length.

**What I want next:** for the pile to be looked at. She's now named, twice over, the shape of a man who won't give the words — will one of those "small repairs" finally fail? I dread that Randi's world closes in before Vee ever looks. *Guess from here:* the retired sentence will come back at the worst possible moment.
