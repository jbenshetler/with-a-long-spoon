## Reader reaction

# Felt read

Oh, this is a lovely chapter. I came in braced — my whole carry-forward had me bracing for the debrief phone call, for Randi cracking, for the cold seam. And instead the book handed me *Sheri*, finally, in person, and she is a delight, and I dropped my guard almost as fast as Vee did. That laugh Vee lets out — "Loud, from the middle of her, apropos of absolutely nothing" — I felt it. The whole first movement is Vee dressing to fight a rival she invented, and the enormous relief of the joke landing: the invented blonde who "didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand." That's a wonderful line. The blonde got dismantled once already (verbally, by Randi), but here it dies for real, by being physically crowded out of the room by a five-foot-nothing woman with crooked makeup.

**Sheri** is the best new thing in the book. She lands instantly and fully — the volume, the calibration under the volume ("there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when"), the "motorboat them things" making Pace *blush*. I've been carrying Sheri for chapters as "the one person Pace can't manage," the unmanaged mirror, and the chapter delivers exactly that and shows me *why* she's unmanageable: she came out last March and lost her whole family and church in a month, and Pace showed up on her porch with donuts and fixed her lock and never handed her one false word. That's the deepest look I've gotten at Pace-as-he-actually-is, delivered by the one witness who owes him nothing and flatters no one.

And crucially — this recolors Pace for me in a *good* direction, after two chapters of my trust eroding. Sheri's account is not the manage-a-room gift, it's the same man doing it where there's no audience and nothing to win. "He never handed me one false word — no *they'll come around*, no *their loss*." Easy words for a hard thing versus a hard thing met with presence. That's the cleanest evidence I've had that Pace's attentiveness is *real* and not just method. It doesn't erase the Randi seam — he's still lying to Vee by omission every day — but it puts weight on the other side of the scale. He held Sheri's grief without a false word; he blushes; he stands his headlights on her door till the light comes on. I liked him more than I have since early on.

The blade in the chapter is small and quiet. "We cycle." A *we*, established, out riding the county "all this while without her." And "She dances." Vee registers that Pace handed her "the one [fact] with a blade in it… like a dried spoon." For a moment I felt Vee's jealousy re-form onto a *real* woman — but the chapter is careful: the hug Sheri gives Pace "wasn't the kind you gave a lab partner," Vee's heat "sat up confused, because the body was all wrong and the hug was not." And then Sheri turns out to be gay (I knew this from carry-forward, but Vee *doesn't*, and the chapter lets Vee arrive at the whole texture of it before the "I came out last March" lands and resolves it). Nicely done — Vee's jealousy gets a real object and then has it dissolved by information, twice over.

**Pace** blushing is genuinely disarming and I bought it completely. "The single most disarming thing she'd watched him do." The man who's easy everywhere, embarrassed only here, only by Sheri. It tells me the Sheri-license is real and singular.

The thing I'm turning over: this chapter is almost *entirely* a relief chapter. Randi is completely absent — not even mentioned except obliquely ("the redhead had opinions" — Sheri's December line about Vee, which lands oddly now because *I* know Randi is the redhead-adjacent... wait, no, Vee is the redhead here, Sheri calling Vee the redhead). The debrief call I was braced for hasn't happened. The cracked architect from last chapter is offstage. So the dread I carried in got *deferred*, not discharged, and I notice the deferral. The warmth here is real and I enjoyed it fully, but part of me is aware the book is giving me a happy, uncomplicated night precisely when I was most braced — and I don't fully trust a reprieve this clean this late. That's earned suspicion, not manufactured: the pattern of this book is warmth-with-a-cold-seam, and this chapter is nearly all warmth. The seam is thin here: only that "we cycle / she dances" blade, and the fact that Pace's whole history with Sheri — "he hasn't been home since he was sixteen," went bad with his daddy — is real information Vee gets that deepens him without touching the *one* thing being kept from her.

**Erotic charge:** low and I don't mind. This is a 0–1 chapter, no sex, and it's right that there isn't. The closest thing to charge is Sheri's look traveling down Vee's body and *enjoying* it — "a soundless, appreciative *oh*, cocked, held, like a word being kept for later" — which is charged but affectionate, not aimed. The chapter isn't trying to be hot and I didn't want it to be.

**Friction:** very little. The prose is doing its thing well. If anything the density of good lines about Sheri risks tipping into her being a bit too perfectly calibrated a truth-teller — "there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when" is the author telling me how to read her, slightly. But it's a small thumb. I also clocked "Fear needs a face, so you give it one" — a hair too neat, Vee narrating her own psychology in aphorism. Minor.

**The title — "Another Round":** on the surface it's the drinks, the ciders that keep arriving, Sheri ordering. But sitting with it: the *last* chapter was "Among Friends" and before that a whole reunion; this is the roadhouse structure repeating — literally "a round" was an early chapter (per my ledger, `a-round.md`). "Another Round" says: here's the same shape again, another gathering, another table, another vetting. And a "round" is also a round of a fight — the chapter literally frames the Sheri/Vee meeting as "the world's most mismatched prizefight." So it's drinks, it's a repeat of the social-vetting structure, and it's a bout Vee walks in armored for and wins by disarming. It doesn't give anything away; it sits pleasant and lands harder once you see the fight in it.

**The book's title and tagline** — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." Nothing in this chapter tightens the trap visibly; that's part of what makes me uneasy about how clean the night is. But there's a quiet structural note: Vee now has *both* wings of Pace's life vetting her and welcoming her — Cassie knighted him, Sheri knighted her — and the one person in Pace's life who'd have the standing to say the true thing (Sheri, the unmanaged one) doesn't know there's a game to tell. Everyone Vee trusts is being brought inside a circle none of them can see the shape of. The reprieve *is* the trap tightening: the more real and good and witnessed it all is, the harder it will be when the seam opens.

**What I want / dread next:** I still want that debrief phone call. Randi's been offstage two chapters now while she's the one visibly cracking — the book is holding her back and I feel the withholding. *Guess from here:* the Brooks & Dunn dance ("Saturday next," "leave them shoes home") is being loaded like a chekhov gun — Sheri's dance was the pending thread from way back, and now it's scheduled on the page. I dread that dance a little, in a good way; something happens there. And I dread the collision of the two worlds Pace keeps in separate rooms — Randi and Sheri have never been in a scene together, and Sheri is the one who can't be managed. If those two are ever in a room, or if Sheri ever meets Randi and clocks something... *guess:* Sheri is the character who breaks the game open, or nearly. She's built for it — she has Pace's full trust, zero investment in the lie, and a nose for men making down payments.
