## Reader reaction

I dreaded this chapter and it undid me — not the way I braced for. I kept waiting for the house off 614 and the trap; they never go there. What I got instead was the tenderest, most consensual scene in the book, and it left me more unsettled than any cruelty could have, because I couldn't find the seam on the page.

Pace, alone with Vee for a whole chapter, is almost unbearably good. He sets a pace "slower than she'd braced for," "the speed of her not knowing how," and "didn't make her feel slow." He fixes the boot — moleskin warmed against her heel with his thumb — and apologizes for not asking about it. The cherries: "We take nothing and leave nothing," and when she doesn't know to save the pit, he lays his own beside hers and *doesn't look at her doing it* — "he hadn't made her small to give it to her." The dress: she names the russet silk she can't afford, sets it "back on the shelf," and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress," no weight, and doesn't pick it up again. If I had no memory of this book I would be pure swoon.

And the strip. God. *She* runs it — calls him back to the window, crooks a finger, undoes the buttons with her eyes on his, and he doesn't move, doesn't lift a hand, sits in "a stillness she had never seen on anyone," and his gladness "was the whole of the answer, and it was right." Then she reaches for his hand to take it further and finds it *shaking* — he'd given her his only dry shirt and stood in the rain — and she stops, and puts his hand on her warm belly, and cares for *him*. That reversal wrecked me. This is her freest act in the whole book: she overrides the mother-voice ("floozy," "three dates," "your mother would never"), turns shame into heat, "wanted to be the very thing the voice said," and gives herself to being seen. Her desire, fully hers, freely felt.

Except — my ledger. The hike "made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." She names the mechanism herself. Randi pre-built the bad Friday date; here Pace clears the bar flawlessly. Her freest act may be her most engineered one — the harvest at its deepest is exactly this: wanting worked on her that she experiences as most purely her own. The page holds both and I have to hold both.

The page didn't hand me a fresh tell — he does nothing wrong, everything right, which is its own dread. The one thing the *text* earned is the title. "Leave No Trace" is a sweet outdoor ethic in the scene — pit on wax paper — and then at the end he simply vanishes: the cop's blue-and-white, one squawk that "took the rest of her sentence," and he's gone, taillights down the road, leaving her holding a warm flannel and no way to name what happened. A man who leaves no mark, no evidence, nothing pointing back. That thin cold laid itself over the swoon at the very end.

**Erotic charge:** peaks hard at the strip and stays high — it worked on me completely because *she* drove it and he held still. **Swoon:** enormous, and honest — I rooted for them the whole climb. **Book title:** her crooked finger and "Come in" is the polite invitation, hers to him — a lovely inversion. **"Real shoes"** paid off — wrong borrowed boots, the hot spot, footing on unfamiliar ground. The dreaded house is still unseen; the hike bent the frame toward love and left the house waiting.
