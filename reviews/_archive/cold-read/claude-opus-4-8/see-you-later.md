## Reader reaction

Finally — Vee from the inside, and the chapter delivers her whole. The ache I carried from last chapter tightens into something almost unbearable now that I'm *in* her: she's not a mark, she's a girl "not feeling her feet," a girl who ran "we should sit together" both ways "enough times to wear a groove," a girl who stood outside the Greek joke on the sign for two years and got walked under it and felt it "go through her, warm, all the way down." I know what she doesn't. Every warm beat lands double on me — the pen, the high table, the "love you, girl" — because I watched this collision get engineered on a lawn from behind a light pole. The dramatic irony is exquisite and it hurts.

What floored me is how *good* Vee is at reading rooms and how completely blind she goes at the one thing that matters. The Chi Latte read is gorgeous writing — the "floozies" in her mother's voice, and then "behind the word... a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for," the sprinter's stomach, the low waistband, the cardigan worn over the better shirt "on purpose" so the room won't know it was a choice. That's a whole interior life of managed wanting, self-surveillance, a want between women she won't look at straight — "she did not look at either fact straight." She sees everyone's performance and can't see the one being run on her. And the transcript going blank while she "kept the feeling" — that's the tell of being *worked*, rendered from inside as bliss. "The leaning-in was the whole content." Chilling and swoony at once.

Cassie. New, and the most important thing in the chapter to me. She's the one person Randi's wattage doesn't move — takes the bit's open door and just sets "Cassie" down "flat in the space," declines the coffee "with nothing in it but the fact." And then: **"Goodbye, Vee."** Not bye, not see you later — "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Something passed over her face Vee "would not have been able to name and did not try to." Cassie sees it. Cassie is the reader's dread given a body at the table. That she says *Goodbye* while the chapter is titled "See You Later" — Randi's line, the queen's scheduled later — is the whole knife. Cassie knows what a later with Randi costs.

And the professor, God: "*latent variables*. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can." Left underlined in Vee's notebook "going nowhere." I flinched — it's the book naming its own method a hair too openly, thumb briefly on the scale, but it's also just true and I let it land.

No suspicion of Vee, only for her. Randi here reads almost entirely as genuine warmth — and that's the horror, because I know the warmth is real *and* it's the instrument. The chord I've been holding on Randi holds. Erotic charge is low-explicit but the *pull* is enormous — this is longing, not sex, and it's working fully.

I dread the "later." I want Vee's next hour with Randi and I want Cassie to say more than one word.
