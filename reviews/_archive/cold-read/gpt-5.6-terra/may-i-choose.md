## Reader reaction

I felt myself soften right alongside Vee here. The dinner is almost overwhelmingly tender in its attention: Pace dressing in the things he already cares for, asking “May I choose?”, ordering without making her ignorance embarrassing, and then simply letting her eat. The hunger landed hard on me—not just the food, but the blessedness of her not having to monitor herself. “She let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could.” That is swoony, and sad, and working very well on me.

Pace earns my trust in the exact moment it matters most: Vee states her rule, and he accepts it without persuasion, sulking, or a waiting game. Then her staying, making the exception herself, feels hot because the page has made that choice unmistakably hers. The charge peaks in the hand-holding—the hand she has watched feeding her, now around hers—and in that final taut few inches after he has already backed off. It is not explicit sex, but it is intensely erotic.

Cassie deepens for me too. Her teasing turns serious at Vee’s explanation, and “That’s not a boy. That’s a man” gives Vee’s relief a name without taking it away from her. Christine’s warmth toward Pace quietly reassures me; he seems known and cared for in a wider life.

I do still carry the reader’s unease about the concealed arrangement with Randi, but this chapter itself does not make Pace feel coercive. If anything, it gives Vee room repeatedly. The only friction is that Vee’s surrender feels so total—she loses the thread, the room, time—that I can feel how vulnerable she is inside the pleasure. But the page does not make it sinister; it makes it restful.

“May I Choose” illuminates the whole chapter: Pace asks before choosing the meal, Vee says yes, and later she chooses the exception to her own rule. It also brushes directly against the book’s title, *A Polite Invitation*: courtesy is part of the seduction here, and the invitation is potent because she can refuse it. *With a Long Spoon* and the packet’s promise still leave me wondering what sort of danger or distance will eventually sit inside all this warmth. I very much want the kiss next—and, from here, I guess I dread how Randi will feel when this becomes real on the page.
