## Reader reaction

Whiplash, in the best way — I was braced for the fallout of the porch confrontation and instead I'm dropped into what reads like years earlier, Randi's memory of a ski trip with her parents and a man named John. It took me a beat to place it (no date stamp, no explicit "flashback" framing — I orient off "Randi" and the mother/father dynamic), but once I did, I was completely inside it. This is the fullest look I've gotten at Randi's interior life and her family, and it recolors everything I already knew about her.

Her parents are drawn in a few brutal strokes — the father's hearty, oblivious approval ("how many hearts she had broken this fall"), the mother "all edges and points," the invented suitors "safer than the real ones." I feel for the girl performing at that table, "eating her revulsion" behind a sorority-trained smile. That single image told me more about why Randi guards herself than any prior scene did.

John himself is a marvel of controlled charm — the dry-cleaning bit, the fake children on the lift, the sleigh conjured out of nothing, the tailcoat, the truffle dinner. I felt the pull of the fantasy right alongside Randi — "she was warm all along that side, where he was" — and then the gut-punch of "and cold everywhere the fur didn't reach." That line, and its echo later ("She was warm only where she touched him... the cold did not belong to the room. It was hers"), is devastating and exact. This whole chapter is building toward that cold, and it lands.

The dancing comparison is the chapter's real needle: "He was a better dancer than Pace." I felt the vertigo of that thought — easier, effortless, no work — set against "Pace's lead was the lightest pressure... That was more work, and tonight, gliding, she thought she was done with the work." Then the kiss at midnight interrupted by "For one second, from nowhere, Pace." That's the tell that this whole gorgeous night is being measured against him even as it's happening — and losing to him even while she's trying to let it win.

The ending turns the whole chapter inside out. "Pace went straight to her... he found the person she kept hidden and did not look away." And then — new, and huge — Vee: "Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of, and her whole body knew it, and she knew exactly what it was." This is the first time the text has let Randi name, even privately, that what she feels for Vee is not just fondness or management — it's desire she recognizes and is choosing to conceal, deliberately, permanently, out of fear of losing her. "She was not brave enough to lose her." I did not expect this chapter to be the one that finally says it plainly, and it landed hard.

The closing texts — Vee's sweet, unguarded "thinking about you 💛" against Randi's curated "it's honestly a fairytale out here," with the one true line smuggled in at the end ("miss your face most though, gorgeous") — is quietly devastating. This is Randi doing to Vee, in miniature, exactly what Pace does to Vee: giving warmth that is real but withholds the actual thing.

I trust the text completely here — no suspicion of manipulation in the telling itself, just Randi's own unsparing self-assessment. This is the most emotionally honest she's been on the page, even though the honesty is entirely private, entirely unspoken to anyone who matters.
