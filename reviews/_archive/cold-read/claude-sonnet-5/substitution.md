## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction**

Well, there he is. After two chapters of hearing about the "pointing game" from Pace and Randi's side, I finally watched it executed live, on Vee, in her own POV — and it's worse than I expected, in the best/worst way a book can manage. Every beat of the coffee-shop tutoring is exactly the tenderness-and-attention play I was braced for, and it still worked on me the way it worked on Vee. The math scene — "he had given her the question and given her back the pencil" — is genuinely lovely craft as a portrait of care, and I felt the swoon in my own chest even while knowing what it is. That's the trap the chapter wants and I walked into it with her.

What chilled me wasn't Pace, oddly — it was Randi's hands. "Twice the cup came up to her mouth and went back down, and no sip happened." That's a woman performing calm while doing something live and risky, and Vee reads it as boredom. I *know* better and it still hurt to watch Vee explain it away. And then Randi's closing move — "a man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call" — is such a precise, expert little push, dressed as a friend's excited aside. It's the same fluency I clocked in the last chapter, now aimed directly at sealing Vee in.

Cassie remains the only person in the room telling the truth, and she does it in six words: "He looked at her — the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." That's devastating and funny at once, and it's aimed *at Randi*, not incidentally — Cassie is naming, out loud, that Pace performed indifference to Randi that doesn't match what I know is actually going on between them. I don't know if Cassie suspects collusion or just dislikes Randi generally, but her bluntness keeps functioning as the chapter's one honest instrument.

The "*the thought did not finish*" beat, used twice — once about what Pace's tutoring style revealed about him, once about "he is here for the math help. He is also—" — landed as the chapter's most unsettling craft choice for me. It's Vee's body/intuition outrunning her conscious narration and then getting swallowed before it can say anything dangerous. It's exactly the blindness this whole arc runs on, staged as a literal unfinished sentence rather than told to me.

Erotically this chapter stays almost entirely PG — the charge is in attention, not touch: the forearms, the cuffs rolled back, "may I call you." It works as tension-before-anything, not heat proper, and I think that's right for where the story is.

Title: "Substitution" — on the surface it's the calculus trick (turn the unknown into a known form, Euler's identity substituted for the ugly formula). But it's clearly also about *people* — Vee borrowing Randi's script for her own name ("Vivienne Thorne. V.T. Yes, like the university"), Pace substituted into her day as tutor-not-predator, the whole scene a substitution of a manufactured "chance" for the real thing. I like this title a lot — it earns its double meaning without winking at me.

I want to keep reading with real dread now that Pace has a face and a voice on the page — he's likeable, controlled, exactly as unnerving as the earlier chapters promised, and Vee has just handed him her number.
