## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction**

Everything about the first two-thirds of this — I was completely inside it. The boot fix, the sandwich, the cherry pits: Pace's attentiveness keeps arriving in exactly this register, competent and unhurried, never asking for credit. "He didn't make her small to give it to her" is such a precise, earned line about the cherry-pit moment — I felt the shame land and then get quietly defused, and it worked on me every time.

The summit landing me the hardest wasn't romantic, it was just the view — "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it... and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it." That's a genuinely good sentence and it bought real credit before the truck scene even started.

Then the truck. This is where the book finally goes all the way onto the page, and I want to be honest about both halves of my reaction. The heat is real — she calling him back, the buttons, the mother's-voice/heat braid ("the heat that knew the voice was right") — I felt that, it's not coy, it's not squeamish. But I also clocked, in real time, that his shivering — his cold, freely offered — is what stops her, not any hesitation of her own. She only pulls back to take care of *him*. That's consistent with who he's been shown to be (the shirt off his own back, standing in the rain rather than sit warm beside her uninvited), and it reads as genuine restraint, not performance — but I notice it's now twice this relationship that his self-denial is what redirects her desire rather than her own second thoughts, and I'm holding that as a texture, not a verdict.

The siren cutting her sentence off felt like the world snatching the moment rather than either of them choosing to end it — mildly frustrating in a good way, a real cliffhanger rather than a fade-to-black.

**Titles**: "Leave No Trace" — on the surface it's the cherry pits, the wax paper, the hiking ethic he teaches her without lecturing. But it recolors: nothing in this chapter *does* leave a trace on paper — no receipt of the truck scene beyond a warm flannel shirt and a memory, the cop interrupting before anything is said aloud. Feels apt and a little ominous both.

**How I feel — overall**
Swept up, genuinely — this is the most the book has let me feel the pull between Vee and Pace as mutual and alive rather than managed from outside. Pace lands as more sympathetic to me than at any point before: the boot fix, the dress compliment, the shirt off his own back, staying still and shivering rather than taking what she offered — it's a remarkably consistent portrait of a man who keeps choosing her comfort over his own, in ways that cost him something real and visible. I trust him more after this chapter than before, while still holding, quietly, the origin-deception that no scene has addressed yet. Vee is glowing to me — her wanting is unguarded and hers in a way it hasn't fully been before, and I loved watching her act rather than only receive. The siren yanking the moment away left me wanting, in the good sense — pulled forward, not frustrated at the book. No new unease was introduced this chapter; the standing questions about Randi's shaping and Cassie's look are simply quiet, not advanced, and I don't want to manufacture worry where the page gave me tenderness instead.
