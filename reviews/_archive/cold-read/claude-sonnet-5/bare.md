## Reader reaction

Short, quiet, and it lands hard precisely because nothing happens in it. Just Vee alone at night with his shirt, trying to get an answer out of cloth. I felt that scene where she puts the phone away with nothing typed — "there was no text that made it come clear" — as the most honest beat of the whole run so far, because it's her admitting the thing can't be solved by asking. That's more devastating than any fight.

The shirt sequence is the whole chapter's engine and it's beautifully cruel to her: she wears it, it works, then she realizes the warmth in it is her own body heat given back, not him at all — "It had promised him close and there was no one close." That's the loneliest sentence in the book yet. And then she takes it off and lies there cold rather than keep the illusion, which felt like the first real act of self-honesty I've seen from her — she won't let herself be soothed by a fake proxy anymore, even though the real cold is worse.

My feelings on Pace don't move — he's not on the page, he's "gone down easy as a man with nothing grinding in him," which stings by contrast and deepens the asymmetry that's been building. My feeling on Vee deepens toward tenderness and worry; this isn't a confrontation, it's the after-hours cost of one, private and unwitnessed by anyone, even Cassie asleep two feet below her.

Erotic charge: none, deliberately — the chapter uses the shirt/skin/cold almost as an inversion of eroticism, intimacy-object stripped of its charge once she sees through it.

Trust/suspicion: no new information about anyone, just Vee's interior state laid bare. I don't distrust Pace here, I ache for her.

Title "Bare" — exact and quietly devastating: bare skin, bare cold, the bare truth she finally lets herself feel instead of the borrowed warmth of his shirt. It recolors nothing, it just names precisely what happens.

I want the next chapter to bring someone to her — Cassie waking, Randi, anyone — because this loneliness feels like it's reaching a point where she needs a witness, not just another private reckoning.
