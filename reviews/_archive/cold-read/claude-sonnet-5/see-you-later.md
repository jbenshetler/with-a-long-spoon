## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurt in exactly the way I think it's supposed to. We're inside Vee now, fully, for the first time, and the whole chapter is her *not knowing* what I know — which is an unbearable position to read from. Every beat that reads to her as luck or grace ("she had been chosen, plainly, for reasons she didn't need to understand, by exactly the person she'd have chosen to be chosen by") lands on me as the machinery I watched Pace build. That gap is brutal and beautifully sustained — I kept wanting to reach into the page and grab her.

Vee herself I like even more now than on first sight. The interiority is rich and specific and sad in the right places — the mother's voice supplying "floozies" and then softening into "that's not the kind of attention a nice girl," the cardigan worn on purpose, the "she did not look at either fact straight." She's smart, self-aware in some registers and completely blind in the one that matters here. The stats-class opening — not looking at the door, the third minute of not looking at the door — is such a good, true rendering of held-breath hope that I was primed to ache for her before Randi even arrived.

Cassie is the chapter's quiet gut-punch and I did not expect that. Four words a day, declines the invitation without sulk, and then: "Goodbye, Vee." Not "see you later." The prose flags it itself — "the whole word, flat and finished, the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday" — and Vee smooths right over it, the way she smooths everything uncomfortable. I don't know what Cassie sees or suspects, but she's the one person in this chapter with clear eyes, and she's being left behind, deliberately, by the text's own structure (her exit right before the "See you later" that closes the loop with Randi). I trust Cassie completely and I'm worried for what she's watching happen to her friend.

Randi is where my feelings have moved the most since last chapter, and not for the better. Last chapter she was sympathetic even inside the engineering — her desire was real, her performance costume was self-protective. Here, watching her *execute* on Vee — the practiced warmth, the "I've decided," the hug with its "one second" duration, the "love you, girl" tossed off like nothing — she reads like a professional. I can't unsee the choreography now that I've seen the planning session. The line that got me coldest: "she'd turned all that warmth on Vee anyway," which Vee reads as generosity and I read as bait matched to the mark's specific vulnerability (status, belonging, being seen).

Erotic charge is at zero here, deliberately I think — this is a seduction chapter with no sex in it, just the seduction of *belonging*, which the book is treating as its own kind of desire and its own kind of danger. It worked on me anyway, just not sexually — the "want" Vee feels looking at Randi ("a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance") reads as romantic/erotic-adjacent even though nothing physical happens, and that ambiguity (is this friend-crush, is this queer desire, is this just hunger to be chosen) is held open by the page and I appreciate that it wasn't resolved for me.

Title: "See You Later" — I clocked it as the closing line before I got there and it lands exactly as intended, both as Randi's throwaway-that-isn't and as a possible dark joke on Vee's whole arc — a story that ends, or a beginning that only looks like a beginning. It didn't spoil anything, just primed the dread correctly.
