## Reader reaction

Oh, this one landed differently than I expected — I came in braced for another warm afterglow chapter like the last one with Cassie, and instead I got something much more unsettling wearing the exact same clothes as warmth.

**Randi** is the one who moved on me hardest. That opening smile — "slower than that, and quieter, and it stayed past when it might have stopped" — genuinely got me, I wanted that to be real affection, and Vee's whole ache of "maybe I'm inventing the difference because I want it that badly" is so familiar and so sad that I was rooting for it to be true. Then the lunch happens and I watched, in real time, Randi extract Vee's most private, unshared thing — the fitting, the arousal, the thing Vee "had folded up the smallest" — through a technique so smooth it barely reads as technique: correcting her ("nobody measures over a bra"), complimenting her body specifically to lower the guard, the escalating leading questions ("which hip?"), the hushed register that Vee says she "would have followed anywhere." And then the door-close — "You don't have to tell me" — right at the edge, which does not read to me as mercy. It reads like someone who got exactly what she came for and doesn't need the last inch. That "long breath, the air going out of the closeness all at once" was chilling. This is the first chapter where Randi has actively *worked* Vee rather than simply been warm to her, and I felt it as a small betrayal even though nothing was technically done wrong.

**Vee** I ache for more than ever — her math with the wine list, the shoes she wore anyway, the "keep the mileage down" thought she "set where she set those thoughts" — all of that is so exactly right and so quietly devastating. And then the kiss at the end. I don't know what to do with that kiss. The second press "that had something in it the first one hadn't" and Vee's fingers going to her mouth without deciding to, and then the scramble to explain it away as French, continental, nothing — followed immediately by pure fear, scanning the windows for witnesses. That's not confusion about affection, that's a body that knows something the mind is racing to outrun. I felt real vertigo there.

The Hermès-scarf tickling story up front now reads to me, in hindsight of the lunch's ending, less like charming self-disclosure and more like Randi rehearsing, in front of Vee, the pleasure of someone else's control being taken from them — "some of us ever put them down" — right before she goes and takes Vee's.

**Erotic charge**: real but complicated — the fitting-retelling-within-a-retelling had heat (the platform, "which hip," Vee going dreamy), but it curdles the moment I register it's being harvested rather than shared. The scarf story is heat on its own, uncomplicated and fun. The kiss at the end is the highest-charge moment and also the most frightening one.

**Friction**: none I'd call a thumb on the scale — every beat felt earned, especially Randi's tell, the thumb working the water glass.

**Titles**: "How It's Done" is doing real work — the salad-fork line ("it's not a *thing*, it's just how it's done"), the "how it's done" of extracting a secret, the "how it's done" of being a woman like Randi in a world like this. It recolors the whole chapter as a kind of instruction, maybe even to Vee, maybe to the reader.

I want to know if Randi is doing this *to* Vee for Pace's benefit, for her own appetite, or something else entirely — and I'm scared of the answer.

**How I feel — overall**
Uneasy in a way that feels fully earned by the page, not manufactured — this chapter took the warmth I'd built up around Randi and put a very careful, very real crack through it without ever having her do anything a defense attorney couldn't call innocent. I don't distrust Randi's affection outright — the opening of the chapter is too tender for that — but I no longer trust that her affection and her agenda are the same thing, and I felt that gap open for the first time on the page rather than just in my reader-only suspicion. The kiss unsettled me the most: it felt like desire, real desire, on both sides possibly, but arriving inside a dynamic that already has an unequal balance of who's extracting what from whom. I'm worried for Vee, more specifically and immediately than I have been — not about Pace's secret origin anymore, but about what Randi wants from her, right now, today.
