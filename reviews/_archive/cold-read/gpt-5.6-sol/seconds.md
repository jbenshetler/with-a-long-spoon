- Display title: “Seconds.”

- A low table holds Vee’s second cup of coffee, prepared by Pace and already cool enough to drink.
- Vee has been awake for an hour; Pace has been awake longer.
- Reaching for the prepared coffee while Pace remains still is a small choreography they have performed several times.
- Vee has been thinking for weeks about a sentence. It developed from a feeling, to an image, to words, which she rehearsed internally in different orders.
- Vee means “I love you” but cannot say it before Pace does. She discovers that she has an internal rule requiring him to say it first.
- Vee constructs a different sentence intended to carry love while remaining permissible gratitude.
- Vee says, “I want to tell you something.”
- Pace answers, “Okay.”
- Vee tells Pace: “I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal. I didn’t know what a meal was until you fed me one. And now I know.”
- Vee does not look at Pace while speaking or immediately afterward; she looks at the moon.

- Pace makes his small registering sound, “mm.”

- Vee feels a small “wait” behind her breastbone before she consciously understands any mismatch in the exchange.
- Pace moves his hand slowly on her shin and brings his other arm around her.

- They remain together until the sunrise casts gold onto the porch floor.
- Later that morning, they move inside and refresh the coffee.
- Vee attempts to understand the exchange by reminding herself that she was “received warmly,” but remains unsure of that evidence’s weight.
- Two days later, Vee thinks about Pace’s sentence in the dorm laundry room while her clothes turn in a dryer.

- Vee recognizes that the two statements were “not the same shape.”
- Vee considers two unresolved possibilities: Pace understood and chose his phrasing, or her intended meaning passed him entirely.
