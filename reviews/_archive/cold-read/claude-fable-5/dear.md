# Cold read — Dear

*scene: scenes/dear.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: off-six-fourteen*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me in two directions at once, and the second direction is the one that scares me. The surface is a warm, funny girls'-smoothie chapter — and I read most of it grinning, because Randi at full wattage is genuinely delightful ("I have forty minutes and I refuse to be responsible with any of them"). But I know what she is. And the chapter's whole trick is that I keep forgetting.

Then: "What is his name." *Snap, snap.* "Paul?" — and my stomach dropped. That's the tell of the book so far. Randi has been Pace's lover and co-conspirator since chapter one; she picked Vee off the quad *with* him. The name-fumble is pure theater, staged for an audience of one, and it's flawless — it manufactures the exact fiction Vee needs: that Randi barely knows this man, that the two halves of her new life are unconnected. Vee then hands over the whole date — "May I choose?", the kiss, "Because of him" — to the one person who scripted it. "You let him choose for you… And you liked it," Randi says, "holding it up to the light" — she's not gossiping, she's *debriefing*. Taking delivery. And Vee "wanted to put all of it into Randi's hands." She literally can't say it better than the trap says it.

The Cassie opening hurt in a quieter way. Cassie catches sight of Randi, "whatever had been in them was folded away," and Vee doesn't watch her go — "because Randi had her by the wrist." Then Vee excludes Cassie from the smoothie place herself ("Not her thing") and rationalizes it smooth: "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the isolation beginning, and Vee is doing it with her own hands. Every yes freely given.

The Brooke scene is the set piece and it earns itself. The room full of matched girls, the cardigan "covered her like an apology," the high chair where "she had been set up on a shelf" — feet not reaching the floor, which the book has taught me to read as Cassie's posture, now Vee's. And then "Vee's my dear friend," the cool damp hand held in plain view — and I felt it work on *me*. It's genuinely moving. Vee's read — "she knew only… that it had cost something, that Randi had spent it on her without appearing to count it" — is heartbreaking precisely because I can't tell whether it cost Randi anything at all, or whether it's the cheapest currency she owns. Brooke, though: this is the same Brooke from chapter 2, the one who may suspect Randi's secret. Her smile that "took it all in… and did not change," the unnamed tall one turning Vee over "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — that scene has a second audience, and I don't think it's over.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the deep-water simile ("the way you stay aware of deep water for a while after you are back in the boat") is a touch on the nose given the pool chapter — but it's Vee's own image, so it lands as continuity, not thumb.

Title: "Dear" before reading suggested a letter. After: it's *"dear friend,"* the two-word coronation — and also "dear" as in *costly*, which is exactly the pun the chapter runs on. Vee decides it cost Randi something. The other sense — what it will cost *Vee* — is the book. Best title yet.
