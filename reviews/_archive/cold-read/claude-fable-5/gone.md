## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

There it is. Twenty-three chapters I've been waiting for the lovers to compare notes, and the book finally lets me into the bed where the whole thing was hatched — and it's not the cold strategy session I braced for. It's worse, and better: it's tender, and everybody in it is slipping.

The chapter is short and I read it holding my breath. First the pleasure of triangulation — I was *in* the shoe store on Saturday, and now I get Randi's retell of it, and it's the same store but recolored: the bigfoot walk, the older couple, the blush. And then the line that made my stomach drop a half-step with her voice: "It was so cute. She was *so* embarrassed." Softer. "That was kind of hot." I've watched Randi extract, file, steer for twenty chapters. I have never once watched her *leak*. And Pace catches it exactly the way I did: "Not the words — the thing that came loose under them and got out a half-step ahead of her, before she folded it back up." All fall she said it the bright way — *for the threesome, for us* — "and that had not been the bright way." That's the ch.1 proposal cracking open on the page. Her forbidden want to taste a woman, which she gift-wrapped as a threesome for him, is now just want, out loud, and she couldn't stop it. The jacket said it would get "as real for the two who started the game as for the girl who never knew there was one." I believed it abstractly. This chapter made me believe it in my body.

And then the knife, quiet as everything Pace does: his lamp speech. "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp... and it never once occurs to her." He's not looking at anything; he's *seeing* it — while his actual lover is lying on his chest. And Randi goes still, "the kind that costs something to hold," and answers "a beat too long, lighter than the stillness": "Yeah. She is." That still is the ch.5 cup-hands strain and the ch.17 thumb-on-glass, but now it's in her own bed, about her own man, and there's nothing to grip. I felt genuinely sorry for her — for *Randi*, the predator — which is a strange, excellent sensation. She designed a game where both of them would fall for the mark, and it's happening on schedule, to both of them, in the same conversation.

Two more things I have to log. Pace's "Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" — low, certain, without heat. He *knows*. He read the mother off Vee's cardigan without ever being told, which is either the most tender line in the book or the most frightening, and I honestly can't split them. And: "She's not ready, though... You'll want to take your time" / "I want her to get there on her own." Even here, in the debrief, the language of consent is the language of the trap. Every yes will be freely given. That's the whole cover.

Erotic charge: real. Not the frame sex — that's warm rather than hot, deliberately post-coital — but the middle: Randi dragging her breasts down his chest, "showing him with her own body the thing she was describing," her voice dropping "half a register... low and hoarse." That's the hottest Randi has ever been, precisely because she's out of control for the first time. The closing ride is brief and lovely, "the blue on her toes still perfect" doing a lot of quiet work.

Friction: almost none. If anything, "He knew what he'd heard" through "that was hers to hold" edges toward the author telling me what I'd already caught — I'd clocked the register drop myself; the paragraph of Pace's analysis half-spends my own discovery. Small tax.

The title: "Gone." Randi says it of Vee — "so gone over you." But the chapter shows me Randi gone over Vee, and Pace gone over Vee ("he was seeing it"), and something in Randi's composure gone, and maybe the game itself gone — replaced by the real thing. One syllable, four directions. Best title in a while.

What I dread next, marked as guesses: Randi now has a jealousy she can't file. The woman who "keeps a hand on every ounce of herself" just watched her lover light up describing someone else. I think the threesome accelerates — Randi will push Vee toward the bed sooner than "she's not ready" advises, either to keep control of the triangle or to get her own mouth on what she wants before Pace's wanting outruns hers. And the credit bill is still in the mail.
