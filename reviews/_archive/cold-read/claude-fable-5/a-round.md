## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This is the chapter the whole book has been walking toward, and it's the best one yet. I want to say that plainly before I say anything careful: it worked on me. The measuring sequence — closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil — is the book's whole method miniaturized, and Vee names it herself: "she was learning the rhythm of it before she'd decided to." That sentence could be the epigraph of her entire courtship, and she says it about a tape measure.

The wet-panties thread is where I braced hardest, because that's where erotica usually goes cheap, and it didn't. It goes through her mother instead — "stripper," "floozie, girls like that," "can he tell" — and by the time she shuts her eyes on the riser I wasn't reading arousal, I was reading a woman waiting to be found out by every voice she was raised with. And then the reveal: "He hadn't looked down. The whole time she'd been dying he hadn't looked down once." That undid me. Her hand at her own collarbone, the stoop coming undone, the rising onto her toes "lifting toward something she had no name for" — that's the chapter's true climax and it's fully clothed in nothing but shame leaving a body. The peak erotic charge, for me, isn't the inner-seam measurement (though the "pre-aroused every time I wore it" joke is the funniest and most human beat the book has managed — she stumps him, and watching her *realize* she stumped him is glorious). The peak is the smile.

And here is my cold half, which never leaves the room now. "He'd thought of it hours before she came" — she reads the pre-warmed thermostat as tenderness and it opens a door in her. I know from chapter one that the house is *always* kept too warm, because Randi is regularly naked in it. The most moving inference Vee makes all afternoon is built on a fact she's misread, and the book lets her misread it without comment. Same with "who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking" — reader, he did not walk past Randi. That exclamation point hurt to read. The chapter is simultaneously the most tender thing in the book and the deepest she's been inside the lie, and both are true at full strength at once. Even his restraint — "Keep still," the line held at cost — I believe the cost is real, and I also notice the line preserves the script. The dress is for the induction; the deferral is always in service of something.

Friction: almost none. The mother's-voice beats verge on over-worked by now ("the whole furniture of shame she'd never thought to question" is one clause of explanation more than I needed), but the pin-prick and the kiss on the rib rescued the back half from any drift toward stateliness.

The title, "A Round" — a round of measurements, the tape going *around* her, her body that "came in" too round for the sport that shaped her. That last one lands hardest: this chapter is the first time the roundness that ended her swimming is received as a gift. Possibly also a round in a longer game, which I wish I could stop hearing. The book title's long spoon: he supped with tremendous discipline today. The tagline — "Every yes was freely given" — she gave a dozen yeses this afternoon, each one real, each one inside a frame she can't see.

Next: I expect Randi to resurface — she's owed a debrief and four chapters of absence — and I half-dread the dress becoming the mechanism that delivers Vee to whatever the threesome plan is. Guess from here: the induction night is the convergence point.
