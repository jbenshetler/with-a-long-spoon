## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter knowing Vee was carrying her repentance to exactly the wrong confessor, and the chapter delivered that with a gentleness that made my skin crawl in the best way. The morning sequence is lovely — the cold water "like a verdict," the apple, "the careful steps of a person carrying something she didn't want to spill," then *I'm going to fix this* putting length in her stride. I like her so much. She's funny at her own mirror ("without the sad-clown thing"), she bins the apple core "like a person raised right," and she walks into the lion's house glowing with resolve.

And then the extraction happens, and it's so warm I almost didn't clock it as one. Vee gives Randi *everything* — the dance, the blonde, the exact words of the wound, Pace's face going, the bartender's absolution. "It came out of her in pieces, the way it does when someone's letting you tell it instead of waiting for their turn" — that line is Vee's experience of being loved and my experience of watching a debrief. Randi now has the full transcript of the fight, Pace's read on it presumably to come from his side, and total control of the repair: *not today. Tomorrow night.* An instruction, not a suggestion — and delivered with a manufactured reason ("he'll smell the panic") that also happens to give Randi a full day. To do what, I don't know, and that gap is humming.

What actually moved me, though — and unsettled me most — is the console she hands Vee: "You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about... That's not the disaster, honey. That's the only thing that matters." It's *true*. It's genuinely wise. That's what makes this book hard to read straight: Randi's manipulations are made of real kindness. "And the thing Vee had been holding clenched since the dance floor let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so." *Because Randi had said so.* The narration knows exactly what it's showing me.

The erotic charge: the shoe fitting is the peak, and it works on me. The whole thing happening *under the skirt where her eyes couldn't follow* — "the cool quick hands somewhere down past the hem," the hand "up under the skirt to the outside of her thigh," the thumb "pressing once into the soft hollow behind her knee on the way down, and Vee's breath did something she hoped wasn't out loud" — that's the hottest passage in the chapter and one of the subtlest in the book, because it's dressed as girlhood dress-up and Vee has no frame for it as anything else. "Standing in her bra in front of Randi was no event; she'd done as much for Pace" — the comparison she makes so innocently is the tell she can't read. And Randi's face when she looks at Vee's bare feet — "something moved in her face, there and gone, that Vee didn't have a name for and was too happy to go looking for" — I have the toe-touch jolt from the Toenails/Sorority chapters in my pocket, so I *do* have a name for it. That's the book trusting my memory, and it paid off.

The Edna Mode laugh is a delight — the makeover energy I'd sensed finally named on the page, by Vee herself. And "Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet" made me bark out loud. Randi *says it*. Fairy-tale villain, in her own mouth, as a joke, and Vee laughs. Same move as "shoe whore" — "the worst words always did, between them" — the truth passed across the table in the register of endearment.

One small readerly stumble: Vee places Brooke as "Randi's sister from the smoothie shop." I know Brooke as the chapter president who audited Randi in Standards. Either Vee has her file wrong (plausible — she met her once, warm-and-diminishing) or I'm meant to notice Vee doesn't know who she's dealing with. Either way, "Vee had told this house nothing in her life and the house had a folder on her anyway" is quietly chilling.

The shoes themselves are practically a thesis statement: "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." That's the dance frame in leather. That's the whole book. And the title — **Broken In** — I took it first as the shoes, but the shoes are *not* broken in; they're perfect. What's being broken in is Vee. The kiss at the door seals it: "she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." That's the sentence the title is about. *With a Long Spoon* — she's supping with the devil and the spoon is getting shorter by the chapter; "every yes was freely given" — nobody forced a single thing in this room, and Randi now owns the repair, the outfit, the timing, and the mouth-kiss habit.

What I dread/want: tomorrow night at Pace's door, Vee dressed head to foot in Randi's choices — Pace's own courtship methodology, mirrored. My guess from here: the reconciliation lands hard, possibly the most intense night yet, and Vee walks into it wearing Randi without knowing whose hands dressed her. And I keep waiting for the threesome proposal to surface — this "fix it" night feels like it could be the on-ramp.
