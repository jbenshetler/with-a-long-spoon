# Cold read — Toenails

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is a small chapter and it knows it — a landing pad after "Famished" — and it worked on me almost entirely through Cassie's cereal bowl. The milk "gone the soft brown of the cereal," the open-window cold after the stuffy hall: after eighteen chapters of Pace's controlled warmth, the dorm's ordinary chill felt like surfacing. I liked being here.

Vee, first. The no-bag detail undid me a little: "To pack a bag the night before would have been to say the thing to herself, plainly, and she had not been willing to say it." That's the exact grain of this girl — the same one who couldn't name "floozie" out loud until Randi handed the shames back to her. And the aftermath is the right kind of happy: hair "its own wild country," laughing in his mirror, the grin at every red light. She's earned the smile and the book let her keep it for a whole chapter. "She had never in her life been so happy" — the line she almost said last chapter, now said flat, closing this one. It's lovely and it made my stomach drop, because the jacket tagline is sitting right there under it like a stone under water.

But the real event here is the *edit*. She retells the night to Cassie — the retell-as-scene move I've been tracking since "Rye" — and this time she ladles the middle out: "clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness." That sentence is the chapter. And the narrator won't let her off: "She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." Vee has learned to curate a debrief. Which is chilling in a way she can't see — she's now doing, in miniature and innocently, what Randi does to *her*: presenting a managed surface to a friend. The apprenticeship is working on her without a teacher in the room.

The zipper beat is the best physical writing here. She goes to undress for a shower, remembers there's nothing under the skirt — the Trade — and stops, "three feet from Cassie," because "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." Clothes as narrative control: I felt that. She zips back up and waits for Cassie to leave. That's charged — not arousing exactly, but hot in the residual way, the night "a second channel that had not shut off once."

Cassie I love more each time. "Was he worth painting your toenails?" — "In October. I have eyes." The honest sensor, exactly right, and the flicker "that crossed her face and was gone before it found a name" tells me she registers *something* even before the grin. And then the terrible dramatic irony, delivered "kindly, certainly, wrong": "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend." Wrong how? Vee hears it as wrong because — what, because "boyfriend" is too small a word? Because it's her brain that's finally *hers*? The book leaves the wrongness unglossed and I turned it over for a while. And "Don't wait up, lover" — Cassie flinging the word affectionately at Vee, unaware how loaded it is. No suspicion of Cassie; she remains the one person in this book with no angle, which is precisely why watching Vee ladle the soup in front of her stung.

No suspicion earned of anyone new here. Pace and Randi are both offstage, and their absence is the point — this is what Vee's life looks like when nobody's staging it, and even then she's performing a curated version, taught by her circumstances.

Friction: nearly none. If anything, "which was not a story but only her body naming what it wanted the moment he touched her" edges toward over-articulate for Vee's own head, but it's within the book's established register. The chapter earns its brevity.

The title: "Toenails" is perfect and sneaky. On the surface it's Cassie's line — the tell, the evidence, "I have eyes." But underneath it's the polish motif I've been tracking since Randi's blue toes in chapter one: the sanctioned rebellion, the frosted plum "her mother would have had a word for. The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." The mother's word losing its power over her toenails is the whole arc in a pedicure.

What I want next: dread and want are braided now. Randi doesn't know about the first night. Vee just proved she can lift the middle out of a story for Cassie — *can she do it to Randi?* That's the collision I'm waiting for. Guess from here: the next Randi debrief is where Vee's new editing skill either holds or is professionally dismantled, and either outcome hurts.
