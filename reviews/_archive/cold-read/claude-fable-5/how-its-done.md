# Cold read — How It's Done

*scene: scenes/how-its-done.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: turned-up*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Randi's back, and the chapter confirms every fear I've been nursing through her six-chapter absence — and complicates it in a way I didn't expect. Because the thing that got me here isn't the interrogation (I saw that coming; the retell-as-extraction is her signature move by now). It's the *tell*: "Across the table Randi was very still. She was smiling, still smiling, but something had gone tight behind it... her thumb had found the base of her water glass." That's the cup-hands from chapter 5 again, the strain leaking through the finished surface. Randi is *jealous* — or moved, or wounded — hearing the fitting told back, and Vee, God help her, reaches past the true reading for the kind one: "Maybe Randi was *moved*, a little... That had to be it." I actually winced. Vee's flaw-hunting instinct got it right the first time ("she thinks I'm awful" — wrong content, right register: something is being held back), and then she talked herself out of the alarm because she wanted the kinder thing to be true. That's the whole book in one paragraph.

The interrogation itself is masterful and horrible to watch. "Nobody measures over a bra... it's just how it's done" — the chapter title, deployed as absolution-on-tap, Randi handing Vee permission the way she always does, and the permission is a lockpick. Then the leading catechism: "Which hip?" "The left one." "All the best ones start at the left hip" — "which meant nothing at all and landed like a benediction." That line is so good it's almost cruel. Randi is walking Vee down step by step to the one thing Vee has never told anyone — and then, at the very lip of it: "You don't have to tell me." A door closing very softly. Vee's secret "risen and reaching and met with nothing."

That refusal is the chapter's strangest, most charged beat and I don't fully know how to read it. Randi extracts everything *except* the thing Vee was finally about to give. Is it mercy? Is it that she couldn't bear to hear it — that hearing Vee soaked for Pace, told in Vee's dreamy voice, was more than the tight smile could hold? Is it technique — leaving Vee unrelieved, the secret still lodged? The text gives me the tight smile and the long exhaled breath and "the hush was simply over," and I lean toward: she couldn't take it. Which means Randi is hurting, in the middle of her own game, and that's the blurb's promise coming true — "as real for the two who started the game."

The bondage story. Heat: yes, genuinely — not the man ("the man was nobody") but Vee's picture of "Randi naked and helpless and laughing... that finished surface finally cracked all the way open." Vee's arousal at *Randi* is on the page for the first time, unmistakably, and Vee has "no shelf for" it. And Randi's little sermon — "somebody takes them, and you *can't* hold them... A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right" — reads as seed-planting for wherever this is going, but it also reads as Randi telling the truth about herself, chapter 1's bench under it. I couldn't tell if the tickling story even happened. Saturday — the day of Vee's fitting. Was Randi at some frat boy's, or is the story an instrument? I flagged it and can't resolve it.

The kiss. The fire lane, "Come here," the hand at the hinge of her jaw, the second press "that had something in it the first one hadn't." And then Vee's two-step, which broke my heart in both directions: first the explanation ("*French*... that's just how women like her say goodbye" — the thermostat move again, the wrong provenance chosen because it's survivable), and then the cold scan of the dorm windows, her mother's voice without the words, counting the dark squares for witnesses. The chapter ends on shame-surveillance and it lands hard.

Money ran under the whole lunch like a bass line — reading the right side of the menu first, "more than her soup and a coffee and more... than everything she was going to allow herself to order," the debit card she will feel on Thursday, tipping well so as not to be the broke girl. Vee paying for the whole thing, including Randi's Rombauer, while Randi lets her — "did not reach for the folder again" — is quietly one of the coldest beats in the book. Randi choreographs generosity everywhere except where it costs Vee actual money.

Friction: almost none. Maybe "her hair was having a torrid affair with the wind" is one flourish too cute. And the velvet-booth setup ("You could have said anything in that booth. The room was built so that you could") tips the author's hand a half-inch — telling me the trap before springing it.

Title: "How It's Done" is Randi's absolving line verbatim, and it's perfect double duty — the etiquette of fittings, and this chapter as a demonstration of how *Randi* does it: extraction, absolution, the withheld door, the kiss. The book title's long spoon — sup with the devil — feels closer than ever; the lunch was literally a supper, and Vee brought no spoon at all.

Wants/dreads (guesses from here): the kiss goes to Pace next — will Randi retell it to him the way Vee retells to Cassie? I dread Vee having no one to tell *this* to; she can't debrief the kiss to Cassie the way she debriefed the fitting, or can she? And the withheld secret — the soaked satin — is still lodged in Vee, undelivered. It will come out somewhere, and I'm afraid of where.
