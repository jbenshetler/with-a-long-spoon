# Cold read — The Induction

*scene: scenes/the-induction.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: school-nights*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I braced for a trap and got — Vee, alone, choosing. I've been dreading this chapter for fourteen chapters, ever since the induction daydream in "Peekaboo," and I fully expected Pace in the back row, or Randi materializing at the coffee urn, the machinery converging on the room. Instead: "Pace was not there." A math conference in Berkeley, three days, "neither would move." I actually exhaled at that line, and then felt something stranger — a little bereft on her behalf, because "he had made the dress for exactly this... and he was going to miss the one wearing it was built for." The book took its engineer out of the room and let her walk unwatched by either of her handlers, and what she does with that is the whole chapter.

And what she does moved me. "She took a breath, and made the second step long." That's the sentence of the chapter. She could have minced it modest — the text explicitly gives her the safe walk, "no one would have known there was anything to know" — and she opens the slit on purpose and *stalks*. The heat "catching up to a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it" — that's been her signature all book (the mirror in ch.21, the underwear shed at the fitting), but here nobody staged it. No Pace pre-empting, no Randi kneeling at her ankle. It's the first big erotic act in this book that belongs entirely to Vee, and it lands as triumph. The murmur — "the sound a dull room makes when it wakes up" — I felt in my chest.

The charge is real and it peaks on that walk — clothed, public, sustained. Not slack anywhere; the chapter is short and every paragraph earns itself.

But my unease didn't leave, it just relocated. Two things. One: "she had stopped being able to tell the difference between what she wanted and what the dress wanted for her." The page said that out loud, and it's the tagline wearing a dress. Her freest act is performed inside a garment he built to make exactly this act inevitable — bare because *the dress required it*. Every yes freely given. Two: she sends him the photo. "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." I love that she keeps a private remainder — but she's also feeding the surveillance willingly, mailing the evidence to Berkeley. He'll open it between sessions and see his design working at distance.

Cassie remains the best supporting player in this book. "Go make your mother cry again." "I'll buy you a terrible sandwich and you can be famous at me." And she took the photo — the one clean pair of eyes is also the one who documented it, which sits nicely.

Randi's total absence from even the sentence-level of this chapter (one flashback beat, the ankle strap) is three chapters running now. That silence is starting to feel loaded. Guess from here: her return is the next move, and the photo — or the induction story retold to her — is the hinge into the threesome on-ramp.

The title: "The Induction" is the flattest, most literal title this book has used, and I think that's the point — it means the ceremony, and it means what's being inducted *in her*: the day the wanting stopped needing a handler. She got inducted into something with no scroll for it.

Friction: almost none. If I nitpick, "because she had" ("a woman who had every part of it handled, because she had") is a shade self-congratulatory — but it's her voice, and she's earned an afternoon of it.
