## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The professor finally reached through the fourth wall. Thirty-nine chapters of thesis-lectures Vee half-transcribes and dismisses — latent variables, Simpson's paradox, the outlier that "comes in through the part that carries forward" — and now the class stops being a Greek chorus and becomes an *instrument*, and the first thing it does is point at Randi. My whole body went cold at "The tag on this one read MIRH." I knew before Vee did — the carry-forward gave it to me: Randi's money, the unlooked-at price tags, the Mercedes, "my dad thinks he was funny." Miranda Holdings, LLC. The name she was given and "filed down to something a person could stand to answer to" — sitting over the worst point in the county.

And Randi's stillness is the best thing on the page: "Not a motion — the withdrawal of motion." I have watched this woman be flawlessly composed for an entire book. This is the second crack after "It doesn't," and it's bigger. "Cheating bastards" — the text tells me exactly what I felt: "they weren't what you said about strangers, that they'd come up out of somewhere with a floor under it." Randi is not shocked by the fraud. She *knows* it. She's been living on it. The mimosas, the boutique, the never-glancing-at-numbers — the whole extraction apparatus Vee has been swept up into is bankrolled by pandemic loans that were supposed to save jobs like Cassie's dad's, and Cassie's dad got laid off. Cassie, sitting three feet away, unknowingly indicting the family funding her roommate's new life. That's brutal and the book didn't underline it once.

And then the thing that actually wrecked me: Vee's response. She sees it, she *reads Randi correctly* — she catches the wrong register of "cheating bastards" — and then she chooses tenderness over truth. She slides the laptop over, hands Randi busywork, "didn't say the name, and didn't tell Randi, then or after, what she'd seen." She constructs, on her own, the most generous possible story — "a rotten thing by a man she'd had the luck to be the daughter of — no hand in it, none of it earned" — a story the page never confirms. That's Vee's whole tragedy in one paragraph. Her kindness manufactures Randi's cover. And Randi "had no idea she'd been looked at" — for once in this novel, *Vee* holds the secret knowledge and Randi is the one being watched without knowing. The tables tipped for one afternoon and Randi doesn't even know it happened.

Suspicion, earned by the text: I do not fully buy Vee's exculpating story, and I don't think I'm supposed to. Whose holdings? "Her father had taken the loans... kept people on with them; that was the whole point" — Randi said that *before* the data, with certainty, and the data said otherwise. Was she lying, or lied to? My guess from here: lied to, and the wound is real — but Vee's version ("no hand in it") is her own invention, and I've watched Vee invent generous versions of people all book.

Cassie was perfect. "Didn't carry things into rooms she meant to walk out of." "My dad got laid off in 2020" — flat, "no handle left out." She's the only person in this book who argues with data and she just won, and doesn't know what she hit.

Erotic charge: zero, and correctly zero. Friction: essentially none — maybe the closing image ("easy to see if you knew to look for it, invisible if you didn't") leans a half-inch toward telling me the moral, but it earned it.

The title: "The Outlier" pays off the earlier lecture directly — and the professor's line "you could set those aside if you could account for them" is exactly what Vee does to Randi's point: accounts for it kindly and sets it aside. The exception proving the rule. And "never all the way gone" — that point is on Cassie's laptop, in the write-up, in a graded project. It carries forward. Book title: "sup with the devil" now has a bank account attached.

Want next: Thanksgiving looms. I dread the write-up — Cassie will cite that point. Guess from here: this knowledge Vee is sitting on will matter at the reveal; she now protects Randi the way Randi has been managing her.
