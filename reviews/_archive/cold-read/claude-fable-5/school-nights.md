## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

After the top drawer, this little vignette is exactly what I needed — the story stepping back to the dorm, the cold two-inch window, and Cassie, who I've been quietly worried about being sidelined for ten chapters. And she isn't sidelined here; she's doing the one thing nobody else in this book does, which is see Vee without wanting anything from her. "You're doing the streetlight thing again... You're a public utility" made me laugh out loud, and it's also — after the lamp speech — a shiver. Pace called Vee the lamp: "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter." Now Cassie, who's never heard that speech, independently names her a streetlight. Two people looking at the same lit girl. Cassie's version is teasing and free; Pace's version was possessive inventory. The rhyme did more to me than the chapter's whole plot.

The chapter's plot, such as it is, is an audit. "You don't come home school nights" — not a question — and Vee going "back over the week she hadn't counted" and finding "her own bed had sat empty more nights than it hadn't." That's the trap's arithmetic done aloud for the first time by someone outside it. And Cassie's little "With not enough on," said *pleasant* — she's noticing, filing, not judging. I trust Cassie completely. She is the only character in this book with no angle, and the text keeps earning it: "She let the maybe stand."

The big beat: "Do you love him," flat, and Vee — who told a sleeping man "Peter" one chapter ago — says "Maybe. I think so." Out loud, awake, to a witness. That's movement. It's smaller than what she knows privately, and that gap ached: she can whisper it to an unconscious man and only half-say it to her best honest friend. Also her verdict on all prior men: "fine." Pace wasn't fine. The word she reaches for after fine and doesn't have — that reaching, hands up "at the size of it," is lovely and a little frightening, because I know what fills that silence: engineered plenty. The soup burn is a perfect miniature — his tenderness genuinely tender AND another instance of him getting there before she does ("before she'd even found the cold herself"). He pre-empts even her own sensations now.

Heat: essentially none, a warm afterglow only, and that's right. Friction: none — it's short, it knows its length. The title, "School Nights," is the audit itself: the nights she doesn't come home, the ordinary-life ballast ("Some of us have class") against the house that's warm as a held breath. That phrase — "warm as a held breath" — recolors the too-warm house motif again: held breath means something waiting to exhale.

Guess from here: Cassie has now clocked it. When the bench or the game surfaces, Cassie is the person Vee will crawl back to through that cracked window's cold. I want the November induction next; I dread Randi's return.
