# Cold read — Bare

*scene: scenes/bare.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: under-the-rug*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one hurt in a quiet, precise way. Three pages, one girl, one shirt, and it lands harder than the fight in "Under the Rug" because it's the fight metabolized — no Pace to argue with, just the residue of him in a piece of cloth.

The phone beat is the best writing in the chapter for me. "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them" — that's exactly the 2 a.m. logic of it, thumb hovering, and the honesty that every draftable text "would be answered small and warm, and would leave her exactly here." *Small and warm* is devastating because it's accurate to Pace: he never fails her in small warm ways. That's the whole problem.

And then the turn I didn't see coming even though the book has been building it for fifteen chapters: the scent-ritual — the thing she *designed*, her own invention, the one piece of intimacy that was hers — curdles on the page. "The heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." I actually felt cold reading that. The flannel has been the book's warmest object since the rain in "Leave No Trace," and this chapter takes it apart in two sentences without raising its voice. "She had everything he did and not the one thing he wouldn't say" — that's the crackers/meal argument, the no-note, the whole acts-not-words ledger, cashed out alone in a cold bunk.

The ending is the chapter: she takes it off and the cold is *worse* "and she did not put it back on." Choosing the honest cold over the borrowed warmth — and the shirt left "down in a heap where a dropped thing lands," not folded, not on the pillow. That un-folding is a small violence against the totem and I believed every bit of it. Also quietly brutal: Pace "gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." Whether that's true or just what 3 a.m. tells her, it stings — and after his broken control in the last chapter, I'm not sure she's right, which makes it sadder.

Heat: none, and rightly — this is the first chapter in a long while where her nakedness is entirely un-erotic. Bare skin as exposure, not invitation.

Friction: almost none. If anything, "It had promised him close and there was no one close" edges toward stating the theme, but the chapter has earned its one abstract line.

**The titles.** "Bare" is doing triple duty — bare skin in the cold, the shirt worn bare against her ("the way she wore it when no one could see"), and the bared truth that the ritual was self-heating. It didn't spoil anything; it recolored on the last page. The book title looms differently now: Vee is supping with someone she doesn't know is the devil, and she doesn't even have a spoon — she has a shirt, and she just dropped it.

**What I want next:** I dread that this is exactly the opening Randi returns into. A girl who just discovered the warmth was her own is maximally extractable by someone who *listens*. Guess from here: Randi comes back within a chapter or two and Vee hands her the pile.
