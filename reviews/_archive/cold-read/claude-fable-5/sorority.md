## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter got me twice — once with laughter, once with a cold drop in the stomach, and the trick is that both happened in shoe stores.

The discount-store sequence is the warmest, funniest thing in the book so far. The yeti shoe, the rhinestone platforms — "Do these go with burgundy?" — the flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal," Randi "folded over the bench, the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give." I laughed with them. And that's exactly what disarmed me, because I came into this chapter *braced*. My whole carry-forward said: shoe date = the net's last stitch. And then the chapter made me have such a good time that I forgot to watch Randi's hands. Which is what Vee does. The book made me be Vee for twenty minutes.

Then the boutique. The moment Vee turns over the plain black heel and "the floor did a quiet thing under her" — that's my dread arriving on schedule. And here's what I have to be precise about: **Randi does not pay.** I predicted she would; the actual move is worse. She kneels — kneels, in her beautiful clothes, "I've got her" — she handles Vee's foot, thumbs the plum polish once ("Mm." That's all — and she *decoded that polish out loud a chapter ago*), she supplies the argument ("A dress a man made *to* you doesn't want to go with everything") — and then she steps aside and lets Vee put it on the credit card with "nothing under it." Every yes freely given. Vee even narrates her own capture: "The shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed." That sentence made me actually wince. The mother's floozy-ledger has been replaced with a new ledger where debt is *virtue* — you owe it to the beautiful thing.

The erotic charge is one moment and it lands hard: the thumb across the toenail, "she felt it go all the way up the length of her... her hand shot out and found Randi's shoulder." Vee nearly came off the bench from a thumb on her toe, in public, from her *girlfriend*, and Randi "looked up at her and smiled and said nothing at all." Randi clocked it. Filed it. Said nothing. That silence is the most predatory beat in the chapter and it's also — this is the trouble — genuinely hot.

And "lover." Randi hands Vee the word — "A dress your *lover* made you?" — and Pace "rearranged in her own chest into something adult and lit." Randi is naming Vee's own relationship for her. With her own lover. The nerve of it.

Two grace notes I don't want to lose: the near-slip is right there — Randi calls the fabric "Charmeuse... not to Vee; to herself" and reads the bias cut with her fingers like someone who *knows this dress*. I flagged in my open questions how close she'd come to betraying knowledge; that's how close. And Cassie's side of the room, "made tight and square... nobody home" — the honest sensor conveniently absent for the extraction.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the same-size-feet reveal ("it's practically a marriage") pressed the merging-of-the-two-women button a touch hard — but Randi *would* say that, so I mostly buy it.

The title "Sorority" — no chapter set foot in the sorority house. So it means the two of them: sisterhood, sharing shoes, "same feet, baby, same size, same everything." Which is either the sweetest thing in the book or the most acidic, since Randi's actual sisterhood-with-Vee is a script she co-wrote. It also whispers Standards — Randi enforcing what's worn. *With a Long Spoon*: Vee just went into unsecured debt to sup at this table. The tagline was literally enacted at the register — nobody made her; she put back the debit card herself.

Ending on "she had never once in her life had a friend like this" — true, and the truest possible indictment. What I dread next: the credit card sitting there next month "with nothing behind it but next month." *Guess from here:* the induction is the convergence — the room turns, and Pace and/or Randi are in it; and the debt becomes the lever that makes some future "gift" impossible to refuse.
