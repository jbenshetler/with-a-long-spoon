## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

The chapter title got me before the chapter did. "Two Towels" — and then there it is, one clause, tucked in the middle of the tour like nothing: "a bathroom, plain, clean, two towels on the rod." Vee walks right past it. I couldn't. Why does a man who lives alone at the end of a gravel road, a man with exactly one right pair of shoes for each thing he does — *one right thing for each thing* — keep two towels? Because someone else showers there. Randi. The book taught me his economy of objects in the same chapter it planted the extra towel, and Vee even articulates the rule herself ("she could have read half of him off that closet floor without a word") and then doesn't apply it in the bathroom. That's the most quietly cruel thing this book has done to me, and it did it in eleven words.

And it wasn't alone. The too-warm house — I've known since chapter one that he keeps it that way, from Randi's chapters — and Vee "almost said something, *did you leave the heat on,*... and didn't. She filed it under the small mysteries of him." Reader, it is not a mystery to me. And the den furniture: "a long low table" in pale white oak, joints cut to lock. She crouched and put her fingers on the joint. I actually flinched. Chapter one told me the bench disguises as a low table. Is that it, moved out of the bedroom for company? Or is the bedroom — the one closed door in the house, the door he named flat as the kitchen and did not open — still where it lives? Either way, her fingers were on his joinery and I know what his joinery is for and she doesn't, and that dread hum ran under the whole tour.

Now the other half, because this chapter is genuinely two things at once: it's the most romantic chapter in the book and I felt it even while braced. The patent monologue — "somebody sick, somebody scared, a kid who can't lie still... they're out of the tube in a fraction of it" — and "he lit up like a six-year-old who'd just found out everything there was to know about dinosaurs." Vee "didn't know what to do with her whole chest," and honestly, same. The error-correcting-codes bit ("Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it") is the best flirting-by-teaching scene yet. And the dress. He didn't buy the dress; he bought *silk* and will build it on her body, and he didn't match the magazine — "He'd matched *her.*" That turn from her small disappointment to recognition is beautiful. And then the shirt reveal — "You made the shirt" — retroactively recharging a detail I'd half-forgotten (the no-tag shirt she'd checked). The book keeps paying off its own litter, which is why I now trust every stray object.

But I can't stop hearing the machinery under the tenderness. "He couldn't make it without seeing her. That was the only thing standing between her and it — her own willingness to be looked at." That's the tagline in miniature: measurement "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light" is the ask, the dress is the open door, and her "Yes — of course, yes... no hedge in it" is another freely-given yes. She wore the satin out there on purpose, leaned at the bedroom door, and he *didn't open it* — he stopped the kiss too, "the stopping was his," and she registered that no boy had ever been the one to stop. He keeps making refusal do seduction's work. It works on her; it half works on me; and it terrifies me because I know he practices.

The "parents" flinch — "a small closing, there and gone, a door easing shut two rooms away" — is the first crack of backstory I've been given on him, and I want it.

Erotic charge: the kiss at the door is real heat, and the measuring proposition is charged, but the chapter deliberately banks the fire — bedroom door shut, want "lit in her with nowhere to put it." Simmering by design. No friction to speak of; the tour never dragged because every room was characterization. Maybe the Sanskrit quip was a touch cute.

Guess from here: the measuring scene is next, in that glass porch light, and it will be the first time he sees her body — possibly the first bed, possibly deliberately *not*, because he defers everything. And I now half expect Vee to be in that house someday when the second towel's owner is explained.
