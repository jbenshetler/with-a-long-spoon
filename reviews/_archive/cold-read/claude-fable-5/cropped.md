## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one is quiet and I liked it a lot — a decompression vignette after the Thanksgiving split, and the title did half the work before I even started. "Cropped." Of course she cropped it. The induction photo goes home to Ohio "stops at the waist" — the leg, the slit, the whole engineered blaze of it cut off for her mother's purse. And the chapter's real ache is that the crop keeps going past the photo: she crops Pace for her father ("I told Dad the parts that fit"), crops the sewing, crops the cooking and the house, crops her own wanting. The line that got me physically was "Honestly, my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's a real, class-true observation — "weird is worse than wrong" is the sharpest sociology this book has done in a while, and it lands because I've met that father (jet engines, burned rolls on purpose) just enough to believe it.

The recolor is what stings: two chapters ago, cropped-vs-uncropped was her private erotic economy — cropped for the world, uncropped for him, "I gave him ALL of it." Now I see the cost of living cropped everywhere else. Her family gets the waist-up girl. Cassie gets *almost* everything — and then hits the wall: "So what is he… Not to your dad. To you." And Vee can't answer. "Both. Probably both." That refusal is the chapter's true ending and it made me sad in a good way, because the thing she can't say cleanly isn't shame — she says so, "I'm not ashamed of it" — it's that no available frame fits what Pace actually is. And I, the reader, know a worse reason the story doesn't stand up cleanly in the telling: it was authored. She senses the shape is strange without knowing why it's strange. That's the dread hum under a completely gentle dinner scene.

Cassie continues to be the best-deployed minor character in the book — "Cassie didn't laugh, which was the right call," and "she had a gift for knowing when a door was closed." And Vee's final beat, grateful "and also, a little, not," is exactly right: part of her wanted to be pushed.

No suspicion earned of anyone new; no erotic charge to speak of — the dress-off-me line flickers, that's all. Frictionless chapter; short, earns its length exactly. Book title / tagline sit quiet here, though "I told them he was my boyfriend. I think I put a question mark on it" is another freely-given-yes she can't quite name.

Want next: Randi's return, the Anthro class, and whether that unanswerable "what is he to you" gets forced into the open.
