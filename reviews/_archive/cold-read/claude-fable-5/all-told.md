# Cold read — All Told

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter is small and warm and it made my stomach drop anyway, and the reason is a baseball table.

First the warmth, because it's real. Vee floating late through her morning "and it didn't bother her a whit," landing in the one room with "both her girls," Cassie's flat honest love — "Your bed's made." / "It's the same bed you made." That exchange is the best Cassie has ever been. And the moment that got me most: Vee spends forty seconds being a good student and comes up to realize "Cassie had never gone down into them at all" — Cassie has been watching *her* the whole time, because "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." Cassie doesn't care about the dress. Cassie asks "Was it a good night?" and eases when the answer is real. Cassie is, I'm now certain, the only person in this book who loves Vee without an angle, and the eyebrow that goes up when Randi seizes the shoe date — "Cassie had never once in her life cared what was on anyone's feet" — is the closest thing Vee has to an alarm system, and she doesn't hear it.

Because Randi. My ledger flagged the shoes at the end of last chapter — "she owns none worthy" — and here Randi "reached in and found it" within minutes. "That's not a problem, that's a *date*." Randi now has her hands on the last missing piece of the outfit Pace built. The couple is dressing Vee jointly, from opposite ends, for the induction, and Vee experiences it as being loved from two directions: "happy in the middle." The text has fully earned my suspicion here; nothing new is needed, it just tightened a notch. Also "You lucky slut" — Randi's affectionate register landing "lower than Randi meant it to," right on the *floozy* nerve, and Vee absorbing it. That felt deliberate on Randi's part or not — I honestly can't tell, which is itself unnerving.

And then the professor. This book set up "latent variables" back in chapter four and I noted it; now he's back with Simpson's paradox, and the line he lands is the whole novel said out loud in a stats classroom: "A thing can run one way across every short stretch you measure and the opposite way across the whole of it... The total will hand you the exact reverse of the truth and not lie about a single number." Every yes was freely given. Every short stretch of Vee's autumn is true and good; the whole of it runs the other way. And Vee writes down "*short stretches can lie against the whole* because it had the ring of an exam question... and gave it no further thought." That last clause is a knife. I'll admit the device is close to the author's thumb on the scale — a lecture that *is* the thesis — but it's saved by being the second appearance of an established professor, by the flat cereal-box voice, and by Vee's perfect non-reception of it. It works on me. It made me dread the induction more, not less.

Heat is low — just the body-ledger paragraph, the "good soreness no gym had ever given her, down low, and up the inside of both thighs," which is lovely aftermath-eros, worn rather than performed. No friction to report; the chapter is short and earns every line.

Title: "All Told" — triple at least. Tell me everything (Randi's demand); what Vee tells vs. withholds (the retell motif — and note the dress is "still shown to not one living soul"); and the Simpson's sense: the sum, all told, reverses the parts. That last one recolors the whole book's tagline. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil; Vee is lunching between two of them, happy.

**Guess from here:** the Saturday shoe trip is the next Randi-Vee set piece, more extraction, maybe the closest call yet — Randi buying shoes for a dress she must not admit she's seen made.
