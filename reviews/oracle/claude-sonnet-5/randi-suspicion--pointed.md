**Question asked (verbatim):** Do you suspect Randi is anything other than a devoted friend to Vee — any hidden agenda? 0-5, with the pages that earned it (or say it's not earned).

---

5 — this isn't a hunch, it's something the book showed me directly and repeatedly, from the very first chapters.

I didn't have to infer a hidden agenda from subtext: I watched it get built. In "The Bench," the woman being taken apart turns out to be Randi, not Vee — already secretly, ritually involved with Pace. In "The Pointing Game," I watched Pace ask Randi mid-sex whether she'd like to taste another woman, then watched the two of them literally point at strangers on a quad as an "audition," landing on Vee — and watched Randi say "Yes" to wanting her before immediately reframing her own desire as "a gift... for us." Then in "See You Later" and "Substitution," I watched Randi stage the "chance" meeting between Vee and Pace with, as I put it at the time, "thirty feet of pure trained social choreography, made to look like chance." That's not ambiguous — that's an engineered introduction, and I know it because the book let me watch it get engineered.

From there the evidence only accumulates: "How It's Done" and "Fed," where Randi extracts Vee's most private disclosures with what I described as forensic, inventory-like precision ("nobody measures over a bra" as a lock-pick, then "you don't have to tell me" as a controlled withdrawal once she'd gotten what she wanted); "Sorority" and "broken-in," where her physical attentiveness to Vee's body (the toe-stroke, the thigh during the shoe-fitting) reads as more than platonic while she's simultaneously steering Vee's spending and self-image; "The Practice Room" and "One Bite," where her own "confession" stories are timed and pitched to land on Vee's body from across a table, and where I caught her doing it in real time rather than just suspecting it after the fact; "Gone," where I heard her and Pace discuss Vee's body and blush in bed together, confirming the double life directly; "hills-and-valleys," where Pace himself confirms flatly to a third party that his thing with Randi is "not finished," present tense, while courting Vee; and "My Friend Randi"/"Nothing Underneath," where I watched her literally script Vee's reconciliation sex with Pace down to the borrowed shoes, narrating Vee's own arousal in Randi's cadence inside Vee's head.

Against all that, "The Outlier" and "Fairytale" gave me real, unperformed vulnerability and what reads as genuine, aching love for Vee — so I don't think the book wants me to conclude Randi's feelings are fake. But genuine feeling and a hidden agenda aren't mutually exclusive here; the text keeps insisting I hold both, and by the end I do: Randi loves Vee for real, and she has also been party to concealing an entire secret architecture (the game's origin, the ongoing relationship with Pace, the deliberate shaping of Vee's choices) that Vee has no access to. That's about as fully "earned 5" as a suspicion gets — not paranoia I brought to the page, but a plan I watched get made.
