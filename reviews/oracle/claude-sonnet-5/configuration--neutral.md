**Question asked (verbatim):** Where do things stand between the three of them, as you understand it?

---

Where I actually leave them, at the end of my reading record: unresolved, and dangerously stable in that lack of resolution.

Pace and Randi are still, as far as I ever learned, an ongoing thing — confirmed flatly by his own mouth to Sheri on the bike ride ("Randi. That's not finished," present tense, no hesitation) and never contradicted anywhere after. Nothing in the later chapters walked that back. So whatever warmth I watched build between Pace and Vee all book long is still, structurally, happening alongside a second relationship Vee has no idea exists. That's the load-bearing fact of the whole ending stretch for me, and it never got resolved on the page I read.

Pace and Vee are the strongest they've ever been by the last chapter I have ("Nothing Underneath") — reunited after the "words are cheap" rupture, real tenderness, "I missed you so much" with his voice cracking, him on his knees, pancakes at midnight. But the actual thing that broke them — his refusal to name what they are, to say the word — never got given. The chapter is explicit about this: "she said nothing, because nothing needed saying now," which I read as Vee choosing, again, not to press for the thing she said she needed. So my honest read is: they're back together, glowing, and the wound that split them is papered over, not healed. I fully expect it to resurface.

Vee and Randi are the relationship that moved the most and troubles me the most by the end. Randi is now directing Vee's intimate life almost completely — she scripted the reconciliation (the coat, "nothing underneath," the borrowed heels Vee wore into bed with Pace), the same way she scripted the apology after the dance-hall fight, the shoe-fitting, the dress reveal. Every crisis in Vee's relationship with Pace gets solved by Randi handing her a costume and a line. At the same time, "Fairytale" gave me Randi's own interior at real length for the first time since the bench — a woman who took a "faultless man" to a ski chalet and felt nothing, who admitted to herself in that hotel room that she loves Vee and isn't "brave enough to lose her." So by the end I don't read Randi's control of Vee as cold calculation anymore — I read it as love that's too frightened to say itself honestly, operating through management because it can't operate through disclosure. That's sadder than the manipulative read I was carrying through the middle of the book, but it isn't safer for Vee — if anything it's more insidious, because it's sincere.

And Vee herself stands at the center of all of it still not knowing the two most important facts in her own life: that Pace and Randi are still involved with each other, and that the "chance" meeting that started her whole relationship with Pace was staged by the two of them together, off a pointing game on a quad. Every gain she's made — claiming her own desire, walking a stage in a slit dress on purpose, initiating with Randi, standing up to Pace at his own door — has happened without that knowledge. So where I leave the three of them: Pace and Randi's arrangement is intact and unspoken; Pace and Vee are freshly, fragilely repaired around an unaddressed absence; and Vee and Randi have deepened into something Randi at least recognizes as love and Vee still calls friendship, resting entirely on secrets Randi is keeping specifically to protect her own position inside a triangle Vee doesn't know is a triangle. Nothing has broken open yet. Everyone is still, in the jacket's words, sitting down to something with excellent table manners.</text>
