# Oracle (grounded) — professor · neutral

**Question asked (verbatim):** Was there anything that read as a running joke or a wink — aimed a little over the characters' heads, at you? Anything you caught yourself smiling at on a second or third appearance?

---

Yes — a few things, and I want to be precise about which felt like a wink at *me* specifically versus just good writing that moved me.

The clearest one is the classroom-lecture device. It first showed up as Simpson's paradox in "All Told" ("short stretches can lie against the whole"), sitting right in the middle of a chapter about who's really paying attention to whom — and I flagged it at the time as maybe a wink, holding it loosely. Then it came back as "outliers... never all the way gone" a few chapters later, and by the time "The Outlier" turned that exact classroom throwaway into load-bearing plot (Randi's father's data point, "invisible if you didn't know to look for it"), I felt the smile land for real — the book had been quietly training me on its own vocabulary for chapters before cashing it in. That's the one I'd point to as a genuine over-the-characters'-heads joke: the professor drones on about stats concepts that are secretly chapter titles-in-waiting, and nobody in the room notices but me.

Second: Cassie's recurring dry tags for Vee — "the streetlight thing," "you're a public utility," "Was he worth painting your toenails?" — these are jokes Cassie makes *inside* the story, but I caught myself grinning at them on repeat the way you do with a friend's running bit, especially because they always land as the one unperformed voice in a book otherwise full of people managing how they're seen.

Third, more structural: the titles themselves became a kind of private game between me and the book — "A Recognized Method," "Under the Rug," "Believe Me," "The Usual" — each one sounding like it might be about the engineered "plan"/management language the book had trained me to be suspicious of, and then turning out to be something warmer or sadder instead (or, in "The Usual," turning that suspicion back onto Pace's emotional evasion rather than the game). By the second half of the book I was actively smiling in anticipation whenever a new title arrived, waiting to see which way it would flip.

I wouldn't call any of these jokes exactly — nothing broke tone or winked broadly — but the lecture-hall device in particular felt like the author trusting me to notice a pattern before the plot needed me to, and rewarding that noticing later. That's the one I remember smiling at on a third appearance, specifically.</text>
