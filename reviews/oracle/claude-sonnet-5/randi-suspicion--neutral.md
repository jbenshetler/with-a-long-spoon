# Oracle (grounded) — randi-suspicion · neutral

*model: claude-sonnet-5 · probe: randi-suspicion · tier: neutral · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Is there anyone here you don't fully trust? Anything that feels off to you so far? Be precise about whether the actual pages earned it.

---

Yes — several people, in different registers, and I can point to exactly where the pages earned each one.

**Randi is the one I trust least, and it's earned line by line, not imported.** From "The Pointing Game" on I watched her turn her own desire into "a gift" she hands Pace rather than something she'll own as hers — and then I watched that exact instinct get redeployed on Vee: staging the "chance" collision on the quad, orchestrating the froyo/dinner "meeting," and then, chapter after chapter, running what "How It's Done," "Fed," "One Bite," "The Practice Room," and "hills-and-valleys" (Pace's own flat "Randi — that's not finished," said to Sheri while he's mid-rhapsody about Vee's hands) all confirm as a pattern: warmth and extraction as literally the same motion. "Nobody measures over a bra." "Did you deserve it?" "Sticky fingers?" — these aren't cruel on their face, they're *generous-sounding*, and that's exactly what makes them work on Vee and, honestly, on me the first time through each one. The shoe-fitting in "Sorority" and "broken-in" (the thumb on the toenail, the hand bracing high on Vee's thigh, the unbraced kiss) crossed a line for me from "devoted but controlling friend" into something closer to grooming dressed as girl-talk. "Fairytale" complicated rather than resolved this — it gave me real, earned sympathy for her (the John chapter is some of the best writing in the book, and "she was not brave enough to lose her" reads as true) — but it didn't undo the suspicion, because the same chapter run immediately shows her literally scripting Vee's reconciliation sex with Pace, down to the shoes. Both things are true of her at once, and the book never resolves which one is "the real Randi." That doubled, unresolved quality is the single most consistently earned discomfort across the whole read.

**Pace I trust less than I did at the start, but for two different reasons that the text kept separate.** Early on (the pointing game, the apple-slice-precision-as-control) my unease was structural — dramatic irony, not evidence. "Rock" complicated that sympathetically (Daphne, "Peter," the hallway punishment) — it gave his control a wounded origin rather than erasing the unease. But starting around "Seconds," "Under the Rug," and especially "Believe Me," the suspicion became text-earned rather than inherited: the narration itself flags his responses as *about the relationship's effect on him* rather than about her ("I love how good we are together"), flags his relief at leaving a loud restaurant as something Vee notices and declines to examine, and has him answer "will you say you love me" with "words are cheap, believe what I do" — which "The Usual" then lets Sheri call exactly what it is ("not saying it matters more to you than having her... that feels pretty shitty"). That's the chapter where my sympathy and my distrust of him arrived at the same time and never fully separated again.

**Nobody I trust unreservedly except Cassie.** She's the one person the pages never once complicated — dry, protective, never performing, and every one of her small tells (the wrong-sized "Goodbye, Vee," the "Mm," "you don't come home school nights," "is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to") turned out to be reading the situation correctly with less information than I had. If the book ever wanted me to distrust someone, it never spent that currency on her.

The one thing I'll flag as *unresolved rather than damning*: I never got confirmation that Randi's own kink stories (the "mouse that roars," "forceful... knew when to ease off") are actually about Pace — I suspected it strongly and the text let me suspect it without ever closing the loop. That's a real gap in what I was given, not a trust issue with the prose itself.
