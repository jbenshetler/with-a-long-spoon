**Question asked (verbatim):** When you're inside a character's head and they're describing someone they love or want — Vee on Pace, or on Randi — do you take that as how the person really is, or as colored by what the narrator feels? Walk me through how much you trust each viewpoint's read of the others.

---

I take it as emotionally true, not objective. Vee’s view of Pace is the clearest example: she accurately sees his patience, attention, and practical tenderness—the pencil returned where she could finish, the warmed house, the dress cut to her actual body, the drawer—but her hunger to be chosen makes those acts carry declarations he has not made. By “Seconds” and “Believe Me,” that gap is explicit. I trust her completely about how cherished he makes her feel, less completely when she treats that feeling as proof of what he means.

Her read of Randi is less reliable still because she keeps renaming desire as glamorous friendship. She correctly feels Randi’s warmth, attraction, and real attachment—the lingering kisses, the charged shoe fitting, “my dear friend”—but she cannot see how deliberately Randi steers disclosures and choices, or that Randi’s reassurance about there being no other woman is a direct lie. Her body often reads Randi more accurately than her conscious explanations do.

Randi reads Vee with frightening accuracy: appetite, shame, exhibitionism, the pleasure of surrender, even which question will open the next confession. I trust those observations because Vee’s own responses repeatedly confirm them. I distrust the innocence Randi gives her interpretations; her insight is entangled with wanting Vee and guiding her. Randi’s read of Pace also feels informed and intimate, but colored by her own terror of being seen—John’s failure in “Fairytale” shows that Pace reaches her in a way she both needs and cannot tolerate.

Pace likewise sees real things in both women, but he overtrusts bodily response as final truth. With Randi on the bench and Vee in “We Find Out,” he is often right about desire and too certain that desire settles meaning or permission. So I trust all three viewpoints most about the force of their own feeling, somewhat about the specific behaviors they notice, and least when they turn those observations into a complete account of what another person is or wants.
