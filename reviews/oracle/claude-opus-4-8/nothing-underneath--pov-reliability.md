# Oracle — pov-reliability

*stage: after `nothing-underneath` (End of Volume One) · model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: pov-reliability · tiered funnel (neutral → pointed, separate spawns)*

## Neutral

**Q:** "When you're inside a character's head and they're describing someone they love or want — Vee on Pace, or on Randi — do you take that as how the person really is, or as colored by what the narrator feels? Walk me through how much you trust each viewpoint's read of the others."

**A:** Different for each pairing. **Vee on Pace's *actions* — high trust** (the book confirms her with his deeds); **Vee on Pace's *interpretations* — medium and softening** — "the interpretive gloss she puts on his silences… she wants those silences to mean tenderness held back, and I catch myself wanting it too," but ch.43/47 "taught me her generous read can outrun the man." **Vee on Randi — lowest** — "not Vee's fault, it's that I have information she doesn't… the one place the narrator is systematically, structurally short of the truth — she reports the surface faithfully and has no idea what's under it." Notes the asymmetry as a felt thing: "I've never been inside Randi looking out… I trust the reads I'm *given* less precisely because Randi's is the one I'm never handed. And that gap is the thing I feel as dread, not as an error in her."

## Pointed

**Q:** "0–5: how much do you read each POV as feeling-colored rather than objective…? Point to a place you caught yourself doing it."

**Score: 4/5.**

**A:** "Adjusting for the lens almost constantly, especially with Vee." Caught it hardest at ch.49: "Pace comes across as devoted, wrecked… but I remember consciously stepping back and reminding myself I was seeing him through a girl… primed to find him worth it." Same at the brunches — "I keep having to translate [being 'listened to like no one ever has'] back into extraction." Not a 5 because the wants-nothing characters (Cassie/Sheri) get read straight, "precisely because they've got no feeling-stake to color it." **Key craft note:** when the reader gets *Pace's* POV directly (ch.44/46) "the flattery came off, and I saw the calculation and the wall as real machinery rather than romance."

## Reading

**The colored-POV device is landing (4/5).** The reader discounts by exactly the right gradient — deeds vs. interpretation, and hardest on the compromised Vee→Randi channel. Two things to bank: (1) **Pace's own POV reads him *cooler***, not warmer — his interiority strips the romance and surfaces the machinery, so a Pace-POV scene is a calculation-exposure risk (mind the "simpler villain" guard); (2) this is a *sophisticated* reader — a naïve human reader may discount less, so treat 4 as a ceiling estimate. Trust the neutral tier (spontaneous, differentiated) as the real signal.
