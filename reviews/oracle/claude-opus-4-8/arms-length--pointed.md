# Oracle (grounded) — arms-length · pointed

*model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: arms-length · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Specifically Pace and Randi — were you able to hold them at arm's length as you read, or did they get past your guard and pull you in the way they pull Vee in? 0-5 for each (0 = I stayed fully at arm's length, an observer; 5 = they completely got past me, I was taken in alongside Vee), with what did or didn't let them reach you.

---

**Pace — 4.**

He got past my guard, over and over, and I said so on the page more than once — "I'm unsettled by how much I like him," "the *may I* that undoes Vee undid me a little too, and I resent that it did." Chapter to chapter the swoon was real and I stopped pretending otherwise: the apple sliced cut-side up, the froyo "and it's froyo," the burgundy dress matched not to the color she named but to *her*, the truck where he gives away his one dry shirt and takes the cold, the cracked "I missed you so much" at the reconciliation. Those landed in my body the way they landed in Vee's, in real time, ahead of thought. What keeps him at a 4 and not a 5 is that I never lost the second track — I always heard the machinery under the tenderness (the pre-warmed room, the "wrapped box on the counter," the withheld bedroom door, "the evening had used her well"), and "Gone" and "Rock" gave me a source and a plan Vee never had. So he reached me *with* the dread running alongside, where Vee had only the reach. He got in; I just always knew I'd been gotten.

**Randi — 3.**

She reached me, but by a different door than the one she uses on Vee. Her *charm* — the mining dressed as girl-talk, "sticky fingers," the thumb dragged once across the toenail, "He made it *to* you" — I mostly held at arm's length; I watched those as operations even while I registered their heat, because "The Bench" and "How It's Done" and "Gone" had shown me the technician underneath. Where she got past me completely was the *wound*: the face going back on "quiet as a deadbolt," the bottle cap under the thumbnail, the ragged "Cheating bastards" aimed too near to be meant for strangers in "The Outlier," and the whole Gstaad chapter where she's warm only where a body touches her. I ached for her hard and couldn't flatten her into a villain — "I believe the love and I distrust the management and they are the same sentence." So she took me in, but through pity and recognition rather than through seduction the way Vee is seduced; my sympathy was fully hers, my trust never was. That split is why she sits a notch below Pace.
