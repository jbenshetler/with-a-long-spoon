**Question asked (verbatim):** How strongly do you sense Randi's feelings for Vee run past friendship — into love, or wanting her? 0-5, with what in your memory puts it there (or the absence of any).

5.

This one I don't hold ajar the way I hold most of Randi — the book took me all the way inside it. It starts as a flicker in "Gone," where she confesses against her own control, "I keep waiting to get bored of her... she got past me before I could help it," and then goes still and tight when Pace lights up describing Vee, and covers it with sex — I read that as plain jealousy. It sharpens in the practice room, where she drives Vee's body to a peak with a story and her eyes on her, and in "Sorority," where she kneels and runs one thumb across Vee's toenail and Vee's whole floor tips — that's seduction, not friendship, done on the body. The recurring goodbye kisses with the "second press," the hugs held "a beat longer and the beat after that."

But the thing that puts it at a 5 and not a 4 is "Fairytale," because there I was inside Randi's own head: "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had at his best," and the naked line about Vee — "her whole body knew it, and she knew exactly what it was," meaning it two ways. And then the decision, cold and deliberate: she's "not brave enough to lose her," so she'll keep the brunches exactly as they are and "have Vee this way" as long as the word keeps its cover. That's not warmth I'm inferring — it's a woman consciously choosing to go on loving a girl in secret. "All the Time" gives the involuntary version, the catch in her breath and the low "It doesn't"; "My Friend Randi" gives "I'm so jealous I could scream," said light so it passes.

The one thing that keeps me honest, and is why I'd never have called it lower: it's braided with the handler's craft so tightly I can't separate the wanting from the managing — but the book's own answer, from inside her, is that the wanting is real and got past her. So: 5.
