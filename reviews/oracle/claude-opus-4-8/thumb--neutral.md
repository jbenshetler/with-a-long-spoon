# Oracle (grounded) — thumb · neutral

**Question asked (verbatim):** Anywhere the writing lost you — confusing, boring, or a moment you didn't buy?

---

Honestly, the book lost me less often than a book this long has any right to. But a few things:

The one real moment of confusion was chronology. "The Pointing Game" genuinely disoriented me — I'd just watched Randi shut down under a duvet in "Standards," decided he could have the face and never her, and then this chapter has her openly, helplessly delighted, "four weeks in," "she was not performing now." I couldn't place it against the retreat I'd just read, and the reading order threw me. I held it open rather than forcing it, but I never fully squared it.

The place I most often felt the author's thumb was the stats lectures. The book kept using the professor to narrate its own machinery over Vee's head — latent variables ("the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer"), then substitution, then error-correcting codes, then Simpson's paradox ("short stretches can lie against the whole"), then outliers ("in everything that comes after"). Each one landed a half-inch too neatly. Individually clever and usually dressed in enough comedy to survive; as a recurring device it started to feel like being handed the reading instructions. Simpson's paradox in "All Told" was the one spot I felt actively handled rather than swept.

Structurally, the thing I watched wear thin was the debrief. Vee-tells-someone-everything recurs a lot — Cassie in "Rye," "What to Wear," "Turned Up," "Toenails"; Randi across the brunch/lunch chapters. "Fed" in particular is very nearly the same set piece as "How It's Done" (brunch, price the drink, Randi's decoy sad story, the extraction, the goodbye kiss), and I flagged that a third run would start to show the gears. It mostly kept earning the repeats by turning something — but I was aware of the shape.

The break-time run (Seconds / Under the Rug / Bare, then the two New Year's vigils) held the withheld-name wound one or two beats past where I'd already felt it — "not the big one and not the small one" got restated across several chapters. Beautiful in isolation, slightly redundant stacked.

Small things I didn't quite buy: the campus cop as a device — a clean cut in "Leave No Trace" that felt a touch convenient, and then the telegraphed fake-out callback in "Nothing Underneath," the one moment that chapter reached for an event instead of trusting the body. And a genuine wobble: in "broken-in," Vee places Brooke as "Randi's sister from the smoothie shop," where I'd carried her as the chapter president from "Standards" — I read it as Vee's imprecise memory rather than a real slip, but I noticed it.

None of it bored me, exactly. The friction was always the same species — the prose occasionally naming a thing it had already made me feel — and the control was strong enough that I forgave nearly all of it in the moment.
