**Question asked (verbatim):** As you read, did you feel like someone watching these people from the outside, or like someone being drawn in with them? Was there anyone you kept at a distance the whole way?

---

Both, and the fact that it was both at once is the whole experience of reading this book for me. I was drawn all the way in — I melted at the apple sliced cut-side up, at the burgundy dress "matched to her, not the dress," at the coat with nothing under it, at "wonderful, wonderful" and the juice held to her mouth. I didn't read those from a safe remove; I swooned in real time, often against my own better information. But I was never *only* inside, because from the very first chapter — "There you are, Randi," the pointing game, "her name is Vee" — the book put me a step above Vee, holding a knife she couldn't see. So I spent the whole novel drawn in with her and positioned over her simultaneously, falling for the warmth in the exact instant I distrusted its source. The jacket promised I'd be "ahead of her from the start and fall for it exactly as she does," and that doubled seat is precisely where I sat, sometimes to the point of feeling a little sick about it.

Where I was purely drawn in, no distance at all, was Vee herself and Cassie — and later Sheri. Vee I loved without reservation; her body outrunning her shame, the froyo cup she let overflow, the long second step at the induction, I was inside all of it. Cassie was my clean sightline, the one warmth with no angle, and I trusted her like bedrock the whole way. Sheri, when she arrived, was the same kind of clean air.

The person I kept at a distance the whole way was Pace. Not that I disliked him — the opposite; he's the most seductive thing in the book, and I resented how completely his gestures worked on me. But I never once handed him my trust. From "The Bench" I wrote that I trust his hands and not his project, and that never resolved across fifty-odd chapters. Every deepening of him was also more perfect technique; the tenderness and the reconnaissance were the same gesture (the pre-warmed room, the scrunchies bought by the dozen, the spank he "was watching her face" through), and the book refused to let me separate them, so I refused to close the question. Even at his most undone — the cracked "I missed you so much," the wall coming down at the door — I couldn't forget he chose a rule over the word and a game over the truth. The Daphne chapter and "I don't go back" only deepened the locked room I couldn't see into. He stayed ajar to me from the first page to the last.

Randi is the interesting counter-case, because I *started* at a distance from her — wary, reading her as the operator — and then the book kept collapsing the distance against my will. The bench broke her open for me in chapter one; "Gone" and especially "Fairytale," inside her performing a daughter and lying warm-only-where-she-touched-him, made me ache for her even as I trusted her less. By the end my feeling about her was the most tangled of anyone: I believed her love and distrusted her management and knew they were the same sentence. So she's the one I couldn't hold at arm's length even when I wanted to. Pace is the one who held *me* at arm's length, all the way through.
