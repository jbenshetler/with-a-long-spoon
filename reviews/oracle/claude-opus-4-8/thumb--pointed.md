# Oracle (grounded) — thumb · pointed

*model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: thumb · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Did you ever feel the author's thumb on the scale — telegraphing, over-explaining? 0-5, quote it.

---

2.

Mostly the control was remarkable — chapter after chapter I wrote "almost none" or "very little" under friction, and the scariest thing about the book was how often the thumb was *invisible*, especially in Pace's scenes where virtue and technique were the same gesture and no line nudged me toward either. When I did feel a hand, it was light and locatable, and it clustered in one recurring place: the stats lectures, which the book kept using as a decoder ring for its own plot. Latent variables — "the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer" — landed "a half-inch too neatly, a small authorial wink." Simpson's paradox in "All Told" was the clearest: "a thing can run one way across every short stretch you measure and the opposite way across the whole of it," and I flat-out named it "the chapter's thumb... I felt it as a thumb even as I enjoyed it." The outlier lecture closed on "easy to see if you knew to look for it, invisible if you didn't," which I called "right at the edge of the author's thumb, spelling out the metaphor one degree past where I'd found it myself."

Outside that device, the flags were tiny and I mostly forgave them in the moment: the over-spelled face-vs-person exposition in the very first chapter ("none of it reached any further than the face, because the face was not her"); "the clean machine" repeated three or four times in "A Recognized Method" until I felt the author making sure I caught it; the pinup sequence in "In His Hands" lingering on the lamp-and-angles "to be sure I saw the composing"; a couple of closing lines ("that was the whole of it," "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone") that re-rang a bell one beat past where I'd already felt it. Individually none of those spoiled anything.

So: a 2, not lower, because the stats-lecture trick was a real, repeated, felt device I clocked as being aimed over the characters' heads at me — and not higher, because everywhere else the book trusted me, refused to editorialize Pace's blindness or Randi's cruelty, and left the things that mattered honestly ajar.
