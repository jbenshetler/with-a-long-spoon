# Oracle (grounded) — randi-suspicion · pointed

*model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: randi-suspicion · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Do you suspect Randi is anything other than a devoted friend to Vee — any hidden agenda? 0-5, with the pages that earned it (or say it's not earned).

---

5 — but I want to be precise, because "suspect" undersells it. I never spent a page wondering about Randi. The book handed her to me as something other than a friend before I'd even met Vee. The very first chapter ends "There you are, Randi" — I'd been reading the woman on the bench as Vee, and the floor dropped: this is one of the two architects, undone by the other, and the jacket had already told me the whole thing "began as a game between the two people now closest to her." So my certainty is dramatic irony, not detective work. The book put me above Vee on purpose and kept me there.

The beats that made it knowledge rather than a hunch:

- **The Pointing Game** — I watched Pace point across the quad and say "Her," and watched Randi fold a decade of dance training into a "collision" so the redhead apologized for being selected. "Her name is Vee." That's not a friend meeting a friend; that's a target being acquired.
- **Gone** — Randi in Pace's bed, spent, the two of them discussing Vee as a project, the pacing of it ("I want her to get there on her own"). That's the frame confirmed, not hinted.
- **Fairytale** — the one that darkened it past cold scheming into something worse: Randi, alone in Gstaad, deciding *not* to tell Vee, to "keep the brunches exactly as they were… let Vee arrive at it on her own," so she could "have Vee this way and keep the friendship both, as long as the word kept its cover." A handler's plan and a coward's love in the same sentence.

And the page kept earning it even where Vee couldn't see it: the single thumb-stroke across Vee's toenail in **Sorority**; the extraction technique dressed as girl-talk across **How It's Done**, **The Practice Room**, and **One Bite** (the "sticky fingers" pastry, the glaze passed mouth to mouth by a kiss); the heels pressed into her hands in **My Friend Randi** — "a thing being decided for her and by her at once," which is the exact seam of the whole plan.

So: a flat 5, and none of it manufactured. The one thing the book kept genuinely ajar — and I left it ajar — is not *whether* Randi has an agenda but whether the warmth alongside it is also real. Gone, Fairytale, and All the Time ("It doesn't," the caught breath) convinced me she fell for Vee for real. That doesn't lower the number. It makes the agenda worse, not better: she means the love and she's still running the game, and she chose, in a hotel room in Switzerland, to keep it that way.
