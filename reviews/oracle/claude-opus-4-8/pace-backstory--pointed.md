**Question asked (verbatim):** (a) COMPREHENSION — How full a picture of Pace's past do you have: family, childhood, how he got here? 0-5 (0 = a blank; 5 = a full history). List what you actually know. (b) PULL — When the book holds his past back, does it pull you toward him or shut you out? 0-5 (0 = purely shut out / frustrated; 5 = purely intrigued, wanting more). Name the one thing about him you most want to know and don't.

---

(a) 3.

Not a blank, but nowhere near a full history — I carry vivid, disconnected shards rather than a life. What I actually know: his real name is Peter, and he takes his mother out of the answer when he gives it. The childhood wound in "Rock" — seventh grade, taught himself trig, asked too many questions, and the teacher moved his desk into the hall for a year and gave him long division; "they took the thing you loved and made it the punishment." Daphne: at fifteen, his assigned nineteen-year-old tutor, two years of it, which he names as luck and "the best two years I'd had" without hearing what he's defending — the darkest, most formative thing I hold about him. The math: a genuine gift, a PhD, a patent (the MRI/error-correcting-codes work), and the woodworking that furnishes the whole house. And a present-day estrangement from his parents — "I don't go back," said flat with the ease shutting off mid-sentence; the something that crossed his face at "parents"; the Christmas exile that rhymes with Sheri's. Plus the unexplained scar high on his inner thigh, a surgeon's line, guarded with "Not tonight." What I don't have: why he's cut off from his parents, where he actually came from and how he got from there to here, what the scar is, and — most of all — the interior that would connect the hallway boy and the Daphne boy to the courtly man. The pieces are sharp; the through-line is missing, kept missing on purpose.

(b) 4.

It pulls me in, strongly — the withholding is generative, not a wall. Every locked box ("a door with a time on it that wasn't now") made me lean closer rather than give up; I kept choosing to hold his questions ajar because I wanted the book to earn them, and it kept earning the wanting. The only thing docking it from a 5 is a real edge of frustration that arrived late — the "words are cheap" / "gone past him entirely" ambiguity, where his opacity started to feel like a man protecting an architecture, and I wanted a floor under him that the book wouldn't give.

The one thing I most want to know and don't: the scar. High on the inside of his thigh, a surgeon's line, and every time it comes up he steers her (and me) off it — "Not tonight." I want to know what it is and, specifically, whether it connects back to Daphne. It's the one concrete locked room I kept physically returning to, and I still couldn't get the door open.
