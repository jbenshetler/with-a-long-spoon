**Question asked (verbatim):** 0-5: how much do you read each POV as feeling-colored rather than objective — a character who loves or wants someone seeing them more flatteringly, or more forgivingly, than they might deserve? (0 = I take each narrator's read of others as straight truth; 5 = I constantly adjust for who's looking and what they feel.) Point to a place you caught yourself doing it.

---

5.

I was adjusting for the looker in nearly every chapter — it was the whole texture of how I read this book, because the jacket put me ahead of Vee from the start and then the POV kept handing me her generous readings of engineered things. The place I caught it most cleanly was Vee's own aside in "A Round": "who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking." She deploys it as proof she was chosen over the most beautiful woman she knows, and I heard it ring the opposite way — of course he walked past Randi, he came home to Randi. That single line did more to my stomach than the sex, precisely because Vee's flattering read and the true read were the same sentence pointed in opposite directions.

But it went both ways, which is why it's a full 5 and not a 4 — I discounted the schemers' self-serving frames too, not just Vee's love-blur. In "Gone" and "Fairytale" I kept having to re-weigh Randi: her warmth reads real from inside her own head, so I had to hold that her tenderness and her handling were the same gesture and refuse to let her POV settle the question in her favor. And Pace's chapters ("Rock," "The Usual") were the sharpest test — his care reads sincere from inside, and the more sincere it read the more I had to adjust, because a sincere seeing wrapped around a rigged game is colder, not warmer. The one narrator I mostly took straight was Cassie; every time she read a room I trusted it, which itself is a feeling-colored choice on my part — I wanted one clean instrument, so I granted her one.
