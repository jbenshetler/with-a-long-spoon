They mostly feel like real people to me, which is why the scheme bothers me instead of feeling merely mechanical. Vee is especially alive: hungry, funny, ashamed, observant, self-deceiving in ways I recognize, and bodily before she is articulate. Cassie may be the cleanest proof of life in the book for me — spare, dry, protective, never ornamental, and often the person I trusted most.

Randi also feels very real, maybe because she never resolves into either villain or wounded friend. I felt her warmth, desire, loneliness, class training, and manipulation all at once. Pace comes closest to construction because he is almost extravagantly capable — math, lifting, cooking, furniture, sewing, patents — and I did sometimes feel the stack. But emotionally he stayed alive for me through his limits: his silence, his wound around Daphne/scar/family, his inability or refusal to spend the words Vee needs.

The thinner people are mostly designed to do a job: John, Brooke’s taller woman, some sorority girls, the bar blonde. But even Brooke, Sheri, Meg, and Kayla had enough texture to land. Sheri especially came alive fast: blunt, grieving, funny, useful, not smoothed into anyone’s fantasy.
