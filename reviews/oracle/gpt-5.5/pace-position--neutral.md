**Question asked (verbatim):** What do you make of Pace? What's going on inside him, as far as you can tell?

---

Pace is the book’s most seductive problem for me. In the room with Vee, I almost always trust his hands: he asks, notices, waits, feeds her, warms the house, gives her the drawer, makes the dress, refuses to shame her wetness or appetite. His care feels real to me, not like theater. The trouble is that his care is also his language, and he seems to believe actions can answer everything words would cheapen. By the time Vee asks “What am I to you?”, that stops feeling noble and starts feeling like a refusal.

What seems to be going on inside him is a mix of devotion, discipline, damage, and fear of naming. I specifically remember “Rock,” where “Peter,” Daphne, and the scar made him feel marked rather than simply masterful; his carefulness suddenly looked built over some old wound. I also remember Sheri in “The Usual” cutting through him: not saying it matters more to him than having Vee. That felt right. He loves Vee, or is as close to love as makes no practical difference in how he touches and tends her, but some locked part of him will not spend the word.

And then there is the worse complication: he and Randi have been managing Vee from behind the curtain. That makes even his tenderness morally compromised. I don’t think he is cruel, and I don’t think his desire for Vee is fake. But he has a dangerous confidence that if every immediate yes is free, the larger secrecy can somehow be survived. He is careful moment by moment and evasive in the whole shape. That contradiction is Pace for me.
