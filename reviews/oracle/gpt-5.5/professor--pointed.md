**Question asked (verbatim):** The statistics professor and his lectures — the paradox, the error-correcting codes, the outliers. How did he land the FIRST time, and had that changed by his third or fourth lecture? A straight teaching device, or a joke/wink shared with you — and if a joke, did you enjoy him or did he wear thin? 0-5 on how much you enjoyed him by the end (0 = groaned, too on-the-nose; 5 = delighted, wanted more), with what changed your read.

---

3.5 — The first time, he landed a little obviously but usefully: in “See You Later,” the “latent variables” lecture felt elegant and maybe “a touch underlined,” because the chapter was plainly about hidden causes in Vee’s behavior. By the third or fourth time, I had accepted him as part of Vee’s weather — the lectures were a wink, definitely, and sometimes very visible, especially in “The Practice Room,” but they usually worked because Vee’s body and relationships were already carrying the idea before the lecture named it.

What changed is that the device got broader and more emotionally useful. “All Told” made Simpson’s paradox click painfully against Vee’s partial tellings, and “The Outlier” was the best use for me because the statistics stopped feeling like metaphor pasted over desire and became a social/moral event: Cassie’s dad, Randi’s family money, Miranda Holdings alone in the white. So he did wear a little thin when the lines aimed too perfectly at the chapter, but by the end I mostly enjoyed the shared wink.
