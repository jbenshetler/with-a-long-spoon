# Oracle (grounded) — thumb · neutral

*model: gpt-5.5 · probe: thumb · tier: neutral · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Anywhere the writing lost you — confusing, boring, or a moment you didn't buy?

---

Mostly it didn’t lose me; the book kept me bodily involved even through quiet aftermath chapters. The places I noticed the machinery were small: a few stats-lecture parallels felt a little underlined, like “latent variables” or the outlier material arriving exactly when the emotional pattern needed naming. I still bought them as part of Vee’s mental weather, but I noticed the author’s hand.

The only recurring credibility strain was Pace’s almost impossible competence: mathematician, cook, woodworker, hiker, powerlifter, patent-holder, tailor of custom silk dresses. In “Two Towels,” especially, “I couldn’t find a shirt I liked, so I made one” briefly stacked one talent too many. But because Vee experiences him through awe, objects, and care, I mostly stayed with it.

Emotionally, I did sometimes resist how perfectly Randi can name and steer Vee’s desire, but that resistance felt intended rather than like a failure. The one thing that truly frustrated me was Pace’s refusal to give Vee words in “Seconds,” “Under the Rug,” and “Believe Me” — not because I didn’t buy it, but because I did. It made me angry at him in the way the book seemed to want.
