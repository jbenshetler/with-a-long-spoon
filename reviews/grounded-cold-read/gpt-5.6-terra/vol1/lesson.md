## Reader reaction

I felt the drop in my stomach with Vee at the Rusty Nail. Up until that blonde came over, the dancing had me completely: the concrete pad, the battered boom box, Pace saying “Don’t think. Just feel the lead,” and then Vee finally letting the movement come through him into her. That was sexy in the way their best moments are sexy—not because he forced anything, but because she chose to stop fighting the structure and found that it held her. “There it is,” landed quietly and beautifully. I was proud of her when she could feel herself doing well.

Then the room turned on her, or seemed to. The blonde’s “Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to” is so neatly barbed that I felt Vee take the wound before she had any chance to reason around it. I sympathize with her even while I winced at “Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?” She knows the venom is there, and I could hear the glass breaking too. The painful thing is that Pace’s answer is plain and decent, and she cannot receive it because she has already made herself into “just next.”

I still trust Pace here. The bartender’s account—that he gets drunk women home, leaves them at the door, and that others have tried to get him to do more—is persuasive, but even before that, his face draining rather than turning cruel mattered to me. He leaves instead of retaliating or making a public scene. The women’s jealousy also makes sense once the bartender says, “Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey.” Vee was not foolish to feel threatened; the place really is full of women who know and want him. But she has misread care as evidence of indiscriminate intimacy, which is especially brutal because care is what she most wants to believe she deserves.

Vee got harder for me to sit with here, but not less dear. Her “Floozies” comes from exactly the old shame she has been trying to shed, and it catches her too: she calls them what she has feared being called. The line “three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut” hurt. She has had so much joy lately, and the old voice can still seize it all in one ugly sentence. The final admission—that she punished him for being the good man she wanted him to be—is honestly devastating. I’m relieved she wakes wanting to go to Randi’s, though I’m nervous about that choice because Randi is the person Vee trusts with these raw humiliations and also the person who knows the secret Vee does not.

Meg lands warmly and efficiently; “Serious dancers wear jeans” is funny and grounding. The brief opening with her gives Vee practical support without taking over. I liked that Pace’s invitation to teach her is explicitly conditional—“If you want to”—and that Vee repeats it because it matters to her.

The erotic charge is mostly in the dance rather than sex: his firm hand at her shoulder blade, her hand pushing against his shoulder, the instruction to “come all the way in,” and the moment she gives the frame her weight. It works on me strongly. The chapter makes bodily trust feel hot, then lets that exact feeling become vulnerable once she sees the other women. The charge goes cold at the bar, deliberately; I didn’t find it slack so much as abruptly stranded with her.

“Lesson” initially means a literal dance lesson, but it expands into something sharper: Vee learning how to follow without disappearing, and then learning what happens when fear makes her invent the worst story before asking. It also makes Pace’s repeated language of consent and guidance echo differently. The title did not give away the rupture; it made the opening feel light and promising, which made the collapse hurt more.

*A Polite Invitation* still feels like a promise of openings Vee can choose, but also of how difficult it is for her to believe a welcome is real. *With a Long Spoon* feels darker to me after this: closeness has consequences, and everyone is handling something that can burn. The jacket’s final beat, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,” is pressing harder now—not because Vee was not free in this chapter, but because her freedom includes the ability to wound herself and someone good when shame takes the wheel.

I want Vee to apologize to Pace directly, though I’m afraid she will first bring the whole thing to Randi and be soothed or steered before she does. My guess from here is that Randi will know exactly how to make Vee feel better—and that this will make me both melt and worry.
