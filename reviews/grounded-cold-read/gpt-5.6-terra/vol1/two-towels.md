The fact that he stops it, gently but decisively, lands hard in the best way. She notices that stopping has always been her job before; I did too. It makes him feel safer, not cooler. The wanting stays lit, though, and I felt it with her—especially once she reaches his bedroom door in satin she chose on purpose and he simply leaves the door closed. The shoes, the knives, the battered mixer, the marked-up pages, the workshop outlines—Vee is learning that Pace has built a whole life with care, and I was pulled along by her astonishment. The dress is where the chapter hit me in the chest. He listened when she thought she was only naming something impossible, and he remembered. More than that, he saw that the magazine’s brick red was not necessarily *her* red. The burgundy being better on her is such an intimate act of attention.
