## Reader reaction

I was swept up by the slow, attentive opening—the apple slices, the way Pace notices what Randi will eat, the way she takes his hands and places them where she wants them. It feels hot because the scene lets her want openly. The first “Show me” lands especially well: she is frightened, awake, intrigued, and choosing.

Then the bench changes the temperature. The consent is unusually clear on the page—Pace checks in, asks for her word, checks her body afterward—but the emotional center turns out to be much more troubling than the physical scene. Randi’s begging, then her quiet tears, hit me hard. I felt for her most when Pace says “You’re beautiful” and she flinches. That is such a small, terrible fracture after all the intensity: being wanted had been manageable; being seen and tenderly addressed as herself is not.

Pace has real appeal to me here. His slowness, care, attention to her responses, and refusal to treat the locked bathroom door as permission all make him feel trustworthy. The line about Daphne—“A closed door was hers, not his”—does deepen that. But I am uneasy with how fully he narrates Randi’s interior condition for her, particularly the confidence of “There you are” and his satisfaction at having taken her “further.” The page earns that unease not because he violates her stated limits, but because he thinks he has brought her somewhere good while she is already deciding to hide from him more completely.

The erotic charge is strongest before and during the early bench sequence: the apple shared between kisses, the patient denial of his hands, her nakedness under the lights, and the build of waiting. The spanking and edging are explicit and sustained, but the heat ultimately becomes inseparable from Randi’s collapse into a need she cannot bear to have witnessed. That makes the orgasm feel less like a triumphant release than the opening of something she immediately has to shut behind a door. I believed it, and it left a knot in me.

“The Bench” is wonderfully direct and then larger than it first appears. It is the physical object where Randi cannot rely on the face and poise she has learned to live through; afterward it becomes an ordinary table again, concealing its purpose in plain sight. That final image is eerie. *A Polite Invitation* now feels like a promise of carefully offered choices that may still lead somewhere emotionally unmanageable. *With a Long Spoon* makes me think, very loosely, of approaching something dangerous with ritual distance—though I do not know yet whether that belongs to Pace, Randi, or the invitation itself. The jacket’s “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.” has absolutely landed: she says yes, and the chapter makes clear that yes can reveal a self she has spent years avoiding.

I want the next chapter badly, but I dread Randi returning to the bright smile and convincing Pace that the night was uncomplicated. My guess from here is that the locked bathroom door—not the bench—is the real turning point: she has learned how much she wants to be seen, and has resolved not to let him see it again.
