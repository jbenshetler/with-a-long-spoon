I felt wrung out by this chapter—in a good way, then in a sharp, frightened way at the end. The climb gives Vee something almost bodily simple: she is tired, hungry, dazzled, cared for without being made small. Pace fixing her heel is especially effective for me because he notices, acts, and lets the care be ordinary. Her immediate shame at having a bare foot in his hands hurts; his quiet “Hot spot. We can fix that” makes the relief land.

The summit swept me up completely. That image of the lake holding the silver sky “flat as a poured thing,” and the world’s vast indifference, gives Vee room to stop performing herself. I loved that Pace sees her against it, then deliberately turns away and gives her “the rest of the looking alone.” That feels intimate rather than possessive.

The food is hot in its own way. The sandwich, the shared water bottle, the cherries: she is allowed to hunger and simply eat. When she spits the pit and flushes, his “We take nothing and leave nothing” could have humiliated her, but he makes it a shared practice instead. I felt very protective of her there. And when she admits she wants the russet silk dress, I ached at how fast she puts it back out of reach. Pace saying only, “That sounds like a beautiful dress,” is almost unbearably gentle. He doesn’t promise it, tease her, or turn her wanting into a debt.

Then the truck turns the chapter incandescent. Vee’s desire is unmistakably hers: she calls him back, holds his gaze, opens her own shirt, lets herself be seen. The erotic charge peaks not in anything physically consummated but in Pace’s absolute stillness—his wanting, his gladness, and the fact that he does not touch her or presume the next step. I was very much with her in that moment. Her mother’s cruel internal voice being braided into the heat makes it complicated without making her choice less real. She wants to defy that voice, but she also wants him. Both are true on the page.

And then her reaching for his hand and finding him shivering moves me even more. She sees him, too. She stops the escalating moment because he is cold and gets them moving. That tenderness keeps the scene from becoming merely a performance of his attention. I feel warmer toward Vee than ever: she is brave, but she is also capable of care when she is finally getting something she wants.

I still cannot fully trust Pace or Randi because I remember what Vee does not know. Yet in this chapter, Pace earns the immediate tenderness Vee feels for him over and over. The secret behind their meeting remains the thing that makes my stomach tighten around all this beauty. Nothing new on the page makes him seem coercive here; quite the opposite. The unease comes from the knowledge I already carry, and from how perfect this day feels to Vee.

The ending scares me. The campus cop’s lights and that one bark of a siren cut through the cocoon so abruptly that I felt abandoned with her under the overhang. Pace being pulled away—possibly just for the fire lane, but the chapter gives me no reassurance—is a real jolt. Vee is left in his flannel, warm and exposed, with the big thing she meant to say swallowed before she can say it. I want to know he is all right; I also dread whatever interruption that siren has made possible.

“Leave No Trace” lands first as the rule about the cherry pits, but it keeps widening. Pace leaves no shame behind when he corrects her; he leaves her heel protected, gives her his dry shirt, even tries to leave her privacy intact. But the chapter itself leaves a mark all over Vee—his flannel, the moleskin, the memory of being looked at. That tension is beautiful.
