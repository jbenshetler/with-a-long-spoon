# Cold read (grounded) — Among Friends

## Reader reaction

The kitchen scene opens so easy I nearly forgot to be suspicious of anything — and that's exactly the trap this book has taught me to notice in myself. Pace washing dishes, Vee talking at his back, and then that flat, unadorned "I want to meet your people" lands like a gift precisely because it's the thing she's been aching for since he wouldn't say the other word back in Believe Me. It's such a small, domestic want compared to "I love you," and yet it does almost the same work — it's naming, it's claiming her in front of witnesses, and the chapter spends the rest of its length proving he means it in the most exhausting, generous way possible: cataloguing every detail he's kept — Meg's boots, Kayla's paint-talking, the Bricklayers, the roommate who couldn't get along with Cassie — items Vee herself barely remembers saying. That's the chapter's real seduction, and it worked on me completely. I felt the same thing Vee felt watching it: *he has been listening this whole time, to all of it, and nobody told her to notice.*

**How I feel about everyone right now.** Pace is, in this chapter, almost unbearably lovable — funny with Danny about lifting numbers ("How's your bench progressing?"), gentle with Theo's jazz, generous with Cassie in a way that actually earns her "You can stay" — and I want to just let myself have that, except the checkpoint won't let me un-know what I know. Every one of these moves — the pre-planned venue, the effortless social engineering, the way he "set them up" and stood back — is the identical machinery the checkpoint told me runs underneath his courtship of Vee (and, presumably, of Randi before her). I don't distrust his *warmth* in the room. I distrust that I can no longer tell warmth from method, and neither, apparently, can he — which might be the saddest reading of him yet.

Randi is the character this chapter really turns the screw on. She's *first to arrive*, at a spot twenty minutes down an unmarked road, seated at the chair facing the door with a beer already sweating — that's not romantic coincidence, that's someone who moves faster than anyone else toward Vee, always. The toast — "meeting Vee was the highlight of my year" — and the "glazed sort of quiet" after it, her fingers turning the glass "a slow quarter at a time," read to me, with everything I know from ch46, as something close to being caught out in public and getting away with it because nobody else in the room has the key. Vee reads it as being moved by friendship; I read it as something closer to grief dressed as tenderness. That gap between what Vee sees and what I see is the chapter's whole ache.

Vee herself I love without reservation here — her plain generosity of imagination (the whole roster of her people, worrying whether Cassie would be "a good sport" at a fancy table, refusing to build a dinner someone would have to endure), and then that one stinger moment: watching Pace at Randi's elbow, laughing, and feeling "a stinger" she recognizes "from Christmas, from a phone that didn't ring" — and dismissing it because "this was Randi, this was her own doing." That's the chapter's most honest and devastating beat for me. Her own body flags the exact right thing and she talks herself out of it in real time, and I sat there wanting to reach through the page and say *don't*, knowing she can't hear me.

**Swoon / pull.** The parking-lot kiss and the truck scene are gorgeous and hot in exactly the way this book does best — desire that's explicit and also completely, unmistakably hers, chosen and re-chosen ("an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second it went on"). I loved the callback to the September storm — his hand trembling on the mountain, hers steady now; her own memory of masturbating on the county road resurfacing right as his hand replaces her own — the book folding its own earlier images back on themselves. The stillness of his hand in her hair when he comes, "the man who did six things at once, down to zero" — that's a genuinely swoony image, control surrendering completely, and it landed on me clean, no unease attached.

**Erotic charge.** It builds slowly and pays off hard in the truck — the undressing, "there was nothing in any of it she could call an accident," her own hand pulling the lace aside as "an offering." That's the peak, and it's very effective; nothing clinical about it. The chapter earns its heat by making us wait through an entire evening of tenderness first.

**Friction.** None that felt like a thumb on the scale — if anything the restraint is the craft here (the stinger gets one paragraph and is let go, not underlined). The only thing I'd call friction is structural rather than sentence-level: I notice I'm now reading every warm gesture from Pace with a second track running underneath ("is this real or is this the method"), and the chapter doesn't resolve that tension, just lets it sit, which I think is the point rather than a flaw.

**Titles.** "Among Friends" reads as almost willfully innocuous on its face — a dinner-party title — until you sit with who's actually gathered and what's actually true among them. Nobody in that room except Vee doesn't know something. It's a title that means "everyone who loves her, in one place" and also, once you've read the checkpoint, "everyone who's keeping something from her, in one place." It didn't give anything away outright, but it recolors the whole chapter in hindsight — a nice, quiet knife. As for the series apparatus — *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — this chapter is the tagline's "warm, wickedly explicit, and psychologically merciless in its tenderness" almost to the letter: the tenderness is not a mask over something colder, it's real *and* it's the mechanism, both at once, and I don't think the book wants me to resolve which.

**What I want next.** I want to see whether that stinger ever gets to finish its sentence — whether Vee's body keeps flagging what her mind won't look at, and whether the accumulating weight of coincidences (Randi first through every door, "we get along... they share an interest... you") ever tips over into something she can't unsee. I don't expect resolution soon, and I don't need one — I'm here for how long she can hold this open before it closes on her.
