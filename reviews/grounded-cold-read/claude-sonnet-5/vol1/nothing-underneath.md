## Reader reaction

Felt read

I came into this chapter still holding my breath from the run before it — the fight in "Believe Me," the cold dorm nights, Randi's Gstaad interlude, the counsel session in "My Friend Randi" — so when Vee wakes from her nap with "not calm... a plan" I was already leaning forward, half dreading and half wanting exactly what happened.

Vee is the whole show here, and she landed on me harder than she has in a while. The dressing scene — the drawer of underwear she almost reaches for out of habit before catching herself ("Pace deserves the pretty one... Pace was getting neither. Pace was getting the one thing no bra in that drawer could beat") — is such a precise, funny, tender bit of interiority. I loved the shoes-on-first joke, I loved Kayla's oblivious "going to see Pace?" nearly stopping her heart, and I loved this line more than almost anything else in the chapter: "Her mother's word came up out of old habit and found nothing to hold to." After a whole checkpoint's worth of "floozie" intruding on her at every charged moment, watching it arrive and simply fail to land is a real, earned turn — not announced, not underlined, just quietly true. That's growth I trust because the text didn't ask me to notice it, I just did.

The masturbation-in-the-mirror scene and then again in the car worked on me completely — the patience-she-learned-from-him turned back on herself, the denial, the "it left her feeling, of all things, powerful." The cop-car scare was a genuinely great suspense beat, real adrenaline, not just titillation — my stomach actually dropped with hers. And the doorstep reveal delivered: the coat opening, the wall coming off Pace's face all at once, "he looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — that's swoon, unguarded, no irony in it for me as a reader in that instant.

But — and this is where I have to be honest with myself — the chapter never actually answers the question Vee drove out there to ask. "What are we?" got no answer in December, and it gets no answer here either. What it gets instead is a flood: his mouth, his hands, his voice cracking on "I missed you so much," pancakes at midnight, and Vee deciding, in that room, that "there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." That sentence is doing something careful — it's present tense, it's *tonight*, and the book has already shown me, twice now, that this exact satisfied-forgetting doesn't hold past the next cold room and the next silence. I don't distrust Pace's tenderness in the moment — it reads as completely real — but I distrust the shape of the resolution: Randi engineered this reunion ("you've got the goods, gorgeous girl") the same way she and Pace engineered the original meeting, and it worked again, on Vee's body, exactly as it worked before. That's not new information, and I'm not accusing anyone of bad faith in this chapter specifically — but the déjà vu of it, a woman's body deployed as the argument because words failed her, sat with me uncomfortably even while the sex itself was working on me.

Randi isn't on the page this chapter, only in Vee's head as coaching and memory, but her fingerprints are all over the outcome — the shoes she chose, the line she handed Vee, even "Those stay on" from Pace at the end feels like it's honoring Randi's staging as much as his own want. I don't fully trust that this "fix" fixes anything real; I think it postpones the actual reckoning, and the text seems to know that too, given how carefully it hedges ("tonight," not "always").

Cassie's single beat at the end — the eyebrow, the "Hmm," going back to her book — is exactly the steady, undemonstrative loyalty the checkpoint promised, and it was a nice quiet landing after all that heat.

Erotic charge: this is the hottest chapter I've read in this run, and it's sustained rather than a single set piece — the mirror, the car, the doorstep, the couch are four separate builds that each pay off, and the cold/heat contrast (freezing porch, his mouth, the wet nipples left exposed on purpose) is doing real sensory work, not just naming body parts. It never went slack for me.

On titles: this chapter is called "Nothing Underneath," and it's doing double duty — the literal fact of the coat, but also a callback to the burgundy dress from the induction, which my checkpoint tells me was "worn... with nothing underneath" as a recurring image of controlled exposure. Seeing that phrase migrate from the dress to the coat recolors both — it's the same gesture (offering herself totally, on purpose, as the argument) reused in a moment of desperation rather than celebration, which makes me read the dress scene a little differently in retrospect: maybe it was never just pride, it was already this. As for the book and series titles — this packet didn't hand me any jacket copy, volume title, or tagline to react to, so I have nothing to say about *A Polite Invitation* or *With a Long Spoon* here; I'm reacting purely to this chapter's own title.

What I want next: I want to see whether "I missed you so much" turns out to be enough for Vee going forward, or whether — as I suspect, based on the pattern of "Seconds" and "Under the Rug" — the ache just goes quiet for a while and comes back the next time the room goes dark and he's asleep and she's awake. I also haven't forgotten that Randi's own New Year's story ended on a very deliberate silence ("and then it got very late... and we went up") and that Randi still hasn't told Vee anything true. I want to know if this reunion buys Pace and Vee real time, or just another round of the same unresolved question wearing a happier face.
