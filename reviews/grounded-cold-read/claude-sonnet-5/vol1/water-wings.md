# Cold read (grounded) — Water Wings

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into "Water Wings" already carrying a lot, and it landed differently than I think it would have on its own. Let me start with the plain surface of it, because the chapter earns that on its own terms before anything else colors it.

Cassie is the chapter's heart, and she deepens beautifully here. The suit-fighting opener ("Who designed this? Genuinely. What man?") is funny and lands exactly right — dry, unbothered, a woman who has made peace with not being looked at and doesn't perform otherwise. But the thing that actually got me was the withheld splash: "Cassie could have pitched it up into the bleachers; she had the lungs, used them all rec season. She'd kept it for Vee." That's not stated as devotion, it's just shown — she has a weapon and she doesn't use it on the catcallers, she uses the gentlest possible version of it on her friend, to shock her out of wherever she's gone. That's real love rendered as behavior, exactly the kind of thing this book does well when it's not performing anything. I trust Cassie completely. I have zero suspicion of her; if anything she's the one person in this whole run of chapters I don't have to watch.

Vee is sympathetic and specific in a way that hurts a little — the swimsuit from sixteen that "fit, and it gave her nowhere to hide," the "There was more of her now. That was the trouble." That's such an economical way to put a body-image ache on the page without a single word of self-pity attached to it. The catcalling ("You won't need floaties with those!") is ugly in exactly the ordinary, low-grade way real catcalling is ugly — not a dramatic assault, just a public grading, ten seconds of it, absorbed and walked through. I felt for her there, and I felt the specific relief of the underwater retreat — "Nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything." That's a genuinely moving image: swimming as the one place her body stopped being a problem, "before she had a body to be a problem." And then Cassie breaking it with the splash, and the fight, and the line "You can't grieve and fight your best friend for a mouthful of pool at the same time; the splashing won." That's the chapter's best sentence and it worked on me exactly as intended — warm, a little aching, genuinely swoon-adjacent in the platonic register. I wanted to stay in that pool with them.

But — and this is where the chapter stopped being simple for me — I read this with dread underneath the whole thing, and I think the text earned that dread rather than me inventing it. In the recent chapters I was just handed, Pace and Randi play a game on the quad where they point at women for a hypothetical threesome, and the one Randi finally, helplessly says "yes" to — the redhead laughing with her head thrown back — introduces herself as "Vee," and turns out to share Randi's stats class. That is the same Vee who, one chapter later, is befriended by Randi with total, engineered-feeling warmth, and two chapters later has an oddly perfect "chance" meeting with Pace in a coffee shop, where he solves her calculus problem and asks, with old-fashioned formality, to call her. Given the naming rule I'm holding to — Vee is Vivienne, one person, not two — I can't read "See You Later" and "Substitution" as coincidence anymore. It reads like Pace and Randi found her on that quad and are now, separately and together, closing in on her, and she has no idea. So when I get to "Water Wings" — a tender, unguarded chapter about Vee's body being catcalled and then healed by her best friend in a swimming pool — I can't just enjoy it as a breather. It plays now like the last untouched, un-surveilled space Vee has, right before (I suspect, and this is a guess) whatever Randi and Pace are building closes around her. The gentleness of this chapter is real gentleness, but I read it with my stomach a little tight, because I think I know something about Vee's situation that she doesn't.

I don't have a shred of suspicion toward Cassie. My suspicion of Randi and Pace isn't in this chapter at all — they're not even mentioned — but it's the lens I couldn't put down while reading it, and I think that's a fair, text-earned unease rather than a manufactured one, given how specific the identity match was in the material immediately before this.

Erotic charge: none, and that's fine and probably the point — this is the one chapter in the run with no heat at all, and it works as a rest note rather than a lapse.

Friction: very little. The catcalling section moves fast — maybe a beat faster than I wanted, I'd have liked one more second inside Vee's face before Cassie's line broke it — but it's a short chapter and the economy is probably right for what it's doing. Nothing felt like the author's thumb on the scale; it's the least "engineered" feeling chapter of the run, which given my suspicion about Vee elsewhere, felt almost pointed — like a held breath.

Titles: I saw "Water Wings" before I'd read a word of the recent chapters, so I carried it the whole way through looking for water and found none until this chapter. Once I got here, the title works on two levels for me. The catcall itself puns on it — "you won't need floaties with those" — water wings as a crude joke about her body needing support it doesn't need. But the chapter quietly inverts that: the actual water wings in Vee's life aren't the ones a stranger imagines she needs for her chest, they're Cassie — the fence she leans on at the edge of the deep water, the splash that hauls her back to the surface. It's a title that looked like it was going to be a cheap joke and turned out to be about who actually holds a person up. That's a nice, quiet reversal, and I liked catching it after the fact rather than being told it. I don't have a tagline or jacket copy in this packet to react to — this read started cold, straight into the recent chapters, no jacket and no checkpoint — so I can't say anything about where "A Polite Invitation" or "With a Long Spoon" are pointing beyond what I'm inferring from the prose itself, which right now looks like: a courtship (Pace and Vee) that may not be what either of them thinks it is, running alongside a friendship (Vee and Cassie) that might be the only honest relationship on the page.

What I want next: I want to know whether Vee ever puts together that Randi and Pace already knew what she looked like before either of them "met" her properly — and I'm bracing for it, because if she finds out the way Randi found something out on that bench, I don't think it will be gentle. That's a guess from here, not knowledge. In the meantime I'm glad this chapter existed at all — it's the first time in this run I got to just like watching two people be kind to each other without an agenda underneath it, and I wanted more of that, plainly, on its own terms.
