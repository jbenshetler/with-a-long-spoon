# Cold read (grounded) — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

Coming off "The Bench" and "Standards," I already had a complicated feeling about Pace and Randi — tender/hot on one hand, faintly claustrophobic on the other — and this chapter sharpens both halves rather than resolving either.

Pace: I trust his hands and I don't fully trust his eyes. The opening is gorgeous and controlled in exactly the way "The Bench" trained me to expect from him — he comes up into her "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended," and that's a genuinely hot, well-earned image of a man using aftershock instead of waiting it out. But then: "He had decided this beforehand." That line landed differently for me than the equivalent moments in "The Bench." There, his patience read as generosity — he was giving her time to arrive somewhere. Here the same patience is aimed at extraction: he asks "Would you like to taste another woman?" precisely when she's undefended, watches her body answer before her mind can, and then narrates his own gladness at having "felt grateful for the glimpse." I don't think the text wants me to distrust him exactly — it keeps giving him warmth, real delight in her joy, scrupulous slowness — but the accumulation of "he chose the spot... for the angle it gave on the doors" and "he had no name for the game yet, only the live private charge of something he imagined they could make together" made me feel, for the first time, that I was watching a man run an experiment on a woman he loves, with her whole-hearted participation but not her full awareness of the terms. That's new unease, and I think the chapter earned it rather than me importing it.

Randi: my sympathy for her, already high after "Standards," went up again and then complicated itself. The single word "Yes" — "alone, with nothing around it — the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — is the best moment in the chapter for me. After watching her manage every micro-transaction in the sorority common room and then lie flat and dry-eyed under a duvet unable to cry, watching her want something with her whole unmanaged body, in daylight, on a public quad, felt like watching a window blow open. I was rooting hard for her there. And then I watched her close it again within one breath — "Uh — yeah... Yeah. I like her" — turning her own wanting into a gift for him, exactly the move she made two chapters ago with "only in a threesome. With you." That repetition genuinely moved me; it's not clumsy, it's the same person doing the same self-rescue in a new key, and I recognized it as Randi rather than as a device.

Vee: barely on the page, but she landed on me the way she lands on Randi — as pure oxygen after a chapter of women "graded against the grammar." "Laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" is doing a lot of work, and it worked on me. I liked her instantly, unreservedly, on no more than a paragraph, which tells me the prose is doing its job. But I also felt a real pang of unease on her behalf, because she has no idea any of this is happening to her — she is being watched, graded, and then maneuvered into an "accidental" collision engineered with the precision of a dance move, and I don't get her consent to any of that, only Randi's skill at extracting it invisibly. "A small collision. Nothing." — except it wasn't nothing, it was designed, and she doesn't know it.

Swoon / pull: two places. The undefended orgasm at the top, and the bare "Yes" near the end — both are places where a controlled woman's body outruns her own permission, and both worked on me as heat, not just as psychology. I also just enjoyed the walk itself — the single burning maple in a field of green, the frisbee and the grease smell, Randi's private catalogue of exactly which women don't qualify and why. It's a genuinely fun, warm, funny stretch of prose even while it's quietly building toward something with teeth.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no suspicion that Pace means Randi harm — every signal says he's paying rapt, generous attention to her pleasure. My suspicion is structural rather than moral: he is always one move ahead, always deciding the terms before she knows there are terms, and now that pattern has widened to include a stranger. That's earned by the text itself ("He had decided this beforehand," "he chose the spot… fast and without seeming to"), not manufactured by me being on guard.

Erotic charge: peaks in the first page (the mouth still wet, the kiss, the question landing in her body before her mind) and again, differently, in the "Yes" at the end — that one is barely sexual on its face and is somehow the hottest line in the chapter. It goes slack, on purpose I think, through the middle of the walk, which reads more like comedy of manners / character study than erotica, and that's fine — it's not trying to be hot there, it's trying to be true.

Friction: the parade of rejected women (matched pair, closed camel-coat girl, library "maybe," room-chairing girl) runs a beat long — each one earns its keep by sketching Randi's taste and her prejudices, but by the third rejection I felt the machinery a little, the sense of a checklist being worked through before the real one arrives. Small thing, not a complaint really.

Titles: "The Pointing Game" is exactly what happens, almost defiantly plain — and I think that plainness is the point. Pace himself insists on keeping this "a game," something with "the wrapper the wanting needed to come out in," never "a solemn name" — so the chapter title performs the same euphemism the characters use on each other and on themselves. It didn't spoil anything for me going in; it recolors now, in hindsight, as one more instance of the book's interest in the names people refuse to put on their own wanting. I wasn't given jacket copy or a tagline in this packet, so I can't react to those, but the series name With a Long Spoon and volume title A Polite Invitation both feel newly apt after this chapter — an invitation extended to a total stranger who doesn't know she's been invited to anything, at a very long, very careful remove.

What I want next: I want to know what happens when Vee is not a stranger anymore but a person with her own interior life on the page — everything so far has been Randi's and Pace's gaze on her, and I'm hungry to see her from the inside, or at least to see whether the book intends to let her stay an object of their game for long. I'm also worried for Randi — the "he could have the face... not ever" note from "Standards" is still ringing, and this chapter's "Yes" is the first crack I've seen in that resolve; I want to see whether the book lets that crack widen or lets her seal it back up. Guess from here, clearly marked as a guess: I suspect this triangle is going to cost someone something, and I don't yet know who.
