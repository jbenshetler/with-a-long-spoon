**Felt read**

This one's a long exhale after a run of chapters that had me practically holding my breath — "We Find Out," "One Bite," "Above Him" all landed hard on me, and "School Nights" is the comedown, and I mean that as praise, not complaint. It's barely a page and it's almost entirely Cassie and Vee in a dorm room at eleven at night, and it did exactly what it needed to: it let me breathe, and it let me feel how far gone Vee actually is, stated plainly instead of shown through another spanking or another confession over mimosas.

Cassie is the one who came out of this strongest for me. She's dry, she's unimpressed by nothing, and she still opens the door every single time — "Go on, then" — because she loves Vee and wants to hear it even when she clearly isn't sold on the whole picture. The line that got me was the ending: "she didn't say a thing against it, and she didn't say I know, only went back to her book." That's Cassie's whole character in one beat — she has an opinion, she is visibly withholding it, and she loves Vee too much to spend it on a "maybe" that isn't fully formed yet. Given everything the checkpoint told me about Cassie's silent wariness of the sorority world, it's a small, controlled thing to watch her restrain herself here, on a question that isn't even about Randi — just about how fast and how total this has gotten with Pace. I trust Cassie completely. She's the only person on this page I don't have to hold at arm's length.

Vee, I feel more tenderly toward than almost any chapter yet, because this is the first time I've watched her try to find language for what's happening to her rather than just be swept through it in scene. "Pace wasn't fine. She reached for the word after it and didn't have one, and laughed at herself reaching." That's such an honest, unguarded moment — not a declaration, an admission of insufficiency, which felt more true than if she'd just said "I love him" outright. And then she does, sort of: "Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast." I don't feel manipulated by this the way I sometimes brace for with Vee and Randi's scenes — this is just Vee, alone with her oldest friend, telling something close to the truth for once, unedited (the checkpoint told me she edits everything emotionally charged for Cassie — and here, tellingly, she still doesn't quite name it, but she doesn't dodge it either).

Nothing here made me suspicious of him — if anything, this chapter is where his tenderness gets a kind of second witness, someone other than Vee's own aroused, grateful narration vouching (implicitly) for the shape of it.
