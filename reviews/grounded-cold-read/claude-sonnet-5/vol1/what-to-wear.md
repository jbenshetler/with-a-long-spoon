# Cold read (grounded) — What to Wear

## Reader reaction

Felt read

This one lands lighter than the last two chapters, and I needed that after the hike and the truck — it's Vee glowing in the gold dining hall, retelling her own story to a new audience, and it's a genuine pleasure to watch her be delighted rather than watched or engineered for a page. Kayla and Meg are a nice surprise: they're not Cassie, they don't carry the wary, watchful thing Cassie carries around Randi. They're just normal college girls being thrilled for their friend, stealing fries, demanding the shirt story "with the middle," scandalized by a two-o'clock text with no information in it. It gave the book a little air. I liked Kayla instantly — "They're all nice until you don't want them and then they're wonderful" is a genuinely sharp, true line, and it made me trust her as an observer even in a small role. Meg's "Everybody's different at the start... I said it about Danny" landed as the one moment with real teeth in an otherwise breezy scene — it's the reader's own dramatic irony voiced by a minor character who has no idea how loaded it is, and Vee "let it go by. It didn't catch on anything," which stung a little on Vee's behalf, because I *do* know why it should have caught.

Pace himself doesn't appear on the page here except through Vee's retelling and one bare text message, and that absence works on me strangely — I miss him a little (which tells me the courtship chapters have their hooks in), but I also notice how the retelling smooths every edge in his favor. Vee narrating "he lifts in the morning, first thing... 'My writing's no good after that'" and doing his flat, certain voice for her friends — that's someone falling for the *idea* of a man as much as the man, performing her own infatuation to an audience and enjoying the performing. I don't distrust Pace more because of this chapter, but I also didn't get anything new from him to lean on; he's a text message and a secondhand voice.

Swoon and pull: real, but retrospective — it's all happening in the telling, not on the page live. The shirt story still gets me even secondhand ("He stood out there with his back to the truck, soaking to the skin, so I can have the dry one" / "Stop," Kayla said). And then the last stretch of the chapter, when the phone buzzes and it's his name and "the fry in her hand was just a thing in her hand now" — that's a lovely small physical detail of infatuation, the world going flat and unimportant around the one thing that matters. The paragraph that actually got me was the quiet one under all the friend-noise: "She'd had the dinners and the trail. She'd had the shirt, folded on her pillow at this exact hour. She'd had every piece of him she'd been handed, and never once been through his door, and now there was a door, and Saturday behind it, and not one word about what it was for." That's the chapter's real center for me — not the girl-talk, but Vee alone inside her own wanting, cataloguing what she's been given and what she hasn't, and letting herself want the unknown thing behind the door instead of being frightened of it.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new to distrust on the page itself, but the checkpoint's dramatic irony sits under every line of this chapter like a hand on my shoulder. An invitation to his actual house — "the house off six-fourteen" I've been braced for since ch9 — reads to me, knowing what I know about the bench and about the "plan," as the next real threshold, and Kayla's guess ("He's not asking you over to eat... He wants you for the afternoon") landed with a jolt I don't think was meant as ominous for the characters but absolutely was for me. I don't suspect Pace of anything worse than what I already knew; I just felt the floor tilt a little closer to where I think this is going.

Erotic charge: low and warm rather than hot — this is a talk-about-it chapter, not a during-it chapter. The heat is all in Vee's memory and anticipation, not on the page in real time. It goes slack exactly where I'd expect a retelling to go slack (I'd already lived the truck scene in the previous chapter and this is the secondhand version), but it earns its keep by showing me how the story changes shape in the mouth of the person who lived it — "she could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care."

Friction: very minor — the dialogue rhythm between Kayla and Meg occasionally reads a touch stagey in its quickness (the rapid-fire "No." / "Stop." / "No." beats), but it suits Kayla's established "bright unbroken stream" characterization, so it didn't bother me as a tic, just something I noticed as construction. Theo is introduced and dispatched in about four lines — efficient, maybe a hair too efficient, but it does its job of establishing that Vee has other options and isn't interested, which matters for how deliberately chosen Pace is starting to feel to her.

Titles: "What to Wear" is doing something sly — on its face it's just the literal question Kayla shrieks across the table ("What do you wear? What did he say to wear?"), but by the end of the chapter it's clearly about not knowing what you're walking into, dressing for an occasion nobody will name. It's a title that seemed throwaway-cute going in and turned out to be about exposure and unpreparedness — fitting, given what this book has already done with clothing and the body (the cardigan, the wet shirt in the truck). It didn't spoil anything for me, but it did recolor itself by the last page. The book title *A Polite Invitation* rang loud here — the text itself, bare and polite and giving away nothing ("would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock"), is literally *a* polite invitation, and I felt the title tip its hand a little, in a way that felt earned rather than cheap. *With a Long Spoon* — I still don't have a strong foothold on it, but "supping with the devil" is the idiom it echoes for me, and this chapter, with its unnamed occasion behind an unmarked door, is the first time that idiom felt like it was actually pointing at something specific rather than just a title.

What I want next: I want to be in the house. I've wanted it since the invitation to hike was first mentioned, and now there's an actual date and hour attached, and I want to see what's behind that door — literally the house, and figuratively whatever the "for what" turns out to be. Guess from here: given the bench, given "the pointing game," given how carefully Pace has paced every threshold with Vee so far, I expect Saturday to be the chapter where either something sexual is finally on the table between Vee and Pace, or where Randi's presence in "the plan" starts to bear more directly on Vee's actual body — but that's pure speculation, nothing on this page tells me which.

**Motifs & images:** the flannel shirt (first appeared last chapter, "Leave No Trace" — recurs here as the thing "folded on her pillow at this exact hour," now a talisman she's keeping); the cardigan/clothing-as-armor motif recurs obliquely in the whole "what to wear" question; food/appetite motif touches down lightly (dining hall described as "gold and forgiving," the fry she stops noticing); Pace's exacting, permission-asking pattern gets a new instance in Vee's own retelling ("he asks me first... and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it") — an echo of "May I—" now folded into how she describes him generally, not just a line he says.

**Symbolism:** the unopened door/unnamed invitation reads as a stand-in for the whole relationship's information asymmetry — Vee has "every piece of him... and never once been through his door" — which, given what I as reader already know about the engineered meetings, feels like it's standing for more than a literal house visit.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens nicely here — we see her performing her own infatuation for an audience, aware she's embellishing ("the story getting better in the telling and did not care"), which is a more self-aware Vee than I'd fully registered before; it doesn't undercut her sincerity, it just shows she knows she's telling a story, which is a good, human wrinkle. Kayla and Meg are new and land as distinct, likable, ordinary — Kayla's motormouth energy, Meg's dry warmth — though they're thin by design (comic-relief/audience function) and I don't expect much more depth from them. Pace doesn't deepen this chapter since he's absent and filtered.

**Pace — within the chapter:** unhurried but not draggy; the girl-talk runs a beat long in the middle (the Theo bit, the "once? all that and it's once" beat) but the final stretch with the phone buzzing picks it back up and the chapter ends on real forward pull.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** this is a needed exhale after two intense, physically charged chapters (the hike, the truck) — a change of key rather than a repeat of the same beat, and it resets tension nicely by planting a concrete new appointment (Saturday, his house) right as the light in the dining hall goes "orange and then thin." Momentum is building toward that date, not sagging.</text>
