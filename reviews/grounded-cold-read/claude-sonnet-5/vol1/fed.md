## Reader reaction

FELT READ

Okay. This one sat me right back down in that specific chair I've been in since "How It's Done" — the one where Randi orders for both of them, and Vee counts the right side of the menu, and I know before a word of confession happens that I'm about to watch Randi take something out of Vee with her bare hands and call it a gift.

Vee, first — I'm still completely on her side and I ache for her a little more this chapter than last. What got me was the small honest flicker she has and immediately loses: "she heard, in the size of that one word, how far gone she already was, how much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to, and a thread of fear went through her at it and showed before she could catch it." That's real self-knowledge, for half a second — she knows something in her has slipped its leash — and then Randi reaches over, covers her hand, and disappears the fear in eight words: "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." I felt the door close on Vee's own doubt right there, gently, kindly, completely. That's the chapter in miniature for me.

Randi is the one who's moved for me, and moved further in the direction I was already wary of. In the checkpoint she's "genuinely, not only performatively, drawn to Vee" — and this chapter, I believe both halves of that sentence more than ever, at the same time, which is uncomfortable in a way I want to sit with rather than resolve. The opening "sad little story" about the ex-boyfriend — beautiful, nervous, in-a-hurry — landed on me as suspect the second time she's done it (the tied-to-the-headboard story in "How It's Done" was the first), and I don't actually have proof it's invented, just a pattern: Randi opens every one of these lunches by handing over a story that costs her nothing, framed as vulnerability, purpose-built to loosen Vee's tongue. I'm holding that as suspicion, not fact — the text doesn't confirm it's fabricated, it just rhymes uncomfortably with a woman we've watched stage a "collision" and orchestrate a "chance" meeting.

And then the extraction itself — "Nobody measures over a bra" logic all over again, this time: "You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands. You did those Friday. For him." That's not a question, it's a diagnosis, delivered warm, and it works exactly the way it worked at the fitting-recap lunch: normalize the shame first ("it's just how it's done" / "that's the best thing there is, being that gone on somebody"), then let Vee hand over the detail she was protecting, then praise her for surrendering it. "So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" is a genuinely unsettling line to me — Randi noticing exactly the shape of what she's doing to Vee's shame and turning it back on Vee as though it's Vee's problem to solve, not something Randi engineered the conditions for.

Swoon-wise — because there is real swoon here, and I don't want to only be suspicious — the retelling of the night itself got me: the sheet held closed at her breast, "the window black behind him, near midnight, neither of us saying a word and nothing missing." That's a genuinely lovely image, quiet and earned, and Randi's response to it — "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." — is one of the only moments all chapter where I felt Randi being simply, uncomplicatedly generous, no hook in it. I wanted to believe that one clean.

The thing that actually moved on me since the checkpoint: the goodbye kiss. "Vee had half-expected it this time, and met it, and when Randi let her go she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them, the way she had the first time." That's a small, exact, devastating detail — the scanning-the-windows fear from "How It's Done" is just gone now, folded into the ordinary. I don't know yet whether that's Vee growing brave or Vee being brought somewhere by degrees without ever choosing to arrive, and I think the book wants me to not know that yet, so I'm leaving it exactly there.

Erotic charge: no sex on the page this chapter — this is all recounting and coaxing — but it's not slack, because the coaxing itself is the eroticism. The heat lives in Randi's hand staying on Vee's, in "which hip," in "the tit goddess was good to you"-adjacent moves, in the deepening kiss at the curb. It peaks for me at "You knew how that night was going to end before you ever got in the car. You drove over there sure. So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" — that's the hottest line in the chapter and it isn't about Pace at all.

Friction: the only thing that nagged at me was the shape of the scene itself repeating "How It's Done" almost beat for beat — meal, Randi's own "confession" first, extraction of Vee's night in escalating specificity, a physical goodbye that goes a little further than last time. I noticed the machinery a little on the second pass through it. It didn't break the chapter for me because Vee's own line at the end — "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." — is new ground for her even inside a familiar structure, but I did feel the pattern more than I felt surprised by it.

Title: "Fed" is doing real double duty for me. There's the literal brunch, obviously, but "fed" also names what Randi is doing for Vee — feeding her the words for her own feelings before Vee finds them, feeding her permission, feeding her "you're lucky" instead of judgment — and, underneath that, what Vee is doing for Randi: "I'm running on fumes here. How was he?" Randi is hungry for the story the way Vee was hungry on the mountain, and Vee lays herself out for her the same way the sandwich got laid out for Vee. Given how loaded food/hunger has been in every chapter I've read of this book, I don't think that's me manufacturing a reading — the chapter earns it. Book/series title: I wasn't handed a tagline in this packet to react to directly, only the checkpoint's own paraphrase of jacket language about "an appetite that can only give her the best of herself by taking it" — and this chapter is exactly that sentence enacted between two women instead of a woman and a man, which is new and which I want to keep watching.

What I want next: I want to know whether Randi's own disclosures are ever real, even once — I'd like the book to let me see behind her "face" the way it did in the bench chapter, even briefly, because right now I only get to infer her interior from the machinery of what she does to Vee. And I'm braced, not dreading exactly but watching closely, for whatever it is Randi's actual desire for Vee is going to ask of her — because this chapter made it clearer than ever that Randi wants something specific out of these confessions, something more than friendship, and I don't yet know if she knows that herself.
