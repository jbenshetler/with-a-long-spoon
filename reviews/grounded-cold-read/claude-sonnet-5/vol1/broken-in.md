# Cold read (grounded) — broken-in

*scene: scenes/broken-in.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch026 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still holding the ache from "Rock" — that gut-punch of Vee sabotaging the best night she's had, the blonde's poisoned little needle of a line, Pace's face "just going," her walking off self-righteous and then lying awake hating herself in his laundered shirt. So "broken-in" opens exactly where I needed it to: a girl curled around a plan, cold water on her face, fixing the sad-clown mascara, talking herself up off the floor. I liked her instantly for that — the "Wouldn't be half bad without the sad-clown thing" line is exactly the kind of dry self-mockery that makes her feel like a real twenty-year-old and not just an ingénue on a leash. Good, I thought. She's not going to wallow. She's going to go fix it. I was rooting hard for her in that first stretch — the apple, the bin for the core "like a person raised right," the cold air she decides to like. It's a small triumphant walk to a big white house, and I felt the length come into her stride with her.

Then Randi. And this is where the chapter does something to me that I want to be honest about in two directions at once, because both are true and I don't want to flatten either.

First, the warmth is real and it worked on me completely. Randi coming down the stairs with no makeup, hair slept-on, giving up five minutes of sleep the second she reads Vee's face — that's not nothing, and the checkpoint has taught me to weigh it. The "That bitch," the wince at the worst version of the story, the way she takes "jealous of them" and turns it, gently, brutally kindly, into "that's not the disaster, honey, that's the only thing that matters" — I felt the knot in Vee loosen right along with her. And the Edna Mode bit absolutely got me — two girls collapsing into a laugh in a cold bright room is one of the most unguarded, likeable things either of them has done on the page. If I only had this chapter and nothing behind it, I'd call this straightforward, lovely female intimacy: the friend who tells you the truth and then dresses you for battle.

But I don't only have this chapter. I have the checkpoint sitting under it like a stone under water, and it kept surfacing at specific moments. "He won't text you, by the way — don't read it as a door closing. If he's the man you've just described to me, he doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." That line stopped me cold. Vee hears it as a friend reading a good man correctly off one story. I hear it as someone who has watched this exact pattern from inside his house, telling her, with total unremarked confidence, precisely how he'll behave — and then scheduling the reconciliation for "tomorrow night," scripting the wardrobe, the mood, the order of it: not sorry, sure. That's not a friend comforting a friend. That's a director blocking a scene, and Vee has no idea she's in one. It's the pointing game again, in miniature, and it landed on me exactly the way the checkpoint's "impression" warned me it would — warm and calculated at once, both fully true.

The shoe-fitting sequence is the erotic center of the chapter for me, and it's doing real, unmistakable work whether or not either woman is naming it. "The cool quick hands somewhere down past the hem, her foot guided in, a strap closing at the ankle... a hand came up under the skirt to the outside of her thigh and held her there" — and then the thumb pressed "once into the soft hollow behind her knee on the way down, and Vee's breath did something she hoped wasn't out loud." That's not subtext, that's text, rendered with real patience and specificity, and it worked on me as heat even though nothing explicit happens — clothed, contained, and charged precisely because it's contained. It also lands as a direct echo of the fitting scene with Pace: "There was a whole architecture in there she'd never once felt in a shoe, a structure that took her foot and held it, told it where to be... Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." I felt the hair go up on my arms reading that — it's the clearest statement yet of what's happening to Vee across this whole book, said about a shoe, and I don't think that's an accident of phrasing even though nothing in the chapter insists I read it that way.

And then the goodbye kiss at the door — the third of these now — where Vee catches herself "a half-beat late: she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." That's the single most quietly devastating sentence in the chapter to me. Not because anything alarming happens on the page — it's a soft, ordinary kiss — but because of the smallness and totality of Vee's self-deception in that one clause, "It was only what they did." I'm not going to resolve for her what it means; the text doesn't, and I won't pretend it does. But I felt the floor tilt a little under her the way it tilted under her literally in the boutique, and I recognized it as the same motion.

Character feelings, in order: Vee — I love her more, not less, for the shame spiral and the fast self-correction; her economic anxiety (letting herself be given the shoes, "you'll be my very own shoe whore," the credit card math she "decided not to finish") keeps deepening her rather than repeating a beat, because now it's tangled with dependency on Randi specifically, not just aspiration. Randi — genuinely warmer to me in the small domestic details (no makeup, bare feet, the freckles she "never gives a thought to") and simultaneously more unsettling than she's been since the bench chapter, because her competence here is so total it stops reading as friendship and starts reading as management. Pace — entirely offstage, but his reported behavior (the plain, honest "Yes. Sometimes. When it's required," refusing to retaliate, leaving quietly) keeps my trust in him high; nothing here contradicts the man the checkpoint described. Brooke — brief but effective: "you're the stats friend," delivered like the house already has a file on Vee, gave me a small chill about how much of this world already knows her without her knowing it.

Erotic charge: peaks hard in the shoe sequence, stays warm through the scarf-tying (Randi's hands over Vee's hands, "cool over warm"), goes almost domestic-tender by the mirror, and closes on the kiss. Never goes slack — even the opening cold-water-face-washing has a bodily, verdict-like weight to it ("the cold went through her like a verdict").

Friction: very little. If I press, the "he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing" line risks reading, to a reader without the checkpoint, as suspiciously omniscient friend-insight rather than the loaded dramatic irony I received it as — but I don't think that's a miss; I think it's exactly calibrated for a reader who does know, which is the position this book has put me in on purpose.

Titles: "broken-in" does double duty and I don't think it's accidental — new shoes broken in over the course of an afternoon, and Vee herself, worn softer, made more compliant, more comfortable being handled and told where to stand and where her foot goes. It doesn't spoil anything outright, but on the second thought the instructions asked for, it recolors the whole shoe-fitting passage from charming to faintly ominous — "broken in" is also what you do to a horse. As for the book and series titles — no tagline text was given to me in this packet, so I won't invent one, but the checkpoint's paraphrase ("the game" that "got real") still fits: this chapter is another quiet turn of that same wheel, an invitation (into the house, the room, the closet, the plan for tomorrow night) extended by someone who already knows more about where it leads than the person accepting it does.

What I want next: I want the reconciliation scene with Pace, obviously — I'm fully invested in wanting these two to find their way back to each other, and I trust him enough that I'm not dreading his reaction, just hungry for it. I also want, more than I did before this chapter, to see whether Vee's ease with Randi's hands and mouth ever gets named by her, even privately — this chapter pushed that thread further than any lunch has, and I felt the pull of it as strongly as the pull toward Pace. Guess from here, clearly marked as a guess: I suspect "tomorrow night" is being staged partly for Randi's benefit as much as Vee's — but the text hasn't told me that, it's only what the pattern makes me want to watch for.

Against the run so far, this functions as a deliberate slow aftermath beat following "Rock"'s emotional spike — a good rhythm change, but one that specifically escalates the Randi/Vee undercurrent rather than simply cooling down, so the book doesn't feel like it's holding still even while Vee herself is recovering.
