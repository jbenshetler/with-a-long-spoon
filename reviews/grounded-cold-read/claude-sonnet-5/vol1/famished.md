# Cold read (grounded) — Famished

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this stretch already primed to root for Vee and ache for her, and this run of chapters cashed that in about as fully as I could have hoped — and then complicated it in a way I didn't expect, mostly around Randi.

**Pace.** He keeps landing on me the way he's landed since the dinner date — as almost unbearably attentive, and I mean that as praise and as a low hum of unease at once. The rain scene in the truck bed (well, cab) is the chapter that got me most helplessly: her rapping on the glass, crooking her finger, unbuttoning under his eyes while her mother's voice calls her a floozie in her head — and then the turn, the thing that actually undid me, is that *he* was shivering the whole time. She reaches to kiss his hand and it's shaking, and the whole erotic charge she built collapses into "Pace, you're freezing," and she takes care of him. That's the moment I fell for the scene, not the unbuttoning — the reversal, that his want cost him something physical and he was going to eat it in silence rather than ask anything of her. Then the dress fitting deepens it further: "Keep still," said a beat too late, after a silence she watches him spend deciding — that's a man visibly not taking the easy yes, and it reads as real restraint, not performance. I trust his tenderness on the page even while I can't unknow, from the checkpoint, that this courtship started as a private game with his girlfriend. That dramatic irony hasn't gone anywhere; if anything, the closer his attention gets, the more expensive that irony feels. The one new thread that snagged me: the scar, high on the inside of his thigh, unexplained, noticed and then just — left. I don't know what to do with it yet, but given the book's established self-harm motif (Randi's bottle cap), it's not a neutral detail to me.

**Vee.** She is the reason I keep reading. The shame/heat braid — "she could not find the seam between the heat and the shame because there was no seam, they ran on the same wire" — is doing exactly the interior work I want from this kind of book: not resolving into either "she's finally free" or "she's finally punished," but staying genuinely double all the way through the sex scene. The line that actually got me — "the shame was still running and she was burning through it... the shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her" — is the best sentence in the whole packet for me. It's honest about desire the way I want erotica to be honest: the shame doesn't disappear because the sex is good, and the sex isn't ruined because the shame doesn't disappear. That's a real interior life on the page, not a rescue narrative.

**Randi.** This is where my feeling actually moved, and moved hard, inside "How It's Done." Up through the checkpoint I had Randi as complicated-but-warm — guarded, performing, but her attraction to Vee reading as genuinely hers. This lunch scene is the first time I felt something close to alarm about her, specifically because of *how good she is at it*. "Nobody measures over a bra... it's just how it's done" — that line is a scalpel disguised as etiquette, and it works exactly the way it's supposed to: it disarms Vee's shame by normalizing it, and in the same breath extracts the confession Randi wants. Then "Which hip?" / "Oh, all the best ones start at the left hip" — meaningless, and it lands like a benediction anyway, because Randi has built the room so completely that meaninglessness reads as intimacy. And then, right at the point where Vee is about to hand over the one thing she's never told anyone — "You don't have to tell me" — Randi closes the door she opened, gently, and the hush is just over. That's not warmth, or not only warmth; that's technique, deployed with real skill, and I felt myself go cold on her for the first time in the book even as I understood exactly why Vee reads it as being loved. The narration flags it too — "something keyed-up in the attention today, a brightness wound a notch tighter" — which tells me the text itself knows something's live in Randi that isn't just curiosity about a friend's boyfriend. Then the kiss at the end — soft, unhurried, and then not ending, a second press with something in it the first didn't have — reads as real desire, not calculation, which is the uncomfortable part: I can't cleanly separate "Randi wants Vee" from "Randi is working Vee," and the text isn't letting me separate them either. I don't think that's a flaw; I think that's the point being earned on the page, and it's the sharpest unease I've felt about anyone in the book so far — precise, not manufactured. Vee's own reaction afterward — scanning every dark window in the dorm for a witness — is a new and very real texture: not shame at her body this time, but fear of being *seen* wanting a woman. That felt true and unforced.

**Cassie**, offstage in this chapter but vivid through the recent chapters, stays my one uncomplicated point of trust — dry, quick, protective without an angle. "Are you sure he's not gay?" got a real laugh out of me, and the tenderness under the comedy ("Oh, Vee") never curdles into pity. She's the reader's ballast.

**Erotic charge.** It's working, and it peaks in two different registers for me: the slow-burn charge of the fitting (the pin, the kiss on the rib, "keep still") and the more sustained heat of the actual sex in "Famished," which stays explicit and stays inside her head rather than going clinical — the oral sequence especially, where his attention to her wetness/shame never tips into either fetishizing her shame or erasing it. The one place I felt the tiniest slackening was partway through the measuring sequence in "A Round" — shoulder, waist, bust, hip, in sequence — before the accidental/deliberate breast-to-cheek beat reanimated it; it's a deliberate rhythm (closeness, withdrawal, pencil) so I don't think it's a miss, just a moment I noticed the mechanism.

**Friction.** A couple of lines read a hair explanatory to me rather than shown — "the beginning of care, the beginning of stepping back — and she did not want his care, not that kind" is telling me what she wants rather than letting the scene do it, and "The expression on his face was the same as the fitting, scaled up" is a slightly tidy authorial callback. Minor, and I noticed them, but they didn't pull me out of the chapter.

**Titles.** "Famished" doesn't surprise me so much as confirm — the word is spoken on the page itself, answering "Are you hungry?", and it slots cleanly into the food/hunger motif that's been running since the apple in the checkpoint. It's satisfying rather than a reveal — I saw it land before I finished the sentence it's answering. As for the book's architecture: *A Polite Invitation* keeps cashing out literally — Pace's whole courtship has been permission-based ("May I—"), and this chapter's whole shape is one long, actually-polite invitation (a text, a yes, a door he opens and steps back from) that ends in her bed. *With a Long Spoon* is the phrase doing the most work on me right now — the proverb about needing distance from something you can't fully trust — and after the lunch scene, it's stopped feeling like background flavor and started feeling like an instruction to the reader about Randi specifically. I wasn't handed a tagline in this packet, so I'm not leaning on one, but the series title alone is doing real interpretive work for me at this point.

**What I want next.** I want to know what Randi does with wanting Vee now that she's kissed her twice and once nearly had the confession — does she go back to Pace with it, or keep it for herself? I want the scar explained, or at least gestured at again. I want to know whether Vee's window-scanning fear becomes a real secret she has to manage, the way the pointing-game secret is one only the reader carries. And under all of it, I keep waiting — dreading a little, wanting it too — for the moment the dramatic irony finally reaches Vee: every gift, every warm thing, still sits on top of a plan she doesn't know exists, and this chapter, for all its tenderness, hasn't touched that wire yet.
