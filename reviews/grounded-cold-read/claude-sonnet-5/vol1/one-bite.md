## Reader reaction

Felt read

I came into this chapter still warm from "Made-Up" — the hallway striptease, the heels staying on, Pace choosing "this one, the other is for the world" — so I arrived at brunch already a little undone, and the chapter used that. It's the most erotically alive thing I've read in this book so far, and also the most unsettling, and for once those two things aren't fighting each other — they're the same sensation.

Vee: my sympathy for her hasn't wavered, but it's gotten more anxious. I love how alive she is on the page — the pride in ordering the cheap mimosa "as though she had ordered a thousand of them," the sheer glee of "I pantsed him," the way she narrates the whole spanking to Randi almost against her own will, feeling each confession land on her body like a second touch. That's such a good, specific observation of how telling a story of your own arousal can re-arouse you, and the text trusts it completely — no apology, no cutaway. And then the moment in the bathroom where she reaches for herself and Randi's face arrives "clear, and fond, and near, as though Vee were the best thing in the room" — and she stops, not from shame exactly but because "she could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was, and the two would not hold in one body." That line got me. It's the first time the book has shown me, not just told me, that Vee's wanting for Randi and her wanting for Pace aren't separate rooms anymore. That's a real turn, and it lands on the body first, exactly the way this book has been training me to expect.

Randi: this is the chapter where I started to feel genuinely uneasy about her rather than just reader-side uneasy on Vee's behalf. Everything she does here — "Some girls just can't wait," "Walk me," "What was the bet" said quieter — reads as pure appetite for the story, and I don't begrudge her that, brunch gossip about a good spanking is a perfectly fine pleasure. But "Sticky fingers?" is a different animal. It's said light, it's maybe just a brunch joke about the pastries — but Vee cannot tell, and neither can I, and the not-telling is the whole point: the text refuses to resolve whether Randi knows exactly what happened in that bathroom stall or whether Vee has simply started reading intent into everything Randi says because her own guard is gone. I don't have proof Randi is doing anything cruel here. But I notice, for the first time, that I don't trust the innocence of a comment the way I would have three chapters ago.

Swoon / pull: real, and I want to be honest about it rather than only suspicious. The recounted spanking scene — the rubbing first, the kissing after, "It stung… but it also concentrated everything" — is tender in exactly the way the earlier scene itself was tender on the page, and I liked hearing Vee choose to tell it that way, correcting Randi's assumption ("It wasn't quite like you said"). There's something sweet in that correction — she's not performing Randi's story back at her, she's protecting the truth of what actually happened, which is gentler than the fantasy. That's a small, real point in Pace's favor that I registered with warmth even inside a chapter that's otherwise circling unease.

Trust vs. suspicion: no smoking gun here, and I want to be precise about that. Nothing in this chapter proves Randi is using Vee maliciously — my unease is built entirely out of what my checkpoint already knows (the bench scene, the pointing game, the pillow talk about "getting there on her own") laid over a brunch that, read with no memory at all, would just be two close friends being wickedly funny about sex. What did earn something new, on the page itself, is the mirroring: Pace checked with his finger before spanking Vee exactly the way — per my memory of chapter one — this scene rhymes with what he did to Randi on the bench. The book isn't telling me that; it's just letting the same gestures recur in a different woman's mouth, and I felt the deja vu land in my stomach before I could name it.

Erotic charge: it's working on me continuously, and it peaks twice — once in the "He checked… Inside?" exchange, which is confession-as-foreplay at its most effective, and again, harder, in the bathroom stall, which is genuinely graphic and then genuinely interrupted, which somehow makes it hotter rather than less, because the interruption is psychological rather than circumstantial. It goes brief-clinical exactly once, on purpose: the "hard clinical blue" bathroom light that "found everything and forgave none of it" is a deliberate cold beat, and it works as a beat, not a slack patch.

Friction: none that felt like a thumb on the scale. If anything the one line that risks being too pointed — "she could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one" — earns its place because it names exactly the ambiguity the whole scene has been building, rather than resolving it for me.

Titles: "One Bite" is doing a lot of work once I look back. Randi takes "one neat bite" of the pastry and sets it down, closing the subject — total control, total restraint, the same portioning discipline the checkpoint tells me opened the whole book with her rationing apple slices. Vee, by contrast, never touches her own pastry at all and still ends up tasting it, secondhand, off a kiss — she gets a "bite" she didn't take, passed to her without her consent or her noticing, which is unnerving once you sit with it. And under both of those, "one bite" is also just Vee's own aborted climax in the stall — a single taste of the thing she wanted, stopped a fingertip short by an image of a woman who wasn't even in the room. Three appetites in one small title, all portioned, none satisfied. It didn't spoil anything going in — it only came alive looking back. The series title, With a Long Spoon, keeps nagging at me the same way it has all along — a long spoon is what you use with something you don't fully trust, and this chapter is Vee eating from Randi's hand at exactly arm's length, believing it's closer than that.

What I want next: I want to know whether "Sticky fingers?" was a knowing shot or an innocent one — I suspect the book will never tell me outright, and I think I'm fine with that, because Vee not knowing is the actual subject. I'm bracing, mildly, for the moment Vee's body stops letting her keep Pace and Randi in separate rooms in her head the way it just failed to in the stall. Guess from here, nothing more: I think that collision is closer than it was three chapters ago.
