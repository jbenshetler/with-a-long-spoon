# Cold read (grounded) — The Practice Room

## Reader reaction

Coming into this chapter off the run I'd just read — the dress reveal in "Peekaboo," the fireside confession about Daphne and the scar, the whole bar-fight-and-apology arc, the tickle-fight-turned-spank in "A Recognized Method" — I was already fully swooning on Pace and Vee. That run did real work on me: the fireside scene especially, where he hands her the hallway-and-long-division story and the Daphne story in the same flat, careful voice, and she does the arithmetic (nineteen and fifteen) and holds the ugly word behind her teeth instead of saying it — that's exactly the kind of interior restraint I want from a book like this. It didn't resolve into judgment, it just sat there, complicated, and I sat with it too. And the bar chapter is the best kind of romance conflict — she's genuinely, unfairly cruel to him for a second, and instead of punishing her for it the book lets her be the one who has to walk the whole thing back, and he meets her with "Thank you for telling me what she said" instead of absolution, which undid me more than absolution would have. By the time I got to "The Practice Room," my guard for Pace was almost all the way down and my trust in him had only grown.

Then this chapter turned the dial somewhere else entirely, and it landed on me differently — colder, more coiled.

The lecture-hall opening did something clever to me before I even noticed it working: the professor's little digression on outliers — "The first kind sits on a single point... The second kind comes in through the part that carries forward. It's in everything that comes after" — is placed right as Randi's knee comes to rest against Vee's under the desk, and I felt the metaphor land on me a half-beat before I consciously registered it as a metaphor. That's a nice trick, though it is a *trick*, laid on with a fairly visible hand — the professor's example doubles so neatly as commentary on whether Vee's attraction to Randi is a blip or something structural that I noticed the machinery even while it was working on me.

The practice room itself is the real center, and it's the most sexually charged thing between Randi and Vee so far — more than the goodbye kisses, more than the lunch confessions. Randi extracting the story of the kitchen spank is familiar (we've seen this pattern since the lunches: draw Vee open, mine the detail), but this time there's no reciprocal softness in it, no "you don't have to tell me" door being closed gently afterward. She goes still "the way a cat goes still" and then, instead of stopping at extraction, she tells her own story — deliberately, at exactly the pitch needed to finish what she started, ending on "Believe me. Very welcome," and watching Vee's face do the rest of the work for her. That "welcome" landing and the "soft wet heaviness she had no vote in" — that's the erotic charge peaking for me in this chapter, and it's not a comfortable peak. It's Randi working a body from across a chair with nothing but a story and her own stillness, and it reads, for the first time this plainly, less like warmth-with-an-agenda and more like an experiment being run.

The moment I trust most in the whole chapter is Vee's, in the bathroom: "Her hand knew the way. It moved before she did. She caught it and put it flat against her own knee and held it there. Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." That's the exact kind of interior life I read for — not "she wanted it" or "she didn't," but a woman catching her own hand and drawing a line she can still, barely, hold, even while her body's already answered the question. I don't need her to win that argument with herself to be moved by watching her have it.

Where I got outright confused, in a way that felt like a real gap rather than deliberate ambiguity I should just sit with: Randi's story about "mine" — "Slight little thing, soft-spoken, apologized to the furniture" — doesn't match Pace at all. Everything I know of him (powerlifter, deliberate, "asks permission," a big careful physical presence) is close to the opposite of "slight" and "apologized to the furniture." So either this is a different, unnamed man the book hasn't shown me, or Randi is inventing/borrowing a story to produce a specific effect on Vee (which would fit the pattern from the earlier scarf-and-restraint lunch story, which already had a slightly performed, too-perfect shape to it). I genuinely don't know which, and I'm holding it open rather than assuming it's Pace under a different description — it doesn't fit him closely enough for me to make that leap, and the text doesn't tell me to.

On the characters: Vee is as easy to be on the side of as ever, maybe more so now that I've watched her hold a line under real pressure instead of just being swept along — that's growth, not repetition. Randi unsettles me more here than at any point since the checkpoint's account of the bench scene and her private collapse — this chapter shows me almost none of her interior vulnerability, only the watching, working side of her, and the ending ("as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And Vee couldn't tell whether she had.") declines to resolve it for me, which I appreciate even as it leaves me uneasy. Cassie is a half-beat of ballast at the top of the chapter and then gone, still the steadiest person in the room. Pace doesn't appear on the page at all here, but the chapter runs almost entirely on the residue of him — Vee's whole state of being is downstream of the weekend I just read, and Randi is, in a way I find newly unflattering to her, harvesting that residue for her own purposes rather than sharing in it.

Trust vs. suspicion: my suspicion of Randi is the sharpest it's been, and I think the page earned it this time rather than my just carrying it in from the checkpoint — the calculated choice of the soundproof room, the story pitched exactly to finish what she started, the stillness with "nearly a pressure, nearly a hand" in it. My trust in Pace, by contrast, is at its highest point yet, built entirely by the chapters just before this one, and this chapter doesn't touch that — it just makes the gap between what Vee has with him and what's happening to her in this room more visible to me as a reader, even though Vee herself doesn't seem to feel any gap yet.

Titles: "The Practice Room" is doing real double duty for me. Literally it's the soundproof music-building cell. But it lands on top of the whole dance-lesson motif from a couple of chapters back — "don't think, just feel the lead" — and I couldn't help hearing this scene as another lesson in feeling somebody else's lead, except this time it's Randi's, and nobody's naming it as a dance. The aside about what the practice rooms "used to" be used for, freshman year, seeds the room's erotic history right before it becomes erotic again in a different register — that's a title that recolors itself as you're inside the chapter, not one that gave anything away up front. I wasn't handed a cover tagline in this packet, so I'm not going to invent one, but *With a Long Spoon* as a series title keeps humming under all of this in a way it hasn't before — something about reaching in carefully, at a remove, for something you're not sure you should be touching, which is exactly what this chapter felt like watching Randi do.

What I want next: I want to see whether Vee tells Cassie or Pace any part of this, or whether it joins the pile of things she's editing out of her other stories — the checkpoint already flagged that shift in where her real confidences live, and this chapter is the starkest version of it yet. I'm dreading, a little, the version of this where Randi's interest stops being deniable to Vee and has to be reckoned with in the open, because I don't yet know whether Randi wants that reckoning or is actively engineering around it. Guess from here, clearly marked as a guess: I think this "practice" is building toward Vee's desire for Randi becoming impossible to file under friendship, and I don't think that reckoning is going to be gentle.
