## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still vibrating from "A Round" — that measuring scene was the most sustained, patient erotic set-piece I've read in this book so far, and "Turned Up" does something smart and slightly disarming with the hangover of it: it hands the whole thing to Cassie and Vee's dorm room and lets it become a debrief, funny and breathless and a little filthy, instead of another scene of Pace's controlled hands on her. I was primed for more of him and got more of *them* instead, and I didn't mind — I actually loved it.

**Vee** is glowing here in a way that feels earned rather than performed. What I like most is how her own voice takes ownership of what happened — "I was grinding on him" is delivered as a punchline, but it's also the chapter's real center of gravity: she is not just receiving Pace's devotion passively anymore, she's naming her own wanting out loud, to someone who loves her, and laughing about it instead of apologizing for it. That's a genuine shift from the girl who, one scene ago, was screwing her eyes shut on a platform bracing for disgust. The line "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" lands as tender and a little devastating — she's absorbed his whole vocabulary of consent into how she experiences being wanted, and it moved me.

**Cassie** continues to be the person in this book I trust without qualification, and this chapter deepens that rather than just confirming it. Her comic timing ("Vee. Are you sure he's not gay?") is genuinely funny — I laughed at "Definitely not gay" and the deadpan "package" diagram bit — but under the joke she's doing real work: she's checking on Vee, following the thread until she finds the thing Vee's been sitting on ("the shirt"), and when the real tenderness surfaces (the heat, the "May I look" line) she goes still and soft and just says "Oh, Vee" and later "I think I have it." She never once undercuts Vee's happiness even while ribbing her mercilessly. That's a real, lovely, unshowy friendship, and it's the only relationship in the book right now that isn't shadowed by something I know and Vee doesn't.

**Pace** isn't on the page here at all — he only exists through Vee's retelling — and that distance did something interesting to my feeling about him. Filtered through Vee's adoring narration and Cassie's teasing, his control reads almost comic (a powerlifter who sews! who turned the heat up hours in advance! who knelt and didn't look down!), and I felt myself smiling along. But I also caught myself doing the thing the checkpoint primed me to do: recognizing the *shape* of what he built in "A Round" — the riser, the sustained exposure, the withdrawal-and-return rhythm, the being-seen-at-her-most-ashamed-and-not-found-wanting — as structurally the same machine as the bench he built for Randi. Nobody on the page draws that line; Vee and Cassie have no idea the bench even exists. But I have that knowledge and it sat under the comedy like a low hum. I don't think the text is asking me to distrust him in *this* chapter specifically — nothing here is new evidence — but the parallel is real, it's mine from having read "The Bench," and I'm not going to pretend I didn't notice it just because this chapter is warm and funny.

**Swoon / pull:** The shirt reveal got me — a whole week of Vee sleeping in and wearing to breakfast a shirt she thought was store-bought, and the tag she checked for and never found, paid off perfectly here as "he couldn't find one he liked, so he made one." And the thermostat detail — Vee realizing after the fact that he turned the heat up *hours* before she arrived so she wouldn't be cold standing half-dressed — got an actual "oh" out of me before Cassie even says it for both of us. That's the kind of specific, quiet devotion this book does really well: not grand gestures, just someone thinking two steps ahead of your comfort and never mentioning it.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion earned by this chapter's own evidence — Cassie is exactly as safe and unimplicated as she's been all along, and Vee is just happy. My unease about Pace is inherited, not freshly triggered: it's the standing knowledge from the checkpoint (the pointing game, the plan) plus my own recognition of the bench/riser echo, not anything this specific scene did to earn new doubt.

**Erotic charge:** Interesting placement — the chapter itself isn't a sex scene, it's comedy about a sex-adjacent scene, and the heat mostly lives in retelling and blush rather than being enacted on the page now. It peaks briefly and specifically at "*I* was grinding on *him*" — that line has real heat in it because of how gleefully, guiltlessly Vee owns it — and otherwise stays at a warm simmer.

**Friction:** None that bothered me. The comic beats (the "package" line, "Are you sure he's not gay") could have felt like a tonal whiplash after such a hushed, charged previous chapter, but it worked for me as release rather than a miss — it felt like how you'd actually process an overwhelming afternoon with your best friend, laughing because the alternative is combusting.

**Titles:** "Turned Up" is doing at least three things by the end and I like all three: the thermostat literally turned up before she arrived; her own body/want "turned up" past anything she expected on that box; and the shirt "turning up" as his handiwork, a small mystery surfacing after a week of not looking at it. It didn't give anything away going in — it only clicked into place retroactively, which I enjoyed. No jacket copy or tagline was included in this packet, so I can't react to it fresh, but I'll say the book title *A Polite Invitation* keeps resonating scene by scene — everything Pace does really is offered as an invitation with a door left open ("he left me a door"), never a demand, and this chapter is Vee explicitly narrating that structure back to Cassie without knowing she's doing it.

**What I want next:** I want the induction dinner and the dress itself — I'm invested in seeing Vee in that burgundy silk, seen and chosen in public this time instead of alone in his sun porch. I also keep waiting, with more anticipation than dread, for the moment (if it comes) when the actual sex happens, given how deliberately it's been withheld through shirt-removal, measuring, grinding, and near-misses. And under all of it I'm still carrying the reader's question the checkpoint left open: whether Vee will ever learn how she got here, and what it will do to this glowing, funny, trusting version of her if she does.
