# Cold read (grounded) — hills-and-valleys

## Reader reaction

Felt read

This one caught me off guard by how short and how cold it was — literally cold, all breath and knuckles and gearing down for a climb, and emotionally cold in a way none of the recent chapters have been. After the sun-porch dress, the fireside confession, the humiliating blowup at the bar, and Randi's tender-handed morning-after repair job, this chapter drops me outside all three of them, on a bike, with a woman I've never met. It works as a breather structurally, but what it actually delivers isn't rest — it's the coldest, plainest look at Pace I've had yet.

Pace himself: this is the first time I've been fully inside his head with no woman in the room to perform care for, and what I find there isn't cruelty, but it isn't the careful, deliberate man either — it's ease. He tells Sheri about Vee — the thumb on the chair joint, the math book pulled off the shelf "like it had burned her" — and "he heard himself going and didn't stop." That's genuinely lovely, actually; it reads as a man caught being smitten, off guard by his own enthusiasm, and I softened toward him for a second. Then Sheri asks the flat, obvious question a friend asks — "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" — and he answers in four words: "Randi. That's not finished." No hedge, no guilt, no acknowledgment that he is, at this exact moment, mid-repair with one woman who publicly humiliated herself over him three nights ago while continuing, uninterrupted, with the other. Sheri does the arithmetic out loud — "Two of them at once. If that's not already complicated, it will be" — and he says nothing back. Not a flinch, not a defense. That silence is the most unsettling thing in the chapter, more than anything Randi has done with her performed warmth, because it isn't performance at all. It's just how it sits with him when nobody he's dating is in the room.

Sheri herself: I liked her immediately, in the economical way you like a new character who's given you exactly enough. Blunt, funny, faster than him on the climbs and never lets him forget it, cataloguing Vee's "backside" the way she'd remark on weather. She's an outside eye with no stake in either romance and no patience for softening anything, which makes her the most reliable narrator I've had access to yet — and what she reports about Vee stopped me cold: "All she did was sit by herself, glaring at the floor, nursing the one drink. Danced with nobody. Left alone." I already knew, from Vee's own account, how bad that night was for her — but hearing it confirmed flatly from someone with no reason to spin it landed harder than the original scene did. It made Vee's humiliation real in a new way, external, witnessed by strangers, while Pace bikes through the cold three days later mostly wanting to talk about how charming she is.

Swoon and suspicion, together and at odds: there is a small swoon here — Pace gushing, caught out by his own feeling, is genuinely winning. But it curdles fast against "that's not finished" and the arithmetic Sheri does that he lets stand unanswered. I don't think the text is asking me to condemn him outright — nothing here is villainous, and Sheri's own take ("if that's not already complicated, it will be") reads more like a shrug than an indictment. But my trust in him, which has been high on craft and care and low on structural grounds since the "pointing game" reveal, took a real hit here — not from anything he did to either woman on the page, but from the sheer flatness of how he holds both of them in his own mind when he's not actively tending one.

Erotic charge: none, and it doesn't need any. This is a quiet, almost administrative chapter — the heat is all in the previous chapters' wake.

Friction: very little. The chapter is tight and knows exactly what it's for. If anything, I wanted one more beat from Pace — some flicker of him processing the Rusty Nail fight itself, some interiority about hurting her — and the chapter conspicuously withholds it. He tells Sheri the good parts (the chair, the book) and skips the fight entirely; we get it only secondhand, through Sheri's own eyes. That withholding felt purposeful rather than sloppy — it's data about him, not a gap in the writing.

Titles: "hills-and-valleys" is doing exactly what a good title in this book seems to do — sitting innocuously on the surface (it's a bike ride, there are literally hills and valleys) while quietly naming the whole emotional terrain underneath: climbs that take the breath and leave no room for talk (echoing the silence after the bar fight), descents where the talking happens easily, and — reading it against Sheri's blunt anatomical joke — even a wink at the "hell of a backside" line. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it recolors gently in hindsight, once I see the ride's rhythm mirrored in the relationships' rhythm. As for the book and series titles — A Polite Invitation and With a Long Spoon — this chapter is the first time I've felt the "long spoon" idea (something I'm holding loosely from the jacket framing, not from the text itself) apply cleanly to Pace rather than to Randi's calculation: he is now the one at a careful remove from the mess he's made, tending two fires with the same equanimity he brings to a climb.

What I want next: I want to be back with Vee walking up to that door tomorrow night, in the black dress and Randi's shoes and scarf, sure of herself the way Randi coached her to be — and I want to know what she walks into, given what I now know that she doesn't: that Randi's visit is "not finished," that Pace was, four mornings after sleeping with Vee, bragging about her to a cycling friend in the same breath he confirmed he's still seeing someone else. I have no idea whether the book means to let that collide soon or let it simmer longer. Pure guess from here: this feels like it's tightening toward a reveal, not because anything explicit was said, but because the dramatic irony has never been stated this plainly by a character before — Sheri just said the quiet part out loud, even if only to a reader who already knew it.

**Cast present (in person):** Pace, Sheri (new character). **Mentioned only:** Vee, Randi ("the pale sorority chick").

**Heat:** 0 — no sexual content; a cold-morning bike ride and conversation.

**Romance:** 1 — a faint, real warmth in Pace's unguarded enthusiasm about Vee ("he heard himself going and didn't stop"), immediately undercut by the flat "that's not finished" about Randi in the same breath.

**Motifs & images:** the walnut chair / furniture joinery recurs as Pace's care-language (first seen in the house-tour and fitting chapters — here it's Vee's own hands finding it, "the joint he'd spent a weekend on"); the math-as-beauty motif recurs (Vee pulling the math book off the shelf, echoing his fireside speech about math being "the language the world is written in"); cold/breath imagery is a first-sighting variant of the book's recurring cold-as-charged-atmosphere pattern (the truck-cab storm, the fireside glass); "leaving no breath for talk" on climbs faintly echoes the held silences elsewhere in the book (Randi's locked door, the drive home after the bar).

**Symbolism:** the ride's rhythm — talk on flats and descents, forced silence on climbs — reads as an unforced but apt mirror for the relationships: ease when nothing's being asked, silence exactly where the hard work is. "Hell of a backside" sitting next to "two of them at once" plays the physical and the structural against each other in one breath.

**Characterization:** Pace deepens in an uncomfortable direction — this is the first time his interiority reads as complacent/at-ease rather than deliberate and careful, and it's a genuinely new facet rather than a contradiction (he's shown, elsewhere, being scrupulously deliberate only when a woman is actually in front of him). Sheri lands fully formed in very little space — blunt, funny, unsentimental, an effective new "outside eye" device. Vee and Randi don't develop here, but Sheri's flat corroboration of Vee's misery at the bar adds a small, externally-verified data point to Vee's arc.

**Pace — within chapter:** Brisk, matched to the actual rhythm of a ride — short bursts of talk between long silent climbs. Doesn't overstay its welcome; ends the moment it's said what it came to say.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate breather after a dense run of emotionally loaded chapters (the dress, the shoe-shopping, the fireside backstory, the bar blowup, the morning-after repair plan) — momentum holds rather than builds, but it's doing real structural work: delivering, from an outside vantage no in-story POV could supply naturally, confirmation that Pace is still fully with Randi even as Vee prepares to "fix" things with him. Not a lull so much as a change of aperture.
