# Cold read (grounded) — Gone

*scene: scenes/gone.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch023 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one turned my stomach a little, in the good, meant-to way — the way a book earns unease rather than just handing it to you.

The two prior chapters ("Peekaboo," "All Told," "Sorority") had me fully melted. The dress hung in the window catching sun "the way light only comes through silk" — I actually caught my breath at that image, the burgundy lit from inside itself. And the moment she strips off her underwear in front of the mirror and walks to him with the slit falling open — that's the book at its best, desire and self-permission arriving together, no shame-word left standing in the way by the end of it. And the shoe-shopping chapter is just pure delight — Randi doing the "full bigfoot" stomp, the older couple watching, the yeti jokes — I was laughing with them, genuinely charmed, rooting hard for this friendship. Even the credit-card moment, Vee paying more than she has for shoes "owed" to the dress, read to me as devotion rather than folly, though it also quietly flagged the money gap between the two women again, which I noted and let sit.

Then this chapter dropped me straight into Pace and Randi's bed, in the afterglow, and reoriented everything I'd just felt.

**Pace** — I still feel the warmth is real, in the sense that his voice about Vee is unguarded and true to what I've watched him do on the page ("She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter... it never once occurs to her"). That's lovely, and it's clearly sincere fondness, maybe more. But hearing it said *to Randi, in bed, right after sex with Randi, about Vee* recontextualizes every tender thing he's done for Vee as something also being narrated back to a third party. It doesn't feel like betrayal exactly — nothing here reads as him mocking her or degrading her — but it does feel like Vee's most private, unguarded self (the blushing, the "she's ashamed... somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body") is currency being spent between two people who aren't her, in a bed she doesn't know exists. That line lands as insight and as violation at once, and I don't think the book wants me to fully resolve which it is.

**Randi** — this is where the chapter really worked on me and also worried me most. "I didn't think I'd like her this much... She got past me before I could help it" reads as completely genuine — I believe Randi is falling for Vee, maybe already has. The "it's a little pathetic. I love it" line is warm and true. But then Randi describes Vee's body to Pace — "What she keeps under there. It's criminal, Pace" — dragging her own body along his to show him what she means, using her own desire as the vehicle for describing someone else's desirability, to the man who is also sleeping with that someone else. That's the moment the scene tipped from complicated-affection into something that felt genuinely transgressive to me — not shocking for shock's sake, but a real three-way entanglement of desire, envy, and shared ownership of a woman none of the three parties have named honestly to each other. And Randi's stillness afterward — "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding" — followed by the flat, delayed "Yeah... She is" — that's the tell that something in her caught and she covered it, fast, by reaching for sex instead. I don't fully know what that stillness was: jealousy? grief that Vee isn't hers to have this way? recognition of how far in she's gotten? The text doesn't say and I'm not going to pretend it does — I just felt it land as a flinch quickly buried.

**Vee** — not on the page this chapter at all, and that absence is the whole engine of the chapter's discomfort. She's being discussed, described, physically re-enacted between two people in bed, entirely unaware. I ached for her a little reading it, precisely because the two prior chapters had just given me so much of her joy and her interiority, and now I'm watching that same joy get relayed and used as pillow talk. It's the dramatic irony the checkpoint told me to expect, but feeling it in real time — cutting straight from Vee's POV happiness to this — hit harder than knowing about it abstractly.

**Swoon vs. unease**, side by side: the dress and shoe-shopping chapters are pure, unqualified swoon — no shadow needed, they worked entirely straight on me. This chapter is the opposite pole: warm domestic afterglow prose (Sade playing, "the room held the smell of the two of them, close and spent") used to house something that felt quietly wrong the longer I sat in it. I don't think the chapter is cynical about Pace and Randi's bond — his hands on her, her staying loose against him, all of that reads as real tenderness between the two of them too. That's what makes it hard rather than simple: I can't just call this cruelty. It's three real affections tangled with real concealment, and the concealment is doing the damage, not the affections themselves.

**Erotic charge** — moderate, and pointedly not the center. The chapter opens post-coital and the one explicit beat (Randi riding him at the end) is brief, almost an afterthought to the conversation, which is clearly the point — the pillow talk is the real content, the sex is punctuation. It works, but coolly; the charge here is more voyeuristic-uncomfortable than arousing, which I think is intentional.

**Friction** — none that felt like a thumb on the scale. If anything the chapter trusts me a lot: it never explains what Randi's stillness means, never has Pace register any doubt about what he's doing, just lets the domestic warmth and the ethical wrongness sit in the same paragraph without a signpost. That restraint is what makes it effective rather than didactic.

**Title** — "Gone" is doing real work. On the surface it's Randi's own line about Vee being "gone over" Pace — smitten, done for. But it also names what's happening to Randi herself in this scene (she's "gone" for Vee in a way she's clearly trying not to fully say out loud, catching herself and covering with "Yeah... She is"), and it hangs over the whole chapter as a kind of dread — how much further gone can any of these three people get before something breaks. I didn't see it coming; it recolors on the way out rather than tipping the hand going in.

**Book/series titles** — *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* both feel more pointed after this chapter than after the shopping chapters. The "long spoon" idiom (supping with the devil) suddenly feels apt in a way it hadn't quite yet — everyone here is reaching across a table they can't quite see the length of. The jacket's line "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" is exactly what this chapter dramatizes from the other side: Randi and Pace's yeses to each other, wrapped around a woman who hasn't been asked anything.

**What I want next** — I want to see Vee again, urgently, after being shut out of her for a whole chapter — partly just to get back the warmth, partly out of something like protectiveness now. I'm dreading, mildly, the moment any of this surfaces to her, but I'm also genuinely unsure whether the book is heading toward exposure at all, or toward all three of them just going deeper into it together — the checkpoint told me not to expect a rescue, and this chapter is the first one that made me actually feel that possibility rather than just know about it.

STRUCTURED BLOCK

**Cast present (in person):** Pace, Randi. (Vee is mentioned/discussed extensively but not present.)

**Heat:** 1 — mostly afterglow and pillow talk; the explicit beat (Randi riding him) is brief and closes the chapter rather than driving it, and the more charged material is Randi's body-language description of Vee's body, which is heat displaced onto talk rather than shown directly.

**Romance:** 2 — real tenderness between Pace and Randi is on full display (his hands in her hair, her total unguardedness with him, "whatever she ran on out in the world, she set it down at his door"), but it's complicated/shadowed by what the tenderness is actually about, so I'm hesitant to call it a peak.

**Motifs & images:** the frosted-plum polish recurs (from ch15/18/20 and the shoe-shopping chapter, now referenced again as "the blue on her toes" — actually stated as blue here, a possible inconsistency worth flagging, though could be a different detail I'm not certain the checkpoint resolves); "the face" motif implicitly present in Randi's public performance vs. this total unguardedness with Pace ("She talks less here than anywhere... she set it down at his door") — a new wrinkle: Randi guards herself from Vee and the sorority but not from Pace, at least not verbally, even as she catches herself physically at the end; Sade/music as atmosphere, first appearance of this specific texture; the shame-word motif surfaces once more via Pace naming it aloud to Randi ("She's ashamed... Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body") — striking that he's now articulating out loud, to a third party, the exact private shame the reader has watched Vee carry silently for many chapters.

**Symbolism:** the closed bedroom door and the low music "under the door" down the hall reads as an echo of the checkpoint's door motif — a literal door between this scene and the rest of the house, this time sealing in the secret rather than being opened as an invitation.

**Characterization:** Randi deepens most here — her stillness/cover is new, sharper information than anything the checkpoint had about her before ("not the loose stillness of after... the kind that takes holding"), and it complicates rather than resolves my read of her. Pace holds steady — warm, perceptive, articulate about Vee's inner life — but this is the first time that perceptiveness has felt uncomfortable rather than purely admirable, since it's being deployed in the wrong room. Neither character flattens; if anything both got harder to pin down, which felt earned.

**Pace — within the chapter:** unhurried, almost entirely dialogue and interiority, no incident — but it doesn't drag, because the tension is in what's being said and not said, not in event.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** a hard tonal pivot right after two of the warmest, most purely swoony chapters in the run so far — feels deliberate, a gut-punch placement rather than a lull, and it lands harder for the whiplash. Momentum-wise this is clearly building toward something, not holding steady; I feel the book tightening.
