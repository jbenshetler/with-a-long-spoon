## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter is almost pure interior weather with Pace, and it lands soft on me in a way that's a little dangerous, because I keep having to remind myself what I know that Vee doesn't.

Pace, right now, in the sex scene: I believe every beat of it. The rolling to lay his head on her breast, the small unhurried kisses that "aren't about sex for him," the fact that his mouth answers her body's involuntary response with a smile instead of taking anything further from it — this is tenderness I trust on the page. I don't feel manipulated by it as a reader in the moment; it reads like real intimacy, real appetite met with real patience. And yet I can't switch off what the checkpoint gave me: this same unhurried, courtly attentiveness is his mode with Randi too, and Vee thinks it's uniquely hers ("No man had ever wanted her body like this one did"). That line hit me two ways at once — genuine ache for her, because I do think in this exact moment he *does* want her like that — and a small, cold undertow of dramatic irony, because "this one" isn't singular the way she means it. That's the book's whole engine working on me at once, tenderness and unease refusing to cancel each other out.

The photograph is the moment that actually worried me, more than anything overtly sexual in the chapter. Vee's condition — "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone" — and Pace's immediate, warm "Never. I'd never share your photograph" — reads, on its face, as a vow kept in good faith, and I have no textual reason to doubt he means it about the photograph specifically. But I can't unknow that he already tells Randi things about Vee's body, in bed, in explicit enough detail that Randi narrated a nearly identical spanking scene to Vee's face days later without either of them realizing the mirror. The photo itself may stay private. The described version of her — what she looks like undone, what sounds she makes, what she likes — may not be staying anywhere near as private as she thinks she's negotiated. That's a genuinely unsettling gap the text earned, not one I invented; the checkpoint told me this directly (ch. 24, and then structurally confirmed by the practice-room scene), so watching Vee extract a promise that covers the wrong thing entirely made my stomach turn a little, gently, under all the warmth.

Then the dinner-table shift at the end is its own small gut-punch, and I liked it a lot as writing. "This is my home," easy and warm, almost lets her not ask again — and I felt her nearly let it lie, the way you do with someone you love when you sense a door you shouldn't push. When she does push, just slightly ("your parents... do you go back to them?"), his face goes level and exact and he shuts it in four words: "I don't go back." No cruelty in it, no performance — just a wall going up cleanly and precisely, the same wall the checkpoint told me shows up whenever "parents" comes up. It's the first genuinely cold beat Pace has had on the page in this whole read, and it's chilling exactly because of how controlled it is — not an outburst, an edit. I trust Vee's read of it ("she had put her hand on something she wasn't meant to touch") completely; it's exactly how it felt to me too.

And then the small flicker of jealousy about Sheri at his Thanksgiving table — "another woman at his table on a day meant for family" — resolves so easily and kindly ("She's alone") that I believed Vee's relief completely, but I also sat with it a beat longer than she did. I don't suspect anything romantic between Pace and Sheri from what's on the page — the checkpoint frames Sheri as a friend who *knows about both women* and worries for him — but the reader-knowledge that Pace's table already, secretly, seats more than one woman gives that throwaway domestic detail a little extra hum I don't think Vee's meant to hear.

Cassie barely appears (only in the immediately-preceding chapter, not this one) but her presence in memory does its usual work — "Do you love him" landing flat and serious against Vee's "maybe... it's all so fast" — and it primes me to watch Vee's own hedging even as this chapter shows her giving him more and more without hedging at all (the nude photo, the drawer, moving in on weeknights).

**Swoon / pull:** The cooking sequence is genuinely gorgeous and unguarded — I felt the pull of it completely, no irony attached. Watching him build the pan sauce step by step while she narrates the wine going in "enough to make her genuinely sad" is warm, funny, competent-man-porn in the best sense, and her tasting-not-just-eating arc ("she knew exactly whose doing that was") is a lovely, earned image of him having changed her for the better in a totally unthreatening way. I melted there without reservation.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new textual reason to distrust Pace's *feeling* for Vee in this chapter — if anything this is the tenderest, least strategic he's read yet. My suspicion is entirely carried over from what I already know (the Randi entanglement, the promise that covers only the photograph and not the story), not from anything new the page did to earn it fresh. I want to be precise about that: this chapter itself plays completely straight with Vee; my unease is memory, not new evidence.

**Erotic charge:** Working well and warmly — the "again," the phone photo, the "you've used me terribly" line are playful and hot without strain. It goes gently slack (on purpose, pleasantly) into the cooking sequence, which trades heat for a different, food-centered intimacy that's just as charged in its own register.

**Friction:** None that felt like a thumb on the scale — the chapter is unhurried by design and I think it earns its length; nothing dragged.

**Titles:** "In His Hands" is doing quiet double duty — literal (his hands arranging her body for the photo, his hands cooking) and thematic (how much of her, materially and emotionally, is now in his keeping — including a photograph she's trusting him to guard). It doesn't feel like it gave anything away so much as it named, cleanly, what the chapter is actually about once I'd read it. For the book/series titles: *A Polite Invitation* and "with a long spoon" both keep humming under everything here — the courtliness of "may I," the promise-extraction that covers less than it seems to, feel exactly like the long-spoon logic the jacket promised: every yes freely given, every yes also incomplete information.

**What I want next:** I want to see whether the photo, or what's said about her, ever crosses into Randi's hands or knowledge — the checkpoint has already primed me to watch for it. I also want the Thanksgiving beat to pay off; two women alone at one table on a family holiday, in a book already built on doubled arrangements, feels loaded even though nothing on the page confirms anything more than friendship. Guess from here, held loosely.
