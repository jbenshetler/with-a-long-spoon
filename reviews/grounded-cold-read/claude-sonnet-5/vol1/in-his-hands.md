## Reader reaction

Felt read

This chapter is almost entirely Vee and Pace, alone, and it reads like the book taking a long, contented breath. I came in mid-orgasm — "the last of it came up on her without warning" — and the prose stayed physically honest all the way through: the shaking, the "deep worked soreness" she'd be glad of tomorrow, her nipple drawing tight under his mouth with "no say from her." I believed every beat of it. It's warm and unclinical and it never once made me feel like I was watching two bodies instead of two people — the small comic exchange ("Again?" / "Who does this to you when I'm not around?" / "Sometimes I take care of it myself. Thinking of you.") is genuinely charming, and her delighted, proprietary "He's all mine" landed on me exactly as it's meant to. I was swooning, plainly, through the whole first half.

Where it got more interesting than simple swoon was the photograph. Pace arranging her — turning her onto her side, setting her elbow, drawing her hair back, kissing each place his hands had just set — is completely consistent with everything I already know about him: deliberate, exacting, a man who "does it himself," who chooses her phone because it takes the better picture. It's tender on the page and I felt the tenderness. But I also felt, underneath it, the specific unease the checkpoint gave me and that this chapter did nothing to earn fresh — I already know Pace narrates Vee's body and her stories to Randi in bed, in detail, as an ongoing joint project. So when he says "Never. I'd never share your photograph," I don't distrust him about the photograph exactly — I distrust the whole shape of the promise, because I know he's already been sharing her, just not in pixels. That's an unease I'm importing, not one the page manufactured; the scene itself gives no reason to doubt him, and I want to be honest that the discomfort is mine, carried in, not something Pace did here.

The parents beat is the other real moment for me. "I don't go back," said with his voice gone "careful, each word set down clean and apart from the last" — that's the same shutting-door reflex the checkpoint told me about (the earlier "parents" question that closes him off), and I like that the book didn't use this chapter to crack it open. Vee "took her hand off it," and I respected that in her — it's a small sign of how far she's come from the Rusty Nail blowup, where she pushed exactly this kind of raw nerve in public. Her jealous flicker about Sheri, immediately self-caught and let go, is the same maturing instinct. I liked Vee a great deal more in this chapter for those two small restraints, on top of already liking her as a woman starved for exactly this kind of attention.

The cooking sequence in the back half is gorgeous and unhurried — the pan's "talk," the lemon zest, the wine "the pan needed... worse than she did" — and it's doing real thematic work, not just filling time: her line about tasting instead of just eating, "there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was," is the food/appetite motif I've been tracking since the apple in chapter one, and it lands as growth, not just cute domesticity. If I'm honest, the sequence runs a little long for how little happens in it, but it's sensuous enough, and thematically loaded enough, that I didn't mind sitting in it.

Randi never appears and isn't mentioned even once — which, after several chapters where she was woven through everything (even "One Bite," which is a Vee/Randi scene start to finish), gave this chapter a strange, almost too-clean quiet. I noticed myself waiting for her to surface somewhere, the way you keep expecting a phone to buzz. Nothing wrong with that as a choice — it let this chapter be pure and undivided — but structurally I feel the accumulating weight of several bliss-chapters in a row (this one, "Above Him," "School Nights") without the other thread moving, and I'm hungry for it to reassert itself.

Trust vs. suspicion: no suspicion of Vee, none of Sheri (still just a warm offstage name). My suspicion of Pace is exactly where the checkpoint left it — not deepened by anything new here, but not relieved either, since a chapter this tender and un-self-conscious about "sharing" is precisely the kind of chapter that makes the reader's private knowledge sting a little more by contrast.

Erotic charge: strongest in the opening page and in the photo-taking (there's real heat in being composed and looked at, not just touched), goes warm-but-non-erotic through the cooking, and I don't think it goes slack anywhere — it just changes registers on purpose.

Friction: very little. The line "which rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end" is a small wink of authorial cleverness that felt slightly more knowing than Vee's usual voice, but it's minor and passed quickly.

Titles: "In His Hands" is doing a lot of quiet work — literally his hands arranging her, cooking, in her hair, and then, once I think about it, everything else that's "in his hands" now: her trust, her image (a literal photograph, sent and gone, "Delivered"), her heart. It reads sweet on the surface and a little more loaded in retrospect, given what I already carry about him — not a giveaway, but a title I'll keep turning over. I wasn't given a jacket blurb or tagline in this packet (only the chapter title up top), so I have nothing to react to on the book/series title front this round — just this chapter's own title, which is the one signal I actually have.

What I want next: I want Randi back on the page — not because this chapter needed her, but because the accumulating Vee/Pace bliss is starting to feel like the calm before something, and I trust the book enough by now to believe that's deliberate. I'm also still holding the open questions from before — the parents, the scar (absent here), Daphne — none resolved, none pushed forward, which is fine; I don't need them rushed. Guess from here, clearly marked as a guess: the photograph feels like exactly the kind of object this book plants and later returns to — I have no evidence it will be misused, only that the book has trained me to notice things given in trust and then watch what happens to them.
