# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch007 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Okay, this one got me and unsettled me in almost exactly equal measure, and the two feelings never quite separate — which I think is the point.

**On the characters, and how my feeling has moved.** Vee is the one I'm most given over to right now. Watching her arc from the coffee shop through the froyo date to this dinner — the hunger that keeps surfacing as an actual, literal, permission-starved thing ("She was suddenly, enormously hungry," "the hollow only opened wider, asked for more before she'd swallowed") — has real tenderness in it, and it lands because the book earned it in small increments: the too-big froyo cup she doesn't correct, the swimsuit that "gives her nowhere to hide," the "floozies" verdict that arrives "whole and in her mother's voice" before she's even finished forming the thought. She is a person who has been taught to ration herself, in food and in wanting and in how much of a sentence she's allowed to take up, and this chapter is the first time I've watched her let the ration go — not eating, "not for once anywhere except exactly where she was." That's lovely and I felt it in my chest.

Pace, though, is where my feeling has genuinely moved, and not entirely in his favor. I liked him uncomplicatedly in the froyo/Substitution stretch — patient, funny about frozen yogurt architecture, the tutor who hands the pencil back instead of solving the problem himself. Here, courting Vee properly — the shower, the shave, the "May I choose?", the deliberate seating, the same exacting attentiveness to her that I watched him give Randi with the apple slices (always the right hand tearing the bread, "some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it") — I feel the same warmth and, underneath it, a colder recognition: this is the same man who, chapters ago, stood at a lamppost and watched his girlfriend collide on purpose with this exact woman, engineered as a shared appetite between the two of them. Vee doesn't know any of that. I do. That knowledge doesn't make Pace's tenderness toward her read as fake — nothing in the prose suggests he's performing with her — but it makes every gentle, exact thing he does for her land differently for me than it would if I'd opened this chapter cold. The "May I" that melts Vee is the same formal grace I watched him extend to Randi on the bench ("Tell me your word," "Take your time"). It's real, and it's also a pattern, and Vee has no idea she's the newest instance of it.

Cassie continues to be the person I trust most without qualification. "That's not a boy. That's a man" lands with real weight precisely because Cassie doesn't tease Vee about this — the withholding of her usual dryness is the tell. And her earlier "Goodbye, Vee" (from Substitution) is sitting in the back of my mind now like a held note; I don't know what it means yet and I'm not going to decide for the text, but it's there.

Randi isn't on the page in this chapter at all — not even mentioned — and that absence is doing real work on me. She's the ghost hanging over the whole dinner. I keep waiting for her name to surface and it doesn't, and the not-surfacing is its own kind of dread.

**Swoon / pull.** The dinner itself worked on me completely, on its own terms, before I let the dramatic irony back in. "May I choose?" is a perfect small moment — the relief of surrender, the specificity of him ordering deliberately rather than performing generosity. The food writing is sensuous without being showy — "each soft sound a closed door with something behind it," the bread torn "with care for the heat." And the ending: "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly, and was only now where it belonged." That's a swoon-line, full stop, and I let myself have it even while some other part of me was flinching. The "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" / "But I'm making an exception" beat is a lovely, earned reversal — her own rule catching up to her a beat too late, exactly as it's described.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I have no suspicion of Cassie or of Vee. My suspicion of Pace isn't that he's lying to Vee in this scene — nothing on the page supports that, he's patient and attentive and doesn't push past her stated line — it's structural: I know something material that Vee doesn't (that he's involved with Randi, and that the two of them chose her together, deliberately, as an object of shared desire), and the chapter never once gestures at that gap closing. That's not the text failing to earn my unease; it's the text withholding on purpose, and I feel the withholding as suspense rather than as a cheat.

**Erotic charge.** Low by the numbers next to the Bench, but working in a different register — all anticipation, no release. The near-kiss, the walk, the held hand as "too much for what it was." It peaks exactly where the chapter wants it to: the last line. Nothing here goes slack; it's paced like a held breath the whole way through, on purpose, matching Pace's own patience.

**Friction.** One small thing: "It was the old reflex, the part of her that always caught the thread before it could drop, that knew where the lines were and stayed inside them... It came back now, here, at the worst possible moment, with its rule already in her mouth." This is a bit more analytical/telling than the rest of the chapter's texture, which mostly shows Vee's interiority through sensation rather than naming the mechanism. It's a small hitch, not a real complaint — I noticed the authorial hand there for a second.

**Titles.** "May I Choose" is doing exactly what I now expect these titles to do — an innocuous surface (a man asking permission to order dinner) that reread-charges the moment I hold it against the Pointing Game a few chapters back, where choosing was the entire structure of an evening and Vee was the thing chosen, without her knowledge, by two people instead of one. On first pass it's just a nice, courteous line. On the pass I can't stop myself from taking, it's asking a much larger and more uncomfortable question about who gets to choose in this book, and whether Vee will ever get to choose with full information. I don't know if that's where the book is going — flagging it as my read, not a prediction. As for the volume/series titles — *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — this chapter is the most "polite invitation" has felt yet: a genuinely courteous, careful seduction, "May I," "may I call you," dressed for it, nothing forced. And the long-spoon idea (which I only have as a title, not an explained image) now reads to me, for the first time, like it might be about proximity to something that will burn you if you're not careful how you reach — which fits a triangle none of the three people in it have named yet.

**What I want next.** I want to know whether Randi knows this dinner is happening — whether this is the threesome plan proceeding exactly as the two of them intended, or whether Pace has quietly stepped outside of it into something that's only his. I want Vee to find out what I already know, and I'm dreading it a little and also want it, because right now she's happy in a way that's only possible because she doesn't have the full picture, and I don't trust that happiness to last. Guess from here, nothing more: I suspect the "pointing game" isn't over, and that whatever comes next puts Vee in a room she didn't know she was walking into.
