## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this chapter still warm from Famished — from that long, slow-built first time, the shame that ran the whole length of her pleasure and never closed her — so Toenails lands as the exhale after. It's a short, quiet chapter, almost a coda, and it works on me differently than the sex scenes have: it's not the heat, it's the morning-after texture of what happens to a girl who's just fallen off the edge of herself. I feel tender toward Vee here in a new register — not the swoony, blushing tenderness of the fitting or the hungry ache of Famished, but something closer to protective. She drove over with no bag because packing one "would have been to say the thing to herself, plainly," and that's such an exact, familiar piece of self-deception that I felt it land in my own chest. She's lying to herself in the smallest, most human way — not about whether she wants this, but about admitting she planned for it.

Cassie is, as always, the character I trust completely and without qualification, and this chapter deepens that trust rather than just confirming it. "That good?" and "Was he worth painting your toenails? In October." — she reads Vee instantly and correctly and doesn't push. And then that one unsettling beat: "for half a second something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name." I don't know what that is. I've been carrying this flicker across several chapters now (the checkpoint told me Cassie has had "something unspoken" pass over her face around Randi and Vee before), and it still hasn't resolved into anything — worry, jealousy, grief, wariness about Pace specifically — and I like that the book won't hand it to me. I'm not going to guess what it is; I'm just noting that it recurred and it's accumulating weight by not being explained.

The scene that actually got me was the small one at the end — Vee reaching for her zipper to shower, remembering she gave her underwear away, and then the moment of freezing: "three feet from Cassie, the zipper half down, and the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." That's a gorgeous, quietly devastating image of the gap between what you can tell and what you can't, dramatized literally as clothing. She zips the skirt back up in front of her best friend rather than let the evidence — the actual missing underwear — become undeniable. It's not shame exactly; it's more like she's protecting the story she's decided to tell, protecting the "kindness" of the edit. I don't fault her for it, but I feel the loneliness of it. She's happiest she's ever been and also, for the first time in the book, keeping something from Cassie — the one relationship that's been unimplicated and safe.

I don't have new suspicion of Pace or Randi from this chapter specifically — they're both offstage, only present as afterimage — but the dramatic irony that's been building since the checkpoint is still humming under everything. Vee tells Cassie "the parts that made a story" and keeps "the middle," and I keep thinking about how she's doing, in miniature and innocently, exactly what Randi and Pace have been doing to her at scale: curating a version of events for someone she loves, believing the omission is kindness. That parallel wasn't stated on the page, but the ladle image — "the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole" — made me sit with it. I'm holding that as my own noticing, not something the text argued.

Erotic charge: this chapter has almost none, and that's clearly the right call after the previous one's sustained heat — it would have been exhausting and cheapened Famished to keep pushing. The one charged beat is retrospective and physical rather than desiring: "no bag... woken in his bed with her clothes on the floor... And the night ran under all of it, a second channel that had not shut off once." That's doing afterglow, not seduction, and it works — I feel her body still humming rather than being asked to hum myself.

Trust vs. suspicion: no new grounds for suspicion of anyone here. My only lingering unease is the same one I've been carrying — Randi and Pace's engineered origin story sitting underneath Vee's total, unguarded joy — but nothing in this specific chapter deepened or resolved it. Cassie remains the one person I don't doubt at all, though that flicker of unnamed feeling on her face keeps quietly accumulating.

Friction: very little. This is a tight, controlled short chapter and it knows exactly what it's doing. If I look for a wobble, it's that "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend" reads a hair pat as a line — Cassie being "kindly, certainly, wrong" is a nice authorial wink (we know exactly what Vee actually forgot), but it toes right up against telling me the joke instead of trusting me to already have it. It didn't bother me much because the payoff (the zipper, the panties) does the real work a beat later.

Titles: "Toenails" is a lovely small-object title in the vein of this book's other quiet-noun titles (Rye, Two Towels) — it points at the frosted plum polish she painted in anticipation, "one knee drawn up... early enough they'd be dry and perfect," from the previous chapter, and by the end of this one it's landed as evidence: "frosted-plum toenails her mother would have had a word for." It's the one visible, undeniable trace of the whole engineered evening that survives into the morning-after — she gave away the underwear, but the toenails are still there, still hers, still proof. I liked catching that on the second pass — the title isn't a spoiler, it's a small closing rhyme. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the series title, *With a Long Spoon* (that proverb about needing a long spoon to sup with the devil), keep humming under all of this sweetness for me — I still can't unhear it, even in a chapter this gentle and joyful. Nothing devilish happened on the page here, but the title sits in the back of my mind like weather.

What I want next: I want to know what Cassie's unnamed flicker actually is — whether it ever gets a name, or whether the book is content to let it stay a texture in her face forever. I'm also curious whether Vee's habit of editing what she tells the people she loves becomes a thread of its own, now that it's been planted twice (Randi at lunch, Cassie here). And, underneath everything, I'm still waiting — with real unease, not dread exactly — for the moment Vee's version of events collides with what the reader already knows. Guess from here: I don't think that collision is imminent; the book seems more interested right now in how deep it can let her joy go before anything cracks it.
