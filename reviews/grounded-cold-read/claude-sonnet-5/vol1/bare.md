## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one hurt in a quiet register, and it hurt precisely because nothing happens in it — no fight, no scene, not even a phone call. Just Vee, awake, alone, litigating an ache with herself in the dark while Cassie breathes on undisturbed below her. I came into this chapter still carrying the ring of "Under the Rug" — the sharp thing she threw at Chili's, the apology-sex, the "he'd given her all of it and not the one thing she'd been asking for" — and "Bare" doesn't resolve any of that. It just lets her sit in it three nights on, alone, with nobody to blame and nothing to fight. That's a harder thing to read than the fight was.

Vee is the only person really *on the page* here, and my feeling for her deepened rather than shifted — the same tenderness-shot-with-worry I've had for chapters now, but sharpened by how unsparing she is with herself in this one. The line that got me was the shirt trick failing: "It was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." That's devastating and exact — the comfort object she's leaned on since the mountain hike (his shirt, her scent-totem this whole book) finally exposed as a closed loop, her own body heat mistaken for his presence. And then the choice she makes with that knowledge — she doesn't put the shirt back on and lie to herself, she takes it *and the one underneath* off and lies bare in a cold room rather than keep faking the comfort. "It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." That's not passive suffering, that's a kind of grim, deliberate self-honesty, and it made me respect her even while it broke my heart a little. She's choosing the true cold over the false warm. That's real growth even though it looks like nothing is happening.

Pace isn't in this chapter at all, and his absence is the whole point of it, which is its own kind of unsettling. Vee pictures him "across town in his own bed, gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him" — and I sat with that sentence longer than I meant to, because I know things she doesn't: the scar he won't explain, the parents he won't visit, Randi, the game. Is he actually sleeping easy, untroubled, a man with nothing grinding in him? Or is Vee just assuming that because that's what his surface always shows her? The text doesn't resolve it and I won't resolve it for it — but the dramatic irony I'm carrying makes that line land heavier on me than it would on a reader without the checkpoint. I want to be honest, though: this chapter itself gives me no new evidence against him. It's not that anything on this page indicts Pace — it's that my accumulated unease supplies the shadow, and the chapter is smart enough to let that shadow fall without asking for it directly.

Cassie gets maybe fifteen words of actual presence — asleep, breathing "slow and easy" below her — but the aside about the shirt ("worn like that for the hall... a girl too far gone in a boy") lands as its own small ache. It implies Cassie has already clocked exactly what's happening to Vee and has decided, out of love, not to say anything. Given the checkpoint's note about Cassie's own unresolved, unspoken feelings, that tact reads less neutral to me now than it might have earlier — it could be simple loyalty, or it could be something with more cost to Cassie specifically. I genuinely don't know which, and the chapter doesn't tell me, so I'm leaving it exactly that open.

Swoon and heat: there isn't much of either here, and I don't think the chapter wants there to be. The nudity at the end isn't erotic — it's the opposite, a stripping of comfort rather than a baring for pleasure. "The cold of the room came down on her bare skin all at once" reads as punishment she's choosing to sit in, not desire. So if I'm honest about where the erotic charge is, it's essentially zero in this chapter — which is fine; not every chapter needs to run hot, and this one earns its coldness.

Where I landed emotionally is closest to grief — not for anything that's ended, but for the specific loneliness of loving someone who is, by every measure she can point to, wonderful to her, and still not having the word. "She had no name for the best thing in her life, not the big one he wouldn't give her and not the small one she couldn't find" — that sentence (carried over almost verbatim from the previous chapter) now gets its full weight in "Bare," because this time she's alone with it instead of in his arms.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new to distrust on the page — no lie caught, no tell noticed. What's there is the accumulated weight of what I already know from the checkpoint pressing against Vee's generous read of his ease. I don't think the text is trying to indict him here; it's using her isolation to let my prior knowledge do quiet work in the background.

The title: "Bare" is about as unshowy and exact a title as this book has given me — she takes the shirt off, twice over, and lies bare in a cold room, and it's also the state of her: stripped of the illusion the shirt gave her, stripped of the forgiveness she'd draped over herself two nights before, left with just the fact of the missing word. It didn't give anything away before I read it — if anything it undersold how purely interior and how sad the chapter would be. As for the series and volume titles — I wasn't given a jacket or tagline in this packet to react to directly, so I'll just say plainly: nothing in this chapter touches the "invitation" or the "long spoon" machinery on the page (no Randi, no game), but the ache Vee's sitting in — a name she can't get, a warmth that turns out to be her own — feels like it belongs to the same architecture the checkpoint's already shown me: things withheld, things not quite what they present as.

What I want next: I want to know whether this insomniac night changes anything when morning comes, or whether — like the dryer-cycle realization after "Seconds" — she folds it up and carries it back down the hall unspoken. Given the pattern so far (three chapters running on this exact ache, each one a little worse), I'd guess (guess from here, no more) that she isn't going to say anything to him either — that the pattern holds and the not-saying keeps compounding. I also find myself wanting Cassie to actually say something at some point, given how visible this is getting to her — though I have zero evidence that will happen.
