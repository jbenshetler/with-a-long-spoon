# Cold read (grounded) — Sorority

*scene: scenes/sorority.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch022 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one's lighter on its feet than almost anything I've read of theirs so far, and I let myself enjoy it almost without guard — which, given everything I already know about Randi, is its own small confession.

**Vee** is glowing in a way that's just plain lovable here — carrying the dress into the dorm to be *seen* rather than just shown ("the wanting got there before the plan, the wanting to be seen in it as she'd been seen in his mirror"), laughing herself onto a shoe-store bench, cracking the yeti joke. I like her a lot in this chapter; she's funny, she's generous, she's not just the ingénue being worked on — she gives as good as she gets in the joke war. But I also felt the floor tilt under her twice: once literally (Randi's thumb on her toe, "felt the floor of her tip"), and once financially, at the counter, swapping the debit for the credit card and "letting none of it reach her face, because the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch." That line stopped me. That's Randi's *face* — the mask the checkpoint told me Randi decided, alone, in a bathroom, she would wear from now on and never let anyone see under — showing up, uninvited, on Vee's face instead. I don't think Vee knows she's doing it. I noticed it because the book taught me to notice it, and it gave me a small, cold shiver in the middle of an otherwise warm scene.

**Randi** is at her most purely charming here, and also — to me — at her most quietly alarming, precisely because nothing overtly manipulative happens. There's no extraction-lunch, no leading question mining Vee for detail. It's just affection, touch, generosity, jokes. And that's what makes me trust her *less*, not more, right now: I know from the checkpoint that her warmth toward Vee is real and also strategic, and this chapter gives me no new data to resolve that — it just deepens the intimacy while the ambiguity stays exactly where it was. The foot-holding moment in the boutique is the one place the mask (hers, not Vee's) seems to slip open a crack: "Mm. That was all. A thumb moved once across the nail of the big toe, light—" and then she says nothing, just smiles and bends back to the box. That's Randi wanting something and not naming it, in front of me and not in front of Vee. I don't distrust her feeling. I distrust what she'll do with it, and what she's already doing with Vee's trust without either of them naming it.

**Swoon / pull.** The comedy in the shoe store completely worked on me — the yeti shoe, the rhinestone platforms ("Do these go with burgundy?"), Randi folded over the bench laughing "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give." That's the first time in a while the book let these two just be delighted by each other with nothing underneath it (or nothing I could see), and I was delighted right along with them. And "He didn't make this for you. He made it to you" — that line landed on me the way it landed on Vee. It's exactly right, it names something true about Pace's craft-as-devotion that the earlier fitting/dress chapters had been circling, and Randi is the one sharp enough to say it in five words. I felt the swoon and I felt slightly used by it in the same breath, because I know why Randi is so good at reading what a gesture means — she's spent chapters mining meaning out of things for a living, in a sense.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new evidence that anyone's actively scheming in this chapter — it reads, on its face, as two friends having a joyful, extravagant afternoon. My unease is entirely carried over from what I already know (the pointing game, the engineered meeting, Randi's private resolve to withhold "herself" while giving "the face"), not from anything new the page did. I want to be honest that the text itself doesn't push me toward suspicion here — it's my memory doing that work. What the text *did* earn, on its own, is the money asymmetry: Randi's blithe re-buying of her own shoes mid-afternoon "like shoes were a thing you simply traded mid-afternoon when you happened past better ones," set right next to Vee going into debt for a pair of straps she can't really afford. That's not manipulation, but it's a real fault line the chapter opened without commentary, and I feel it as an ache on Vee's behalf, not a plot alarm.

**Erotic charge.** Low but present, and it peaks in exactly one place — the foot in Randi's hands, the thumb on the polish, "felt it go all the way up the length of her." Everything else is warm rather than charged: the dress unveiling is more devotional/joyful than erotic (that heat already got spent last chapter, in "Peekaboo"). The touch of silk between Randi's fingers, "Charmeuse... And a good weight," reads sensuous but appraising, almost proprietary — she's reading the dress the way she reads people.

**Friction as a reader.** None that felt like the author's thumb on the scale. If anything I want to flag it as the opposite of friction — the shoe-store comedy earned its length; I'd have read three more minutes of it. The one place I noticed the machinery slightly is the sheer efficiency of "Same feet. Same size, same everything... it's practically a marriage" landing right before the boutique — it's a very clean pivot device to get them into the intimate scene, but it didn't feel forced, just tidy.

**Titles.** "Sorority" puzzles me a little in the best way — there's no actual sorority scene here, no sisters, no chapter house. What I have instead is two women in an ad hoc, private sisterhood of their own making — the joke about "practically a marriage," Vee's closing thought that she's "never once in her life had a friend like this." I think the title is pointing at *that* — an invented, intimate sorority of two, standing next to (and maybe quietly displacing) the real one Randi already belongs to and that the checkpoint told me watches her carefully. It didn't spoil anything for me; it just sat there making me ask the question rather than answering it. As for the book's title and tagline — I wasn't given a tagline in this packet, so I can't react to one; *A Polite Invitation* still feels, this chapter, like it's naming Vee's whole experience of being drawn deeper in by people being generous to her — the shoes, the dress, the friendship — before she's been told there's a cost or a design behind any of it.

**What I want next.** I want to keep watching that face Vee just borrowed without knowing it — whether it becomes a habit, whether Cassie (who is nowhere in this chapter except as an empty, too-tidy bunk) notices it when Vee comes home with a credit-card dress code she can't really afford. And I'm bracing, without evidence yet, for the money thread to snap taut later — a guess from here, not a prediction the text has earned.
