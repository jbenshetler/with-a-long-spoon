# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this chapter still raw from the run of recent chapters — the "I love how good we are together" gut-punch, the Chili's disaster, "Believe Me," the two lonely New Year's Eves running in parallel (Randi's numb "fairytale" with John, Vee's cold twin bed under the swim trophies). So when this chapter opens with Vee crying the whole way home from the airport because "every mile of it ran toward a town that had Pace in it and not one inch of him for her," it landed exactly where it should — I ached for her immediately. The stripped side of the room, Cassie gone, "the empty of it met Vee at the door like more of the same" — that's about as bleak as this book has let her get, and I felt it.

Then Randi. This is the chapter that moved my feeling about her the most since the checkpoint. The reunion itself swept me completely — the sprint down the stairs, hustling Vee up and away from Brooke's practiced warmth, the door shutting, and then the hug that goes "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that, and neither of them let go." I felt that one in my chest. The detail of Vee breathing in Randi's actual smell — "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" — and it steadying her, wanting more of it, not less — that's real intimacy, not performance, and it worked on me completely as romance even though nothing sexual happens. Randi sweeping her own unpacked Gstaad haul off the bed without a glance, because the wrecked girl in the doorway matters more than any of it — I believed that instantly, no hedge.

And then the counsel itself is *good*. Genuinely good. "Words are cheap" thrown back at Vee's situation — Randi reads Pace's silence not as indifference but as a man "holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him," and having just come out of "The Usual," where we watched Pace tell Sheri almost exactly that ("I can't"... "not saying it matters more to you than having her"), I know Randi is dead right. That's a strange, double-edged pleasure as a reader — trusting the diagnosis because we've been shown the evidence Vee hasn't, while also quietly wondering *how* Randi is this precise about a man Vee doesn't know she shares. Nothing on the page confirms Randi has talked to Pace about this — I want to be careful not to invent that — but the accuracy sat oddly with me in a way the earlier chapters trained me to notice. It's a suspicion the text itself earned by being this good a read, not one I'm importing.

The Sheri material broke my heart a little differently — Vee has built a jealous fantasy (blonde, tan, model-built) entirely from fear, and we know, because we were just at that diner table, that the real Sheri is nothing like that and nothing is going on. Randi's response — "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one... that doesn't make her true" — is compassionate and correct, and I felt for Vee being managed out of a fear that (unbeknownst to either of them, as far as the page shows) really was groundless. That's dramatic irony working in Vee's favor for once, almost sweetly.

Where it turned harder for me was the coat. Randi walking Vee — a woman raised on "floozie," on food-policed shame — toward showing up bare under a coat, letting her hit the wall of "that's such a slutty thing to do" and then talking her down off it word by word into "that's the bravest thing a woman can do." I don't disbelieve Randi's tenderness in the room. But I clocked the machinery: this is the second time now (per what I remember — the borrowed heels and scarf after the bar fight) that Randi has literally dressed Vee for a reconciliation with Pace, handed her the shoes, staged the body as the apology instead of words. It's generous and it's controlling at once, and the chapter doesn't ask me to pick — it just shows Randi being unmistakably right about Pace and unmistakably the one deciding, again, what Vee's body will do to fix it.

Then the Gstaad retelling gutted me a little, because I already have the real version. We watched Randi feel nothing with John, watched her call it "a good sandwich eaten fast," watched her decide the flaw was hers to carry back out of that hotel room cold. Now she hands Vee the same story with every true thing filed off — the marble floor, the silver trees, the man who danced like nothing, the dress "the kind you tell your daughter about" — and lets Vee envy exactly the wrong thing: the ease, the fairy-tale forward motion, "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it," which Vee reads as the thing Pace withholds from her. I know, and Vee doesn't, that this ease was hollow and John never reached her at all. The only crack Randi lets show is the joke — "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — which reads to me now as almost the truest sentence in the whole recap, buried in a punchline. That's Randi's whole method in miniature: give the funny, true-shaped line and let it stand in for the thing she won't actually say.

**Swoon / pull:** The hug. Unambiguously. And Vee finally getting to laugh — "really laughed for the first time all day" — at the bedroom joke. Those two beats are where the chapter had me completely.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion of malice toward Vee from Randi — if anything her counsel here reads as caring and correct. But my checkpoint-level unease about the shared plan hums under this scene in a way I can't quite dismiss: Randi's read of Pace is too exact for a woman only hearing about him secondhand, and the wardrobe-staging (heels again) is a repeated device I now recognize as *hers*, not incidental kindness. I'm holding that open rather than deciding it.

**Erotic charge:** Low-key but present — heat 1 at most, all in the hug and the coat-and-nothing-under exchange, nothing explicit on the page. The charge is more romantic than sexual: the held embrace, the goodbye kiss now "unremarkable... only what they did," which is its own small devastation dressed as routine.

**Friction:** None that snagged me. The device of Randi finishing a sentence and making Vee say the ending herself ("You've got that good long coat" / "...And nothing under it") is a clean, earned bit of craft, not a thumb on the scale — it makes Vee complicit in her own shame-and-want rather than lectured into it.

**Titles:** "My Friend Randi" reads almost too plain against what's actually on the page — the clinging hug, the kiss, the wardrobe control, Randi's ache disguised as a fairytale. Said in Vee's own voice it would sound like devotion; from outside it, after everything the checkpoint has told me, it sounds like the one word doing the most work in the whole book is "friend." It doesn't give anything away so much as it quietly understates everything, which after the fact felt sharper than a more pointed title would have. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* still feel exactly on theme here — an invitation extended with impeccable manners (the coat, the heels, "no words, no fight") that is also, underneath, an exercise of will on someone else's body and heart.

**What I want next:** I want to be in the room — or explicitly not — when Vee shows up at Pace's door tonight. I want to know whether his "not saying it matters more to you than having her" (Sheri's diagnosis, which the reader has and Vee doesn't) actually breaks this time, or whether the coat buys another silence. Guess from here: given how thoroughly the last few chapters built toward the word he won't say, I don't expect him to say it even now — I expect the body to answer again where the sentence won't.
