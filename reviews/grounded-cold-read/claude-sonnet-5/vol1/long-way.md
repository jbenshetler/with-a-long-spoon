# Cold read (grounded) — The Long Way

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter carrying a lot from the run before it — the bench, the confession in the bathroom mirror, Randi sealing herself back behind her face, the sorority interrogation she slid out of by inches, and then the pointing game that turned into something startlingly tender when Vee walked out of the dining hall laughing with her head thrown back. By the time "The Long Way" opens, I already have more information than Vee does, and that gap is doing almost all the work on me emotionally.

Vee is easy to love on the page. Her interiority is warm and specific — the "is he desperate" deflection because it's "easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting," the froyo cup getting away from her and her not caring, the confession "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo," followed immediately by her own wince and self-correction. That's a person I recognize: someone whose hunger (for attention, for being chosen, for permission to want things without a policing voice attached) has been rationed her whole life, suddenly getting to spend a little of it. I felt real tenderness toward her, and real worry for her in the same breath, because I know things about the man walking beside her that she doesn't.

Pace, in this chapter alone, is lovely — unhurried, curious, listening "like the sentence had been worth waiting out," not deflecting when she hands him something true and a little embarrassing. It's consistent with the man from the bench and from the tutoring scene: he doesn't perform, he attends. The "may I call you," the paying for both cups before she found her wallet, the callback at the very end — "Your tart's holding up" — landed on me exactly the way it's built to land, as a small act of noticing that answers her admission without making a scene of it. I felt the swoon. I let myself have it. And then, a half-beat later, I felt the other thing: I cannot un-know that this same man built a bench for his girlfriend two chapters ago, that he ran a whole afternoon experiment in which he pointed at strangers on a quad and asked Randi "what about her," that Vee is, structurally, the answer to that game. The tenderness is real on the page — nothing in his behavior with Vee reads as performed or cynical — but I don't trust the shape of how she got here, and the chapter doesn't ask me to stop distrusting it.

Randi is the one I feel most unsettled about, precisely because this chapter gives me only her surface — warm, funny, egging Vee on ("You should go," "he wants to see you naked," "you're not unsure and we all know it"). After the bathroom mirror scene, I know that surface can be load-bearing armor. I don't know whether her enthusiasm here is genuine appetite for the thing she asked for in bed, a performance to keep herself in control of what's happening, or some mix she hasn't sorted out herself — and the text is honestly agnostic about it, so I'm holding it open rather than deciding for her.

Cassie is quietly becoming my compass. She doesn't get swept up in anything — "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" lands as real affection without illusion, and her flat "Goodbye, Vee" from the previous chapter is still sitting in me, a small wrongness I haven't resolved. I trust her eye more than anyone's account, including the narration's, when it comes to what's actually happening between these people.

Erotic charge: essentially none in this chapter, and I don't miss it. This is a chaste, deliberately gentle scene, and it works as tenderness rather than heat — the charge is emotional, in the waiting-out of a sentence, in a boy paying attention to a girl's shame about a dripping cone. That's the right register for what the chapter is doing.

Friction: none that felt like the author's thumb on the scale. If anything I want more — I want to know what Randi is actually feeling under the "he wants to see you naked" banter, and the chapter is right not to give it to me yet, in Vee's POV. That withholding felt earned, not evasive.

The title, "The Long Way" — it's right there in the text, "they walked the long way, not toward anything," so it isn't a puzzle so much as a description of the chapter's pace: no rush, no destination named. Read against Pace's established patience (with Randi on the bench, with Vee's math), it fits him as a person — he takes the long way with everyone. But knowing what I know about how Vee arrived at this froyo shop in the first place — a pointing game, an engineered meeting at exactly the coffee shop where she happened to be stuck on a problem — "the long way" also sits oddly against how efficiently this was actually arranged. I don't think the title gave anything away so much as it let me feel, freshly, the gap between how slow and organic this feels to Vee and how much design I already know sits underneath it.

On the book/series titles: I wasn't given a tagline in this packet, so I have nothing to react to there directly. "A Polite Invitation" as a volume title does land newly for me after this chapter, though — an invitation is exactly what's been extended to Vee, repeatedly, by people who are considerably more polite about it than forthcoming. I don't know yet whether that's ominous or just accurate, and I'm content to let it sit unresolved.

What I want next: I want to know what happens when — or if — Vee finds out that Randi and Pace are already something to each other. I'm dreading that reveal a little, for Vee's sake, and I'm curious whether Randi's warmth here is going to curdle or hold once the game stops being hypothetical. Pure guess from here, not something the text has told me.
