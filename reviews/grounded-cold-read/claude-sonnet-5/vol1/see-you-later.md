# Cold read (grounded) — See You Later

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I read this chapter with my stomach in a knot the whole time, because I know something Vee doesn't, and the chapter is built entirely to make me feel that gap. We just watched Pace point at her across a quad like she was a menu item, watched Randi's whole body answer "yes" before her mouth caught up, watched the two of them stage a "collision" so clean it "looked like nothing." And now here's Vee, radiant and grateful, believing she has been noticed by chance and chosen for herself. Every warm thing Randi does in this chapter — the arm squeeze, "I'm going to copy off you all semester," the extravagant "I've decided" about coffee, the hug on the sidewalk — I can no longer take at face value, and it hurts to watch Vee take it at absolute face value, because she has no reason not to. That's the particular ache of this book: nothing here is a lie in the crude sense (Randi really does seem to be enjoying Vee, really is warm), but I know the warmth was manufactured to land exactly like this, and Vee is walking straight into a room whose furniture was arranged for her.

Vee herself is enormously sympathetic to me here — maybe more than in any chapter so far because we're finally, fully inside her ordinary hunger rather than her sexual awakening. The "not looking at the door" bit, the three minutes of performed indifference, the "I made myself wait one full beat so it would not come out the size it actually was" — that is such a precise, humiliating, universal piece of social self-management that I felt it in my own chest. She's a smart, funny, watchful girl (I love her deadpan gift for Cassie — "Cassie says about four words a day... You'll learn to read them like tea leaves" is genuinely charming, warm, generous toward her friend even while performing for a new audience) who is starving for exactly this kind of attention and has been trained her whole life, by her mother's voice living in her head, to distrust her own wanting even as she reaches for it. The Chi Latte scene — reading the room, cataloguing the other girls' bodies with that ugly little word "floozies" arriving in her mother's voice, and then immediately undercutting it with her own quieter, more honest assessment ("she had more than they had") — is such exact, unflattering, recognizable interior work. This is a young woman constantly grading herself and everyone around her against a scale she didn't choose and can't put down, and the chapter lets me feel both her hunger and her shame about the hunger without resolving either.

Randi is where my suspicion sharpens rather than eases. I liked her charm in this chapter — genuinely, she's funny, the "yes, like the adjective... yes, my parents had a sense of humor" bit is a good bit — but I can't unsee the woman from The Pointing Game standing at that pole with Pace watching her work, or the woman in Standards lying reflexively and expertly to Brooke, or the woman in the bathroom mirror deciding that the self that begged would never come out again. So when Randi turns "the whole lit face" on Vee and says "Love you, girl," I don't experience it as simple warmth — I experience it as a performance so accomplished that even I, holding the prior chapters in my head, can't quite catch the seam. That's unsettling in a way that feels earned by the text, not manufactured by me: I have three chapters of hard evidence that Randi is capable of exactly this, professionally.

Cassie is the one new note in this chapter and she landed on me hard. Her flat "Cassie," refusing the bit Randi offered; her declining coffee without sulk or edge; and then that "Goodbye, Vee" — capital-G goodbye, not "bye," not "see you later" — which Vee herself registers as wrong, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday," and then papers over with a laugh. I don't know what Cassie knows or suspects, if anything — the text doesn't tell me, and I'm holding that open — but her plainness reads to me right now as the only ballast in the chapter, the one person not performing, and her instinct (if it is an instinct and not just a person who says four words a day and meant only four words) unsettled me more than anything else on the page. I want to trust that "goodbye" was just Cassie being economical and odd. I'm not sure I do.

Swoon and pull: the whole Chi Latte sequence is genuinely swoony on Vee's side — the gold Greek sign resolving into a joke she's finally let in on, the high table, "the transcript was blank... she had been keeping the feeling" — that's lovely, exact writing about what it's like to be so happy you can't retain the actual conversation. I felt swept up in her happiness even while dreading it. That doubleness — pure reader pleasure in her joy, pure reader dread about its source — is the chapter's real charge, more than any erotic charge (there's none here; this chapter is entirely PG, all social, and it doesn't need heat, because the heat is retrospective, coming off what I already know these two are capable of).

Trust vs. suspicion: I trust Vee completely — she has no guile in this chapter, nothing but appetite and shame-management. I do not trust Randi, and the text has earned that for me across three chapters now, not manufactured it. I have no read yet on Cassie beyond unease.

Friction: none really — this chapter is clean and controlled, no thumb on the scale that I caught. If anything it under-explains, in exactly the right way: it never once tells me what to feel about the dramatic irony, it just lets me sit in it.

Title: "See You Later" is doing exactly what a good title in this book seems to do — it's the throwaway phrase Randi tosses off on the sidewalk, and by the time I reach it, it's landed as the chapter's whole engine: not "let's do this again" (a thing people say and don't) but a flat scheduled fact, "a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see." Vee reads it as a promise. I read it as confirmation that Randi is already operating on a plan Vee has no access to. It's a title that gives nothing away on its own but recolors the entire chapter once you've finished it — the most satisfying kind.

Book title / series title / tagline: "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" both feel like they're closing in now — this chapter is the invitation actually being extended, politely, warmly, completely without Vee's knowledge that it's an invitation at all rather than an accident. The jacket line "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one" is no longer abstract to me — I just watched it happen, beat by beat, disguised as a coffee run.

What I want next: I want to see whether Cassie says anything more, whether that "goodbye" pays off or was just texture. I want to watch Vee and Randi's friendship deepen while dreading exactly how it deepens, given what the jacket copy already told me about "lunches too intimate to name." And I'm braced for Pace to enter Vee's life on-page at some point soon, dreading it a little because I already know what he is capable of building, literally, with his hands.
