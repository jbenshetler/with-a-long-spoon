## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I should say up front: my packet gave me no jacket copy and no memory checkpoint this time — just the three chapters immediately before ("The Bench," "Standards," "The Pointing Game") and then this one. So I'm reading close to the beginning of my knowledge of this book, with those three chapters vivid in my mind and nothing else to lean on. I don't have a tagline to react to, so I won't invent one.

This chapter landed on me very differently than the three before it, and that difference is the whole experience of reading it. Those chapters were interior, charged, claustrophobic — Randi on the bench being taken apart and then folding herself back behind her face; Randi performing composure for Brooke over coffee; Randi and Pace playing a game on the quad that ends with Pace pointing at a laughing red-haired girl and Randi saying, helplessly, "Yes" before she can dress it as anything else. Then this chapter opens on an ordinary stats lecture and a girl named Vee who doesn't look at the door, and it takes me a full page to understand: this is her. This is the girl. I already know something Vee does not know, and that gap is where the entire chapter lives for me.

Vee herself I liked immediately and ached for a little. She's rendered with real specificity — the girl who gets to class early, who has "the right pen," who does the social labor of smoothing over Cassie's flat affect because that's a skill she's built and can't turn off. The Chi Latte sequence is the chapter's best writing for me: her cataloguing the room, calling the underdressed girls "floozies" in her mother's voice before she's even finished forming the thought, and then immediately, in the very next beat, admitting to herself that she has more than any of them and could wear what they're wearing — and then closing the thought because "a nice girl" doesn't. That double move, judgment and hunger in the same breath, both borrowed from her mother's voice, is exactly the kind of interior self-deception I read for. She isn't a type; she's a girl actively managing herself in real time, and I trust the prose to keep giving me that.

Randi, seen now from outside for the first time, is chilling in a way that's earned rather than imposed. Everything she does with Vee — the practiced bit about her own name, the instant total-wattage warmth, "I love that, I need that," the hug and the "love you, girl" — is recognizable to me as the identical machinery she ran on Brooke in "Standards," just aimed downward instead of sideways. It's the same face. I know, because I just watched Pace point at Vee across a lawn and heard Randi's helpless, unrehearsed "Yes," that this warmth is not simple. Whether it's calculated recruitment, genuine infatuation, or (most likely, and most uncomfortable) both at once — the chapter doesn't resolve that, and I won't resolve it for it. What I can say honestly is that watching Randi turn her whole lit face on an unsuspecting Vee, knowing what I know and Vee doesn't, produced real dread in me — not manufactured suspicion, but the specific dread of watching someone be loved-bombed by a person I've just watched perform an identical warmth as a tool.

Cassie is the chapter's other unresolved current, and she landed hard on one line: "Goodbye, Vee." Not "see you later," not "bye" — and Vee feels the wrongness of it too, "the way you feel a stair that isn't where your foot expected," and smooths right over it. I don't know what Cassie knows or senses, if anything — the text doesn't tell me, and I'm not going to decide for it whether that's protective instinct, plain social flatness, or something else. But it sat in me as the one note of the chapter that felt aimed at the reader rather than at Vee, and I noticed it land.

Swoon and pull: yes, real swoon, and I want to name it honestly rather than only the dread. Vee's crush on Randi — "a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance" — is genuinely lovely and genuinely queer without any hand-wringing about it, exactly the kind of desire-between-women-as-simply-available-as-any-other that I go looking for. The high-table image, the "later doing in her chest what the right pen had done," the whole hour Vee can't reconstruct in words but keeps as pure feeling — that's swoon, and it worked on me even while the dread ran underneath it. I don't want to flatten the chapter into only its ominous half; the wanting itself is rendered so warmly that I felt it as wanting, not just as a trap closing.

Erotic charge: low and appropriately so — heat 1 at most, all of it in the register of infatuation and physical noticing (Vee's own body-awareness in the coffee shop, the assessment of the other girls) rather than any sexual activity. It didn't need more than that; the charge here is social and emotional, not bodily, and it would have felt wrong to push it further given whose eyes I'm behind.

Friction: one moment made me feel a small authorial thumb on the scale — the professor's aside about "latent variables. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" landing in Vee's notebook, underlined, going nowhere, right before the chapter pivots into exactly that dynamic with Randi. It's a nice image and it did land a small chill in me, but I noticed the reach of it — it felt slightly too on the nose for a line that's ostensibly incidental classroom noise.

Titles: "See You Later" is the chapter's own closing line, and knowing that going in did nothing to spoil it — if anything it sharpened the ordinary phrase into something with weight the moment Randi actually says it, tossed off, walking backward, "the queen having decided there would be a later." It's a title that works exactly by being a thing people say and don't usually mean, made real. As for the book and series titles: I have no tagline to react to this time, but "A Polite Invitation" fits this chapter uncannily well on its own — the whole hour is a sequence of invitations (the seat, the coffee, "I've decided," "go meet her") that are warm on the surface and, given what I already know from "The Pointing Game," possibly instrumental underneath. I don't yet know if that reading is fair to Vee's story or just my dread talking; I'm holding it open.

What I want next: I want to know what Randi actually wants from Vee — recruitment, genuine attachment, or something she hasn't sorted out for herself, the way she hadn't sorted out what to do with what happened on the bench. I want more of Cassie, whose flatness feels load-bearing rather than incidental. And I'm bracing, without being told to, for the moment Vee finds out what "later" actually means — a guess from here, entirely built on the previous chapter's game, not on anything this chapter states.
