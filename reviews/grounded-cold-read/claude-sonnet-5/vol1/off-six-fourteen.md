# Cold read (grounded) — Off Six-Fourteen

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This chapter is a exhale after a very long held breath, and I felt my own shoulders drop reading it. After the bench, after Standards, after the whole engineered afternoon of "May I Choose" — after watching Randi be pointed at strangers on a quad like she's shopping and watching Pace measure and place and withhold with two different women in two different registers — Off Six-Fourteen is just two girls in a dorm room with the window cracked, and it's the first scene in a while where nobody is performing for anybody. That's its whole charge, and it worked on me completely.

Vee is glowing in a way I trust. The spin into the bean bag chair, "Look at me," throwing a pillow at Cassie — this is the first time I've seen her be uncomplicatedly, physically happy on the page, not managed-happy, not performing-warmth-for-Randi happy. I liked her a lot here. And the thing she keeps hammering at Cassie — "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose" — read to me as completely sincere and also, given everything I now know that she doesn't, almost unbearably poignant. She's proud of her own agency in a scene I watched get built for her from thirty feet away, by two people comparing her to a dish they hadn't ordered. I don't distrust Vee for a second. I ache for her instead.

Cassie is the chapter's real gift. "How much did he give you to drink?" landing exactly where it needed to — not cynicism, just the flat-floored care Cassie always supplies under her deadpan — and then immediately, genuinely glad once she has the real answer. Cassie clocking "off six-fourteen" as not-an-address, asking "is he rich," telling her to borrow *good* shoes — this is a friend actually doing the work of a friend, low-key, undemonstrative, and it's the warmest relationship in the book right now for me. I trust Cassie completely. Her flat "Goodbye, Vee" a few chapters back and her look at Vee crossing the bar at Randi's phone — I'm holding those, unresolved, as things Cassie is quietly tracking that Vee isn't.

Pace, in this chapter, only exists as Vee's report of him, and the gap between what I watched him do (the pointing game, the studied "May I choose," the deliberate hand always the same hand, the withheld kiss that reads as strategy dressed as patience) and what Vee experienced (a man who let her have her rule and didn't push, who was made "obvious" by moonlight and a tree) is where the unease actually lives for me — not manufactured, earned by the last several chapters. I know "May I choose" is a practiced move because I watched an almost identical please-and-patience with Randi on the bench. Vee doesn't know that. The chapter doesn't editorialize about it at all — it just lets me sit with knowing more than she does, which the jacket copy told me to expect and which the actual craft now delivers.

Swoon / pull: the swoon is real and I didn't want to armor against it. "I'm making an exception" is a genuinely lovely, hard-earned button — a small rebellion against her own reflexive rule, delivered as her choice, on her feet, closing the distance herself. I wanted that for her. I also swooned a little at Cassie's plain unglamorous loyalty — "Borrow good ones" as a whole vote of confidence. This chapter gave me the tenderness the jacket promised without irony, and I let it.

Erotic charge: near zero, and rightly so — this is an after-scene, all glow and no skin, and it's a smart contrast to land right after the bench and the pointing game, both of which were relentless. The heat here is entirely in memory and reenactment (the tea, the tree, the kiss) filtered through Vee's telling, which keeps it warm rather than clinical.

Friction: none that felt like a thumb on the scale. If anything the chapter trusts its silences — it never once tells me to be suspicious of Pace, it just lets the accumulated weight of what I already know do that work. The only small tug: Vee not knowing where Pace lives or whether he's rich, "off six-fourteen" as literally all the information she has, feels like the text quietly flagging how little she actually knows about him under all the intimacy — but it's Cassie who does that flagging, in character, not the narration nudging me.

Title: "Off Six-Fourteen" is such a plain, funny, non-swoony title for a chapter that's mostly swoon, and I liked that mismatch — it points to the one concrete, slightly ominous fact in an otherwise dreamy recap: she can tell her best friend exactly nothing solid about where this man lives except a road number and "past where it goes to gravel." It reads like Cassie's dry joke made into the chapter's frame, and it lands as a small, deliberate note of remove/distance rather than foreboding exactly — though knowing what I know about how deliberately Pace and Randi have built this, I can't quite let it go as innocent either. Series title and tagline keep pointing the same direction they always have — everything given freely, nothing that can be called taken — and this chapter is the clearest demonstration yet of just how freely and how genuinely Vee is giving it.

What I want next: I want to see the hike — the house off six-fourteen, what it looks like when Vee finally walks into the space I've already seen through Randi's eyes on the bench. I'm dreading that a little, in the good way: I know things about that house's furniture, its bedroom, that Vee doesn't, and I expect her first real look at his house to be quietly loaded for me even if it's ordinary for her. Guess from here, nothing more.
