## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into "Rye" still wet from "Leave No Trace," honestly — that hike chapter worked on me hard, and this one is the exhale after it, so my first feeling is almost physical relief: good, someone gets to process this out loud, and it's Cassie she gets to process it with.

Vee: I am completely in her corner right now, more than ever. The mountain itself moved me — "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain" landed as real pride, not a line, and I liked that Cassie gave it back to her plainly ("Look at you," and meant it) instead of turning it cute. And I loved watching her narrate the truck scene to Cassie without flinching from it — "And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." That "Me," twice now (it echoed her insistence in ch9 that the kiss was hers, not talked into), is becoming her refrain, and I trust it: this is a woman noticing herself wanting things and refusing to let the mother-voice own the story of why.

Pace: he isn't on the page in this chapter — only in Vee's retelling — but the retelling kept me tender toward him. The boot fix, the cherry pits, "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and then not picking the dress back up, the dry shirt handed over and then his own body given to the rain so she could change unwatched — all of that reads, moment to moment, as real care, attentive down to the smallest physical fact of her (a hot spot on a heel, a soaked bra). And yet I can't fully let go of what I'm carrying from before this chapter — that his interest in her began inside a game he built with Randi. Nothing in "Rye" itself indicts him; it's all secondhand tenderness, filtered through Vee's glow. But I notice the retelling has no cop, no Randi, no crack anywhere — it's the version of the day Vee needed to tell, and I'm aware that's a choice on her part (or the chapter's), not proof that nothing else is there.

Cassie: still my safest harbor in this book, and she went slightly deeper here. The jokes — "I had your blood type," "No windowless van" — are exactly her, protective wariness turned into comedy so it doesn't have to be a confrontation. But then: "Cassie didn't say anything. Cassie was rapt," and after the shirt story, "'Vee,' Cassie said finally. Soft. Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." That line got me. I don't know what's under it — tenderness for her friend, something else stirring, the same unnamed thing the checkpoint told me sometimes crosses her face — and I'm going to leave that exactly as open as the page left it, but I felt it, a small held breath in an otherwise light scene.

Swoon/pull: the swoon is really carried over from the hike chapter and confirmed here in the retelling — the boot, the "beautiful dress" line that he simply lets lie on the rock without cashing in on it, the standing-in-the-rain gesture. In "Rye" itself the swoon shifts register: it's the pleasure of watching someone tell their best story to the one person who'll actually listen right. "And then we ate," she said, "the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" — that line made me smile outright; it's such a precise, earned bit of characterization of what food means to her.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing on this page gives me new grounds to doubt Pace or Randi — this chapter doesn't even put Randi in the room, which itself is a little conspicuous to me only because the checkpoint has trained me to notice her absence. My suspicion here is inherited, not earned fresh by "Rye" — I want to be honest that the text itself offered me pure, unclouded tenderness, and any unease is me carrying old knowledge into a scene that, read alone, has no shadow in it at all.

Erotic charge: low and warm rather than hot, because this chapter is retelling, not reliving — the heat lives in what Vee describes (the unbuttoning, the watching, the shaking hand) but the scene itself is two friends in dry sweats in a cool dorm room. It works on me more as afterglow than as arousal — the charge is in the pride and vulnerability of saying it out loud, not in fresh sensory detail.

Friction: none that bothered me. If I'm hunting for a wrinkle, it's that the retelling omits the police stop entirely — no cop lights, no interrupted "Pace—" — which I noticed only because I'd just read it happen. That's a real gap, but I'm holding it open rather than deciding what it means; maybe it just wasn't the part of the day that mattered to tell, maybe there's more to it later.

Titles: "Rye" is such a modest little word to hang a chapter on, and it clicked into place for me exactly where I expected the food motif to keep paying out — "that sandwich... we didn't leave a crumb, we didn't leave a pit" — rye bread standing in for the whole day's exactness and plainness and unearned nourishment. It doesn't give anything away; it just quietly confirms that food is still the book's love-language. As for the bigger titles — I wasn't handed an exact tagline in this packet, so I won't invent one — but *A Polite Invitation* keeps resonating with all this permission-asking (the boot, the dress line, even Vee's own "come in" gesture flipping who does the inviting), and *With a Long Spoon* keeps quietly needling at me under all this warmth, reminding me there's supposed to be a reason for caution here that neither "Leave No Trace" nor "Rye" has shown me directly yet.

What I want next: I want to know what Vee almost said before the siren cut her off — that unfinished "Pace—" is sitting in me like an open parenthesis. I want to see Randi again and see whether she's still watching this unfold from the outside, and whether her own feelings for Vee are moving anywhere. And I'm bracing, a little, for the gap between how genuinely good this feels on the page and what I already know about how it started — that seam hasn't opened yet, but I don't expect it to stay closed.
