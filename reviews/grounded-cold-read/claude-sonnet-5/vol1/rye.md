## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Okay, this one got me. Two chapters really — "Leave No Trace" and then "Rye" as its landing — but I'm reading them as one continuous arc since Rye is Vee retelling the hike to Cassie only minutes after it happened, still dripping.

**Pace.** He keeps being *right*, in a way that's starting to feel less like a device and more like a genuine, worked-for tenderness — and I notice I still can't fully separate those two readings, which is exactly the discomfort the checkpoint warned me to expect. The boot fix is the clearest example: he crouches, unlaces, finds the hot spot, cuts moleskin, relaces tighter at the ankle, looser at the top, gives the toe a pat that means only "done." No commentary on her body, no making her feel like she wrecked the day. That's just — good. Actually good. Then the cherry pits: "We take nothing and leave nothing," gentle, no shame attached, and he sets his own pit down beside hers without looking at her do it — deliberately not making her watch herself be watched. And then the truck: he gives her his only dry shirt — the one he packed for *himself*, with no house to go home to after — and then gets back out into the rain so she can change in privacy. That's a genuinely selfless gesture and it lands as one. I felt the swoon of it fully. And yet the checkpoint's undertow is still there, quiet under all of it: this is a man who is extremely good at reading a woman's exact private hunger and answering it precisely — which is also, structurally, what a plan looks like from the inside. I don't distrust him on the page. I just can't stop hearing both notes at once anymore.

**Vee.** This is her best chapter yet for me. The dress moment on the rock undid me a little — "That kind of red. It just looked like the dress. Like the one," then immediately talking herself out of even wanting it, putting it back on the shelf "the way you put a thing back and keep walking" — and Pace's only response, no fanfare: "That sounds like a beautiful dress." He doesn't buy it for her, doesn't make a moment of noticing her want, just confirms it's allowed to exist. That restraint is doing a lot of work on me. Then the truck scene — the unbuttoning, on her knees on the bench seat, her mother's voice narrating every button ("floozy," "what kind of girl") running underneath her own heat — is the most explicit, most interior piece of desire in the book so far, and it's *hers*. She calls him back in. She chooses the reveal. The shame-voice doesn't stop her, it fuels her, and that felt honest rather than punishing — desire winning an argument with shame rather than shame being erased. I loved that she isn't rescued from the voice; she just outvotes it, this once.

**Cassie.** Still my anchor of trust. "You had a whole witness statement ready" / "I had your blood type" is so exactly her — funny armor around real protectiveness — and then the pivot when Vee gets to the truck: Cassie goes still, stops being dry, and just says "Vee." "Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." That line wrecked me a little. It's not warning, not judgment — it's just witnessing, carefully, like she knows this matters more than teasing can hold. I trust Cassie completely and I notice I'm leaning on her reactions almost as a barometer for how worried I'm supposed to be — and right now she doesn't seem worried, just moved. That reassures me more than it should, probably.

**Swoon / pull.** The unbuttoning in the truck is the erotic peak of the chapter for me — slow, cold, wet, absolutely earned by everything leading up to it, and it works because it's staged entirely as *her* choice, her eyes holding his, her hands not looking down until she wants to. The stillness on his face — "want and gladness and a stillness she had never seen on anyone," not leaning in, not moving — is somehow hotter than if he had touched her. And then the small gut-punch: he was shivering the whole time. He'd been standing outside in the rain, freezing, so she wouldn't have anyone looking at her, and even once inside he doesn't take anything for himself — she's the one who has to say "Pace, you're freezing," has to be the one to care for him back. That reversal — her noticing his cold before her own wanting — felt like the truest tender beat in the whole chapter.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new suspicion earned here — the text gives me nothing but generosity from Pace on the page. But the checkpoint's dramatic irony sits over every scene like weather; I read the moleskin kit, the flannel, the standing-in-the-rain, all as sincere, and also can't unknow that his attentiveness to her exact hungers is the same skill that engineered the "chance" meetings. I'm holding both, as instructed — not resolving it, because the page hasn't resolved it either.

**Erotic charge.** High, and unusually well-paced — it builds physically (the burn, the cold, the hunger, the rain) before it turns sexual, so the desire feels *earned by the body* rather than dropped in. The food writing (the horseradish "climbing… a beat behind the rest," the cherries, "the plain animal rightness of needing something and being handed it") is doing real work as foreplay before there's any actual foreplay — hunger as the whole grammar of this book, exactly as the checkpoint flagged, and it's still working on me.

**Friction.** Almost none. If I press, the campus-cop interruption at the very end of "Leave No Trace" felt slightly convenient as a scene-break device — cutting off "Pace—" right as she's about to say something big — but it didn't bother me; it read as realistic bad luck, not authorial thumb.

**Titles.** "Leave No Trace" is doing double duty and I liked catching it after the fact — it's the literal hiking-etiquette line ("we take nothing and leave nothing," the wax-paper pit ritual) but it's also clearly about what this whole relationship is built on: nothing is supposed to show, no evidence, no mess. Given everything the checkpoint told me about staged coincidences and hidden games, "leave no trace" landed with a small chill once I thought about it sideways — not on the page, but in my head, after. "Rye" is quieter — just the bread from the hike — but it's warm, plain, unpretentious, and it fits that this chapter is the domestic afterglow, the retelling, not the event itself.

**What I want next.** I want to keep watching Vee's body win these small arguments with her mother's voice — that felt like real growth, not just heat. I'm hoping the induction dinner and that russet dress come back; Pace's non-reaction to it felt like a held door, and I want to know if anyone walks through it. And I'm still waiting, with real dread, for the moment the checkpoint has been priming me for — when Vee finds out any of this was a plan. This chapter made that eventual reckoning feel worse to imagine, because it made Pace's care land as so specific and so kind that I don't want it to have been calculated, even though I know it partly was.
