## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still warm from the induction — Vee walking the slit open on purpose, deciding pride and heat were the same thing by the end — and this chapter cashes that decision in immediately. She texts him first ("I miss you. Can I come by early?") and feels the "nakedness of having asked," and I felt that little vertigo with her — this is a woman who has never initiated, doing it, and being scared of doing it. That's such a small, exact detail, and it told me right away this chapter was going to live in her interior as much as in the bed.

Pace, opening the door in gym shorts, not put together, caught off guard — I liked him more here than almost anywhere else in the run. The checkpoint has trained me to see him as "a wall," deliberate, always in control of the tempo, and this chapter's whole first half is about Vee finding the one place the wall gives: she pokes him and "the smallest thing, a tightening at the corner of his eyes, there and gone, but she caught it" — and the line that really got me was "she had just watched it flinch." That's a genuinely sweet, funny, human beat — mutual, unrigged, nobody managing anybody. I felt real affection for both of them in the chase through the house: her discovering she's faster than him, "up on the balls of her feet in the heels," flashing him on purpose and being astonished at her own nerve — "some small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it." I was rooting for them, plainly, in that stretch. It's the most playful, least freighted the two of them have been on the page.

Then it turns, and turns carefully. The "bet" is a clever piece of business — not coercion, a game with an escape hatch built in, and the escape hatch actually gets used: she says "Don't," and the hand stops. He doesn't take it away, but he doesn't move it either, and he waits — "let the quiet run on, a slow second and then another" — until she nods. That nod ("Surprising her, her own head went up and down, small") is the whole book's established grammar of consent (room to choose, stop before taking) doing exactly what it's supposed to do, and I trusted it completely in the moment. What I loved most, though, was that her mouth kept lying after her body had already answered — "It's not," she says, into the sheet, "even as she rose to meet his hand, and she could not have said which one of them she was lying to." That's precisely the kind of interior material I read for: not just heat, but the shame that keeps performing denial after the body has already voted, and the book lets that stand without resolving it into either triumph or humiliation.

The erotic charge is very high for me here and it never goes clinical. It peaks at the finger — "He slid into her and her body took him without a question, smooth and hot and already given, as though it had been waiting all afternoon to be asked and was relieved, finally, to answer" — and again at the held-up, glistening finger and his face reading it as "true... fine... hers, and good," which does real work against the mother's-voice shame motif I've been tracking since the truck striptease. It doesn't slacken in the bent-over waiting either — that stretch is quiet on purpose, no "theatre," and I felt the absence of spectacle as its own kind of intimacy.

And then — the moment that actually chilled me a little, cleanly, on the page itself, not just from what I know as a reader: "in the not-seeing, in the waiting, Randi's story rose up in her like something she'd swallowed: would she have to count out loud? Was that the theatre of it..." I know things Vee doesn't. I know Randi told that story about her own sex life with a partner the checkpoint has never confirmed but strongly implies is Pace himself. So here is Vee, in Pace's bed, unknowingly bracing for a version of a scene that may be the very scene Randi lived through with this same man — and getting something quieter, gentler, entirely her own instead. "What came was nothing like the story." I don't think the text wants me alarmed exactly — it reads first as psychologically true (of course a vivid friend's story surfaces at a moment like this) — but it also lands as the sharpest dramatic-irony beat in the chapter, and it kept my old unease about Pace and Randi alive without the chapter ever mentioning the secret directly. That's well done and it unsettled me more than anything overtly "wrong" on the page.

The aftercare undid me a little. The juice, held to her mouth before anything else; "wonderful" said over and over, "quieter each time," until word and hand-in-hair are the same thing — that's tenderness I believed completely, no cynicism allowed into the frame. And then the bathroom monologue afterward is genuinely funny and endearing — "Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together... My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky" — Vee being wry and self-aware about her own patterns is one of my favorite notes in the whole book so far, because it's not shame performing, it's her actually laughing at herself, which the earlier chapters haven't given her much room to do.

The scar gets a lovely small continuation: she touches it again, doesn't ask, and the text frames that as its own kind of trust — "the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep." I still don't know what it means (the "not tonight" mystery stands), but I liked that the not-asking is now framed as Vee's choice rather than his withholding.

No new suspicion of Pace or Vee individually from what's on this page — everything he does here is careful, responsive, and stops when asked. My suspicion is entirely the inherited, checkpoint-level kind (the secret Randi relationship, the engineered meeting), and this chapter sharpened rather than dulled it, purely through that one intrusive thought about Randi's story — a very effective, quiet piece of dramatic irony rather than anything the prose editorializes about.

Title: "We Find Out" is lifted straight from Pace's own line — "Either you admit you lost, or we find out" — so on the surface it's just the bet resolving. But by the end I read it doubled: what actually gets "found out" isn't a bet's outcome, it's Vee's body's answer, which had already been decided "hours ago" and was "only waiting for her to stop asking." The title pointed exactly where the chapter went — not a giveaway so much as a promise kept.

I don't have a cover tagline in this packet to react to, and there's no jacket copy included this round — so I'll just say the book's title and series title aren't doing anything active for me on this particular chapter; this one felt purely domestic and interior, no politeness, no invitation-language on the page, just two people and a house.

What I want next: I want to know whether this gets reported to Randi in the same currency Vee usually pays her — the confession economy between them has been so consistent that I'm bracing for Vee to hand this exact scene over, not knowing she might be describing her own lover back to the person who may know him better than she does. That thought is genuinely uncomfortable to anticipate and I mean that as praise for how well the earlier chapters set the trap. I also still want the scar's story, and I'm curious whether Cassie — entirely absent this chapter — will ever get told any of this, or whether the editing-out of Cassie (established in the checkpoint) will keep hardening.
