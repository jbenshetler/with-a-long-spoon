# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one got me, and it got me slowly, which is the whole trick of it. The hike itself — the boots, the moleskin, the summit — I was ready to just enjoy as tender-competent-man content, and it is that, but it's doing something sneakier underneath. Every single beat of care Pace shows her (the pace he sets, the boot fix, "That sounds like a beautiful dress," the cherry pit ritual) reads as pure, unforced tenderness on the page, and yet I can't unknow what the checkpoint told me — that his attention to her hungers is *calibrated*, that this whole courtship started as a private game. So I sat there loving the boot scene and also flinching a little at how expertly he reads her, how he "didn't sell her the top," how he lets her find things out at exactly the pace that will land hardest. That's the book's central discomfort working on me exactly as designed, and I want to name it rather than pretend I don't feel it: I trust his tenderness completely as *felt*, in the moment, and I still can't fully trust the shape of it.

The mother's voice intruding — "what kind of girl lets a man," "floozy," "your mother would never" — landed hard for me. That's the shame architecture from the checkpoint (her mother, her body-consciousness) doing exactly what it's supposed to: making her wanting cost something even as she chooses it. The cherry-pit moment is small and perfect — "We take nothing and leave nothing" — the shame of not knowing a rule, and him quietly making his own pit disappear onto the paper without looking at her, so she isn't singled out. That's the kind of care that actually undoes me a little: not grand, just precisely aimed at not humiliating her.

The truck scene is where the chapter goes from warm to genuinely erotic and genuinely moving at once. I want to be honest about the swoon here — the finger crook, "come in," her holding his eyes through every button, the way she gives her body to be seen rather than hides it — that's real desire on the page, hers, chosen, deliberate, and it reads as a true yes even with the shame-voice narrating against it the whole time. It's explicit without losing interiority; I felt both the heat and the psychology simultaneously, which is exactly what I want from this book. And then the turn — she reaches for his hand to keep going and finds him shaking, freezing, and the whole register flips from erotic to tender in one beat. "You're freezing." "We should go. Get warm. Please." That's the chapter's real gut-punch for me: her wanting him to see her collapses instantly into wanting to take care of him. That felt like real intimacy, not performance, on both their parts — which makes me trust *this specific moment* between them completely, separate from whatever the larger plan is.

Then the ending: the cop lights, the siren, the sentence cut off, him pulled away before either of them can say the thing that was clearly bigger than either of them expected. That's a genuinely good disruption — comic-adjacent (campus cop, fire lane) but it lands as loss, not comedy, because we don't get to hear what she was about to say. I felt robbed right along with her, standing under the overhang holding her wet shirt.

Cassie doesn't appear at all this chapter — not even mentioned except that the boots are "Meg's," which made me notice Meg lent boots but wasn't in the last chapters directly involved; that's a small continuity texture, nothing more.

Randi is entirely absent here too, and I noticed I didn't miss her — this chapter needed to be just the two of them, uncomplicated by her, and it worked precisely because of that.

**Swoon / pull:** high. The summit view, the sandwich with the ambush of horseradish, the cherry pits, the flannel shirt, the whole slow-burn confession-that-isn't-a-confession in the truck — this is the book at its most purely swept-up-in-it. I wanted them to have that scene. I wanted the sentence at the end to get finished.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion earned by the text itself this chapter — Pace is nothing but careful and generous on the page. My unease is entirely inherited from the checkpoint (the game, the engineered meeting), not from anything he does here. If I'm honest, this chapter is working hard to make me forget that unease, and it nearly succeeded — which is itself worth noting as a reader.

**Erotic charge:** Builds from near-zero (bootlaces, sandwiches) to genuinely charged in the truck — the unbuttoning is slow, sustained, physically detailed, and psychologically loaded with the mother's voice running under it. It peaks right at "the last button" and then pivots, cleanly, into something that isn't sex at all — his shivering, her wrapping his hand against her belly. That pivot doesn't deflate the charge; it just changes what the charge is *for*.

**Friction:** Very little. The siren interruption felt almost too tidy as a device — engineered exactly to cut her sentence off — but I'll allow it; it earned its abruptness by not resolving into either comedy or tragedy, just genuine loss.

**Titles:** "Leave No Trace" is doing real work — on the surface it's the hiking ethic (the cherry pits, "we take nothing and leave nothing"), but it's clearly aimed past that, at the game the checkpoint told me about, at meetings staged to look like accident, at a courtship built to leave no visible fingerprints on the ground it's walking on. Once I had the line in the chapter, I couldn't read the title as innocent anymore — it recolored the whole hike backward. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* and the tagline about "every yes was freely given" sit right on top of the truck scene — her yes there is unmistakably hers, urgent and chosen, and that's exactly what makes the tagline's "that was the problem" so unsettling in retrospect rather than in the moment.

**What I want next:** I want to know what she was about to say. I want to know whether Randi finds out about this chapter — whether it gets reported back, performed for her over lunch the way ch10 implied everything does. And I have a low simmering dread (guess from here) that this hike, this specific tenderness, is going to get relayed to Randi in some form, and that relay will feel like a betrayal of what just happened even though Vee doesn't know there's anything to betray.
