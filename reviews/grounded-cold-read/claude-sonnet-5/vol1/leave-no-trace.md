# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one landed on me hard, and almost entirely through the body — hers and mine both. It's just Vee and Pace, alone, no Randi, no sorority machinery, no social chess — and after several chapters of watching Vee be maneuvered through other people's careful choreography, that absence felt like relief. For one chapter the "plan" recedes and what's left is a mountain, a blister, a sandwich, rain, and two people.

Pace keeps doing the thing that both moves me and makes me want to fold my arms. The boot-fixing scene got me completely — "He found the raw place, pink and risen, and made a sound, hmm" and then the relacing, "tight near the ankle, loose across the top," and the apology afterward ("Should've done that at the lot. I'm sorry. I didn't think to ask about the boots") — that's such specific, unglamorous care. Then the cherry pit: "We take nothing and leave nothing," said gentle, no shaming, and him quietly laying his own pit down beside hers so she isn't singled out in her not-knowing. And then the enormous one — giving her his only dry shirt, the one he packed for after, and then getting back out into the rain himself rather than sit in the truck in his wet clothes with her. I felt that gesture in my chest before I understood it, same as Vee did: "Then she looked down at the flannel in her lap. His shirt, and then the truck. Both." That's swoon, unguarded — I wanted to reach through the page and pull him back into the cab myself.

And then she does. That's the chapter's real turn for me. Vee has spent nine chapters as the one being looked at, chosen, engineered around — and here she's the one who raps on the glass and crooks a finger and says, with her body, come in. The unbuttoning scene is the erotic center of the chapter and it worked on me precisely because it's not staged for anyone but her — her mother's voice is right there the whole time ("floozy," "what kind of girl," "your mother would never") and she does it anyway, deliberately, with her eyes up, making him watch her decide. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her" (about the cherry, but it's the same sentence underneath the whole chapter). That's the line I'd underline. The heat peaks there — not in contact, there's no touching at all, but in the sheer nerve of her choosing exposure as an act of her own hunger rather than someone else's gaze.

Then the shiver. "He was shivering. He'd been shivering." That undid the whole gallant tableau in one clean stroke and made him human again — not a saint standing in the rain out of some performed chivalry, but a man who was actually, physically cold and did it anyway, and didn't tell her. Her turning the care back onto him — "Pace. You're freezing." — felt like the first moment in the book where she is taking care of him rather than only receiving it, and I liked her more for it.

Where's my suspicion? Honestly — nowhere new, and I want to be exact about that. Nothing in this chapter behaves like calculation. There's no coincidence engineered here, no absent third party pulling strings; it's a mountain, real weather, a real blister. If anything this chapter felt like the first thing Vee's been given that nobody arranged — the valley didn't care that she was standing there, and I liked that the text says so plainly: "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it... and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it." That's the one unimpeachable good thing in the book so far. But I can't fully unknow what the checkpoint told me — that his patience, his calibration to exactly what she needs, his never pushing past a no, is a pattern he has practiced before, on Randi, deliberately. So even while nothing here rang false, I noticed myself watching his restraint in the truck — sitting still, not leaning in, letting her set every inch of it — through two lenses at once: as tenderness, and as a technique I've already seen work. That's not the text's fault; it's dramatic irony doing exactly what it's built to do.

The ending cut me off exactly where it meant to. "Pace—" and then the cop lights, the siren, gone. I actually felt the loss of that sentence — we'll never know what she was about to say, and neither will he, not yet. It's a clean, almost cruel little interruption, and it works: the ordinary world (a campus cop enforcing a fire lane, of all mundane things) reaches in and ends the private universe the mountain built. She's left holding his shirt, which is the one piece of him that didn't disappear with the truck.

Titles: "Leave No Trace" is stated right on the page as hiking etiquette — the cherry pit on the wax paper — and then the chapter spends the rest of its length quietly disproving it. They leave traces everywhere: the moleskin on her heel, the shiver in his hand, and most of all the flannel shirt she's still wearing when the truck pulls away, holding "the smell of soap and of a man." It's a lovely, unforced irony — the ethic he taught her about the mountain is exactly the thing their afternoon refuses to obey. I don't think it gave anything away before I read it; it recolored gently, on the way out, rather than telegraphing on the way in.

On the book/series titles — I wasn't handed a tagline in this packet, so I'm going only off what the checkpoint remembers of the jacket ("a game between the two people now closest to her," "an appetite that can only give her the best of herself by taking it"). A Polite Invitation feels present again here in reverse: for once it's Vee issuing the invitation — the crooked finger, "come in" — rather than receiving one that was arranged for her. With a Long Spoon I keep hearing as a warning about proximity to something dangerous, and this chapter is the first one that made me forget the warning for pages at a stretch, purely on the strength of how good the tenderness felt. That's probably the point, and I noticed myself falling for it exactly the way the earlier chapters told me I would.

What I want next: I want to know what she was about to say. I want the induction dinner and the dress — the "black dress in the back of my closet" she settled for instead of the russet silk one — to come back around, because Pace's silent "That sounds like a beautiful dress" felt like a held door, not a closed one. And under all of it I still want to know when — or whether — Vee finds out any of this started as a pointing game. This chapter didn't move that plot forward at all, and for once I didn't mind; I'd have followed these two up that mountain for another chapter easily.

Symbolism: the indifferent, uncaring vastness of the summit view read to me as the first entirely un-engineered good thing in Vee's story — nobody arranged the mountain. The cherry pit / wax-paper ritual doubles as a small parable about not being made to feel "common" for not knowing a rule, which sits right against the class anxiety of the dress conversation a page later.

Characterization: Pace deepens without flattening — the shivering hand kept him from tipping into pure gallant-saint territory, and his refusal to pick the "beautiful dress" comment back up (letting her keep her own modest choice rather than rescuing her with money) is consistent with his established never-push restraint. Vee deepens the most — this is the first time she initiates a wholly physical, wholly hers act of desire rather than following someone else's lead, which tracks forward from her initiating the ch8 kiss but goes further.

Pace — within the chapter: the ascent/blister/summit/food/descent/storm/truck structure earns its length; nothing dragged, each beat had a distinct emotional job. The drop-off interruption lands fast and clean.

Pace — chapter to chapter: a clear change of texture after several checkpoint chapters of social maneuvering (sorority scrutiny, staged meetings) — this is the first fully two-hander, no other named character on the page, and it reads as the relationship's momentum finally outrunning the plot machinery around it, at least for one chapter.
