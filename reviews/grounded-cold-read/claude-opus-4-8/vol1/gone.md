## Felt read

Oh, this one got under me. After a long warm stretch inside Vee's happiness — the dress, the mirror, the shoe store, all that thawing joy — this chapter pulls me back behind the curtain, into the warm house with the lamp low and Sade going under the door, and puts me in bed with the two people who made her. And the thing that unsettles me is exactly that it *isn't* cold back here. I braced for a war-room, for two schemers tallying their progress. What I got instead was two people who love the girl they're gaming and can't quite manage it, and that's so much worse and so much better than a con.

**Randi.** She's the whole chapter for me. "I didn't think I'd like her this much... I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come. She got past me before I could help it." That is the woman who resolved, on the bench, to hide her real self forever, admitting out loud that something got in under the face without her permission. It moved my feeling about her more than any chapter since I learned what she was. She retells the shoe store — the yeti bit, the bigfoot stomp, the older couple, Vee going red — and I've just *read* that afternoon from Vee's side, from inside the delight, and now I hear it come back out of Randi as genuine tenderness: "It was so cute. She was *so* embarrassed." And then the pivot that chilled me: "That was kind of hot." The embarrassment eroticized, the shame turned into a thing she wants. That's the seduction engine idling out loud.

Then "She's got it bad. It's a little pathetic. — I love it." That's the cruelty and the tenderness in one breath, and I believe both halves. And the line I keep circling: when Pace does his little aria about how Vee brightens a room, "she has no idea she does it... she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her" — Randi goes **still.** "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding." And "Yeah... She is," a beat too long. That's jealousy. That's the maker of the game catching her own partner glowing about the toy, and something tightening. And then she deflects straight into sex — "So soon?" — riding the feeling down into her body where she can control it, because feeling it plain is the one thing she swore she'd never do. The checkpoint told me she fears being *seen* above everything; here I watched her get seen by her own wanting and reach for hips instead of words. That's the ache of the chapter.

**Pace.** He confirms what I dreaded and does it gently, which is the unsettling part. "She's not ready, though. She'll get there. You'll want to take your time." — so there *is* a "there," a destination Vee's being walked toward that she doesn't know is a destination. And his answer: "I want her to get there on her own." That line is the most disturbing thing in the chapter precisely because it sounds like principle. He wants Vee's yes to be *real*, unforced, hers — inside a frame that is a lie to its foundation. He's engineering an authentic consent to a false premise, and he thinks the authenticity absolves the premise. It doesn't. But God, it's coherent with everything I know of him: the "May I," the pencil handed back, the door he won't open before she opens it. He extends his courtliness right into the con and it makes the con worse, not better. And "Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" — said low, certain, without heat — is true, and kind, and he's using the truth. He sees her clearly and that's the instrument.

**Vee**, who isn't on the page, is all over it. Everything I just watched her feel — chosen, funny, the funniest woman in Virginia, "never once in her life had a friend like this" — comes back to me refracted through the two of them talking her over in bed, and it hurts. The froyo, the dress, the shoes she put on a dead credit card to be worthy of the gown — and here are the two people who set the whole autumn in motion, warm and post-coital, calling her cute and pathetic and gone.

**Swoon / pull.** Strange to say of a chapter this dark, but the erotic pull is real and it's *Randi's*, not the con's. The moment that got me was her dragging her own body up the length of him to show him what Vee keeps under the cardigan — "It's *criminal*, Pace" — and the read that lands isn't the picture of Vee at all: "not the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi, who kept a hand on every ounce of herself out in the world, wanting something out loud in his bed and not able to stop it." *That's* the heat — her losing her grip. The woman who rations herself, spilling. And Pace not taking up "the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold" — the threesome hovering in that unspoken rest, and him declining to reach for it. The scene knows exactly where its charge is: in Randi coming undone, which is the one thing she'll never let Vee, or the world, or maybe even herself see.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** This isn't new suspicion — it's confirmation of everything the checkpoint already told me. What's *new* is that the plan has a crack in it now, and the crack is Randi's own heart. She got past me before I could help it; the stillness when Pace shines on Vee. I now suspect this thing is going to wound its own maker — that Randi is caught in the same trap she built, and that her jealousy and the "take your time" threesome plan are on a collision course. I don't trust the plan to hold, and I don't mean morally — I mean the people running it are feeling things that will break it.

**Erotic charge.** Working, and cleanest at Randi's loss of control. It goes slightly abstract at "there was nothing quiet left in either of them now" — the closing sex is more atmosphere (Sade, lamplight, "the blue on her toes still perfect") than sensation, and that's fine; the real heat already happened in the pillow talk. Peak: the body-drag and "criminal."

**Friction.** Almost none. "That was kind of hot" is nearly on-the-nose, but it's so in-character for how Randi processes — turning tenderness into appetite so she doesn't have to feel it as tenderness — that I bought it. Nothing felt like a thumb on the scale; the jealousy is done entirely in a held stillness and a beat too long, no narrator underlining it. That restraint is why it landed.

**The titles.** "Gone" — on the page it's Randi's "she's so gone over you," Vee gone over Pace. But read back over the whole chapter it means more: Vee is *gone* — past the point of return, taken, and doesn't know it. And Randi is gone too — "got past me before I could help it." Everyone in the warm house is further gone than they mean to be. It didn't spoil anything before I read it; it recolored after, which is the good kind of title. **A Polite Invitation** and **With a Long Spoon** — this chapter is the proverb made flesh: he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon, and the spoon here is the *distance* these two think they're keeping from the thing they're feeding on. Randi's whole afternoon-in-a-store, her stillness, prove the spoon isn't long enough — she's supping and getting caught. The devil isn't Vee; it's the feeling itself, the fondness that "got past" the calculation. (No cover tagline was included in my packet, so I can't react to one.)

**What I want / dread next.** I want back to Vee, and I dread it, because I now hold the room she doesn't. I dread the induction — she's rehearsed walking in late in that dress, and I've now heard the two of them plan the "there" she's being walked toward; those two threads want to meet. My guess from here: Randi's jealousy is the fault line, and the plan cracks not because Vee finds them out but because one of the makers can't hold the face. I want to keep reading badly.
