## Reader reaction

## The felt read

I read this whole chapter with my chest doing two things at once, and I need to be honest about both because the doubleness is the entire experience.

On the surface — and the surface is beautifully made — this is one of the most swoon-worthy seduction scenes I've read in a while. Vee's POV is so *inside* her that I couldn't hold myself apart from it. The text he sends with the quiet built in — "a man saying a thing he'd already decided and leaving all the rest of the room to her" — and her pressing the phone to her chest, grinning at a dark screen "in a way she'd have died to have anyone see." That's just a girl falling, rendered exactly. The dinner is a slow tender undressing that never touches a button: the froyo was daylight, this is *evening*, "he'd asked her for it like something he meant to do properly." The showered-and-shaved detail — "his jaw still faintly blushing from the razor, that color that meant *recently*, that meant *for this*" — landed on me warm and low. And the food. God, the food. The bright green thing and the smoke-dark thing, "one to wake you and one to fold you under," her hunger opening "into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." The hand at the end — "less like something beginning than something resolving" — and the exception she makes to her own rule. As pure romance craft, that final beat, her feet not going, the hand pulling taut and stopping him, *I'm making an exception* — it's a lovely, earned melt. I felt it.

And I cannot take one clean sip of it, because I know what Vee doesn't.

I know this man is Randi's. I know the two of them stood on a quad four days ago and *pointed at Vee* — that Randi's helpless "yes" the second Pace pointed at the redheaded girl laughing by the dining hall was Vee. I know the coffee-shop rescue was hunting dressed as chance: Cassie said *there's a cute guy looking at you*, and I watched Randi's hand go busy on her cup, the sip that didn't happen, twice, while "her brain helpfully provided the explanation *she's bored*." So every tender thing in this chapter sits on a hook I can see and Vee can't. When he says "May I choose?" over the menu and the relief of surrendering the choice "went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would" — I felt the swoon *and* the cold drop under it, because handing him the choosing is exactly the thing being harvested from her. When she eats freely for the first time, "not watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was," her mother's *that's plenty* absent for once — it's genuinely moving, a girl let out of a cage. But I have just watched him do this *precise* thing to Randi on the bench, sliced apple, portioned appetite, "a girl who had been portioning herself... who tonight had finally let someone feed her." He has a method. Feeding the hungry, careful girl until she hands herself over is the method. Watching him run it on a second girl, warmer and more open than the first, with the first one somewhere off-page having already gone to pieces in a locked bathroom over it — that's the dread, and it's real, and the page earned every ounce of it by showing me the machinery first.

So my feeling about **Pace** has hardened without a single thing in *this* chapter being unkind. That's the unsettling part. On this page he is nothing but good — attentive, unhurried, the "may I," the street side, the not-explaining-the-menu, the grace to not-see her elevator stumble. If I had only this chapter I'd be halfway in love with him with Vee. But I've read the bench, I've read the pointing game, and the tenderness now reads to me as *technique* — frictionless, total, and aimed. The detail that crystallized it: he eats always with the right hand, "some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it, one more thing he knew that she didn't." Vee finds that charming. I found it chilling — a man of rules and control, and she keeps clocking, admiringly, how much he knows that she doesn't. The whole date is an asymmetry of knowledge and she reads the asymmetry as safety.

**Vee** I ache for, plainly and without complication. She's the most sympathetic person in this book so far — the girl outside the joke on the coffee-shop sign finally let in under it, the swimmer who grieves a little underwater because the pool is "the one part of it that had never been taken." She is being taken. She's so hungry — for food, for warmth, for being the thing a person waits on — that her defenses are down before he's done anything. Her rule ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days") breaking in real time is the sweetest and saddest beat in the chapter: the "old reflex" that keeps the lines coming back "at the worst possible moment," and her deciding, herself, to let it go. She thinks she's choosing. The book's whole architecture tells me the choice was arranged for her.

**Cassie** is the one clean, warm thing I trust completely — and she keeps being the truth-teller. "That's not a boy. That's a man." She teases Vee about everything and won't tease her about this, which is its own alarm bell. From the earlier chapters her flat "*Goodbye, Vee*" still sits oddly in me, and here she's again the one who reads a room straight. I keep wanting her in the room. She's the friend who'd see it.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** this is the one book where "I have no suspicion" would be a lie — but I want to be precise. My suspicion is not of *this chapter*, which does nothing false. It's the suspicion the earlier chapters loaded into me, riding under a scene that is, on its own terms, gentle. The page didn't manufacture a shadow; the shadow is structural, and this chapter's very sweetness is what makes it land. That's the most sophisticated thing about it.

**Erotic charge:** it works, but sideways — displaced almost entirely onto food and hands. The bread torn "with care for the heat," the drip caught off the side of his hand and brought to his mouth mid-sentence, the tea in brass cages "made so you could hold them while they were still too hot to hold." The heat is all in *restraint* and in her watching his hands, and it's effective precisely because nothing happens. The peak for me was the hand at the very end, which is chaste and read as more erotic than most explicit scenes because of everything freighted onto it.

**Friction:** almost none — the prose is controlled and unshowy. If I reach: the interiority occasionally spells its own subtext a half-step too clearly for me — "the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there." I mostly bought it because it's Vee's habit of self-narration established already, but "the bill of a thing to come due" is the author leaning toward me, since I *know* a bill is coming.

**The titles.** "May I Choose" is his three words at the table — and it recolors the second I sit with it. He asks permission to choose *for* her, and she says "Yes. Please," and the whole book is about who chooses and who is chosen. Vee thinks the choosing she's doing (the exception, the yes) is hers; the title quietly points at how much of it is his, and Randi's, made before she walked into the lobby. It also rhymes with his "May I call you?" and the "may" she heard as a word from another vocabulary — the politeness that keeps sounding like courtesy and functioning like a leash. Which is exactly where **A Polite Invitation** points: this dinner *is* the polite invitation, extended so gracefully she can't feel it closing. And **With a Long Spoon** — sup with the devil, use a long spoon — got literalized on me here: a shared meal with a man you should not eat with without one, and Vee has no spoon at all; she hands him the choosing and eats from his hand. The cover tagline wasn't in my packet, so I've only got the two titles and the series line to go on, and they go a long way.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here):** I dread the collision — Randi and Vee both wanting Pace, Vee not knowing Randi has him, the "threesome" Randi offered him being *Vee*, and Vee walking in guileless. I want Cassie to see it. I half-expect the next chapters to run the two girls toward each other socially while Vee has no idea her new best friend selected her. And I dread, specifically, the moment Vee learns the meet-cute was staged — that "the cute guy in line" was pointed at her. This chapter made me love the seduction and distrust the seducer at the same time, and I want to keep reading mostly to find out how much it's going to cost her.
