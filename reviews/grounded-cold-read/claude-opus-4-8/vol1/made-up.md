## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one just swept me, plainly, and I want to say that before I say anything cautious, because the caution isn't really the point of this chapter. After the heat and the near-dark undertow of "We Find Out" — the spanking, the shame doubled, the "wonderful, wonderful" — this is a quieter, cleaner high, and I loved it for being Vee's. She drove out here with no overnight bag and the burgundy dress, and that detail undid me a little: "She'd brought no overnight bag, nothing for a night she wasn't letting herself plan on — and she'd brought the dress." That's the whole woman in one line. She won't admit she's staying, but she'll cross the state with the thing she most wants him to see.

The engine of the chapter is that Pace missed the induction — the dress crossed a stage "for everyone but its maker" — and so Vee stages a private second performance, a two-costume runway in his den, and I found it genuinely tender and genuinely hot. What got me was the reversal of who's running it. All book long he's been the one who says "May I," who chooses, who sets her down where he wants her. Here she puts out one flat hand and he sits back into the recliner — "she watched him make himself do it" — and stays put. She's the lead now. She gives him the induction walk on purpose, "given and taken back and given," and his stillness, the hand that "came partway up and he put it away again," is the erotic center. "He stayed where he was and let the looking be the touch." That's the book's whole grammar — asking before taking, the choice left with the other person — handed back to Vee to run on him. It felt like a real turn, not just a sexy tableau.

The second costume — the heels and nothing, the walk down the hall bare — is where the heat peaked for me, but it's a warm peak, not the shame-scalded kind. "The heels stayed on. That was all of it... and knowing that, and going anyway, was the entire costume." The costume is her own nerve. That's the growth the whole book has been aiming at: the wet, the plenty, the freckled fullness she spent her life hiding, and here she stands in his lamp and "did not cover one inch of it." The last line landed the swoon clean: "the blush went down and down, and she did not cover one inch of it." I wanted this for her.

Pace's line — "The other is for the world. This is just for me" — is exactly the register I trust him in scene-to-scene, and it worked on me even as the back of my mind flagged it. Because I know what I know: he chose her before she knew there was a choice, he ran the pointing game, Randi is out there being told all of this. "This is just for me" is a lovely thing to say to a woman standing naked in your lamplight who trusts you completely, and it is also, faintly, a proprietary sentence from a man who has been quietly making her his the whole time — the made dress, the made table, the made chair, the scrunchies that say "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." The chapter doesn't ask me to feel the chill, though. It's soft-pedaled, one degree under. I noticed it; I didn't feel unsafe. That restraint is what makes the book work on me.

The one image that carried a small private shiver was the boots: she slides into his too-large boots to fetch the dress and stands there "feeling like a child playing dress-up in a parent's closet." Right before a scene about grown, chosen, adult desire, that flicker of the child in the parent's clothes sat oddly against the whole Daphne shadow I'm carrying — 19 and 15, the tutor, the "best years." I don't think the chapter meant me to weaponize it, and I won't; but I felt the cold of the field come in with it.

Trust vs. suspicion: no new suspicion earned here. Randi isn't on the page, isn't even named — this is the one strand that's just Vee and Pace, no lunch to retell, no one drawing her open. That absence is almost a relief, and also makes me notice how rarely these two get a scene with no third party braided through it. The only unease is the standing one I carry into every Pace scene, and the chapter didn't feed it so much as let it idle.

Friction: almost none. It's short and it earns its length. If I reach, "like a door coming open" for his attention is a touch familiar — doors open a lot in this book — but it's the book's own vocabulary, so it reads as motif, not tic. The sunrise/sunset line is the closest thing to a scripted charm-line, and I let him have it because it's genuinely his kind of courtliness.

Titles: "Made-Up" is doing double and triple work, and I like it. Costumes, Halloween, made-up faces — the surface. But "made-up" is also the dress and everything else in the house that Pace made — made up out of his hands, with no tag. And there's the third turn I can't unhear given the whole book: made-up as invented, a fiction, a woman assembling a self. Vee's "the entire costume" was her nerve — the self she's making up as she goes, the floozie her mother warned her about, chosen on purpose now. It doesn't give anything away; it recolors gently on the second thought. A Polite Invitation and With a Long Spoon — the spoon still reads to me as "sup with the devil, use a long spoon," and every warm scene like this one is exactly the polite, freely-given step the jacket promised: "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." Nothing in this chapter was taken. She brought the dress herself. That's the whole engine, and it's why the tenderness reads as the trap even when it's just lovely.

What I want next: I want to stay in this uncomplicated warmth another beat, and I don't expect to be allowed to. Guess from here: the induction photo she sent him uncropped, the thing that's "hers" in the frame — I suspect that privacy is going to get pried open, probably by Randi, probably over a lunch. And I keep waiting for the moment the two closest people in her life resolve into one fact in front of her.
