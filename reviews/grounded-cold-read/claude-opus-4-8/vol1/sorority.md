## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one worked on me and then, in the last two pages, closed a cold hand around my chest — and both of those are the chapter doing exactly what it meant to.

The first two-thirds is joy, and I let it be joy. Vee showing the dress to Randi in the cinderblock dorm room, choosing — again, that little swing of the wanting getting there before the plan — to *put it on* rather than just hold it up on the hanger. "The wanting got there before the plan, the wanting to be *seen* in it as she'd been seen in his mirror." I love that she keeps choosing the seen version of herself. And Randi's reading of the dress is genuinely gorgeous writing: the fingers on the charmeuse, "a good weight," and then the line that lands the whole chapter's title of tenderness — "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." I felt that land the same beat Vee did, because it names the exact thing she cried over in "Peekaboo" without a word for it. That's a real gift Randi gives her. The unbearable part is that I can't tell, and the page won't tell me, whether it's a gift or a lure — and I'm choosing, this chapter, to let it be both and leave it ajar.

The shoe store slapstick is the most purely delighted these two have ever been on the page, and it disarmed me on purpose. The yeti canoe-shoe, the pink-rhinestone six-inch platforms held to the light — "Do these go with burgundy?" — the flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." I laughed, and I loved that Vee is the funniest woman in Virginia on her own turf, the TJMaxx wall where "the ground was hers." That mattered to me: for once we're in a place Vee owns, and she's the master of it, and Randi's the one out of her depth blaming the shoe for being cut small. It's the most equal they've felt.

And then the shoe-size reveal — "That's me. That's me exactly. Same feet, baby." — and my stomach did something. It's charming on the surface, "practically a marriage," but I felt the machinery of it: same size means *we can share*, means the boundary between them keeps dissolving, and it arrives right as Randi overrides Vee's sensible tan heel. "Which is exactly why you're not buying that." The whole day pivots there from friendship into steering.

The boutique is where the swoon curdled into dread for me, and the page earned every inch. Vee "holding a quiet the exact shape of what was in her account" — that's the loneliest sentence in the chapter, and it's true and specific and it hurt. And Randi is *right there* before the quiet can set, with the word: "A dress your *lover* made you?" I noticed she reached for the exact escalation — *lover*, the word Vee hadn't let herself use — the way she's reached for every escalation, one word past where Vee had gotten to herself. That's the pattern I keep clocking: Randi always names the thing one notch further in than Vee has dared, and Vee feels the notch click and follows.

Then the kneeling. Randi taking the box, going down on one knee on the pale floor "in her own beautiful clothes" — and I felt the deliberate rhyme with Pace kneeling at the fitting, and I don't think it's accidental to Randi at all. She holds Vee's foot, looks at the frosted-plum polish — the polish Vee painted, per my memory, "for a man who'll have your feet in his hands" — and moves one thumb once across the nail, and Vee's floor tips and her hand shoots to Randi's shoulder to stop the room swinging. "Randi looked up at her and smiled and said nothing at all." That's the erotic peak of the chapter and it's the most quietly alarming thing in it, because it's so small and so controlled and so plainly *known* by the one doing it. Randi felt exactly what that thumb did. She's mining Vee's body now, not just her secrets.

And the purchase. God, the purchase. Vee putting the debit card back — "the card she paid her own way with, the one that only ever spent what was already behind it" — and taking out the emergency credit card with "nothing under it," knowing "the paying-it-back part" will be sitting there next month. "The shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed." I watched her build the rationalization in real time and I believed it and I dreaded it, because the same permission-to-want that's been her liberation just walked her into debt she can't cover, in a shop she'd never have entered, steered there by Randi. The self-deception is doing something new here: before it freed her; here it costs her actual money she doesn't have. And Randi beside her buying loafers "a multiple of Vee's, and then several times that again," never looking at the number, "shoes were a thing you simply traded mid-afternoon." The contrast is merciless and the chapter knows it — Vee carrying her box against her chest "like it might get up and leave her," and Randi's easy "you can put it in the trunk, it's not going to *spoil*." That last laugh is real and it broke my heart a little, because Vee ends on "she had never once in her life had a friend like this," and the whole chapter has been quietly showing me the price of the friendship — a maxed card, a dissolving boundary, a thumb on her toe.

TRUST/SUSPICION: My suspicion of Randi is at its highest and, crucially, the *text* earned it this time, not my carry-in dread. The thumb across the nail is deliberate seduction dressed as a shoe fitting. The *lover* escalation, the steering off the tan shoe, the shoe-size "marriage" — these are moves, and I watched them be moves. But I want to hold the door the page holds: the dress-reading tenderness and the shoe-store laughter also read *real*, and I can't collapse Randi to pure predator without flattening what's genuinely on the page. She may be both, all the way down. Cassie remains my clean sightline and she's barely here — only the tight-made empty bunk "nobody home," which felt like a small ache all by itself.

SWOON: Highest on "He made it *to* you," and on the dress worn out of the bathroom into the middle of the room to be looked at. HEAT peak: the single thumb-stroke on the toenail, more charged than a lot of explicit scenes because it's stolen and controlled.

FRICTION: Almost none. The one thing I'll flag is that the Simpson's-paradox echo isn't in this chapter (that was "All Told") — here the thematic freight is lighter-handed and better for it. If I reach for a quibble, "a different arithmetic had taken the wheel" leans a hair toward telling me the rationalization instead of just letting me watch it — but honestly the watching is so good around it that I forgave it instantly.

TITLES: "Sorority" — on the surface just the induction-dress errand, the world Vee's being welcomed into. On reflection it's colder: a sorority is a chosen sisterhood you buy your way into, that dresses you and ranks you and puts you on show, and this chapter is Randi inducting Vee into *her* sisterhood of two — same size, share everything, "practically a marriage" — at a cost Vee can't afford. The book title *A Polite Invitation* and the tagline in my packet, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," sit right on top of this chapter: every yes today was Vee's — she chose the shoes, she chose the credit card, she chose to follow Randi out the door — and every one was steered. The series title *With a Long Spoon* I still read as "sup with the devil, use a long spoon" — keep your distance from what you dine with — and Vee has no spoon at all; she's eating out of Randi's hand. That's the dread, and it's earned.

WANT/DREAD NEXT: I want the induction itself, the room turning as Vee's daydreamed. I dread the credit-card bill and, more, the moment the boundary Randi keeps dissolving finally gives — the thumb on the toe felt like a threshold. (Guess from here, marked as a guess:) the shoes they now "share" and the same-size beat feel planted to pay off in something more than footwear.
