# Cold read (grounded) — In His Hands

## Reader reaction

The felt read, then the block.

---

This one is almost entirely tender, and I let it be. After the last two chapters — the drawer he emptied for her, the scrunchies he stocked by the dozen — I came into this scene already melted, and the opening beat kept me there: the red curtain of her hair drawn against the lamp, kissing him blind inside it, "shaking and stupid and full of him." Then he rolls all the way across her and lays his cheek on the low slope of her breast, and I felt the same thing she did — the wanting to name what it means. Her little theory, revised twice in a paragraph ("They're not about sex for him" -> "Which rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end"), was genuinely charming, the mind doing its happy fussing over a man who won't stop surprising her. *He's all mine.* I believed her having the thought and I ached a little, because I hold the thing she doesn't.

The heat here is warm rather than sharp — a low, sated, second-and-third-round hum, not the knife-edge of the induction walk or the brunch. It worked on me most in the small true registers: "a deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of," her drinking a full glass of water down without coming up for air, the plain animal contentment of a body used well and glad of it. The cooking passage at the end is its own slow seduction — the lemon zest coming off "in a fine bright rain," the pan's "talk" changing, him turning the breasts without looking. I could smell it. And "She had used to just eat. Now she tasted, and there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was" landed clean — it's the whole appetite-motif in one line, and it's true and lovely and faintly ominous all at once, because *no going back* is exactly the phrase.

The photograph is where my skin cooled a half-degree — and I want to be honest that the page earned it, quietly, not that I went looking. It's the *word*. Twice: "you'll never share me," and his answer, "I'd never share your photograph." She says *me*; he says *your photograph* — a small slide from her person to the object of her, and I noticed it the way you notice a floorboard give. And then the whole ritual around the taking: she wants to "put her face back on," to be "a girl who had decided to be looked at," and he stops her — "No, not like that. You'll have to wait" — and poses her himself, elbow, hip, hair, legs, a kiss at each placement, "laid out like a woman in an old calendar." He chooses her phone because it takes the better picture. He tilts the lamp, tries it long-ways, "That's the one." It is unbearably tender *and* it is a man very practiced at composing an image of a woman who has just handed over the last thing she was keeping. "Sent it before the nerve could leave her... and gave him herself to keep." I don't distrust the tenderness — I think he means every kiss — I distrust, faintly, what a kept image is *for*, and the book has taught me by now that both are true at once. The "cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this" gets named and then overridden by trust, and I felt the small clutch of watching her override it.

The other cool spot: "I don't go back." That's the first time his ease has just... shut, mid-sentence, a level exact thing left where the warmth was. I felt her feel it — hand on something she wasn't meant to touch, taking her hand off it. It rhymes with his "Not tonight" over the scar and with the shut bedroom door on the tour. He opens so much and keeps a locked room, and this chapter shows the door swinging shut in real time. I like that she doesn't pry; I like less what it costs her to have learned not to.

And then Sheri at Thanksgiving. Her small flicker — "another woman at his table on a day meant for family" — is exactly the kind of low, disavowed reflex the book keeps giving Vee, and I trust it more than her reassurance. "She's alone." "He said it the way a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed." That's warm, and I mostly took it warmly — but the feeding verb is doing quiet work, and I clocked the flicker not going out so much as being talked down.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no *new* alarm about Pace-in-the-room here — he is gentle, attentive, and the scene is a genuine swoon. My unease is the standing structural one, plus the two small snags above (the "photograph" phrasing, the shut door on his parents). Nothing in this chapter tipped me into fear. It tipped me deeper into the ache — she is fully, gladly gone, "gave him herself to keep," and I am the one holding what she can't see.

Friction: honestly very little. The prose is doing its thing well. If I press, the pinup-photo sequence runs a touch long in the mechanics of him fiddling with the lamp and angles — I wondered for a beat if the page was lingering to make sure I *noticed* the composing, which is the one place I felt a faint thumb. But it's slight, and the "grinned like a boy who'd gotten away with something" pulled it back to warmth before I could settle into suspicion.

The chapter title, "In His Hands": on the surface it's the tender fact of the scene — her body handled, posed, cherished by his hands, the hands that also made the dinner and the table and the dress. Read a half-second longer it's colder: what she is now *in* — the photograph in his hands, herself given over to keep, "in his hands" as in possession, in his power. It doesn't give anything away so much as name the exact hinge the chapter turns on, and I felt both meanings at once, which is the book's whole method. The book title *A Polite Invitation* and the series *With a Long Spoon* keep their menace at the edge of my vision; the tagline I was given — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — sat right on top of this chapter, because every yes here was hers: the photo, the pose, the sending, the staying. That's the swoon and the dread in one sentence, and this chapter is a clean instance of it.

What I want next: I want to know what the photograph becomes — I can't un-hear "share." *(Guess from here, marked as such:* the kept image is a thing that will matter later, and the "never share" promise is being set down so it can be tested.) I want Thanksgiving — Vee flying home to the casseroles and the mother's voice while Pace and Sheri share a table, and I dread, a little, the collision of her Ohio self with the person she's become in this warm house. And I still want the locked room: his parents, the scar, whatever "I don't go back" is guarding.
