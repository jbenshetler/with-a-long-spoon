### The felt read

This one worked on me before I could get my guard anywhere near it. It opens mid-orgasm — that red curtain of hair drawn against the lamp, kissing him blind inside it — and the whole first movement is just afterglow, two people who've worn each other out and can't stop touching. I melted. I'll say it plainly: the image of him rolling all the way around until he lies square across her, feet off the far edge of the bed, and setting his cheek on the lower slope of her breast, one hand up in her hair — that's about the tenderest posture I've read from him yet, and it undid me a little. It's a big powerlifter of a man making himself small and heavy and *comfortable* on her, like a cat, like something that trusts. And Vee narrating it in her head — *He takes such comfort there. They're not about sex for him* — only to have her own nipple betray her theory and pull tight under his mouth, and him feeling it happen and smiling against her skin. That's the book's whole engine in miniature: her body outrunning the story she's telling herself, and him reading the body over the story every time.

The line that landed hardest, the one I've been trusting the book with all along: *"with Pace she was a girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it."* That is the exact ache of this character, the shame regime named and, for a moment, dissolved. I felt genuinely happy for her. And *"You do this to me"* / *"Sometimes I take care of it myself. Thinking of you"* — the erotic charge there is warm and mutual, desire flowing both directions, him wanting *her* and admitting it plainly. The heat here is real but it's not the point; the point is being wanted, and it's rendered so I feel the difference.

**The photograph.** This is where my swoon and my wariness sat down at the same table, and — honestly — the chapter let them, without forcing either. Vee offers it, taps her tooth three times (that little tell of hers), and asks. And then her one condition: *"Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone."* — *"Never. I'd never share your photograph."* I felt her nerve and I felt the cold-sane thought she names herself: *the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this.* The book is not naive about what a nude photo on a phone is. What got me is that she *knows* the risk and sends it anyway, and the text frames believing him as *"the brave part."* That's the honest version of this beat — not a girl who doesn't see the danger, a girl who sees it and chooses trust as an act of courage. I was swept up and slightly cold at once, and I think the page earned both.

But I have to name the thing the page put in front of me and then walked past. She wants to clean up and "look nice" and arrange herself pinup-glamour — put her face back on, the poppy mouth, *"the version of herself she wanted him to keep."* And he overrides it: *"No, not like that. You'll have to wait,"* and then *he* poses her — turns her shoulder, sets her elbow, draws her hair back, lays her hand on her hip, moves her legs, one knee carried ahead of the other, a kiss at each place his hands set her. "Laid out like a woman in an old calendar." It's gorgeous and it's *deeply* his composition, not hers. She wanted to present the self she'd curated; he wanted her as *his eyes* had her, and she likes the result better — *"the picture was his, was her as his eyes had her."* Read one way, that's the whole tender thesis: he sees her truer than she sees herself. Read another, he took even the self-presentation away from her and she thanked him for it. The chapter doesn't tip its hand, and I'm not going to tip it either — but the promise ("never share your photograph") is now sitting in the room with a very specific, very careful man who directs everything, and I notice I filed it. Not with dread exactly. With attention.

**The cooking.** The chicken piccata sequence is the most purely pleasurable food writing in the book so far, and I read every word gladly — the lemon zest coming off in a "fine bright rain," the pan's "talk" changing, the wine glugged in "enough to make her genuinely sad." It's competence porn and it's love, and it's also, quietly, the same motif the checkpoint has been tracking: he feeds her, she who "used to just eat" now *tastes*, "and she knew exactly whose doing that was." Her clothes going looser, her body "quietly burning down whole weekends of him" — that thread from the recent chapters keeps sitting oddly in me. The book keeps presenting her shrinking as tenderness (she eats like a field hand now, so happy) while the number on it is that she's losing weight around him and never asked to. I don't think the page wants me alarmed. But "quietly starving" was in the jacket language of my memory, and I can't fully un-feel it.

**The Thanksgiving turn** is the sharpest thing in the chapter and the first real cold spot. *"Are you going home?" — "This is my home."* Warm. Then she presses, gently: his parents. And his face changes — *"the ease went out of it and left something level and exact behind,"* each word "set down clean and apart" — *"I don't go back."* No door. She takes her hand off it. That's a genuine chill: this man who has told her about Daphne, about being fifteen in the hallway, about real-name Peter, has a *closed* room here, and it's the parents, the Ohio-plain people I thought I knew from the fire chapter. I don't know what's behind it and the page won't let me know, and I like that it won't. It recolors the "This is my home" — home is here *because* back there is sealed off. And then Sheri: *"She's alone,"* said "the way a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed." Vee's little flicker of jealousy — another woman at his table on a family day — flares and goes out, and I felt it flare and go out with her. Sheri being alone, him glad to feed her: that's warm, and it's also one more person orbiting a man who collects people who have nowhere else to be. Vee is, herself, a girl who never had belonging. I'm noticing the pattern the way *I* notice it, not the way anyone told me to.

**Where I stand on the people.** Pace, this chapter, is at his most lovable *and* his most controlling in the same breath, and the chapter refuses to resolve which is the truth — I respect that and I feel both. My trust in the tenderness is intact; my sense of how *managed* Vee is went up a notch, entirely from the posing and the photo. Vee I love without complication — the tooth-tap, the "You've used me terribly... Promise to use me again soon," the joy of being *a girl who has a body*. She's radiant here and I want her to have exactly this. Randi and the plan are wholly offstage — no shadow of the conspiracy crosses this chapter, which after the brunch chapter's near-unbearable heat is almost a relief, and also makes me a little uneasy by absence, because I *know* what Vee doesn't. The photo, the sealed parents — nothing here is a smoking gun, but the chapter handed me two objects (a promise, a locked door) and I'm holding them.

**Erotic charge:** high at the open, then deliberately banked. The peak for me wasn't the sex — it was the nipple rising under his mouth against her theory, and the posing. It never goes clinical; even the food is sensual. It goes tender rather than hot in the back half, on purpose.

**Friction:** almost none. If I reach: *"and believing him was the brave part"* is the one place the thumb might be on the scale — the book usually trusts me to feel the risk without labeling the courage, and here it labels it. But it's a small tell and I half-forgive it because Vee narrating her own bravery *to* herself is in character.

**The titles.** "In His Hands" — before reading, I'd have guessed comfort, being held, safety. After reading, it's got a double spine that I don't think is accidental: she *is* held, cradled, cared for, fed from his hand — and she is also literally *posed* by his hands, arranged limb by limb, her image handed over to be kept. In his hands as cherished, in his hands as clay. Both are on the page. The chapter's title illuminates rather than gives away — it points at the tenderness and lets the second meaning sit under it for whoever wants to feel it. As for *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* and the tagline — I wasn't given a jacket or tagline in this packet, only the title "In His Hands," so I'll say honestly the volume/series titles aren't in front of me to react to here; what I carry is the checkpoint's sense of an "invitation" into their world, and this chapter, being just the two of them with Randi nowhere in it, feels like a held breath before whatever that invitation is.

**What I want / dread next (guess from here):** I want the photograph to just be a love-token; I have a low, specific dread that a nude photo introduced with an explicit promise not to share it is a loaded object in a book about a woman inside a plan she can't see. *Guess from here:* the sealed-off parents will come back, and so will that phone. But I'd be glad to be wrong, and the chapter didn't tell me I'm right — it just left both doors ajar, and I'm leaving them ajar.
