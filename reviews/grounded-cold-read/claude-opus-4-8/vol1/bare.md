## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one is short and it hurt, and I mean that as praise. After three chapters running — Cassie's careful "what is he, to you" and Vee's "Both. Probably both," then the cracker-and-meal sentence that came back the wrong shape, then the whole ugly Chili's night and the too-loud bedroom question — this chapter is the pressure finally arriving somewhere with nowhere else to go. It's just Vee in a cold bunk with a shirt, and it's the loneliest thing I've read in the whole run.

What got me first, in the body, was the physical logic of it, because the chapter is built entirely out of one woman's small movements with a piece of cloth and I felt every one. The shirt worn *over* her sleep shirt — "for the hall, for the walk to the bathroom, for the mornings Cassie was up first and might look and wouldn't have to say anything, a girl too far gone in a boy" — that's the public flannel, the token she can be seen in. Then she strips both shirts off and puts his back on "with nothing under it, the cloth straight onto her, the way she wore it when no one could see." That escalation, from the shirt-as-costume to the shirt-as-skin, is unbearably intimate and I went right down into it with her. This is the flannel that has carried the whole thing since the rain — slept in nightly, washed when it went stale, handed back to be re-scented. It has never once failed her before. That's why the next beat lands like a floor giving way.

Because it fails her here. "And it was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." God. That's the sentence. The whole comfort of the token was always a loop she was running on herself, and once she sees the loop she can't unsee it — "she was here holding a smell in the dark. It had promised him close and there was no one close." The flannel was the book's warmest object and this chapter quietly guts it. I didn't want it to be true and I believed it completely.

And then the ending, which I've been turning over since I read it. She takes the shirt off "up over her head, and the cold of the room came down on her bare skin all at once, and it was worse. It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." She chooses the cold. She chooses the true bad thing over the false warm thing. That's a real move — that's a person deciding she would rather have accurate cold than counterfeit heat — and the detail that finished me was the shirt "left where it fell, beside her, not folded, not set back on the pillow, down in a heap where a dropped thing lands." Every other time the flannel has been *tended* — slept in, washed, handed over deliberately. Here it's dropped like refuse. That heap is the whole chapter's verdict. She hasn't said a word, decided nothing out loud, but her hands have registered a change her mouth won't.

How I feel about Vee right now: closer to her, and more afraid for her, than I've been. What's moved is that she's finally *alone* with the doubt — no Pace to warm it away, no Randi to steer it into arousal, no Cassie to soften it. Just her and the cold and an honest read on a shirt. I've watched her all fall put the uncomfortable things into "the place where she put things she did not know what to do with, and the place was not empty." This chapter is her taking one thing back out and looking at it in the dark and not being able to file it again. The phone beat — thumb over the empty field, waiting for words that don't exist, because "you couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them" — that's her testing every exit and finding them all locked. I ache for her.

Pace isn't on the page and the chapter is *about* his absence, so my feeling toward him got colder by proxy — not because he did anything cruel here but because the last three chapters have shown me the exact shape of what he withholds. He "gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him, and she was here holding a smell in the dark." That "nothing grinding in him" is the accusation the whole chapter can't make out loud. And I still can't forget what I know that she doesn't — that the shirt, the rooms kept warm, the plate set down once he'd built the hunger, all the things she breathes off the cloth, were the instruments of a plan. When she says "she breathed him in and none of it told her what she was to him," I know the flat awful answer she can't reach: she was, at the start, a chosen mark. The chapter doesn't say that. It just leaves her breathing in a man who engineered the very hunger she loves him for feeding. That gap between what she can feel and what I know is doing all the damage, and it's doing it beautifully.

Cassie's presence is tiny and load-bearing. She's asleep below, window cracked her two inches, breathing "slow and easy and asleep," and that easy sleep is the counterweight to Vee's not-sleeping — the sane life going on undisturbed a bunk away while Vee lies awake in a thing she can't name. The whole shirt-as-costume opening exists *because of* Cassie's watching eyes ("the mornings Cassie was up first and might look"). Cassie is the reason Vee needs a public face for this at all. I trust her exactly as much as ever, which is completely, and I notice Vee still hasn't told her the real thing — "Both. Probably both."

Swoon: almost none, deliberately, and that's correct — this is the morning-after of the swoon, the reckoning. But there's an ache here that's as strong as any heat in the book, and it's earned inch by inch, no thumb on the scale. Erotic charge: zero, by design, and I didn't miss it — the sex was two chapters ago and its whole point was that even in his total undoing "he hadn't said it," so this chapter correctly withholds his body the way he withholds the word.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new to distrust *on the page* — no one's warmth curdled here because almost no one's here. What the run has done is quieter and worse: it's let Vee's own instrument (the doubt, the accuracy of her body) turn on the relationship, without her being able to act on it. My suspicion of the plan is unchanged; what's new is that Vee's suspicion of her *own feeling* — "her heart was going at a speed the facts hadn't earned," from the chapter before — has now hardened into a woman lying naked in the cold rather than lie to herself with a warm shirt. That's not manufactured unease. The page built every plank of it.

Friction: almost none. The prose is spare and repetitive on purpose — "the big one and not the small one," the name she can't find recurring — and it earns the repetition because Vee is genuinely stuck in a loop and the language loops with her. If I reach for a quibble, the doubling of "not the big one and not the small one, only every breath of him now and the silence at the center where the word should be" is very close to the same phrasing from the previous chapter's close, but here it reads as her mind actually re-running the same groove, so I let it stand. It didn't feel like the author explaining; it felt like her not being able to get out.

The title: "Bare." Before I read it I'd have guessed it pointed at exposure, being seen — the book's whole "May I look" thread, the nude photo, the dress. It turns out to mean the opposite kind of bareness: no one looking at all. She strips to nothing not to be seen but because the covering has stopped covering, and bare-in-the-cold is the one honest condition left to her. "Bare" also catches *barely* — barely holding, and the bareness of what she actually has of him, which she "could not have held up to anyone." It reads clean and a little cruel and it doesn't give anything away ahead of the page; it only closes over the chapter after. Good title.

The book's titles: *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon one keeps getting truer. "Who sups with the devil should have a long spoon." Vee is at the table, being fed the finest meals of her life, and this chapter is the first time the spoon feels too short — she's close enough now to be burned by exactly what nourished her. The tagline I wasn't given one, so I'll leave it. But "a polite invitation" rings differently after this: every door held open ("May I"), every asked-first, was still an invitation into something she couldn't see the shape of, and here she's finally feeling the walls of it in the dark.

What I want and dread next: I want Cassie to wake up and I dread it too, because Vee still can't say the true thing to her. I want Vee to keep this cold clarity and I'm afraid Pace (or Randi) will arrive and warm it back into the pile before she can act on it — that's been the pattern all fall, the doubt steered back down every time. *Guess from here:* this sleepless night is a hinge, and either she carries this bareness toward asking a question she can't un-ask, or she lets the next warm morning file it away again — and I think the dropped-in-a-heap shirt is the page telling me which way she's tipping. I dread the plan surfacing while she's already this raw. Whatever comes, I'm reading straight into it.
