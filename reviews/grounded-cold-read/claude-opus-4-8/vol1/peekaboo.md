## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one just swept me. I went in braced — I always go in braced now, because I know what these two did to get here — and the chapter walked me right past the brace and into the swoon anyway, and I let it. That's the honest report: body first, I melted.

The whole first movement is the best kind of erotic misdirection. She's dressed to be "lovely and soft to touch," her body has half-made the decision before she's on the step, and when he opens the door there's that lovely fumble of "the hello after, which they hadn't done yet" — the awkwardness of two people who've slept together once and don't have a ritual for it yet. That detail is so *true*. And the kiss goes deep and certain, door not even shut, "both of them already past stopping" — and then he breaks it, and I braced for something withholding or controlling, and instead it's *the surprise*: "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth one more second." I did not expect to find Pace boyish. That landed on me warmly and complicated my unease in a way I liked. Her body "had assumed left, toward the bedroom" and he pulls her right — that's a beautiful little swerve, wanting redirected into something even more intimate than sex.

And then the dress. God, the dress. The prose earns every second it spends on it: the sun through the silk, "the burgundy lit from inside itself and the plum beneath it deepening, a wine-dark glow with a shadow alive in it." But the thing that actually got *me* — same as it got her — is "that he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like no trouble at all." That's the whole man in one sentence, and it's genuinely moving. I felt the tears come up in her because they came up a little in me. Craft as devotion, effort concealed so the beloved never has to feel the weight of it.

The dressing sequence is where the heat and the tenderness fuse. "It came down her like water finding its level" — and then the detail that undid me: the hidden lining, "oyster-pale where it showed at all... a soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away." He built modesty *into* the thing at exactly the places she'd be shy, without being asked. That's not a schemer's detail. That's someone who has thought about her body with tenderness rather than appetite. I believe the feeling on this page even while I know its rotten foundation, and holding both at once is the exact ache the book keeps handing me.

The two undressings are the erotic spine and they're wonderful. First she takes her toes as high as she has because the dress "wanted a height she wasn't giving it" and "that woman could wait" — a promise to herself. Then the slit falls open to the hip, and there's the pale edge of underwear "cutting across the long line," and her two thoughts: keep the steps small so it won't show — and then the second "came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed." She strips the underwear off *to be looked at more*, and the narration names it exactly: "the shame of having stripped herself bare on purpose to be looked at, and it crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all, and she let it come." This is precisely the interior life under the wanting that I read for — the *floozy* flaring up automatic, her face going hot, and her staying anyway until "the looking stopped being shame." That's her whole arc rendered in a single mirror. I was rooting for her out loud.

And the walk to him — "not the careful walk — she strode, the slit swinging open... the length of her given and taken back and given" — is the swooniest beat in the book so far for me. His grin going "into something quieter, harder to look at and impossible to look away from." She takes his face, hooks the bared leg around him, "and after that there was nothing left for either of them to decide." The sex itself is a graceful cut-to-black, and I didn't mind, because the *charge* peaked in the mirror and the stride; the actual coupling is the resolution of a chord already struck.

The coda is where the tenderness turns almost unbearable, and also where the shadow crept back in for me — not because the page put it there, but because of what I carry. The image of them both stopping mid-undress — "*wait, the dress,*" — and hanging it "with the two-handed care you'd give something alive" is delicious, a shared reverence for the thing he made her. And then: she knows without being told he was up all night finishing it "while she slept across town, not knowing." That "not knowing" is a small knife for me. *She doesn't know so many things.* The line means she didn't know he was sewing — but I read the second meaning underneath, and the chapter didn't force it; it just sat there for me to find. Same with the very last words: "getting away with something." She means she's happy, she's lucky, she's stolen a joy she was raised to think she couldn't have. I read it and felt the floor under it: *someone* is getting away with something, and it isn't only her.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Honestly? This chapter gave me almost nothing new to be suspicious *of* — Randi isn't here, Pace does nothing calculated on the page, and every gesture reads as real devotion. My unease is entirely imported from what I already know, and I want to be fair about that: the text did not earn fresh dread here. It earned swoon. The only genuinely *page-earned* flicker is that closing "getting away with something," which is doing double duty and knows it — but even that is more dramatic irony than a tell. Pace deepened toward *warmer* for me this chapter, not colder. The boyish grin, the up-all-night sewing, the built-in modesty lining — these make him harder to simply distrust, which of course makes the whole thing more frightening, not less.

**Where it worked as heat:** a solid, sustained charge, peaking at the mirror and the bare-legged stride, cooling deliberately into afterglow. Nothing went clinical. If anything the restraint at the actual sex was the right call — the anticipation was hotter than any explicit beat could have been here.

**Friction:** almost none. The prose occasionally leans hard on its own lyricism ("a wine-dark glow with a shadow alive in it") but it earned the indulgence. The one place I felt the author's hand at all is the *floozy* flaring "automatic" — I've now seen that shaming-voice beat land in so many chapters that its arrival is starting to feel scheduled rather than surprising. It still works, but it's the one note I can feel coming.

**The title.** "Peekaboo" — before reading I'd have guessed something coy or childish. After, it's lovely and a little sly. The obvious read is the slit: the leg given and hidden and given, *peek*-a-boo, the game of showing and covering that the whole dressing scene turns on. But it also catches the *surprise* — the covered-eyes, here-it-is reveal of the dress, his kid-with-a-secret grin. And underneath both, for me, the darker peekaboo: what's hidden and shown, what she can see and what's kept just out of frame. The dress that hides its own labor. The man up all night she didn't see. It's an innocent word sitting on top of a chapter entirely about display and concealment — which fits everything I've come to expect from how these titles work.

**Book & series titles.** *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon one keeps sharpening for me: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* This chapter is a whole meal shared with no spoon at all, everybody eating straight from the same warm dish, and I keep waiting for the burn. The tagline I wasn't given one in this packet, so I'll leave it. "Polite invitation" reads more ironic every chapter — everything here is invitation, courtesy, *May I*, the choice always left to her — and I know the courtesy is the trap's smoothest surface.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Vee to have exactly one more chapter this happy before it breaks, and I don't expect the book to grant it. *Guess from here:* the induction daydream — walking in late, in this, letting the room turn — feels planted to be paid off, and I suspect the actual induction is where two worlds collide, where Randi's sorority and Vee's dress and Pace's hand all end up in one room and something cracks. I dread that Randi will see the dress and *know*, or that Vee will. But that's me predicting; the page only gave me a daydream and a happy woman watching silk go dark on a door.
