## Reader reaction

FELT READ

After the bench — after the enormity of what Randi went through facedown in Pace's bedroom, after "there you are, Randi" and the decision she made at the mirror to go back behind the face and never come out — this chapter is a hard, cold, brilliant landing. It's the morning after, in daylight, in the sorority common room, and it is almost unbearably good at showing me exactly what the face is for by putting it to work under fire.

I came in braced from the last chapter, and this delivered on the dread in a way I didn't expect: not more heat, but the aftermath as social performance. The whole common-room scene is a four-minute interrogation disguised as girl-chat, and the reason it lands so hard is that I've just seen the underside of this woman. I know what's behind the smile now, and I get to watch her deploy it flawlessly while I know the cost. "Randi's smile arranged. The arrangement was a fraction late." That word "arranged" — used three times, a smile that gets *arranged* like furniture — is doing so much. It's the same performed face from the mirror, and now I'm watching the machinery run in real time, catching only on the fractions of a second.

Brooke unsettled me more than anyone in either chapter. The text earned that. She's warm — "She had a voice the way other people had a perfume" — and every warm thing she does is a soft blade. The not-standing: "Brooke was the chapter president and did not need to stand to stop someone in her own common room." The way she stacks the questions with no seams, drops the paper, then "And — I just want to make sure I have this. *Pace.*" The engineering guess "landing him a notch down without seeming to reach for it," then the recalibration when it's math-PhD "absorbing it without conceding the notch." The fake-scandalized bit about his section. This is a status audit conducted at full smile, and Randi — who sits on Standards herself, who *knows exactly how much authority it takes to stay seated* — reads every move from the inside and can't do anything but comply. I don't trust Brooke an inch, and I love that the chapter didn't tell me not to; it just let her be pleasant and let me feel the teeth.

There's no swoon here, and there shouldn't be — this isn't a scene that wants to sweep me up, it wants me to feel the vise. But it worked on me the way the bench worked on Randi: I ached for her. The detail that broke me was small. "*He's hard to say no to,* she had said, in the kitchen... The line had landed perfectly. *He's hard to say no to,* she heard, in her room, in a different key. It was not a key she could sing in." The same words she'd weaponized as a cute deflection coming back at her true, and her not being able to follow the thought past the words — "beyond the words there was a quiet she had no way of crossing." That's the whole tragedy of the last chapter continuing into daylight. She got seen once and it was so much that she's spent the morning proving she can un-see herself.

The second half — the empty single room — is quietly devastating in a completely different register than the bench. No one to perform for, and she still can't stop, or rather she can't do anything at all. The circling: vanity, bed, closet, sink, none of them landing, her hand "partway lifted toward the row" of clothes and coming down with nothing to do. "She had come to the closet to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what." And then the duvet: "It was not — it was a duvet." That broken sentence killed me, because I know what she's comparing it to — the cashmere blanket she keeps at Pace's, the one he wrapped her in. She's under an adequate synthetic thing in a cold room in last night's clothes with her shoes on, and the prose won't let her (or me) name the comparison, only feel the lack.

The last movement is the one that will stay with me. "It did not feel like a decision... it felt like there being no door." After a whole chapter about doors — the quiet click that was "already wrong," the deadbolt she turned in Pace's bathroom, the lock — the closing image is that there's no door left to shut against the crying, because she's become so good at not-going-there that "the breath was held" without her holding it. That's the mirror-decision from last chapter completed: not a wall she maintains but a reflex she's lost access to. Terrifying, and rendered without a shred of authorial editorializing. It just lets her lie down and close her eyes.

Feelings that moved: my sympathy for Randi deepened hard. Last chapter I was moved by her; here I'm almost frightened for her, because I can see the cost has gone somewhere she can't reach. Pace is entirely absent — mentioned only as the object of the audit — and it's the right absence; this is the world she goes back to, the one he doesn't get to see. Interesting that in his POV last night he read her portioned appetite so tenderly and thought "all he had done was find it, and answer it," and now I'm watching the house that trimmed that appetite in the first place, the machine she has to keep passing. It complicates my read of him without touching him: he found something real, and she has nowhere to keep it.

Erotic charge: essentially zero, by design, and that's correct. There's no heat in this chapter and it would have been wrong to force any. The charge here is dread and social suffocation, and that's plenty.

Friction: almost none. This is tight, controlled work. If anything I felt the "arranged" / "fraction-of-a-second" motif get worked hard — three or four beats of the same catch — but it's a repeated tell that reads as intentional patterning of her performance, not the author over-explaining, so I'll allow it. The one spot where I felt a hand near the scale is "a woman who would one day be some CEO's third wife" about Brooke — a very sharp, very knowing line that steps slightly outside Randi's flustered POV to render a verdict. It's a great line; it just briefly felt like the narrator's judgment rather than Randi's, though given how coolly Randi reads status, I half-buy it as hers.

The title — "Standards." Beautiful and it earned its double meaning on the page rather than before it. Standards is literally the sorority board Randi sits on, the disciplinary/appearances committee — so the chapter's title is the institution conducting the audit. But it also means the standard she's held to, the standard of the face, the impossibly maintained gloss. And it quietly indicts: Standards is the body that would judge exactly the woman she was on the bench last night, and she is one of its judges. The title didn't give anything away going in — "Standards" read as neutral — but on finishing it recolors into the whole apparatus that requires the performance I just watched. That's the innocuous-surface, reread-charge thing working well.

The book's title *A Polite Invitation* and the series *With a Long Spoon*, with the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — this chapter puts unexpected weight on the series title. "He who sups with the devil must use a long spoon" is what I hear under it, and after two chapters I'm feeling the spoon: distance kept, the thing handled at arm's length so it can't touch you. Randi keeps the whole world at spoon's length via the face; the tragedy is Pace got past the spoon once. And "polite invitation" — the jacket told me there's a plan Vee doesn't know about, a game between two people. I haven't met Vee yet in these pages; everything I've seen is Randi and Pace. So I'm holding the jacket loosely, but I notice Randi is described in the jacket as Vee's "glamorous new friend" who steers her toward Pace. That means the woman I've spent two whole chapters aching over — this immaculate, self-erasing, Pace-involved Randi — is, per the marketing, going to be the one doing the steering. That reframes everything and I'm not going to let it, yet; I'll let the pages get there. But it sits in me as dread.

What I want next: I want to meet Vee, and I half-dread it, because the jacket says Randi will hand her to Pace, and I've now watched Randi be a person who can smile through anything. I want to know whether the thing that opened on the bench stays sealed or leaks. And I want, honestly, for someone to be tender to Randi in a way she can actually receive — while suspecting the book has no intention of granting that, and might be showing me precisely why she can't. Guess from here: the "game between two people" is Randi and Pace, and this immaculate morning-after control is exactly the instrument she'll turn on Vee. I dread that and I'll follow it.
