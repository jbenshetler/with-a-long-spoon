## Water Wings — Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one caught me off guard by how much it softened me. After the run I've just been through — Pace on the bench taking Randi apart and Randi sealing herself back behind the face, the pointing game, the engineered "tutor" meet-cute — I braced for more machinery, and instead I got a girl at a pool with her actual friend, and it undid me a little. No Pace. No Randi. Just Vee and Cassie and chlorine, and it's the warmest, cleanest air the book has let me breathe.

The thing I felt first, before any thought, was the ache of Vee's body being a problem she can't set down. "It fit, and it gave her nowhere to hide." Then the bleachers open up — "You won't need floaties with *those*!" and "Legs for *days*" — and I felt the recoil in my own chest, the fifty yards of held breath she walks through with her chin level and the towel carried "down at her side — not up against her," practicing not-hiding while being jeered at for having a body. This is the same surveillance Randi lived under in Standards, the same gaze, and I'm starting to feel it as the book's weather: these women are always being graded, and the grading comes dressed as compliment or concern.

And then the underwater passage, which is just lovely. "The room switched off." "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." I went all the way under with her. That she *grieves, a little* down there — for the sixteen-year-old in the green-and-white suit, for a body that used to just be hers — landed as the truest interior beat in the whole stretch. It's the one place in this book so far where nobody wants anything from her. After watching Pace read Vee's *wanting* off her like weather and Randi turn her warmth on and off like a tap, a scene where Vee is unwatched and unwanted felt like mercy.

**Cassie.** She's the reason this chapter works on me, and she deepened enormously here. Before this she was four words a day, a ballcap, and a flat "Goodbye, Vee" that landed a stair-step wrong. Here I got her whole loving logic without her ever raising her voice: the plain navy suit worn because "it had a job to do," the wading in only to her ribs — "This is as far as Cassie goes, and the water knew it" — and above all the splash. She doesn't ask what's wrong, because "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger"; she shoots a low sheet of water across ten feet to make Vee mad enough to come back into her body. She *could* have pitched the comeback up into the bleachers — "she had the lungs" — and she kept it for Vee. That's tenderness I trust completely, and it's the exact opposite of the warmth Randi and Pace deal in. If those two are the devil's dinner, Cassie is the one person at the table who isn't eating.

There's a flicker in Cassie I'm holding open, not closing: "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman." I can read it as pure dry putdown, and mostly it plays that way. But it sits next to the earlier detail that a certain kind of look "slid off Cassie every day of her life," and next to how precisely Cassie named who Pace was looking at and who he wasn't. Something in Cassie may love Vee more than platonically, or may simply see clearest of anyone what's closing in. The page leaves it ajar and I'll leave it ajar — I'm not going to pin a feeling on her the text didn't pin.

**Trust/suspicion.** None of it is aimed at anyone *in this chapter* — the two people in the water are the safest pair the book has. My dread is all imported from the wings: I now know Vee was Randi's pick on the quad and Pace's engineered target in the coffee shop, and Vee knows none of it. So the effect of dropping this pure, unwatched scene right here is that I ache for her *more*, not less. This is what's about to be reached for. Cassie's odd flat "Goodbye" from two chapters back rings louder now — I think Cassie sees the shape of what Randi is, and she's the one being edged out of the frame.

**Swoon / pull.** Not romantic swoon — there's no lover here. But the chapter genuinely swept me: the underwater quiet, "something in her chest that had stood at attention all afternoon quietly sat down," and the splash-fight where "you can't grieve and fight your best friend for a mouthful of pool at the same time; the splashing won." I rooted for Vee to get one hour where her body could "wait up on the deck with the rolled towel." I wanted her to keep it. I'm afraid she won't.

**Erotic charge.** Essentially nil, and rightly — this isn't that kind of scene. There's a faint undertow in "the love of a woman," but nothing on the page is working erotically, and I didn't want it to be. It's a breath between the heat, and it earns its place by giving me Vee's insides.

**Friction.** Almost none; the prose is controlled and un-showy and I believed every line. The one place I felt a faint thumb was "They will never know the love of a woman" — it does a little signaling work above its weight, and I noticed the noticing. But it's also just something a dry, protective friend would say, so I'm not marking it as a tell, only as a line I paused on.

**The titles.** "Water Wings" is the catcall — "You won't need floaties with *those*." So the chapter is named after the ugliest thing said in it, which is a small cruelty until you turn it over: Vee is the one person in that pool who needs no floaties, who belongs in water, who's held by it without anything holding her. The heckler meant her body would float her; the chapter means the opposite — the water carries her *free* of the body. And there's a third turn: water wings are for children, for being kept safely up — and Cassie, wading only to her ribs, keeping the joke for Vee, is Vee's water wing, the thing that keeps her up. The title starts as a jeer and the chapter quietly reclaims it. Nicely done.

My packet gave me no jacket and no tagline, so I can't react to cover copy — I only have the book title, ***A Polite Invitation***, and the series title, ***With a Long Spoon***. After Substitution both landed hard. "A Polite Invitation" is Pace's whole method — "*May I* call you," the froyo, the courtesy that Vee hears as belonging to another vocabulary; politeness as the wrapper the wanting comes out in (his own phrase from the pointing game). And "With a Long Spoon" I can only hear through the proverb — sup with the devil and bring a long spoon. So the series title is telling me straight out that these lovely dinners are a devil's table and the safe move is *distance* — which makes this pool hour, the one place with no devil in it, feel like the calm before. It points me toward Vee walking, warm and unarmed, toward exactly the thing you're supposed to keep a long spoon between yourself and.

**What I want / dread next.** I want more Cassie, and I dread her being written out of Vee's days as Randi and Pace close the circle. *Guess from here:* the froyo date from The Long Way goes well, Randi keeps "choosing" Vee, and the two of them converge on her from both sides while Cassie watches from ten feet off, unable to shoot a splash big enough to break it. I hope I'm wrong about how dark it goes. I don't think I am.

- *Water / floating / weightlessness* — first sighting; the chapter's central image, opposed to the bench's fixed, exposed rig.
- *Cassie's flatness as love* — the not-asking, the ballcap, wading only to her ribs; **repeat/deepen** of her spare register ("Goodbye, Vee").

**Symbolism:** The water as the one un-taken part of Vee — a self prior to the body-as-object, invited by the page itself ("before she had a body to be a problem"). The dark stripe on the pool bottom where the shallow gives out, crossed deliberately, reads as a threshold. Water wings themselves — the title — as the tension between being kept up (childhood, safety, Cassie) and needing nothing to hold you (Vee's real competence).

**Characterization:** Cassie deepened the most — from cipher to the book's clearest source of unpurchased love, her care rendered entirely as action. Vee deepened — her body-shame, her mother's installed voice, and a pre-object self I hadn't seen. Nobody flattened; nobody served a plot gear, because the plot machinery (Pace/Randi) stayed offstage, which is exactly what let these two breathe.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Short and well-judged; it doesn't overstay. The catcall-to-water-to-splash arc earns its length, and the underwater beat gets just enough dwell to land without turning purple.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate, welcome change of key after five chapters of convergence machinery — a held breath. It sags nowhere; if anything its calm sharpens the dread, because it's the first scene in a while with no predator in the room. Momentum is *held*, not built, and that feels intentional.
