## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one just melted me, and I'm going to say that plainly before I let the part of me that knows too much get a word in.

It's a quiet chapter, three small movements, and almost all the heat is in the rearview — the sex happens in memory, "unhurried, the wine still in both of them," a week of wanting spent at the door before they made the kitchen. So it isn't a scene that works my body; it's a scene that works my chest. And it lands. The thing I keep circling is Vee lying on her side in the cooling sheet, watching him sleep, and *deciding* — "She loved him. There." She doesn't get to say it out loud, or she does, but only to the sleeping shape of him: *"Peter."* Barely the breath of it. That undid me a little. She reached for his real name — the one he gave her by the fire, the one she keeps like a secret about a person she's decided to keep — and set it against his ear where it costs her nothing and he can't answer. I ached for her cleanly there.

And then the passage I'll carry: she "counted all the ways he'd said it without saying it: the door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the warm tomatoes, the coming back to her." That's the redundancy idea made tender — say it enough different ways and the noise can't kill the meaning. She reads his devotion off a dozen wordless channels because he's never once used the one channel that would settle it. "He felt it in everything he did and said it in nothing." As a swoon it's real; as a thing I know more about than she does, it's the exact ache of the whole book — because *silence* is also where the plan lives, and she's building a case for love out of a man who withholds the words. The page gave me the swoon straight. The dread is mine, imported. I want to be honest about which is which: the chapter itself earned almost no suspicion. It earned the melt.

The drawer sequence is the best thing here. She finally brings the bag and her whole "disordered self" colonizes his one clean counter — the razor squared to the edge, and now her spilled pins and open cream all over it. She braces for having been *too much*. The next Friday the scrunchies are gone and the bottom drops out of her morning: "He'd minded after all." And then she opens the drawer and it's *hers* — her scrunchies, her pins in a little dish, room left over "for more of her, laid out like something prepared and waiting" — and below it his whole ordered life "pressed in and fitted too tight," folded down into half its space. "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead." I felt that land in my throat the way it lands in hers. It's the made-thing-with-no-tag again — the flannel, the dress, the bench — a labor of devotion with the label cut out, so she has to *find* the love rather than be told it. No note. There is never a note.

That "never a note" is where my two feelings sit right on top of each other. It's the most romantic gesture in the book so far, and it's also, structurally, the same move every gift has been: something built to hold her, offered without a word that could be pinned down or held against him. I don't think the page is winking at me about that. I think it's just letting the gesture be beautiful. But I can't fully un-know that a man executing something also gives a drawer, and that "he never said" is doing double work.

**Pace** — my feeling didn't move so much as deepen along the grain it was already on. Tenderer than ever here: the salmon and warm tomatoes, the coming back to her an hour later "like she was something he'd been turning over the whole time," the drawer. Nothing on this page asks me to distrust him. Everything I carry does. That gap is the whole experience of reading him now.

**Vee** — I love her more for the weight-loss detail than for anything erotic. She eats "like a field hand at his table now," she who spent years pushing a plate away, and her *clothes have gone looser* — her body "quietly burning down whole weekends of him," the bras keeping the secret. The appetite motif turned inside out: fed at last, and shrinking, and glad, and not having asked for any of it. There's something almost frightening in "she'd never asked it to" — her body deciding things without her again, the way it decided in the dark all week in the last chapter. She's happy, "happier than she'd known a person was let to be," and I believe it and flinch at the phrasing — *let to be*, the old mother-voice sizing the ceiling on joy.

No one else is on the page. No Randi anywhere — a real relief after the brunch chapter, where I could barely breathe for how thoroughly Randi worked her. This is the one clean-lit room in a while, and I let myself sit in it.

**Erotic charge:** low and warm, not the point. The chapter is post-coital afterglow and morning intimacy; the actual sex is summarized ("some nights he started with his mouth… it always came around to her first"). It simmers rather than burns, and that's the right choice — this is a love chapter, not a heat chapter.

**Friction:** almost none. The one place the prose lingers a half-beat long is the bra/racks explanation — "the racks had never once believed a build like hers and her budget had never once stretched to the tailor who would." Gorgeous, but it explains the loose-clothes fact a touch more thoroughly than it needed to. Tiny.

**The titles.** "Above Him" reads three ways and I like that it holds all three. Literally, she's on her side *above* his sleeping body, saying his name down at him. Then the drawer: he gave her the *top* drawer, "the easy one, the one your hand goes to first," and folded his own things into the one below — she is, now, physically above him in his own house. And there's the small power that's been surfacing since last chapter — she found she was faster than him, watched the wall flinch, pantsed him — a woman getting, for the first time, some inch of the upper hand on a man who has led everything. The title illuminates rather than gives away; it only turns faintly ironic under what I know — she feels ascendant, chosen, *on top*, and I know she was chosen the other way. **"A Polite Invitation"** and **"With a Long Spoon"** are the shadow over all of this: a long spoon is what you sup with when you sup with the devil, and a drawer cleared out and made ready is nothing if not an invitation, courteous, into a house. This chapter is the invitation looking most like pure welcome. I was not given a cover tagline in this packet, so I've nothing to weigh there.

**What I want / dread next.** I want, badly and against my better sense, for the drawer to just *be* a drawer — for the tenderness to be the meaning and not the bait. Guess from here: the very completeness of this — love named in her chest, a place made for her — feels like the top of an arc, and the series title tells me what tops of arcs are for. I dread that the next turn brings Randi, or the plan, or the thing Vee still can't see, right back into this room she thinks is finally just hers. But I'm not going to pretend the chapter cast that shadow itself. It didn't. It handed her a drawer, and I'm glad she has it.
