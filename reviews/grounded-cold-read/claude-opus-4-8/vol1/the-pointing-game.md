## The felt read

I finished this one with my pulse up and my skin cold at the same time, and I want to be honest about both, because the chapter earns both and won't let me keep them separate.

First, the body. The opening worked on me — genuinely. He comes up over her "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing," slides in "before he kissed her," kisses her with his mouth still wet from her and she "took herself off his lips and kissed him back harder for it." That's good, warm, wet writing, and the second orgasm — the one the *question* pulls out of her, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — is one of the more genuinely erotic things I've read in a while, because it's an orgasm made of *wanting a thing she won't let herself want*, arriving ahead of her defenses. The heat here isn't friction, it's exposure, and that's exactly the register I read for. The taste-kiss on the walk — him pointing at the library girl, then turning and kissing her mouth without having wiped his face, and "he knew the moment her own taste reached her because her breath caught" — I felt that one in my stomach.

And the pointing game itself is, on its surface, delicious. The follow-and-lead metaphor from their dancing threaded all through it — "a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves, weighting forward, already leaning into a turn that hasn't been called yet" — and then the payoff, "the follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step." Her going from offended propriety (he's *pointing*, in daylight, at a stranger) to "she'd stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." That escalation is beautifully paced. I was swept up in the craft of it. When Vee finally comes out of the dining hall — the burning-copper hair, the "lush in a way that had nothing curated about it," the head thrown all the way back laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — and Randi's loose walk just *arrests mid-step*, and the bare "Yes" arrives "with nothing around it" — I felt the whole afternoon land. And the collision! The engineered shoulder-bump executed so cleanly that it's *Vee* whose hand flies to her mouth, *Vee* apologizing. "A decade of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually taught... folded down into three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing." I grinned with her. Her feet doing "a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do." The squeaked "*Yes.*" She's lit up and I was lit up for her.

Now the cold. I cannot read this chapter naively as a swoon, because I've just come off *The Bench* and *Standards*, and I know what Pace does not: she has already decided, in his bathroom mirror, that the one he found is "going back behind [the face], and she was not coming out again. Not tonight — and this arrived without words, quiet as a deadbolt going over — not ever." So every tender thing he does here sits on top of a woman who has privately resolved to give him only the mask. And this chapter is Pace, entirely inside his own warm competence, *reading her more truly than she reads herself and using it to steer a third person into her life.* "He had decided this beforehand" — sliding into her while she's undefended. He asks the taste question specifically to catch her body's answer before her mind can dress it. He chooses the retaining wall "fast and without seeming to, for the angle it gave on the doors." He orchestrates the meeting and then stands at a light pole "far enough back to be nobody" and *watches.* The prose keeps calling it a game, a gift, gentleness — "she deserved that gentleness. He wanted it for her" — and I believe he believes it. That's what unsettles me most: the page holds his self-conception completely intact. He isn't twirling a mustache. He thinks he's the most generous man alive. And he's manufacturing a person's entire desire-life like a woodworker fitting a joint.

So where does that leave my trust? It's the first time in these three chapters I've actively distrusted the *point of view* — not because he's cruel on the page (he's scrupulous, he checks in, he respects closed doors, the Daphne memory from Bench even shows him having learned restraint) but because his tenderness is indistinguishable, from the inside, from control, and he cannot tell the difference. And the thing being handed toward Randi's hands is a *real girl with a name and a stats schedule.* Vee walks on for two paragraphs and I already ache a little for her — she's the one alive, unmanaged, unguarded creature in a chapter full of curated women, the single sugar maple "burning orange in a field of things that had not yet decided to change," and she's just been selected, bumped, and befriended by two people running a game she has no idea exists. That's the unease the page truly earned, not a shadow I brought.

I'll hold one thing ajar, though, the way the page does: Randi's own *yes* is not only performance. Her body meant it twice before her mouth could move it. So this isn't simply a woman being puppeted into something she doesn't want — she wants it, helplessly, and the horror and the heat are the *same fact*, which is the line I read for. What she'll do with wanting it — given that she's decided never to be seen again — I genuinely can't call, and the chapter is right not to tell me.

Erotic charge: peaks at the opening (the question-orgasm) and the mid-walk taste-kiss; goes appropriately clothed-simmer through the pointing, never slack. It never went clinical for me.

Friction: almost none. If I reach, the interiority occasionally over-narrates his mastery — "he was watching her learn that she could," "she was shopping," "the follow had felt the music and broken frame" — a few of these tip from *shown* toward *the author confirming Pace read her correctly*, and because we're locked in his flattering self-view, a touch less confirmation would let me distrust him more actively on my own. But it's a small thumb, and the chapter is mostly disciplined.

The titles. **The Pointing Game** is exactly what it says and also worse than it says on the second thought — it's a children's-game lightness ("I point. You pick.") laid over a man selecting a woman for a woman, and by the end "pointing" has become literal aiming: "'Her,' Pace said, and pointed." Innocuous on the surface, cold on reread. **A Polite Invitation** (the volume) now rings loudly — this whole afternoon *is* a polite invitation, extended so courteously that the person being invited (and the person being pointed at) can't see the shape of what they're saying yes to. **With a Long Spoon** — I wasn't given a tagline or jacket copy in this packet, so I'm reading the series title cold, but "he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon" surfaces on its own here, and I don't yet know who's supping with whom: Randi thinks she's holding Pace at spoon's length behind her face; Pace thinks he's gently feeding her; Vee is about to be invited to a table she didn't know was set. The spoon points three ways and I can't yet say whose it is.

What I want next: I want Vee's chapter — I want out of Pace's head and into the one person on that quad who was just *acted upon* without knowing it. And I dread, a little, watching Randi try to hold "never coming out again" against a wanting her own body keeps confessing, with a real red-haired girl now standing where the wanting can reach her. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the "threesome, with you" reframe she performed — handing the appetite to Pace so it isn't *hers* — is going to be the fault line, and Vee is going to be a person, not a gift, in a way that breaks the game's tidy geometry.
