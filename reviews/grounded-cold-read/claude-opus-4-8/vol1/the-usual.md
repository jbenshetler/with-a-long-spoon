# Cold read (grounded) — The Usual

*scene: scenes/the-usual.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · memory: ck-ch040 + raw ch041..ch047 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

After three chapters of Vee alone in the cold and Randi alone in Gstaad, both of them lying awake inventing the other's fabulous life, this chapter walked me straight into the one head I'd been most starved for and least likely to be given: **Pace's.** And the first thing it did was answer, quietly and completely, the exact thing Vee spent her whole Christmas terror getting wrong. She lay in her twin bed building a blonde with a model's body to put at his stove; and here he is, in a double-wide diner eleven miles from anywhere, missing her so plainly that a woman who wants nothing off him takes one look and says *from this seat, that feels pretty shitty.* I ached for both of them across that gap. She's inventing his infidelity; he's sitting in griddle-smelling dark saying *I want Vee. I miss her.* That dramatic irony is the sweetest and cruelest thing the chapter has, and it landed hard.

**Pace, up close.** This is the first sustained time inside him, and he deepened enormously. What I got: a man who pays out to everyone exactly what they want — *to be wanted, or to have a problem solved* — without ever letting the cost show, and who needs Sheri precisely because she's the one person who wants none of it. That's a real, load-bearing piece of him I didn't have before. And his central wall got named for the first time from outside, with no cushion: *She wants a declaration. She wants me to say it.* — *Is that so hard?* — and then he does the thing Vee felt him do, lays up reasons **brick on brick**, every one true, "and she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something." Sheri won't take the bricks: *She didn't ask you for reasons. What is it you want?* — *I want Vee.* — *So say it.* — *I can't.* — *Then not saying it matters more to you than having her.* He looks down. There's nothing to argue with. I have never trusted the machinery under this man, and yet watching him lose this exchange, I felt for him like I hadn't yet.

But here's where I stayed honest instead of just melting: **the chapter refuses to tell me why he can't.** "I can't" is a closed door, and I don't get to see behind it. And I can't unknow what I know — that there's a plan, and there's Randi, "the brunette" Sheri names as casually as she names the redhead. He won't *lie* (*Most men'd just lie* — *I'm not them*), and I keep circling the thought that maybe *I love you* would be a lie he refuses to tell while Randi and the game still exist — that the honesty grammar and the withholding are the *same* thing, not opposites. That's a **guess from here**, and the page leaves it genuinely ajar; I'm not going to nail it shut. But it's why his "I can't" reads to me as both wound and evasion at once, and why the warmth of this chapter never fully bought my trust.

**Sheri** is the gift I didn't expect. In my memory she was blunt, watchful, a person who could burn you — here she's given a whole interior and she's wonderful: queer, estranged from her people, still raw over **Paige**, the design-school girl who *kept picking at it — call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas.* "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." She's the mirror to Pace's own I-don't-go-home, and quietly to Vee's whole aching Christmas, and to Randi eating her revulsion at that breakfast table — this entire stretch of the book is people exiled from where they came from, performing a face over it. Sheri wears too much makeup slightly off, *like she'd dare you to mention it*, and I loved her immediately. Crucially she's the clean outside witness: she knows Pace has two women, she reads it as plain two-timing, not a coordinated game, and she thinks Vee's complaint is dead simple and Pace is being a coward about it. Having someone *say that on the page* was a release.

**Swoon / pull.** Not romantic swoon — this is two friends demolishing two cheeseburgers — but the pull is real and it's ache. I wanted him to just *say it*, felt Sheri's exasperation as my own, and then got disarmed by the turn into warmth: *the philosophy of your food. Lord almighty. Get over yourself.* — and he laughs, "the first full one of the night," and I laughed too. His attempt at the true shape of it — *The air's different when she's in the room. It — crackles* — and Sheri flattening it, *You mean the sex is good*, and him holding the line, *That's not what I meant. But, yes. It is* — that's the most likeable he's ever been to me. It's also, I noticed, the closest he'll come to a declaration: he can say all of it to Sheri and none of it to Vee.

**Erotic charge.** Basically nil, and honestly right for the chapter. What heat there is is comic body-talk — *that backside*, *those long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you* — Sheri needling, Pace going red and pushing on anyway. It's charming, not arousing, and I didn't want it to be more.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No suspicion of Sheri at all — she's the truth-teller of the scene. My unease sits entirely on the unexplained "I can't," and it's *earned* unease, not on-guard reflex: the page deliberately withholds his reason the same week it lets him ache for Vee, and I can't help hearing Randi in the silence.

**Friction.** Very little. The opening is the one oblique spot — *he would not bring her down today* and *whatever he'd carried in from the truck could keep* — I couldn't fully pin whether "her" is Vee-the-subject he's deciding not to drag into the evening (which is how it plays out) and what's literally in the truck. I let it stay ambiguous and it didn't cost me. One faint thumb: *Everyone he knew wanted something off him... He'd come in tonight needing it more than he'd have said* — a hair explain-y, but it earns itself in the next beat.

**The title.** *The usual* is the waitress's line — *already writing, because there was no question and never had been.* On the surface it's the coziest thing in the book: an established, unweighted, unquestioned belonging, a thing so settled you don't have to name it. Which is the exact ache-mirror of Vee, who is starving to know *what she is*, whether *boyfriend* is even a word with business near it. Pace **has** "the usual" — with Sheri, the relationship that needs no declaration — and cannot hand Vee the settled named thing she's begging for. The title also, a little coldly, brushes the fact that Pace himself has a *usual*: two women, a pattern. Warm on the surface, aching underneath — it did its job and gave nothing away before I read it. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* keep pointing where they've pointed: the courteous engineered offer, and *he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon* — dangerous company kept at courteous arm's length. This chapter, with Pace unable to say the one true word while the game sits unspoken under him, felt like the long spoon in his own hand. (No cover tagline was included in my packet, so I've nothing to hold it against.)

**What I want / dread next.** I want the term to start so Vee gets Randi back (her one circled day) and I dread what Randi does with a Vee who arrives cracked-open and un-named. I want to know *why he can't* — the page owes me nothing but I'm hungry for it. And I'm bracing for the collision between Pace's genuine, articulate, diner-confessed love and the fact that he'll walk back into his warm house and say none of it, while Vee decides at midnight that it's already over. Guess from here: the "I can't" is tangled in Randi and the plan, and whenever it finally gives, it won't give clean.

**Motifs & images:** *"The usual"* (first sighting, and the title) — the unquestioned, un-named belonging. **Cooking as building-then-giving** (repeat) — *the garlic in the oil... the plate set down only once she was aching* echoes Vee's own midnight recollection two chapters back and the whole food/appetite motif. **Feeding a single/double plate wrong and sliding them "back to true"** (first) — arithmetic of who gets what, faintly rhyming the stats-outlier thread. **Won't-go-home / estrangement** (repeat, and now a cluster) — Sheri's family, Paige's *call your mama*, mirrors Pace's own exile and the whole span's Christmas-away-from-home ache (Vee's twin bed, Randi's breakfast table). **The withheld word / "words are cheap, believe what I do"** (repeat) — now shown from his side as *I can't*. **The amber glass tabletop** (first) — the frozen mid-century family in matching wool, laughing past the frame; a preserved togetherness under glass.

**Symbolism:** The griddle-quiet Pace is *grateful for* vs. the jukebox someone always feeds — the Christmas song that *presses the bruise* — reads as the noise of the sentimental family world he and Sheri are both exiled from, arriving uninvited. And "the usual" itself: the named, settled thing Pace can serve a friend across a diner table and cannot give the woman he loves. The page invited both; I didn't have to manufacture them.

**Characterization:** Pace deepened the most he has all book — first real interior, the pays-out-without-showing-cost engine, the refusal to lie, and the flat unexplained wall of *I can't*. Consistent with everything, and the *why* is held back (restraint, or withholding from me too — I can't yet tell which). Sheri went from sharp cameo to a fully-grave person with her own grief; she landed clean and true. No one flattened.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Well-paced. Slow warm build through nothing-talk and food, the Paige confession earning the pivot, then Sheri's cornering as the taut center, then a genuine laugh to release it. Earned its length; nothing dragged.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate and welcome tonal turn after three interior chapters of ache — the POV swing to Pace answers Vee's Christmas terror directly and gives the reader relief she can't have. Momentum holds rather than spikes; it's the exhale in a run of held breaths, and it re-arms the central deadlock (the unsaid word) from the other side rather than repeating a beat.
