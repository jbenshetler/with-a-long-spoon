## Reader reaction

## The felt read

This one worked on me before I could get my guard up, and I'm going to be honest about that first, because it's the truest thing about the chapter.

**Vee.** God, I'm so far in with her. The whole ascent is her body being coaxed into wanting — the burning thighs, the hot spot on her heel, the hunger the hike *builds on purpose* ("The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for") — and by the time she gets to that summit and stops because "her body stopped her," I stopped too. The passage where the world falls open and "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it... and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it" — that landed hard on me. It's not just scenery. It's a girl who has spent her whole life being *watched* and *judged* by an internal voice, standing in front of something enormous that has no opinion of her at all, and finding that indifference is a mercy. That's the interior life I read for, and the chapter handed it to me clean.

And then the truck. The truck scene is the swoon. I want to be careful here because I'm supposed to name the swoon *when it's there* and not manufacture shade, and it's there. When she works out what he's done — gave her the one dry shirt *and* got out into the rain so she could change unwatched, "His shirt, and then the truck. Both" — I felt it in my chest. That's the gesture, and it's a real one. What makes the scene extraordinary is what she does with it: she doesn't take the privacy. She raps on the glass, crooks a finger, and undresses *while holding his eyes*, with her mother's voice screaming *floozy* the whole time — and the voice IS the heat, the shame and the wanting are the same current. "She looked down anyway, and his eyes went with hers." That's her taking the thing her whole life told her not to want and choosing it in the open, on her knees, on her terms. It's the most powerful thing she's done in the book. I was completely swept up.

The turn at the end of it — reaching for his hand to start "the next thing" and finding it *shaking*, finding he's been shivering the whole time out in the rain and then standing here — undid me a little. "He'd been a long way from the question of whether he was cold." She stops her own want to take care of him. That reversal, her tending him, felt like the truest tenderness in the chapter.

**Pace.** Here's where I have to hold two things at once, and the chapter earns both. On the page, alone, this is the most disarming he's been. The moleskin. Lacing the boot differently. The cherry pit — she spits it, doesn't know the rule, and instead of correcting her he just quietly lays his own pit beside hers on the wax paper "and didn't look at her doing it," so she learns it without being made small. The russet dress: she talks herself out of it, sets it "back on the shelf," and all he says is "That sounds like a beautiful dress," no weight on it, and *he doesn't pick it up again*. Every one of those is a man declining to make her small. I felt the pull of it exactly the way she does.

And that is precisely what keeps me from resting. I know what she doesn't — that this same flawless attentiveness is the instrument. I keep waiting for the con to show its seams and it *doesn't*, which is worse, not better. The dress especially sits in my gut: a man who now knows, to the exact shade of brick-red, the one thing she wants and can't afford. On the page it's tenderness. In my memory it's a data point being filed. I can't tell you which it is, and the chapter refuses to tell me either, and I think that's deliberate and I'm going to leave it ajar the way it left it ajar. The shivering hand is what saves my sympathy for him — you can fake a gesture, it's harder to fake being genuinely cold and undone. But I've learned in this book not to trust exactly the moment I most want to.

**The cop.** That ending. The siren "took the rest of her sentence with it" — she was about to say the big thing and got a squawk of interruption instead, and he's pulled away into the rain with lights behind him before either of them could land it. As a beat it's almost funny (parked in the fire lane) and then it's not, because it robbed them of the one thing the whole day was building toward — her *saying* it. I felt cheated of it right alongside her, which I think is the point.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new tell from the *text* this chapter — nobody lied, nothing cracked. My wariness is entirely carried in from what I already know, and the chapter is smart enough not to feed it cheaply. If anything it starves my suspicion on purpose and lets the sweetness run, which is its own kind of unnerving. The one thing I'll name: the title.

**The title — "Leave No Trace."** On the surface it's the hiking ethic, and the chapter gives it to me straight and gentle: "We take nothing and leave nothing," the pit on the wax paper. Lovely. But the second I hold it against everything I know, it goes cold. *Leave no trace* is also what you'd want if you were running an operation on someone — no evidence, nothing she can point to later, every kindness deniable as just kindness. He carries out the crumpled paper. He carries out the pits. He gives her his shirt and stands in the rain and it all reads as pure care and leaves *no mark you could ever use against him*. The title didn't give the plot away, but it recolored the whole day for me on the walk back down — the perfect gentleness that leaves nothing behind is exactly the gentleness that can't be caught. That's the chill under a chapter that otherwise made me melt.

**Book / series titles.** *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — I don't have the tagline in my packet, so I won't invent one. But "sup with a long spoon" is the old line about dining with the devil, and this chapter is *literally a shared meal* handed across a rock, hunger built and then fed. The long spoon is the safe distance you're supposed to keep. Vee has no spoon at all — she's eating out of his hand, cherry by cherry. That's the dread the sweetness is riding on.

**Erotic charge.** Peaks hard in the truck and stays clothed the whole time, which is the achievement — it's a striptease that's really about being *seen*, and the eroticism is almost entirely in her shame turning into heat and her choosing to be looked at. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." That's the interior wanting rendered onto the page the way I most want it. It never went clinical. The food is nearly as erotic as the undressing — the horseradish ambush, the cherry acid-then-bitter — appetite and desire are the same appetite here and the prose knows it.

**Friction.** Very little. The fire-lane cop is a hair convenient as an interruption device — the exact instant she opens her mouth to say the big thing, the siren eats it — but it's set up (he "didn't take a real spot") and it stung rather than clunked, so I'll allow it. The repeated "Push and lift / Push and drop" refrains toe the line of over-doing but mostly earned their rhythm as the body learning the trail. Nothing felt like the author's thumb.

**What I want / dread next.** I want her to get to *say* the thing the siren took. I want to see that russet dress come back — and I dread it, because if it arrives, gift-wrapped, I'll know exactly what it means even if she doesn't. *Guess from here:* the dress is a planted seed and it'll return at the induction dinner, and the question is whether Pace gives it or Randi does, and either way it'll be the con wearing tenderness's clothes. I also dread the induction generally — a public room where her name is read at the front, in front of "finished women," feels like the kind of stage this book likes to put her on. Mostly I just want to keep reading, which is the honest headline.
