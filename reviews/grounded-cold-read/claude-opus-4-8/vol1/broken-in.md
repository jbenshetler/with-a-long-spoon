## Reader reaction

## Felt read

This chapter caught me off guard by how *tender* the recovery is and how much dread I felt the whole way through it anyway. After the wreck of "Lesson" — Vee tearing into Pace, then sitting in the ruin of it all night — I came into "broken-in" braced. And the opening beats are lovely, plain, unshowy: the cold water "went through her like a verdict," the sad-clown mascara she jokes at in the mirror, the apple from the bowl, the bare legs in October she decides to just *be*. I liked her here. She's climbing out of a shame-hole by her own hands, and the prose lets her have small dignities — fixing the clown, finding a bin for the apple core "like a person raised right." That "person raised right" self-narration is Vee's warmth exactly, and I felt genuine affection for her walking across that rinsed-blue morning "carrying something she didn't want to spill."

But then she goes to Randi's, and this is where my body split from Vee's. I felt her relief — the clenched thing "let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so" — and I did *not* trust it. That line is the whole chapter for me. Vee's peace is manufactured by someone I know is running her. Randi's reframe is genuinely brilliant seduction-craft: she takes Vee's worst hour and turns it into proof of love — "you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about," "you were so far ahead you couldn't see the race." It's kind, it's *true* enough to land, and it steers Vee exactly where the plan wants her. And then the tell I couldn't unsee: **"Not today." "Not a suggestion — an instruction, and Vee stopped."** Randi delays the reconciliation a full day — dresses it as pride-management ("he'll smell it on you") but she's *timing* it, staging it, and she even scripts Pace's non-response ("He won't text you... he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing"). She knows how he operates because she *lives* it. Vee hears a wise friend. I heard a stage manager blocking a scene. That gap is where the whole chapter did its work on me.

Which is the awful, effective thing: I was swept up *and* uneasy in the same breath, and I couldn't get the page to earn me out of either one. The dress-up sequence is warm and funny in a way I couldn't resist — the "Edna Mode" bit genuinely made me grin, "I have to see what I'm working with," the two of them "gone" laughing in the cold bright room, the small dark one and the tall red one. The froth is real froth. But then the froth keeps sharpening into the seduction under it, and I feel the hand on the tiller.

The erotic charge is the surprise of the chapter — I didn't expect heat *here*, in a shoe-fitting the morning after a fight over another man. But the shoe scene got me. Randi crouching, the long skirt falling over the work so Vee "couldn't see any of it, and there was only the feel — the cool quick hands somewhere down past the hem," the hand up under the skirt to the outside of her thigh, the thumb "pressing once into the soft hollow behind her knee on the way down, and Vee's breath did something she hoped wasn't out loud." That's the exact grammar of the fitting with Pace — kneeling, tending, being looked at, involuntary arousal she can't quite hide — transposed onto Randi, and *Vee still won't look at it.* "something moved in her face, there and gone, that Vee didn't have a name for and was too happy to go looking for." The book keeps handing me Vee's want-she-won't-see, and here it's dialed up: she's aroused by a woman kneeling at her feet and files it under a nice morning. It worked on me and unsettled me at once — which is, I think, the register the whole book is playing in.

The word-play made me flinch in a good way. "Shoe whore" as an endearment, "the worst words always did, between them" — that's the mother's *floozy* being detoxed and handed back as love, and Randi's the one doing it. And the "scarf... criminal on you" echoes Randi in "Gone" saying "It's *criminal*, Pace. What she keeps under there." Randi used that exact word to Pace *in bed, about Vee's body*. Here she says it to Vee's face about a scarf. I don't think Vee can hear the double, but I did, and it went cold through me.

The end — the kiss on the mouth in the foyer, and Vee "caught up to something a half-beat late: she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed... It was only what they did" — that's the seduction's whole progress in one image. She's stopped flinching. The thing that used to need explaining ("a French thing") is now just habit. And "See you soon, gorgeous." I felt the trap tighten and I felt Vee happy inside it, both fully.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** My suspicion of Randi isn't manufactured here — the checkpoint told me she's Pace's lover mining Vee, and this chapter *shows* it in her timing and her scripting of Pace's behavior. She knows too much and manages too precisely. But — and this is the thing I'll hold open — her fondness reads real *too*. "Gone" already told me she can't get bored of Vee, that Vee "got past me before I could help it," and here the warmth doesn't feel faked, it feels like a woman enjoying her mark and genuinely liking her. Both. I don't fix it more than the page does.

**Friction:** Very little. Once, "Vee would have walked into traffic for the woman who said *dinner in Paris* about her" — a touch cute, but it's Vee's own voice, so I let it go. Brooke's "the house had a folder on her anyway" is a nice chill — the machine's reach — though I noticed the checkpoint had Brooke as Randi's chapter *president*, and the page here has Vee place her as "Randi's sister from the smoothie shop." A small wobble I read as Vee's own imprecise memory, not a real snag.

**Titles:** "broken-in" — before the chapter I'd have guessed boots, dance floor, wearing something till it fits. After: it's the *shoes*, worn/held/steadying, "a structure that took her foot and *held* it, told it where to be... Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." And on the reread-turn it's *Vee* being broken in — to the shoes, to the not-bracing at Randi's mouth, to being led. That last line — no flinch — is the sole of the whole title. It gave a little away once I saw it, and I didn't mind. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / the closing tagline **"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem."** — that tagline is *loud* over this chapter. Every yes here is Vee's: the shoes she'll wear, the plan to fix it tomorrow, the kiss she meets without bracing. All freely given. All arranged. "With a long spoon" — you sup with the devil with a long spoon — sits right under Randi crouched at Vee's feet.

**What I want / dread:** I want the reconciliation at Pace's door — badly, because Vee wants it and the dance itself was real and good. But I dread it being another staged set-piece, walked-in-on-cue, Randi's blocking. *Guess from here:* tomorrow night at his door is going to be a high, and the higher it goes the more I'll feel the drop the jacket keeps promising. And the shoe-fitting's un-named heat makes me think Randi is the next threshold, not Pace.
