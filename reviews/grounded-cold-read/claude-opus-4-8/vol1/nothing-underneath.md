## Felt read

I came into this chapter wrung out on Vee's behalf. That whole recent run — the crackers-and-meal sentence he didn't answer, the Chili's disaster, the "Believe Me" walkout, then the long cold New Year's split-screen — had me aching for her and genuinely unsure the man would ever open his mouth. So the first thing I have to say plainly is: this chapter swept me up. It made me melt and then it made me nervous about how easily I melted, which is exactly the sick-sweet register this book keeps hitting on purpose.

The mirror scene is the heart of it and it's the best thing in the chapter. Watching Vee dress herself into an offering — the underwear drawer sliding shut on "the neat colored rows of everything not invited," "last thing, only thing" about the heels, the frosted plum toenails she kept up "over Christmas in her old bedroom for a man she'd no longer been sure would ever see them again" — I was completely with her. And then the long, deliberate self-touch, building and denying herself because "it was not hers to finish," wanting to "carry all of it out into the cold unspent and put it in his hands wound exactly this tight" — that landed as hot *and* as the real turn of her arc. This is the girl who spent since fourteen "keeping it folded small" finally taking "all the too-much of herself... and finally use it for the one thing it had always been for." The heat is high and it's *earned* by months of shame; it isn't decoration. Erotic charge peaked here and again on the doorstep, and it did not go slack.

I also just plain laughed, more than once. The drawer overruling her own hands. Kayla with the laundry basket mourning her underwear while Vee stands there "wearing not one stitch of the underwear Kayla was mourning." And the cop — the blue-and-red coming up behind her while her hand is between her legs on the county road — which is a lovely, wicked rhyme with the campus cop who broke up the truck night back on the mountain. That fright "wound tighter for the scare" is the book teasing her (and me) one more denial before the door.

The reunion itself: the wall coming down "all at once," him looking at her "like a starving man, and... like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — I felt that in my chest. The door banging open in the wind, neither of them shutting it, him going to his knees on the cold boards, carrying her three hobbling steps toward a bed a "country away" and giving up on the couch — graceless, "which was the truth of them tonight." It's the least composed I've seen him and I loved him more for it. And the aftercare undid me a little: "Those stay on," the midnight pancakes made-not-poured, and "I missed you so much," and his voice cracking and not coming back. That crack is the closest he has ever come.

**But here is where I stay honest, because the page earned this too.** He still didn't say the word. She drove out there because she could not get an answer to "what am I to you," and the chapter *resolves that question by dissolving it* — "tonight there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." That's not him answering. That's her setting the question down and calling the fullness enough. And I cannot un-know what I watched in the previous chapter: this entire coat-and-nothing-underneath plan came out of **Randi's** mouth, coached line by line, right down to "you've got the goods, gorgeous girl." Randi — his co-architect, the woman who staged the whole collision, the woman I just watched sit hollow in a perfect hotel room in Gstaad realizing she's in love with Vee — handed Vee the exact script that walks her back into the arrangement. So the reconciliation that melts me is *also* the machine self-correcting: the one time Vee pushed at the truth, she's been soothed off it, by the handler, with her own body as the peace offering. The warmth in this chapter is real — I don't think the tenderness on the couch is faked *within the scene*. The dread is structural, and the text absolutely earned it by showing me the strings last chapter.

**Feelings that moved:** Toward Vee, deeper tenderness and real admiration — she's funny and brave here in a way she wasn't allowed to be, and the reclaiming of her body is genuinely joyful to read. Toward Pace, my split widened rather than closed: the cracked voice moved me, and yet he took everything she offered and *still* wouldn't spend the one word, and I noticed the scene lets his body answer so completely that the withheld word almost stops registering — which is precisely what should worry me. Randi's off the page but her fingerprints are on every button of that coat, and after Gstaad I feel for her and fear her in the same breath. Cassie's one raised eyebrow and "Hmm" at the end is the sane world clocking exactly what happened and declining to bless it — I trust her read completely.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** earned, not free-floating. My unease sits on the fact that Vee "won him back" by executing Randi's design and surrendering her only real question. No suspicion of the tenderness itself — of the frame around it.

**Friction:** almost none. The interior monologue occasionally re-treads the same "you've got the goods" beat a touch more than it needed, and the mirror sequence is long — but for me it earned its length as the pivot of her whole shame arc, so I won't dock it.

**The title.** "Nothing Underneath" is the wink and the wound at once. Literally: nothing under the coat. But the entire recent arc was Vee terrified there's *nothing underneath* his warmth — no name, no word, "the silence at the center where the word should be." Tonight she answers that fear not by finding out but by having nothing underneath and asking nothing more. It didn't spoil anything before I read (I already knew the plan), but on the far side it recolors hard: the bareness is her stopping *looking* for what's underneath him. That's a bleak reading sitting right inside a warm scene, and I think it's the true one.

**Book and series titles.** *A Polite Invitation* — the coat opening on the doorstep is the invitation made flesh, and "polite" is doing quiet, awful work given how much of this was arranged for her. *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." Vee has no long spoon; she just drove forty minutes to sup with her whole bare self, and I'm not sure she knows there's a devil at the table. My packet didn't include a cover tagline, so I've nothing to react to there.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here):** I want Vee to keep this reclaimed body-joy — the confidence is the best thing that's happened to her. I dread that the reconciliation being *Randi-authored* means the next move belongs to the plan, not to Vee, and that the bench (still sitting in that bedroom, still a "low table" to her) is closer now that she's been handled back in. And I keep waiting for the collision of the two women's real feeling — Randi coaching Vee into Pace's bed while herself aching for Vee is a fuse, and I don't think it burns clean.
