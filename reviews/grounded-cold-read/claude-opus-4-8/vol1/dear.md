## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one worked on me quietly, and then it made me nervous — and both of those are the chapter's doing, not my guard being up. It's a Vee chapter, all daylight and warmth, a girl spilling her good news over smoothies, and I read most of it grinning right along with her. But I've read the earlier chapters. I've stood behind Pace at his bench and behind Randi at her mirror, and so I bring things into this room that Vee cannot possibly have, and the whole chapter runs on that gap. That's the flavor of it: Vee happy, me happy for her, and a thin cold line of unease coming in the window the way Cassie's crack of night air does.

Let me start with Vee, because I love her here. She's the most nakedly wantable of these people precisely because she's the least defended. Watching her tell the froyo — sorry, the dinner — story is watching a person hand her whole chest across a table: "The whole night went soft. I stopped keeping track of myself. I can't say it better than that." I felt that. I've been inside that dinner with her already; I know the softness was real, that the hand around hers "felt less like something beginning than something resolving." So when she reaches for the dignified version — "I made an exception" — and it comes out as "…Yeah. Because of him," I ached for her a little. Not because it's wrong. Because it's true, and she's giving the truest, most costly version of herself to Randi, of all people. The swoon and the dread are the same sentence.

Now — my feeling about Randi has moved hard since the earlier chapters, and this is where I have to be honest rather than on-guard. I don't think the page has fully damned her. But the page has armed me. I watched Randi at the bench become real, beg, cross something she couldn't uncross, and decide in a locked bathroom to go back behind the face and never come out again. I watched Pace hand her a want-for-a-woman she couldn't survive owning, and I watched her, on a walk across the quad, point that want at a redhead named Vee — no, wait. Let me hold what the page actually gave me. On the quad it was *Pace* who pointed and said "Her." Randi said "Yes." Vee is the woman from that afternoon. And now here is Randi, in a smoothie shop, pulling every true thing out of Vee with the gentlest little levers — "You let him choose for you. And you liked it," "Because you couldn't not. Because of him" — naming Vee's wanting for her, out loud, more precisely than Vee can name it herself. Reading that as innocent girl-talk is no longer available to me, and the text is the thing that took it away. Randi is *good at this*. I watched her run this exact warmth on Vee in the lecture hall and at Chi Latte. And I know what it's in service of, or at least I know a shape it could be in service of, because I sat in the bed while Pace asked the question and she moved the wanting onto him and made herself its "generous instrument."

So: trust vs. suspicion. I have real, earned suspicion of the *machine* here, and it's earned by prior chapters, not manufactured over this one. This chapter, taken purely on its own surface, is a lovely scene of two friends. But I can't read it on its own surface — I've been given a memory that recolors every warm beat. When Randi says "Vee's my dear friend" and takes her hand, cool and damp, in front of Brooke, I felt two things at once, cleanly split: Vee's version, which is real and moving — nobody has "chosen her out loud like that in a long time" — and my version, which noticed that Randi deployed the hand-hold at the precise social moment it would do the most work on Vee. Did it *cost* Randi something, as Vee believes? Maybe. The Standards chapter showed me Randi genuinely under Brooke's thumb, managing that room from a lower rung, so claiming a dorm-nobody as a "dear friend" in front of the chapter president might genuinely be a small expenditure of status. I'll hold that open. The page holds it open. Vee reads it as a gift freely given; I read it as possibly a gift, possibly a placement, and I don't get to close that, and I like that I don't.

What I want to flag as swoon, because it's real and I won't shortchange it: the Pace material Vee reports secondhand is doing a slow, warm number on me even at one remove. "May I. Not can I. Who even says that." I've now watched that "May I choose?" land in three registers — at the restaurant, in her retelling, and earlier as "May I call you?" — and it keeps working, the small courtly formality that reads as care rather than affectation. And "he goes about six years old, the way a little boy tells you about dinosaurs" — that's Vee handing over the thing she loves, and it made me like Pace more, and *that* is its own low-grade dread, because I've seen the bench, and I've seen him decide beforehand to enter Randi while she was still coming and undefended, and I've seen him say "good girl" at the exact moment a woman's real voice broke through. The tenderness is not fake. That's what unsettles me. It's real tenderness with an appetite underneath it that plans.

Cassie. I trust Cassie completely and I'm now actively afraid for what her absence means. The chapter opens by removing her — "I'll see you at the dorm," easy, already going — and it makes a small point of the look that goes past Vee to Randi and gets "folded away." Cassie is the one who said the flat, wrong, whole word "Goodbye, Vee" back in See You Later, the one who reads the look that "slid off Cassie every day of her life." Vee explaining Cassie's absence — "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one" — and feeling "something turn over, small, and she let it lie" is the most quietly alarming beat in the chapter for me. Vee is being drawn into a room that has no place for the one person who sees clearly. I dread that drift more than I dread any single thing Randi does.

Erotic charge: low and that's right — this is a clothed, social chapter. The heat is all reported and refracted (the dinner, the kiss "Because of him"), and it simmers rather than burns. Where it landed on me bodily was the retelling of the softness at the table, because I already have the full-body version in memory. The smoothie shop itself is anti-erotic on purpose — air conditioning, blenders, the wrong-size feeling — and that contrast is deliberate and effective.

Friction: almost none. The prose is doing its thing well. The one place I felt the thumb, lightly, was the recurrence of the high-window-stool image — "she had been set up on a shelf… a thing the room could turn and look at." It's the third bright-window-perch in the book (Chi Latte's high table, and here), and this time the narration spells the meaning out a touch more than it did at Chi Latte, where "that's where you sit if you belong here" was lighter. It didn't break anything; I just felt the motif being pressed. And "like a person things happened to. Good things." then "This had color in it" is the narrator naming Vee's uplift a hair more than Vee would — but it's close enough to her voice that I let it go.

The title. "Dear." Before the chapter I'd have guessed a letter — a salutation. After, it's plainly the hinge word: "Vee's my dear friend." And it does the double thing these titles apparently do. On the surface it's the warmest word in the chapter, the one that picks Vee out loud and gives her a place. On the reread-in-my-head it's cold: *dear* as in costly, dear as in what-a-thing-costs — Randi spending something, or placing something, and the word "dear friend" as the label being affixed to Vee like a hanger earning its place in that bone-to-oxblood closet. The title points straight at the hand on the table. It didn't give anything away I didn't already fear; it sharpened it. Clean, oblique, does its job.

The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the series, *With a Long Spoon* — I don't have the tagline in my packet (no jacket copy reached me), so I'll only work from the titles. "With a long spoon" I hear as the old line about supping with the devil: you sit at the table, but you keep your distance, you use a long spoon. Which is exactly the thing nobody in this book is managing — Randi went all the way down on the bench, Vee is kissing a man two days in and handing Randi her whole chest, and the long spoon is precisely the tool none of these hungry, portioned-out people can keep hold of. "A Polite Invitation" reads to me now like the *form* the danger takes here: everything arrives as courtesy. "May I choose." "We should all get dinner sometime." "See you later." "Vee's my dear friend." The invitations are all polite. That's what makes them hard to refuse and hard to see. So the titles feel like they're taking me toward a table already set, where the politeness is the trap and the appetite is the point, and I'm equal parts leaning in and bracing.

What I want and dread next: I want more of Vee's interior, and I want to see the hike — Pace's house, "off six-fourteen," the thing Cassie flagged with "is he rich." I dread the geometry closing: Pace pointed at Vee; Randi said yes; Randi is now the one running warmth on Vee daily. Guess from here, marked as a guess — I think Vee is being brought toward Pace and Randi both, and that the "threesome, with him" that Randi built as a survivable wrapper is being assembled around this specific girl, and that Vee doesn't know she's the woman who got pointed at on the quad. I could be wrong about the shape. But the two threads — Pace's daylight game and Randi's daylight warmth — are pointed at the same person now, and I can't unsee it. I'll follow it in, and I'll grieve Cassie being left on the deck with the rolled towel.
