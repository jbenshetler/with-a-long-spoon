## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one is a brunch, and I braced for the pattern — Randi mines the erotic detail, Vee climbs a slow ladder over a tablecloth until she has to bolt to the bathroom — because that's what the last two brunches did to me and to her. And the chapter knows I'm braced. It sets the table exactly the same way (mimosas already beading, the rim-touching rite, the prices read down the right-hand column first) and then quietly does something different, and the difference is the whole point of it. There's no scene to tell this week. "There was no dress, no swat, no afternoon that started one way and ended another. There was just — all of it." So instead of a discrete confession Randi can strip one "yeah" at a time, Vee tries to describe *being happy*, and I found that genuinely moving in a way the earlier extraction scenes were more unsettling than moving.

Because here's what got me: Vee, on her own, unprompted, finally names the thing. "You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*, and now you're not." That's Randi saying it, and God help me it landed as absolution even knowing whose mouth it comes from — "You're just finally hungry out loud." The whole book's hunger motif, the policed plate, the mother's voice, and here it gets said out loud and reframed as the one thing that finally went *right* in her, not wrong. And the yolk breaking over the Benedict right on that beat — "the middle, when it goes everywhere" — her hand going back for more before she'd decided anything. That's the appetite loosening, rendered in food, and it worked on me straight, no shadow. I was just glad for her.

The swoon, real and uncomplicated, is the middle stretch where she can't find a story because the happiness has no edges. "Like my mouth had been asleep since I was born and nobody told me. Like I'd been eating with the sound off." The sleeping — actually sleeping, whole nights, "I go down like somebody reached over and switched me off. I didn't know I *could*." That undid me a little. This is a girl who has been holding a face her whole life, and the thing she's marvelling at isn't the sex, it's that she can be *unguarded* — can float, can go under, can be quiet without hurrying to fill it. I ached for how astonished she is that this is allowed.

But the chapter earns its unease too, and precisely, not by manufacture. The tell is the moment Vee describes being wanted "with the body attached. Not — the other way around," and it *lands on Randi.* "Randi's eyes had gone bright and a little unfixed... a catch in her breath she didn't quite smooth over." And then: "It doesn't," Randi said. Low, and not quite even. That "it doesn't" stopped me cold. Vee reads it as the story reaching her friend, and feels *proud* — but I heard it as Randi hearing a description of being loved the way she is *not* loved, or the way she can't let herself be, or the way she gave up on the bathroom-locked night in chapter one when she decided Pace could have the face and never the real self. Vee is unknowingly describing, to Pace's other lover, the exact thing Randi walled herself off from having. That's the knife under the velvet this week, and it's Randi's own hurt, not a plot gear — it made me pity her more, not fear her more, which is the trap the checkpoint already flagged me for.

The photograph thread is the other place the chapter tightened on me. She shows the cropped one, then — because Randi's "you only gave him *half*?" makes her afraid of being filed as the girl who flinched — she hands over the *full* uncropped nude. "I gave him *all* of it." And Randi looks past what a verdict needs, "past a glance," and the "Miss November / Miss November" pinup framing. I felt the thing Vee doesn't: that this picture, which she gave Pace under the promise "you'll never share me with anyone," is now in Randi's hands, and Randi is Pace's lover. Vee thinks she's found the one person she can hand the unedited thing to. I know the edit she's making is showing it to exactly the wrong safe person. That's dramatic irony doing its clean, sickening work — but I'll hold it open: the page doesn't confirm Randi will carry it to Pace, and I won't fix that more firmly than the page does. It just sits there, warm and wrong.

The goodbye is where the Vee-Randi strand moved, quietly but really. For the first time *Vee* is the one who won't let the kiss end — "leaning up the last half-inch after Randi as she started to draw back, taking a second of it for herself." The checkpoint told me Vee never lets herself notice how much she wants Randi; this week she noticed, "decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." That's a small, real threshold crossed. And Randi holding the eye contact after, "a beat past what a goodbye needed, until Vee had almost begun to feel it as strange" — the two of them are both reaching now, and neither says it. The "Just us," Randi said — softer than the plan called for, a small roughness under it, is the same crack as "it doesn't." Randi keeps leaking.

On Pace: he's offstage all chapter, present only as the content of Vee's rapture, and the rapture is so total ("I'd confess anything, honest, just to have him hear me") that my checkpoint-knowledge of the staged meeting hums under every line. She's describing being *seen* to the woman who helped arrange the seeing. I don't distrust the tenderness — the salmon, the slowness, the coming-back, all of it in the recent chapters reads real — I distrust, as I have all along, what it's *for*, and this chapter's genius is that it lets Vee be purely, defenselessly happy while keeping me one room over with the door open.

Where it went slack: honestly nowhere badly. If anything the middle monologue risks being a *list* of wonderful things — sex, food, quiet, sleep, attention, music — and there's a stretch ("what's he got *playing*") that's the lightest, most filler-ish beat. But it's doing work: it's the deceleration before the photo, the pressure bleeding off. The one line I'd call slightly thumbed is "I used to be a person with thoughts" — cute, a touch sitcom, pulled me out for a half-second. Small.

Erotic charge: lower and warmer than the last two brunches, on purpose. There's no bathroom flight this time, no body climbing to a peak it can't discharge. The heat is diffuse — the yolk, the "sheets in both hands before he's even really started," the small rock in the seat when she says "pictures" — simmering, not spiking. That's right for a chapter about happiness having no edges. It didn't peak; it glowed.

**The titles.** "All the Time" — on the page it's the sheer *quantity* Vee keeps circling: the amount of it, twice a night, three, mornings, afternoons, "it doesn't *stop*," the wanting that grows by feeding. But reading it back after "it doesn't," I hear a second face on it: *all the time in the world* is exactly what Pace has with her (the slowness, "like he's got nowhere to be") and exactly what Randi does not — Randi gets a Saturday morning, "one hug to carry her the whole week." All the time, and no time. The title sits on the fault line between the two women. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on the second thought, which is the good kind. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon still reads to me as "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon," and this chapter is the politest, warmest possible version of the sup: a girl handed exactly what she's starved for by the two people spooning her toward something she can't see. The tagline I wasn't given one, so I'll leave it. The "invitation" — the checkpoint's open question about being welcomed into their world — didn't advance in plot here, but Vee reaching for the *anthro class together, just us* is her, unknowing, inviting herself deeper in.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here).** I dread the photograph — I dread it surfacing between Pace and Randi, Vee's "you'll never share me" quietly already broken. I want, achingly, for Vee to keep this happiness a while longer before the floor she can't see gives. And I'm watching Randi's cracks — "it doesn't," "just us" softer than the plan called for — with the growing sense that whatever this plan was, Randi is no longer safely on the outside of it. I'd guess the thing that breaks first isn't Vee's illusion but Randi's composure.
