## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This is the softest, sunniest chapter I've read in a while, and it worked on me the way sun works on you through a window when you're a little drowsy — I went warm before I noticed I was being warmed. Which, with these two, is precisely the thing I should be watching. But I'll be honest about the pleasure first, because the pleasure was real.

Vee at this table is radiant, and I love her here. What got me most wasn't the picture reveal or even the sex talk — it was her trying to explain the thing that has no shape. "There's not — it's not a thing that happened. It's more like. It's just how it is now." That's a person genuinely happy for the first time and finding out happiness is the hardest thing to narrate, because it has no edges, no scene, no swat, no dress. She's been trained by these lunches to bring Randi *content* — a story, a reveal — and this week she has nothing to perform because she's just been living inside good weather. That moved me. And her worry underneath it — "Is something wrong with me? Am I turning into some kind of sex pervert?" — is the whole book's wound in one line: the girl raised on "floozie" who cannot yet believe her own appetite is allowed. Randi's answer is beautiful and it undid me a little: "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud... You've been starving your whole life and calling it fine." That is the truest, kindest thing anyone has said to Vee in the book, and it is also — I can't unsee this — exactly the line a person running a long campaign of opening someone up would say to keep the opening going. Both at once. The absolution is real *and* it's fuel. That doubleness is where I live all through this chapter.

The heat here is low and diffuse, not the peak-and-crash of the brunch chapter. The eggs Benedict is the erotic object as much as anything — "the yolk broke and ran, thick and gold... her hand had gone back for more before she'd decided anything." That "before she'd decided anything" is the whole Vee engine, and the book keeps welding appetite for food to appetite for touch until they're one hunger. It's doing it a touch openly by now — I clocked the machinery a little ("Like I'd been eating with the sound off," "He woke you up") — but it's earned enough real estate over the book that I let it land.

The moment that actually made me sit up: Vee describing being wanted — "Like he wants me. With the body attached. Not — the other way around" — and Randi going bright-eyed and unfixed, the catch in her breath, the low "It doesn't." That's the crack in the glass. I've watched Randi mine Vee for twenty-odd chapters with total control, the professional listener whose glass goes forgotten on purpose. Here the story lands on *her*, involuntarily, and she can't fully smooth it. Vee reads it as pride — that her Saturday could do that to a woman like Randi. I read it as Randi being caught by the exact thing she came to extract. "It doesn't" — doesn't what? Doesn't want her with the body attached? Doesn't happen to Randi? The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but it's the first time in a long while I've felt Randi's own hidden breakage press up against the surface without her permission. I don't fear her here so much as ache for her, which the checkpoint warns me may be its own trap. Fine. I'll be trapped honestly.

The picture reveal is a lovely little escalation done through pride and dare rather than seduction. Randi doesn't push — she does the opposite, slides the phone back, "You don't have to show me," and *that's* what flips Vee. The withholding is the pull. Same move as the pastry, the "sticky fingers" — Randi opens a door by pretending there isn't one, and Vee walks through under her own steam and calls it her own idea. "It was my idea." "Course it was." That exchange is chilling and tender in the same breath: it *was* her idea, and it was also engineered that she'd have it. And then the second flip — Vee showing the full uncropped photo because she won't be "a girl who flinched" at this table. Her pride is being used as a lever and she experiences it as courage. I find this genuinely unsettling in the way the book is best at: nothing is taken, everything is handed over, and the handing is steered.

And then the goodbye — the one real *move* Vee makes. For the first time she is the one who won't let the kiss end, leaning up the last half-inch after Randi, "taking a second of it for herself." The disavowed thing surfaced, just barely, and she "decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." That's the jacket's promise arriving on the page: she's wanting the woman beside her without letting herself know it — except this time she let herself know it for one second and shrugged. And Randi's answering beat — holding the eye contact "a beat past what a goodbye needed," and the earlier "Just us" that "came out softer than the plan called for, a small roughness under it" — tells me Randi is losing her own footing too. "A small roughness under it, there and gone" and Vee "took it for the ordinary pleasure of a plan made." That gap between what Vee registers and what I register is the entire engine of the book, and it's humming.

The Anthro-elective thing is quietly ominous in the nicest packaging. Vee, not wanting the morning to end, reaches for a way to keep Randi — signs them up for a class together, "It'd be like stats, except we'd have picked it." Stats is where Randi was *installed* into her life by design; now Vee is voluntarily building another one, thinking it's her own reaching. "So it's just us." "Just us." She's weaving Randi deeper into her weeks and it feels like love and it is also her walking further into the enclosure, cheerfully, holding the gate for herself.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no new reason to doubt anyone's *feeling* — everything reads sincere, Vee's joy, Randi's real catch of breath, the warmth between them. My suspicion is entirely structural and I've held it since the reveal chapters: I know this table is a listening post, and no line this chapter un-knows it. What's new is that the instrument seems to be feeling the music now. The "It doesn't" and the "Just us" are the text earning my sense that Randi is coming undone inside her own operation.

Friction: very little. This is a talky, plotless-by-design chapter and it mostly justifies its length through voice. If I'm honest, the middle stretch of Vee cataloguing the good — the sleeping, the attention, the music, the no-TV — runs a hair long and a couple of the summations tip toward telling me the theme ("He woke you up," "eating with the sound off"). I didn't disbelieve any of it; I just felt the author's hand steadying the frame once or twice. Small.

Titles. "All the Time" — before the chapter it means nothing; after, it's got at least three faces. It's the *amount* — "We have so much sex, Randi," the thing that doesn't stop, "the more we do it the more I want it." It's also the ordinary "see you all the time / see you soon" of the friendship, the once-a-week ration Vee lives the gray weeks for. And it's Randi's time — the luck Vee can't get over, that a woman who could be anywhere sets her Saturday down "in a dark green room to hear the small news of Vee's life." All of it, all the time — an appetite with no off switch, which the jacket promised me ("an appetite that can only give her the best of herself by taking it"). It didn't give anything away; it colored in after. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon is the one you sup with the devil, and this whole chapter is a supper: the yolk, the hollandaise, the mimosas that touch rims first by private rite, Randi taking one neat bite and setting it down. The tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — sat on this chapter like a lid. Every yes here is Vee's: the pricey Benedict she finally let herself order, the uncropped photo, the last half-inch of the kiss, the class she signed them into. Freely given, every one. That's exactly the problem, and the chapter is almost demonstrating the tagline.

What I want / dread next: I want to know what "It doesn't" was going to be, and I don't think I'll get it clean. I dread — as a guess from here — the Anthro class becoming the room where Vee's disavowed want for Randi finally can't stay disavowed, now that she's already caught herself leaning that last half-inch. And Thanksgiving is coming, the folks, the mother's voice, Pace's flat "I don't go back" and Sheri alone at his table — I expect the gray weeks and the color weeks to collide there. Mostly I want to keep sitting in this booth, which is precisely how they've got me, and I know it.
