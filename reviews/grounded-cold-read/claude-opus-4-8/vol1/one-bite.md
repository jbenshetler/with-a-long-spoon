## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one got me by the throat, and it did it slowly, which is the cruelty of it. It's a whole chapter of two women at a brunch table with mimosas, and by the end I was as wound up as Vee was, and just as unsure whether I'd been let in on something or maneuvered.

Let me do the body first, because the body is the whole point of this chapter. It works on me. It builds exactly the way it builds *in* Vee — "one careful confession at a time." Every retelling of the spank scene puts his hand back on her and I felt her thighs press together under the cloth before she even named it. "He checked." Two words, and I went as still as Randi did. The construction is so clean: Vee tells the story to get the thrill of telling it, and the telling *is* the arousal, redoubled, because now there's a witness. By the time she gets to "He made me strip. Slowly. Down to nothing but the heels," the porch has become, in her words, "abruptly far too bright and far too full of people to be a body in," and I was right there with her — the erotic charge peaks not on the remembered spanking but on the present-tense fact of being unable to sit still around it in public. That's the good stuff. That's desire rendered all the way onto the page.

And then Randi tips it over on purpose. Her Radford story — the blond Robert Redford, "he is just starting to find *my* dark corners," the abandoned bed with "the engine running and nobody left to drive it," and then the kill shot: "Dress up, panties to the side, knees wide, doing a private dance." I clocked exactly what that is. Randi has spent the whole meal drawing Vee up a ladder rung by rung, and then she hands her the image that sends her over. "The picture came into Vee entire." Vee flees to the bathroom. That's not accidental. Randi *aimed* that.

The bathroom scene is where the chapter turned from swoon to ache to something colder. Vee, soaked "of course, always," gets herself almost there — and it's *Randi's face* that arrives, "fond, and near, as though Vee were the best thing in the room," and it stops her. "It was not the thinking, it was the looking. She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was." That undid me a little. Vee's desire for Randi is now so present it can *interrupt her own hand* — and she still won't look at it straight. She stops, "cowed and obedient," blots herself, goes back up "someone caught." God. The disavowal is doing real work; the wanting is louder than she'll allow and it's making her miserable in the exact spot where she should be having a private, harmless pleasure.

And then "Sticky fingers?" — and the pastry. This is the moment the chapter earned its unease, and it earned it fully, not on my paranoia. Randi says the damning thing, lets it land with full force, lets Vee's face flood and the floor leave the room — and *then* points to the pastries, as if that's all she meant. "She could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." I can't tell either, and I love that the page holds it open. But then the last image closes my heart around one reading: Randi took *one neat bite* and never touched the pastry again, and at the very end, on Vee's own lip, is "a thread of sweetness, sugar and butter and something warm under it, the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." The kiss carried Randi's bite of the pastry into Vee's mouth. **One bite.** That's the title, and it's devastating — and it tells me the "Sticky fingers" line was not innocent. Randi knew. The pastry was the deniability. She let Vee off the hook with one hand and pressed the hook in deeper with the other, and sealed it with a mouth-kiss that transferred the one bite she took. That's a woman feeding someone from her own mouth while pretending she said nothing.

So where does that leave me on **Randi**? My wariness, which the checkpoint already had simmering, hardened here — but not into simple villainy, and that's what keeps it good. Because the tell of the chapter is also that Randi's *own* story might be the truth: "your own skin is the whole party," "I am ready to *go*." She may genuinely be down in her body telling that, not only baiting. And her "best friend in the whole world" line lands on Vee — and on me — as real warmth even as I distrust its timing. The horror isn't that Randi is cold. It's that she's warm and it's a tool and possibly also true, all at once, and I can't separate the strands, and neither can Vee, and that's the trap the whole book has been building: Simpson's paradox made flesh. Every single beat of this brunch reads as generous friendship; the whole reads as something being done *to* Vee.

**Vee** I ache for more than ever. She's alive and funny — "yada-yada'd straight past the good part" is Randi, but Vee's crowing about the pantsing, her "I was crowing about it, too," is pure her. And she's more herself than she's ever been, giving the induction walk, giving Pace the two costumes — she's coming into a real ownership of her own desire. But this chapter shows the cost: the more open she gets, the more Randi can play her, and the one desire she still won't name — for Randi — is the exact lever. The bathroom is the whole tragedy in miniature: she can get almost anywhere alone, but she can't finish under Randi's imagined gaze, because that gaze is the thing she wants and won't admit.

**Pace** is barely here — mentioned only, in the retold spank scene (which I'd just read in full as "We Find Out," so it landed doubly). But it's telling that his tenderness gets flattened in Vee's mouth into "he checked" and "he took care of your fire" — the private, treasured thing becomes *material* the moment it hits Randi's table. And I know, as Vee doesn't, that it goes straight back to Pace in bed. Every intimate detail she hands Randi over mimosas is being couriered to the co-author of the plan. That knowledge sat in my stomach the whole meal.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** the text earned it this time, cleanly. I'm not on guard out of habit — the pastry/kiss image is a literal, physical proof that "Sticky fingers?" was not the innocent slip Randi let it pass for. That's the page handing me the unease, not me manufacturing it. What I *don't* know, and won't pretend to: whether Randi's warmth is a pure instrument or a real thing she's also weaponizing. The page leaves that ajar and I'll leave it ajar.

**Swoon** is real too, and I won't shortchange it: the mimosa moment early — "the both of them the same, the same drink, the same easy morning, and something small in Vee sat up and warmed itself at it" — is genuinely lovely, the pleasure of being matched, and I felt it before I remembered to distrust it. And the "best friend in the whole world" beat swept Vee up and, honestly, half-swept me: "picked her, out of everyone, for the closest place there was." The chapter is expert at making the warmth land as warmth. That's why the last line hurts.

**Friction:** almost none. The prose is doing a lot of "and her body did the thing it had done for him, gave without being asked" — the involuntary-body-tell is on nearly every beat, and once or twice I felt the machinery of it ("something low turned over once, lazily, and settled lower"), the metronome of arousal-tells getting close to a tic. But it mostly reads as accumulation, not repetition, because the *public* setting keeps re-charging it. No line pulled me out. Nothing over-explained — in fact the restraint at the end ("She sat down. She did not find out.") is exactly right; it refuses to tell me what Randi meant, and that refusal is the chapter's spine.

**Titles.** "One Bite" — before reading, a small innocuous thing. After: it's the whole chapter folded into two words. The one bite Randi took of the pastry, carried on her mouth into Vee's on the kiss; the "one bite" of the hook Vee took at the induction bar in an earlier chapter; feeding someone from your own mouth. It recolors the apple-slice bite-and-kiss from the very first bench scene, too — feeding as intimacy and as control at once. The title gave nothing away going in and detonated on the last line. That's the mechanism working perfectly. As for the book — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon* — I don't have a cover tagline in this packet, so I'll only react to what I have. "With a long spoon" I keep hearing as the proverb: you sup with the devil with a long spoon. This chapter is the proverb enacted — Randi feeding Vee across a table, and the spoon not being long enough. "Polite invitation" — the whole seduction has been nothing but courtesy, "May I," the warm table kept for you, "have one before I get to both." The politeness *is* the trap. This chapter made that thesis feel less like a promise and more like a closing door.

**What I want / dread next:** I want Vee to be safe and I know she isn't, and I no longer expect a rescue — I've stopped reading for one. *Guess from here:* the pastry-kiss is a threshold; Randi's "Sticky fingers?" told Vee she's seen, and Vee "did not find out" whether she was forgiven or exposed — I think the not-finding-out is the point, that Vee is now being kept permanently uncertain, kept reaching. I dread the moment Vee learns Randi and Pace know each other, because everything she's handed across these tables will re-read as testimony she gave to the prosecution. And I dread — more precisely — that Vee's desire for Randi is the exact thing that will be used to spring whatever the "invitation" is. But I'm guessing; the page hasn't told me. What it told me is that Vee walked out with sugar on her lip from a pastry she never touched, and didn't yet know how it got there. I did. That's the ache.
