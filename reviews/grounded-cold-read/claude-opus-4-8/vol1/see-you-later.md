## Reader reaction

## The felt read

I came into this chapter carrying something Vee doesn't have, and that made the whole thing ache in a way the prose itself is much too sunny to admit. I *watched* Randi get pointed at this girl yesterday. I watched her stop mid-stride on the quad, watched the bare *yes* come up out of her before she could dress it, watched Pace tip his chin and say *go*. So when this chapter opens inside Vee — Vee who thinks a girl bumped into her by accident, Vee who's been wearing a groove into "we should sit together," was it *meant* or *said* — I'm reading a love story from the mark's side of the con, and I know it's a con, and I still got swept up in it anyway, which is the horrible clever thing this chapter does to me.

Because it *works*. That's the part I have to be honest about. The right pen. *I'm going to copy off you all semester, I'm just telling you now so it's not weird later.* The hand on the forearm, once, twice, the way it lands "like praise she'd worked for." Randi is a genuinely wonderful thing to be chosen by, and the chapter renders the intoxication of being chosen so cleanly that I felt it in my own chest even while the back of my neck was cold. Vee at the high table, "up in the window, feet off the floor, her body presented at the height the whole room could see" — I know that feeling, the vertigo of suddenly belonging somewhere you'd only ever pressed your face to the glass of. And the detail that undoes me a little: she can't reconstruct a single word of the best hour she's had in months. "The leaning-in was the whole content." She wasn't keeping the words, she was keeping the feeling. That's such a true and such a *dangerous* thing to be — a person who registers being wanted as pure weather, with no transcript, no purchase, nothing to hold to account later.

My feeling about Randi moved here, but sideways, not toward trust. On the quad I'd seen her want genuinely leak through — the helpless *yes*. So I can't read this as pure cold assignment; the page won't let me flatten her that far, and I don't want to. But watching the warmth "swing over and land, total, undimmed" on Cassie and then come *back* to Vee, watching her run the little name-bit with its built-in laugh-slot — I'm seeing the apparatus now, the same warmth-as-perfume that Brooke had in "Standards," and it reads as technique. What I can't resolve, and won't pretend to, is how much of Randi actually wants Vee and how much is executing Pace's game. The page holds that open. She may not know herself. That ambiguity is the most alive thing about her.

Vee I just want to protect, which is its own kind of dread, because I can't. She's warm and sharp and lonely and good at reading a room — that lovely brutal passage cataloguing the Chi Latte girls, the "floozies" arriving "whole and in her mother's voice," the cardigan worn over the better shirt "on purpose," the room not knowing it was a choice. She sees *everyone* accurately except the one person aimed at her. She's got her mother installed in her mouth, portioning her, the way Randi portions her own appetite with thin apple slices — and here's Randi handing her a lavender latte and a table in the window, feeding her exactly the belonging she starves herself of. It's the same hunger Pace found in Randi, being used on Vee now, one rung down the ladder.

**Where the unease got earned, hard: Cassie.** Cassie is the whole nervous system of this chapter for me. She won't take the bit's open door — just "Cassie," flat, one word, pencil back up. She declines Chi Latte with no sulk, no edge, "a person declining a thing that wasn't hers." And then: *"Goodbye, Vee."* The whole word. Not *bye,* not *see you,* not *text me* — "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Something passed over her face that Vee "would not have been able to name and did not try to." I named it, or I tried: Cassie sees it. Cassie clocked Randi across a stats row and knew. That "Goodbye" is a person watching a friend get led off somewhere, saying the goodbye you say when you're not sure the same person comes back. The page earned every bit of that chill honestly — it's not manufactured, it's *placed*, one flat word against Randi's engineered warmth, and it's the truest thing anyone says in the chapter.

Which sets up the title. **"See You Later"** is Randi's parting line — "not *let's do this again,* which was a thing people said and didn't… a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see, the queen having decided there would be a later and that Vee would be in it." Read straight it's the sweetest promise in the chapter. Read against Cassie's "Goodbye" it's a sentence. The two farewells are the whole book in miniature: one person promising a *later* she's already scheduled, one person saying *goodbye* because she can see where the later goes. The title gave nothing away by itself, but the chapter armed it, and now it reads cold.

**A Polite Invitation** — this chapter *is* the invitation, extended, accepted, glowing. Nothing rude about it, that's the point; the whole horror is how courteous and warm the recruitment is. And **With a Long Spoon** — I keep landing on the old line about supping with the devil and needing a long spoon for the distance. Vee has no spoon. She's climbing up onto the high stool with both hands wrapped around a warm cup. The tagline wasn't included in my packet, so I'll leave that lever alone.

Thumb-on-the-scale, once: the professor's send-off. *"Latent variables. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can."* Vee even writes it down, "underlined once, going nowhere." I clocked exactly what it was doing — Randi's motive, Pace entirely offstage and unmentioned, all the variables Vee can't measure — and it's a touch too neat, the class content rhyming that hard with the plot. It's dressed as drone so it mostly gets away with it, but I saw the hand.

Erotically this is low and I don't mind — it's a 1, all simmer. The charge that *is* here is Vee's want rendered warmly and without apology: "a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance," with "nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her.*" Desire between women, plainly on the page, glad and unexamined. It doesn't peak into heat; it doesn't try to. What it does is make me feel the *pull* Randi is exploiting, which is more useful to the dread than any sex would have been.

What I want next: I want Cassie to be right and I want to be wrong, and I don't think I'm going to get the second one. I dread the moment Pace enters Vee's frame — right now he's a total blank to her, "another of his good ideas" from a chapter she wasn't in. *(Guess from here, marked as such:)* the invitation lands Vee somewhere near that bench, and the same thing that got done to Randi gets done to Vee, with Randi as the instrument instead of the subject — and the open question I'll be reading for is whether Randi feels anything true toward her under the assignment, or whether Vee is only the gift she's been sent to fetch. I want the pull to keep being this well-rendered even as it curdles. And I want to know what Cassie knows.
