## Felt read

This is the second time I've watched Randi walk Vee into a bright room, order without looking at the card, and gently take a girl's night apart over a drink — and knowing what I know, I read the whole chapter with a low hum of dread under the warmth. But I want to be honest first about the warmth, because the chapter earned it: I was, against my own better sense, glad the whole time. That's the trap of these two women together and I fell in it right along with Vee.

The thing that actually landed on me, the swoon, wasn't the kiss or the recounting of Pace — it was **"I wanted it, and I took it. For once."** After ten-plus chapters of Vee holding her arms down at her sides, apologizing for her own body, hanging the russet dress back on the shelf, painting her toenails and hiding them inside flats — to hear her say that flat and plain, no hedge, turning her flute on the cloth, felt like the appetite arc finally arriving somewhere. And Randi's "Good," with her hand over Vee's, letting the word settle. I *wanted* Vee to have that. The trouble is I can't fully trust the hand that gave it to her, and the chapter knows I can't, and that doubleness is the whole flavor.

Because I know Randi's "sad little story" is a lie. She didn't run into a beautiful old boyfriend who was in a hurry. Her man is Pace — the patient, unhurried, *singular* one Vee is describing across the table. So the structure of this scene, once I hold that up to it, is quietly awful: Randi is sitting there coaxing out, detail by detail, the account of her own lover being tender with another woman — the toenails painted for him, the underwear bought for him, "you knew how the night was going to end before you ever got in the car." And she does it *smiling*, and hands Vee an inverted mirror-story where she got the nervous fumbling boy and Vee got the god at the stove. Whether that costs Randi anything, the page will not tell me this time. In "How It's Done" I got the tell — the thumb working the water glass, the tightness behind the smile when Vee said *he looked at me and I was fine.* Here there's no crack. Randi is seamless, frictionless, warm all the way down. I genuinely can't tell if that's because she's better armored now, or because the game is simply going the way she built it to go, or because she's swallowed the sting so completely it doesn't surface. The chapter leaves that ajar and I'm going to leave it ajar too. But the absence of the crack is its own small chill.

What's new and clearly *moving* is Vee's pull toward Randi. Last time Randi kissed her at the curb, Vee's whole body went cold scanning the dorm windows for who might have seen. This time: "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them." That one sentence is the tell that the thing the blurb promised is underway — Vee is past the shame reflex about *this*, has folded the mouth-kiss into the ordinary, explained it away and stopped needing to. She's being drawn toward Randi the same undefended way she was drawn toward Pace, and she reads it, as always, as luck. "The friend she'd wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting to get. Who liked her, some days, better than she managed to like herself." I ache at that line precisely because I don't believe the premise under it.

On the characters, where I stand now: **Vee** — still wholly mine, more so, because she's happier and more herself than I've ever seen her, and the happiness is real and earned and that's exactly what makes it hurt to watch it being harvested. **Randi** — the seamlessness here actually unsettles me more than the crack did last time; when she's this smooth I trust her least, and I feel the tragedy of her the least, which may be the point or may be the chapter holding her at arm's length. **Pace** isn't on the page at all, only recounted, and even recounted he does the thing — "he wasn't in a hurry, at all, like there was no clock anywhere in the world" — and I both melt and flinch. **Cassie's** absence is felt: she got "the good version, the whole clean one," and Randi got the middle, and the fact that Vee gives Randi more than she gives Cassie is its own quiet alarm, because Cassie is the clean note and Randi is the operation.

Erotic charge: this is a clothed chapter and the heat is all undertow — the recounting kept deliberately soft and quick ("a hand waved at the air, *and then, well*"), the frosted-plum decoded, the mouth kiss at the curb. It works on me as intimacy-charge more than sexual charge, and where it peaks is Randi's reading of the underwear — "You didn't buy those hoping he'd be a gentleman. You bought them hoping he'd give you no reason to keep them on." That's the register these two share: Randi naming Vee's want out loud and handing it back to her clean, and Vee glowing at being seen. It's genuinely hot as *knowing*, not as skin.

Friction: my one real reservation is structural — this is the second Randi-brunch-extraction in a short span, and the beats rhyme hard (menu right-side-first, the ordered drink, the coaxed confession, the curb kiss, "See you soon, gorgeous"). It didn't bore me, because the *content* has advanced — Vee doesn't resist this time, the extraction is frictionless where before it snagged, the kiss is met instead of startling — so it reads as the same ritual gone deeper rather than a rerun. But I felt the risk of the repeated shape, and if there's a third of these I'll start to feel the machinery. I didn't catch the author's thumb anywhere in the prose itself; it's controlled and clean.

The title. **"Fed"** does a lot on a second beat. Surface: Vee is fed — brunch, mimosa, sated, glowing, the "famished" of last chapter answered. Under it: *Randi* is fed — "give me something to live on," "I'm running on fumes here" — Vee hands over the account, and Randi eats it. Who's feeding whom is the whole hunger motif turned one more click: the girl who's finally been fed is being fed *on*, gently, at the same table, and doesn't feel the second thing happening. That recolored the chapter for me the moment I set the word against it. **"A Polite Invitation"** keeps ringing true — the entire seduction runs on courtesy, on being invited and saying yes, and this chapter is one more invitation (Randi picks the place, "sit, sit," "tell me everything") accepted without a seam. **"With a Long Spoon"** — I keep hearing the proverb under it, *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon,* and every one of these meals makes that louder: Vee keeps supping, warmly, with people I'm increasingly sure are the devils in the story, and she brought no spoon at all. I wasn't given a cover tagline in this packet, so I can't react to one — there's no jacket copy here, only the chapters — but the two titles plus that series name are pointing hard at a reckoning I can feel coming and Vee can't.

What I want and dread next: I want Vee to keep the thing she found — the *I took it, for once.* I dread that the very openness that gave it to her is the surface being read and worked. **Guess from here, marked as a guess:** the pointing-game thread (Pace asking Randi if she'd like to taste another woman, "only in a threesome, with you") plus Randi's escalating mouth-kisses makes me think Vee is being walked toward being shared — a gift for both of them — and that she'll go toward it undefended, reading it as being chosen twice over. And somewhere in there I dread the moment Vee learns the two of them knew each other all along. I'll follow her that far in either direction.
