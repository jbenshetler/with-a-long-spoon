## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one is short and it's a coda — the debrief. After the whole slow gorgeous ascent of "Leave No Trace," "Rye" drops me back into the dorm room with Cassie, and it works exactly the way a debrief should: it lets me feel Vee re-live the thing out loud, and it lets me watch Cassie watch her fall.

I came out of the hike chapter genuinely swept up — that scene in the truck, the flannel, him standing in the rain with his back turned, her calling him back and undressing on her knees while he sat still and only looked — landed hard on me. So I was braced coming into this one, half-expecting either a comedown or Cassie's tuning-fork alarm to finally ring. Instead I got something warmer and a little more heartbreaking: Cassie's guard comes all the way down. That's the move of the chapter. The whole first half is their comfortable bit — the windowless-van joke, "I had your blood type," the witness-statement banter — Cassie in dry sweats being the dry one, doing "the file-revising thing." And then Vee slows, the way she "slowed at the tree the other night," and Cassie "went still and stayed that way," and "Cassie was rapt." That's what got me. Cassie, my one clean skeptical note, the person I've been trusting to smell the wrongness — she hears this story and she's disarmed too. Not fooled about danger; there's no danger in this telling. Just moved. That landed as ache more than reassurance, because I'm sitting here holding the whole plan that Vee and Cassie both can't see, and now even Cassie is charmed by Pace's care.

How I feel about Vee: still all the way on her side, and this chapter deepens the tenderness because I'm watching her be happy in the specific way of a person who's never let herself have anything. "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain." That's not about Pace at all — that's her, reclaiming a body she's spent the whole book apologizing for and covering with cardigans. "And then we ate.' She said it the way other girls said 'and then we kissed.'" — that line is perfect, it's the whole thesis of her hunger folded into one aside. I love her here. I also notice she's still narrating around the real thing: she tells Cassie about the shirt, the rain, taking her top off — "Me." — but the private line from the truck, *I want him to see me,* the heat-under-the-cold that wanted to be the very thing her mother's voice named — that she keeps. Even to Cassie she gives the gesture and not the appetite under it. That's honest to her.

Pace: I don't distrust the care in this chapter because he isn't in it — he's only in Vee's telling, and in her telling every detail is exactly the disarming thing. The kit, the moleskin, the back turned in the rain, the not-touching. "He looked at me like I'd handed him something." My wariness about him is structural, not on the page here — I know what I know, and the very flawlessness of his attentiveness is the thing that keeps a cold finger on my neck. But this chapter didn't add new suspicion; it just let me feel how good the con feels from inside, which is its own kind of dread.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new earned this chapter on the suspicion side. What it did instead was quietly spend my last reliable skeptic. Cassie's "Vee," soft, "not 'Oh, Vee.' Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break" — that's Cassie choosing not to warn her. She sees Vee is gone on this and she doesn't reach for the brake. "Borrow real boots next time. / Keep the shirt." That's blessing, not caution. It unsettled me precisely because it's warm — the one person positioned to say *wait* says *keep the shirt.*

Erotic charge: low and deliberately so — this is a clothed retelling, a 0-to-1. The heat all lives back in the hike chapter. What's here is afterglow, and the flannel three-sizes-too-big doing the work of a love-object. It's not trying to be hot and I didn't want it to be; it would've been wrong.

Friction: almost none. It's a tight, well-judged little scene. If I reach — the banter runs a hair long before it turns ("I'm reporting what I observed") — but it's charming and it earns the pivot, so I won't dock it. No thumb on the scale that I felt.

The title: "Rye." Innocuous on its face — the bread in the sandwich, "Rye bread and this horseradish." But sitting with it: rye is the dark, sour, seeded bread, "deeper and more sour than the soft white loaf at home." It's the grown-up bread, the acquired taste, the thing you only want once your hunger's real ("I think you have to almost die on a mountain first"). Naming the chapter for the bread and not the kiss or the shirt tells me the book knows what its subject is — Vee's appetite, fed. It doesn't give anything away; it just quietly points at the through-line. I also can't help hearing *Catcher in the Rye* faintly, the catcher standing at the edge of the cliff to keep the kids from going over — and here's Pace at the black rock edge, and the person who'd catch her (Cassie) just told her to keep going. That may be me over-reading; mark it a guess.

Book title / series: "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" — the spoon one keeps ringing louder ("he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon"). Every warm feeding in this book is the devil's supper from where I sit. The tagline I was given — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — is doing exactly what it promises: this chapter is a stack of freely-given yeses (the shirt kept, the top unbuttoned "on purpose. Me."), all of them hers, all of them real, and that realness is the trap. The jacket line "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" hangs over Cassie saying "keep the shirt."

What I want / dread next: I want the plan to keep getting realer and I dread the moment Vee learns it, because this chapter just made the fall more complete — even the skeptic's on board. Guess from here: Randi's the one who gets the fuller version of this story, and I dread that debrief more than I dread Pace, because Cassie's telling was clean love and Randi's listening will be something else.
