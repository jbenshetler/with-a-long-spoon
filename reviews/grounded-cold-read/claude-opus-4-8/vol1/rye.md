## Reader reaction

## The felt read

After the drenched, breath-held intensity of the truck cab, this chapter is a exhale — a short, warm debrief in the cool dorm room, Vee telling Cassie everything. And I found I badly wanted exactly this. There's a particular pleasure in watching a woman come home glowing and unload the whole day to the one person she's fully herself with, and "Rye" gives me that pleasure clean, without a shadow forced onto it.

**Vee.** I love her here. The physical-comedy register — lowering herself into the chair "like a much older woman," "My legs are going to file a complaint," "a blister like a second toe" — is her at her most undefended and delighted, and it's a lovely counterweight to how starved and self-policing she's been. What got me most is the line the book itself points at: *"And then we ate." She said it the way other girls said and then we kissed.* That's the whole architecture of this girl in one beat. For Vee, the eating *is* the erotic climax — the horseradish, the shared water, the cherries, "we didn't leave a pit" — and I felt the ache of that, that the appetite is the thing she's been kept from, so being fed on a mountain is the seduction. She narrates the actual near-erotic moment (taking her wet shirt off) almost shyly, "Me. And he looked at me like I'd handed him something," but her voice goes brightest over the sandwich. That's true to her and it's quietly heartbreaking.

**Cassie.** This is Cassie's best showing and it deepened my love for her. The whole opening is her running the protective bit — "No windowless van," "I had your blood type," "I had a whole witness statement ready" — and it's genuinely funny, but it's love wearing a joke, exactly her mode from the pool and the froyo debrief. Then the turn: when Vee slows down (the book flags it beautifully — "as she'd slowed at the tree the other night"), Cassie "went still and stayed that way," "was rapt." And her landing line, *"Vee," Cassie said finally. Soft. Not "Oh, Vee." Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break* — that undid me a little. It's the closest thing to worry in the room, and the book is careful *not* to let it become "Oh, Vee." She doesn't warn, doesn't puncture. She says "Borrow real boots next time" and "Keep the shirt." She lets Vee have it. That restraint is its own kind of love and I trust it completely.

**Swoon / pull.** The swoon is real and I let it sweep me. Retold, Pace's gestures land almost harder than in the moment — the moleskin kit ("of course he had a kit"), getting out into the rain with his back to the cab "so I wouldn't have anybody looking at me," giving her his only dry shirt and then not asking for it back. And "I kept the shirt. I didn't ask." The flannel three sizes too big, swallowing her — I felt the warmth of that as much as Vee does. This is the chapter rooting hardest for them, precisely because it's filtered through Vee's joy and Cassie's rapt silence.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Here's where I have to be honest with myself. Nothing in *this* chapter earned suspicion — it's warm all the way down, and I let it be. But I carry what I carry: I know Pace and Randi chose Vee as a game, and so the very perfection of these gestures sits differently for me than it does for Vee. "He didn't make it weird. Didn't make me feel like I'd wrecked the day" — that flawless attentiveness is the exact instrument I've been uneasy about since the froyo. The chapter doesn't wave at this; it just lets Vee be happy, and the unease is mine, brought in from outside, not planted on the page. I want to name that cleanly: the text here is unshadowed. I'm the one standing in the doorway with my arms crossed. And there's a small ache in that — Cassie's "just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break" is the one place the chapter itself seems to know how fragile this is. Whether that's Cassie sensing the con or just sensing that her friend has fallen hard and could get hurt the ordinary way — the page leaves it ajar, and I'll leave it ajar.

**Erotic charge.** Low and that's right — this is afterglow, not heat. The charge is almost entirely re-narrated ("I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me."), and it works as warmth and tenderness rather than arousal. It doesn't try to re-stage the cab scene, which is the correct call; recounting the striptease at full heat would've cheapened it. The one live spark is Vee gushing about his forearms — "I think there's a whole situation under there" / "I'm reporting what I observed" — which is charming and clothed and made me grin.

**Friction.** Almost none. It's a slight chapter by design and I didn't want it longer. If I reach: it is *entirely* recap of a chapter I just read, so a couple of beats ("He fixed it. Right there on the trail") are things I watched happen forty minutes ago in reading-time, and there's a faint sense of the book letting me enjoy the highlight reel twice. But Cassie's presence transforms it from recap into relationship, so it earned its place. No line felt like the author's thumb.

**The titles.** "Rye" — I like it. On the surface it's just the bread in the sandwich, the dark sour loaf "deeper than the soft white loaf at home." But sitting with it: rye is the grown-up bread, the one you have to acquire a taste for, the not-from-home bread — and this whole chapter is Vee acquiring tastes, being fed things her mother's egg-white world never offered. It also chimes with the appetite motif running the whole book. It's innocuous and warm and doesn't give anything away; it points inward, at the feeding. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon one still hums under everything for me: "sup with the devil, use a long spoon." That's the promise that all this courtesy and feeding has a devil somewhere in it, and the long spoon is the safe distance nobody in this chapter is keeping. Vee has no spoon at all; she's eating from his hand. *A Polite Invitation* — the politeness is everywhere (the "May I," the back turned in the rain, the shirt not asked for back), and I keep reading "invitation" as the thing being extended *to* Vee, courteously, into something she can't see the shape of.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the induction dinner and that russet dress — she set it down on the rock "and he didn't pick it up again," and I don't believe for a second that dress is done. *(Guess from here:)* Pace clocked it; I'd bet the dress reappears, and I half-dread the gift of it, because a gift like that is exactly how the net tightens while feeling like grace. I also want Randi back on the page — she's been offstage two chapters now and I feel the con humming just out of frame. Mostly I want Vee to get to keep this one clean happy day a little longer before the book collects on it.
