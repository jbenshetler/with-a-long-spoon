# Cold read (grounded) — Turned Up

## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one's a comedown chapter in the best sense — after the sustained, breath-held heat of the measuring scene, we drop back into Vee and Cassie's room and get to *watch Vee tell it*, and the pleasure of that is real and specific. I came out of "A Round" wrung out and a little overwhelmed on Vee's behalf, and this chapter let me exhale with her. It's mostly a debrief, and I usually brace for those to feel like recap — but it doesn't, because the telling is its own event. The way Vee can't sit still, up and down off the desk, hands cupping her own breasts and flying outward to demonstrate "*out*. Just out," is a girl still lit up from the inside, unable to contain a thing that happened to her. That physicality carried me.

**Vee.** I love her here, plainly. The comedy is a relief valve and she knows it — she narrates the bra coming off as slapstick ("here you go, I guess") precisely because the real thing underneath is too big to hold up to the light directly. But the chapter keeps catching her out. She keeps sliding from the joke to the true thing and having to stop: "He held it up next to my face. To see if it was right for me." "*For me to stand on.*" And the moment that got me was the heat — she hadn't meant to say it, "or maybe not at all," and then out it came: "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold... He thought of it then." That's the tell that this isn't a girl bragging about a good date; it's a girl handling evidence of being *thought about* that she doesn't quite have the equipment to receive. Her feeling hasn't moved so much as *deepened and gotten more frightening to her* — she's past dazzled and into something that reorganizes how she carries herself, which "A Round" already showed physically and this chapter lets her almost-name: "Like I was supposed to be there."

**Cassie.** She remains the cleanest, most trustworthy note in the book, and this chapter is the most I've loved her. She does two jobs at once and does them beautifully: she's the appreciative audience Vee needs — "He *sews*," set down like a fact she's filing — and she's the one person who won't let Vee only-joke. Watch her key changes: "Vee. The shirt." "You did." "No, I think I have it." She laughs with Vee and then goes still at exactly the beats where Vee is telling the truth, and the hand on the knee, "once, and took it back," is the perfect Cassie gesture — she loves Vee and she doesn't maul the moment. I trust her completely, and I trust her *reading* of Vee.

And here's where the chapter did something quietly to me. Cassie's closing riff — "He's a bodybuilder who makes dresses... He's had you standing in his living room in your underwear, and he picked up a *tape measure*. Vee. Are you sure he's not gay?" — plays as pure comedy, and it lands as comedy. But I felt the small cold draft under it, and I don't think I'm manufacturing it, because Cassie has been the tuning fork this whole book. She's circling the actual anomaly: a man's restraint that is *too* complete, too engineered, a self-control that reads to an outside eye as *something not adding up*. The book hands her the wrong answer (gay) so she'll say the true thing (this doesn't compute) out loud and laugh it off. I know what Vee doesn't. So Cassie fishing for the misfit and landing on the one explanation that's wrong — while the real explanation is a man running a long, patient, coordinated seduction — gave me a genuine chill inside the laughter. That's earned by the page, not by my guard.

**Swoon / pull.** The swoon here is the *shirt*. "You've been sleeping in that shirt all week." "You wore it to breakfast on Tuesday." "It's a comfortable shirt." "Vivienne." "Don't." That's a girl in deep and a friend who sees it, and it's tender and funny and true all at once. And the button-press at the end — the shirt folded on her pillow "because she'd put it there without thinking," the dress "wasn't even finished yet," the good quiet neither of them breaks — that's a lovely, aching last beat. I rooted for her happiness even while I know what's around it. That doubleness is exactly the book's signature and it's working on me.

**Erotic charge.** Deliberately low and I didn't mind — this is a clothed, comic chapter and its heat is retrospective, secondhand. The one flare is Vee's confession, saved for the very bottom: "*I* was grinding on *him*." That's the chapter's real reveal — she's been telling it as a thing done *to* her and the truth she's been sitting on is that she was the aggressor, again, the way she was in the truck. It recolors her whole "poor me on a box" routine — she's not just charmed, she's *hungry*, and she knows it, and it delights and slightly scares her. Consistent with everything the recent chapters built.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new suspicion earned *by this chapter* on Pace — if anything the surface warmth only thickens. My wariness is entirely carried in from the checkpoint (that all of this is engineered, that Randi and Pace are lovers who chose Vee). What this chapter *does* is let Cassie voice, in a joke, the shape of the wrongness — and that sharpened my unease precisely because it came from the character I trust and stayed unresolved, a joke that lands and then just... sits there. Nobody in the room can see what I see, and the chapter doesn't pretend to.

**Friction.** Very little. It's a slight chapter by design and it earns its slightness. If I'm honest, the "May I look" gloss — "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" — is the one place I felt the book underline a motif it's already made me feel; I didn't need it spelled that cleanly, Vee's body had already said it in "A Round." Small thumb on the scale. Otherwise the dialogue is clean and the rhythm of Vee-can't-sit-still is right.

**The title.** "Turned Up" — first read, it's the radiator, the heat he turned up hours early so she wouldn't be cold, which is the chapter's tenderest disclosed detail and the one Vee didn't mean to say. But it turns over immediately: Vee herself is *turned up* — the volume of her, the wanting dialed past where she can manage it, and the reveal that she was the one grinding. And there's a third turn I feel more than prove: a thermostat set in advance is *provisioning*, a room prepared for an outcome someone already knew was coming. The same detail that reads as care reads, from where I sit, as staging. That triple sit is exactly the kind of innocuous-surface title the book likes, and it did recolor on second thought without giving anything away.

**Book / series titles + tagline.** I wasn't given a jacket/tagline in this packet, so I'll hold those loosely from memory: *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon*. The spoon — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" — keeps ringing louder the warmer these chapters get. Every gesture of Pace's is a courtesy, an invitation politely extended and freely accepted, "he left me a door," "He asked" — and that's precisely the machinery the proverb warns about: the danger that comes at you *as* graciousness, close enough to eat with. This chapter, with its turned-up heat and its open doors, feels like sitting down to exactly that table.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the dress finished and I dread the dress finished, both. (Guess from here:) the induction — Vee's name read out at the front of a room, her mother down for it, in a gown built on her body by Pace — feels like it's being loaded as a set piece, and I can't tell if it's a triumph or a trap or both. I also want to know what Cassie does with the misfit she just laughed off; she's too sharp to let it stay a joke forever. And I keep waiting for Randi to re-enter this braid — she's been offstage for these hike/dress chapters, and the checkpoint's whole engine is that she and Pace are running this together. The longer she's absent while Pace works Vee this tenderly, the more I dread what her return costs.
