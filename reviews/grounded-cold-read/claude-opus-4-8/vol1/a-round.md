**Pace.** Here's where the two readings run at once and won't resolve, and I'm going to leave them unresolved because the page does. But I have a memory, and the memory keeps turning the generosity over to its other face. He'd cranked the heat "hours before she came, probably," built a riser "set where the light fell, the room arranged around it," had the tea hot — a set, dressed and lit, waiting. Everything arranged. So the whole tender hour reads double for me, and I can't tell where care stops and operation starts, which is the wire this book keeps walking. I'll say plainly: **the page itself gave me no new tell** — Pace is nothing but kind and restrained here. The unease is my inheritance from before, not something this chapter earned fresh. What the chapter *did* add is a colder note I trust: the near-toppling. "A man on the very edge of a thing he could have fallen into so easily." If his footing is genuinely going — the way Randi's went on the bench — then the con may be turning real on *him* now too, and I honestly can't tell if "Keep still" is discipline for the plan or a man holding on. The page leaves it ajar. I'll leave it ajar.

**The riser** gave me a small chill on its own. A smooth, finished, built surface, in a woodworker's house, that she steps up onto to be worked on and seen — I've met that object before, and the last one turned out to be an instrument disguised as furniture. Here's another pedestal built to put a woman exactly where he wants her, at exactly his eye level. She reads it as thoughtfulness. I read the echo.

**Friction.** Very little, but the last stretch states its theme out loud where I'd have trusted the body: "she had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." Earned, but I felt the author underline it. And his perfection gets pointed at a lot — "He'd thought of that. He'd thought of it hours before" — twice in a paragraph. Small thumb.

**Titles.** "A Round" sits mostly innocent — *around* the waist, *around* the bust, and the literal rounds of measurement, the same circuit run over and over (his own MRI speech from the last chapter: "say it more than once so the noise can't kill it" — redundancy, repetition, a round and another round). But with what I carry, "a round" also reads as *a round of a game* — one move in something being played across a table Vee can't see — and that quiet second meaning is the whole flavor of this series. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, is right here on the page: the least-information text she read a thousand times, "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock," and the courtly "May I." The invitation is always polite; that's the point of the dread. *With a Long Spoon* — someone's supping with the devil and needs a long spoon, and this whole book is about a girl being *fed*, undefended, no spoon at all. (No tagline was included in my packet, so I've nothing to weigh there.)

**What I want / dread.** I want the induction, her in the burgundy, her name read at the front of a room. I dread the con landing *on top of exactly this* — the more he builds for her, the higher the fall. Guess from here, marked as a guess: this afternoon is either the deepest set of the hook or the place where his own footing starts to go, and the book is deliberately refusing to tell me which. I'm pulled hard to keep reading, and a little afraid of it.
