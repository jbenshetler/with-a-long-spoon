## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This is a short, sweet chapter, and it made my skin crawl — which is exactly the doubled thing I think it wanted from me, and it earned every bit of it honestly. On its own surface, the froyo date between Vee and Pace is genuinely lovely. He studies the flavor placards "with the same attention he'd given her calculus"; he says "There's a system... You put chocolate on tart and it's an argument the whole way down," and I smiled. "I've thought about most things," said without apology — I laughed with her. They walk "the long way, not toward anything," and he "asked her things the way her grandfather used to ask things, as though the answer mattered and there was no wrong one, and then he waited through all of it." That last bit landed soft on me — being the thing a person waits on, when you've never been waited on. And then Vee's confession, which ached: "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo." And he doesn't pretend it didn't happen, doesn't inflate it — he just lets the true thing have its space and says, "Your tart's holding up." As a pure tender beat, that's beautiful writing. If I knew nothing, I'd be rooting hard.

But I don't know nothing. I've read four chapters, and I know the provenance of every ounce of this warmth, and that is the horror sitting under the sweetness. This is the *exact* attention I watched him run on Randi in "The Bench" — the noticing, the unhurried patience, "I'm not stopping, I'm taking my time." It undid her. And here's the froyo cup "getting away from her — too much, the cone of it leaning. Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." That is Randi's portioned apple all over again — the girl who won't let herself have, being taught to have, by the same man, with the same method. The feeding is his signature, and it's aimed now at a lonelier, more defenseless target. I know Randi *pointed at Vee* on the quad and handed her to him as a "gift." I know "love you, girl" is a tool. So this whole gentle date is the machine clicking forward, and Vee thinks she's just the luckiest girl on campus.

My feeling about Pace has curdled hard across these chapters, and this one deepened it precisely because he's so *good* at the tenderness. He is not a boor; he's a craftsman, and that's worse. The froyo-as-structure speech — tart on the bottom "holds up under the heavy ones" — reads to me like the bench: he's assessing what will bear load. "Your tart's holding up" right after she's exposed herself is him checking the structure of her. That chilled me more than any cruelty would have.

Randi I distrust completely and the text has earned it — "The rule's for boys you're not sure about. You're not unsure," closing the trap in the softest possible voice, "and that was the whole debate." She steers with her whole body while looking like a friend. "He wants to see you naked" — she's the one narrating Vee toward the bed she's being brought to.

Cassie is the one clean instrument in the room and I love her for it. "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" gives me the whole shape of Vee — a caretaker who spent a semester up until two keeping a business major alive in a video game. Vee is a *healer*, a giver, self-spending. Which is exactly what makes her prey, and Cassie, who "everyone's look slides off," is the only one watching straight. Her flat "Goodbye, Vee" from the prior chapter still hangs over me.

Vee herself I ache for, plainly and without irony. She's warm and hungry and lonely and reaching, borrowing Randi's very name-bit ("Vivienne Thorne, V.T.") — already being remade toward the woman grooming her. "She let herself want it to light up." My whole reader-body wants to reach into the page and tell her to run.

Trust vs suspicion: no suspicion — certainty. But it's certainty the earlier chapters built brick by brick, not paranoia I brought. And I'll be honest that the tenderness still half-works on me even knowing better; that's the ugliest, truest part of the experience.

Erotic charge: low, and rightly so — this is courtship, not heat. The only spark is Randi's innuendo. The real charge is dread.

Friction: almost none — the chapter is admirably restrained and doesn't editorialize its own menace. The one place I felt a light thumb was "It was easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting," naming her interior a touch directly, but it's mild.

Titles: "The Long Way" fuses Vee's register (she "went home the long way, warm the whole way there" last chapter) with Pace's method — he takes the long way *on purpose*, unhurried so it never feels like being driven at. The title names his patience as the weapon it is. *A Polite Invitation* keeps ringing through the "may I call you," the froyo, the courtesy of it — the seduction conducted entirely in good manners. And *With a Long Spoon* — he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon — leaves me with the dread that none of these girls has one; they're supping bare-handed and calling it froyo.

What I want/dread next: I dread the collision — the moment Vee learns Pace and Randi are together (she has no idea), or the threesome that "Pointing Game" set in motion actualizes. Guess from here: this two-front courtship (Randi as friend, Pace as suitor, neither knowing the other is known to her) tightens until Vee is inside their arrangement without ever having chosen it. I want to keep reading and I dread wanting to.
