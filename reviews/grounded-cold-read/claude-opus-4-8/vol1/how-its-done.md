# Cold read (grounded) — How It's Done

## Reader reaction

Well. That was the coldest thing this book has done to me yet, and it did it while I was smiling, in the sun, with the top down. I need a minute.

Here's what got me: the last two chapters I read — the hike, and then the fitting — were the most swept-up I've been all book. The fitting especially worked on me completely: Vee up on the box, the wet spot she couldn't stop, the shame turning over into power when she opened her eyes and Pace was just *glad it was her*. That was a genuine erotic and emotional peak, and I loved her for coming through it undefended. And this chapter's entire function is to have Randi walk in and *harvest* that peak. One small "yeah" at a time. I know — because I know what Vee doesn't — that the fitting was built to produce exactly the arousal it produced, and now here is Randi at a velvet-lined table debriefing the asset. "He measured you himself?" is the *opening* of an interrogation dressed as girl-talk, and I felt the floor go out from under the whole lovely afternoon I'd just had with them.

The craft of the extraction is what chilled me most. "Nobody measures over a bra... it's not a *thing*, it's just how it's done" — that's the title, and it's a lie precisely calibrated to dissolve Vee's shame so she'll hand over the next piece. Vee even names the mechanism to herself: "if it was just *how it was done*, if everyone knew, if it was ordinary — then there was nothing to be ashamed of... then she could say it." I watched Randi build the ramp and I watched Vee walk down it, and I couldn't warn her. The "challenges?" hook, the "It was cold in the house" / "Cold. Hmm" — that catch was surgical. Then, right at the moment Vee's deepest folded-up secret is *half out of her mouth* — "I—there was—" — Randi says, gentle as anything, "You don't have to tell me." And the narration nails it: "a door closing very softly." She denies Vee the release. She leaves the secret "half out of her with nowhere to go." That is *withholding* as a technique — keep her leaning, keep her hungry, keep her needing to give it to you next time. I have read seduction on this page for eleven chapters and this is the first time I flinched at it as *cruelty*.

And here's the thing I have to hold open, because the page holds it open: I genuinely cannot tell whether Randi is running the debrief for Pace, or feeding her own hunger, or both at once. The tell that undid me was the tightness. When Vee glows — "he looked at me and I was fine" — "something had gone tight behind" Randi's smile, "her thumb had found the base of her water glass and was working the edge of it." That's jealousy, plain and involuntary. Randi was the woman on the bench, opened and made to beg; now she's watching Pace do the tender version of the same thing to Vee, and Vee is *lit up* about it, and it costs Randi something to sit and collect it. So she's the operator and the wounded one in the same body, and I pity and distrust her at full strength, both louder than they've ever been. I'm not going to resolve which one drove the kiss.

The kiss. That's the new beat, and it's the one I'll carry. Not the cheek thing — "Randi's hand came up and found the hinge of her jaw... kissed her on the mouth. Soft, and in no hurry," and then the part that made me sit up: "a small drawing-back, the barest pause, and then back again with a little more, a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't." My checkpoint told me Vee had an unexamined pull toward Randi; this is the chapter it surfaces from both sides. Vee's own heat showed earlier, over the bondage story — "Randi naked and helpless and laughing, her wrists tied over her head... the heat the picture put low in Vee... a heat she had no shelf for." So the pull is real and it's mutual on the page, and I can't tell if the kiss is the game escalating (the pointing-game "taste another woman" setup finally reaching for its target) or Randi's own want breaking containment. Both are live. The dread and the charge are the same feeling.

What kept it from being purely a horror show is that Randi's charm still *works on me*, same as it works on Vee — the dark-green Mercedes tucking itself in around her, the singing at the light, "overdue reserve" delighting Randi, "God, I love you." For the length of one song I got swept up right alongside Vee, "a girl in a beautiful car in the sun with the most glamorous woman she knew" — and then the cold thought lands at the curb, Vee scanning the dark dorm windows for who might have seen, and I was cold with her. That whip from swoon to dread inside two paragraphs is the book operating exactly as it means to.

The money thread did quiet, devastating work too. Vee reading "the right side first. The column of prices... the way you read a road in fog, looking for what was passable," against Randi who "had read, it seemed, only the left side." Vee paying with the debit card and feeling it "on Thursday," tipping well so she won't be "the broke girl who tipped badly." The one thing she won't do is let Randi buy her seat at the table. That's Vee's whole dignity in a gesture, and it's the one place in the chapter she isn't being played — she's choosing the cost herself.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** no ambiguity left about Randi's *actions* — I watched the con run in close-up and it's predatory. What stays open, honestly open, is her *motive*, and I'm keeping it open. New detail I clocked and can't read yet: the white-gold band at her wrist "she had never once seen her without... the one fixed thing in a wardrobe that changed itself daily." First sighting. I don't know what it means and I won't guess it into a fact — but it's planted like it wants to matter.

**Friction:** almost none. The one thing I noticed is how explicitly the narration renders Vee talking herself out of her own instincts — "She had it explained away almost before her mouth was her own again," the "*French,* she thought" rationalization of the kiss. In a lesser book that'd be the author's thumb. Here it *is* the subject — Vee's self-deception is the thing I'm reading for — so it landed as interiority, not telegraphing. I'll allow it.

**Titles:** "How It's Done" meant nothing to me going in; now it's Randi's disarming lie ("it's just how it's done") *and* a demonstration of how the whole thing is done — technique, start to finish. It pays off on the reread, gives nothing away in advance. Clean. *A Polite Invitation* — the lunch is exactly a polite invitation, extended by the sweetest possible predator. And *With a Long Spoon* rings the old proverb loud here: sup with the devil and bring a long spoon. Vee is at the devil's table with no spoon at all, supping intimately, ladling her soul out in "yeah"s. The series title has never felt more earned or more ominous. (No cover tagline was included in my packet, so I can't react to one.)

**What I want / dread next:** I want to see Cassie again — she's my one clean instrument and she's been absent two chapters now while the net tightens. I dread the induction and that burgundy dress becoming a stage. *Guess from here, marked as a guess:* the kiss is the game reaching for the "taste another woman" beat the pointing game set up — with Randi's real hunger now tangled into it so she can't tell her own want from the assignment either. I don't think Vee is anywhere near finding out, and that's what aches.
