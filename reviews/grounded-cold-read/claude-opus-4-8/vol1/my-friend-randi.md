## Reader reaction

### The felt read

Oh, this one worked on me two ways at once and left me a little sick with it, which by now is the flavor I trust this book to deliver.

The opening got me straight in the body — Vee crying most of the way in from Roanoke with the heater going and nothing on the radio, "every mile of it ran toward a town that had Pace in it and not one inch of him for her." That line is exactly her ache. And the empty dorm, Cassie's side stripped bare, "the empty of it met Vee at the door like more of the same" — she comes home to nothing and lies on top of a made bed in her plane clothes going over it and over it. I've been living inside her insomniac spiral for four chapters now (the laundry room, the two cold beds, the twin bed in Ohio), so by the time she can't stand her own company and walks across campus to Randi's, I went with her the way you go with someone you've stopped being able to talk out of anything.

And then the trouble. Because I know what Vee doesn't, and this whole chapter is Randi being the person Vee runs to — and Randi is the co-author of the thing Vee is running from. Watching the handler receive the mark, warm and open-armed, and steer her gently and expertly back into the arrangement, is the most disturbing thing the book has done to me in a while, and it did it without one villainous note on the page. That's the craft of it. Randi doesn't lie. Her read of Pace is *true* — "a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." I just came off Sheri getting the exact same read across a diner table ("that feels pretty shitty," but also: he's gone on this girl like she's never seen). So Randi isn't feeding Vee a lie to trap her. She's feeding Vee the *truth*, and the truth happens to walk Vee right back into the bench. That's so much worse than a lie. I couldn't find the seam to be angry at, and that's the point.

The swoon is real and I'm not going to pretend it isn't just because I distrust the woman delivering it. The hug undid me a little — Vee pulling a breath off the warm skin of Randi's neck, "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet," and it steadying her, and her not wanting to stop. The embrace held "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that, and neither of them let go." Randi sweeping her new Gstaad things off the bed without a look so the wrecked girl comes first. And here's where my knowledge cuts *for* the tenderness instead of against it: I read "Fairytale," so I know Randi's warmth here isn't only strategy — she is genuinely, terribly in love with this girl, past the plan, and can't say it any more than Pace can say his word. So the too-long hug is the handler *and* the woman who lay in a perfect man's perfect bed feeling nothing and thought, *Vee is her gorgeous.* Both hands on the same gesture. That's the ache.

The naked-under-the-coat sequence is where the heat lives, and it's the shame-to-want engine running one more time, beautifully. "It's such a slutty thing to do" — the word costing Vee, flushing her to the ears — and then "Vee, hot-faced, felt the other thing turn over under the burn, as it always did once the shame was lit, the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's the whole mechanism of her in one sentence, and it still works on me. Randi walking her down off the ledge — "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it" — is genuinely persuasive, warm, and also, I can't forget, *the handler dressing an act of self-exposure as courage so the mark will perform it.* The heels pressed into her hands closed the loop hard: "Randi always knew the shoes." She did. She bought her the first pair too. The shoes are a leash and Vee reaches for them grateful.

But the passage that flattened me was the retelling. This is the whole reason the book made me read "Fairytale" first. Vee, wanting to give something back into the warmth, asks for the real New Year, and Randi *tells* it — the lift line, the children named on the first day, John the better skier who spent none of it proving so, the pale blue dress "the kind you tell your daughter about," the ball. And I'm sitting there with the interior version, knowing that the whole time up on that mountain Randi was measuring John against Pace and finding him a beautiful nothing. Then: "And then it got very late," she said, "and we went up." And that was all she said about it. She looked down at her hands. Vee "read the quiet as discretion, the one thing even Randi kept behind the door." I know it's the *opposite* of discretion — it's the room where she felt nothing, where "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best." And then the kill line, delivered as a joke Vee laughs at: "He was perfect. Truly. A masterpiece... Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in. What's a girl to do?" Vee laughs — really laughs, for the first time all day — and I wanted to put the book down. She's laughing at a woman confessing the thing she can't confess, in the only register she can get away with. That's the most naked Randi has been on the page and Vee can't hear a word of it.

And the cruelest small stitch: Vee keeps hearing her *own* wound in Randi's story. "You were naming children on the first day" — "it went into her sideways all the same, because it was the exact thing she didn't have," a man who'd build the future out loud in the first hour and not fear one word of it. She envies the *forward motion* — the exact thing Pace won't give her, the word. She doesn't know she's envying a night that meant nothing to the woman who lived it, or that the man who *can* reach Randi is the same man withholding the word from Vee. The two of them are aching over the same locked door from opposite sides and neither can see the other doing it. That's the geometry of this whole book and this chapter put it in one room.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** My suspicion isn't a suspicion anymore, it's knowledge, and this chapter is the coldest use of it yet — not because Randi turned cruel but because she stayed warm while doing the plan's work. What genuinely unsettles me is that I can't cleanly separate the love from the manipulation in her, and I don't think she can either. Brooke gets a small flag from me too — "She always seemed to be passing through," the warmth arriving "ahead of the chapter president," Vee shrinking, holding the rest "behind her teeth, because whatever this was, it was not for down here." That's a house that files people, and Vee felt herself filed ("the stats friend"). Cassie's absence is felt as a loss of ballast — the one sane voice is in another state, stripped bed, and Vee walks straight to the person Cassie warned her about. That placement isn't an accident I can prove, but I felt it.

**Friction.** Almost none. If I'm honest, the one place I braced for the author's thumb was Randi's diagnosis being *so* right, so clean — "Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do" — a mark's confidante shouldn't have Pace mapped that precisely on the evidence Vee's given her. But I know *why* she has him mapped (she's his lover), so the too-perfect read reads as dramatic irony, not a slip. A first-time reader might feel Randi is improbably wise; I felt the floor under it.

**The title.** "My Friend Randi" — it's what Vee calls her, "the only girl she could talk to about real things," and the chapter is her running to her friend and being held. Read straight it's tender. Read with what I know, it's the mask itself as a title: *my friend* is precisely the thing Randi is not, or is not only, and the whole chapter is the friendship doing the plan's work while being real friendship at the same time. It didn't give anything away — it sits sweet on the surface and turns to ash on the reread, which is the move.

**The book / series titles.** No jacket or tagline came in this packet — just the chapter title — so I've nothing to quote there. But *With a Long Spoon* rang loud this chapter: sup with the devil and bring a long spoon. Vee is at the devil's table with no spoon at all, and the devil is pouring her tea and telling her she's brave. *A Polite Invitation* — the whole thing has been invitations, doors, "may I," courtesy as the delivery system for the trap; here the invitation is one Randi hands Vee to hand to Pace (show up as the answer, nothing under the coat), the mark taught to invite herself the last inch of the way in. That's what those titles promise and this chapter cashed a piece of it.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Vee to go to him tonight and I dread it in equal measure, which is exactly the split the book keeps engineering. *Guess from here:* she does it — the coat, the heels, nothing underneath — and Pace opens the door and it works, the reunion is real and tender and I'll be swept up in it even knowing what set it in motion. What I dread is the bench, still sitting in that bedroom, "the low table of odd proportions," waiting. Randi just walked her back toward the house that has it in it, and handed her the shoes to walk in. I don't think this reconciliation is the trap springing — I think it's the trap being reset, warmer than before. And somewhere in me I want to know what breaks first: Randi, who can't run the one scenario to its end; or Vee, when she finally sees the pile of small repairs she's been not-looking at.

### Structured block

**Cast present (in person):** Vee; Randi; Brooke (comes through the front hall, gives warmth); the unnamed blonde with wet hair who answers the door. Mentioned only: Cassie (offstage, stripped bed, away with family), Pace, Sheri, John, Vee's parents.

**Heat:** 1 — no sex on the page, but the naked-under-the-coat coaching runs the shame-into-want engine ("felt the other thing turn over under the burn... the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it"); charged, clothed, simmering.

**Romance:** 2 — clear intimacy between the women: the long-held hug, the breath off Randi's neck, Randi putting the wrecked girl before her new things, the goodbye kiss. Plus the buried ache of Randi's concealed love, which I feel even though Vee can't. Not a declared peak — the declaration is exactly what's withheld, in both couples.

**Motifs & images:** *The goodbye kiss* (repeat, from Ch 17 on) — now "unremarkable... only what they did." *Randi's scent* — "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" (repeat of the cold-citrus scent from checkpoint). *The black heels* pressed into Vee's hands (repeat — Randi bought her the first dear black pair, Ch 23; "Randi always knew the shoes"). *"Gorgeous"/"my love"* — Randi's signature (repeat). *The house warmed hours ahead so she's never cold* (repeat — recurs across the recent chapters as Pace's tell). *Words are cheap / the word withheld* (repeat and inverted — "Words are the whole war"). *The built/imagined rival* — the blonde tan model Vee assembles from spite (repeat from "Old Acquaintances"), here named as invention and dismantled. First sighting: the *coat-as-invitation*, "show up already being the answer."

**Symbolism:** The retold New Year as a hollow shell — Randi narrating the fairytale with its meaning surgically removed ("and we went up," eyes down, "the bedroom's the room I actually live in") reads as the whole book's method: a perfect surface offered to someone who can't see the missing center. The heels as a courteous leash — a decision made "for Vee and by Vee at once," which is the plan's signature everywhere.

**Characterization:** Randi *deepened* hard — the reader who read her interior last chapter now watches her run it under a smile, and the gap between what she says and what we know she means is where all the pressure is; she's more sympathetic and more dangerous in the same breath. Vee held and deepened — her spiral, her shame-reflex, and her sideways envy of "forward motion" are all consistent and gaining resolution. Brooke got a sharp, economical touch (warmth that files people). No one went thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Earns its length. The slow build of the coaching and the retelling both need their room, and the chapter gives it without dragging; the one place it could feel like a therapy-session Q&A (Randi's step-by-step reframe) is saved by how much steering is happening under the counsel.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building, and turning a corner. Four chapters of separate insomniac aches (Vee's beds, Randi's perfect empty bed, Pace at the diner) all converge here into one room and one decision — "Tonight she would go to him." After so much held-apart interiority, this is the pivot that points the next chapter at a reunion. Momentum up, not sagging; the run of parallel loneliness paid off rather than repeated.
