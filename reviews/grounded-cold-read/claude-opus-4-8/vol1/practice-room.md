## The felt read

This one worked on me and scared me in the same breath, and I want to be honest that the fear only arrived *because* the heat did — the chapter earned both.

The shape of it is almost unbearably simple: Vee tows Randi into a soundproof piano closet to tell her the good news, and by the end Randi has talked her into wet, aching arousal with nothing but a chair, a question, and a story. I watched it happen in real time and I couldn't look away, and that's the tell — I was *inside* the seduction, not above it. The move that put ice down my back was **"So. Did you deserve it?"** Vee laughs it off — *"Probably I deserved it"* — and only "a moment late" does she realize what she just agreed to, and "Randi's smile brightened." That's the whole engine of this book compressed into three lines: a good thing (playful, warm, the kitchen swat) handed back to her one degree turned, and Vee signing for it before she reads the receipt. I've been carrying dread about "the drop" since the checkpoint, and this is the first chapter where I could feel a hand actually *building* the thing that will hurt her, in front of me, warmly, while Vee thanks her for it.

And the horror underneath — the thing I know and Vee doesn't — is that the fantasy Randi "confesses" (**"Over his knee... Skirt up. Panties down... I had to count... it made me buck... always, toward the end, his hand would slip"**) is not a story about some slight soft-spoken ex. It's *Randi and Pace.* It's the bench. She is handing Vee her own real life dressed as girl-talk, seeding the exact shape of what these two do, priming Vee to walk into it and call it her own idea. That recontextualizes the whole cozy weekend I just read — the kitchen spank that made Vee's body say "two or three things at once she had not agreed to have it say" wasn't spontaneous discovery, or it was, but Randi is right there to catch it and pour it into a mold. I felt genuinely sick and genuinely turned on, which I think is the point and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

**Vee.** I love her and I'm frightened for her in a way that sharpened hard this chapter. What moved: the *reversal of the hand*. The narration names it beautifully — "For weeks it had gone the other way: Randi's hand at her elbow... Now it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist... towing the most interesting person she knew toward the door." Vee thinks she's driving now. She isn't. She's just been drawn so far open she seeks the drawing-out herself. And Cassie — my clean sightline — gets brushed off *"to the side of Vee's head,"* saying "see you in the room" while Vee's already gone. That physical staging gutted me. The one safe person is being left in the aisle.

The bathroom is where the chapter peaks and where it broke my heart at once. Vee, bolted in, *soaked "by nothing but a woman talking in a chair,"* her hand knowing the way and moving before she does — and she stops it. "Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." That's the interior life I read for: she is right at the edge of a knowledge she won't look at squarely — that she wants *Randi*, that this is desire between them and not confession — and she holds her own hand down and calls it fatigue. The last line, "She put it down to that," is the self-deception laid bare, gentle and devastating. I ache for her.

**Randi.** I trust her less than ever and find her more compelling than ever — exactly where the checkpoint left me, but deeper. What's new is watching her *technique* with the lights on. The "Mm" she "set down on the desk between them to pick up later." The way she refuses the love-tap framing — *"Tell me about the one he gave back. His."* — and reconstructs the scene forensically: "His hand was wet. You said he was doing dishes. So his hand was wet." She is a detective of Vee's arousal. And then the flicker I can't fully close: "Sometimes it's the mouse that roars," her face going "fond and far away" — is she genuinely somewhere real, remembering the bench, wanting it back? The checkpoint told me she was terrified of being *seen*, and here she's the one doing the seeing, safe behind the story. I hold it ajar: I can't tell if the far-away look is nostalgia, hunger, or performance, and I don't think the page wants me to. That uncertainty is the most interesting thing about her.

**Pace** is offstage but all over this — and this is the first time I've felt him operating *through* Randi without being in the room. His kindness on the porch ("Leave them on... I'm not going to insult the loan") is still genuinely swoony to me; that's the cruelty of the design, that none of the tenderness reads as fake, and yet Randi is next-door repackaging his hand on Vee's backside into doctrine. My unease about him is now a low constant, not a spike.

**Swoon / pull.** The romantic swoon this chapter is mostly borrowed from the recap of the porch and the kitchen, and it still lands — "Beauty made for the gods and loaned out." But the *live* pull of the chapter is not romance, it's the erotic-magnetic thing between the two women, and it swept me up completely. The charge is Randi's voice doing to Vee what a hand would do. That's rare and it's working.

**Erotic charge.** High. 3. It peaks twice: the reconstruction of the wet hand ("It went right through me... And I'm sure he saw it," Vee's voice dropping into "something lower and slower she didn't entirely recognize"), and the bathroom. What makes it work is that it's almost entirely *talk and interior* — no one touches anyone below a knee-against-knee — and I was more aroused than in some of the explicit scenes, because the wanting is rendered all the way onto the page and the shame is right there braided into it. It never goes clinical. If anything the restraint is what turns the screw.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Fully earned suspicion now, not vibes. The "did you deserve it?" sequence and the wet-hand interrogation are *on the page* as manipulation — I'm not importing it from the checkpoint. The one thing I hold open, deliberately: whether Randi is only working Vee, or is also, underneath, genuinely hungry for her and for her own past. "That was kind of hot," she told Pace a few chapters back about Vee's blush; I believe some real want is tangled in the craft, and I won't flatten it to pure con.

**Friction.** Almost none. The one place the thumb pressed very lightly was the outliers lecture — "The second kind comes in through the part that carries forward. It's in everything that comes after." It's a gorgeous, exact image for what's happening (the kitchen swat isn't a one-off you lift out; it's the second kind of outlier, in everything after), but coming right after "All Told"'s Simpson's paradox and the earlier latent-variables lecture, the stats-professor-as-oracle is starting to feel like a repeated device I can see the seams of. It didn't pull me out — it's too well matched — but I clocked it as a pattern.

**The titles.** "The Practice Room" — on the surface, the soundproof music cell they duck into. On second thought it opens twice: it's the room where "everybody used to... you know," which Vee can't say and which primes the sexual register before a word of the real content; and it's a *practice* room in the other sense — Randi rehearsing Vee, running her like a fit model (as in "broken-in"), practicing on her the exact extraction Pace once practiced on Randi. It didn't give anything away before I read it, but it recolored hard on the last page: this is where Vee is being *practiced into* what she'll become. Quietly chilling. **A Polite Invitation** and **With a Long Spoon** — the spoon proverb ("sup with the devil, use a long spoon") sits heavier every chapter; I keep waiting to learn who's holding the long spoon and who forgot theirs, and this chapter's answer is that Vee came without one. The tagline isn't in my packet, so I'll say nothing I wasn't given.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Cassie back in frame — she's the only one who'd name this, and she was left in the aisle, which frightens me. I dread the moment the spanking-fantasy Randi planted becomes something Vee *asks Pace for*, thinking she invented the wanting. *Guess from here:* the "endgame" the checkpoint flagged — the threesome, "only with you" — is being walked toward one soundproof room at a time, and Randi is deliberately closing the gap between what she does with Pace and what Vee will do, so that when it arrives Vee will feel she chose it. I don't want a rescue and I'm not owed one; I want to keep following Vee that far inward, even if inward is somewhere dark.
