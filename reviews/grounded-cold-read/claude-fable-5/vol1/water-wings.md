# Cold read (grounded) — Water Wings

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the first chapter in the book that has no Pace and no Randi in it at all — not present, not even named — and I didn't realize until I finished it how much I needed that. After five chapters of watching warmth get manufactured — Randi's wattage aimed at Vee, Pace's staged stroll to the coffee-shop table — here is one chapter of care that nobody engineered, and the difference is the whole point of my reaction. Cassie fighting her way into a navy suit "like it had insulted her family," Cassie keeping "Tossers" low "just for the two of them" when she had the lungs to pitch it into the bleachers — that's the first tenderness in this book I haven't had to squint at. It landed on me hard, precisely because I've spent three chapters watching Vee glow under attention that I know is choreographed.

Because I do know. That's the dread this quiet chapter swims inside. I watched Randi go still on the quad when Pace pointed — "Yes," the word coming out "alone, with nothing around it" — and then I watched her build a collision so clean "the redhead's hand flew to her own mouth," and I watched Randi texting with "the private, absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention" right before the bell tinkled and Pace walked into the coffee shop as a stranger. Vee doesn't know Randi and Pace know each other. The bare "Randi," no adjective bit, no parents joke — she couldn't do the bit for a man who already knows her. So every warm thing Vee has received lately has a wire in it, and this chapter gives her — gives me — the one place with no wires: "the water was the one part of it that had never been taken."

The underwater passage is the best interior writing Vee has gotten yet. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem" — that sentence hurt in the good way. The suit from sixteen that fits and "gave her nowhere to hide," the towel carried "down at her side — not up against her," the face she puts on for the bleacher walk: Vee is learning to perform, and I noticed with a small chill that she's learning it partly from Randi — in Substitution she borrowed Randi's name-bit word for word ("V.T. Yes, like the university"), and here she does the entrance "like a girl who had *meant* to arrive." Then she crosses the dark stripe where the shallow gives out, loses the floor on purpose, and gets to be nobody's object for the length of a held breath — "Nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything." And she "grieved, a little." The chapter doesn't say for what, and I'm not going to fix it: the body she used to have, the girlhood before the body was a problem, maybe something she can't name. It's left ajar and it's right that way.

Cassie. Cassie is quietly becoming the character I trust most in this book. Her flat "Goodbye, Vee" in the lecture hall — "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday" — has been sitting in me for two chapters, and this chapter tells me why I trust her: she watches Vee surface "with her face on wrong" and doesn't ask — "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger" — she sinks her cupped hands and shoots a sheet of water across ten feet instead. That splash fight is the most loving act anyone has performed in this entire book, and it costs nothing and asks nothing. "You can't grieve and fight your best friend for a mouthful of pool at the same time; the splashing won." And then there's "They will never know the love of a woman" — a joke at the tossers' expense on its face, and maybe only that. But from Cassie, who says four words a day and watched Randi's coffee cup "without watching it," and whose goodbye was too final for a Wednesday — I hear a second frequency under it, and the page neither confirms nor denies. I'm leaving it exactly as open as the chapter does. But I noticed.

Swoon: yes, real, and platonic — the shriek-laugh, "the ugly one," Vee making a sound "she hadn't made in a while." I rooted for these two the way I haven't let myself root for anyone since the bench. Erotic charge: none, and none intended; the bleacher line "You won't need floaties with *those*!" sexualizes Vee without a flicker of heat, which is precisely its function — it's the world's looking, the kind that wants something, set against the water's not-wanting.

Suspicion: I have no new suspicion born in this chapter — there's no one here to suspect — but the standing dramatic irony colors everything. The line "Something in her chest that had stood at attention all afternoon quietly sat down" doesn't say what stood at attention. Maybe Randi. Maybe just the day. Held open; I hold it open.

Friction: almost none. "Tossers" made me blink — a Britishism from nowhere — but it's characterful enough that I let it go. The chapter is very short, and I briefly wondered if it was slight; by the end I didn't think so. It earns its size.

The title: "Water Wings" is lifted straight out of the catcall, which means the ugliest line in the chapter names it — and then the chapter quietly reverses it. Water wings are for people who can't hold themselves up; Vee, under the surface with her hands finding "the old shape... the slow scull, palms feathering the little figure-eight," needs nothing to hold her. The title gave me a half-beat of recognition when the catcall arrived, but it didn't spoil anything — it recolored. *A Polite Invitation* keeps sharpening: Randi's "Come to Chi Latte with me... I'm not asking, I've decided," Pace's "May I call you?" — this book is full of invitations whose politeness is the mechanism, and Vee keeps saying yes to things she can't see the whole of. *With a Long Spoon* — I keep hearing the proverb about supping with the devil, and I keep wondering which of these three needs the spoon. No cover tagline was given in my packet, so I have nothing to react to there.

What I want next: I want Vee to keep the water. What I dread — and this is a guess from here — is the machinery closing: the froyo turns into dating, Randi engineers the closeness, and someday Vee learns that the collision on the lawn was choreography. I think Cassie will be the one standing in the shallow end when that happens, "Like a person." I'll keep reading for her almost as much as for Vee now.
