# Cold read (grounded) — broken-in

## Reader reaction

The felt read

After the wreckage of "Lesson," I came into this chapter braced for shame-spiral, and instead the book handed me something sneakier: a repair chapter that is secretly a seduction chapter, and not the seduction I was pointed at. Vee sets out to fix things with Pace, and what actually happens on the page is that Randi undresses her, kneels at her feet, and dresses her back up — and Vee walks out lighter, armed, and more thoroughly Randi's than she has ever been. I felt the warmth of it the whole way through and only afterward noticed my pulse had been in the wrong place — not on Pace at all.

The morning is beautifully done small. Cassie's "low *Hrm*... the sound Cassie made instead of a question" is perfect — four words a day, and here she doesn't even spend one, just pauses in orbit below the bunk and lets Vee fake sleep. That pause cost Cassie something, I'd bet, and Vee let it pass, and that's now twice in two chapters she's chosen not to hand Cassie the true version. I felt a little ache there. The cold-water verdict, the "sad-clown thing," the bare legs in October chosen "with the conviction of the converted" — Vee snapping herself out of the funk by decision is exactly the girl who said "I wanted it and I took it," and I was rooting for her stride before she ever reached the white columns.

Then the house. Vee walking into the sorority house for the first time — the composites, "the lobby of a museum that hadn't yet decided whether to let her up to the paintings" — is lovely, and Brooke at the door gave me a genuine prickle: "Oh — you're the stats friend. Not a question. Vee had told this house nothing in her life and the house had a folder on her anyway." That's the text confirming Brooke is still collecting, and Vee registering the folder without knowing what it means. I know what it means. Brooke has "Pace" filed from Randi's Saturday-morning interrogation, and now the stats friend has shown up at nine a.m. with a repaired face. I don't know what Brooke does with discrepancies, but she keeps them, and this chapter just gave her another.

The confession scene works on me two ways at once, which is this book's signature move. As pure friendship it's genuinely good: Randi getting up five minutes after waking, bare-faced, freckled, "That *bitch*," the hand held through the worst of it, the reframe that unclenches Vee — "You were so far ahead you couldn't see the race, and you ran it anyway, because you're *in* this." That reframe is true. It's also exactly what a handler says. And then the line that stopped me cold, that Vee sails right past: "*of course it was, he can dance, the bastard.*" Randi has never, in Vee's world, met Pace for more than one bare word in a coffee shop. How does she know he can dance? Vee is too wrecked and too grateful to hear it. I heard it. Same with "If he's the man you've just described to me, he doesn't sulk... he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing" — she isn't inferring the man from Vee's description; she's describing the man from her own bed, three chapters ago in "Gone," down to the room-leaving. The dramatic irony is almost unbearable and the page never underlines it once. That restraint is why it lands.

And the "Not today." I want to be honest about my divided read here. As advice it's plausibly good — don't show up panicked, go in sure. But it's also Randi asserting scheduling authority over Vee's reconciliation with Randi's own lover, "not a suggestion — an instruction," and Vee obeys because Randi said so — "the thing Vee had been holding clenched since last night let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so." The text put that "because" there on purpose and I felt it as a small cold key turning. Whether Randi wants the day for Vee's sake or for some purpose of her own, the page holds open, and I'm holding it open — but the *power* of the instruction is not ambiguous at all.

Then the fitting, and here's where the heat lives, and it is genuinely hot in the way this book does best — indirection, tending, the body answering before the mind files a report. Vee topless in front of Randi is "nothing... dress-up, the oldest game there is" right up until "Randi looked, and that was the part Vee felt." The shoe-fitting under the long skirt is the erotic center of the chapter and one of the most charged clothed sequences in the book so far: Vee can't see any of it, only "the cool quick hands somewhere down past the hem," the hand to the outside of her thigh holding her balance, the thumb pressed "once into the soft hollow behind her knee on the way down, and Vee's breath did something she hoped wasn't out loud." It rhymes deliberately with Pace's fitting in "A Round" — closeness, touches, withdrawal, a woman stood on a spot and tended — and with Randi on one knee in the boutique. Randi is now fitting Vee the way Pace fits Vee, and Vee's skin "came up singing," and she has stopped even performing not-noticing. And the shoes themselves carry the book's whole argument in miniature: "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." That's the dance frame from "Lesson." That's the menu handed over in "May I Choose." That's the trilogy title, honestly. I don't think it's the author's thumb — it's Vee's own sensation, in her own register — but it's the thesis wearing a cognac strap.

The jokes are doing real work too. "Edna Mode'd me" made me laugh out loud, and the shared collapse — "the small dark one and the tall red one, laughing in the cold bright room" — is real warmth; I believe these two delight each other, plan or no plan. But listen to what the play says: "You'd be my very own *shoe whore*" — my own; "Move in. I mean it"; and above all, when Vee jokes she has no evil stepsisters for this Cinderella moment — "Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet." Randi said that. Out loud. Smiling. It's the closest the book has come to a confession played as a joke, and Vee laughs, and I sat there with my stomach doing something complicated. The page offered it and moved on; I'm not going to fix it harder than the page did, but I felt it land.

The goodbye kiss is the quiet milestone: "she hadn't braced... Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." From explained-away-as-French, to met, to *unmarked*. That progression has been so patient I almost missed the arrival. Under all those framed composite faces, too — kissed on the mouth in the foyer of the Standards house, and this time Vee doesn't scan for windows. Something in her has been broken in.

Which is the title. "Broken-in" is the borrowed boots and the shoes worn soft, the shoes that must be broken in before the big night — and it's Vee. The dance lesson broke her into the frame; last night broke her open; this morning Randi finishes the job the way you finish leather, warm hands and steady pressure until the thing holds the wearer's shape. It even reaches back to the shared shoe size — same feet, "practically a marriage" — Randi's shoes are already broken in to Randi, and they fit Vee perfectly. I find I can't decide if that image is tender or terrible, and I suspect that's the point. *A Polite Invitation* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" hang over this chapter more nakedly than over any so far: every single thing Vee accepted this morning — the counsel, the delay, the shoes, the scarf lesson, the kiss — she accepted gladly, gratefully, and I watched her do it wanting her to have all of it. The jacket said I'd fall for it exactly as she does. Still am.

What I want next: the reconciliation at Pace's door, tomorrow night, in the black dress and the borrowed shoes — I want it badly, and I notice I want him to forgive her, which after everything is its own evidence of how well this book has worked on me. Guesses from here: the reconciliation goes well, possibly the most fully — she's going in "sure," and sureness is what he answers to. And somewhere ahead, the induction in the burgundy dress. What I dread, mildly: that Vee walked to Pace's door once in this book without noticing whose hands dressed her for it, and someday the folder Brooke is keeping, or the slip Randi keeps almost making ("he can dance, the bastard"), comes due.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens — the self-administered cold-water discipline, the honesty of "I saw her dangling the hook, and I bit it anyway." Randi deepens most: bare-faced, freckled, fast out of bed for her friend — and simultaneously slipping ("he can dance, the bastard"), instructing, possessive-playful ("my very own"), naming herself Evil Queen. Both truths on the page at once, neither canceled. Cassie in one wordless beat stays perfectly herself. Brooke consistent: warmth as intake procedure.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Well judged. The morning routine is brisk, the confession earns its length by being funny and sore at once, the fitting slows exactly where the chapter wants my attention. Nothing dragged; the scarf lesson flirted with lingering but paid off in "dinner in Paris."

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Strong. After the rupture of "Lesson," this is the necessary exhale — but it converts repair into escalation on the Randi front rather than marking time, and the enforced one-day delay leaves the Pace reconciliation cocked and loaded. Momentum building on both axes at once, which is the run's best trick.
