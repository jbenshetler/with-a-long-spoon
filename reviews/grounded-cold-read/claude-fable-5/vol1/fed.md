# Cold read (grounded) — Fed

## Reader reaction

Fed. After "Famished," of course it's "Fed." And the thing that got me about this chapter is that I honestly could not tell you, by the end, who was feeding whom — which I think is exactly the point, and it left me warm and uneasy in the same breath, the two feelings running on the same wire the way Vee's heat and shame do.

The surface of this chapter is one of the loveliest things the book has done with the Vee–Randi thread. Vee arrives glowing, does her right-side-of-the-menu arithmetic — "the small arithmetic, the kind that ended in Thursday, and found that Thursday would survive it" — and orders a mimosa like "stepping into a current that was already moving." I love how the book keeps faith with her money. It never forgets she's broke; the second mimosa she "did not price at all" is a whole tiny arc of loosening in six words. And the confession scene worked on me completely as warmth: Randi refusing the shame-frame, walking Vee down from "he was patient, he was gentle" to the toes, the bought underwear, "You drove over there sure," and then the question that turns the whole thing over — "So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" When Vee says "I wanted it. And I took it. For once," I felt it land like the tree-kiss and the truck-window did: Vee claiming her own want out loud, the mother's voice answered at last by another woman's voice saying "Good," hand over hers. That is the swoon of the chapter and I won't pretend it didn't get me. Vee's mother installed "floozie"; Randi is installing "lucky." As medicine for Vee it's real. I watched it heal her a little in real time.

And underneath it my skin was crawling, because I know what Vee doesn't, and this chapter is merciless about making me hold both. "How was he?" — Randi is asking Vee to review Randi's own lover in bed. Vee answers "hungry. For me... Like singular" — to the woman he is not singular with. "I'm running on fumes here" plays as brunch comedy and reads, from where I sit, like something closer to the literal truth: Randi opens every one of these meals with a framed-single disaster story (the lacrosse monologuer, the tickling idiot, now the beautiful nervous ex "in a hurry"), and I've stopped believing any of them are simply true. I've now watched the technique three times — self-deprecating sex story to open the door, then the warm catechism of small leading questions ("Did you do anything to get ready for him?" is this chapter's "Which hip?") — and the repetition no longer feels like the author repeating a beat; it feels like Randi's method becoming visible to me as method. What chilled me most, oddly, is what the page withheld: last chapter I got the leak — the tight smile, the thumb working the glass. This time Randi's surface never cracks once. "Randi had gone still, all listening" at the sheet-and-midnight-kitchen picture, and "she looked at her, like the planning was the part she liked best" — and I genuinely cannot tell whether that stillness is hunger, jealousy, tenderness, or professional satisfaction, and the page will not tell me, and I'm leaving it exactly that ajar. A perfect performance is scarier to read than a leaky one.

Two details did real work on me. First: the bistro has a sun porch. The fitting happened in a sun porch. Vee gets undressed — literally there, verbally here — in a bright glass room both times, and I don't think that's an accident of setting. Second: "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." Randi is curating which memories Vee keeps, telling her which version of her own life to frame. It's said with what reads as real feeling — and it's also editorial control over another person's interior, and both are true at once.

The curb kiss is the quiet escalation and it landed hard precisely because it's so small. Last time Vee explained it away ("It's a French thing") and then stood scanning the dorm windows in cold dread. This time she "half-expected it... and met it," and "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them." Two sentences, and the mouth-kiss has become ritual, normal, met. My body registered that as the most consequential thing in the chapter — more than any of the brunch talk. Something is being habituated, and Vee is doing the habituating gladly, and the checkpoint's memory of Randi's want ("Yes," bare, on the quad) sat behind every second of it.

Also worth saying: Vee kept the middle again. She ladled it out for Cassie and she folds it "back into its tissue" for Randi too — the sex itself stays hers. But Randi got something arguably more intimate than the sex: the premeditation. The toes, the drawer, the sureness. Vee thinks she protected the private thing; I'm not sure she protected the right one.

Erotic charge: mild but live — this is a retold-heat chapter, all clothed, and the retelling ("wrapped up in him, watching him cook") glows more romantic than hot. The one genuinely charged current is between the two women — Randi's frank appetite for the details, the kiss — and it simmers rather than peaks. After "Famished," that's clearly by design: exhale, digest. Fed.

Friction: almost none. If I reach for one, it's that a third consecutive Randi-lunch-extraction scene skirts sameness — Smoothie Bird, the green-velvet place, now this — and a reader less charged up on dramatic irony might feel the beat repeating. For me the escalation inside the repetition (door closed softly last time, door walked through this time; kiss dreaded last time, kiss met this time) kept it earning its place. No line felt like the author's thumb; Randi's dialogue stays perfectly deniable, which is the discipline this book runs on.

The titles. "Fed" after "Famished" is the best title pairing yet, and it cuts every direction at once: Vee fed — brunch, mimosas, absolution, "Good"; Randi fed — "Give me something to live on," running on fumes, taking the report of her own lover's mouth secondhand; and the long motif behind both — "Let's go feed you," the girl the jacket called "quietly starving," now saying she's never been so happy. Being fed is not the same as being nourished, and the title is quiet enough to let me wonder which this was. *A Polite Invitation* — this whole chapter is polite invitations, and I keep feeling the capital-I Invitation still ahead, the one all this warmth is a runway for. *With a Long Spoon* — the proverb about supping with the devil keeps gaining teeth: Vee at yet another table Randi picked, eating what Randi's warmth serves. (My packet carried no tagline this time — I only hold the jacket's remembered fragments, "quietly starving" and the promise that nothing would be taken she didn't hand over — and this chapter is precisely that promise executing: she is handing it over, gladly, piece by piece.)

What I want and dread next: I want the dress finished and the induction — Vee walking into a room in burgundy silk, seen. I dread, and am pulled toward, whatever the "I wanted it, I took it" lesson is *for* — my guess from here is that Randi is building Vee a self-story of agency precisely so that when the real invitation comes, Vee will meet it as a woman who wants and takes, and will never be able to say anything was done to her. The awful elegance of that possibility sat under the whole warm brunch like the check in its leather folder. Also: Cassie got only a mention this chapter, and the gap between what Cassie is given and what Randi is given keeps widening; that bill is accruing somewhere too.
