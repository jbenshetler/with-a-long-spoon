# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt me in the exact way this book has been promising to hurt me since chapter 3, and it did it with a hug.

Vee crying most of the way in from Roanoke, the dorm room dark, Cassie's side stripped bare — the chapter piles the aloneness on fast, and it earns it, because we've just spent two chapters watching her not-sleep in two different cold beds. So when she walks across campus "not trusting herself to look in the mirror on the way out" and Randi comes down the stairs fast the moment she sees the state of her — I felt the relief in my own chest before I felt the horror. That's the trick of this whole book and this chapter is its purest dose yet: the comfort is real, and the comfort is the other woman.

Because that's the knife, isn't it. Vee sobs into Randi's neck about the imaginary blonde Pace must be cooking for, and the actual other woman is the one holding her, stroking her hair off her face "like she would a child's," saying "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." I put the packet down at that line. The woman who doesn't have a face is Randi — I've watched her put "the face" back on at two different mirrors now. She's telling Vee the exact truth and it's the deepest lie in the room, and I don't think she's even doing it cynically. Every piece of her comfort is accurate. Pace *isn't* done with Vee — I just sat in the diner and heard him say "I want Vee. I miss her." The invented Sheri *is* a fear with a face painted on. Randi's read of Pace — "a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — is dead-on, and it's dead-on because she knows him in bed, which is the one credential she can never show. Her advice runs on stolen authority and lands as friendship. I don't know what to do with how much I believed her while knowing everything.

The hug. "She held the hug past the end of it, past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that, and neither of them let go." And Vee breathing her in — "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" — and not wanting to stop. After "Fairytale," where Randi decided to keep the word under cover and "let Vee arrive at it on her own," I read every held beat of that hug as Randi spending the only currency she's allowed herself. And Vee taking the smell of her the way she takes the flannel. The book didn't say a word about what either of them was doing and it didn't need to. That's the swoon of the chapter for me, and it's swoon with a dropped floor under it.

The coat plan is the other engine, and it works on me exactly the way it works on Vee. Randi walking her down off the ledge — "Say it." — making her put "slutty" in her own mouth, then converting it: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do." That is the shame-to-heat wire the whole book has been running since the truck, except now it's Randi's hand on the dial, out loud, in real time. And Vee feels it happen — "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." I believed her yes; the page shows her arriving at it herself ("And nothing under it" — she finishes the sentence, Randi only left it hanging). But I also watched the recruiter recruit, one more time, and "Randi always knew the shoes" — she's dressing Vee for Pace again, black heels pressed into her hands like the boutique, like the cognac pair. The raccoon line ("like a raccoon who lost a fight") made me laugh out loud in the middle of all this, which is its own kind of craft.

Then the Gstaad retelling, and this is where the chapter got genuinely sad. I just read that night from the inside. I know what "and then it got very late, and we went up" is covering — the cold that belonged to her and not the room, the mouth left printed on a man who never found her. Vee reads the stop as discretion, "the one thing even Randi kept behind the door," and Randi converts the whole dead center of it into a quip: "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." That line is the face put back on in narrative form — the truth turned exactly inside out, because the bedroom with John is the one room she *wasn't* alive in. And Vee, aching, envies the fairytale — "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it" — the precise thing Pace won't give her, held up by the one person who knows how hollow it rang. Each of them looking at the other's life and wanting it. Neither telling.

Trust and suspicion: unchanged in direction, sharpened in degree. I don't doubt Randi's love for Vee anymore — sweeping the Gstaad haul to the floor because "the wrecked girl in her doorway came first" settled that. What I can't settle is whether the coat plan is a friend repairing the best thing in Vee's life, or the project quietly resuming its forward motion, and I honestly think the book means it to be both in the same gesture. Brooke's beat at the door — warmth arriving "ahead of the chapter president," filing Vee as she goes — kept the sorority fuse warm without pressing it.

Friction: almost none. The retelling of Gstaad runs a little long given I read the original two chapters ago, but the edits are the point, so I forgive it. If anything, Randi's advice being *this* uniformly right shaved a hair off the realism — but then, she has sources.

The title: "My Friend Randi." It's Vee's possessive and it's heartbreaking on both ends — the friend she runs to, and the "friend" she has no idea she has; it also rhymes against Randi's own "Vee was the best friend she had ever had" from the chalet. It didn't give anything away; it just aches more after than before. *A Polite Invitation* — the coat with nothing under it is about the politest invitation a body can issue, and I notice the chapter's whole plan is an invitation composed for Vee by someone else. *With a Long Spoon* I still hold through the proverb-shaped dread the jacket left me with; no tagline came with this packet, so I won't quote one. Sup carefully, Vee.

What I want next: the doorstep, obviously — I'm pulled forward hard. Guesses from here: he opens the door and it works, maybe past working, into the best scene of the book; or something is on the other side of that door that isn't him alone. And somewhere behind it all, Randi just sent a woman naked under a coat to her own lover, and I don't believe the book will let that gesture stay simple.
