# Cold read (grounded) — Rye

*scene: scenes/rye.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch011 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

This chapter is four pages of two girls in a dorm room and I read it grinning with my hand somewhere near my sternum. After the mountain and the rain and the strip in the truck cab — a chapter that left me wrung out — "Rye" is the exhale, and it turns out the exhale is where the feeling lives.

What got me first: Vee tells Cassie *everything*. I braced, without noticing I was bracing, for her to edit — to keep the truck for herself, to give Cassie the mountain and the sandwich and stop at the rain. She doesn't. "And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." That "Me" is the same "Me" from the kiss retelling — "I pulled him back and I did it. Me" — and it lands even harder here because what she's claiming now is riskier. Her mother's voice was all over the cab in the last chapter, *floozy* in the exact word, and here, dried off, in front of the one person whose opinion could actually cut her, the voice is simply — absent. She reports the strip with her "whole face open" and no shame anywhere on the page. That's the swoon of this chapter for me, more than anything about Pace: watching Vee narrate herself as someone who wants things and acts on them, to a witness, out loud, and not flinch.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. She comes in doing her bit — "I had your blood type" is a perfect Cassie line, funny and also literally true of her love, she keeps a file on the people she'd grieve — and then the page does this beautiful quiet thing: "Cassie, who had been doing the dry thing, the file-revising thing, went still and stayed that way." She stops performing skepticism because Vee has slowed the way she slowed at the tree, and Cassie knows what that slowing means. Then: "'Vee,' Cassie said finally. Soft. Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." That's my favorite sentence in the chapter. The narration even tells me which inflection it *isn't* — no warning, no pity, no *oh honey*. Just the name, handled carefully. And Vee's "I know" — which could mean *I know this is big* or *I know what you'd say* or *I know I'm gone* — the page leaves it as all of them and I'm leaving it there too. Then the closer, four lines of pure Cassie liturgy: "Borrow real boots next time." / "I'm keeping the shirt." / "Keep the shirt." That's a blessing. Whatever shadow was on Cassie in "Goodbye, Vee" and the folded-away look at the Smoothie Bird, tonight she gives the blessing, and I believed it. If anything hurt, it's only my own knowledge that Cassie is blessing a thing she doesn't have the full file on — the one witness statement she can't take is the one that matters.

Because that's the other thing this chapter did to me, quietly: Randi is nowhere in it. Not mentioned once. A whole chapter with no machinery, no performance, no cold-citrus anything — just the two honest people in this book talking to each other — and it read like opening a window. Which is itself a little damning of everything else, and I felt that without the book saying it.

The retell also let me double-check Vee's account against what I watched happen, and she's accurate — that mattered to me. "He stood out there with his back to the cab so I could change, getting soaked on purpose so I wouldn't have anybody looking at me" is exactly what he did; she understood the gesture correctly and then *chose* to overturn it. She keeps the agency where it belongs: "I knocked on the glass and I called him back in." Nobody did anything to her. Again. The book keeps letting her say that and keeps making it true at the level of the scene, while I sit here knowing about the pointing game, and that gap is the ache I've been carrying for eight chapters now. It didn't sharpen here — this chapter is too kind for that — but it didn't go away either.

Heat: mild by design. The strip is present only as narration, secondhand, and even so "he looked at me like I'd handed him something" put a little charge back in the room. "And then we ate," delivered "the way other girls said *and then we kissed*," is the book winking at its own thesis and I laughed out loud — feeding IS the erotic register of this book, and Vee has learned to speak it.

Friction: almost none. It's short, and it's the second consecutive post-date debrief with Cassie in the pattern (ch 9 was the same shape), so a stricter mood might call it a repeated beat — but the content escalates so much (a kiss retold vs. a strip retold) and the chapter is so lean that it earned itself. If I squint, "and meant it" after "Look at you" is the narrator underlining something Cassie's dialogue already carried. That's the whole complaint.

The title: "Rye." The bread. She names it herself — "Rye bread and this horseradish" — and I love that the chapter is titled for the sandwich and not the strip. It's the book's whole value system in three letters: the meal is the intimacy; the food is what she leads with; hunger met is the love language. It also kept the title from giving anything away — I expected another food chapter and got the bravest retelling in the book so far. *A Polite Invitation* — my jacket memory is "quietly starving," and the promise that nothing would be taken she didn't hand over. This chapter is her handing things over with both hands and narrating the handing, proudly. *With a Long Spoon* keeps its distance for now; nobody supped with any devil tonight, unless the devil's flannel counts — she's wearing it buttoned to the collar and I can't make myself want her to take it off.

What I want next, and dread: the russet silk dress from the summit is sitting in my head like a loaded gun — "That sounds like a beautiful dress," no weight on it at all, and I'd bet real money (guess from here) that dress finds its way to her, and I both want the scene and worry about what it teaches her to accept. And Randi has to come back. Vee will retell this to her too — some version — and Randi will draw out the strip the way she drew out the kiss, and I already flinch at "Because of him" getting a sequel. Guess from here: the induction dinner and the dress are being set up as the occasion where the two halves of Vee's life meet.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Cassie — dorm room, that's the whole cast. Mentioned only: Pace (the entire subject), Meg (via the boots, implicitly), Vee's mother (offstage phone-cry, from the prior chapter's telling; here only implicit in the shame that didn't come).

**Characterization:** Vee deepened — the unedited retelling, shame conspicuously absent, is a real developmental beat, not a repeat of ch 9 but its escalation. Cassie deepened — the shift from bit to stillness to blessing is the most interior she's been while saying almost nothing; the earlier shadow isn't contradicted, just quiet tonight, and the page leaves it quiet. **Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate landing after two big physical chapters (the dinner/kiss, the hike/strip). Debrief-with-Cassie is technically a repeated slot in the rotation, but the escalation inside it keeps momentum; the run still builds. What it defers — Randi's absence, the dress — is starting to feel like held breath, in a good way.
