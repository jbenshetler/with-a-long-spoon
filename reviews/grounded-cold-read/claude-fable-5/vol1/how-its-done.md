## Reader reaction

This chapter did something to me that I want to be precise about: it swept me up and scared me in the same continuous motion, and it never let me separate the two. That's the book working at its highest pitch so far.

Start with the sweep, because it's real. The Mercedes with the window that drops itself half an inch to meet the door — "like the car was tucking itself in around her" — is seduction rendered as engineering, and I felt Vee's lift when the girl on the Hahn steps held the look a beat and Vee "felt herself become, for that beat, a person who rode in cars like this with people like Randi." I have been that girl in someone else's beautiful life. The restaurant is gorgeous — green velvet eating the sound, Sinatra doing witchcraft (the song choice made me laugh out loud, and I don't think it's an accident, and I don't care, it's perfect), the booth "you could have said anything in." And Randi's softened smile at the curb, "a thing spent rather than handed around" — Vee's careful skepticism about it, "maybe she was inventing the difference because she wanted it that badly," is exactly the honesty this book keeps giving her, and it made me trust her narration even while I trusted nothing else in the scene.

Because the center of this chapter is an interrogation conducted entirely in warmth, and it is the most quietly frightening thing the book has done since the bathroom mirror. I watched Randi extract the fitting from Vee one soft door at a time — "Nobody measures over a bra... it's not a thing, it's just how it's done," normalizing the shame so the confession can pass; then "Were there any — challenges?" with "its little hook in it"; then the leading catechism, "I bet he started at your hip. Didn't he." "Which hip?" "The left one." "Oh, all the best ones start at the left hip" — which the page itself tells us "meant nothing at all and landed like a benediction." That line is diabolical and I adored it and my skin crawled. Vee being "warmed and confirmed and led, one small yeah at a time, down and down" — I felt the technique working on me too. That's the horror of it: the questions feel like love. Cassie asked almost the same questions last chapter and they gave Vee something; Randi's take something, and Vee can't feel the difference because from inside, they're identical.

And then the cruelest, most interesting beat: "You don't have to tell me." Right at the lip of the deepest secret — the thing Vee "had folded up the smallest and pushed the deepest," never told even Cassie — Randi closes the door "very softly," and the laugh that always comes doesn't come, and Vee's secret is "still half out of her with nowhere to go." I don't know what that was. Mercy? Technique — leave her aching to finish the confession next time? Randi protecting herself from hearing it? The page holds it open and I'm holding it open, but the effect on me was almost physical: I wanted Vee to get to set it down, and the denial of that release felt like edging, conversationally. Which — given who Randi learned from — may be exactly what I watched.

The moment I keep turning over: Randi gone "very still," the smile with "some muscle she was holding," her thumb working the edge of the water glass — the same small hand-pressure tell as the bottle cap under her thumbnail in the aftercare, and I flinched recognizing it. Something in Vee's happy telling — "he looked at me and I was fine" — cost Randi something, and I genuinely cannot name what. Jealousy of Pace having that afternoon with Vee? Of Vee having that Pace — the one who turned the heat up hours early, when what Randi got was the bench and a self she's deadbolted away? Wanting? Vee cycles through two readings ("she thinks I'm awful" → "maybe Randi was moved") and settles on the kind one because she wants it true, and I don't get to settle at all. Good. I don't want to.

The tickling story I flat-out don't believe as told — or rather, I can't place it. Randi is Pace's secret girlfriend; a "beautiful idiot" and a Hermès scarf frames her single again, same as the "catastrophe date" at the Smoothie Bird. Is it invented curriculum? An old story redated? True, and Randi's leash longer than I knew? What I do believe is its function: it's a lesson, delivered laughing — "A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." She is teaching Vee the vocabulary of surrender over tomato soup, and Vee's response is the tell of the book: the heat that rises in her is "not the man, the man was nobody, but Randi, Randi naked and helpless and laughing... that finished surface finally cracked all the way open." Vee wants the real Randi — the one only Pace and I have seen. The want "she had no shelf for and would have died before naming at this table." That sentence hurt.

The money thread got me in a different place. Vee reading the right side of the menu "the way you read a road in fog," the Rombauer costing more than everything she'll allow herself, and then insisting on the check anyway — "the one thing she would not do at this table was let Randi buy her seat at it" — and Randi, watching, warm, not reaching for the folder again. That last clause is quietly devastating. Randi let her pay. Let the broke girl feel the drop she'd "feel on Thursday" rather than cost her the dignity — or rather than trouble herself; I can read it either way and both sting.

And then the kiss. I'd be lying if I said it didn't land on me as heat first — the hand at the hinge of her jaw, "soft, and in no hurry," and then the thing that makes it a real kiss and not a goodbye: the drawing-back, "the barest pause, and then back again with a little more, a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't." That second press is the whole chapter in miniature. Vee's two fingers to her mouth "without deciding to," the frantic explaining-away ("French... that's just how women like her say goodbye" — Vee, no), and then the cold pivot: had anyone seen? Her scanning the dark windows, counting them, is the first time Vee has ever had something to hide, and I realized with a small chill that this is what it feels like to be inducted into Randi's world — the world of alibis and watched windows and Brooke's warm surveillance. The chapter ends with Vee learning, in her body, the surveillance-fear Randi lives inside. That's the induction happening early, before any dinner.

Friction: almost none, honestly. If I squint, "the door, swung open and waiting" names the mechanism a touch plainly — but it's Vee's own half-knowing cognition, and she still walks through, so I'll allow it. The chapter earned every inch of its length; the lunch never dragged because the surface conversation and the under-conversation never once stopped moving against each other.

The title: "How It's Done" is Randi's own line — "it's not a thing, it's just how it's done" — the phrase that unlocks Vee's first confession. But of course the title is also the chapter: this is how it's done, a full demonstration of the craft — the softened smile, the story-as-lesson, the normalize-then-ask, the withheld release, the kiss placed and then explained away for her by her own hungry mind. It didn't spoil anything going in; coming out, it recolors the whole lunch into a masterclass I watched without being able to warn anyone. A Polite Invitation — the invitation is visibly arriving now, one course at a time, and the jacket's "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" has never sat heavier. The spoon at this lunch was not long enough by half.

New small thing I'm carrying: the white-gold band at Randi's wrist, "the one fixed thing in a wardrobe that changed itself daily," no stone, only weight. First time I've noticed it noticed. Guess from here, held loosely: it's from Pace, and it means something neither of them says.

What I want next: I dread and need the debrief I didn't get to see — does Randi carry this lunch back to Pace, and in what voice? I want Vee to get to tell someone the folded-up thing before Randi harvests it. And I'm afraid of those dorm windows — of Cassie, specifically, behind one of them. Guess from here: someone saw.
