# Cold read (grounded) — All the Time

*scene: scenes/all-the-time.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: ck-ch030 + raw ch031..ch037 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me like the mimosa Vee describes — "cold and bright and barely there" — and then I got to the curb and realized how much had actually been poured.

The setup is almost nothing: another Saturday brunch, mimosas, Randi in a dark green velvet room with Tony Bennett up in the rafters. Coming two chapters after "One Bite," which was also a brunch debrief, I braced for a repeat beat. It isn't one, and the reason it isn't is the thing Vee herself names: "There's no scene... It's just how it *is* now." She has nothing episodic to hand over, so what comes out instead is the whole shape of her happiness — and that monologue is the swooniest sustained stretch the book has given me in a while. "Like my mouth had been asleep since I was born and nobody told me. Like I'd been eating with the sound off." "I've got sex brain. I used to be a person with thoughts." "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around." I believed every word, I was happy for her, and the eggs Benedict with the yolk going everywhere — "this is the best part. The middle, when it goes everywhere" — is the feeding motif at its most shameless and I grinned at it. Vee ordering past the soup, letting her eye climb the price column: that's her whole arc in one small purchase.

And then the chapter turns its knife twice, both times quietly.

First: "It doesn't." Vee says "God, that sounds unhinged," and Randi — eyes gone bright and unfixed, a catch in her breath she doesn't quite smooth — says "It doesn't. Low, and not quite even." I felt that in my chest. Vee has just described, without knowing it, the exact thing Randi locked herself out of in the bathroom mirror in chapter one — being wanted with the self attached, being found and not flinching from it — and it lands on Randi like grief, or longing, or both. The page doesn't say which and I'm not going to say either; what the page gives me is a mask slipping in public for the first time, and Vee misreading it as pride that her Saturday story could move a woman like Randi. Same with "Just us" at the end — "softer than the plan called for, a small roughness under it, there and gone." Twice in one chapter the real thing leaks through the face. I don't trust Randi's hands and I believe her feelings more than ever, and this chapter made that split ache harder, not resolve.

Second: the photo. This is the chapter's real event and it's conducted so lightly I almost didn't feel the temperature drop. Watch the sequence: "You did *nothing* worth a picture last week?" — then the retreat, "You don't have to show me," sliding the phone back — and *that* is what changes Vee's mind. I recognized that move; it's the same soft door from the Mercedes lunch ("You don't have to tell me"), except now it works as bait instead of a lock. Then the cropped one isn't enough — "you only gave him *half*?" — and Vee, stung at the idea of being filed as a girl who flinched, hands over the whole uncropped nude: "I gave him *all* of it." The line lands as triumph in her mouth and something colder in my stomach. Just last chapter Pace promised "I'd never share your photograph" — and here is Vee sharing herself, freely, proudly, to the one person who debriefs with him. Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over. The tagline keeps being the whole book: "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." And "Showing him your toes, eh?" — softer, after the legs — made the hair on my arms stand up, because I remember the boutique, the thumb across the frosted-plum nail. Randi looked at that picture longer than a verdict needed, and the page let me watch her look.

The dramatic-irony line that actually hurt: "It'd be like stats, except we'd have picked it." Oh, Vee. Stats *was* picked — just not by you. And now she's building the architecture herself, inviting Randi into a second class, engineering the very access that was once engineered onto her, with Cassie explicitly out of the frame ("So it's just us"). Even her opening self-accounting — "She still couldn't get over being the one Randi picked" — uses the word *picked* like the book is daring me to say it out loud.

And the kiss. This is a real escalation and the prose knows it: Vee is the one who won't let it end, "leaning up the last half-inch after Randi as she started to draw back, taking a second of it for herself." And then — this is the part that moved me most on the Vee-and-herself front — "She noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." For thirty chapters she has smoothed this away, pinned her own hand to her knee, refused to say yes to it. This is the first time she notices and *lets it stand*. It's one sentence and it's enormous. Randi holding the look after, "a beat past what a goodbye needed," until Vee "had almost begun to feel it as strange" — the page held that door ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but something is moving on both sides of that kiss now.

Erotic charge: real but secondhand — the retold "sheets in both hands," the nude in Randi's hand, the prolonged kiss. It simmers rather than burns, and that's right for the chapter; the heat here is all in what's being handed across a tablecloth.

Friction: honestly minimal. Randi's "Does he have a brother?" made me wince in the good way — the joke only I can hear the inside of. If I reach for a complaint, the music/TV stretch is the chapter's one flat patch, but it reads as cooldown before the photo and I didn't stall on it. Small thing I clocked: Vee "said nothing about the number" on the check — the credit card is still down there, quietly compounding, and nobody in her world knows.

The title, "All the Time": it's Vee's answer to everything in this chapter — how often they have sex, how often she wants it ("the more we do it the more I *want* it"), the sex-brain running under her Tuesdays. But it recolors on the way out: Randi's "Just us," the second class, the kiss Vee wouldn't end — this is also a chapter about people wanting more time than they're allotted, wanting each other *all the time*. It illuminates without giving anything away. *A Polite Invitation* has never fit better — this chapter is wall-to-wall polite invitations: "You don't have to show me" is the politest and most effective one in the book so far, and now Vee is issuing invitations of her own ("Sign up with me?"). *With a Long Spoon* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — every yes in this chapter was freely, even joyfully given, and I counted three of them that scared me.

What I want next: I want Vee's noticing at the curb to go somewhere — she has finally let a want stand without arguing with it. What I dread, as guesses from here: that the uncropped photo comes up in a bed I'm not supposed to be thinking about; that the anthro class is where Randi's leaking feeling and the old design finally pull against each other; and that Cassie, now formally outside the "just us," sees the shape of it first.
