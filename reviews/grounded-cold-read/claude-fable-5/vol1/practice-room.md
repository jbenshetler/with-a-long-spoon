# Cold read (grounded) — The Practice Room

## Reader reaction

This chapter did the thing this book keeps doing to me: it made my skin go warm and my stomach go cold in the same paragraph, and I couldn't tell you which one arrived first.

Start with the reversal, because the page makes a point of it and it landed on me exactly as pointed: "For weeks it had gone the other way: Randi's hand at her elbow on the lawn... Now it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist." Vee has learned Randi's own move — cutting a person "gently out of the herd" — and she performs it on Randi without knowing where she learned it, while Cassie is left "saying *see you in the room* to the side of Vee's head." That little clause hurt. Cassie is the person who spent forty seconds of a Simpson's paradox lecture watching what a dress had done to her friend, and here she is being talked past, gathering her granola bar and her wrong textbook. Vee doesn't see it. I did, and it's the first time Vee's turn toward Randi has cost someone on the page in real time.

Then the practice room itself, and God, the room is perfect: "When the door shut the building went away. Not muffled. Gone. The kind of quiet you could hear your own blood in." A soundproof cell that freshmen "used to. You know." in — Vee's neck going hot before anything has even happened. The book keeps building these little chambers where the outside rules lapse (the truck cab, the sun porch, the alcove booth), and this is the smallest and most airless one yet.

And what happens in it is the most precisely uncomfortable seduction scene I've read in this book, and nobody touches anybody. Randi goes "very still and very interested, the way a cat goes still" — the same tight stillness I clocked in the velvet restaurant — and then she does surgery. "So. Did you deserve it?" And Vee laughs and agrees and "a moment late, she realized what she'd just agreed to." That sentence is the whole novel in miniature. Then Randi's forensic reconstruction — "His hand was wet... So his hand was wet" — putting Vee bodily back in the kitchen, and Vee's voice dropping "into something lower and slower that she didn't entirely recognize," and the horrible-wonderful recognition: "she wasn't only telling it. She was doing it again. Here. Now. In a piano closet with Randi's eyes on her."

Here's the cold under the warm. I heard Randi in Pace's bed two chapters ago: "She's not ready, though. She'll get there. You'll want to take your time." I cannot read this chapter as anything but Randi taking her time — the spanking story is a script being handed over, beat by beat, skirt up, counting out loud, the hand that "would slip... Right between," ending on "welcome. Believe me. Very welcome." Whether the mouse-who-roared ex is invented outright (her disaster-date stories have been cover fiction before) or some true history re-costumed, I genuinely can't tell — the slight, soft-spoken man is nothing like Pace, so it isn't simply the bench in a wig — but I know exactly what the story is *for*, and Vee doesn't, and that gap is where the chapter aches. The worst of it: it isn't a lie doing the work. Vee's response is real. Her body "had no vote." That's this book's signature cruelty — every yes freely given is the problem, and here is Randi manufacturing the conditions under which a yes will one day feel entirely like Vee's own idea. Because it will be.

And yet — I have to be honest about the other thing running in the scene — Randi's own want keeps leaking. The "fond and far away" face. Letting Vee hang on "Have you ever—" *kindly*. Watching her flush "enjoying it." And then the practiced exit, breezy, "as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And Vee couldn't tell whether she had." Neither can I, and I notice the page is careful to leave it exactly there, so I will too.

The bathroom is the best interior writing in the chapter and maybe in several chapters. "Her hand knew the way. It moved before she did. She caught it and put it flat against her own knee and held it there. Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." Yes to *what*, Vee? The kink? The story? The woman telling it? The sentence refuses to say and I believe that refusal completely — it's exactly how a person polices a want she won't look at squarely: not by not wanting, but by withholding the ratifying act. She soaks through sitting in a chair listening to a woman talk, and her entire defense is a technicality. And then she goes back in.

The ending is quiet and devastating in a two-inch way: the goodbye kiss "so worn-in now that Vee barely clocked it" — remember when she scanned the windows in cold fear? — and then "Vee's arms were slow coming down. She hadn't meant to hold on." Followed by the self-report I don't believe and am not supposed to: "She was still so full of him, of the whole weekend. She put it down to that." The chapter lets her have the alibi and lets me watch her file it.

Erotic charge: very high for a chapter with no sex in it. It peaks twice — Randi's "Right between... very welcome," which got me exactly where it got Vee, and the caught hand in the bathroom, which is somehow hotter for being refused. Nothing went slack.

Friction: one thumb-on-scale moment, lightly. The professor's outlier lecture — "The second kind comes in through the part that carries forward. It's in everything that comes after" — is the fourth time this stats course has narrated the novel over Vee's head (latent variables, Simpson's paradox), and this one is the most pointed yet; I felt the author looking at me. But the book has made a ritual of it and I've come to take it as liturgy rather than telegraphing, so it's a wince and a grin at once. That's my only snag; the Randi story's slightly staccato attribution ("Mine," Randi said, a beat after "the mouse that roars") read as her savoring the reveal, not as clumsiness.

The title: "The Practice Room" is doing at least three jobs and I admire all of them. The literal soundproof cell; the room where people "used to. You know." — practice of another kind; and the queasy third meaning that only assembles after the last page: this whole hour was *practice* — Randi rehearsing Vee in a role, Vee's body running the scene "again. Here. Now." before any scene exists. It gave nothing away going in and recolors everything coming out, which is this book's best titling mode. As for *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil with a long spoon; every yes freely given was the problem — this chapter is the most literal enactment of the jacket yet: the invitation is being drafted right in front of me, politely, in a friend's warm voice, and Vee is being taught to want to accept it before it's ever issued.

What I want next, and dread: I want the induction — the dress, the room turning — but what I now dread-want is the shape forming out of these three chapters: Randi in Pace's bed saying take your time, the swat that lit Vee up, Randi scripting the over-the-knee story, the word Vee "didn't own yet." A guess from here: the "threesome" architecture is moving from pointing to grooming, and Vee's own appetite — for the spanking, and for Randi, whose kiss she now holds onto a beat too long — is going to arrive at the door before the truth does. And I dread Cassie's face when Vee stops coming home at all. The wrong textbook is staying with me.
