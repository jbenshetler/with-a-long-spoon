# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch007 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

This chapter did the thing I keep being embarrassed by in this book: it made me completely happy for a woman I know is standing inside a trap — or inside something shaped like a trap, built by people who so far mean her nothing but pleasure, which may be worse, and I genuinely cannot tell yet, and the not-telling is the whole experience.

Start with what swept me, because it swept me. The text that "could keep." Vee reading it three times, pressing the phone to her chest — "a man saying a thing he'd already decided and leaving all the rest of the room to her." After watching every other boy in her life demand an answer "right then, on the phone... so they'd know where they stood," this lands like water on a burn. And Cassie — Cassie who teases her about everything and "didn't tease her about this" — delivering the verdict: "That's not a boy. That's a man." I felt Vee's flush from the inside. That's the swoon, plain, and I'm naming it.

The dinner is the best sustained sequence of the chapter. The unreadable menu — "beautiful nonsense. I have no map" — and then the title arriving out of Pace's mouth: "May I choose?" I actually held my breath at that, because I've heard this grammar before. This is the exact mechanism he ran on Randi — "What are we doing tonight," the evening handed across, the apple sliced thin because he'd noticed how she ate. Here he weighs the menu "not because he needed it but to weigh what he was choosing for her," and Vee's relief at surrendering the choice goes through her "more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." The page plays it as tenderness and it IS tenderness — and I've also watched this man build a bench in secret and wait weeks to reveal it. His care and his patience are the same instrument. The chapter never says that. I felt it in my teeth anyway.

And the eating. This book's hunger motif came home hard here. Randi got a permitted apple, sliced thin, portioned; Randi said "I can't eat that" to a cheeseburger. Vee's hollow "kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" — and she eats, unwatched, unportioned, "she wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was." After Water Wings, after the bleachers, after the suit that "gave her nowhere to hide," watching Vee get fed until she's full nearly undid me. The line that gutted me most, though, is the quiet one: "she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong... the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there, and she let it not be there." Because I know something she doesn't. There is a bill. Or there might be. Randi is nowhere in this chapter — not present, not even mentioned, not once in Vee's head — and that absence sat behind every warm sentence like a held note. Vee thinks she's in a love story with a stranger from a coffee shop. I watched that stranger get pointed at her by his girlfriend on a quad.

The ending is close to perfect. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — the old reflex arriving "at the worst possible moment, with its rule already in her mouth" — and he doesn't argue, doesn't lean, just smiles "like she'd told him something true and fine about herself" and starts them back. And then her feet don't go. The hand pulls taut. "But... I'm making an exception." She chooses. After a chapter titled with HIS asking — after an evening she handed to him — the last choice is hers, freely made, and it's glorious, and it's also exactly how this man works: he engineers the space in which the other person's yes arrives feeling entirely their own. I watched him do it to Randi on the bench. I can't decide if that's generosity or the most refined control I've ever read, and I notice the book is refusing to decide for me.

Characters, where they've moved: Vee keeps deepening — the elevator doors closing on her because she forgot to get off, the "bill coming due" interiority, the mouth-watering at words she can't read, "content for once not to know" (which is romantic on the page and chilling one inch below it, because there is so much she doesn't know). Pace is consistent to the point of unease — the same unhurried attention, the same courtliness ("May I" is now a signature; "May I call you?" last chapter, "May I choose?" this one), and one new strange detail: "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." The page flags it as a pattern and doesn't explain it, and I'm filing it — a man with private rules he keeps even while eating. Christine knowing him warmly humanizes him; a man a tired restaurant woman is glad to see is not nothing. Cassie broke my heart a little in two lines — after "Goodbye, Vee," her plain benediction here reads like a person handing over something that costs her.

Suspicion, precisely: nothing in THIS chapter earned doubt on its own. Every ounce of my unease is imported from the chapters before — the Pointing Game, the engineered collision, Randi's deadbolt decision. That's the book's design working on me, not a false note.

Friction: almost none. If I push, the middle of the dinner ("the evening went soft at the edges... she couldn't, later, have laid out what they talked about") deliberately echoes Vee's blank-transcript hour with Randi at Chi Latte — the repetition is clearly meaningful (Vee dissolves the same way under both of them, which is its own red flag about Vee), but it's the second time I've been told the words didn't stay, and a third would wear.

Titles: "May I Choose" seemed innocuous until the table, then snapped into place — and then kept opening: he asks it, she grants it, and then the last beat quietly reverses it, her exception being the chapter's real answer. It gave nothing away and paid off twice; that's a good title. *A Polite Invitation* — this whole chapter is one; his every move is a politely extended invitation that leaves the room to you. And *With a Long Spoon* — I keep hearing the proverb about supping with the devil, and in a chapter that is literally an hour of a man feeding a hungry girl, that echo made my skin prickle. I was given no cover tagline in my packet, so I have nothing to hold there.

What I want next: the collision. Vee's two worlds — Randi-the-friend and Pace-the-man — cannot stay partitioned, and Vee doesn't know they're one world. Guess from here: the threesome frame from the Pointing Game will surface as another polite invitation, and Vee will say yes to it believing she's choosing. I dread how much she'll like it. I dread more what happens when she learns the geometry of how she was found.
