# Cold read (grounded) — Cropped

## Reader reaction

After the last two chapters — the MIRH bomb going off silently in Randi's face, and then the hallway undressing and the flannel handed over to be refilled — this chapter is quiet the way a held breath is quiet, and I was glad of it. Two trays, a cafeteria, dark already down over the parking lot. And then it turns out the quiet chapter is about the thing the whole book has been doing: who gets which version of her.

The title told me where to look before the page did — "Cropped" — and then the page delivered it almost immediately: "I cropped it before I sent it. The version they have stops at the waist." My stomach did a small drop at that, because I was at the brunch where Randi stung her with "you only gave him half?" and Vee answered by handing across the whole nude — "I gave him all of it." So now I can see the full ledger: her parents get the waist-up crop, Cassie gets the edited middle, Randi gets everything, Pace gets the body itself. Nobody gets the whole of her in words — that's the ending of this chapter — but the photo has become a literal map of her disclosures, and the chapter knows it without ever saying so. That restraint is why it worked on me.

The parents material is the best writing here and it moved me more than I expected. Dad burning the rolls on purpose. Mom crying twice, carrying the printed photo in her purse, taking it out "to show people who'd already seen it" — that detail is so exactly a proud mother that it warmed me — and then, in the same purse, the other mother: "She didn't say anything, just touched her own collarbone while she looked." I've been hearing "floozie" arrive in Vee's head at every arousal for forty chapters; here the source of that voice appears in the flesh and doesn't even need words, just a hand at her own collarbone. Vee cropped the photo at the waist precisely so the slit — the deliberate long stride Cassie photographed — never reaches Ohio. She protected the bravest thing she's done from the person who taught her to be ashamed of it. That's not a plot beat, it's a character beat, and it landed.

And Dad "pricing" the dress — for a girl who reads menus price-column-first, of course her father prices gifts. The economics of the family run right through her. Then the line that made me laugh out loud and then sit with it: "Honestly, my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's Vee at her sharpest — funny, and true about her father, and true about how strange Pace actually is. Her little essay on weird versus wrong ("Weird is something that doesn't fit anywhere in how he sees the world") ran a touch polished for cafeteria speech — I felt the author's articulacy in it for half a second — but Vee is the applied-math girl who's been finding words for herself all book, and it stayed inside her voice by a hair.

"I'm not ashamed of it. The opposite, if anything." I want to flag this because it's the arc, said plainly: the girl who wore a cardigan like an apology just told her best friend she's proud that her boyfriend sews. And yet the chapter refuses to let that be a victory lap, because Cassie — bless her four-words-a-day precision — asks the one question Vee can't crop: "So what is he... Not to your dad. To you." And Vee, who whispered "Peter" into his sleeping ear, who admitted love to herself in chapter 35, has "nothing that fit in Cassie's frame, not the right word for what he was... not the version of herself that stood up cleanly in the telling." I don't read that as shame — she just disclaimed shame — I read it as the truer problem: there is no clean version, because the story of how it went includes the fitting and the swat and the bet and Famished, and the word for what he is might be bigger than "boyfriend," which she could only say to her parents "with a question mark on it. Out loud. Like I was asking them." That question-mark line hurt in the good way.

"Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" "Both. Probably both." — and then the last two sentences, which are the whole chapter in eleven words: "She didn't push at it. Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not." That "a little, not" got me. She wants to be made to say it. She wanted Pace to fight the five study nights, and now she wants Cassie to push past the door she's holding shut. Everyone in her life keeps courteously honoring her closed doors, and some part of her is starving for someone to knock anyway. Given who taught the whole cast that a closed door is sacred, there's an irony under this scene I can feel but the page never touches.

Cassie deepened a notch for me here, in her small way. Her own homecoming — "they don't quite see me as an adult yet," the sister's baby, the dork husband — is the first time she's had a life reported on the page rather than a reaction, and her interviewing style ("Careful, exact, not prying," waiting instead of prompting) is the anti-Randi. Randi extracts by feeding stories back; Cassie extracts by silence and one scalpel question, and takes no for an answer. Reading them side by side across these chapters, I trust Cassie's method with a whole heart — and I notice Vee opens further for the other one.

No suspicion earned by anything in this chapter itself — no Randi, no Pace on stage, nobody performing. The unease I carry in is all inherited: the cropped photo now exists in a purse in Ohio, the uncropped one in two phones, the full nude in a third, and I know things about the man who made that dress that her proud father would price very differently.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and rightly — one flicker at "Pace takes that dress off me," which is memory-heat, not scene-heat. This is a connective, interior chapter and it doesn't pretend otherwise.

Friction: almost none. The middle run of Vee's paragraphs goes four or five quoted blocks with Cassie nearly silent, and it edges toward monologue — but "That pulled a short laugh out of Cassie" arrives just in time, and honestly Vee monologuing at Cassie is how their friendship has always metabolized things.

Titles: "Cropped" is a perfect fit — it names the photo in the first minutes and then quietly widens to every trimmed version of herself she distributes; it recolors backwards too, all the way to the "edited version" she gave Cassie in chapter 19. It didn't spoil anything; it taught me how to read the chapter. *A Polite Invitation* keeps accruing — her parents just received the politest, most cropped invitation into her life imaginable. *With a Long Spoon* I still hold through the devil-supping proverb I remember from the jacket (my packet this time carried no tagline text beyond the title, so I won't quote one); the "quietly starving" note I remember from the jacket copy is literalized here again — a whole chapter at dinner where the food is barely eaten and the real hunger is for a word she can't say.

What I want next: the dread is unchanged and specific — Brooke and the white pickup, the anthro class that's "just us," MIRH sitting in the project write-up, and now this new soft fuse: Cassie's question is going to come back. Guess from here: the next time someone asks Vee what he is, it won't be someone she can say "both" to and be let off — and I half expect the person who finally makes her say it out loud will be Randi, which is the most dangerous possible audience for the true answer.
