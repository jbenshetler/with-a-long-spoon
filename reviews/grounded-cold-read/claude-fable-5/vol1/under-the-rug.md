## Reader reaction

This one hurt, and it hurt in the exact place the last two chapters have been pressing. After "Seconds" left her sentence unanswered and filed in the pile she doesn't look at, this chapter opens with the word itself — "warm all through and the word was right there, simple, wanting out" — and then spends its whole length showing me what a person does instead of saying it. What she does is misfire. "Is this what we are now?" comes out "at the wrong size for the room," and I winced in recognition, because that is precisely how a held-back declaration exits a body: sideways, sharp, aimed at the wrong target. "She lay still and listened to herself not having whispered" is as good a line about regret as this book has produced.

How I feel about the two of them, right now. Vee has never been more human to me than in this chapter, and I mean that as the highest compliment even though she behaves badly in it. She picks a fight she knows is false — the book tells me straight out: "It wasn't true. Some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold that night, knew who'd carried a pie up whose porch steps after — and she said it anyway, because she needed the line of it to hold." That's the interior life I read this book for: the want she won't look at squarely, converted into an accusation she can hold. And then the devastating little self-administration afterward — "she told herself the thing that let her go: that she had forgiven him. It sat well in her — *forgiven*, warm and large and easy to wear." She hasn't forgiven him anything; he did nothing to forgive. She has manufactured a shape she can rest inside, and the chapter is honest enough to let the shape collapse by the last page: "It did not feel like plenty now."

Pace — something new came onto the page here and I want to sit with it carefully. The Chili's scene showed me a Pace I have never seen: eyes dragged off her face by every moving screen, the fork squared and re-squared against the table edge, a "flicker of plain irritation" he hides by dropping his eyes "as if he didn't want her to have it," and then shoulders that come down the moment he's back in the truck. His own account: "Not the sound of it. The whole of it, all at once. In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you." I believed him. It landed as true on the page — the man who lives alone past the gravel, builds his furniture, keeps his house quiet, "I don't go back" — a whole prior pattern suddenly has a floor under it. The bringing-back of his eyes "had a cost in it she could suddenly see, a small effort where there had never once been effort with him." That's not a man bored with her. That's a man paying full price to be in a room that's eating him.

And yet — here is my one live suspicion, and I want to be precise that the page didn't state it; my memory did. Vee's actual question was "I'd just like to know if we'll ever be seen in public again." I know what she doesn't: there is a sorority full of women who know Randi has a boyfriend named Pace, a sister who's already clocked the white pickup, and a whole arrangement that survives on Vee being kept off the public board. His "too loud" explanation felt sincere — and it is also, incidentally, perfectly convenient for a man with structural reasons not to be seen out. The chapter holds both without winking, and I'm holding both too. I don't disbelieve his sensory truth. I just notice it never has to compete with the other reason.

Swoon — yes, real, and complicated in the best way. The eating-scene inversion got me before the bed did: the nachos "to food what a billboard is to a painting," every color "squeezed out and stirred together at once until you got the one muddy brown you always got" — and then the gutting turn, "He had ruined it for her, was the thing. Not just this." The feeding motif that has run since the apple slices in chapter 1 just showed its cost: he has recalibrated her whole palate, and there is no going back to fine. And then the sex. The oral scene is patient and specific and worked on me, but the peak — for heat and for meaning at once — is his control finally breaking: "he got over her and into her almost in one motion... like a man who'd run clean out of the strength to wait... shaking, undone, hers." She has been wanting that exact loss of control, I've watched her want it, and the book gave it to her fully — and then twisted the knife with perfect economy: "with nothing left in him to keep behind any wall, he hadn't said it." Everything but the name. The post-coital pages — his spend still in her, "marked all over with where he'd been," awake while he sleeps easy — are the ache the whole chapter was built to deliver, and I felt it in my chest, not my head.

Also worth saying plainly: Randi does not appear and is not named once, and I still felt her — this is the first chapter in a long while with no second channel on the page, and the claustrophobia of just-the-two-of-them is part of what makes Vee's "we don't leave the house" land.

Friction — almost none. If I reach: the narration is very explicit about Vee's self-deceptions ("It wasn't true. Some back room of her knew"; "she told herself the thing that let her go") where the book sometimes trusts me to catch them cold. But this chapter is *about* her curating her own account of the night, so the naming felt like the subject, not a thumb on the scale. I bought every beat, including the meanness — "the needing made her mean" earned itself.

The titles. "Under the Rug" is exact and a little cruel: the false accusation swept under, the fabricated "forgiveness" laid down over the night like a rug over a stain, the sentence from "Seconds" already in the pile she doesn't look at — this chapter adds a whole room's worth. It didn't give anything away going in; coming out, it recolors the ending, because the last page is the rug failing — "the not-knowing ground in her and would not quiet." *A Polite Invitation* keeps accruing: everything here was offered, asked, answered — "Okay. Where do you want to go tonight?" is politeness as a wall you can't punch. *With a Long Spoon* — the devil-supping proverb the jacket handed me — sits over the whole book; I wasn't given a tagline line in this packet beyond the jacket's "quietly starving," which this chapter literalizes brutally: she is starving *at* a full table now, fed everything but the one thing.

What I want and dread next, guesses from here: I think the withheld word is now a live fault line — three chapters running have circled it (naming him to her parents, the crackers-and-meal sentence, now "the name"), and something has to give: either she says it and finds out what his silence is made of, or the pressure finds the Randi channel — because the last time Vee had something she couldn't resolve, she took it to brunch. I dread, specifically, Vee saying "if we're just sex" or "will we ever be seen in public" to Randi, who knows exactly why they aren't. That conversation would cost someone something.
