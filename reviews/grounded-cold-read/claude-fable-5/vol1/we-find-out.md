## Reader reaction

This one got me. I want to be careful and honest about the order in which it got me: first the play, then the heat, then the tenderness, and only afterward — sitting back — the one line that dropped a cold coin down my spine.

The opening is Vee reaching first, which the chapter itself flags — "she had never once been the one to reach first" — and the smallness of *I miss you. Can I come by early?* sitting unanswered long enough that "she felt it, the nakedness of having asked" is exactly the Vee I've been rooting for: brave in increments, and this is an increment. Then she comes at three when he said four. She's learning to take. I loved her for it before anything else happened.

And then the chase, which is the most purely joyful thing this book has done in a while. The discovery sequence is wonderful — she hits his thigh and the wall *flinches*, "she had never once seen anything she did land on him... and she had just watched it flinch." That standing-off astonished part is the book's best instrument: Vee watching Vee become someone. For a few pages the power ran her direction and it was giddy and I believed every second of it.

Then he catches her, and one open swat, and she goes still — and he says **"There it is."**

That's the coin. I know that construction. "There you are, Randi" is what he said on the bench the night he broke Randi open. Here it's "there IT is" — not her, *it* — the thing he's been waiting to feel arrive in her body since the kitchen swat, maybe since the beginning. It's said warmly and it lands warmly on her and I still felt watched by a connoisseur. This is the double-vision the book keeps forcing on me: in the scene, a man delighted to find his lover wants what he wants; in the aggregate, a man who has done this before, expertly, to the friend who told Vee the spanking story in the first place. And that story *rose up in her* right on schedule — "Randi's story rose up in her like something she'd swallowed: would she have to count out loud?" The pipeline worked. Now the consent, because I care about this more than anything and the page did the work. The bet — "Either you admit you lost, or we find out" — could be a trap in a worse book. Here: she says "Don't," and **"The hand stopped. He didn't take it away."** He asks. She says no to admitting. He waits, "let the quiet run on." And then "her own head went up and down, small. She dreaded it, and some part of her wanted him to know anyway." And the hand goes up "slow enough that she could have stepped out of it the whole distance and didn't." That is a yes rendered all the way onto the page — not extracted, not assumed, given in the body with room to revoke it at every inch. Her mouth keeps saying "I don't" while her hips lift to meet his hand, and the book is scrupulous that the lie is to *herself*: "she could not have said which one of them she was lying to." I'll note one honest wrinkle: "No. Slow. That's part of the bet too" — "It hadn't been part of any bet he'd named." He expands the terms downstream of her yes, and the page notices it even as she doesn't argue. That's not a violation; it's the shape of him — he sets rules and she assents inside them — and I'm glad the prose kept its eyes open there instead of smoothing it.

The title: before reading, "We Find Out" told me nothing. Her yes was a small nod, freely given, and "the ease went through her worse than any sting had." *With a Long Spoon* keeps humming underneath: this supper was delicious, and I still know whose table it is.

What I want next: I dread the debrief. "No Randi to be told" — but there will be a Randi to be told, Monday, and I both want and fear the scene where Vee tries to ladle this out and Randi asks "Did you deserve it?" again. Guesses from here: the scrunchie plenty and the bag mean cohabitation-by-degrees; the "There it is" makes me think the bench is closer than Vee knows; and Randi's attachment plus this news feels like pressure building toward whatever the invitation was always for. Also, quietly: the scar got touched again and not asked about — "the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep" — and I think that door has a time on it that's coming.
