# Cold read (grounded) — Turned Up

## Reader reaction

This one made me grin at the ceiling the way Vee grins at the underside of the top bunk. After the long held breath of "A Round" — an entire chapter of standing still under his hands — this is the exhale, and it's Cassie's room, which by now is where Vee's happiness goes to be counted out loud and made real.

What got me first is what Vee *doesn't* tell. She gives Cassie the burgundy, the riser, "the bra too!", the heat, the hem — and she never once mentions the wet satin, the soaked spot, the whole slow catastrophe of her own body that she nearly died over on that box. The thing she calls "the thing she'd been keeping all the way at the bottom" is the grinding — which means the satin is kept *below* the bottom, untold even in triumph. That's the interior work I read this book for: the confession she stages versus the shame she still can't hand anyone, not even Cassie. And she narrates the near-miss of it — "I kept waiting to feel wrong about it… I didn't" — as if the not-feeling-wrong were simple, when I watched it cost her a screwed-shut-eyes eternity one chapter ago. She's not lying. She's telling the story the way she needs it to be true. "She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care" was the line back in the dining hall, and this chapter quietly runs on it.

The swoon, and it's a real one: "He said it and then he waited… He left it open. He left me a door." Vee said that. In a book where Randi locks doors behind her, where Daphne "built a door," where the one closed door in his house was the bedroom he didn't open — Vee, knowing none of that freight, reaches for *door* as her word for being asked without the word. And Cassie's verdict, flat and exact: "He asked." Then "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" — she's internalized his grammar now, she hears "May I" in a look. That's the courtship working at the level of language, and it melted me even as the checkpoint in my head whispered what else his patience has built.

The "Oh, Vee" landed hard because of "Rye." There, the narration made a point of it: "Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name." Here, when the thermostat detail comes out — the detail Vee "had not meant to say this part yet, or maybe not at all" — Cassie goes very still and finally says it: "Oh, Vee," her face gone soft. The escalation is measured in two words across two chapters, and it's Cassie who gets to mark it. That's beautiful, economical writing, and it also scared me a little, because Cassie softening on Pace means the last skeptic is coming over, and I remember what Cassie doesn't know.

The comedy genuinely works. "Are you sure he's not gay?" with the finger-counting — "He's a bodybuilder who makes dresses" — is broad, but it's earned broad, the exact joke a best friend makes, and Vee's rebuttals are her boldness fully arrived: "He doesn't kiss like he's gay," then the ass-pat "brisk and clinical, like she was indicating it on a diagram," then the reveal — "*I* was grinding on *him*." I laughed out loud. And I'm holding one thing exactly as ajar as the page holds it: "I could feel his… *package*. Right here. When he was pinning the back seam." I didn't see that in "A Round" — I saw her "bent into it more than the dress asked" when he knelt behind her. Either the page gave me less than Vee got, or Vee's story is getting better in the telling again. The chapter doesn't rule, so neither do I — but the ambiguity is delicious either way, because both readings are *her*.

Where the chapter aches, it's entirely dramatic irony, and the page never has to lift a finger: Randi is nowhere in this chapter — not mentioned once — and that absence is why the warmth feels so unguarded. Nobody in that dorm room knows about the bench, the pointing game, the plan. He built a bench for one woman and a riser for the other — furniture for each of them, each disguised as something innocent — and I can't unknow that while Vee says "Like I was supposed to be there." No suspicion of anyone *on this page* — the page is clean, the chill is all mine, carried in from chapters these two have never read.

Friction, honestly: this is the second full Cassie-debrief in four chapters (after "Rye," with "What to Wear" as a friends-variant between), and the front half re-treads material I just lived through — the bra, the riser, the measuring rhythm. I skimmed a little at "He started at the top and worked down… Close, and then not close," because I'd had it at full resolution one chapter ago. The back half redeems the beat completely — the shirt clicking into place for Cassie ("Vee. The shirt."), the heat confession, the grinding reveal — but the event-then-debrief rhythm is now a visible pattern, and I'd feel it as a sag if the dress delivery gets the same treatment.

Erotic charge: low flame, but present — it's all retold, clothed, dorm-lit, and yet "I locked eyes with him. A beat. And then I let him look" put a little heat back in the room, and the grinding confession is charged precisely because she's gleeful about it. Nothing clinical; it just isn't this chapter's job.

The title: "Turned Up" is doing at least four things at once and I admire all of them. The heat, turned up hours before she arrived — the chapter's emotional payload, the proof of premeditation-as-care. Her chin coming up on the box, the posture that "came undone" upward. Vee turned up at his house at last. And the volume of her — the laugh, the boldness — turned up a notch a chapter. It gave nothing away going in; it recolored on the way out, which is this book's habit with titles. *A Polite Invitation* keeps sharpening: "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock" — "the least information a human being has ever sent me" — is the politest invitation yet, and she read it a thousand times. *With a Long Spoon* — the devil's-supper proverb — hums under the shirt folded on her pillow: she's sleeping in something he made, wearing his making against her skin, and calling it comfort. (My packet this time carried no jacket tagline to quote; what I hold from memory is the jacket's promise that nothing would be taken she didn't hand over — and this chapter is her handing it over, delighted, in her own words, to her best friend.)

What I want next: the dress finished; the call; the induction with her name read out at the front of a room in silk mixed for exactly her. What I dread — guesses from here: Randi has to re-enter this glow, and the plan with her; the induction dinner or the dress night feels like where the two halves of Vee's life converge; and Cassie, newly softened, is the one who'll be standing closest when whatever's coming lands.
