# Cold read (grounded) — Dear

## Reader reaction

This chapter did something to me that almost no chapter of anything does: it made me feel two temperatures at once, the whole way through. Through Vee's eyes it is warm — a golden afternoon, a new friend, the giddy retelling of the best date of her life. Through mine it is cold water down the spine, because I know what Vee doesn't: the man she's glowing about is Randi's, and the pointing game came first, and every question Randi asks over that smoothie is a debrief.

The moment that actually made me put my hand over my mouth was small: "How's it going with the, the hottie. What is his name." Randi snapped her fingers twice, reaching after it and not catching it. "Paul?" — Randi performing not-quite-remembering the name of the man whose bench she begged on. And I recognized the move, because I watched Brooke do the exact same name-fumble to Randi in the sorority common room ("I kept thinking you'd said *Chase*"). Randi is running Brooke's interrogation technique on Vee, warm as sunlight, and Vee hands over everything. Whether Randi learned it from Brooke or they both drank it from the same well, it landed on me like a signature.

Then the fabricated Friday. "My love life is a disaster. Friday. A date. Tall, dreamy, dressed like money…" I have no way to be certain it never happened, but everything in me reads it as a cover story — a decoy love life so Vee never wonders who Randi actually goes home to. And the story's *moral* is the part that made me go still: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." That is, nearly word for word, an advertisement for Pace — the listener, the carer — planted right before Vee testifies about him. Whether Randi built it as steering or whether that's simply her real creed leaking out sideways, the page doesn't say, and I'm holding it open. Either way it worked on Vee like a key.

And here is where I have to be honest about the swoon, because the chapter has one and it got me even knowing what I know. "Vee's my dear friend," with the cool damp hand closing over Vee's on the white tabletop, in front of Brooke — that landed. Because I know something Vee doesn't there, too, and it cuts the *other* way: Brooke is surveillance. Brooke grilled Randi about her weekends four chapters ago. For Randi to take a strange, cardiganed, non-Greek girl's hand in front of her chapter president and name her *dear* is not free. Vee intuits it — "She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it in front of them. She knew only… that it had cost something" — and Vee is *right*, more right than she can know. So the tenderness may be real even while the friendship is a recruitment, and that doubleness is exactly the kind of thing I read this book for. I don't get to resolve it. The page doesn't.

The Cassie thread hurt most, and it hurt because the book made Vee do it to herself. Cassie's look going past her to Randi and coming back "folded away" — that's the third time now Cassie has seen something and pocketed it ("Goodbye, Vee" still sits in me like a stone). And then Vee, unprompted, gatekeeping: "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one… Not her thing." Randi actually offered to drag Cassie along; it was Vee who closed the door, then reasoned herself comfortable — "nobody ever called that anything but healthy. The thought settled her comfortably down into herself. Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That last line is this book's whole method in miniature — Randi under the duvet not-crying, and now Vee letting the turned-over thing lie. These women are twins in the one skill that will ruin them.

Characters, where I stand: Vee I love and fear for — her happiness is real and partly manufactured, and watching her carry "she had a place now, and that the place was here, next to Randi" home like a prize is nearly unbearable. Randi I cannot simplify anymore: predator's tradecraft, and possibly the truest feeling she's had since the bench. Her drink "the color of a bruise" — beet, wheatgrass, *egg white* — her mother's regime in a cup, ordered like a sentence handed down, while Vee orders oat milk "because Randi was standing right there." The influence is already metabolic. Brooke remains the most quietly frightening presence in the book; her smile "took it all in… and did not change." The tall companion who inventoried Vee like an object and said nothing — the page doesn't name her and I won't guess — but "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" is a line I'll keep.

Erotic charge: low and deliberate, and right for the chapter. The one live wire is Randi drawing out Vee's confession — "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Not a question. Randi holding Vee's surrender up to the light and savoring it, then "Mm," and letting go. There's appetite in that beat, unmistakably, and Vee reads it as kindness. And "Because you couldn't not. Because of him" — Randi supplying Vee's feelings in words a half-step truer and more helpless than the ones Vee had prepared, and Vee taking them. That's seduction grammar, aimed at a friendship. It simmered.

Friction: very little. The room-read at the Smoothie Bird runs close to the Chi Latte room-read structurally — bright matched girls, Vee covered "like an apology," the high table shelf — but it's earned as an echo, because Vee's position in it has changed: last time the high table felt like belonging, this time she "understood at once that she had been set up on a shelf" and got rescued by the hand. If the next chapter gives me a fourth bright-room-of-girls set piece I may tire; not yet.

The title: "Dear" is a beautiful trap. Going in it meant nothing; then "Vee's my dear friend" lands and it seems solved; then the older sense arrives — *dear* as in costly. "She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it." A girl raised to "register cost by appearance" spending something real, and a friendship whose price Vee hasn't seen yet. It gave nothing away and recolored everything after. *A Polite Invitation* keeps sharpening — this whole book is made of courtesies with machinery behind them ("May I choose?", "May I call you?", and whatever invitation is coming for Vee), and *With a Long Spoon* — the proverb about supping with the devil keeps getting closer to the table. My packet included no jacket copy or tagline this time, so I've only the titles themselves to steer by.

What I want and dread next, as guesses from here: the hike off six-fourteen puts Vee at Pace's house — the house with the bench in it — and I dread and need that geography collapse. I expect the "threesome" architecture to keep assembling under Vee as friendship and romance in parallel, each hand innocent-looking. And I keep waiting for the moment the two lines cross visibly — Vee saying "Pace" to Randi one time too warmly, or Brooke connecting a white pickup to a stats-class girl. Cassie is my heartline; I'm afraid the book is teaching me why she said goodbye.
