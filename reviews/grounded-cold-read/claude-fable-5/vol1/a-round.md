## Reader reaction

I read this whole chapter with my shoulders up around my ears and my heart going, and when I hit "He hadn't looked down. The whole time she'd been dying he hadn't looked down once," I put my hand over my mouth. That's the chapter. Everything else is the staircase up to that landing.

What this chapter does — and does almost unbearably well — is take the oldest scene in erotica, a woman undressing for a dressed man, and run it entirely through Vee's interior weather: the mother's voice arriving on schedule ("A small ugly word arrived from the back of her mind, in her mother's register: stripper"), the body doing what the body does, the soaked satin she "learned to manage out of existence in public and could not, here... manage out of anything." I have never seen involuntary arousal written as shame this honestly — not as a naughty thrill, but as "the slow appalling spread of evidence," her hope made visible, "sized to be seen across the room." That paragraph hurt. And then the book does the thing I'd have said couldn't be done: it redeems the exposure without ever letting him take anything. He's one slow knee-turn from "where she was wettest," she screws her eyes shut braced for the grin she's "been bracing for her whole life" — and when she opens them he's craned all the way up, waiting for her eyes, having never looked down. "Delighted by her. The whole fact of her, up there." I melted completely. And then her spine rearranges itself — the apologetic stoop coming undone, the rising onto her toes "lifting toward something she had no name for" — and his little *hmm* and the sideways cut of his eyes at her heel. That whole exchange, wordless, is the best thing in the book so far.

The eroticism is superb precisely because it's rhythmic and withheld: "closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil" — he engineers a tide and she's "standing on nothing but the wanting it had left stacked up in her with nowhere to go." The under-bust measurement, her shallow breath lifting her into his hands; the calloused fingertips arriving "all at once"; his hands taking "the whole shape of her" for one held beat. And I laughed out loud — actually laughed — at her bra question and "I was worried I was going to have to come over here pre-aroused every time I wore it." That joke is a hinge: she makes him laugh, she stumps him, and the power starts flowing the other way. Her deliberate breast against his cheek, his blush, the fought grin, the pin, the kiss on the bead of blood, and then "Keep still" — "a beat too late, after a silence she watched him spend deciding." Pace toppled-adjacent is new. We've never seen the restraint cost him on the page before, and it's the sexiest he's ever been.

Now the cold water I carry in with me. Vee's aside — "a man who picked her — who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking" — is the cruelest sentence in the chapter and the book knows it. He didn't walk past Randi. Randi said "Yes" to her on the quad. Randi is in this chapter for exactly one clause and she's wrong in it. And the furniture: I could not stop thinking about the bench. A woodworker's house where the purpose-built object is always already there — "of course there was a riser in the corner of a woodworker's house, of course he'd built it" — a woman placed on furniture he made, worked slowly past her performance until something real comes up. The fitting is the bench in daylight, down to the aftercare-shaped tea and blanket. Even the pre-warmed house — which undoes her with tenderness ("something in her let go even before he'd touched her once") — is the same hand that staged a collision and turned up a thermostat hours early. Nothing in this chapter tips; his want reads real, his restraint reads real, the not-looking-down is genuinely honorable. My unease is entirely imported knowledge, and it's the right kind: the chapter itself is all yes, hers, freely and gladly given — and I know she's saying yes inside a room she can't see the shape of.

One honest flicker of friction: the competence stack. Patent, powerlifter, master joiner, cook, and now — a tailor who can cut and drape silk to couture standard. Last chapter piled it; this one adds another floor, and for half a page I felt the wish-fulfillment gloss. I mostly forgive it because "hard to say no to" is the point — his perfection is Randi's warning in my ear — but I noticed. Nothing else snagged. The measuring sequence is long and earns every inch; the rhythm IS the erotics.

New depth I'm grateful for: the swimming backstory finally has its wound — her body "came in, all at once, into curves the clean line had no use for — too much woman for the sport," and that was "the end of the swimming she'd loved." That recolors the pool chapter's small grief retroactively. The thing her body cost her is the thing he's now measuring, prizing, getting right. "Watched the part of her she'd been apologizing for become, in his hands, something worth getting right" — that's the chapter's whole argument in one line, and the closing beat lands it: "she had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting."

The title: going in, "A Round" read like a round of drinks. Coming out it's the tape going around her — waist, bust, the knee-walk around her body — and it's her roundness itself, the curves she was taught to apologize for, finally measured and wanted. Quiet, exact, no spoiler in it. The book's title keeps sharpening: "would you come to my house Saturday" was a polite invitation; "I'd like to make it for you... If you'll let me" is a polite invitation; "Do you want to get started?" — every rung of her descent into his world is asked for, politely, and she keeps climbing down gladly. And *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — hums under all of it: the kinder he is, the shorter the spoon gets. (No cover tagline appears in my packet, so I won't quote one; my memory holds only the jacket's "quietly starving" and its promise that nothing would be taken she didn't hand over — and this chapter is that promise performed to the letter, which is exactly what frightens me.)

What I want next: the bedroom door, eventually, on her terms — after this chapter I believe she'll open it herself. What I dread: Randi's re-entry. She's been offstage four chapters and the silence has weight now; the plan is still running somewhere under all this tenderness, and Vee just fell further in love on a purpose-built pedestal. Guesses from here: the finished dress at the induction dinner, possibly with Randi or Brooke in the room to see it; and the fitting itself — Vee half-naked, measured, radiant — feels like something Randi will eventually hear about and not survive calmly.
