## Reader reaction

Reader reaction — "One Bite"

The last line did me in. I read it twice to be sure I understood what I was tasting: "the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." Vee never ate the pastry. Randi took "one neat bite," said "Delicious" in a way that closed the subject, and set it down — and the sugar reached Vee's mouth anyway, by way of the goodbye kiss. That is the most quietly devastating image this book has produced in a while: the want Vee will not name, delivered onto her lip secondhand, physical, undeniable, and only discoverable after Randi is gone into traffic. I sat with a small chill after that one, and also — I'll be honest — heat. The chapter earns both at once, which is this book's signature move, but I don't think it's ever fused them this tightly.

How I feel about the two of them right now. Vee I love more each chapter and fear for more each chapter, and this one moved both needles. Watch what she's doing at the top: the mimosa as "the cheapest yes on the card, and no one needed to know that was why" — that's my girl, still doing Thursday math under the tablecloth, still broke, still performing solvency, and Randi's pleased little "Two" lands on her like grace when it may just be arithmetic Randi doesn't even see. And then the retelling. Vee walks into her own trap grinning — Randi flicks one word at her, "Vanilla," and Vee spends the whole weekend defending him: "He is not vanilla." "He spanked me." From there Randi does the thing I've watched her do since the fitting lunch: "You just yada-yada'd straight past the good part... Walk me." One small yeah at a time. "What was the bet." "And how was it settled?" "He checked." I know from my whole history with these two that this is a debrief — that these extractions plausibly feed the project, maybe feed Pace himself — and the chapter never once says so, and Vee never once suspects, and the horror of it is that the extraction is also genuinely fun. They're shrieking, the next table is glancing over, it reads like the best girls' brunch of Vee's life. Same numbers, opposite verdicts. The stats professor would be proud of me.

Randi, this chapter, is a masterpiece of held ambiguity, and I want to name the line where I stopped breathing: "Sticky fingers?" Vee comes back from the bathroom — where she has just had her hand inside herself — and Randi says that, and the floor leaves the room for Vee and for me simultaneously. And then: the pastries. "They came while you were up. Go on, have one." The page gives me exactly what it gives Vee: "she could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one. She sat down. She did not find out." I'm not resolving that. I can't. Either Randi knows precisely what Vee went to do and named it with a pastry alibi ready — which would be the most frightening thing she's done since the mirror in chapter one — or it was innocent and Vee's shame supplied the rest. What I can say is that Randi's Radford story was not innocent. "Dress up, panties to the side, knees wide, doing a private dance" — delivered "warm, amused, her clear eyes right on Vee's" — is the third story in a row (headboard, practice room, this) told at Vee like a hand on her, and it detonates on schedule: "Her body... simply reached the top." Whether the story is even true, whether there was a blond boy at Radford at all — I have no idea, and I notice I don't believe her by default anymore, which is its own milestone. I don't trust her hands; I still, somehow, believe her feelings, because "I couldn't bear it if there was something between us. You're my best friend in the whole world" reads sincere to me even while it functions as a leash.

The bathroom scene is the chapter's real event and it wrecked me a little. This is the practice-room bathroom again — cold water on the wrists, the refusal — except escalated: last time she pinned her hand to her knee and would not begin; this time she begins. Panties down, "already soaked, of course, always, her body so far out ahead of her permission that it shamed her even here, alone, with no one to give the shame to." And then, at "the very first give of her own body opening to her own hand, Randi's face arrived behind her eyes. Clear, and fond, and near." And she stops. Not because she wants to stop — "the peak one unfair inch off" — but because "she could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was, and the two would not hold in one body." That sentence is the whole novel's interior war in one line. She's learned to be seen by Pace and not found wanting; she cannot yet survive being seen — even imaginarily — by Randi while wanting. "Frustrated and cowed in the same breath... low and ashamed and obedient." Obedient to what? Her mother's voice? Randi? The word choice made my skin prickle. And the detail that she can't even splash her face because "the face in the mirror was a careful face, built that morning to be looked at" — Vee has acquired a face that must be maintained. Randi's motif, arriving on Vee. That frightened me more than the arousal did.

Swoon / pull. It's here, complicatedly. The brunch itself is delicious — the shrieking, "Oh, you *brat*," the matched mimosas, the sun on the glass porch. Vee retelling the spanking and feeling it re-arrive in her body ("the print of his hand... low and bright and not remotely finished") is genuinely hot, a chapter-length afterglow of "We Find Out" that kept that chapter's heat alive in me too. And the "best friend in the whole world" beat swept me up even though I could see the wires — because it sweeps Vee up, "kindly this time, sweetly, in a direction she had no guard up against at all," and I have loved watching this lonely girl be chosen since chapter four. The kiss, "a beat past what a friend's would... then her lips once more with a little more in them" — that's the most openly the page has ever described the kiss as more than a kiss, and Vee still files it under friendship. The gap between what her tongue finds on her lip and what she lets herself know is the ache I read this book for.

Erotic charge: high and unusual — 2-ish on my scale but distinctive in kind, because the sex is all retold, imagined, or interrupted, and it's hotter for it. The peak for me is the double peak: Randi's "knees wide" story landing, and then the stall — the build, the stop, the ache "beating down there like a second pulse" carried back to the table unspent. The chapter deliberately denies release, to Vee and to me, and the denial is the point. Nothing went slack.

Friction: very little. The fantasy of the stall door slamming — "every fork going still, every face turning" — flirts with overwroughtness but it's Vee's catastrophizing, so it plays. If I squint, the retelling section runs long, but the length is the mechanism: I was supposed to feel the hour of climbing. One thing I flag as a genuine open question rather than a flaw: Randi at a mixer with a blond boy, taking him upstairs — she's Pace's lover. Nothing on the page has ever promised me they're exclusive, and the story may be fabricated anyway, so I hold it open — but it snagged.

The titles. "One Bite" — before reading, innocuous; after, it lands on Randi's "one neat bite" of the pastry, set down, subject closed — the woman who portions everything, appetite included, next to the girl who was "Famished." And it lands on the kiss: Vee got her one bite of the pastry — and of Randi — without ever choosing to. There's an apple somewhere behind this title, a first bite of a thing you can't untaste, and given the series title — *With a Long Spoon*, sup with the devil — and the tagline, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," I notice this chapter is precisely about a yes Vee did not give: she refused it in the stall, pinned it down, and it reached her mouth anyway. *A Polite Invitation* — "Sticky fingers?" followed by a plate of pastries may be the politest invitation in the book so far, if it was one at all. The title gave nothing away; it recolored everything after.

What I want / dread next. I want Vee to finish the sentence she's never finished — "Randi. Have you ever—" — and I'm starting to think the book is walking her there one stall at a time. Guesses from here: the taste on her lip is the kind of evidence that doesn't fold up and put itself away, so I expect it to surface — in a dream, in the dark at Pace's, or the next time Randi kisses her. And I dread the debrief on the other side: what Randi does with "He checked," and whether it goes back to Pace's bed. Cassie hasn't seen any of this. Someday she will.
