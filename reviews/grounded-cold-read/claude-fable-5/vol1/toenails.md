# Cold read (grounded) — Toenails

## Reader reaction

The morning-after chapter, and it's tiny, and it did more to me than its size has any right to.

First, the glow. "The smile had been there ever since. It had come back at every red light on the way home." I believed that smile completely — the no-bag detail sold it ("To pack a bag the night before would have been to say the thing to herself, plainly, and she had not been willing to say it"), which is so exactly Vee's machinery of permitted-and-unpermitted knowing that I grinned at the page. And the last line — "She had never in her life been so happy" — landed on me two ways at once, which by now is this book's signature move on me: I felt the happiness as real and earned, her body finally hers, the mother-voice defanged ("The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning" — that's genuine change, paid for on the page in Famished), and at the same time I felt the cold underneath it that she can't feel, because I know whose game built the runway to that bed, and I know what happened in a fire lane with Randi's hand at her jaw that she has also filed away. Her happiest morning is the reader's most watchful one.

But the thing that actually moved in me this chapter is Cassie, and what Vee did to Cassie. This is the third debrief in this run — Rye, Turned Up, now this — and the whole point of this one is that it isn't like the others. "What she kept was the middle. She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness." That ladle image is gorgeous and it chilled me, because I've watched a woman curate a surface for a whole book now — Randi's face — and here is Vee, on her happiest morning, learning the same craft. She told Randi more than she planned at lunch and now tells Cassie less than she always has. The channel of her confidence is visibly rerouting, and Cassie doesn't know it: "Cassie said it kindly, certainly, wrong" is the saddest line in the chapter. Cassie has never once been wrong about Vee before. She's wrong now because Vee made her wrong.

And that flicker again — "for half a second something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name." That's the same unnamed thing from the dining hall goodbye, back for a beat and gone. I still don't know what it is — love, loss, plain clear sight — and the page keeps refusing to name it, so I keep it unnamed and it keeps aching.

The zipper moment is the chapter's best piece of construction, felt from inside: she can edit a story, but she cannot edit her own body — "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." The panties she traded away have become evidence by their absence. So she zips back up and waits her best friend out of the room before she can undress. That's the first physical secret she's ever kept from Cassie in her own room, and the room even registered it — she waits, the door "stuck and gave," and only then "she let the towel fall, and stepped out of the skirt at last." The privacy is sweet and it is also a wall going up, and the chapter lets both be true.

Trust and suspicion: nobody in the room earns suspicion — this is Vee and Cassie, the two people I trust most. My unease is all imported from what I know offstage, and I notice Randi is not mentioned once in this chapter — not the lunch, not the kiss, nothing — and that silence reads loud to me. Vee is now keeping a curated version for Cassie, a fuller version for Randi, and the whole truth for no one.

Erotic charge: low and deliberately so — the night runs "under all of it, a second channel that had not shut off once," memory-heat rather than scene-heat, plus the small charge of her bare under the skirt at the zipper. After Famished, this is the right register; anything hotter would have cheapened both chapters.

Friction: almost none. If I reach — the debrief-with-Cassie beat is on its third appearance in six chapters, and a reader could tire of the shape; this one saves itself by inverting the content (the debrief is now a performance), but I'd notice a fourth.

The title: "Toenails" meant nothing walking in, then Cassie detonated it — "Was he worth painting your toenails?" … "In October. I have eyes." Perfect. The polish was painted in Famished as a thing Vee "chose without thinking too hard about why," and the title turns out to name the tell — the body's evidence of intention, readable by anyone who looks, the very thing Vee can't ladle out of a story. It gave nothing away in advance; it recolored on contact. As for A Polite Invitation and With a Long Spoon — I don't have the jacket in front of me this time, only my memory of its promise that nothing would be taken from her that she didn't hand over. This chapter is that promise made literal and unnerving: she handed over the panties, the night, the happiness, all freely, "Trade" — and she's gladder than she's ever been. The long spoon keeps whispering that gladness at this table has a price sheet I've seen and she hasn't.

What I want next: the dress finished; the induction in burgundy. What I dread, marked as guesses from here: Randi learning the consummation happened outside her choreography — that keyed-up tightness at lunch makes me think this news will not land as simple gladness; Cassie noticing the seam she didn't pull at; and the fire-lane kiss, which is still sitting in Vee unexamined like a coal in a coat pocket. The pull to keep reading is strong — this chapter is a held breath, and I can feel the book inhaling.
