## Reader reaction

Felt read

After "Famished" — which left me flushed and half-holding my breath — this little morning-after chapter landed on me like the cold bright room it opens into: crisp, funny, tender, and with one line in the middle that quietly scared me more than anything in it had any business doing.

First, the joy. Vee's grin "was the entire story," the smile coming back "at every red light on the way home," no bag packed because packing one "would have been to say the thing to herself, plainly" — that's her exactly, the girl who has to keep her own wanting deniable right up to the moment she acts on it, and I loved her for it. And the last line — "She had never in her life been so happy" — I believe it. The page earns it. Her body finally got to be loud and greedy and wasn't punished for it; even her mother's word gets defanged here: "The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." That's the arc of the whole book so far in one sentence, and it made me genuinely glad for her.

But the line that stopped me cold: "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness. It was a kindness. She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." That double beat — "It was a kindness" stated, then immediately undermined — is the chapter's real event. Vee has always given Cassie everything: the tree, the truck, the bra, the grinding. Now, for the first time, she's editing. She's learned to keep a middle. And I couldn't help hearing the echo of Randi at the restaurant — "You don't have to tell me," the door closing softly, the secret left half out of her with nowhere to go. Vee wanted to hand that secret to someone and was refused; now she's the one ladling it out of her own story. The book didn't say any of this out loud — no telegraphing, just the ladle — and that restraint is why it worked on me.

Cassie broke my heart in small ways twice. The opening flicker — "something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name" — is the same unnamed thing from "Goodbye, Vee," and the page again refuses to name it, so I do too: love, or loss, or just clear sight, still held open. And then "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend. Cassie said it kindly, certainly, wrong" — that word "wrong," dropped flat at the end of the sentence, hurt. Cassie has always read Vee "fast and whole." This is the first time she's read her wrong, and it's wrong because Vee has started curating what there is to read. The friendship's perfect transparency cracked this chapter, quietly, and nobody wailed about it. "Don't wait up, lover" is such an affectionate exit line and it lands on a girl who is, for the first time, keeping something from her.

The zipper moment is beautifully built: the near-comedy of it (nothing under the skirt, three feet from Cassie) resolving into the real stakes — "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." The body as the unfalsifiable evidence again — the wet satin at the fitting, the trade, and now the absence of the green. Vee can edit her mouth; she cannot edit her skin. She zips back up and waits Cassie out. Small scene, perfectly weighted.

Trust and suspicion: no new suspicion of anyone here — this is Vee and Cassie, and Pace is only warm residue. What unsettles me isn't a person, it's a direction: Vee learning the face. The checkpoint in my head keeps whispering that the woman who taught her the pleasure of a kept secret is the one running a game on her, and Vee alone in the emptied room, naked, smiling, happier than she's ever been, reads — through no fault of the page's tone, which is pure sunlight — like a girl standing at the top of something. The jacket's "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" has never sounded louder, and the chapter never once mentions Randi. That's craft, not a thumb on the scale.

Erotic charge: low and warm — this is afterglow, not heat. The night runs under the chapter as "a second channel that had not shut off once," which is exactly right; it kept the charge alive without restaging it. No friction to report. If I reach for a quibble, the chapter is slight — but it's slight the way a rest in music is short, and after "Famished" I wanted exactly this size.

The title: "Toenails" is Cassie's line — "Was he worth painting your toenails?" / "In October. I have eyes." The polish is the tell, the one piece of premeditation Vee couldn't ladle out of the story because it's on her body. And it recurs: Randi's blue toenails in chapter one were "her one step out of line," the single honest thing under the performance — now Vee has her frosted plum, chosen "without thinking too hard about why." Polish as the body's confession. The two women keep rhyming, and it's starting to feel deliberate in the best way. The book's title and the series title sit heavier every chapter — a polite invitation Vee keeps RSVPing to with her whole body, a long spoon nobody has handed her.

What I want next: I want the dress finished and the induction. I dread — marked as a guess from here — the moment the Randi channel and the Pace channel meet: Vee is now carrying secrets in three directions (the wetness from Cassie, the kiss from everyone, and soon, surely, something from Pace or Randi about the other). And I keep waiting for Cassie to say the thing her face keeps almost saying.
