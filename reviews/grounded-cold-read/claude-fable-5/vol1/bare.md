## Reader reaction

This one hurt, and it hurt in the exact place the last three chapters have been pressing. It's barely three pages — a girl in a bunk bed, a shirt, a phone she doesn't use — and it landed on me harder than the whole Chili's fight did.

What got me first was the logic of the flannel, laid out step by step like she's doing a proof. She's worn his shirt over her own for the hallway, "just a thing she wore. It hid what it was" — and I remembered, with a small jolt, that this is the shirt she made him wear all weekend so it would smell of him again, back at the end of "the flannel, washed because it had gone to nothing." She engineered this comfort. She asked for it, he gave it, he put it on over his own shirt right then — one of the most purely swoony beats in the book. And now the book turns that same object over and shows me the underside: "the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." That sentence took the air out of me. Every prior appearance of the shirt — the rain, the rationing, the refill — was warmth arriving from him; this is the first time the warmth is exposed as a loop she's running on herself. "She was here holding a smell in the dark." I believed every stage of it: the shirt over the shirt not enough, the shirt on bare skin working "for a while. It always worked," and then the one thought she can't unfeel. That's the interior honesty I read this book for — she isn't deceiving herself tonight, and it's worse for her that she isn't.

The phone paragraph is the other place it got me. Thumb over the empty field, waiting for words that don't exist: "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them." And here's where my reader-only knowledge makes it crueler than she knows. She thinks the shape won't come clear because he won't say the word. I know the shape can't come clear because the shape includes Randi — the word he won't say sits on top of a whole architecture she's never seen. Whether the withholding is the wound (Daphne, "I don't go back"), or the game ("I want her to get there on her own"), or something simpler and sadder, the page doesn't say, and I'm not fixing it. But watching her interrogate a shirt for an answer that's actually locked in a bench-shaped table across town — that's the double vision this book keeps making me live in, and it's working.

The ending is the title doing its work. She strips the shirt off, and "the cold of the room came down on her bare skin all at once, and it was worse. It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." She chooses the cold over the comforting lie — chooses bare. And the shirt "down in a heap where a dropped thing lands; not folded" — this from the girl who folded the green panties spot-centered and handed them over as a trade, who folds her laundry in the dryer chapter. Not folding it is the loudest thing she does all night. Cassie breathing "slow and easy" below, the window at its two inches, the grey window, no sleep. Quiet devastation, perfectly sized.

Do I suspect anyone new? No. Nothing here is "off" — the unease is the accumulated one, and it belongs to the story, not to a false note. I still hold open what happened in "Seconds": whether "I love how good we are together" went past him or was chosen. This chapter doesn't resolve it, and Vee knows she can't test it, and I'm with her in the not-knowing.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and rightly. The shirt straight onto bare skin — "the way she wore it when no one could see" — flickers with intimacy for a breath, and then converts entirely to grief. I didn't want it to be sexy; it wasn't trying to be.

Friction: almost none in the chapter itself. My only wobble is at the run level — this is the fourth consecutive chapter circling the unsaid word (can't name him to her dad, the cracker-and-meal sentence, the fight and the nameless dark, now this), and I can feel the beat being held long. It escapes sagging because each chapter is smaller and more alone than the last — from a dinner table to a porch to his bed to a bunk in the cold — a spiral narrowing to one girl and one shirt. But this is the bottom of the spiral, or should be. Something has to move next.

Titles: "Bare" is exact and it deepens on the page rather than giving anything away — bare skin, yes, but really bare of cover, the shirt that "hid what it was" gone, the comforting story stripped with it. She ends the chapter as unadorned as the book has ever let her be, and she stays in it on purpose. That's character. *A Polite Invitation* keeps darkening for me — everything she has was offered, courteously, and every cost is one she's paying alone in the dark; nothing was taken from her, and look at her. *With a Long Spoon* — supping with the devil — sits over this chapter like weather: she's been fed the meal of her life and is starving at midnight. (My packet carried no jacket copy or tagline this time, only the chapter title, so I can't quote the tagline here.)

What I want next: honestly, I want Pace to say it — and I don't trust that wanting, because I know what's under him. What I dread, as a guess from here: that the person who ends up warming this cold is Randi. Vee is bare, unslept, and unnamed, and Randi is the one who names things for her. If the next brunch catches her in this state, the one door Vee keeps refusing — the yes she won't say even to her own hand — is standing right where the cold is. That's a guess. But the book has been walking her toward it all fall.
