# Cold read (grounded) — hills-and-valleys

## Reader reaction

Two pages, and I feel like I've been let into a room I didn't know the house had.

First, the pure pleasure of it: Pace has a *friend*. Not a Christine who feeds him, not a bar full of grateful dance students — a peer, someone who's been riding on his left for two years and can say "Listen to you" and make it land. Sheri arrives fully formed in about six sentences — "the whole small engine of her standing on the pedals and driving the grade," "not as winded as would have suited either of them" — and I liked her immediately, which I did not expect to say about anyone new this late in the book. She remarks on Vee's backside "the way other people remarked on the weather," she offers to hate Vee on Pace's behalf like it's a service she keeps stocked ("You want me to not like her?"), and Pace's answer — "She couldn't take your heat" — is the most relaxed I have ever seen him. This is what he sounds like with someone he owes nothing.

And that's exactly what makes the middle of this chapter melt me and the end of it chill me, in the space of a single ride.

The melt: "he was past the name before he'd decided to be." Pace — the man who answers the actual question, who sets every word down separately — *gushes*. The walnut chair, her thumb down the grain of the joint he spent a weekend on, the math book she "not put it back like it had burned her." He heard himself going and didn't stop. That is precisely the register Randi used in "Gone" — "She got past me before I could help it" — and now both of the people who built this trap have confessed, to a third party or to each other, that Vee got in past the machinery. I felt genuinely warm reading it. He's gone over her, involuntarily, audibly, and "He didn't know what there was to listen to" is the perfect deadpan cap — he can hear a wrong note in an engine and can't hear this one in himself.

The chill: "Randi. That's not finished."

Four words and my stomach dropped. Partly because of what it confirms — that Sheri *knows about both women*. Pace has a person he tells the truth to, and it isn't Vee. His famous honesty, the May-I courtliness, the door-left-open ethic — it's all real, and it's also selectively deployed; there's a whole ledger of candor being spent on a cycling partner while Vee gets the curated version. But mostly it's the word itself. This is a man whose whole life is finishing things — the bench, the shirt, the dress "finished at some hour with no business being awake in it." In his mouth "not finished" doesn't only mean *ongoing*. It carries the workshop smell. Randi as a project with an endpoint. I'm not fixing that reading — the page leaves it as flat as he says it — but the resonance is there and it made the back of my neck prickle. And then Sheri runs "the arithmetic anybody could run" — "Two of them at once… If that's not already complicated, it will be" — and *he doesn't answer*. The book keeps doing this: putting the plain truth in an outsider's mouth (the latent-variables professor, Simpson's paradox two chapters ago — "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them") and letting the person it's about ride past it in silence. It worked on me again.

There's quiet plot cargo here too, and I felt the click of it: Sheri "kept an eye on her" Thursday. So Pace now knows what I know — that Vee sat alone, glared at the floor, nursed one drink, danced with nobody, *left alone*. Whatever happens at his door tomorrow night (and the timing is lovely — this is Saturday morning; Vee is coming Saturday night in the black dress and Randi's cognac shoes), he'll open that door already knowing she didn't shrug him off and go dancing. The reconciliation is being softened from his side before she ever knocks, and neither of them knows the other is ready.

One flicker of suspicion I want to be honest about: Sheri was at the Rusty Nail Thursday, and she has a "bleached length of… ponytail." For a half-second I wondered if she was the blonde who put the hook in Vee. I don't think so — the blonde's sugar had "something under it that wasn't" a smile, while Sheri's whole mode is frankness, and her neutral report and "You want me to not like her?" don't read like a woman who sabotaged the date — but the page doesn't close it, so I'll leave it ajar. More likely she was just one of the regulars on the rail. Either way, it adds to the pattern I keep noticing: Vee is *watched*, constantly, by people whose connection to her she can't see.

Also filed, uneasily: "I kept an eye on her" sits right next to my memory of Randi's "She's not ready, though… You'll want to take your time." Everyone around Vee is managing information about her. Even the benign version of this chapter — a friend reporting kindly — rhymes with the malign architecture underneath.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and that's right for it. "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that" is appraisal, not heat — though it did make me file Sheri's way of looking at women somewhere.

Friction: almost nothing. One sentence made me reread — "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years; he'd seen her do it, and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything" — I *think* "worth everything" means her scorn costs its target everything, but the compression wobbled for me. That's my whole complaint. The chapter is very short and earns its shortness; I'd have read more of these two, but it doesn't owe me more.

The title: "hills-and-valleys" — presented lowercase and hyphenated, oddly, like a label rather than a title, which I'll take as it comes. It's the ride's own rhythm first — "they talked where it let them, on the flats and the long descents, and went quiet on the climbs, which took the breath for themselves" — conversation as topography, the hard truths saved for where there's breath. It's also the week Vee just had, peak to trench and climbing again. And with Sheri's opening remark, I can't pretend it isn't bodies too. It sits nicely oblique; it gave nothing away. As for *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — the jacket's supping-with-the-devil warning, every freely given yes being the problem — this chapter quietly sharpens it: the devil's table has a *staff*, and some of them are lovely. The invitation Vee is about to make at his door tomorrow night, dressed head to toe by Randi's hands, is going to be as freely given as anything in this book, and I already know how much that "freely" is worth.

What I want next: the door. Vee in the black dress, the scarf tied with tension, "sure" and not sorry. What I dread, as a guess from here: Pace knows quality the way Randi does — will he look down at those cognac shoes, those straps, that architecture, and *recognize whose closet they came from*? Randi dressed Vee for this reconciliation in her own things. That feels like a fuse laid without anyone deciding to lay it.

**Characterization:** Pace deepened twice in two pages — the involuntary fondness (new) and the flat candor about running two women (confirmed to a witness for the first time). Sheri lands instantly solid: physicality, history, loyalty-as-weapon, all economical. No one thinned.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Brisk and complete; a very short chapter that moves two real pieces (his audible falling; his knowledge of Vee's lonely night) without padding. The one compressed sentence about Sheri's burning is the only stumble.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Excellent placement. After two long, dense Vee chapters ("Lesson," "broken-in"), this outside-POV inhale on the very morning of the reconciliation holds the tension taut rather than sagging it — the same trick "Gone" pulled after the shopping day, and it still works: every cutaway to the conspirators' side recolors the warmth I just enjoyed.
