This chapter left me cold in exactly the way Randi is cold beside John—not because it failed to sweep me up, but because it swept me into the fairytale and then let me feel how perfectly empty it is for her. For a while I wanted the mountain stranger to be real magic. Their invented marriage and children in the lift line are charming; his hand under her elbow, which “she did not need” but takes anyway, made me smile; and his skiing is genuinely attractive because “he had the run to spare and spent none of it proving it.” That lands. So does the dress, his stunned hands crossing over his heart, the white-tie ball, the dancing, and especially the sleigh arriving through falling snow. It is almost aggressively swoony, but Randi wants that excess, and for most of the night I wanted her to have it.

My feeling about Randi deepened sharply. The opening breakfast makes me ache for her: her father’s hearty affection is inseparable from the life he expects her to perform, while her mother is “all edges and points,” exquisite and withholding. Randi orders almost exactly the kind of carefully portioned food Pace once prepared for her, but here it feels like inheritance rather than care. The white-gold bracelet “locked on her wrist” disturbed me before I knew why; by the dance it catches against her hand, and by then it feels like the acceptable life fastened to her body.

I also felt the pull of how easy John is. He is handsome, capable, socially fluent, decisive, and generous with his skill.
