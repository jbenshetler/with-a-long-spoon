## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter like a drop through the floor. The heat is absolutely there, but for me it is inseparable from a colder, uglier knowledge: Vee is absent, and her absence is being used. The chapter title, “In Her Place,” lands almost too cleanly once Randi appears on the green sheets. She is literally in Vee’s place, on Vee’s sheets, inside the afternoon Vee had promised, and then emotionally underneath Vee too, making herself the woman who is used, punished, cleaned away, and returned to absence.

Pace is the hardest for me here. I still feel his capacity for care; even with Randi, he checks the safeword, reads her body, gives aftercare, strips the bed before Vee comes. But this chapter moved my trust in him downward. The line that chilled me was “His mind was on the road.” He is touching and tending Randi while part of him is tracking Vee’s imagined approach, and then later he accepts the lie because “she was warm on him and the fear had nowhere left to live.” That is not nothing. He has been managed, yes, but he also lets the management become permission.

Randi feels both desperately exposed and actively dangerous. Her need is real. Her crying afterward got to me; it didn’t feel fake. The punishment opens something in her, and the line “not prettily, not performed” made me believe the collapse. But she also engineers the whole scene on deception: the false text, the fake deadline, Vee drunk and tucked away, the green sheets. The tenderness she shows Vee offstage makes the betrayal more intimate, not less. Her morning smile while Vee praises her care is the chapter’s nastiest little blade.

Erotically, yes, it works, but it works dark. The strongest charge for me is not simply the sex; it is the inversion of place and ownership: Randi on Vee’s green sheets, Randi asking to be measured against Vee, Randi coming apart when Pace says, “She’s perfect… for your first woman.” That line is brutal and effective because it gives Randi exactly the wound she came seeking. The heat peaks there for me, and again in the phone call, where Vee’s innocent gratitude overlays what Randi is doing. That scene is almost too charged because it is so ethically poisonous.

My suspicion is no longer vague. The text has earned it. Randi is not just steering; she is staging, substituting, erasing, and savoring the fact that Vee doesn’t know. Pace is not innocent of the arrangement anymore either, even if Randi drove the scene. The chapter makes the hidden arrangement feel less like a wicked premise and more like a live harm approaching Vee.

The friction I had is that the scene is so perfectly calibrated to the book’s machinery that I could feel the design more than usual. “Vee’s name still in the room with them both” is powerful, but it also tells me very directly how to read the green bed. Still, the chapter mostly earns that directness because the whole situation is that blatant: the symbols are not subtle because Randi is not being subtle.

The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels darker now. The “invitation” is no longer just seduction by open doors and careful asking; it is also an invitation sent in someone else’s name, with the guest of honor asleep somewhere else. *With a Long Spoon* feels more apt than ever: desire here is something you approach with distance because it may feed you and burn you at once. The closing blurb beat, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,” is ringing loudly now, because Vee’s yes is not present in this chapter, and that absence matters.

What I want next is Vee arriving warm and unsuspecting, because that is where the ache is. What I dread is exactly that: Vee making herself open again in a house that has just been scrubbed clean of what happened there. My guess from here is that the bed, the sheets, or the rhythm of Randi’s care will leave some trace even if the literal evidence is gone.
