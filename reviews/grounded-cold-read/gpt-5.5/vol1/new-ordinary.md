## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter first as relief. Not relief from heat, because the heat is immediate and very alive, but relief that Vee comes back through the door and Pace is simply there. The five nights apart make the whole opening ache: her little resentment that he didn’t fight her on school nights, her wanting him to need her out loud, and then the kiss that answers without making a speech. That felt very Pace, and very Vee: she wants words, but she is also learning to read the shape of care in what he does.

The sex worked on me strongly because it was so ordinary in the life they are making and still not ordinary to her body at all. The hallway undressing is funny and messy and hungry — the jeans stuck at her hips, the white socks, the bra caught and unwound — and that made it warmer than if it had been smooth. I liked the comedy of it because it didn’t cool the scene; it made their familiarity hotter. The peak for me was not just him finding “the proof of her right through” the green satin, but the way she can be ridiculous and still wanted. The chapter lets her be hopping, laughing, undignified, and burning alive all at once.

Pace lands beautifully here, maybe almost dangerously beautifully. He does not push against her need for school nights, does not punish her absence, does not tease the flannel confession into shame. He just takes in what she is really saying and answers by wearing the shirt. That is swoony as hell. But I also feel the old split more sharply because of how domestic this is getting. The top drawer, the bag, the shirt that cycles between his body and her dorm bed: this is becoming a life. And I know Vee still does not know the central fact about Randi and Pace. The warmer the house gets, the colder that hidden fact feels waiting outside it.

Vee herself feels deeper and dearer to me here. She is not just discovering sex now; she is discovering habits, territory, routine, need. “Her space” matters. The top drawer matters. The shirt matters almost more than the sex, because she has to say what she wants in a small, embarrassing way: not “make me come,” not “look at me,” but “make this smell like you again.” That request made me ache. It is childish only in the sense that the need is pure and exposed. She wants a portable piece of belonging for the nights she has to be a student again.

The food was less electric than the sex but just as important. The pizza could have dragged if it were only another cooking passage, but it didn’t for me because her tasting has changed so much. “The flat orange sameness” versus all the separate flavors felt like a clean way of showing what Pace has done to her senses. She doesn’t just consume anymore. She notices. She counts. She can taste the difference between being fed and merely eating.

I have no new suspicion of Pace from his behavior in this chapter. On the page, he is generous, patient, and tender. My suspicion is structural, from what I already know: he is letting this “new ordinary” become ordinary while Vee is still missing the map. Randi is absent in person, but she is there in the green satin, in the learned permission, in the whole widened sexual vocabulary Vee now carries. Cassie is absent too, and I felt that absence because the chapter is about where Vee really lives now, emotionally and practically, and it is not the dorm.

The title, “The New Ordinary,” feels exact. It points to the repeatedness of this: the drawer, the coming back, the sex before food, the food after sex, the shirt exchange, the school nights negotiated around his house. It does not give anything away so much as quietly name the danger and sweetness of normalization. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, still feels like it is widening from a social invitation into a sexual and domestic one, but the politeness is uneasy because not everyone has been told what room they are entering. *With a Long Spoon* still makes me wary: warmth at the table, yes, but also distance, caution, maybe eating with someone dangerous. I do not have the tagline in this prompt, so I can’t react to it exactly.

What I want next is almost unbearable: I want Vee to keep having this, because it is making her more alive. I also dread how much more there is to lose now. My guess from here is that the “ordinary” will make the eventual revelation worse, not better, because Vee is not just falling for a man anymore. She is moving in by inches.
