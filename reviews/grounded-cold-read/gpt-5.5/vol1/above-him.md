## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter as a quiet exhale after all the bright, dangerous brunch heat. It is not less charged, exactly, but the charge has shifted into domesticity, into the terrible sweetness of being made room for. Vee’s happiness lands on me before anything else does. The opening has that post-sex looseness and fullness, but what really catches is her taking inventory: “the door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the warm tomatoes, the coming back to her.” I believed her love because the chapter lets it arrive through her body first, through being fed and taken slowly and wanted again, not through a grand thought she forces onto the page.

Pace feels almost painfully lovable here. The sex is mostly remembered rather than shown, but his pattern is vivid: he brings her “all the way over and usually more than once before he took a single thing for himself.” That works on me because it is desire as attention, not politeness laid over hunger. The food afterward matters just as much. Salmon, dill, warm tomatoes, Greek wine: the care is practical and sensual at once. And then the drawer. God, the drawer. That is the swoon of the chapter for me. He sees the mess of her in his clean bathroom, says nothing, then by the next week has moved his own things down and given her “the top one. The easy one. The one your hand goes to first.” That is more intimate than a speech would have been.

My trust in Pace emotionally rises here, because this is such a precise kind of welcome. But structurally I still cannot forget what Vee does not know. The ache is that she reads his unsaid love as enough, and on the page, in this room, it almost is. “He never said. There was never a note.” That silence is beautiful and a little frightening, because silence is where his tenderness lives, but also where the secret lives. The chapter earns both feelings. I don’t suspect his drawer gesture. I do suspect the larger silence around him.

Vee breaks my heart a little with how ready she is to become a “guest” again the second the scrunchies vanish. Her fear is so small and so old: that her presence is too much, that her body and bottles and hairpins have overrun some clean place she was only temporarily allowed into. So when she finds the drawer, it hits hard. It is not just storage. It is permission to leave evidence. Permission to recur. Permission to be expected.

Erotically, this is a low-flame chapter rather than a peak. The remembered sex is warm and bodily, and the phrase “a man might come back to her an hour later” has real heat because it is about being wanted after already being had. But the erotic center is not graphic sex; it is aftermath, appetite, hair wrecked from the night, his smell, his robe, her kit on his counter. The peak for me is emotional-erotic: Vee realizing she loves him beside his sleeping body, then whispering “Peter” where he cannot answer.

The title, “Above Him,” first points simply to position: she is watching him sleep, emotionally hovering over him, holding a truth he does not hear. But it also points to the drawer, maybe: her things above his, her place literally above his in the bathroom. That makes the title tender on second thought. It did not give anything away for me; it clicked only after the last section.

*A Polite Invitation* feels sharper here because the invitation is no longer only sexual or social. It is domestic. A drawer is an invitation. A scrunchy form by the tap is an invitation. “With a Long Spoon” still carries that promise of careful distance from something dangerous, and the blurb’s closing beat, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,” hums under this chapter hard. Vee’s yes to the drawer, to loving him, to having a place, feels freely given. That is exactly what makes me dread the eventual cost.

What I want next is almost embarrassingly simple: I want him to say it. I want Vee to have the words. But my guess from here is that the unsaid things are going to keep accumulating: love unsaid, the scar untold, Randi and Pace hidden, the plan unnamed. This chapter makes me want to stay in the warmth, and also makes the warmth feel more breakable.
