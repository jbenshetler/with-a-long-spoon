## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel small and warm in the best way. After the charged machinery of the last chapters, this is almost shockingly simple: a text, frozen yogurt, a walk. But because I’m carrying everything from before, the simplicity feels loaded without being sinister on the page. Vee’s stomach dropping when the phone lights up got me immediately. She wants him so badly already that she has to make a joke against it: “Is he desperate?” That felt painfully true, not coy.

I like Vee more here, or maybe I feel closer to her. The way she looks for a flaw because wanting is too exposed — that is such a recognizable little defense. And then the frozen yogurt cup getting away from her, “too much, the cone of it leaning,” with her mother’s voice usually cutting in and today not arriving: that landed. It’s tiny, but it feels enormous for her. Appetite again, but this time in daylight, in a stupid bright shop, not on a bench, not in bed. She lets herself have too much and then “found she didn’t care.” I felt that as a real looseness in her body.

Pace is very attractive here, and in a quieter way than in “The Bench.” I don’t feel suspicious of him in this chapter from Vee’s side; the page gives me gentleness, attention, and restraint. “He asked her things the way her grandfather used to ask things” is the line that made me trust the moment, because it tells me what the attention feels like to her: not predatory, not dazzling, but patient. And when she says the embarrassing thing — “This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it’s froyo” — I loved that he doesn’t rescue her from it, mock it, flirt it away, or turn it into a speech. He lets it sit, then gives her back something small and concrete: “Your tart’s holding up.” That made me melt. It is almost absurdly tender.

Randi feels complicated but not newly alarming in this chapter. She is still steering, plainly: “You should go,” and “You’re not unsure.” Given what I know from the recent chapters, I can’t forget that she and Pace have an unseen line between them. But in this particular scene, with Vee at the table, Randi’s push also feels like the friend Vee wants her to be: confident, amused, authorizing desire. Cassie, meanwhile, remains the little grounding wire. Her healer joke is funny, but it also makes her feel like the person who remembers Vee’s actual history, not just the new glow around her.

The erotic charge here is low but alive. After that, the charge shifts into appetite: sugar, tart, “too much,” the mother’s absent voice. It’s not hot-hot, but it is sensual and very effective.

I had almost no friction. The chapter is short, and it earns that. If anything, I wanted a little more of the walk, but the cut is clean: it gives me the first real taste of what being with Pace in public feels like to Vee. The title, “The Long Way,” feels literal first — they walk “not toward anything” — but emotionally it points at Vee taking the long way into trust, into wanting, into letting herself be cared for without making it a joke. It also echoes the book’s larger promise to me: *A Polite Invitation* is not one blunt invitation, but a series of small permissions. *With a Long Spoon* still carries that wary, wicked sense of distance and danger, and the blurb’s closing beat — “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.” — is hovering hard now, because this yes feels sweet and real and already partly arranged by people who know more than she does.

What I want next is to stay with this sweetness a little longer, even though I dread the hidden structure under it. My guess from here is that Vee is going to fall fast because Pace’s attention answers a hunger she barely knows how to name, and Randi’s approval makes the fall feel socially safe. I want it for her, and I’m uneasy because I know she doesn’t know the whole shape of what she’s walking into.
