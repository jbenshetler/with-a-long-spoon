## Reader reaction

I came out of this chapter warm and a little breathless, but not in the same way as the Pace chapters. This one is intimate in a more sideways, social, almost dangerous way: Randi doesn’t undress Vee, not really, but she keeps finding the exact place where Vee is most susceptible to being named. “He didn’t make this *for* you. He made it *to* you” absolutely lands. It made me feel how deeply Randi understands the gift of the dress, and also how much power she has because she can put Vee’s half-formed feelings into finished sentences.

Vee is so happy here, and I believe her happiness. The dorm room shrinking under Randi’s brightness, the Stevie Nicks poster suddenly visible again, the garment bag becoming sacred and then ordinary again: all of that put me right inside the feeling of being young and wanting your glamorous friend to see the new self you’ve been given. I loved that Vee doesn’t just show the dress on the hanger. She needs to be seen *in* it. That felt honest, not vain. She has been learning to want being looked at, and here she chooses it.

Randi is wildly attractive in this chapter. Funny Randi especially gets me: the yeti joke, the rhinestone platforms, the shared shoe size turning into “God help us both, it’s practically a marriage.” That laugh “the one that costs her something to give” made me soften toward her. It felt real. But the suspicion is still there because I know too much about how this started. She is so good at making Vee feel chosen that I can’t unknow the earlier choosing. Still, this chapter didn’t make her feel cold to me. It made her feel warm and dangerous at once.

The strongest erotic charge is not the dress itself, though the dress is charged; it’s Randi on one knee with Vee’s foot in her hands. The toe-polish moment is tiny and devastating: “‘Mm,’ Randi said. / That was all.” Her thumb across Vee’s big toe made the whole room tip for me too. It’s not explicit, but it’s very intimate, and because Vee has painted those toes before going to Pace, the touch feels like Randi finding an already-erotic part of Vee and quietly claiming a little knowledge of it. Vee grabbing Randi’s shoulder to steady herself is the peak for me.

The money part gave me the real unease, maybe more than the sexual tension. Randi steers Vee beautifully, but also steers her into spending money she does not have. The shoes are right; I felt that. The tan heel really would have been an apology. But watching Vee put away the debit card and take out the credit card, knowing “she did not have the next part, the paying-it-back part,” made the glamour suddenly cost something. And then Randi buys shoes several times more expensive without looking. That contrast hurt. It doesn’t make Randi malicious on the page, but it makes their worlds feel sharply unequal.

The title, “Sorority,” feels bigger than the house now. This is initiation, but not the official honor-society one. Vee is being inducted into Randi’s world: cars, boutiques, shoes without visible prices, a female intimacy that is half friendship and half something Vee cannot name. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps feeling more exact: nobody is forcing Vee through the door, but the doors are being opened with exquisite timing. *With a Long Spoon* still sounds like danger handled elegantly, intimacy with something you maybe shouldn’t get too close to. The blurb’s closing beat, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,” is ringing very loudly here, because yes, Vee buys the shoes herself. That is exactly why it unsettles me.

What I want next is the induction. I want the room to stop. I want Vee to get the triumph she’s imagining. I also dread the bill, the look, the next escalation, the way Randi’s “friend like this” may be true and still not innocent. My guess from here is that the shoes and dress together are going to make Vee visible in a way she cannot fully control once it happens.
