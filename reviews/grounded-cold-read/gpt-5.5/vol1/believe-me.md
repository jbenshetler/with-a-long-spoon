## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter in my chest more than anywhere else. After the last three chapters, Vee’s need has been gathering pressure so steadily that when she finally walks into Pace’s house without her bag, it already feels like a line has been crossed. The house is doing everything it has always done to me as a reader: low lamplight, warmth, food, music, wine waiting, his care already arranged around her body before she arrives. Usually that makes me soften. Here it hurts, because she hates that it still works on her. That was the ache of the chapter for me: all the tenderness is real, and it still is not enough.

Vee feels braver here than she has in a while, but not triumphant-brave. Bare-brave. She comes in cold, keeps her coat buttoned, doesn’t bring the bag, doesn’t accept the door kiss, and asks the question directly: “What am I to you?” I was proud of her and frightened for her at the same time, because she finally stops letting sex, food, warmth, and being cherished answer for the missing word. She names exactly what he gives her: “Like my body comes with me attached.” That line landed hard. It is such a beautiful description of why Pace has felt trustworthy to her sexually, and why I have trusted him there too. He has never made her feel split between body and self. But then the chapter turns the knife: if her body comes with her attached, does her life outside his house come attached too?

Pace still feels tender to me, but this is the first time his refusal really wounds past what I can excuse as quietness or awkwardness. “You’re the most important person in my life” should be enormous, and part of me did melt at it, because I believe he means it. But Vee is right: it does not answer the question. His next move, “Do any of your friends have a relationship you’d rather have than this one?” made me angry on her behalf. It is true, and manipulative without necessarily feeling calculated. It pulls her away from the specific thing she asked and into gratitude. That is exactly the trap she has been in: this is better than anything else she knows, so how dare she ask for more?

“Words are cheap. Believe what I do.” That is where the chapter peaked for me emotionally, and not in a swoony way. It is a devastating Pace sentence because it sounds like his whole ethic: action, care, proof, craft, warmth, meals, heat turned up before she arrives. But Vee’s answer is better: “Words are cheap, and you still won’t spend them on me.” I felt the whole book so far narrow to that. He can make a dress, build furniture, feed her, learn her body, leave the door unlocked, warm the house for hours, and still refuse the small public/shareable name she needs in order not to feel hidden.

The swoon here is wounded but not gone. The opening still has pull: the truck in the drive, the lit windows, the food, the piano “that moved like water,” the two glasses already poured. The ache is that I still want to go inside that house, even while Vee is right not to be soothed by it. Pace’s face opening when he sees her still got me. His almost-reaching for her at the end got me too, because I believe he wants to fix the hurt, but the chapter makes clear that his hands cannot fix this. “Not the hand in place of the word” is the whole refusal of the old magic.

Erotically, this chapter has no explicit sex, but the charge is everywhere because all the recent sex is still in the room. The heat is in absence and memory: “the food, only his hands, only the warm,” “we have sex, so much of it,” “the best of my life.” It does not arouse so much as expose the cost of arousal having been allowed to answer too many questions. The erotic history between them gives this confrontation its weight.

My trust is split. I still trust Pace’s tenderness in the moment; I do not think he is faking the care. But I do not trust his refusal to name things, and I especially do not trust the larger situation because Vee still does not know about Randi and Pace. This chapter would hurt even if Pace had no secret. With the secret behind it, “Believe what I do” becomes almost unbearable, because Vee cannot believe all of what he does; she does not know all of what he does. The text earns that unease. I am not inventing it from suspicion. The secrecy already exists.

I had almost no friction with the chapter. It felt clean, painful, and earned. If anything, the only moment that made me push back was Pace’s “Do any of your friends…” question, but that pushback is against him, not against the writing. It is precisely the kind of true-but-not-answering thing that would make me furious in Vee’s place.

The title, “Believe Me,” feels bitter after the chapter. It points straight at Pace’s “Believe what I do,” but Vee is asking for something more like: believe me when I say the words matter. Believe me that warmth inside the house is not the same as being named outside it. Believe me that I need a relationship I can carry into speech. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, now feels colder and more dangerous than it once did: all these invitations into warmth, sex, food, friendship, the house, Randi’s confidence, Pace’s bed, may be polite and beautiful, but Vee is starting to feel the terms she was never given. The series title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps feeling like a warning about intimacy with something dangerous or difficult to trust. No tagline was included in this packet, so I cannot react to one exactly.

What I want next is for Vee not to go back too quickly. What I dread is that Pace, Randi, or both will comfort her just enough to put this under the rug again. My guess from here is that this is the first real rupture that cannot be solved by sex, food, or the house. And because she left without going back in, I badly want to see who she goes to: Cassie, Randi, or no one.
