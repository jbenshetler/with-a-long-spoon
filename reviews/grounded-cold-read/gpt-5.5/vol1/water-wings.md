## Reader reaction

I felt my whole body ease in this chapter. After the pressure of Pace and Randi, and then the charged, almost enchanted way Vee has been pulled toward Randi and then Pace, this was such a relief because Cassie is suddenly not just the blunt friend on the edge of things. She is care. Dry, unsentimental, perfectly aimed care. I loved her here.

Vee, right now, makes me ache. The suit “fit, and it gave her nowhere to hide” is such a clean, painful sentence. It gets at the whole chapter: her body as something that draws comment, something she has to manage, something she can perform with if she has to. The walk past the bleachers made me tense immediately, because she knows how to make an entrance but that does not mean she wants to be consumed by the room. The floaties line is gross, and “Legs for days” is the same old public entitlement dressed as compliment. I was so glad Cassie answered it privately instead of making Vee into a scene: “They will never know the love of a woman.” That landed warm and funny and loyal.

The swoon here is not romantic, exactly, but it is deeply intimate. Cassie keeping the joke “just for the two of them” is the kind of friendship tenderness I trust. She does not rescue Vee in a grand way. She gives her a way to breathe without making her explain the wound.

The underwater passage is the peak for me. It is not erotic in a direct way, but it is bodily and sensual: “the swallowing green,” “the light coming down through the surface in slow sheets,” “the slow scull.” It made Vee feel most like herself, or like someone she used to be before being looked at became labor. “She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem” hurt. That line feels like the chapter’s center. The grief is small but real, and because the page does not overname it, I trusted it.

I have no suspicion of Cassie. If anything, this chapter moved me harder toward trusting her. She sees Vee’s face “on wrong” and does not ask, because “asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger.” That is such a sharp understanding of her. Cassie’s method is not analysis, not comfort-speech, but play: she shoots water badly enough to invite outrage, and Vee comes back into the world laughing. That felt earned.

The erotic charge is low, though there is body-charge everywhere. Vee’s suit, the comments from the bleachers, the fact that she has “more of her now” all keep the body present. But the chapter’s attention is not ogling her. It gives me the discomfort of being looked at, then the privacy of not being looked at, then the joy of being bodily without being consumed.

The title, “Water Wings,” is tender and a little ironic to me. Vee does not need floaties; she can swim, beautifully, and the stupid bleacher joke says the opposite for the wrong reasons. But Cassie is a kind of water wing here, not because Vee is helpless, but because she helps her stay buoyant when having a body gets too heavy. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, still feels like it is pointing toward invitations that look socially graceful and consensual on the surface but carry enormous emotional force underneath. The series title *With a Long Spoon* feels darker beside that, like closeness with danger kept at a ritual distance. I was not given a cover tagline here, so I cannot react to one exactly.

What I want next is more Cassie. I want to see whether Vee can feel the difference between being chosen by Randi and being known by Cassie. My guess from here is that Vee is going to keep mistaking intensity for belonging, and Cassie may be the person who notices what it costs before Vee can say it.
