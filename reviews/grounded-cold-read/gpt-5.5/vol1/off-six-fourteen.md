## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter still lit up from the kiss under the tree, and the chapter lets me stay in that light without hurrying to make it heavier than it is. Vee is incandescent here. Not polished, not managed, not even especially graceful: she is spilling, spinning, throwing pillows, laughing at herself, and the joy of that landed on me very directly. After the recent chapters where so much of her wanting has been watched, measured, or half-hidden even from herself, it is such a relief to see her come home and *tell* someone, breathless and proud: “He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me.”

That “Me” is the center of the chapter for me. It matters so much that she is not only swooning over Pace; she is swooning over her own agency. She keeps returning to the fact that he did not pressure her, did not turn up the charm, did not make the moment happen for her. “Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.” I believed her. I also felt how badly she needed Cassie to believe her, not defensively exactly, but with the urgency of someone trying to preserve the clean shape of the memory before anyone can smudge it with suspicion.

Cassie is wonderful to me here. I trust her. Her first instinct is protective, and it is not mean or joy-killing; it is practical love. “How much did he give you to drink?” is funny because it is Cassie, but it is also serious enough that I was glad she asked. Same with the state troopers line. She lets Vee have the romance, but she keeps a hand on the rail. That combination makes me feel safer as a reader. Cassie does not romanticize Pace just because Vee is glowing. She asks where he lives, what his name is, whether Vee has shoes. She is dry, but she is paying attention.

My feeling about Pace, oddly, gets warmer even though he is not physically present. He comes through Vee’s report as careful in exactly the way he has been careful on the page: he lets her no stand, and then lets her exception be hers. The thing that makes me melt is that she experiences his restraint as permission rather than rejection. Still, there is a small practical prickle around the hiking plan. Not moral suspicion, not yet. The text has earned my trust in him so far with Vee. But “off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel” is so vague, and Cassie repeating it as “the whole address” made me feel the gap. Vee is too blissed out to care that she does not really know where she is going. Cassie notices for both of us.

The erotic charge here is not sexual on the surface, but it is charged with afterheat. The chapter understands that desire continues after the touch, in the body trying to narrate what happened to it. The peak for me is not the kiss itself, since we already saw that, but Vee reenacting the choice through speech until it breaks into movement: “arms going up, hair going everywhere,” the room too small for her. That made me grin.

I don’t have real suspicion of Pace here. I have concern around Vee’s innocence, but the chapter itself keeps insisting on the consent of that kiss in a way I accept because it comes from Vee’s own need to say it truly, not from an outside lecture. If anything feels “off,” it is only that she is already trusting the next step almost entirely through feeling: “He’ll have water,” “he’s not going to walk me off a cliff,” “I know where he lives and everything.” It is sweet, and it is also not quite enough information. Cassie’s skepticism feels earned, not paranoid.

The title, “Off Six-Fourteen,” lands first as a joke about the vagueness of Pace’s location, but by the end it feels like a little threshold marker. Vee is going somewhere off the mapped campus routes, off the places she knows how to read. It points toward Pace’s private world: west of town, gravel, trail, real shoes. It does not give anything away exactly, but it does make the chapter tilt toward the next crossing.
