## Reader reaction

I came out of this chapter very physically convinced: cold, wet, hungry, legs burning, heel rubbed raw, then that sudden flare in the truck where shame and want braid so tightly that I could feel why Vee does it before I could judge it. The chapter worked on me less as “a hiking date” than as a sequence of being cared for without being reduced: Pace notices the trail is hard, fixes the boot, teaches the cherry pit rule without humiliating her, gives her his dry shirt and his privacy at the same time. That is exactly the kind of attention that would undo Vee.

Pace is almost dangerously good here. I mean that in both directions. On the page, in this chapter, he is tender, patient, practical, and erotic because he does not rush to claim what is offered. “He walked at the speed of her not knowing how” landed hard for me: it is a beautiful sentence about hiking, but also about the way he courts her. The moleskin scene should maybe be nothing, but it becomes intimate because Vee’s mother-voice rushes in with shame about her sock, her foot, “what kind of girl lets a man,” and Pace simply handles the problem. That made me trust him in the moment.

But because I remember what Vee does not know, that trust cannot be clean. The care is real; the secrecy is also real. That is the book’s ache right now. Pace’s restraint in the truck is gorgeous on the surface. He stands in the rain so she can change. He waits to be called back. He keeps his eyes where she puts them. He does not touch. And yet the whole scene hums because he has already been part of choosing her without her knowledge. I am aroused by his stillness and unsettled by the larger frame at the same time.

Vee moved me a lot here. She is not passive in this chapter. She says his name when the boot hurts. She eats. She tells him about the honor society and the russet silk dress. She calls him back into the cab. She shows him what she wants him to see. The truck scene peaks for me because it is hers: “I want him in here with me” and then, deeper, “I want him to see me.” That second sentence is the erotic center of the chapter. She is not just cold and needing a shirt; she is wanting to be witnessed as desiring, as improper, as the girl her mother’s voice condemns. The word “floozy” is ugly and exact, and the way her body answers it makes the heat feel psychologically specific rather than generic.

The erotic charge is strong without explicit sex. It peaks when she unbuttons the wet shirt while holding his eyes. The cold makes everything sharper: wet cotton, sheer bra, gooseflesh, the drop of rain between her breasts. It is exposed, but not careless. Pace’s “want and gladness and stillness” made me swoon because it lets her offering land without turning immediately into taking. Then the turn, when she realizes his hand is shaking because he is freezing, brings tenderness back into the heat. She wanted more, but care interrupts desire without killing it. “We should go. Get warm. Please.” That “please” feels like a different kind of intimacy.

Randi is absent in person, but she is not absent from my unease. This date deepens Vee’s attachment to Pace, and that means the hidden Randi/Pace/Vee arrangement tightens around her. Cassie is also absent, and I missed her practical skepticism here, especially because Vee is out west off 614, in the truck, in borrowed boots, with a man whose deeper context she does not have.

The food motif keeps working beautifully. The sandwich is almost erotic in its own right: the horseradish bloom, the shared bottle, the hunger made by exertion. “The plain animal rightness of needing something and being handed it” could describe the whole Pace/Vee dynamic. The cherries add a tiny social wound with the pit, but Pace repairs it by placing his own pit beside hers. That made me melt. He teaches without making her “common,” and that matters because her inner voice is so ready to punish her.

The title, “Leave No Trace,” first means the literal hiking ethic: take nothing, leave nothing, even cherry pits. But after the truck scene it feels more charged and sadder. Vee is absolutely being marked by this day: the moleskin on her heel, the flannel on her body, the desire in the cab, the sentence she does not get to finish. The title almost argues against the truth of the chapter. They may leave no pit on the rock, but Pace leaves traces everywhere on Vee, and she leaves traces on the flannel too: “her own wet hair where it had been pressed to her neck.” It did not give the chapter away, but it recolored it by the end.

The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels more pointed than ever. Pace’s whole mode is polite invitation: “Stand,” “Stay close,” the offered shirt, the privacy, the waiting gaze. But the larger invitation is still not fully honest because Vee does not know who is inviting her into what. The series title *With a Long Spoon* keeps feeling like a warning about dining with something dangerous or morally tricky while still being drawn to the table. I do not have a cover tagline in this prompt, so I cannot react to one exactly.

The ending frustrated me in the good way. The campus cop’s lights and siren steal the sentence she was going to say, and I wanted that sentence badly. I expect, as a guess from here, that the flannel becomes an object with charge. I also dread the way this date gives Vee more evidence that Pace is safe, because he is safe in the moment and not safe in the whole truth.
