## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter as a seduction, but not sexually explicit yet: it’s social, atmospheric, almost narcotic. The heat is in being chosen. After the bench, after Randi’s private recoil and decision to put the face back on, watching her turn that full practiced warmth onto Vee made me feel both swept up and uneasy. And the chapter earns both. From Vee’s side, it is gorgeous. Randi appears like a door opening into the life Vee has been standing outside of: “the kind of somebody a campus orbits,” the girl who knows the room, the coffee shop, the joke in the sign, the high table. I absolutely understood why Vee “walked back to her dorm not feeling her feet.” Randi’s attention would feel like sunlight if you were Vee.

But because I remember Randi from the last chapters, it also hurts a little. Randi is not simply being cruel, and I don’t think she is only faking. The warmth seems real as it happens; that’s part of her danger and part of her charm. She really does delight in people responding to her, and maybe in Vee specifically. But she is also using a form she knows works. The bit with Cassie shows it beautifully: “Hi — I’m Randi. Yes, like the adjective…” It has timing, polish, a place for the other person to laugh. Cassie refusing the script made me like Cassie immediately. She feels like a person with her own gravity, not impressed into orbit.

Vee, meanwhile, landed tenderly for me. Her wanting is so naked because she doesn’t quite name it as wanting. She thinks in status, belonging, clothes, rooms, who gets to sit where. But under all that, the chapter lets me feel the physical pull: Randi’s citrus smell, her hand on Vee’s forearm, her shoulder against Vee’s shoulder, the warm breath during lecture. The erotic charge peaks for me less in any single touch than in Vee’s helpless attention: “the better thing was right here at her elbow.” That’s desire before it has given itself permission to be desire.

Cassie’s “Goodbye, Vee” gave me the strongest prickle. It felt wrong on the page because Vee feels it as wrong: “the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday.” I don’t know what Cassie sees, but she sees something. I trust her more than anyone in this chapter, maybe because she does not perform warmth. She says little, but the little cuts through.

The coffee shop scene really worked on me. The sign, **Χ ΛΑΤΕ**, is such a good little threshold: Vee has passed it before, but with Randi she can suddenly read it, suddenly belong inside the joke. That made me ache. The place is shallow and statusy and bright, but for Vee it becomes enchanted because Randi brings her through it. I also loved the bite of Vee’s judgment of the underdressed girls: “Floozies,” in her mother’s voice, immediately followed by the longer look she doesn’t want to admit. That felt like the chapter quietly opening Vee’s own contradictions: envy, desire, superiority, shame, all tangled together.

The title, “See You Later,” lands as a promise and a spell. Cassie says “Goodbye, Vee,” which sounds too final; Randi says “See you later,” which feels like election, like Vee has been placed into a future. The title gave me the chapter’s emotional hinge without ruining it. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels sharper now: Randi’s invitation is polite, bright, socially graceful, and maybe not innocent. The series title *With a Long Spoon* keeps feeling like a warning about dining with danger at a distance, but Vee is not keeping distance at all. I don’t have a cover tagline in this prompt, so I can’t react to it exactly.

What I want next is to see Vee and Randi together again, badly. What I dread is Vee getting swallowed by the feeling of being chosen before she can ask what she herself wants. My guess from here is that Cassie will matter because she is the one person near Vee who isn’t dazzled by the performance.
