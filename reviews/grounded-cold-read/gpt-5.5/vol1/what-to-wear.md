## Reader reaction

I felt warm through almost all of this, in that giddy friend-table way where Vee’s happiness is contagious before I have time to get nervous for her. This chapter is lighter than the hike and the truck, but not lesser; it lets Vee be witnessed by girls who are not Randi or Cassie, girls who can tease her and be practical and swoony without seeming to have a hidden agenda. I liked seeing her laugh “with her whole body” again, because that was the first thing Randi wanted on sight, and here it belongs to Vee before anyone else gets to claim it.

Meg and Kayla both landed well for me. Meg is especially easy to trust: “Meg, who followed the Pace situation in installments like her shows,” is funny and affectionate, and her line about Danny — “Sometimes they even are” — keeps the scene from turning into pure fairy-tale reinforcement. She does not puncture Vee, but she also knows enough not to sanctify the beginning of a man. Kayla is chaos in the best dorm-dining-hall way, all speed and curls and absolute certainty; her panic over “what to wear” feels comic, but also exactly the right social register for the next threshold.

Vee herself is adorable here, and I mean that with real ache under it. She is proud of Pace, proud of his doctorate, proud of his lifting, proud of his discipline, and she is not even hiding it. The line that got me most was: “It’s making me lose my mind, is what it is.” That feels so true to this stage of wanting, especially because the erotic charge is almost entirely in restraint. Pace has kissed her once, has looked at her once in the truck, and because he has not pushed, every small permission feels hotter. “He asks me first. The small things.” That still works on me. It also still worries me, because I know he and Randi have arranged more than Vee knows, but inside Vee’s experience it is intensely seductive.

Pace is not in the room, but he dominates the chapter anyway. His text — “would you come to my house Saturday. Two o’clock” — made my stomach drop in the good way and the bad way at once. The door motif is suddenly not symbolic in the background anymore; there is literally his door now, his house, Saturday behind it. I feel the pull. I want her to go. I want to see what happens. But because I remember that house, and the bench, and Randi’s night there, I cannot receive the invitation as innocently as Vee does. The chapter title made that worse in a deliciously anxious way: “What to Wear” starts as a girl-talk problem, but it also points toward undressing before anyone says the word.

Randi’s absence is loud to me. Vee is telling the Pace story without her here, and the story feels cleaner for that. No cold citrus, no public claiming, no beautiful hand on Vee’s hand. That makes me realize how much strain Randi brings into every scene now, even when she is warm. With Meg and Kayla, Vee is not being selected; she is being teased by friends. I trust this table more than I trust Smoothie Bird.

The erotic charge is mostly anticipatory, but it works. The peak for me is not the remembered truck strip itself, though the retelling carries that heat; it is the moment the phone buzzes and “His name” turns the fry into “just a thing in her hand now.” Her body has already answered before she says anything. Then: “He’s asking me to his house.” That is the chapter’s whole flare. It is not explicit, but it is charged because the book has taught me what Pace’s house can mean.

I had very little friction. The scene is talk-heavy, but it earns the length because the talk changes the stakes. It takes the private hike/truck experience and makes it social, funny, almost realer because Vee can narrate it and blush and be caught out. If anything, Kayla’s “Bring a change of clothes” is very on-the-nose, but it is also exactly what Kayla would say, so I bought it.

The book’s title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels brighter and more dangerous now. This text is literally a polite invitation: spare, formal, almost comically underexplained. The series title *With a Long Spoon* still makes me think of distance from danger, of supping with something not safe, and this chapter sharpens that because Vee is being invited closer without knowing what she is approaching. I do not have a cover tagline in what I was given here, so I cannot react to one exactly.

What I want next is simple: I want Saturday. I want the clothes, the room, the door, the first sight of his house through Vee’s eyes. I dread the secret catching up with her there. My guess from here is that the house will not be casual. Maybe not sex immediately, because Pace’s restraint is part of the heat, but the invitation feels like a threshold crossed.
