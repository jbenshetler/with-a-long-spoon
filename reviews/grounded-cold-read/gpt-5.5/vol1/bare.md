## Reader reaction

The chapter lands on me as a cold little ache after all the heat. I feel Vee’s need so physically here that it almost hurts to read: the phone light too bright, the empty text field, the shirt first as comfort and then as proof of absence. There is no sex in the scene, but it is intensely bodily. She is not thinking abstractly about whether Pace loves her; she is trying to get his body back onto her skin and discovering that even that cannot answer the question.

My feeling about Vee moves toward protectiveness and sorrow. She is so far inside him now that even the practical little disguise of the shirt over her own shirt matters: “a girl too far gone in a boy.” That line stings because it knows both the tenderness and the humiliation of it. She wants to ask, but she already knows any text would be “small and warm,” and that is exactly the problem. Warmth is no longer enough. Pace’s warmth has become almost unbearable because it does not name itself.

Pace is not present, and that absence changes him for me. I do not suddenly distrust his tenderness, but the chapter makes the limit of it feel stark. Everything he has given her is in the shirt: “The things he made with his hands. The rooms he kept warm for her. The plate he set down once he'd built the hunger in her.” That catalog makes me swoon a little even now, because yes, those things are real. But then the chapter turns the screw: “none of it told her what she was to him.” I don’t think the text is asking me to decide he is false. It is making me feel that care without a name can become its own kind of deprivation.

Cassie’s presence below Vee matters even though she is asleep. I feel the distance from Cassie sharply: she is right there, breathing, safe and loyal, and still Vee is alone with this. The bunk bed image makes Vee seem young in a way Pace’s house often does not. Up top, naked and cold, unable to sleep, she feels exposed without being seen.

The erotic charge is strange here: no explicit activity, but the shirt is intimate enough to feel almost sexual. The peak for me is when she strips down and puts his shirt back on “with nothing under it,” because I know from the whole run of the book how charged clothing and skin and scent are for her. It should be comfort, even a little private pleasure. Instead it curdles. The realization that “the heat in the shirt was her own, given back” is devastating. It takes an object that had been full of Pace and makes it empty, or at least insufficient.

I don’t feel suspicion in the sense of a new plot suspicion. I feel dread. The page has earned that completely, not through anything hidden but through the accumulation of the last chapters: her almost-confession, his not-quite-answer, the public/private panic, and now this sleepless stripping away of the substitute for him. Vee is beginning to know that all the evidence she has is not the answer she needs.

The title, “Bare,” feels exact. It is literal: she ends naked in the cold. But it is also emotional. The shirt, the phone, the routines, the warmth, the private proofs all come off or fail her. She is bare of reassurance. It also recolors the chapter before it: after being so marked by him, she now lies unmarked by any name he has given her. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, still feels like a promise of being asked in, ushered across thresholds, offered things beautifully. But this chapter makes the politeness feel dangerous by omission: what has been invited, and what has not been plainly said? The series title *With a Long Spoon* keeps feeling more apt as the intimacy deepens and the danger stays present. I was not given a cover tagline in this prompt, so I cannot react to one exactly.

What I want next is for Vee to ask directly, but I do not expect her to. My guess from here is that she will keep trying to live on substitutes: shirt, drawer, food, sex, warmth, Pace’s careful listening. What I dread is that the unnamed thing will keep growing until it has to break out sideways, maybe with Randi, maybe with Cassie, maybe with Pace at the worst possible moment.
