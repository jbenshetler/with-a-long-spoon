# Cold read (grounded) — Fairytale

## Reader reaction

I came into this still bruised from Vee walking out of Pace’s house, and this chapter turned the ache sideways by putting me inside Randi’s acceptable dream and letting it fail in real time. It’s lush, almost too lush on purpose: snow, champagne, white tie, sleigh bells, silver falling at midnight. I did feel the pull of it. John is exactly the sort of man who can make a woman’s life arrange itself into a story around him: “a suitable man, a good family,” tickets to the ball, a table appearing, a sleigh appearing, the whole world made smooth by his competence and money. For a while I understood why Randi wanted to let herself believe it.

But the warmth never really took. Not the real kind. The chapter keeps giving me beautiful surfaces and then showing me Randi behind the surface, cold, composed, revulsed at breakfast, making up boys for her father because “the invented ones were safer than the real ones.” That breakfast made me feel for her sharply. Her father’s hearty entitlement, her mother’s edged perfection, the bracelet “locked on her wrist”: it all made the fairytale feel like a cage before John even arrived.

John himself unsettles me in a quieter way. I don’t think the chapter makes him a villain. He is charming, capable, attentive in the socially perfect ways. But he assumes. He inserts himself into the lift line, tells her to “play along,” creates the fake marriage, gets the table, arranges the sleigh, takes her up to the hotel without asking where the night is going. Randi likes some of that, and the chapter lets that be true. “She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked.” That line landed hard because it is erotic and sad at once. She wants relief from having to choose, from having to be exposed by wanting. But after everything I know about Pace making Vee say yes aloud, the absence of asking feels bright-edged.

The sex with John is almost devastating because it is not bad. If it had been bad, Randi could reject the fairytale easily. Instead: “The best: a faultless man asleep in a faultless room, everything done to her and done well.” That is the bleakest part. Her body can have pleasure and still remain untouched underneath. The line that most hurt was “This man had taken a beautiful woman to bed, brought her pleasure, but never found her underneath.” That makes me trust the chapter’s unease. It is not moral panic about sex; it is about being beautifully handled and not known.

My feeling about Randi moved a lot. I’ve been wary of her because of Vee, because of the secret with Pace, because she keeps choosing the shape that lets her have Vee without giving Vee the truth. I still feel that. But here I also felt her loneliness without ornament. She is not just manipulating because it is convenient; she has built a whole life around never having to risk being plainly known. “She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be.” That does not excuse what she’s doing to Vee, but it makes it hurt more. Her “Vee was her gorgeous” got to me. It’s possessive, tender, cowardly, and real.

Pace, though absent, becomes clearer by contrast. “Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best.” After the recent chapters, where I was aching over what Pace would not say to Vee, this line complicated me. It does not erase his withholding. It does remind me why both women are so caught: he finds what is hidden. He listens for the person under the body. But the line “he would never once belong in the life she had built to keep her hidden” also makes me dread the collision between the warm house and the approved world.

Vee’s text nearly broke the chapter open. After all the glitter, her little message is so plain and kind: “thinking about you 💛 hope it’s beautiful out there. i’m here if you want to talk. or not.” That is the real warmth in the chapter. And Randi answers by giving her “the fairytale,” true but not the thing. That is exactly Randi’s danger: she can tell the truth in a way that hides the truth. I loved and hated “Every word of it true. None of it the thing.”

The erotic charge works, but not as heat in the usual swooning way. The skiing and dancing have more erotic life than the sex: matching turns, trading the lead, his hand at her back, her body giving up the steering. The hotel scene is explicit enough to know what happened, especially the lipstick ring, but it is emotionally cold by design. The peak charge for me is the waltz, because it seduces Randi into believing easier is better. Then the sex drains that belief.

The chapter title, “Fairytale,” absolutely points straight at the beautiful lie Randi is handed and partly writes for herself. It did not give everything away, but it primed me to watch the perfection suspiciously. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps feeling more dangerous now: invitations, yes, but polished ones, socially acceptable ones, invitations where refusal may be technically available but emotionally arranged against you. The series title *With a Long Spoon* feels increasingly apt around Randi especially: she is dining with devils of class, family, desire, and cowardice, and she keeps trying to keep her distance while still eating. I was not given a cover tagline in this prompt, so I can’t react to one exactly.

What I want next is almost painful: I want Vee to be held honestly, by someone. I want Randi to tell her, and I dread it because I believe Randi is right that once said, “they would not be this again.” My guess from here is that Randi will keep trying to let Vee “arrive at it on her own,” which feels less like patience than cowardice dressed as tenderness. And after Vee’s rupture with Pace, I dread Randi becoming the warm place Vee runs to while still hiding the central fact.
