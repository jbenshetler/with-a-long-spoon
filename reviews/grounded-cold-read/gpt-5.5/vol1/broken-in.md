I came into this chapter still sore from Vee’s mistake at the dance, and what landed first was how physically depleted she is: the alarm “like a klaxon,” the cold water “like a verdict,” the mascara gone clownish. I felt her shame in my body before I thought about it. But the chapter also lets her gather herself in such a Vee way: apple, bare legs in October, long coat, stride lengthening because *I’m going to fix this.* That steadied me. I wanted her to fix it too.

Cassie, even barely present, remains wonderfully herself. That low “Hrm” instead of a question is such a Cassie sound: she sees the shirt, the wreckage, the not-sleep, and she chooses not to make Vee perform an explanation. I trust her completely.

Brooke makes me alert, but not exactly suspicious. The warmth is immaculate, and “the house had a folder on her anyway” is funny but also unnerving. Vee entering the sorority house feels like stepping into a system that already knows how to classify her. Brooke’s hospitality is real enough on the surface, but I don’t relax around her.

Randi is the center of the pull and the danger here. I felt the swoon of her immediately: no makeup, freckles, robe, bitter-orange room, “Oh, honey. Come up.” She is so good at giving Vee exactly the kind of attention that makes Vee become possible to herself. And when Vee tells the story, Randi’s outrage on her behalf feels delicious: “That *bitch*.” I wanted that for Vee. I wanted someone to be angry with the blonde, to take Vee’s side before correcting her.

But Randi also scares me more in this chapter, because the guidance hardens into command. “Not today” stopped me the same way it stopped Vee. The advice might be right, even wise: don’t rush over in panic, go in sure. But the way Vee’s clenched shame releases “because Randi had said so” is the line that makes me uneasy. Vee is outsourcing her inner weather to Randi. Randi is dressing her, timing her apology, scripting the emotional posture she should bring to Pace. It is intimate and thrilling and too much power in one set of hands.

The erotic charge absolutely works for me, and it peaks not in nudity exactly but in tending: Vee in her bra and skirt, Randi kneeling under the long hem, fastening shoes Vee can’t see. “Somebody kneeling to *tend* to her” is the live wire. The hiddenness of it, the hands under the skirt, the bracing hand high on her thigh, the thumb behind the knee, all of that feels more charged because Vee keeps telling herself this is dress-up. It is dress-up, but it is also desire moving through the socially acceptable channel of styling. Randi knows that channel too well.

I loved the laughter too. The Edna Mode bit gives them a real friendship texture, not just predation or glamour. Vee making Randi crack is still one of the sweetest recurring pleasures in the book. That “small dark one and the tall red one” laughing in the room made me melt a little. It also makes the unease harder, because the warmth is not fake. Randi really does like her. That may be the problem.

Pace is offstage, but this chapter makes him feel vulnerable by absence. Vee’s account of his face going, his not coming after her, his setting the drinks down and leaving, has weight. I still trust his restraint in that moment. I also notice how Randi defines him back to Vee: “If he’s the man you’ve just described to me…” She’s interpreting Pace while hiding that she knows him intimately. That secrecy keeps souring what would otherwise be a beautiful rescue.

The title, “broken-in,” means several things to me at once. The shoes are literally broken-in by Randi, already shaped by her life before Vee wears them. Vee is also being broken in to a way of moving through the world: expensive shoes, scarf tension, walking in sure, letting herself be dressed and led. After the dance lesson, “broken-in” has a bodily echo too: learning that structure can steady her, that being held can make her freer. But it also worries me. A person can be broken in.

*A Polite Invitation* keeps feeling more dangerous now: the politeness is all over this chapter, the bell, Brooke’s welcome, Randi’s room, the styling, the kiss goodbye as “only what they did.” The series title *With a Long Spoon* feels like a warning about dining with danger while pretending it’s manners. I don’t have a cover tagline in the supplied text, so I can’t react to one exactly.

What I want next is Pace’s door. I want Vee to apologize in her own words, not Randi’s, though I’m not sure she knows the difference right now. My guess from here is that the outfit will work on him, but I dread that it will also make Randi present in the room between them: her shoes, her scarf, her timing, her idea of “sure.”
