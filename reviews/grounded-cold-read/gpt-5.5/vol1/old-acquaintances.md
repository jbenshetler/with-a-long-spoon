## Reader reaction

I felt this chapter as cold first. Not metaphorically, at least not only that: the too-short bed, the lavender plug-ins, the furnace going into savings mode, the old room that still knows “Vivienne” but not Vee. It made me sad before it made me anxious. She is back in a house that loves her, but loves a version of her that has been outgrown, and that is lonelier than if the house were simply hostile.

Vee hurts here in a way that feels earned by the last few chapters. The “what am I to you” question has not resolved; Pace’s tenderness, which I still believe in, is suddenly useless because it cannot cross the exact distance she needs crossed. I don’t feel suspicious of him as cruel or faithless from the page, but I do feel the absence he has made. “He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now.” That landed hard. His respect for space, which has been one of the things I trusted most about him, curdles when what she needs is not space but pursuit, naming, some sign that he is also unsettled.

The swoon in this chapter is mostly remembered swoon, which makes it ache. The photos on her phone carry so much: the couch laughing picture, the induction stride, the burgundy dress “lit like it held its own light.” I felt the old pull of Pace most in the memory of the fra diavolo, the garlic and pepper blooming, the sheet slipping and her not fixing it. But the chapter refuses to let even that stay cleanly hers. When the blonde takes her place at the table in Vee’s imagination, it is brutal, because it shows how little ownership Vee feels over her own happiness right now.

Randi is piercingly double here because I have just seen the other side of that text. Vee receives the “fairytale” message exactly as Randi intends her to receive it, and that hurts. Vee’s generosity toward Randi is real: “i'm here if you want to talk. or not.” She even protects the Miranda Holdings knowledge inside herself. But she also envies Randi’s visible life, the wealth, the mountains, the being-seen. I do not blame her. From this childhood bedroom, even poisoned glamour looks like oxygen.

Erotically, this chapter has almost no present heat, but it is haunted by sex. The strongest charge is in memory and jealousy: Pace cooking after sex, the sheet, the imagined blonde in Vee’s place, the sense that his food and his touch share a rhythm of building hunger and then feeding it. It is not arousing in the warm way earlier sex was; it is erotic because Vee cannot separate desire from fear. Her body still wants the arms around her, even while her mind poisons the image with “whose he had them around instead.”

The chapter title, “Old Acquaintances,” feels pointed toward New Year’s, but also toward the old selves and old rooms that come back to claim you. Vee is an old acquaintance to this bedroom now, not its girl anymore. Randi’s “fairytale” is the book’s title-world too: polite invitation, wealth, approval, manners, all the beautiful doors people are invited through. *With a Long Spoon* keeps feeling like a warning about dining close to danger, or accepting what is offered while knowing it may poison you. I don’t have the cover tagline in this prompt, so I can’t react to it exactly.

What I want next is for Vee and Pace to have the conversation he has been avoiding, or failing, or refusing to have. What I dread is that she will reach for Randi first because Randi has a date on the calendar and Pace does not. My guess from here is that the triangle is tightening not through sex yet, but through loneliness: each woman is turning toward the other because the man they both want cannot give the word cleanly.
