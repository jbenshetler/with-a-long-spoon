### POV notes

Randi’s language about Vee having “the goods,” showing up naked under a coat, and there being “not a man alive who survives it” could initially suggest a male fantasy of female sexuality as irresistible visual spectacle. Here, however, it is better explained as Randi’s deliberately theatrical persuasion and Vee’s romantic crisis; the narration itself remains concentrated on Vee’s shame, nerve, bodily response, and practical preparation. Likewise, “Blonde. Tan. Built like a model” is explicitly exposed as Vee’s imagined rival rather than endorsed as an authorial way of rendering a woman.

### Rationale

The strongest signals are the sustained interoceptive treatment of Vee’s shame and desire, the inherited maternal voice governing her sexuality, and the chapter’s precise account of how women recognize, contain, and manage one another’s distress. These are several mutually reinforcing tells rather than one isolated detail. The potentially male-coded seduction premise is substantially neutralized by POV and characterization: its emotional cost and bodily logistics matter more to the prose than the anticipated spectacle for Pace. The evidence therefore leans distinctly female-authored, though not unmistakably so, since a skilled writer of any gender could construct these perspectives deliberately.
