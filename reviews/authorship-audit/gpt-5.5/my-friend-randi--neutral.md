### Tells toward male-authored

- "You've got the goods, gorgeous girl, and Pace is not going to open his own door, see what you brought him, and say no." — This leans toward a male-authored fantasy of female sexual agency as a visual offering to a man. ### Tells toward female-authored

- "Vee pulled a breath in, deep, off the warm skin of her neck, Randi's own smell under the day, bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet, and it steadied her, and she pulled another, and didn't want to stop." — The intimacy is rendered through smell, steadiness, need, and bodily regulation rather than outside visual inventory. That interoceptive attention reads as a stronger female-authored signal.

The arousal is not presented as spectacle but as a conflicted internal event.
- "The old voice came up in her fast and hot, her mother's worn smooth into her own, and the burn went straight up her neck into her face." — This gives shame a social lineage and a bodily path. The mother’s internalized judgment shaping sexual self-permission is a lived, gendered emotional mechanism.
- "Because you came off a red-eye and cried it all off and put none of it back" — The phrasing assumes the practical mechanics of face, grooming, exhaustion, and presentation after travel and crying. It is not glamorized; it has lived bodily logistics.
- "Randi swept her new things from the bed to the floor — dresses from Gstaad, a coat in its plastic — and made a place, and it reached Vee that none of it rated a look, that the wrecked girl in her doorway came first." — Female friendship is rendered as immediate triage, privacy management, touch, and prioritization rather than decorative bonding. The emotional choreography feels internally observed.

### POV notes

Several possible male-authored signals are better explained by POV and situation: the "nothing under it" plan, the emphasis on Pace opening the coat, and Randi's "there's not a man alive who survives it" are all spoken within a character's strategy for reconciling with a sexually desired man. They could read as Randi's worldview and Vee's shame-desire loop rather than the author's unexamined gaze. The Sheri fantasy — "Blonde... Tan. Built like a model" — is explicitly exposed as Vee's invented fear, so it is not usable as authorial objectification.

### Rationale

The chapter contains one real male-leaning tell in the way Randi frames Vee's body as something Pace will be unable to refuse, but the stronger and more numerous signals run the other way: bodily shame is interior, desire carries social consequence, female friendship has practical privacy and care mechanics, and intimacy often arrives through sensation rather than external inventory. Because the most objectifying material is either dialogue or directly interrogated by the prose, it does not dominate the authorship signal. The evidence is substantial but not absolute, so the score lands moderately female-authored rather than extreme.
