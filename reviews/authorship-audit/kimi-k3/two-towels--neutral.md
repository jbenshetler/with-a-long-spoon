### Tells toward female-authored

- "she'd dressed that morning with a door just like this one in mind, had put on, under everything, the satin a girl wears hoping the man won't hand her a reason not to show it to him" — Lingerie rendered as intention and strategy rather than as visual inventory; the garment exists in the text as her plan, never as his sightline. A male-authored version of this scene almost invariably describes the satin on the body.
- "the want still lit in her with nowhere to put it" and "Something in her dropped... she went, and didn't let her face do a thing" — Desire and its deflection tracked interoceptively; arousal carries social consequence (his stopping, the shut bedroom door) instead of spectacle.
- "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs." — A compressed sociology of heterosexual gatekeeping written from the side that performs it; this is lived female-position knowledge, not an observation a man typically narrates from inside.
- "the reflex of a woman who'd had the shade fixed for weeks... The magazine shade would have fought her. This one didn't." — The mechanics of color, complexion, and the magazine-beauty economy ("the girl in the spread had been poured into" the dress) rendered as insider knowledge — how a shade works *against* a wearer is a tell that survives the POV defense only partially: a man writing her POV faithfully could attempt it, but the fluency and speed of the judgment reads authored, not researched.
- "the good-enough black dress she'd worn to everything" — The economics of a woman's occasion wardrobe in five words; compression of lived female experience rather than description of it.
- "She'd been in garages men used this way — an uncle's, a boyfriend's father's — and they ran to one kind of thing" — Reading character off men's spaces (closets, kitchens, garages) as a practiced female survival-and-evaluation skill; the chapter is fundamentally about vetting a man through his domestic objects, a distinctly female-authored narrative engine.

### POV notes

The chapter is close-third on a woman, so her interiority is in principle available to any skilled author. The strongest potential counter-read — a man writing a perceptive woman well — was considered for the fashion/color material, but the interoceptive handling of desire, the gatekeeping line, and the fact that the man is *never* described through an evaluating female gaze on his body (no inventory of him at all; he's read through shoes, knives, and joints) tips the balance: the attention economy of the whole chapter runs the way women writing women tend to run it.

### Rationale

The chapter builds romance entirely out of female-position epistemology: desire felt rather than displayed, clothing as intention and economy, male character audited through domestic order. There is no moment where the prose looks at the woman from outside; even the mirror moment is about cloth-to-complexion harmony, not body display. No male-authored tells appear anywhere. The tells toward female authorship are numerous and independent (interoception, gatekeeping sociology, wardrobe mechanics), warranting a strong positive score and high evidence strength, held short of +45/+50 only because a sufficiently skilled male author writing this POV is not logically excluded.
