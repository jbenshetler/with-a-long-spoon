### Tells toward female-authored

- "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice" / "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." — Emotional consequence carried through female friendship as the scene's actual engine, with the man's body barely described at all while both women's inner states stay legible.

### POV notes

The male-gaze candidates are nearly all Sheri's spoken lines. Pace's own narration sexualizes no one: Vee is described via "the air's different when she's in the room" and what she understands about his cooking, not her body; the waitress gets "a tired face that had kept its smile." A woman writing a male-POV chapter could still give the lesbian sidekick exactly this crude register as characterization, so the body-part talk is discounted. The residual male-leaning hint is only that the trope of the earthy lesbian buddy who appraises the hero's women is one male authors reach for — but the writing of Sheri underneath the banter (defensive makeup, plain-spoken poverty, unperformed eating) cuts against that reading.

### Rationale

The chapter's strongest signals are insider female texture: makeup worn as dare, roots as economics, appetite without apology, and a female friendship that functions as the scene's emotional spine. The objectifying language exists but is consistently placed in a character's mouth rather than the narration, and the narrator's own gaze is notably non-spectacular. This reads more like a female author writing a male POV with a deliberately raunchy female foil than a male author, though the lesbian-buddy device and the concentration of body-part jokes keep it from a higher score.
