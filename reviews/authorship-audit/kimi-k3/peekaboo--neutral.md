### Tells toward female-authored

- "She'd dressed to be lovely and soft to touch, without saying why even to herself... She'd carried the low warm hum of it the whole drive, so that by the time she reached his step it was most of the way to a decision her body had already made." — Desire rendered as embodied anticipation with social-denial framing ("without saying why even to herself"), not as visual spectacle; arousal is interoceptive and narratively consequent.
- "none of the give she'd worn her whole life without once naming it. Everything she'd ever worn had been cut for no one in particular." — Lived mechanics of off-the-rack fit versus a bias-cut garment; this is knowledge of how clothes actually sit on a body, felt from inside the skin.
- "she'd known since the fitting he'd built this to be worn bare... then the bra" — The logistical reality of what a backless/bias dress permits under it; clothing-body mechanics a writer without that wardrobe experience rarely gets this quietly right.
- "*Floozy.* The word flared up automatic and her face went hot — and she stayed anyway, made herself look, and somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame." — Shame at being seen rendered from inside, and worked through rather than erased; the male-authored version of this scene almost never includes the internal censor.
- "The shame of it went through her hot, the shame of having stripped herself bare on purpose to be looked at, and it crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all, and she let it come." — The shame-to-arousal conversion is classic interior female-desire writing; the looking is hers, and the self-exposure carries emotional cost and charge simultaneously.
- "and there, where the slit had opened, was the pale edge of her underwear, cutting across the long line" — The underwear-vs-slit problem and the decision to remove it is exactly the kind of unglamorous clothing logistics women handle and male authors typically skip.
- "the dumb happy kind that always beat her to it" — Crying at a gift as an involuntary, slightly embarrassing bodily event she has a history with ("always"), not a decorative feminine flourish.
- "she knew without being told that he'd been up the night before... She watched him breathe and didn't wake him." — Female-friendship-style attunement and caretaking rendered without being announced; also the daydream of the induction where "the looking didn't cost her a thing" centers her social vindication, not his prize.

### POV notes

The chapter is close-third on the woman, so her interiority is the POV's job — but the question is whether a male author would build this POV this way. The cited tells are not merely "a woman's thoughts"; they are specific embodied knowledge (bias-cut fit, lining placement, bra compatibility, heel height versus hem, underwear lines under a slit) plus a shame/appetite dialectic that the prose treats as primary. "she was the one who'd taken the edge away" and the erotic charge being self-generated by her own choice rather than conferred by his gaze survived the POV-defense test: the man's desiring response ("the grin went off his face into something quieter") is present but brief and seen from outside, correctly subordinated to her experience.

### Rationale

This is a sustained, technically fluent rendering of a woman's body as felt rather than looked at: cloth temperature against skin, the "shimmy" to settle a bias cut, the politics of underwear and hems, desire braided with shame and agency, and even post-coital attention going to the garment's care and the social fantasy it enables. There is no moment of external inventory, no anatomical falsity, no spectacle-arousal; the one explicitly erotic gesture (hooking the bared leg around him) is initiated and narrated from her side. The evidence is dense, varied, and mutually independent, warranting a score near the top of the scale.
