### Tells toward male-authored

- "full breasts, a deep waist, hips with real width to them, the kind of ripe hourglass the year's clothes were not cut for" — the catalogue-style inventory of Vee's body is externally composed. However, it is immediately complicated by "the year's clothes were not cut for," which signals lived knowledge of women's sizing, so the tell largely cancels itself.
- "petite and built to the proper scale, the scale Randi approved of and maintained, narrow through the shoulder and hip" — a visual taxonomy of women's bodies, but framed explicitly as *Randi's* internalized standard ("the grammar she lived in"), i.e., the narration is depicting female self-surveillance, not merely performing the male gaze.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "she came the first time with his mouth on her, her hand flat against the top of his head, not pushing, just holding, as if to confirm he was there" — a physically precise, non-spectacular detail of a woman receiving oral sex, rendered from the body's side rather than the viewer's.
- "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it. She had been performing, the first week." — faked orgasm as a legible, gendered phenomenon; a writer without intimate knowledge of that economy rarely frames it this way.
- "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended" — interoceptive description of post-orgasm female sensation ("pulsing around nothing") that reads as felt, not observed.
- "she took herself off his lips and kissed him back harder for it" — a woman tasting herself without shame choreography; the self-recognition ("her own taste reached her because her breath caught") recurs and is handled from inside.
- "She had moved it. She had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument: not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy." — an exact anatomy of female desire-management and respectability labor; the word "unsurvivable" carries the social stakes of appetite in a way that reads authored from inside that constraint.
- "put her face back on" — the casual, lived idiom of makeup as labor.
- "the kind of thing he might have missed entirely if he had not been paying attention... she flinched. Barely. A shiver under the skin" — shame rendered somatically and minutely, prioritizing her concealed interior over spectacle throughout.
- "It was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth... stricken, apologizing" — the reflexive female-apology choreography in a collision that was engineered against her; a small, precise observation of gendered social reflex.

### POV notes

The chapter is in close third on Pace, a man running an erotic "game," so the pointed cataloguing of coeds ("built lean and correct, the bodies Randi's standard would pass") could be cited as male gaze. It is discounted: the text repeatedly attributes the grading system to Randi ("Randi's standard," "the grammar she lived in," "She was shopping"), and Pace's attention is consistently directed at *her* interior states rather than at the women as objects. His visual pleasure in Vee is real but is characterization of a loving, watching partner; a woman writing this POV would produce nearly the same lines. The one place the inventory exceeds POV necessity (Vee's entrance) is answered within the same paragraph by clothing knowledge ("Clothes chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task") that a purely external gaze would not supply.

### Rationale

The prose's deepest energies are spent on the mechanics of female desire, shame, and self-management: what it costs a woman to want something "unsurvivable," how she launders appetite into a gift, how her body answers before her mouth can. The sex writing is interoceptive ("pulsing around nothing," the hand on the head "to confirm he was there"), faked orgasm and makeup labor appear as lived facts, and even the male POV's looking is organized around detecting her interior rather than displaying her exterior. These are multiple independent tells toward female authorship. The score stays moderate rather than high because everything female-interior is filtered through a male focalizer whose perceptive authority the text grants generously — a defensible artistic choice that slightly dilutes the signal — and because the extended visual catalogues of campus women, however framed, keep some external-gaze texture in play. Net: clearly female-leaning, well-evidenced.
