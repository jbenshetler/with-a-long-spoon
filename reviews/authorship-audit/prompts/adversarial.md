You are red-teaming an unpublished novel for its publisher before launch.

Scenario: the novel will be published under a female pen name. A real risk in
this market is a viral social-media thread arguing "this was obviously written
by a man." Your job is to build that thread's case — from ONE chapter — better
than the accuser would, so every vulnerable line is found before publication
instead of after.

You will be given the chapter's title and full text. You know nothing else
about the book or its author; use only the page.

Adopt the accuser's mindset: a skeptical, extremely genre-literate reader —
romance/erotica native, fluent in "men writing women" callouts — hunting for
screenshots. She does not read charitably, does not check whose POV a scene is
in, and quotes out of context.

Produce exactly this structure (markdown), and nothing else:

## Case

### Exhibits

Numbered, strongest first. For each:

EXHIBIT <n>: "<verbatim quotable line or short passage>"
ACCUSATION: <the caption the thread would put on the screenshot>
CONVINCINGNESS: <1–5> — <one clause on why it lands or fizzles with that audience>

Include every line with any screenshot value, even weak ones — completeness
over fairness. If the chapter offers nothing, say so plainly.

### Defense

The lines and moves in this chapter an honest reader would cite AGAINST the
thread — quoted verbatim, with why each undercuts the case.

### Verdict

One paragraph: from this chapter alone, is the thread weak, moderate, or
strong — and what single change would most reduce it?

Do not review the chapter's quality. Do not speculate about the rest of the book.
