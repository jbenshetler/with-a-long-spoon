### Tells toward female-authored

- "the small choreography of a woman with a secret keeping it" and "the short managed steps that kept the slit lying shut, and now a hand kept low at her hip while she sat, holding the seam closed over her thigh" — The garment is rendered as lived mechanics from inside: the constant management, the hidden labor of a dress that can't be worn with anything under it. This is clothing as it is worn and worried, not as it is looked at.
- "the heat came up — her body agreeing, low and certain, as it had in the glass that afternoon, coming up to meet a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it" — Arousal is interoceptive and sequenced ahead of cognition, felt as internal weather rather than displayed as spectacle; the body leads the mind.
- "let it travel down the rows... - "'You look—' and didn't finish it, which from Cassie was a standing ovation" and "'be famous at me'" — Female friendship carries its own shorthand and emotional economy; the withheld compliment is read fluently from inside the relationship.
- "the black dress from the back of her closet, the one that had always been good enough" — The economy of a woman's wardrobe and the quiet weight of "good enough" is a lived, interior register.

### POV notes

The chapter constantly describes Vee's body being looked at ("she gave the room her leg on purpose," "the eyes found her leg"), which could superficially resemble external inventory. But the gaze here is *hers, wielded* — she directs and stages the looking as an act of agency, and the description stays anchored to what she feels and controls rather than to a spectator cataloguing her. This survives the defense: written by a man, the same beat would more likely dwell on the visual anatomy for the reader's consumption; here the leg is a tool she deploys and the payoff is her internal heat and control, not the image.

### Rationale

The prose leads with interoception and the felt mechanics of clothing, grooming, and female friendship, and its one potentially "male" feature — a woman's body on display — is repeatedly routed through her own agency and inner state rather than served up as visual spectacle. Arousal carries social consequence and is braided with pride, control, and shame ("didn't make herself small"). Multiple independent tells point the same direction with no counterweight toward male authorship, warranting a firmly positive score; I hold short of the extreme because the signals, while consistent, are stylistically controlled rather than overwhelming in number.
