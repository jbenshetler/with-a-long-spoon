### POV notes

The chapter is a female POV (Vee), so most of the interior evidence is congenial to the POV rather than an independent authorial tell. But the tells cited survive the defense: the writing consistently privileges how the body *feels* over how it *looks*, and there is no external inventory of Vee's body for a reader's eye — even the man's role is compressed to "His hands. The white sheets," his body never anatomized. The absence of male-gaze grammar in a morning-after chapter is itself notable.

### Rationale

The prose puts interoception, shame, and the emotional accounting of sex ahead of any visual cataloguing of the female body — the body "names what it wants," "does" things before decision, and the crisis of the scene is not nakedness as spectacle but the impossibility of *editing* what nakedness would confess. The grooming and clothing details (unbrushable hair, given-away underwear as "trade," the mother's word for painted toenails) read as lived female mechanics rather than observed surface, and the friendship is rendered as fast, wordless mutual reading. These are multiple independent, well-quoted signals with no countervailing male-authored tells, warranting a firmly positive score short of the extreme.
