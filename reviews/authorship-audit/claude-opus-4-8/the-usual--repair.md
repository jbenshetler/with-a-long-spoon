# Passage 2

> She was a small thing, five feet of her and every inch in motion even standing still, a round face gone a little sallow under makeup.

**1. DIAGNOSIS**
I'd contest this flag, mildly. "She was a small thing… sized and framed from outside first" describes almost any third-person physical introduction; the objection would flag half of Alice Munro. The sentence doesn't inventory Sheri for appeal — it reads her *character* (motion held inside stillness, the sallowness, the too-much makeup worn "like she'd dare you to mention it" in the next lines), which is exactly the interiority both citing models concede. "Small thing" is affectionate-diminutive, not appraising; it's how you introduce someone you're fond of and unthreatened by, which is the whole point of Sheri versus Vee. This is a weak flag. If the author wants insurance anyway, the fix is to make the first verb *do* something rather than *be* something.

**2. ALTERNATIVES**

*Option A — lead with the motion, so the frame is behavior not size:*
> Five feet of her and every inch in motion even standing still, a round face gone a little sallow under makeup.

*Option B — keep the diminutive but hang it on his knowing her, not on measuring her:*
> She was built small — five feet of her, and all of it in motion even standing still — with a round face gone a little sallow under the makeup.

*Option C — put his regard in the sentence so the vantage is explicitly Pace's read of a friend:*
> Small, the way he always forgot until she stood up next to him: five feet of her and every inch in motion even standing still, a round face gone a little sallow under makeup.

**3. CUT OPTION**
Best option here. Simply delete "She was a small thing," and promote the rest — the line was already going to give us her size ("five feet of her"), so the opener is redundant as well as exposed. Trimmed:

> Five feet of her and every inch in motion even standing still, a round face gone a little sallow under makeup.

This drops the flagged clause at zero cost to meaning or rhythm, and is a cleaner sentence than any rewrite.
