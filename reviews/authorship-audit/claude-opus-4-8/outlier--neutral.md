### POV notes

The POV character (Vee) and all three principals are women, but that alone is not authorship — a male author can write a female POV. What survives the "would this read the same if a woman wrote it" defense is not the fact of the female cast but the *mode* of attention: no body is inventoried, no woman is ever described as she looks to be desired, and the emotional labor of shielding a friend from her own shame is the chapter's engine. There is no candidate male tell to discount here — nothing in the prose lingers on female physicality or renders arousal as spectacle.

### Rationale

The chapter offers no male-authored signals at all: female characters are never anatomized, sexualized, or seen from the outside as objects, and the one moment of acute bodily attention (Randi's stillness) is felt inwardly rather than looked at. On the positive side, the prose is organized around the lived mechanics of a female friend-group and around shame managed through discreet care — interoception first, consequence over spectacle — which the rubric names as female-authored tells. These signals are genuine but moderate in strength: friendship and empathy are writable across genders, and the chapter contains none of the strongest female markers (clothing/grooming mechanics, appetite, arousal with social cost). That yields a modest positive score with mid-range evidence.
