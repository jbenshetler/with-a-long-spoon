### Tells toward male-authored

- "that backside. I got maybe four seconds of her at the bar... I still think about it more than's decent" / "the one with the big tits" / "Bet those long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you" — This is the body-inventory register (backside, tits, legs as discrete spectacle), the closest thing here to porn-grammar appraisal. But see POV notes: it is entirely dialogue from a queer female character, not narration.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "Her hair was two colors, bleached pale and grown dark at the root, permed at the ends where it fell over the collar of a blue chambray shirt." — Lived grooming mechanics: roots growing out of a bleach job, a perm settling at the ends. Rendered as knowledge of maintenance and its lapse, not as look-at-her.
- "a round face gone a little sallow under makeup. Too much of it, slightly off, and worn like she'd dare you to mention it." — Makeup read as social armor and self-presentation with attitude, its meaning inhabited from inside rather than merely catalogued.
- "Couldn't get right with me not talking to my people. Kept picking at it... call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas." — The chapter's emotional core is a woman's grief, estrangement, and a two-month relationship with another woman ("Paige. Junior in the design school"), given genuine interiority and consequence; Sheri drives the scene rather than decorating it.
- "Sheri ate like she rode, all of her folded down into it, no ceremony and no apology" — Appetite in a woman rendered without shame or apology, and without eroticizing it.

### POV notes

The crude anatomical appraisal of Vee — "backside," "big tits," "long legs" — is the single strongest male-authored candidate, but it is placed wholly in Sheri's mouth (a woman openly attracted to women) and the male POV character actively resists it ("That's not what I meant"). Pace's own account of his desire is routed through the emotional and intellectual — "The air's different when she's in the room. It crackles" and "she gets what it's for... The philosophy of it" — with the sexual admitted only under Sheri's needling. A male author indulging the gaze would typically seat that inventory in the POV narration; here it is deflected into a female character's voice and the narrator declines to render Vee's body at all. So the body talk survives as characterization, not an authorial tell.

### Rationale

The narration never inventories a female body for its own sake: Vee, the ostensible object of desire, is described only relationally and emotionally, and the one character who ogles is a woman whose desire carries social sting. What the prose does render firsthand is grooming as maintenance and armor (roots, perm, defensive makeup) and a woman's grief and estrangement from inside, with the female character owning the scene's moral weight. These are moderate, mutually reinforcing signals toward female authorship; none is a knockout, and the material is filtered through a competent male POV that could plausibly belong to a skilled writer of either gender, so I land modestly positive rather than emphatic.
