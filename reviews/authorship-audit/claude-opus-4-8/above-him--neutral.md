### Tells toward female-authored

- "her clothes had gone *looser* for it. Not all of them. Her bras, the band riding higher than it used to sit. The few good pairs of underwear cut to fit her and not to a guess." — Weight change registered through the lived mechanics of how garments actually shift on a body — the band riding up rather than the cup — and knowledge of the difference between clothes bought to fit and clothes bought "to a guess." - "worked at the wreck of it the only way it could be done, one piece at a time. Brush first, from the ends up, a little higher each stroke, until it ran clean. ### POV notes

The chapter is a close female POV, so most of the "tells" are inseparable from characterization — but the instruction is to cite female-authored signals that survive as authorial craft, and the clothing/grooming/body-economics details do: they reflect knowledge a writer must possess, not merely a stance the narrator holds. The one male body described ("The broad chest he kept hidden all day," "the old scar was a paler line") is rendered sparely and affectionately, not inventoried as spectacle; I did not read it as a tell in either direction.

### Rationale

There are no male-authored tells: the female body is never inventoried from outside as looks, arousal is never visual spectacle, and the woman's interior life dominates the page. Pushing toward female-authored are several independent, technically accurate renderings of lived female mechanics — bras riding up with weight loss, the off-the-rack sizing problem for an unusual build, the ends-up brushing and pins-in-teeth of putting hair up, the scrunchy as tracked daily object — plus sex rendered as emotional and social consequence rather than choreography. These are the kinds of details that are hard to fake from outside and read as interoception-first. I hold back from the extreme because a skilled writer of any gender could research or observe these, and the evidence, while consistent, is concentrated in clothing and grooming rather than spread across many independent domains; hence a strong-but-not-maximal +39 at evidence strength 7.
