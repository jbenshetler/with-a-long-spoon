# Passage 1 (lines 11–13)

> "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that."
>
> It was the same thing he'd have said, if he were the kind to say it. Sheri was the kind. She remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather, and she'd been doing it across this handlebar for two years.

**DIAGNOSIS** — The flag is right, and it's precise about where: not Sheri's line (that's earned character) but the narrator stepping in to co-sign the grade. "It was the same thing he'd have said" makes the POV endorse the appraisal as shared appetite, so the reader can't tell whether the chapter is observing the ranking or agreeing with it. The tell is ratification, not vocabulary.

**ALTERNATIVES**

a) *(Break the endorsement into distance — he recognizes the impulse without joining it.)*
> "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that."
>
> He'd noticed. He noticed the way you notice weather you're not going out in. Sheri noticed the way other people talked about weather — out loud, to whoever was on the other handlebar, and she'd been doing it across this one for two years.

b) *(Keep his complicity but locate it as a thing he's tired of in himself, which is interiority rather than co-sign.)*
> "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that."
>
> The thing he didn't like was how fast he'd have agreed. Sheri said it out loud so he didn't have to, and she'd been sparing him that way, across this handlebar, for two years.

c) *(Let the ratification stay implied by his silence and put the narration entirely on Sheri as a phenomenon.)*
> "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that."
>
> He let it sit. Sheri remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather — idly, accurately, expecting no argument — and she'd been doing it across this handlebar for two years.

**CUT OPTION** — Strong here. Deleting the ratifying clause and keeping only the Sheri-as-weather characterization removes what convicts while keeping the beat:

# Passage 2 (line 27)

**DIAGNOSIS** — Partial contest. The "small engine / driving the grade" register is genuinely his craftsman idiom and reads as admiration-of-capability, not appraisal — I'd defend it. The fix is to keep the engine and re-anchor the tail to work rather than spectacle.
