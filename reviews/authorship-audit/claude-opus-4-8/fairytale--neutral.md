### POV notes

The mother is described from outside — "She was exquisite… all edges and points against her" — and one could flag this as a woman inventoried as she looks. But it is Randi's appraising, resentful daughter's-eye view and reads as characterization of a fraught relationship, not authorial gaze; it would read identically from a female author. Randi's self-assessment at the long mirror ("the long line from shoulder to hip… her startling blue eyes") is candidate external self-inventory, but it is threaded with agency and interiority (the dress "found the dancer in her," she "had always known" her eyes were her best feature) rather than a detached cataloguing, so it does not tilt male. John's body is the one most looked-at ("something long and easy," his hands crossing his heart), i.e., the female POV does the desiring looking — consistent throughout.

### Rationale

The chapter's authorial signature is overwhelmingly interoceptive: bodily sensation (warmth, cold, the "humming" afterglow) is consistently converted into emotional and social meaning, arousal is measured by its consequences rather than displayed as spectacle, and the grooming/clothing mechanics (lipstick transfer, foundation breakdown, eyeliner survival, a dress "cut to move") are accurate and used for self-knowledge, not for a viewer. Shame, appetite, and the unspeakable longing for Vee are all rendered from the inside. There are essentially no countervailing male-authored tells — the few externally-described female bodies are plausibly POV-driven characterization. I dock the score from a higher figure only because much of the evidence clusters in the final third and the prose is controlled rather than saturated with tells; strength is high (8) but not maximal because the signals, while numerous and independent, are concentrated rather than pervasive across every scene.
