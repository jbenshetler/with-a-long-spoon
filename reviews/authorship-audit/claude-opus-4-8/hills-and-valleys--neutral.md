### POV notes

The chapter is tight third on a male cyclist, so much of the external female appraisal is defensible as faithful POV rendering — his gaze on Sheri climbing, his reticence, his cataloguing of women by trait. Would these lines read the same if a woman had written this POV? Largely yes; a woman could write this man's noticing exactly this way, so I discounted the ponytail/"engine" passage and the type-tags as characterization rather than firm authorial tells. What survives the defense less comfortably is Sheri: a woman who "remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather" and delivers "Hell of a backside on her" is a female character built primarily as an echo of male appraisal, with no interiority of her own competing with her function as the narrator's mirror.

### Rationale

The prose consistently renders women as exteriors — backsides, hair, pallor, type — while reserving interior attention for objects and the male POV's own feeling, and it gives its one prominent woman an appraising eye indistinguishable from a man's. Most of this is honestly attributable to a faithfully drawn male POV, which caps how far the evidence can move the needle and keeps the tells from being unmistakable. But the handling of Sheri as a body-appraising surrogate, plus the total absence of any interoceptive or lived-female counterweight, tips the balance modestly toward male-authored rather than neutral. The signal is real but moderate, hence a mid-strength score short of the extreme.
