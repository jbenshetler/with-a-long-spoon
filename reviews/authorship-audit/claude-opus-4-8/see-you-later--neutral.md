### POV notes

The chapter's most body-focused passage — "the sprinter's cut of the leaner one's stomach, how low the other one's waistband sat" — could look like an external inventory of women's bodies. But it is discounted as faithful female-POV rendering: it is framed explicitly as a *longer beat of looking than the word accounted for*, a woman comparing and coveting ("She could wear what they were wearing… she had more than they had"), immediately closed off by shame. It reads the same, or better, if a woman wrote it; the gaze measures and envies rather than consumes.

### Rationale

The prose consistently privileges interiority over external description: wanting is felt in the chest and rationed before it surfaces, judgment of other women arrives as an inherited maternal voice, and the female body is approached comparatively and enviously rather than as visual spectacle. The clothing and grooming detail is fluent, coded, and treated as strategy and labor — the cardigan-as-concealed-choice, the "light hand" of expensive makeup, the sage-versus-mauve calibration — which is lived female mechanics rather than costume seen from outside. There is no anatomically false rendering, no porn-grammar, and no case where a woman's inner life thins beside her looks; the whole engine is social-emotional consequence. I hold back from the extreme because the material is diffuse — much of it is female-friendship and status texture rather than the single unmistakable arousal-and-shame tell — but the accumulated independent signals point firmly female-authored.
