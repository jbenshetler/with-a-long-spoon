### POV notes

The "She saw what he saw" inventory is the strongest male-tell candidate, but it is explicitly framed as the character seeing *herself* being seen — the self-consciousness of a woman who has deliberately called the gaze and is monitoring her own exposure ("she gave it to him to see"; "she knew he could see what her body was doing"). It is immediately wrapped back into interior consequence (the mother's "floozy," the involuntary press of the thighs), so the spectacle serves the character's shame-and-appetite loop rather than the author's appetite. It would read much the same written by a woman inhabiting that self-watching; I discount most, though not all, of its weight.

### Rationale

The prose is dominated by interoception, lived female-body and clothing mechanics, and an arousal that never floats free of social and emotional cost — the mother's verdict, the class shame of the russet dress, the felt overshoot of the warming flannel. These are numerous, independent, and hard to fake from outside. The one externally-inventoried body passage pulls toward male-authored but is structurally a self-seeing move that carries immediate interior consequence, so it cannot outweigh the accumulation of inside-first evidence. That leaves a solidly female-authored reading, held back from the extreme only by that single spectacle beat.
