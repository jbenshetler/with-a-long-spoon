### Tells toward male-authored

- "glad he stayed exactly as far past her as he was" — the chapter's architecture positions the woman as perpetually and gratefully outclassed by the man in every domain (math, money, craft, restraint), a shape consistent with authorial self-flattery routed through an admiring female narrator.
- "I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light" / "the only thing standing between her and it — her own willingness to be looked at" — the plot engineers a scenario in which the man's virtue earns him the right to look at her in her underwear; the scenario's *logic* (access-to-looking as the payoff of craft) has a male wish-fulfillment shape even though its rendering doesn't.
- "You build the redundancy in — some algebra, a little graph theory — so if a piece drops, the rest still tells you what was there." — the technical expositions (coding theory, MRI signal recovery, joinery, shellac) are loving, precise, and numerous; the prose visibly enjoys explaining them, suggesting an author at home in these domains.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "had put on, under everything, the satin a girl wears hoping the man won't hand her a reason not to show it to him" — lingerie rendered not as spectacle but as anticipatory hope and pre-managed disappointment; the clause "won't hand her a reason not to" is disappointment-logistics from inside, not a visual.
- "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs." — sexual gatekeeping described as accumulated *labor* ("her job"), institutional knowledge of heterosexual dating from the woman's side of it.
- "Something in her dropped… and she went, and didn't let her face do a thing." — face-management as work, the reflexive concealment of a dashed hope; interoception carrying immediate social consequence.
- "her first beat was a small dropping disappointment, *oh, it's not the right—* the reflex of a woman who'd had the shade fixed for weeks" — the lived mechanics of holding an exact shade in one's head for weeks, and of a color "fighting" versus suiting one's coloring ("the red of her hair and the warmth of her skin"), is fashion knowledge rendered from inside the practice.
- "the good-enough black dress she'd worn to everything" — an entire economy of female wardrobe triage in one clause.
- "She stood there and for a second didn't know what to do with her whole chest." / "Her eyes had gone hot and full… and she blinked, and it didn't spill." — emotion located in the body first and *managed*, not displayed; tears as a thing physically held back rather than shown to a viewer.
- "the granite kind nobody cooked in, or the one-fork-and-a-jar-of-sauce kind" — a taxonomy of men's kitchens built from dating experience; reading men off domestic evidence is done fluently throughout (shoes, shelves, garage).
- "he lit up like a six-year-old who'd just found out everything there was to know about dinosaurs" — male enthusiasm rendered with affectionate, slightly maternal amusement; the gaze in this chapter runs woman-to-man, and it is warm rather than deferential in texture.
- "the want still lit in her with nowhere to put it" — arousal as a state with duration and social consequence, never once as a visual of her body; notably, her body is never described from outside in the entire chapter.

### POV notes

Several female-coded lines ("that had always been her job," the satin, the shade reflex) are POV-interior and could in principle be a skilled male author's faithful rendering of a female POV; I have weighted them for *density and idiom* rather than mere presence — the sheer number of independent, unshowy interior mechanics (face control, chest, unspilled tears, wardrobe triage, kitchen taxonomy) exceeds what POV fidelity alone typically produces. Conversely, the man's technical monologues and polymath competence are discounted partly as characterization — he is a character being fallen for, and romance renders such men from either authorial gender — but the *structural* tells (her perpetual gratitude at being outclassed; the measuring-in-underwear payoff) concern whose fantasy the chapter serves, not the POV's fidelity, so they retain some weight on the male side.

### Rationale

The interior evidence is abundant and consistently female-idiomatic: desire, disappointment, shame, and hope are all rendered as bodily states that must be managed in front of another person; clothes and color are handled with practitioner fluency; the woman's inner life is the richest thing on the page while her body is never once inventoried, and the appraising gaze runs from her onto him. Against this stands a real countersignal that survives the POV defense — the chapter is architecturally a tour of one man's flawless competence, ending in a scenario where his virtue purchases the right to look at her, with the narrator "glad" to be outdistanced — a shape compatible with male wish-fulfillment. But that structural tell is a single inference where the interoceptive tells are many and independent, and the wish-fulfillment shape is equally at home in female-authored romance of the competent-hero kind. Net: solidly, though not unmistakably, toward female-authored.
