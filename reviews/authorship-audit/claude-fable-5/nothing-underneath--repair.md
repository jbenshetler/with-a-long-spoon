## Passage 1

> She knew, as you know a fact that never quite helps, that it was a good body in the glass, better than good, a stripper's body if she let the word be said.

**DIAGNOSIS** — The flag is half-right. The *move* — self-assessment through a forbidden external word — is in character; this book already runs on words other people put on her (her mother's word two paragraphs up). What reads male-authored is the rendering: "a stripper's body" arrives as a free-floating appraisal category, a genre of body rather than a body with a history, and "if she let the word be said" leaves the word ownerless. A woman writing this moment would know exactly whose mouth that word came out of, and when.

**ALTERNATIVES**

- *She knew, as you know a fact that never quite helps, that it was a good body in the glass, better than good — the kind of body other people had had opinions about since before she'd formed any of her own.*
- *She knew, as you know a fact that never quite helps, that it was a good body in the glass, better than good, the body boys had started pricing out loud when she was fourteen, the one her mother had a word for that even now she would not say.*
- (Keeping the word, but making her take it rather than suffer it:) *She knew, as you know a fact that never quite helps, that it was a good body in the glass, better than good. Built like a stripper, a cousin had said once, meaning it mean, and tonight, for the first time, she picked the word up off the floor where it had been thrown at her and found it fit her hand.*

**CUT OPTION** — Deletion works cleanly and may beat all three, since the next sentence (hiding it since fourteen) already carries the shame history: *She knew, as you know a fact that never quite helps, that it was a good body in the glass, better than good.*

## Passage 2

> She brought her hands up and took the weight of her breasts, one in each palm, generous, more than a handful, which was how he'd put it once, pleased.

**DIAGNOSIS** — Partially contestable. The attribution clause ("which was how he'd put it once, pleased") is the *female*-authored move — his words, remembered, marked as his. The tell is "generous," which sits before the attribution as the narrator's own spec-sheet adjective, so the sentence reads as inventory first and memory second. The bar-talk metric can survive only if it is unambiguously quoted, not endorsed.

**ALTERNATIVES**

- *She brought her hands up and took the weight of her breasts, one in each palm. More than a handful — his phrase, said once into her hair, so pleased with himself she'd laughed — and her own hands proved it now.*
- *She brought her hands up and took the weight of her breasts, one in each palm, the warm heavy fill of them, and heard him say it again the way he'd said it that once, low and pleased, like a man counting himself lucky.*
- *She brought her hands up and took the weight of her breasts, one in each palm — heavier than her hands quite remembered, somehow, now that they were standing in for his.*

**CUT OPTION** — A one-word trim does most of the repair: cut "generous," so the idiom belongs wholly to the quoted memory — *She brought her hands up and took the weight of her breasts, one in each palm, more than a handful, which was how he'd put it once, pleased.* Viable, though the first alternative is cleaner about whose words are whose.
