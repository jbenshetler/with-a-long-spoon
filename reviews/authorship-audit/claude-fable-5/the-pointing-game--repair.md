## Passage 1

> He stayed with it longer than she needed, reading the slackening in her thighs, her breath going from held to spilled. **He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it.** She had been performing, the first week. She was not performing now.

**DIAGNOSIS** — "He knew the difference" is the stock authority-grant of male-authored erotica: the narrator certifies the hero as an infallible instrument for reading female orgasm, with no evidence offered and no daylight between his confidence and the truth. The two sentences that follow it already carry the claim — as *inference* — which is exactly how a woman writing this man would let him have it: certainty earned on the page, or certainty owned as his.

**ALTERNATIVES** (drop-in for the flagged sentence):

*A — evidence-anchored; the claim becomes a read, not a credential:*
> The first week she had come the way a woman comes for a man she is still auditioning — timed, a little musical, some part of her thighs never quite forgetting him. There was nothing addressed to him in this. This was graceless, and he trusted graceless.

(If used, cut the original's last two sentences — this version absorbs them.)

*B — free-indirect; the certainty stays absolute but is marked as his:*
> He flattered himself that he could tell a woman performing release from a woman delivered to it — though it wasn't flattery if it was true, and with her it was true.

*C — mechanism instead of credential:*
> A woman performing release keeps a little of herself back to run the performance with. Nothing in her was held back now.

(Reads as his working theory rather than the narrator's law, because it's a *how*, not an "he knew.")

**CUT OPTION** — Deletion beats all three rewrites. The surrounding sentences make the identical claim inferentially, and the paragraph tightens:

> He stayed with it longer than she needed, reading the slackening in her thighs, her breath going from held to spilled. She had been performing, the first week. She was not performing now.

## Passage 2

> He came up over her before she was done, while she was **still swollen and still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended.** He had decided this beforehand.

**DIAGNOSIS** — "Pulsing around nothing" is her interoception ventriloquized from outside her body: vaginal emptiness is knowledge only she has, and he isn't inside her yet (the next sentence has him sliding into "all that grip and flutter," which is where that sensation legitimately becomes his). The triple inventory — swollen, pulsing, undefended — itemizes her post-orgasmic state as a tactical opening surveyed from above. Note "swollen" alone is defensible: his mouth was just on her; that's lived observation.

**ALTERNATIVES** (drop-in for the full first sentence):

*A — externalize; his senses only, opportunism intact:*
> He came up over her before she was done, while the aftershocks were still moving through her in slow rolls he could see in her belly, while she was too far inside her own body to compose a face for him.

*B — keep "nothing," relocated to what he can watch:*
> He came up over her before she was done, while she was still moving with it, hips lifting faintly at nothing, no part of her yet returned to its post.

*C — "undefended" owned as his word, and his want named with it:*
> He came up over her before she was done, while she was still somewhere he couldn't follow, open in a way she never let herself be open — undefended, he thought, and wanted her exactly then.

**CUT OPTION** — A one-clause trim is competitive with any rewrite: drop only "and still pulsing around nothing," keeping the earned observation and the framing word:

> He came up over her before she was done, while she was still swollen, still given over to her body, undefended. He had decided this beforehand.

## Passage 3

> The question reached her body before it reached her, and it took her all at once — she came, hard, her sex clenching around him, her legs drawing him in, her arms coming up to lock him against her, **far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it.** […] She had wanted it, and **her body had said so before she could make the wanting mean anything safer.**

**DIAGNOSIS** — The orgasm itself is defensible — she is post-orgasmic, hypersensitive, being slowly worked back up; a second climax triggered mid-stroke is physiology, not porn. The male-authored tells are the two narratorial rulings around it: the narration *certifies* that his words, not his body, caused it ("far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it"), and then certifies her body as a truer witness than her speech. Both verdicts should be his — a woman writing this man would let him believe it absolutely while leaving a seam.

**ALTERNATIVES:**

*A — for the first span; the causal ruling becomes his stunned read:*
> she came, hard, her sex clenching around him, her legs drawing him in, her arms coming up to lock him against her — and he had barely been moving; nothing he was doing accounted for it. The question had gone straight through her.

*B — for the first span; certainty owned, alternative acknowledged and dismissed:*
> she came, hard, around him, before he'd given her anything new to come on, her arms locking across his back as if he'd said something dangerous. Maybe it was only that she was still lit from the first. He didn't believe that.

*C — for the second span; the verdict marked as a wager, not a fact:*
> She had wanted it — he'd have staked the whole afternoon on it — and it had gotten out of her before she could make the wanting mean anything safer.

**CUT OPTION** — Trimming both rulings works nearly as well as rewriting them; the timing carries the inference without the narrator's signature:

> …her arms coming up to lock him against her. He kept moving and let her have it.
> …She had wanted it, before she could make the wanting mean anything safer.

## Passage 4

> She had not taken back the wanting — she could not, her body had said it twice and she knew he'd heard. She had moved it. She had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument: not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner.

**DIAGNOSIS** — I largely contest this flag. The paragraph is preceded by "And he understood what she had done, because it was beautiful" — everything after that tag is already branded as his construal, and the dramatic irony *requires* his reading to be rendered at full confidence. The one word that genuinely overreaches is "unsurvivable": a verdict about her inner life delivered in aphorism, where his authorship has faded from the reader's ear. The repair is a single marker of ownership, not a recast.

**ALTERNATIVES** (minimal interventions; everything else stands):

*A — tie the verdict to the chapter's own established motif ("the grammar she lived in"), marking it as his model of her system:*
> …not a woman who hungered for another woman — nothing in her grammar could survive that — but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner.

*B — one clause of narratorial air on the framing sentence upstream (replaces "And he understood what she had done, because it was beautiful"):*
> And he understood — or believed he did, with the whole of himself, which in him was the same thing — what she had done, because it was beautiful.

*C — soften only the coercion of "she could not":*
> She had not taken back the wanting — how could she, her body had said it twice and she knew he'd heard.

(The rhetorical question is his voice reasoning, not the narrator ruling.)

**CUT OPTION** — Viable and clean: delete "she could not," the paragraph's single hardest authority-grant, and leave the aphorism to stand on the "he understood" tag:

> She had not taken back the wanting — her body had said it twice and she knew he'd heard. She had moved it.

Full deletion of the paragraph is off the table; it's the hinge.

## Passage 5

> Tall, and built on a lavish scale, lush in a way that had nothing curated about it and nothing apologized for — full breasts, a deep waist, hips with real width to them, the kind of ripe hourglass the year's clothes were not cut for and that did not care.

**DIAGNOSIS** — Appraisal is the scene's grammar and stays; the tell is the *form* of the appraisal: the breast–waist–hip triplet is measurement syntax (a parts manifest in catalog order), and "ripe" is produce vocabulary. Everything on either side of the em-dashes — "nothing curated," "nothing apologized for," "the year's clothes were not cut for" — already reads as a woman's lived knowledge of how clothes and bodies negotiate. The dissenting model is right about that clause; the fix is to let it do the work the parts-list is doing.

**ALTERNATIVES** (drop-in for the full sentence):

*A — gestalt over inventory:*
> Tall, and built on a lavish scale, lush in a way that had nothing curated about it and nothing apologized for — abundant everywhere the year's silhouette wanted scarcity, an hourglass the clothes were not cut for and that did not care.

*B — keep one anatomical note, kill the list:*
> Tall, and built on a lavish scale, lush in a way that had nothing curated about it and nothing apologized for — a genuine hourglass, deep at the waist and generous everywhere the waist wasn't, the kind of figure the year's clothes were not cut for and that did not care.

*C — route the scale through Randi's grading system, which the chapter has built:*
> Tall, and built on a scale Randi's whole grammar was organized against — lush, nothing curated, nothing apologized for, an hourglass so frank the year's clothes had no answer to it and she had plainly stopped waiting for one.

**CUT OPTION** — Strong; arguably beats the rewrites. Delete the triplet and "ripe," keep the frame:

> Tall, and built on a lavish scale, lush in a way that had nothing curated about it and nothing apologized for — the kind of hourglass the year's clothes were not cut for and that did not care.

## Passage 6

> the shoulders met, **Randi sleek and narrow against the redhead's lush curves and the half-head of height she had on her,** brought together so cleanly that the smaller woman seemed the one put upon

**DIAGNOSIS** — At the instant of contact the narration freezes both women into contrasting silhouettes composed for the watcher — "sleek" versus "lush curves" is centerfold pairing grammar, and he's a hundred feet away reading bodies like a spec sheet. The watching itself is the scene and stays; the fix is to render the contrast in the kinetic vocabulary the chapter has already given him (dance, scale, execution) rather than in shapes.

**ALTERNATIVES** (drop-in for the flagged span):

*A — scale only, motion carries it:*
> the shoulders met, Randi slipping in under the half-head of height the other girl had on her,

*B — contrast kept, moved into his dance-floor register:*
> the shoulders met — Randi precise against the redhead's easy amble, giving up a half-head of height and taking every advantage of it —

*C — minimal vocabulary swap:*
> the shoulders met, Randi small and exact against the redhead's scale and the half-head of height she had on her,

**CUT OPTION** — Deletion likely beats any rewrite: the sentence's own closer already encodes the size contrast ("the smaller woman seemed the one put upon"), so the span can go entirely:

> and the shoulders met, brought together so cleanly that the smaller woman seemed the one put upon.
