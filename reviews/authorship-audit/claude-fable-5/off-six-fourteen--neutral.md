### POV notes

The POV is a close third on Vee, a woman, so pure interoception (warmth, the cold line off the sill, the confession's "quieter" voice) is partly discounted: a skilled male writer working this POV would also put sensation first. Likewise the drink question and the state-troopers line are *dialogue content* any careful author could include. What I did not discount is the *texture around* those beats — that the safety dossier is felt by Vee as "its own small proof of something — that she had this," that the drink question sits inside Cassie's gladness rather than interrupting it, and that the whole scene runs on friendship choreography (sock feet, the *mm*, the pillow taken "without unfolding") rather than on either woman's appearance. Those survive the "would this read the same from a woman?" test in the affirmative direction — they read as written from inside the experience, not observed from across the room.

### Rationale

Every signal in the chapter points one way, but the signals are correlated (one scene, one register), which caps the score short of certainty. The prose never once looks *at* either woman — no hair color, no figure, no face beyond expression — while it registers everything they feel, and it treats the machinery of a women's debrief (the drink check, the dossier, the borrowed shoes, the agency claim "I did it on purpose") as native ground requiring no exposition. A male author faithfully rendering this POV could produce the dialogue, but the incidental fluency — safety-ritual experienced as comfort, joy that needs the whole body, intimacy in a pillow absorbed without unfolding — is the kind of texture that most reliably tracks lived experience.
