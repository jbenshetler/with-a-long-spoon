## Audit

SCORE: +15
EVIDENCE STRENGTH: 6

### Tells toward male-authored

- "Her breasts were still shining faintly in the lamplight from the work of it, lifting and falling" — the lifting and falling is felt, but "shining faintly in the lamplight" is a camera positioned outside her; she is flat on her back watching the ceiling and could not see this. The body is rendered as it looks, not as it feels, in a moment that belongs to sensation.
- "the faint pale pink of her, the color so slight it was scarcely there at all... came up bright" — a color inventory of her own nipple at a moment when it is under his mouth and invisible to her. The felt component ("drew tight... with no say from her") is there, but the passage's center of gravity is visual spectacle she has no sight-line to.
- "You've used me terribly... Promise to use me again soon." — in aggregate with three rounds in one evening, the eager nude, and zero friction anywhere, the scenario has the frictionless shape of male wish-fulfillment: the insatiable woman who begs to be "used" and asks for nothing that costs him.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "a deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of" — pure interoception: sex registered as aftermath in the body, projected forward into tomorrow, with an emotional valence attached to the soreness itself. This is the inside of the experience, not the view of it.
- "the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this" — arousal carrying social consequence. The reflexive risk-calculus around a nude photo, framed as universal female knowledge ("any girl had"), is lived rather than imagined, and sending it is explicitly named "the brave part."
- "with Pace she was a girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it" — objectification articulated from inside as the norm being escaped; the sentence presumes a history of being reduced to a body, which is experiential knowledge, not gaze.
- "put her face back on, the eyes and the poppy-red mouth, and worked at herself in his mirror until she'd found the version of herself she wanted him to keep" — the mechanics of makeup as self-curation, "put her face back on" being idiomatic insider phrasing, and the psychology of authoring the image of oneself another person will retain.
- "The flicker came before we could stop it" — actually: "The flicker came before she could stop it, small and low and all her own, another woman at his table on a day meant for family" — jealousy rendered as involuntary somatic event, owned with mild shame, then examined and extinguished. Shame and appetite from inside.
- "Something turned over low in her, half-lit" — desire as interoceptive event, vague and bodily, not visual.
- "it landed warm under her breastbone" — emotion consistently mapped to precise interior locations throughout the chapter.

### POV notes

- "laid out like a woman in an old calendar" and "a girl who had decided to be looked at" — discounted as male gaze. She has explicitly requested pinup ("She had wanted pinup, glamour, a girl on a calendar"); the objectified framing is her own chosen self-presentation, faithful characterization of a woman performing to-be-looked-at-ness on purpose.
- "her as his eyes had her, and she didn't want to give the phone back" — the idea that she can only find herself beautiful through his gaze could read as male-authorial assumption, but it is presented as her noticing this about herself, which is a coherent (and self-aware) female psychology, so I discount it.
- The wish-fulfillment architecture cuts both ways and I weight it lightly: the insatiable, nude-sending girlfriend is a male fantasy shape, but the tender domestic god who cooks, poses her with kisses, says "I like you exactly like this," and carries a wounded, unaskable past is equally a fantasy shape written for women. Scenario is not prose.

### Rationale

The interoceptive evidence is dense, varied, and consistent: soreness anticipated with gladness, jealousy as an involuntary flicker owned and named, desire "turned over low in her, half-lit," the makeup-and-mirror self-curation, and above all the nude-photo beat, where arousal is inseparable from risk calculus and trust ("the cold sane thought any girl had," "believing him was the brave part") — that is arousal with social consequence, rendered from inside. Against this stand two genuine visual slips — the lamplit shining breasts and the color-graded nipple, both described from a vantage the POV character does not have — plus a frictionless erotic scenario whose shape I largely discount because it fits female-directed fantasy as readily as male. The slips are real and keep me well short of a strong score, since they are exactly the fingerprint of a skilled male author writing an otherwise convincing female inside; but they are two moments against a chapter's worth of sustained interoception, lived mechanics, and consequence-aware appetite. The balance tilts moderately female-authored.
