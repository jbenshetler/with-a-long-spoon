## Audit

SCORE: +32

### Tells toward male-authored

- "the still of a man holding a line at cost" — the taciturn provider is granted a noble interior (his silence has "cost," his hands "settled everything") even at the moment the scene indicts him; a reader could take this as authorial sympathy with the strong-silent male self-conception. Weak, and discounted below.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "Her shoulders had climbed toward her ears somewhere back on the highway and she left them there." — tension registered as proprioception she notices and chooses to keep, not as appearance; the body as felt state, and the odd, true detail of *permitting* the tension.
- "There was a taste in her mouth like a coin held under the tongue." — dread rendered as interoception before any visual; the chapter consistently reaches for how things feel from inside rather than how she looks.
- "she had kept her up two nights running, holding a smell in the dark that told her nothing." — desire encoded as scent-memory and epistemic anxiety (what does his care *mean*), not image; arousal carrying interpretive stakes.
- "Like my body comes with me attached. Like it's just fine as it is. Nobody ever made me feel that." — the lived experience of being looked at without being reduced, named as rare; this articulates the inside of female objectification (and its relief) rather than performing the gaze itself. Her body is never once described visually in the whole chapter.
- "How do I say what this is to Cassie? To my father? What do I tell my mother" — the relationship's crisis is its social legibility to her network; naming, and what women in her life will be told, is the actual battleground, which is the +50 rubric's "arousal carrying social and emotional consequence."
- "She did not want him to have it, the crying, on top of everything else he had of her and would not name." — crying framed as a possession he could take, shame and ledger-keeping rendered from inside; a very interior accounting of emotional exposure.
- "He would have brought the heat up hours ago... so she would never once be cold under his roof" / "She kept her coat buttoned to the throat." — thermal comfort as love-language and the buttoned coat as armor: the mechanics of women's cold, clothes-as-boundary, deployed with unshowy accuracy.
- "she went on over him, because she had it now and she wasn't going to lose it." — the felt fragility of holding one's argument against a man's answer, the fear of losing the thread mid-confrontation, written from inside that experience.

### POV notes

Most candidate tells must survive the "faithful female POV" defense, since a skilled male author could render Vee's interiority. The strongest survivors are structural rather than local: across the whole chapter there is not one line of external inventory of her body, arousal exists only as consequence and memory, and the climactic grievance is about *naming to other women* — choices at the authorial level, not the character's. Conversely, "the still of a man holding a line at cost" and the idealized wordless provider (cooking, warming, "hands that settled everything") could read as male self-portrait, but the chapter's architecture turns that archetype against itself — "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me" — so his nobility is her perception under interrogation, and I discount it as POV/characterization rather than authorial endorsement. Note also that this idealized taciturn man is itself a staple rendered-from-her-side fantasy; it cuts neither way cleanly.

### Rationale

The chapter's signals point consistently in one direction: sensation precedes image everywhere (coin under the tongue, shoulders held high, heat closing over her, the wet felt before the knowledge of crying), the woman's body is never described from outside while her interoception is described constantly, desire is inseparable from its social consequence (what to tell Cassie, her mother), and the single most distinctive line — "Like my body comes with me attached" — names the inside of being seen-without-being-reduced in a way that reads as lived rather than imagined. No genuine male-coded tells survive scrutiny; the one candidate (the ennobled silent man) is both the character's fond perception and the thing the scene dismantles. What keeps the score from climbing higher is that every local tell is defensible as expert POV craft, and a first-rate male literary writer could plausibly produce all of it; the evidence is coherent and multi-sourced but not unmistakable. That yields a solidly positive score with good, though not overwhelming, strength.
