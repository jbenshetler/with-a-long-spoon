### Tells toward female-authored

- "her mouth was bare, the color of it left on him; the rest of the face had thinned and smeared. The eyeliner had held. Of course it had." — Precise, lived cosmetics mechanics: lipstick transfers, foundation "thins and smears," eyeliner outlasts everything. This is grooming knowledge rendered casually and correctly, the exact category male-authored female POV most often fudges or omits.
- "She began to put the face back on, and it came back as it always did, even, exact" — Makeup as maintained armor, "the face" as a put-on-able object — femininity as ongoing labor, from inside the labor.
- "Pace's lead was the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for and good enough to catch, and it left the rest of the way to her." — A follower's-eye discrimination between two men's dance leads, felt through the hand at her back. This is partner dancing from the follow position, technically exact and emotionally load-bearing.
- "the cold came up under the chair and through her jacket and settled against the small of her back" / "her thighs burning by the third turn, her cheeks going numb above her collar while she sweated under the shell" — Interoception first, consistently: cold, burn, sweat located in specific body regions rather than the body seen.
- "a ring of her lipstick, where she had taken him in her mouth and felt her own mouth do it from a long way off" — A sex act that male-authored prose typically stages visually is rendered instead as dissociation — she watches her own mouth from a distance. Arousal and its absence carry the whole emotional weight.
- "her body was still loose with how good, still humming in the ordinary places. And it had meant no more than a good sandwich eaten fast" — Physical pleasure fully credited and fully separated from meaning; the sex scene itself is elided entirely in favor of its consequence.

- "her mother... stood to give her a perfunctory hug, all edges and points against her" / "eating her revulsion" — Even the family hugs are felt as texture and swallowed affect, not observed.
- "It was cut to move. It found the dancer in her... the pale blue of the dress woke them up and gave them to the room" — The dress chosen for what it does — to her line, her eyes, the future story she'll tell a daughter — not for how it exposes her. Clothes as narrative and self-knowledge, never as inventory.

### POV notes

The POV character is a woman, so in principle every interior line above could be a male author rendering female POV faithfully. I discounted nothing as a male tell, because there were no candidates: at no point is Randi's body catalogued from outside (the mirror scene stays on the dress, her eyes, and strategy), and the one naked body described post-coitally is *his*. The remaining question is whether the female-leaning material is merely competent POV ventriloquism. I weighted most heavily the details that go beyond empathy into procedural knowledge — which makeup survives sex, how a follow reads two different leads through pressure, the exact social physics of a friendship that would "learn to watch itself" — because these are the places ventriloquism usually thins out, and here they are dense, exact, and structural. The idealized towering love interest ("Her hand disappeared into his glove") is characterization of Randi's fantasy, not evidence either way, though its shape cuts, if anything, away from the male-tell profile.

### Rationale

There are no male-authored tells at all — no external inventory of the female body, no visual staging of arousal, no anatomical falseness — while the female-authored tells are numerous, independent, and of the strongest type: interoception carrying every scene, cosmetics and clothing rendered procedurally rather than decoratively, partner dancing felt from the follow, sex elided in favor of its emotional accounting, dissociation during a sex act rendered from inside, and a female friendship whose potential loss outweighs the entire romantic plot. These operate across separate domains (body, grooming, dance, sex, friendship), which is what makes them additive rather than one skill repeated. I hold back from the maximum only because a genuinely skilled writer of any gender could in principle assemble all of this, and the chapter's fairytale-pastiche framing shows an author working deliberately in constructed registers; but the pileup of unforced, low-visibility procedural detail is exactly what deliberate construction rarely fakes. That yields a strong positive score on strong evidence.
