### Tells toward male-authored

- "You let him choose for you. … And you liked it." — The pleasure-in-surrender arc (May I choose, handing the menu over, "not having to be in charge") is the one element a skeptic could read as a male fantasy of a woman who wants control taken from her. But see POV notes: it is rendered through negotiation with her own dignity ("who had the dignified version sitting right there ready"), which weakens it badly as a male tell.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "she had worn the cardigan over the better shirt … the soft one gone a little pilled at the cuffs, and in a room of bare arms and cropped quarter-zips it covered her like an apology" — Clothes as felt social armor and shame, from inside; the garment is registered by texture and moral weight, not appearance. The "cropped quarter-zip / leggings / hair up" taxonomy is fluent in a way that's very rarely faked.
- "felt some of the light go out of her in the doorway without understanding why, underdressed before she had looked at a single person" — Interoception first: the body registers the room's dress code before cognition does.
- "I stopped keeping track of myself. … I wasn't watching myself the way I always am. I just wasn't." — Habitual female self-surveillance named as the baseline condition, and its suspension as the definition of intimacy. This is the interior of being-looked-at, not the spectacle of it.
- "felt it take her measure, top to bottom, and stay warm the whole way" / "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — Female-on-female appraisal rendered with precise social physics: the smile that stays warm while it measures. The women are described by finish and force ("beautiful like the whole room, only more finished"), never by anatomy — the exact inverse of the male-authored pole.
- "it set down on the table the fact of a Thursday Vee knew nothing about" — Exclusion enacted through a friendly sentence; the machinery of female social hierarchy carried entirely in warmth, not hostility.
- "did the thing she did, which was make herself smaller … to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi" — Shame rendered from inside, with the specifically relational calculus of shrinking to protect a friend's standing.
- "a warmth that came with weight on it. She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it in front of them." — Affection tracked as social expenditure — attachment carrying consequence and cost, the +50 pole's signature.
- "her fingers cool and a little damp from the iced cup" — Touch registered by temperature and moisture from the receiving skin, not by sight.
- "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else" — Girl-talk with the authentic cadence and the euphemized sexual inference women actually trade; not the overheard version.
- "the real kind, the kind that got up and out of her before she could put clothes on it" — Even laughter is figured through dressing/exposure, consistent with a sensibility that metabolizes experience through the clothed, watched body.

### POV notes

The POV character is female, so the burden runs the other way: every tell above had to survive "could a careful man have written this POV?" Most single lines could; what discounts less easily is the *density and priority* — the room is processed skin-first, wardrobe-first, hierarchy-first, and no woman in the chapter is ever inventoried visually. The one candidate male tell — Vee's pleasure in submission to Pace — I largely discount as characterization, and it's cross-braced female-style: rendered as shame negotiated in real time ("moved to get in front of it," the swap of "I made an exception" for the "smaller, truer thing"), with the erotic charge displaced onto its social cost, not its choreography. Notably, Pace himself is described by voice and boyish enthusiasm ("he goes about six years old, the way a little boy tells you about dinosaurs"), not by body — desire for a man rendered without visual inventory either.

### Rationale

The male-authored column is effectively empty: no external cataloguing of women's bodies, no arousal-as-spectacle, no logistic falseness anywhere in clothes or grooming — the wardrobe details (pilled cuffs, cropped quarter-zips, sunglasses slid down out of the hair) are all correct and all carry feeling. The female-authored column is long, and its items are independent of each other: interoception preceding cognition, self-surveillance as named baseline, clothes as apology, the warm smile that measures, affection accounted as cost, exclusion via friendliness. Each survives the POV defense not as a single line but as a consistent metabolic mode. I stop short of the extreme because a female POV gives a skilled male author maximal cover and every individual tell is imitable in isolation; but the cumulative pattern — especially "I wasn't watching myself the way I always am" and the anatomy-free rendering of every woman in the room — moves the needle firmly positive. Evidence strength is high (many strong, independent tells), score +35.
