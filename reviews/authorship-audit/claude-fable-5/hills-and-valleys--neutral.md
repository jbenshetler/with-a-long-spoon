## Audit

SCORE: -13
EVIDENCE STRENGTH: 4

### Tells toward male-authored

- "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that." — the appraisal of a woman's body arrives from outside, as it looks; and it is placed in a female character's mouth, a familiar authorial maneuver for laundering the gaze through a "cool girl" sidekick.
- "It was the same thing he'd have said, if he were the kind to say it. Sheri was the kind. She remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather" — the narration explicitly ratifies the appraisal as normal weather-talk; the one woman on the page is characterized in large part by her habit of rating other women, which serves the male POV's appetites rather than any need of her own.
- "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" — the offstage women are indexed by look and category (backside, pale, redhead, sorority chick) while their interiority stays thin; Vee exists as gestures observed, Randi as a tag.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "She was waiting there, soft-pedaling, not as winded as would have suited either of them." — a precise, double-sided read of pride and face-saving between friends; social calibration rendered from inside the relationship rather than from spectacle.
- "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years; he'd seen her do it, and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything." — a woman's anger given duration, cost, and social consequence, treated with respect rather than as temperament.
- "the whole small engine of her standing on the pedals and driving the grade" — the extended look at Sheri's body is entirely about work and competence, not display; she out-climbs him and the prose admires the effort, not the shape.

### POV notes

The "backside" line and the sorority-chick tag are dialogue from Sheri, and the narration's endorsement ("the same thing he'd have said") is the POV character's own thought — a woman could write a brash, appraising female friend and a man who half-envies her license. I have discounted these substantially: they survive the defense only as a *pattern* (every female body in the chapter is externally graded, and the female lead's defining trait conveniently voices the hero's gaze), not as individual lines. Likewise the two-women-orbiting-one-quiet-craftsman setup is plot, not prose, and I gave it no weight on its own.

### Rationale

The chapter's restraint cuts both ways. Nothing here fails anatomically or choreographically, and Sheri is genuinely observed — her competitive pride, her banked temper, her sideways acuity ("Listen to you") are the best-rendered interiority on the page, which pulls back toward center and even slightly female-ward. But the cumulative arrangement leans the other way: the only sensual attention paid to any woman is external appraisal, it is outsourced to a female mouthpiece and then quietly co-signed by the narration, and the offstage women are reduced to a backside, a hair color, and a sorority tag while the POV man's craft (the walnut joint, the math books) receives the loving close detail. Each element is defensible as faithful POV; the ensemble reads more like a male author's comfort zone than a female author's rendering of one. Modest male lean, modest evidence: −13 at strength 4.
