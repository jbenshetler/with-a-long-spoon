### Tells toward female-authored

- "She felt it as an adjustment somewhere behind her breastbone, a small *wait*, the kind her body produced before her mind had caught up with what her body was already registering." — Interoception first: the body detects the emotional mismatch before cognition names it, which is the signature move of the whole chapter, not a one-off flourish.
- "Her body did a small thing it did not have a name for, before she had decided to think about anything at all." — Same mechanism repeated independently two days later; emotional knowledge routed through unnamed somatic events rather than through visual or declarative registers.
- "She had imagined saying them out loud and felt the heat come up the back of her neck at the imagining." — Shame and appetite rendered from inside as physical heat, before any words are risked.
- "the rule was that he said it first, and she did not know the rule was a rule until she had bumped against it from the inside, and now I know... the not-saying had its own gravity and the gravity was, by now, part of her shape." — A gendered social rule experienced as an internalized constraint discovered by collision, not observed or explained from outside; this is lived rather than researched knowledge.
- "the way a man rearranges himself when he wants you against him for a particular reason... the deliberateness of being held" — The generalizing "a man... when he wants *you*" is the perspective of someone who has been on the receiving end of male tenderness many times; the physical intimacy is rendered entirely as what it feels like to be held, never as what she looks like being held.
- "small flickers, moments across the fall when what she knew about him had had to do small repair work to keep functioning. This joined them. She did not look at the pile." — The maintenance labor of preserving a good image of a partner, catalogued from inside with its avoidance intact; affection carrying accumulating social-emotional cost.
- "her body knew what to do with being held this way... and the small *wait* behind her breastbone went into the body where small *waits* go when the quilt is warm" — Comfort and doubt processed simultaneously and somatically; arousal/warmth has consequence (it suppresses the warning signal), which is the +50 pattern of the rubric.
- Notably, the woman's body is never once described from outside in the entire chapter — no inventory, no mirror, no appearance at all — while *his* body is the one given sensory texture ("warm and rough, the steady weight of a man").

### POV notes

His line "What you do — what your body does — I've never" converts her emotional confession into a sexual compliment — but that is his characterization, not an authorial tell; a writer of either gender could put that sentence in a man's mouth. What I credit instead is the *framing around* it: the author stages the mismatch so it is detected somatically and deferred, never editorialized. Likewise "the steady weight of a man who was not in a hurry" is faithful female-POV appreciation of a male body and moves nothing by itself; I cite it only for the asymmetry (his body textured, hers invisible). A skilled male author could in principle write all of this, so I hold the score well short of the extreme despite the density.

### Rationale

Every load-bearing moment in this chapter runs through interoception — breastbone, neck-heat, the unnamed "small thing" in the laundry room — with cognition arriving late and often refusing to finish ("either end of it hurt"). The chapter's subject is itself a distinctly inside-out rendering of a woman's emotional economy: the unwritten rule about who says "I love you" first discovered "from the inside," the engineered substitute sentence, the pile of "small repair work" that keeps a partner's image functional, warmth actively metabolizing doubt. Meanwhile the female body is never inventoried and the male body is the one given sensory detail, inverting the classic male-authored asymmetry. These are numerous, independent, and mutually reinforcing tells on the +50 side of the rubric, with nothing on the other pan. I stop at +35 rather than higher only because sustained interiority of this kind is achievable by a very good writer of either gender and there is no logistical/anatomical knowledge here (clothes, grooming, female friendship) that would be hard to fake — the evidence is strong but of one kind.
