### Tells toward female-authored

- "worn like that for the hall, for the walk to the bathroom, for the mornings Cassie was up first and might look and wouldn't have to say anything, a girl too far gone in a boy. Over her own shirt it was just a thing she wore. It hid what it was." — This is the lived social choreography of a shared girls' room: the layering of a boyfriend's shirt not for warmth but to encode it as neutral clothing, pre-empting a roommate's silent judgment. The shame is anticipatory, wordless, and managed through garment logistics — appetite carrying social consequence, rendered from inside.
- "pulled them both off together, hers and his, and dropped hers down the side, and put his back on with nothing under it, the cloth straight onto her, the way she wore it when no one could see" — Precise, unglamorous mechanics of undressing in a bunk (both shirts peeled as one, hers dropped down the side), and a clean distinction between the public and private ways of wearing the same garment. Nothing is staged for a viewer.
- "the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." — The fetish object is dismantled interoceptively: warmth traced to its actual source in her own body. This is desire audited from inside the skin rather than admired from outside it.
- "the cold of the room came down on her bare skin all at once, and it was worse. It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." — A young woman is naked for the entire final movement of a chapter titled "Bare," and the prose supplies no visual inventory whatsoever — no shape, no glimpse, no mirror. The body exists purely as temperature and endurance; nakedness is punishment self-administered, not spectacle.
- "gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him" — The asymmetry of who lies awake carrying the relationship is observed with a flat, experienced bitterness; the man's ease is characterized from the outside as a category ("a man with nothing grinding in him"), which is how this imbalance reads from the sleepless side of it.
- "There was nothing she could put in there that would come back as what she needed... the ones she thought of were all small, and would be answered small and warm, and would leave her exactly here." — The exact futility-calculus of drafting a text that cannot ask its real question: relational uncertainty as the engine of insomnia, with the phone's social mechanics ("his name near the top, where it stayed") rendered with insider precision.

### POV notes

The strongest defense against every tell above is that this is simply a faithfully written female POV, and a skilled male author could produce any single one of these lines. I discounted nothing outright, but I weighted accordingly: the smell-catalogue of the boyfriend ("the sawdust and the salt of the work") and the idealizing list of his provisions are conventional enough that either author could write them, and they moved the score little. What resists the POV defense is cumulative and structural rather than line-level: the total absence of external visual rendering of the woman's body at the one moment convention invites it, the shirt-as-concealment-from-the-roommate logic, and the deflation of the shirt's warmth as her own heat returned. Those are choices about what the prose notices, not what the character would notice — and they consistently notice from inside.

### Rationale

There are no male-coded tells at all: no outside-in inventory of the female body, no visual arousal, nothing anatomically or socially false. On the other side, there are several independent female-coded signals — clothing worn as social camouflage within a female friendship, interoception governing every physical beat, longing that terminates in self-punishing cold rather than release, and a naked body rendered entirely as felt temperature in a chapter that all but dares the author to look. Each tell survives the "could a woman have written this POV" inversion trivially and mostly survives the reverse question, though a very good male author writing close-third female interiority remains conceivable, which caps the score short of the extreme. One chapter, one register, one consciousness — but the tells point one way with no counterweight, so a firm positive score at moderate-strong evidence is warranted.
