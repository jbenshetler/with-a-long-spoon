## Audit

### Tells toward male-authored

- "blonde, tan, a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's" — the rival is conjured, and Vee's own body classified, in an external show-floor taxonomy (bodies sorted by how they display), which is a gaze-first habit; it's mitigated but not erased by the framing that Vee "built her from spite."
- "the leg out of the slit clear to the hip and the burgundy lit like it held its own light, and at the end of the leg the heel Randi had knelt and buckled to her foot" — the chapter's most charged image of Vee is a camera-ready spectacle of her own body, and the kneeling heel-buckling carries a faint fetish gleam; the emphasis is on how the leg looks in the frame, not how the dress or shoe felt.
- "the dress Pace had built for her actually did" — her signature moment of self-possession is authored by a man who "built" her presentation, and the surrounding testimonials ("he doesn't take advantage... wasn't him they were jealous of, honey") assemble a flatteringly flawless male object.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "the smell of it coming up and drawing the hunger out of her before she'd have called it hunger" — desire rendered interoceptively and pre-verbally, appetite arriving in the body before it has a name; this is the +50 pole's signature move.
- "her body quiet and the evening having used her well; the sheet slipping and him seeing and her not fixing it" — the aftermath of sex given as felt state and small social choice, with zero visual inventory of her body; arousal carries consequence and ease, not spectacle.
- "She read it twice for anything that gave her away, found nothing, and sent it. Put the phone facedown. Turned it back over to be sure it had gone." — the micro-mechanics of managed vulnerability between women (the message "demanded nothing — an open hand offered"), and of the secret kept for Randi's dignity, are rendered with lived precision.
- "she'd cut off at the waist herself before she let it go — scissored the leg away, the exact thing that had made her feel like herself" — shame negotiated from inside: the same body split into two audiences, one house, and she names herself as the one who divided it.
- "the casserole and the potatoes and the good meat her mother made because it was Christmas and it was love and had never once been the thing Vee was hungry for" — food, mother-love, and unnameable appetite braided together from the inside rather than observed.
- "And it turned while she was in it. The one at the table in the falling sheet stopped being her." — jealousy staged as bodily displacement inside her own memory, an interior mechanism rather than a pictured rival scene.
- "the fight, if it was a fight, her mother's question in her own mouth by then, *what am I to you*" — relational ambiguity tracked with obsessive emotional bookkeeping, including the inherited quality of the question itself.

### POV notes

The stripper/fashion-model line is explicitly the character's spite-construction at midnight, so much of its force is characterization — a woman could write a woman flagellating herself in exactly that vocabulary; I discount it to a partial tell because the taxonomy itself (not just the spite) is display-oriented. The induction-photo spectacle is likewise partly defensible as Vee's own hard-won pleasure in being seen ("her body come up to meet the moment"), a beat women authors write often; I count only its residue — the camera-framing and the buckled heel. The idealized Pace is weak evidence either way: the flawless nurturing older man is as at home in female-authored romance as in male self-flattery, so I give it little weight.

### Rationale

The interoceptive evidence is dense, varied, and structural rather than decorative: hunger preceding its own name, post-coital quiet, the scissored photograph, the twice-read text to Randi, jealousy as substitution inside her own remembered body. These are many independent tells all pointing the same way, and several concern precisely the mechanics (female friendship's careful diplomacy, the split self performed for parents) that the scale's positive pole names. Against that stand two or three genuinely gaze-flavored moments — the stripper taxonomy, the lit leg and kneeling heel — each of which survives only partially after the POV defense, plus a faintly wish-fulfilled male love object. The negative tells are real enough to hold the score well short of the pole, but the balance of quality and quantity favors female authorship clearly: +22, with strength 7 for numerous strong tells complicated by a few credible counters.
