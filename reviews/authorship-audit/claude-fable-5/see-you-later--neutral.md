### Tells toward female-authored

- "She had worn the cardigan over the better shirt on purpose this morning, as she did most mornings, and the room did not know it was a choice, and she let it not know" — The lived mechanics of women's dressing as private, layered social calculation — concealment as a daily, deliberate act the room can't see — rendered entirely from inside rather than as appearance.
- "*Floozies,* Vee thought, the word arriving whole and in her mother's voice" / "*that's not the kind of attention a nice girl,* and the picture closed itself" — Internalized maternal policing of female dress, experienced as an inherited voice that closes down a thought mid-sentence. This is shame-from-inside, the +50 pole's signature move.
- "she had more than they had, and better than any of them, the leggings and the crop would do on her what they did not do on the mauve one" — Other women's bodies appraised competitively — *how would that look on me* — not as spectacle. This is the female measuring gaze, distinct in grammar and purpose from the male one.
- "three in the sage and one in a dusty mauve, hair up in the big scrunchies, faces done exactly and a shade too loudly, the matched uniformed prettiness of girls who had gotten ready together and would leave together" — Precise fashion-color vocabulary, makeup calibration ("a shade too loudly"), and the social institution of getting ready together — insider fluency in female group ritual.
- "Her face was done, with the kind of light hand that takes skill and time and money to manage" — Knowing that "natural" makeup is expensive labor is knowledge from the practicing side of the mirror.
- "It had a shape to it, a place at the end where the other person was supposed to laugh and hand their own name back and the two of you were off" — Micro-anatomized female social choreography — the bit, the beat that runs long, the fluency that "came up under it before she'd decided anything" — the whole chapter runs on this granularity.
- "the want had nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her,* and it sat there in Vee's chest, warm, unexamined, glad" — Intense girl-on-girl want rendered as longing for proximity and belonging, carrying social consequence, never converted into visual or erotic inventory.
- "a hand flat between Vee's shoulder blades for one second" / "*Love you, girl.*" — Female physical affection and its idiom captured tactilely and idiomatically exactly; the hug is felt, not watched.
- "she made herself wait one full beat so it would not come out the size it actually was" — Interoception-first management of eagerness; feeling precedes and governs performance throughout.

### POV notes

The most male-reading passage — "a sports bra and a little open shirt... bare midriffs, a lot of leg, worked-for bodies deliberately underdressed and wanting to be seen" and "the sprinter's cut of the leaner one's stomach, how low the other one's waistband sat" — is a candidate outside-inventory of female bodies, but it fails the tell test: the gaze is explicitly Vee's, framed as envy-plus-judgment ("the kind other girls measured themselves against"), and it resolves into self-comparison and the mother's-voice shame spiral rather than lingering as spectacle. That's characterization of a woman reading a room of women, and it's executed with the competitive grammar women's appraisal actually uses — so it discounts as POV and, if anything, its accuracy counts toward the female column. Similarly, Vee's rapt attention to Randi's clothes and scent is POV ("Vee noticed clothes, always had"), though the specificity of what she notices is authorial.

### Rationale

There are no tells in the male direction that survive the POV defense, and a dense accumulation in the other: the chapter is built interoception-outward — feeling arrives first, is sized, managed, folded down — and its expertise is precisely in the domains the +50 pole names: the daily politics of clothes (the cardigan as invisible choice), inherited body-shame in the mother's literal voice, women appraising women competitively rather than lecherously, the fine choreography of female social rhythm, and want between girls rendered as belonging with consequence rather than spectacle. Any one of these a skilled male author could research or imitate; the score reflects that many independent tells all point the same way, at a granularity (the light-hand makeup economics, "a shade too loudly," the bit's open door, "hand their own name back") that reads like fluency rather than study. I hold back from the +40s only because everything here is, in principle, achievable by an exceptional cross-gender ventriloquist and no single line is dispositive on its own.
