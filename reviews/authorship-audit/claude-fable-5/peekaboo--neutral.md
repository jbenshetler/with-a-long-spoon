## Audit

SCORE: +33
EVIDENCE STRENGTH: 7

### Tells toward male-authored

- "in the daydream she didn't fold her arms or find the wall; she stood in the middle of the light and let them look... It just felt like what she was for." — The scenario's deep structure (male craftsman bestows a perfect garment; the woman's arc terminates in gratitude, tears, and displaying herself as his made object) has the shape of a maker's wish-fulfillment fantasy, and "what she was for" flirts with defining her purpose as being looked at. It's weak because it's framed as her own guilty daydream with the shame negotiated on the page, but it's the one place the chapter's center of gravity tilts toward the man's achievement rather than her experience.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "no slack standing off the waist, none of the give she'd worn her whole life without once naming it. Everything she'd ever worn had been cut for no one in particular." — This is a lifetime of ready-to-wear fit rendered as a bodily absence you only notice when it's gone; it's felt knowledge about women's clothing, not observed knowledge.
- "At her hips it caught the way good cloth does and water doesn't, and she worked it down with a low shimmy, both hands smoothing it over herself" — The actual mechanics of getting a bias-cut gown over hips — the catch, the shimmy, the two-handed smoothing — reported as routine procedure rather than spectacle.
- "cool against her bare skin for half a second and then warm with her own heat" — Interoception first: the dress arrives as temperature and weight before it arrives as an image.
- "there, where the slit had opened, was the pale edge of her underwear, cutting across the long line the dress had made of her. Her first thought was to keep her steps small, so it wouldn't show." — The visible-panty-line problem, and the instinctive first response of managing it with gait, is a small, unglamorous, lived detail a mirror-fantasy version of this scene would never include.
- "the pumps at the back of her closet, the sensible pair that went with everything, the leather gone soft and the heels worn low... On this they'd have read like an apology" — A woman's shoe inventory audited by register ("read like an apology") — clothes as social grammar, not as costume.
- "*Floozy.* The word flared up automatic and her face went hot — and she stayed anyway, made herself look, and somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame." — Shame at her own reflection arriving involuntarily, in internalized vocabulary, and being worked through rather than around; shame and appetite from inside.
- "The shame of it went through her hot, the shame of having stripped herself bare on purpose to be looked at, and it crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all, and she let it come." — Arousal carrying moral and social weight, with the shame→desire conversion tracked as process and consented to ("she let it come").
- "she'd known since the fitting he'd built this to be worn bare" — Bra logistics handled as unremarked practical knowledge, the kind that goes wrong or goes missing in outside-in renderings.
- "the low warm hum of it the whole drive, so that by the time she reached his step it was most of the way to a decision her body had already made" — Desire as diffuse, ambient interoception preceding conscious intent, not visual trigger.

### POV notes

The mirror passage — "the neckline crossed and dipped and set her chest out frank in the gold light," "gave her whole leg to the room, calf to the jut of her hip" — is external body description, but I discount it entirely: she is looking at herself in a newly hung mirror, so an outside-view register is exactly what the scene requires, and even there the description is interleaved with interoception (breath, heat, blush). Likewise "she looked like a lot of woman who knew exactly how much she was" is her own reclaimed self-appraisal, not an authorial camera. The sex itself is elided almost completely ("after that there was nothing left for either of them to decide"), so no choreography tells exist in either direction. The one candidate male tell I kept (the maker-fantasy structure) I kept at reduced weight precisely because a female author writing a fantasy of being seen and fitted could produce the identical page.

### Rationale

The chapter's signals cluster heavily on one side and, crucially, they are independent of each other: fabric mechanics (the hip-catch and shimmy), garment-fit phenomenology (the "give she'd worn her whole life without once naming it"), the underwear-line problem and the gait-management reflex, the shoe register ("read like an apology"), bra logistics, and two separate passages where shame arrives involuntarily and is metabolized into appetite on the page. These are the rubric's +50 markers nearly point for point, and most would survive even a skilled ventriloquist test poorly — the unnamed lifelong "give" of ready-to-wear and the keep-your-steps-small instinct are the kind of details that come from wearing clothes, not watching them. What holds the score short of the +40s is the premise's faint maker's-fantasy shape and the closing "what she was for," which introduce a real, if weak, possibility that this is a very good male author writing his ideal reader of his own gift; and the general caution that a single elided-sex chapter offers no anatomical stress test. Net: strong, multi-tell female-authored signal, moderated for the structural counter-read.
