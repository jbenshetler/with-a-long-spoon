### POV notes

The tight third-person female POV means every one of the above *could* in principle be a man rendering a woman faithfully, and I discounted candidates on that ground where possible. What survives the defense is not any single line but the pattern of omission: a chapter about the morning after a first night contains zero visual inventory — no mirror scene describing what she saw, no description of the dress despite it being the chapter's engine, no rendering of her body as it looks to anyone. The one bodily passage is purely proprioceptive and includes the bra as felt infrastructure. A writer working from outside almost always leaves fingerprints of the external view somewhere; here the external view never once occurs to the narration. I did not count the "love nest" / "lucky slut" banter itself as a tell — that dialogue could be written by anyone — only the interior *landing* of the word.

### Rationale

There are no male-coded tells at all: no inventoried female body, no spectacle, nothing anatomically or socially false. The positive column is dense for so short and chaste a chapter — interoceptive soreness with the bra rendered from inside, shame arriving as social consequence of a friend's joke, two distinct female friendships operating on different channels (Cassie reading beds and faces, Randi reading outfits and occasions) with the protagonist happily triangulated between them, and clothes treated as logistics and love rather than image. Each tell is independent, and the systematic absence of any external gaze in a chapter that constantly gestures at appearance (the dress, the mirror, the shoes) is itself strong evidence. What keeps this short of the +40s is length and the absence of any sexual scene to test the hardest criteria; the tells are strong and consistent but drawn from one narrow morning. That yields a firmly positive score with solid but not maximal evidence strength.
