## Audit

SCORE: +15
EVIDENCE STRENGTH: 5

### Tells toward male-authored

- "He lifts in the morning, first thing, before anything. Says it clears his head. Then he writes for six hours and stops. … 'My writing's no good after that.'" — The absent hero's résumé (powerlifter, near-finished math doctorate, monastic writing routine) is itemized with a specificity the scene doesn't need; it has the flavor of an author's aspirational self-portrait admired through a woman's mouth.
- "I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." — A woman driven wild purely by a man's minimal, flawless consideration is a recognizable male wish-fulfillment structure: her desire functions as a mirror held up to his virtues (dry shirt, standing in the rain, waiting for "the real answer"). The text half-defuses this by having Vee flag it herself ("I can hear it sounding like nothing"), which weakens but doesn't erase the tell.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "what she even owned that said his house without saying trying too hard" — Clothes as semiotics, not spectacle: the calculation of what an outfit *communicates* and the fear of visible effort is lived female dress-logic, and it's tossed off in a subordinate clause rather than explained.
- "That's not a message, that's a ransom note." / "There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat." — The forensic group-decoding of a man's text, escalating to the "bring a change of clothes" verdict, renders the mechanics of female friendship as a working institution with roles and rituals, from inside.
- "Meg, who followed the Pace situation in installments like her shows, pointed the fork at Vee now. 'Okay, but the shirt. You have to tell the shirt.'" — Friends holding serialized custody of each other's romances, with a canon and known skipped middles, is intimate friendship-machinery, not observed-from-outside girl talk.
- "Twelve." She hadn't even had to count — Desire measured in involuntarily-tracked days, and the shame of being caught tracking, is appetite rendered from inside rather than displayed.
- "Her heart was doing something quick and light and stupid, and she let it." / "the fry in her hand was just a thing in her hand now" / "Vee felt the smile arrive before she'd cleared it." — Consistent interoception-first rendering: the body reported as sensation and involuntary event before any external view.
- "She'd had the shirt, folded on her pillow at this exact hour." — A kept garment as private talisman, placed rather than mentioned; the erotics run through object and anticipation, with zero visual inventory of anyone's body.

### POV notes

The women's physical descriptions ("round-faced and blonde, a head of curls," "dark-haired and level") are characterful and unsexualized, so they yield nothing either way. The heavy orbiting of the conversation around men could look like a male author's idea of what women discuss, but it's the scene's declared subject and is executed with insider texture, so I discount it as premise rather than tell. Conversely, much of the girl-talk itself, however good, could be written by a skilled author of either gender rendering a female POV faithfully — I've only credited lines whose granularity (dress semiotics, day-counting shame, the "installments" custody of the story) goes beyond what faithful POV imitation typically produces.

### Rationale

The female-lean evidence is broader and more independent: interoception consistently precedes external description, desire carries shame and social consequence (being "caught out" at "twelve"), clothes are handled as communication rather than display, and the friendship operates with insider mechanics (serialized retellings, assigned roles, the hair ritual) rather than as a backdrop. Against that stands one real countertell — the offstage paragon whose flawless routine and effortless chivalry reduce the narrator's desire to a tribute, a structure that often signals a male author writing his own reception — partially blunted by the text's self-awareness about it. No line inventories a woman's body from outside, nothing is anatomically or logistically false, and the women's inner lives are the whole substance of the chapter. That nets to a moderate female-authored lean with several corroborating but individually non-decisive tells: +15, strength 5.
