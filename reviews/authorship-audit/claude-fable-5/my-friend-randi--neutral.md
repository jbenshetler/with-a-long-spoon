## Audit

### Tells toward male-authored

- "You've got the goods, gorgeous girl, and Pace is not going to open his own door, see what you brought him, and say no." — The chapter's plan resolves into the woman as a visual delivery for a man's eyes — the naked-under-a-coat doorstep trope — and the wisdom-voice of the scene endorses it without friction.
- "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do" — Framing a woman's peak bravery as wordless sexual self-presentation to a man is a familiar male-fantasy valuation, placed in the mouth of the chapter's authority figure.
- "And wear these. Just the thing for tonight." — Black heels pressed into her hands as the one accessory to nothing-under-a-coat is a pin-up image, styled for how it will look at his door rather than how it will feel walking across January in it.
- "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in. What's a girl to do?" — The sexually omnivorous best friend who has narrated "exactly what a man's mouth had done and where" across a dozen tables edges toward the archetype of male-imagined girl talk.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it" — Shame and appetite braided and rendered from inside, arousal as an internal event with social cost, not a spectacle.
- "Randi's own smell under the day, bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" — Perfume-note literacy (citrus top, green edge, explicitly not sweet) is precise material knowledge of how women smell to each other, not how they look.
- "tissue paper and a good bag, sweaters stacked where no drawer had claimed them" — The lived clutter of unpacking after shopping — "a good bag" as a class marker — is fluent, interior women's material culture.
- "a pale metallic blue, the kind you buy to tell your daughter about someday" — A dress valued for its future as a story told between women, not for how it sits on a body.
- "She held the hug past the end of it, past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that" — Physical intimacy between women measured in duration and pressure, felt rather than seen; neither woman is visually inventoried anywhere in the chapter.
- "there was a word for that girl and it was already in her mouth… The word cost her; she'd made herself say it and it left her flushed to the ears" — The slut-word arriving as an inherited maternal voice, with the cost of saying it tracked in the body, is shame from the inside.

### POV notes

"Blonde. Tan. Built like a model" is Vee's own three-a.m. invention and is immediately dismantled by the text ("fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one") — a self-aware treatment of the inventoried rival, so it counts for neither side. Randi's relentless reframing of the fight around the man's fragility ("You backed him into a corner in his own house") could be a male author's reflex to make the woman apologize for wanting words — but the chapter seeds Randi with her own unreadable stakes (holding the hug too long, the withheld hotel night, looking down at her hands), so her man-centered advice is plausibly characterization of Randi rather than the author's verdict, and I discount it. Likewise "so much sex I was exhausted… my grades were slipping" and "dynamite in bed" are in-character girl-talk register, not narration.

### Rationale

The chapter contains both fantasies at once — the man who heats the house and pours the wine, and the woman who arrives naked under a coat — so the plot machinery roughly cancels. What tips the score is the texture: the camera never inventories a woman's body from outside, while the interior channel runs constantly — the hollow chest, the burn up the neck, shame igniting want "sharper for it," a friend's scent parsed in perfume notes, a hug measured in beats past its natural end, a dress valued as a story for a future daughter. That is the +50 anchor's territory (interoception first, arousal with consequence, shame from inside), rendered fluently. Against it stand the heels-and-coat choreography and "the bravest thing a woman can do," which carry a genuine male-fantasy scent even after granting Randi her characterization defense. The female-leaning tells are more numerous and more embedded in the prose's involuntary texture; the male-leaning tells are concentrated in one character's advice, which the text visibly frames as suspect. A moderate positive score with mid-range confidence.
