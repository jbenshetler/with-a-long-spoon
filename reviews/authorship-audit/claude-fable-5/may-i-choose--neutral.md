### Tells toward female-authored

- "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." — Habitual self-surveillance around food, rendered as an ambient default that only registers when it lapses. This is appetite-and-shame written from inside the machinery, not observed from across the table.
- "that low background readiness she carried everywhere, the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there, and she let it not be there." — Women's ambient vigilance around men rendered as a bodily baseline whose *absence* is the event. An outside-in writer tends to dramatize the threat; this dramatizes the release of a load the reader was never shown being picked up, because the carrier stopped noticing it years ago.
- "the small constant work of keeping up her end" / "the old reflex, the part of her that always caught the thread before it could drop" — Conversational and emotional labor treated as unpaid maintenance work with muscle memory, including its ill-timed reappearance at the kiss. Independent of the vigilance tell and pointing the same way.
- "the dress and then the other dress, Cassie cross-legged on her bunk delivering verdicts, the shoes borrowed off a girl down the hall because nothing of Vee's was right" — Lived mechanics of getting dressed for a date: the sequence (two dresses, a friend as jury, shoes borrowed down the hall) is logistics known from doing it, and it costs her lunch — the detail is load-bearing, not decorative.
- "She teased Vee about everything; she didn't tease her about this." — Female friendship rendered as calibration: the precise knowledge of what a best friend will and won't joke about is an interior map of intimacy, not a bystander's sketch.
- "the damp-dark edge of his hair, his jaw still faintly blushing from the razor, that color that meant *recently*, that meant *for this*" — Desire that reads *effort and intention* off a man's body rather than inventorying the body itself; the eroticized object is the care ("down to the part of him she'd only get close enough to smell"), a characteristically inside-out grammar of female wanting.
- "The other ones always made you answer. Right then, on the phone... So they'd know where they stood." — Pattern-recognition of low-grade male control tactics from the receiving end, stated as accumulated field data rather than discovery.
- "the hollow had been opening in her since midday without her noticing... It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." — Hunger as interoception first, then as metaphor for deferred appetite generally; the chapter's whole erotic argument runs through her gut, not through anything visible.
- "It was too much for what it was. And underneath the too-much, stranger, was the other thing" — A hand-hold carrying disproportionate emotional consequence, with the disproportion itself examined from inside. Arousal here has a social and emotional bill attached ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days... a rule that had never been breached before"), and breaching her own rule is framed as self-permission, not conquest.

### POV notes

Two candidate tells were considered and discounted. First, a skeptic could read the flawlessly competent, frictionless man — "May I choose?", the ordering, the street-side walking, the surrender she finds restful — as male authorial self-idealization. But his body is never inventoried, his interiority is entirely opaque, and every scene's payoff is her internal economy (relief, appetite, the vigilance switching off); the idealization is the shape of *her* desire faithfully rendered, so it moves nothing. Second, her own body is never described from outside — no mirror, no self-inventory of looks — which could simply be POV discipline by a writer of either gender; I weighted it only lightly, folded into the interoception tells above rather than counted separately.

### Rationale

Nearly every paragraph runs on interoception — ribs, hollow, warmth, the heat of a glass through brass — and the chapter's substantive tells are independent of one another: self-monitored eating, ambient vigilance and its release, conversational labor as reflex, dressing logistics with a friend as jury, desire read off a man's grooming as intention rather than anatomy, and appetite/permission as the erotic engine. Each survives the POV defense: a man could write any one of these, but the density, the load-bearing accuracy of the small mechanics (borrowed shoes, the un-teased subject, "so they'd know where they stood"), and the total absence of any outside-in rendering of the woman's body push well past what faithful POV craft alone predicts. Nothing on the page points the other way. That warrants a strong positive score, held short of the extreme because a very disciplined male author writing close-third female POV remains conceivable, and the evidence, while plentiful, is all of one fabric — a single evening, a single consciousness.
