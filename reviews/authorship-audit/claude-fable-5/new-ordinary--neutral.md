## Audit

### Tells toward male-authored

- "Tonight he barely had to work at it. She went up fast and broke fast" — the arc of the woman whose stated independence ("a careful story about needing time to study") is disproved by her helpless, instant responsiveness to the man is a classic male-fantasy structure: her body exists to vindicate his effect on her. It is rendered from inside, which blunts it, but the shape of the fantasy is worth logging.
- "He went into her easily, all of him ... He always had a slowness left in him when she had none" — the male partner is flawless throughout the chapter (patient, huge, silent, a virtuoso cook, never needy, never wrong); this degree of idealized male competence, with the narrative eye lingering lovingly on his craft ("a short practiced snap of his wrist"), can read as authorial self-insert. However, the identical idealization is a staple of female-authored romance, so this cannot carry much weight on its own.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "Her body had kept a running tally her mouth would never once have read out, and the first taste of him called all of it due at once" — desire rendered as interoception and self-concealment first, spectacle never; nothing about how she looks, everything about what her body has been doing to her.
- "the brush and the face soap and the cardboard form of green scrunchies and the loose pins" — the cardboard form scrunchies are sold on is a lived grooming detail; this is the drawer as it is packed and used, not as a man imagines a woman's things.
- "The jeans had been sensible in the car and were the enemy now ... cold from the drive, and tight, and nothing to brace against" — the actual mechanics of getting out of cold tight denim standing up, followed by "one-legged, hopping, the denim inside out at her ankle" — undressing as logistics and indignity rather than reveal. A male-written striptease almost never goes inside-out at the ankle.
- "she had to come out of it herself, turning, unwinding like something off a spool" — the bra removed by the wearer's own real motion, not magicked off; the garment behaves correctly.
- "her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private" — orgasm carrying immediate social/emotional consequence (exposure, shame, the "far too soon" mortification), the +50 pole's signature: appetite and shame fused, from inside.
- "she took him without question now, the old bracing habit long gone" — a quiet reference to a learned history of tensing against penetration, the kind of accumulated bodily knowledge about sex-as-a-woman that is rarely invented from outside.
- "It stopped smelling like you. I wore it every night I wasn't here and it went stale, and then it went to nothing" — the borrowed-shirt scent economy, including the specific decay curve of a worn garment's smell and the humiliation of asking for it back refilled, is intimacy rendered through lived female practice; and her arousal/attachment costs her something ("The foolish hot prick of tears").
- "some unreasonable corner of her had wanted him to fight, to want the nights badly enough to say so out loud" — ambivalence about her own boundary-setting, grace received as a kind of debt; relational interiority given equal weight with the sex.

### POV notes

- "his hands came up and took her breasts through the satin, both at once" and "found the proof of her right through it" — his actions are foregrounded here, but the register stays hers ("her head went back onto his shoulder and her eyes shut and she gave it a moment she had never decided to give it"): the touch is reported as felt and consented-to in real time, not displayed. Faithful POV, not a gaze tell; discounted.
- The extended cooking sequence reads like authorial hobby knowledge, but it is filtered through her watching and her tasting ("This came apart on her tongue into all its separate pieces and let her count them"), so it tells us nothing gendered; discounted.
- Her body is never once described visually — no mirror, no inventory, no adjectives on her figure even mid-undressing. That absence is itself consistent with female authorship but I have weighted it under the positive tells rather than double-counted it here.

### Rationale

The chapter's evidence runs strongly one direction: interoception precedes and outranks the visual at every beat, clothes obey real physics and cost real dignity, orgasm arrives freighted with shame and self-exposure rather than staged as spectacle, and small artifacts (the scrunchie card, the shirt's fading smell, the old bracing habit) carry the kind of lived specificity that is hard to counterfeit from outside. Against this stands one genuine structural counter-signal — the frictionless, idealized man and the woman whose asserted autonomy her body promptly refutes, a shape that can serve male wish-fulfillment — but that same shape is endemic to romance written by women about women's fantasies, and every sentence executing it is rendered from her interior, so it cannot outweigh eight independent inside-the-body tells. I discount somewhat because a very skilled male author could study his way to most of these effects, which keeps me off the extreme; the balance lands solidly but not unmistakably female-authored.
