### Tells toward male-authored

- "Did he tell you he taught the class? He's the reason half of us can dance." / "All us girls love him." / "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." — Every female character in the chapter exists to testify to the male lead's desirability and virtue. This is world-construction, not Vee's perception, so it survives the POV defense: the author has built a story in which the man is universally beloved, morally spotless, and vindicated by an impartial witness, while the woman's function is to doubt him and be proven wrong. That deep structure reads like male wish-fulfillment.
- "Doesn't take advantage — I'd have heard otherwise. Not that plenty haven't tried, hoping he would." — Women queuing up hoping to be taken advantage of by the honorable man is a classic male-fantasy note, delivered by a third party rather than filtered through Vee's jealousy, so it can't be discounted as POV distortion.
- "The basic step is back on the right, back on the right, then back on the left... The *and* is just a change of weight" / "Give me something to push back against... Being jelly doesn't work." — The leader's pedagogy is rendered verbatim, in complete instructional detail, with the fluency of someone who has taught from the lead's side; the follow's learning is comparatively impressionistic. Weak lean, but it suggests the author's embodied dance knowledge is the man's, not the woman's.
- "He made them feel feminine, as he'd made her feel. He made them feel safe, and like real people." — The itemized catalog of what the man bestows on women ("feel feminine," "like real people") reads more like a man's theory of what women want from men than a woman's self-report.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "Dana on Third has ropers. She's your size, close enough — dance girls trade, everybody's danced in everybody's boots." — Casual, lived mechanics of women's communal clothing logistics, offered without explanation; this is the texture of experience, not research.
- "a keyhole at the neckline that on Meg would have been a suggestion... her hand started up and she put it back down. The keyhole had stopped suggesting." — The same garment reading differently on two women's bodies, rendered through the wearer's reflexive flinch rather than through what the neckline displays. Interior, not spectacle.
- "up close the makeup was doing a lot of work, the kind that held up in the dark and would be too much by daylight, the smile fully up and something under it that wasn't" — Precise female-evaluative literacy about makeup's conditions of failure, plus reading another woman's smile as performance.
- "that particular awareness of other women tracking a couple on the floor" — Names women-watching-women as a distinct, familiar register of attention; a small, confident piece of insider knowledge.
- "She'd slept in it and not washed it, rationing the smell of him, until what was left of him went stale and wrong... It came back smelling of fabric softener and no one." — Attachment routed through scent and laundry, ending on "no one"; interoceptive, consequence-laden intimacy rather than visual desire.
- "She waited under the one good light by the door, where the dark didn't quite reach, her arms crossed" — Unremarked female safety-calculus in a parking lot at closing time, presented as habit rather than plot.
- "there'd be questions after, tonight or in the morning, and Vee would have to put words to what she'd done, and she couldn't. She called a cab." — The cost-accounting of female friendship (help arrives instantly, but with emotional invoicing) rendered from inside.

### POV notes

Several apparent male tells were discounted as faithful POV: "bare midriffs, shirts cut down to there... bodies that knew they were being looked at and moved like they liked it. Floozies." is Vee's jealousy doing the inventorying, not an authorial camera — the passage is explicitly framed as self-harm ("She knew what she was doing, and she did it anyway"). Likewise "she'd spread her legs like a slut" is internalized shame in a wounded woman's own idiom; women write and think this register, so it moves nothing. The submission language of the lead ("Don't think. Just feel the lead") is the actual vocabulary of partner dancing and is rendered from the follow's kinesthetic side ("The pattern came through his body before it arrived in her feet"), so it doesn't count against either direction.

### Rationale

The surface texture is heavily female-coded: boot-trading, the keyhole neckline read on two different bodies, makeup that fails by daylight, the good light by the door, the rationed shirt-smell — many independent, unshowy mechanics of the kind that usually come from living them, with interoception consistently preceding image. But the load-bearing structure pushes the other way, and structure is authorial where texture can be assembled: a man who taught half the room to dance, whom "all us girls love," whom women try to tempt and fail, whose accuser is a jealous woman corrected by a chorus of other women until she resolves to apologize — that arc, plus the leader's-side fluency of the dance pedagogy, reads like a male author's fantasy wearing an unusually well-researched female POV. The two bodies of evidence genuinely pull against each other; the structural tells are fewer but harder to fake by diligence than textural ones, so I land modestly on the male side rather than at zero, with mid-strength evidence reflecting real signal in both directions.
