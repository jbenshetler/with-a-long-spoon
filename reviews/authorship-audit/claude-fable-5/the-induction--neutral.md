## Audit

SCORE: 12
EVIDENCE STRENGTH: 7

### Tells toward male-authored

- "he had made the dress for exactly this, her name read out in a good room, and he was going to miss the wearing it was built for" — the chapter's architecture is a recognizable exhibitionist fantasy with an absent male maker: the woman's body as the display surface for a man's craftsmanship, the photo sent back to him afterward. This is structural, not POV-level, and it's a scaffold far more common in male-authored erotica.
- "she gave the room her leg on purpose — the whole length of it, calf to hip, nothing under the silk and nothing to guess at" — for one extended beat the body is rendered as it looks to the room rather than as it feels; the camera briefly leaves her skin and joins the audience.
- "She had good legs." — flat external self-appraisal in free indirect voice; a small note of the woman grading her own parts as inventory.
- "'How did I look?' It was the only thing in her head." — the protagonist's post-triumph interiority collapses entirely into her appearance, at the one moment (an academic honor) where a competing inner life would be cheapest to show.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "the short managed steps that kept the slit lying shut, and now a hand kept low at her hip while she sat, holding the seam closed over her thigh" — precise lived mechanics of managing a difficult garment from inside it, the ongoing physical negotiation rather than the visual result.
- "Vee had stood there and resettled forward onto the balls of her feet, because the dress wanted the height" — the proprioception of four-inch heels rendered exactly: weight shifted forward, felt not seen.
- "It felt different now, three rows from a microphone, in a room full of somebody's parents." — shame and appetite from inside; the private joke curdling into social exposure is the arousal carrying consequence, not spectacle.
- "the heat came up — her body agreeing, low and certain, as it had in the glass that afternoon, coming up to meet a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it" — interoception first, and arousal framed as the body ratifying a decision rather than as a picture.
- "took Vee's phone out of her hand to hold, and looked her up and down and said, 'You look—' and didn't finish it, which from Cassie was a standing ovation" — female friendship rendered in its working grammar: phone-holding, withheld praise decoded fluently, affection by understatement ("her eyes had gone bright at the corners... which she'd deny to the grave").
- "The black dress from the back of her closet, the one that had always been good enough, was still on its hanger at home" — wardrobe as biography; the history of a "good enough" dress is closet knowledge from the inside.
- "did not once make herself small" / "mincing it modest" — the vocabulary of women's socialized self-diminishment deployed as the chapter's actual stakes.
- "Vee took the phone and knew to the heartbeat what second it was. None of that was in the picture. Or all of it was, and only she would ever see it there." — the gap between how she looked and what she felt is the point; inner life explicitly outweighs the image of her.

### POV notes

The commando premise, the deliberate long step, and "the eyes found her leg" were all candidates for male tells and were largely discounted: Vee is characterized throughout as someone staging this for herself, and the display is bracketed by dread before ("careful the whole way here") and ambivalence after ("whether that was pride or only the same heat still running"), which is faithful rendering of an exhibitionist character, not authorial gaze. "She had good legs" and "How did I look?" also partially survive as voice — women do think both — so I weighted them lightly. The Pace-as-maker frame, however, sits above POV: the author chose a structure in which the heroine's body and its unveiling are a man's artifact, and that choice can't be laundered as characterization.

### Rationale

The sentence-level evidence runs consistently female: the chapter's texture is interoceptive (heat, heartbeat, weight on the balls of the feet), its garment logistics are accurate and experienced rather than observed (the seam held shut at the hip, the ankle strap done by a kneeling friend, the retired good-enough dress), its female friendship has real working parts, and its erotic charge is priced in shame, social risk, and private meaning rather than offered as spectacle. Pulling against that is the fantasy's architecture — a woman bared by design of an absent man, aroused by a room's gaze, the trophy photo sent to him — plus a few beats where the prose steps outside her body to admire it and an interiority that narrows to "How did I look?" at its peak. Those structural tells are real but fewer, and the closing line's refusal to separate pride from heat is the kind of ambivalence the display-fantasy version rarely bothers with. Net: moderately female-leaning execution over a male-leaning scaffold — a positive score well short of confident, on substantial evidence.
