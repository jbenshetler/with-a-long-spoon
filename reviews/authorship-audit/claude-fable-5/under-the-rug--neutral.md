### POV notes

Several candidate male-tells were discounted as faithful POV. "What a man could do to a woman when he was this good at it" and "her body answering his with no say from her in it" center male skill and female passivity, but they are Vee's own anxious thesis — the chapter exists to interrogate exactly that framing, so it's characterization, not authorial gaze. Likewise "She belonged to him... learned to wait for him and open for him" is a surrender fantasy narrated by the character who is troubled by it, and its ownership is immediately turned into her central question ("But did he belong to her?"), which a purely male-gaze rendering would not bother to ask. The step-by-step catalogue of his technique reads at first like choreography, but every step lands as sensation in her body, so it survives as her experience of being attended to. The two "stood up for him" / "open and wet" slips above were retained because they are external even to *her* likely sensation, and so are the only lines that read the same whichever body is narrating.

### Rationale

The chapter's erotic material is dense, which is where male-authorship tells usually surface, and almost none surface: the female body is rendered nearly throughout as it feels rather than as it looks, arousal is inseparable from its emotional consequence, and the aftermath — cooling sweat tightening on skin, semen felt as warmth and as claim, the sleepless accounting beside a sleeping man — carries lived detail of a kind that external observation rarely supplies. Against this stand only two brief patches of external grammar ("stood up for him," "open and wet") and a notably sympathetic interior model of the male character, none of which approaches the inventory-and-spectacle pattern of the −50 pole, and both patches are partially absorbed by the possession theme the character herself is narrating. The counter-tells are enough to keep the score well short of the extreme, and the persistent possibility of a highly skilled male author writing close female POV caps confidence, but the accumulation of independent interoceptive and social tells — meanness owned, grievance self-consciously fabricated, the mother-test of a relationship's name — points clearly toward female authorship. Evidence strength is 7: many independent tells, most strong, a few contested.
