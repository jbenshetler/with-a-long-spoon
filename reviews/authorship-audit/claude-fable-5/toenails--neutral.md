### Tells toward male-authored

- "*Trade*, she'd said, and folded them in thirds and set them in his palm" — the surrendered-underwear gesture is a familiar male-fantasy beat; but here it is rendered from her side, with logistical and social consequence (driving home bare, the zipper crisis), which mostly defuses it. A weak tell at best.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "Was he worth painting your toenails?" / "In October." — reading freshly painted toenails in closed-shoe season as a tell of anticipated intimacy is a piece of women's grooming semiotics rarely rendered this casually and correctly; it's the roommate's fluency, not just the narrator's, that signals lived knowledge.
- "her hair its own wild country, no brush in the house that would go through it, her face bare because there had been nothing to do it with" — the no-overnight-bag problem itemized by its real logistics (the *right* brush, nothing to do her face with), grooming as machinery rather than image.
- "the thing her body had done before she could decide to let it" — orgasm as interior event with agency slippage, felt rather than seen; zero visual spectacle anywhere in the chapter.
- "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness." — the calibrated disclosure ritual of close female friendship: what you hand your friend ("the old easy words she'd always handed Cassie about a man") versus what you keep, and the self-forgiveness that manages the keeping.
- "Cassie listened with her chin on her knees and did not pull at the seam." — the friend registering the omission and honoring it; female friendship rendered as mutual reading and tact, both inner lives fully live.
- "three feet from Cassie, the zipper half down, and the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." — clothes carrying social consequence: undressing in front of a friend as an act of disclosure, the body itself threatening to out the edited story.
- "frosted-plum toenails her mother would have had a word for. The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." — inherited maternal judgment on female appetite, held and set down from inside; shame as lineage, not decoration.
- "The heat climbed Vee's neck." — interoception-first embarrassment, consistent with the chapter's whole sensorium (cold room, cereal smell, the night "running under" everything as a second channel).

### POV notes

Interoception alone was discounted as insufficient — a skilled male writer rendering a female POV faithfully could give us heat on the neck and the body's second channel. What survives the "would this read the same from a woman?" defense is the surrounding social machinery, which exceeds POV duty: the October-toenails code legible instantly to *another* character, the ladle-precise calibration of what a woman tells her best friend versus what she keeps, and the insight that undressing in front of that friend would un-edit the story. The underwear "trade" was flagged above as the one candidate male tell, and largely discounted because it is narrated through its consequences to her rather than as spectacle for him.

### Rationale

The chapter contains no external inventory of any female body — Vee is never described as she looks, only as she feels, and Cassie exists as a reader of signs rather than a shape on a bunk. Against that absence stands a dense cluster of independent tells: grooming logistics rendered as problem-solving, a women's dress-code semiotics both characters speak fluently, arousal that carries social consequence into the very next scene (the zipper she cannot lower), disclosure between women treated as a craft with rules, and maternal judgment on appetite metabolized from inside. Each of these could individually be a gifted male writer's research; together, and with only one weak counter-tell (the folded underwear, itself narrated through consequence), the weight sits firmly on the female-authored side without reaching the unmistakable extreme.
