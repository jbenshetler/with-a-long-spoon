### POV notes

The photo-cropping, the collarbone touch, and the father "pricing" the dress are all things a disciplined male author could supply as faithful close-third rendering of a young woman — each individually fails the "would it read the same if a woman wrote it" test as a decisive tell. I weight them only in aggregate: their density, their unglossed casualness ("You know how she does"), and the total absence of any physical description of either woman (no mirror moment, no inventory of the dress on the body despite the dress being the subject) go beyond POV duty into habit. The idealized Pace was likewise discounted partially: he is filtered through what a father would approve, so the résumé is partly Vee's rhetorical construction, not raw authorial fantasy.

### Rationale

Almost everything here points one direction, but softly. The chapter is built on mechanics the rubric names as female-authored signals — managing the family gaze on one's own body (the cropped photo, the collarbone touch), a female friendship conducted through precise restraint, and interiority that ends on an unresolved, contradictory feeling — while conspicuously declining every opportunity to describe either woman from outside, even with a dress at the story's center. Against that sits one soft counter-tell: the frictionlessly accomplished boyfriend, which faintly resembles a male author's proxy but is partly explained by Vee's curated telling. Because every pro-female tell is individually survivable as skilled POV craft and only persuasive in aggregate, I place the score at a moderate +15 rather than higher, with mid-range evidence strength: several independent soft tells, no strong anatomical or interoceptive clincher.
