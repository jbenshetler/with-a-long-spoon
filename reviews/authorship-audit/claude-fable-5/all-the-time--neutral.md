### Tells toward male-authored

- "you think a guy who wants it that much would be, like, quick… He takes *forever*… he's *strong*, he can hold me anywhere he wants me, and then a minute later he's so gentle I could cry" — the male lover is a frictionless checklist of what men imagine women praise: stamina, "two, three" times nightly, strength-plus-tenderness, no flaw, no cost. The construction of the fantasy object (not the POV rendering it) has the shape of authorial wish-fulfillment.
- "The more we do it the more I *want* it… I've got sex brain." — escalating female insatiability produced by a superhuman lover is a staple of male fantasy architecture; the author *chose* this arc, POV doesn't dictate it.
- "Okay, *Miss November*" — the painted mouth forming each word slowly" — the scenario stack here (woman hands her uncropped nude to a beautiful friend, girl-girl mouth-kiss goodbye, "…Does he have a brother?") is a male-fantasy tableau at the plot level, and "the painted mouth" flickers toward gaze even filtered through Vee.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "The smell had her before the booth did: coffee and warm bread and something roasting far back in the kitchen, a smell that reached the stomach before the eyes." — interoception first, consistently: the body registers before the eye does, throughout the chapter.
- "Vee read the right-hand column first — the prices, always the prices first… the safe cheap thing you ordered so you could afford the chair" — the lived economics of being the poorer woman at the table, and the "illicit lift" of ordering the good thing, is appetite-and-shame rendered from deep inside, exactly the interior class/hunger texture rarely faked.
- "You're just finally hungry out loud… You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*, and now you're not, and you've gone and decided that's the flaw." — female friendship doing its real work: absolving a woman's appetite of shame. The shame itself ("Am I turning into some kind of — sex pervert?") is voiced from inside, not staged for display.
- "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around." — a precise articulation of the felt difference between being desired as a self versus as a body; this is the inside of the experience, not the view of it.
- "her thumb over it, working up to it, the thrill and the shame running together — before she turned the phone around" — the nude-photo beat is rendered as risk, pride, and shame with social stakes ("Randi… would file her as a girl who flinched"), not as spectacle; the photo itself gets three spare details, no inventory.
- "Randi's eyes had gone bright and a little unfixed… a catch in her breath she didn't quite smooth over" / "'Just us,' Randi said… a small roughness under it, there and gone" — Randi's desire carries emotional consequence and cost, read through micro-signals Vee half-misses; arousal here is social weather, not visual display.
- "her body gave one small rock in the seat, forward and back, that she caught only after it had happened" / "her foot going under the table" — the body betraying feeling before the mind votes, a persistent inside-out habit of the prose.
- "cream coat and a top the deep red-purple of mulberries" / "So *that's* what you've been hiding under all those loose clothes." — precise, casual fluency with women's clothes as social signaling (dressing down, hiding a body).

### POV notes

Vee's rapturous, flawless rendering of the lover is largely defensible as characterization — she is besotted and narrating to impress a friend, so the gush itself was discounted; only the *authorial choice* of a costless superhuman lover plus escalating insatiability was counted, at reduced weight. Vee's attentiveness to Randi's mouth, hair, and "bitter orange" scent was fully discounted: her half-conscious attraction to Randi is the chapter's engine, so that gaze is characterization, not authorship. The sex monologue's near-clichés ("sheets in both hands") were discounted as the voice of a woman who has "only read about" such things reaching for available language.

### Rationale

The prose mechanics run female with real consistency: sensation arrives through the stomach, skin, and involuntary body before the eye; money-shame, appetite-shame, and the absolution of both by a female friend are rendered from inside with lived specificity; the erotic charge between the women is carried as emotional consequence (Randi's catch of breath, the roughness under "Just us") rather than spectacle; and even the nude-photo scene — the chapter's riskiest set piece — is written as interior negotiation of pride and flinching, not as display. Against this stands the scenario's architecture, which no POV defense covers: a flawless, tireless, strong-then-gentle lover who cooks and curates and awakens a woman into insatiability, capped by shared nudes and a lingering kiss between two beautiful women — a stack of beats with an unmistakable male-fantasy silhouette. A male author skilled enough to sustain this level of interoceptive texture is possible; a female author writing an eroticized fantasy (especially one seeding Randi's unrequited longing, which is the chapter's most emotionally serious thread) is at least as consistent with the page. The sentence-level evidence outweighs the scaffolding-level evidence, but the scaffolding is loud enough to hold the score to a moderate positive rather than a strong one.
