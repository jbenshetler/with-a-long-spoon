### POV notes

The sheer-bra inventory is the only externalized description of her body in the chapter, and the text explicitly motivates it: "She looked down anyway, and his eyes went with hers. She saw what he saw." She chooses to borrow his gaze as part of the act of offering herself — a self-aware maneuver, not default authorial camera placement. Everywhere else the camera sits inside her skin, so I discount this heavily. Pace's near-flawlessness could be male wish-fulfillment or a female-drawn ideal love object; the text gives no way to decide, so I let it move nothing. "The way a man's eyes come back when a woman has called them" reads as knowledge from the calling side, but I weight it lightly as it could be observation from either side.

### Rationale

The chapter's default mode is interoception — heat, cold, hunger, blister, shiver, the wet bra as sensation — with external appearance appearing only once, and there under explicit diegetic license ("she saw what he saw"). The strongest independent tells are the ones hardest to fake from outside: shame about the sock, the mother's voice fused to arousal so that the shaming *is* the heat, hair as a managed nuisance, borrowed boots as mechanics, a coveted dress carrying class rather than cleavage. Arousal here has social consequence at every step — the voice, the mother, the "three dates" arithmetic — which is the far positive pole of the scale. The lone male-leaning candidates are either framed away by the text itself or ambiguous between wish-fulfillment and romance-object idealization, which the rules bar me from resolving by base rates. Many strong, mutually independent tells point one way with only weak, explainable counters, so I score well into positive territory but short of the maximum, since a skilled male author writing deep female POV could in principle produce most of this; the sock, the "always her hair," and the voice-as-heat fusion are what push it to +38 with high evidence strength.
