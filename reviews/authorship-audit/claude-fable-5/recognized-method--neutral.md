## Audit

SCORE: +40
EVIDENCE STRENGTH: 8

### Tells toward male-authored

- "something went through her that she had no name ready for... Her nipples drew up tight, to aching points" — a woman electrified by a single spank is a staple of male wish-fulfillment, and the visible-through-thin-cotton detail flirts with spectacle. It is a weak tell, though, because the moment is rendered as sensation and exposure rather than image (see rationale).
- "A woman who came to the door wringing her hands handed a man a problem to solve" — a doctrine of female deference that a male author might write with relish. But it is explicitly attributed to Randi as coaching, and the chapter itself watches it fail ("the clean machine had no part for this"), so it functions as characterization, not authorial ideology.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "She worked her flats off and did up the straps by feel... The shoe had a structure built into it that took her foot and held it and steadied her." — heels described by what they do to the foot and the stance, not how they look; this is the lived mechanics of women's clothes, and the scene opens with it.
- "she'd done the knot twice in the dorm mirror before it sat right" — the retry as the unit of grooming labor; a small logistical truth about scarves almost never invented from outside.
- "the heat came up her neck in a sheet" / "the small wrongness of it went through her like a missed stair" — interoception carries the scene; embarrassment and vertigo are physical events before they are thoughts.
- "she had not once thought about what they would do to the inch between them" — the social consequence of a clothing choice arriving as shame; dress choreographed against a status field, not a mirror.
- "a small vanity lifted its head, absurd and unkillable: he'd noticed the scarf. The scarf was working." — vanity coexisting with mortification, tracked honestly from inside; the outfit as a project with a success condition.
- "her body saying two or three things at once that she had not agreed to have it say" — arousal rendered as loss of authorship over one's own signals, immediately followed by covering the exposure ("to put her mouth on his... to cover the thing he'd seen with a thing she was choosing"). Arousal here has social consequence and demands management — the +50 pole exactly.
- "her one bra was folded over the chair where she was saving it for Monday like the last clean thing in the world" — the unglamorous underwear logistics of an unplanned stay, a detail written from having lived it, not imagined it.
- "she smelled of him head to foot, nothing of her own left anywhere on her skin, and she had not minded it. She had gone a little still over it" — ambivalence about self-erasure inside intimacy, held without resolving; interiority given more weight than any description of her body.
- "her hair... had become a thing with its own weather, and she had nothing to put it up with, nothing to pull it back" — the missing hair tie as crisis, plus "she heard how small it was even as she said it and could not stop saying it" — shame about the shame, nested and accurate.
- "to pack would have been to admit she'd come hoping for this, and the hope was too big and too new to say even to herself" — appetite managed through plausible deniability to oneself; desire rendered as negotiation with shame rather than as spectacle.
- "Randi had told her to go in sure. Not sorry, Randi said; sure." — female friendship as a working infrastructure of loaned clothes and tactical coaching, present and load-bearing rather than decorative.

### POV notes

The strongest male-candidate material — the spank-to-arousal beat and the deferential apology doctrine — sits inside a female POV, so the question is whether the *rendering* betrays an outside hand. It doesn't. The spank's aftermath is felt ("nearly pain," "a bright hot line"), instantly complicated by exposure and a scramble for control, and the woman's response is to *reclaim agency* by kissing him — the opposite of the passive receptivity male-fantasy versions supply. The deference doctrine belongs to Randi and collapses on contact with the actual conversation. The idealized male love interest ("Beauty like yours made for the gods and loaned out") is a candidate tell in the *female* direction — he is filtered entirely through her response to him, his body barely described — but I have weighted it lightly since a male author writing a romance-coded POV could construct him.

### Rationale

The chapter is saturated with independent, mutually reinforcing tells on the female side and offers nothing on the male side that survives the POV defense. The body is consistently rendered as it feels rather than as it looks — heels as structure underfoot, embarrassment as heat in a sheet, arousal as a thing the body says without permission — and every physical event immediately acquires social and emotional cost that must be managed. The logistics are flawless and unglamorous in a way that reads lived rather than researched: the twice-tied knot, the single bra saved for Monday, the hair with no tie in the house, the unpacked bag as a hedge against her own hope. Even the material most exploitable as male fantasy (the spank, the deference coaching) is handled with an interiority and a shame-grammar that a male author faking this POV almost never sustains across a full chapter. I stop short of the maximum because the idealized dominant man and the arousal-from-discipline beat keep a narrow lane open for a skilled male romance writer, but the accumulated weight of small, unfakeable domestic and bodily truths puts this firmly in female-authored territory: +40, with strong evidence (8) from many independent tells.
