### Tells toward female-authored

- "Her stomach went before she did, the drop of a stair missed in the dark." — Desire and anticipation registered as interoception first, before thought or visual; the body is rendered as it feels, not as it looks.
- "It was easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting." — Appetite framed through its shame-management strategy, from inside; wanting is a thing to be endured socially, not a spectacle.

- "She bit her lower lip, knowing the cup would drip, and then found she didn't care." — The overloaded cup becomes a small liberation from monitored appetite; the emotional consequence of eating too much is the scene's real subject.
- "It's the rule. You wait a day. You don't answer the first one." / "The rule's for boys you're not sure about." — The texting-rules negotiation among women, including the meta-knowledge of when the rule applies, reads like transcribed rather than imagined female-friendship mechanics.

- "She wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on." — Attention received is processed as emotional consequence and mild disbelief; the romance is measured entirely in how it feels to be listened to.
- No woman in the chapter is physically described at all — three female characters with distinct voices and zero external inventory, while their inner lives carry every scene.

### POV notes

Several candidates required the POV defense. The interoceptive opening and the appetite/shame material could in principle be a skilled male author writing close-third female POV faithfully; each line individually survives as characterization. What tips them toward authorial tells is their density and their unremarked-upon quality — the mother's food-policing voice and the froyo-cup liberation are never explained or flagged as significant, which is the signature of lived rather than researched detail. Pace's flavor "system" monologue is characterization of the male love interest, not an authorial tell in either direction, and was discounted. Randi's "naked" line was weighed carefully: a male author might write female friends talking about sex, but the register here — teasing, unerotic, instantly deflated by "It's frozen yogurt" — is the social use of desire, which pushed it to the female column rather than neutral.

### Rationale

Everything in this chapter points one way, and nothing points the other. The prose leads with interoception ("Her stomach went before she did"), treats appetite as a site of shame and small rebellion (the mother's voice, the leaning cup), renders female friendship as a functioning machine with rules and roles, and gives its women thick inner lives with no external description whatsoever — the +50 profile almost point for point. The restraint is on the strength score, not the direction: each individual tell could be produced by a very good male author writing this POV attentively, and the chapter is short, chaste, and low-stakes, so the hardest discriminators (arousal, bodies, clothes under pressure) never get tested. But the tells are multiple, independent, and — critically — unadvertised; the food-shame material in particular is dropped in with the offhandedness of experience rather than the emphasis of research. That earns a firm but not extreme +30 on moderate evidence.
