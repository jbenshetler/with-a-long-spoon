> It was the same thing he'd have said, if he were the kind to say it. Sheri was the kind. She remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather, and she'd been doing it across this handlebar for two years.

**DIAGNOSIS** — The audit is right about where the problem sits and it isn't in Sheri's mouth. "It was the same thing he'd have said" makes the narration countersign the grade: the book itself agrees that "backside" is the salient fact about Vee, one scene after Vee was given hands, curiosity, and a math book. Sheri's line is earned voice; the ratification is the male-authored move — the prose can't resist confirming the appraisal is correct. A woman writing this POV keeps him a looker but lets the appraisal land on him instead of through him.

**ALTERNATIVES** (each replaces the narrator paragraph; Sheri's dialogue stands as written)

*A —*
> Sheri remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather, and she'd been doing it across this handlebar for two years. He'd never minded it. He minded it a little now, and didn't look at why.

*B —*
> That was Sheri; she remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather, had done for the two years she'd ridden at his elbow. Usually he let it pass without noticing he'd let it pass. This one snagged somewhere on the way by.

*C —* (keeps the "he'd have said it" logic but turns it so the point is what the appraisal misses)

**CUT OPTION** — Deletion works cleanly here and may beat all three rewrites. Cut the first two sentences and let the weather sentence carry the whole load:

The characterization survives intact; only the co-sign goes.

## 2. Passage 2 — scenes/hills-and-valleys.md:27

> the whole small engine of her standing on the pedals and driving the grade, the bleached length of her ponytail swinging across her back with each pump of her legs

**DIAGNOSIS** — I'd contest half this flag. "The whole small engine of her" is the POV's craftsman idiom, it's about output not surface, and it's framed by "never as much of her as the work she got out of it" — that's admiration of competence, which is exactly how women write men watching women they respect. What convicts is the tail: "swinging across her back with each pump of her legs" stacks hair-from-behind onto body-motion in a single breath, and "pump of her legs" is porn cadence in a sentence that didn't need a body at all.

Everything the audit liked stays; the pump goes.
