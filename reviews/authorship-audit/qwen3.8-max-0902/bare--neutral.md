### POV notes

The social wardrobe awareness (the first tell) could be argued as faithful characterization of a young woman managing her image for a roommate. However, the *mode* of rendering — the logistical specificity, the silent social negotiation, the absence of vanity or visual self-regard — reflects authorial attention rather than character performance. A male author rendering this moment would more likely externalize it (what Cassie might think) rather than internalize it as a series of spatial and temporal calculations. The interoceptive focus throughout is likewise beyond what POV alone explains; it reflects a sustained authorial priority on felt experience over seen experience.

### Rationale

Every signal in this chapter points in the same direction: the body is known through temperature and texture, never through visual inventory; longing is rendered as a structural problem (the gap between need and available expression) rather than as desire for a person; clothing is handled with logistical and social precision; emotional consequence is carried through physical objects and sensations. There is no counter-evidence — no moment of visual spectacle, no external inventorying, no thinning of interior life. The signals are independent of each other (wardrobe logic, phone scene, shirt scene, cold-air ending) and mutually reinforcing. The restrained register prevents a higher score, as exceptional craft could theoretically produce some of these effects from any author, but the consistency and density across multiple distinct modes of attention make a strong cumulative case.
