### Tells toward male-authored

- "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it." — The confident external authority over the legibility of female orgasm, rendered as settled male expertise. However, this is deeply embedded in the male POV's self-concept and could be a woman rendering a man's belief about himself.

### Tells toward female-authored

- "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner." — The word "unsurvivable" carries the full social weight of female queerness under heteronormative constraint; this is not a word chosen from outside that stakes. The analytical precision about how women reassign ownership of their own desire to make it socially legible feels like knowledge from inside.

- "'I'm hungry,' Randi said. 'Do you want a cheeseburger?' 'No.' Easy, automatic, not even a glance back at the tray. 'I can't eat that.'" — The automaticity of food restriction, rendered without drama or commentary, as simply the ambient condition of being a woman in a body. The "not even a glance back" captures the practiced invisibility of the labor. This is a lived detail, not an observed one.

- "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — A profound observation about the female condition of perpetual visibility and the rare relief of forgetting it. This names something women know from inside.

- "She had decided that morning how much of herself the day would get, and it would get exactly that, and not a strand more." — The daily labor of calibrated self-presentation, named with precision. The scrunchie as "the one flourish she'd allowed herself" extends this: femininity as a budgeted resource, not a natural state.

- "she gathered herself and reached for the warm lower register, trailed one finger down his chest, tilted into him and pressed a slow kiss to his jaw. It was good. It was practiced and it was warm and it was not cartoonish" — The naming of seduction as *work*, as a skill deployed strategically, with the authorial voice acknowledging its quality ("it was not cartoonish") in a way that implies intimate familiarity with the effort behind it.

- "her feet did a thing on the brick, a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do, there and gone" — The involuntary body as truth-teller, noticed with tenderness. The prose treats this micro-expression as more real than anything Randi says, which is an interoceptive priority.

- "She had wanted it helplessly, ahead of herself" — "Ahead of herself" precisely names the temporal gap between female desire and social permission to own it. The phrasing implies that the self who wants and the self who is allowed to want are not the same entity, and that this is a structural condition, not a personal failing.

### POV notes

The visual inventory of women's bodies (the redhead's "full breasts, a deep waist, hips with real width," the sorority girls' "high-waisted, cropped" outfits, the pre-law girl's "camel coat") initially reads as male-gaze objectification. However, these descriptions are explicitly framed as Pace's observational task — he is literally scanning women for Randi to evaluate. The chapter's premise *requires* external visual assessment. The descriptions are also notably non-sexual in register; they assess social type and body language more than erotic appeal. The POV defense holds for nearly all visual description.

The one exception is the opening sex scene, where Pace's strategic thinking ("He had decided this beforehand") and his reading of her body ("all that grip and flutter") could signal male authorship. But even here, the prose's interest is in the emotional and social dynamics rather than in physical spectacle, and the scene's real subject is the question he asks and what it does to her — which is interiority, not surface.

### Rationale

The chapter's deepest intelligence is about the social mechanics of female desire under constraint: how wanting arrives before permission, how women reassign ownership of their appetites to make them survivable, how performance covers truth, and how the body leaks what the mouth manages. These insights are rendered through a male POV, but the *precision* and *interiority* of the observations — the cheeseburger, the word "unsurvivable," the labor of the morning's self-presentation, the observation about forgetting one can be seen — feel like knowledge from inside the female condition rather than sympathetic observation from outside. The male POV functions almost as a structural device that allows the author to render female shame and its management from a slight remove, but the authorial intelligence selecting *what* to notice and *how* to name it signals female authorship with moderate confidence. The counter-signals are weak and mostly absorbed by POV defense.
