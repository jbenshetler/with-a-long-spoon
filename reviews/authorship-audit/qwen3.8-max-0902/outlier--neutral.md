### POV notes

The POV is Vee, a woman, so the attunement to female friendship could in principle be faithful characterization rather than authorial signal. However, the *specificity* of the protective mechanics — the strategic busywork, the refusal to name the thing, the ethic of not making someone carry shame in front of others — goes beyond what POV alone explains. These are not just things Vee notices; they are rendered with the authority of someone who has practiced this ethic, not merely observed it. A male author writing a female POV could produce attunement; the precise *tactics* of protection (giving hands something to hold, asking answerable questions) feel sourced from experience rather than imagination.

### Rationale

The chapter's tells cluster in a single but rich register: the emotional and social mechanics of female friendship, rendered with a specificity that suggests lived experience. There is no inventorying of bodies, no visual spectacle, no thin inner lives beside external description — the women are rendered entirely through their interior states, their relationships to each other, and their management of shame. The prose consistently privileges felt sense over sight, and the climactic emotional moment (Randi's discovery) is tracked not through what she looks like but through what her stillness *does* to the room and to Vee's body. The absence of any interoceptive body material (no clothes, no grooming, no physical sensation beyond emotion) keeps this from reaching the highest scores, but the social-emotional tells are numerous, independent, and strongly directional.
