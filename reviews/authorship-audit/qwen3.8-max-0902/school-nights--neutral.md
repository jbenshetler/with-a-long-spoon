### Tells toward female-authored

- "Her hand went to the spot as she told it, the side of her breast, without her knowing it moved." — Involuntary interoceptive gesture; the body remembering a sensation before the narrator consciously recalls it. This is the body narrating itself from inside, not being described from outside.

- "And Vee tried to say it, and what came was the small word, fine. The ones before him had been fine. Nice enough, a good enough time, and she'd gone along thinking that was the whole of what there was to have." — Emotional calibration of past desire against present, the discovery that previous satisfaction was a settled-for lesser thing. This is appetite rendered as self-knowledge arriving late, not as spectacle.

- "She put both hands up at all of it instead, at the cooking and the watching and the whole of himself turned on her until the rest of the room went dark around her." — The failure of language replaced by a physical gesture, and the feeling of being attended to rendered as a spatial/proprioceptive experience rather than a visual one.

- "And Vee, who hadn't stopped talking since the door, heard herself go small." — Self-overhearing; the interior noticing its own shift in register. Interoception applied to one's own voice.

- "Cassie listened with no face on at all and let Vee talk herself out." — Female friendship rendered through restraint and economy; care expressed as the withholding of reaction, which is a specific social grammar.

- "the house stayed warm now, warm enough that she never once thought about how much she had on" — Comfort defined as the *absence* of self-consciousness about the body, rather than any description of the body itself.

### POV notes

The chapter is in Vee's POV, so interiority is expected. However, the *specific quality* of that interiority — the involuntary hand, the self-overhearing, the way satisfaction is measured against past settling — reads as self-knowledge rather than imaginative construction. The friendship dynamic (Cassie's flat delivery, the dry nickname, the way "I don't get it" is delivered "kindly, though") feels observed from participation rather than assembled from outside. These survive the POV defense because a writer imagining this POV from outside would more likely reach for visual or dramatic markers of intimacy rather than these quiet proprioceptive and social ones.

### Rationale

Every signal in this chapter points the same direction: the prose consistently privileges how things feel over how they look, locates emotion in the body's involuntary responses, treats female friendship as a specific social grammar with its own rules of restraint, and renders desire as a recalibration of inner standards rather than as visual or physical spectacle. No counter-signals exist. The tells are of a similar family (interiority, interoception, social nuance) rather than wildly varied, which keeps evidence strength from reaching the highest tier, but their consistency and specificity are notable.
