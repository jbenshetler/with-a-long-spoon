- "Not the hand in place of the word." — A compressed, precise articulation of the central gendered conflict: physical comfort offered where language and naming are what's needed. The compression itself — the refusal to explain further — signals confidence in the reader's recognition of this dynamic.

### POV notes

The POV character is female, so the interiority is expected. However, the *quality* of that interiority is the tell: the specific way the body is rendered (tension held, taste, involuntary wetness), the specific framing of the conflict (naming, social legibility, the body as accepted rather than inventoried), and the specific power dynamic (his silence as a form of control she cannot break) all go beyond what POV alone demands. A male author writing this same female POV would be more likely to render her distress through what she observes about *him* rather than what she feels in her own shoulders and jaw and mouth.

### Rationale

Every bodily signal in this chapter is interoceptive: tension stored in shoulders and jaw, taste on the tongue, the wet of tears arriving before awareness. The central conflict is about language and naming — the woman's need for the relationship to be spoken into social existence — and the man's refusal is rendered not as cruelty but as a limitation she can see and articulate precisely. The body is never inventoried from outside; it is experienced as something that is "just fine as it is," which is itself a statement about what it means to be a woman whose body is typically not treated that way. The emotional logic is consistent throughout: wanting that carries social consequence, crying that arrives involuntarily and is resisted as a surrender of power, and the final refusal of physical comfort in place of words. These signals are independent and mutually reinforcing, pointing toward female authorship with moderate-to-strong confidence.
