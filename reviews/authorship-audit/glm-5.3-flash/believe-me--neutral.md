### Tells toward female-authored

- "She felt the wet before she knew she was crying, hot and then over, down her face, anger in it as much as grief and she couldn't have told him which." — Interoception first: tears arrive as bodily sensation before recognition, and the mingled anger/grief registers as an internal problem she must manage, not a spectacle for the reader.

### POV notes

The male character's emotional reticence ("Words are cheap," "Believe what I do") and his caretaking via heat and food could in a cruder text read as gender essentialism, but here they are filtered entirely through her interpretation ("she had worked that out a long while back without his ever saying it") — characterization observed by the POV, not authorial inventory. The sex itself is referenced only retrospectively and through her feeling ("the best of my life"), never choreographed, so no male-gaze tell survives.

### Rationale

Every physical and emotional event in the chapter is rendered from the inside: tension discovered in the shoulders, a metallic taste, tears felt before they are known, wanting experienced as a problem of attachment and naming. The one scene of the female body in play — crying — is about concealment and cost, not display. His interiority stays deliberately opaque to her, which is the point, but the prose's sympathies and its sensory vantage sit wholly with her. The tells are numerous and mutually consistent, though most are register-level rather than singularly diagnostic; hence a strong positive score without maximum confidence.
