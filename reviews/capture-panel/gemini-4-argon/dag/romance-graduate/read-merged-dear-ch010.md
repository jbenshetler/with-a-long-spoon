REACTION

Oh, my chest hurts.

*"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose."*

That line hit me like a physical blow. Because from inside Vee's head, in that little dorm room with the bean bag chair and her best friend, it is the purest, most triumphant feeling in the world. A twenty-one-year-old girl who has spent her whole life managing men, bracing for them, keeping her rules up so she doesn't get taken advantage of, finally feeling the electric joy of *choosing* to reach for what she wants. And the sick, brilliant, devastating thing about this book is that both things are completely true at once: she *did* do it because she wanted to, and she *was* completely set up to do it.

Look at the technique on Pace, though, now that we have both chapters side by side. In Chapter 1, at the front door with Randi: *"He started to back off — that was what he did, he backed off and let her decide — and she did what she had started doing, which was to lean in and bring her mouth back to his and kiss him harder."* And then under the tree with Vee in Chapter 9, he does the exact same thing. It's his signature move. Not because he's a cartoon villain twirling a mustache,- because he genuinely understands how starved these girls are for agency, and he knows that if you leave the last inch empty, their own hunger will pull them across it harder than any push ever could.

And I love Cassie so much. Every girl needs a Cassie. Not a cliché lecturing roommate, just a dry, grounded friend who asks *"How much did he give you to drink?"* and *"Off six-fourteen. That's the whole address."* and *"Rich people hike. Poor people walk."* (Which is also a sneaky little breadcrumb for us—Pace has a small house with cheap white laminate counters and drives a white pickup, but he wears shirts cut from expensive cloth, builds furniture out of dark walnut, and lives out past where the road goes to gravel. Who *is* this guy?)

This is a tiny two-page chapter—another little coda, like Chapter 7 was after the froyo date—and it works so well because it lets the emotional high of Chapter 9 actually land and breathe in the daylight of her real life. Plus, we know from Chapter 3 that he took Randi hiking too (*"Four weeks in she'd learned he did things... Hiking. The gardens."*). He's taking Vee out to his territory next, out off 614, near the house.

However, I will say this to the book: I am loving the slow-burn psychology and the emotional intelligence here, I really am, *and* it has been seven chapters since anybody took their clothes off (except for a one-piece swimsuit). I am not skimming yet because the writing is this good and every scene is doing real work, but I came here for both the depth and the heat, and I'm ready for the temperature to start climbing again on this hike.
