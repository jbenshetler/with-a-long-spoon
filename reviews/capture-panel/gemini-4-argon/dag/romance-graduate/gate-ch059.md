REACTION

Oh, my chest hurts. This is the exact kind of quiet chapter I would fight someone to defend, because every single paragraph is doing real emotional work without an ounce of melodrama.

First of all, the hangover caretaking at the start—the glass of ice water on a folded square of paper towel so it doesn't ring the wood, the memory of him crouched in front of her in the bathroom saying *drink this, all of it,* putting his own soft undershirt over her head and kissing her forehead in the dark after he thought she was out, and then baking cinnamon-raisin bread from scratch while she slept off three ciders—is peak romance competence porn. He just quietly handles things.

And then the conversation across the counter completely wrecked me, for two totally different reasons. One is the sheer tragedy of Pace's actual history clicking together in Vee's head: why he graduated four years early (because he couldn't go home for the summers after sixteen, when things went *not safe* with his father), why he taught himself to cook (no dorm, no dining hall, eating alone at eighteen while everyone else his age was bonding over cafeteria trays), why Daphne was his entire world for two years when he was fifteen (and I love that Vee is mature enough now to hold both truths at once—how he experienced it as luck, and the ugly grown-up word for what Daphne actually did to a fifteen-year-old boy with nowhere else to go). And when she gets up and hugs him the exact way Sheri hugged him in the taproom—both arms, whole weight committed, not saying "I'm sorry" because that would force him to comfort her or perform being comforted—that is the grown-up emotional intelligence I am starving for in this genre.

*However.* Beneath all that genuine tenderness, the dramatic irony is screaming at me! Pace stands there and tells her, *"Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else,"* and explains that *"Women misunderstand it. The friendship. It gets awkward."* And Vee sits there thinking, *No Randi to bring it to,* while planning how she's going to lay the whole Sheri story out for Randi on Tuesday! Except Pace *does* have someone else he talks to about Vee—he was literally inside Randi on Thursday night talking about Vee kissing Randi's neck! Even in his most honest, stripped-bare moment in the kitchen, Randi is the massive, invisible omission sitting right between them. That final image of Pace holding Vee with his chin on her head, looking out over her at the middle of the morning, "neither happy nor sad... somewhere else with it, thinking," gave me chills. What is he thinking about? His past, or what he's leaving out right now?
