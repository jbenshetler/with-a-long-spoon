REACTION

God, that moment underwater hit me right in the chest. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." If you grew up developing early or big, you know that exact grief—the transition from just being a kid who swims to being public property that boys on bleachers feel entitled to yell at. And the detail of rolling the towel tight and carrying it down at her side instead of holding it up over her chest! That's such a real, proud, stubborn twenty-one-year-old move. Refusing to let them see you cover up, even when you want to.

I love Cassie more every time she's on the page. She doesn't do a big feminist speech at the bleachers, she doesn't ask Vee "are you okay" when Vee comes up from the bottom looking sad—she just murmurs *tossers* for Vee's ears only and then splashes her so she can't stay stuck in her head. It makes that weird flat *"Goodbye, Vee"* from Chapter 4 hurt even more in hindsight, because THIS is what a real friend looks like, and Vee is about to walk right into Randi and Pace's trap without having any idea what she's trading away.

And speaking of Pace: *"Don't answer tonight."* Excuse me?! Oh, he is dangerous. That's the jacket blurb coming to life right there—*until choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done.* He knows she's a girl who gets swept along, or who says yes because she's flattered and polite, and he literally will not let her give him a reflex yes. He forces her to take it home, sleep on it, sit on a wet locker room bench in a towel thinking about his hands, and *choose* to text him the next day. Also, "every boy she'd ever gone out with sat down like a dropped backpack" made me laugh out loud because it's so true. No wonder she's done for.

It's a quiet chapter, a character chapter, and right after the two-page interlude in Chapter 6 I definitely clocked that we haven't had a real full-length scene between the leads since the coffee shop, and no real heat since Chapter 3. Normally two short/quiet ones in a row would start making me antsy. But the writing here is so grounded and specific that I didn't mind at all—it's setting up Saturday night, and I want to see Saturday night *badly*.
