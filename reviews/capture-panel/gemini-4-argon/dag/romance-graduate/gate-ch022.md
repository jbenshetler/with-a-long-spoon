REACTION

God, my stomach dropped through the floor at the end of this chapter.

First of all, I have said out loud before that the best scene in a book can be two people just talking, and this chapter is the exact proof of why. There isn't a single sex scene in here—just Pace's hand resting warm and unasking over her bare breast under the quilt—and yet I was completely breathless the entire time. We *finally* get Pace's interiority opened up, and what comes out is so much heavier and more tender than I was braced for.

*Peter.* He's twenty-two! He went to college at *fifteen* because staying home was scarier than leaving, after a seventh-grade teacher stuck his desk in the hallway for an entire school year doing long-division worksheets because he taught himself trig to write a fractal graphics program. And Vee—god, I love Vee's emotional intelligence in this scene so much—doesn't pity him or make him take comfort he didn't ask for. She just names it cleanly (*"They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment"*) and presses her shoulder once against his arm. And when he talks about Gabriel's Horn (filling a shape with a cup of paint that has an infinite surface area you can never paint) and coastlines and Newton's three sentences connecting a falling leaf to the stars... I finally felt why math is a poem to him.

And then *Daphne*. Holy shit. Nineteen and fifteen. His assigned college tutor when he was a fifteen-year-old boy alone in a dorm full of grown men (which is also why he started powerlifting—to make himself big enough that nobody would mess with a kid in a men's dorm!). Hearing Pace lay that out in his flat, careful voice, defending Daphne before Vee even says a word, calling being groomed at fifteen *"luck"* and *"the best two years I'd had"* until she turned twenty-one and walked into bars he couldn't enter for four more years... it broke my heart wide open. And again, Vee swallows the ugly grown-up word because it isn't hers to put on him, and just fits herself along his side with her palm over his chest. And when he holds her hand down over the long scar on his inner thigh and says *"Not tonight,"* and she turns her palm up to meet his right on top of the scar without pulling away? That is peak intimacy. That is a 3 on romance all day long.

Look at the dates, too: Monday night (Oct 19) was Randi in his bed, eating every bite of her chicken and couscous, feeling close to him again. Tuesday night (Oct 20)—literally the very next night—is Vee under his quilt giving him her childhood and receiving *Peter*, the hallway, and Daphne, things I bet he has never given Randi like this.

And then those last three paragraphs walked in with a steel chair. Vee lying awake wrapped around him, thinking about Newton's "all one piece," thinking how lucky she is that six weeks ago she *accidentally* bumped into Randi outside the dining hall, and the very next morning Randi was sitting next to her in stats, and ten days later she complained out loud about an integral and Pace *just happened* to cross a coffee shop to help her... *"for no reason she had done anything to deserve."*

I actually put my hand over my mouth. She thinks the universe handed her a miracle, and every single beat of how they met was engineered in Pace's bed before she even knew their names. And now what lies underneath the game is totally real—Peter is giving her his real name and his deepest wounds—which means when she finds out those first two weeks were a setup, it's going to destroy all three of them.
