REACTION

God, I love how this book uses a table conversation to strip a character bare without anyone ever raising their voice.

First of all, the sheer dual-layer tension of this dinner is delicious. On the one hand, Randi is literally getting off on pulling the explicit details out of Vee (*"Did you take it all? Didn't spill a drop?"*), and on the other hand she's barely surviving hearing about Pace doing the exact things to Vee that we know he does to her—or worse, things he *only* does with Vee. Watch Randi's hands at the table! When Vee talks about Pace's wet hands in the apron, Randi feeds the edge of the glass under her fingernail and bears down on it; when Vee talks about Pace kissing the inside of her wrist and staying there, Randi's fingernail rides the rim of the glass until it sings and she has to pull her hand into her lap. And she takes *one bite* of her food and leaves the rest of the plate untouched while Vee licks hers clean!

And then the "gross" story. I had been waiting since Saturday morning to see what would happen when Vee brought that to Randi, because we *saw* Saturday morning—we know Vee's tongue hunted in his mouth for the salt of herself and she came apart from it three minutes after calling it gross. Watching Vee try to turn her own panic/shame into a cute thirty-second comedy routine for the loft, expecting Randi to validate her squeamishness (*"You think it's gross too, don't you"*), and Randi just setting down a flat, level *"I don't"* on the tablecloth... I literally held my breath. No lecture, no teasing, no pushing. Just: *"It was powerful. It turned me on."* And right there in the booth, Vee's body betrays her whole Victorian comedy bit and clenches hard at the memory of his wet mouth coming down on hers. The way Randi only asks *"How does he act?"* and makes Vee hear herself say out loud, *"He likes it. Actually — he's enthusiastic,"* and then just says *"Mm"* and eats her bread! That is master-class psychological maneuvering. She left Vee still holding the whole thing so Vee has to walk home with her own lie echoing in her ears.

Also, two smaller beats that made me ache: Vee briefly catching the irony of Randi saying *"You picked well"* when Vee remembers looking up from her books to find Pace already walking across the floor toward her (*"the whole thing decided before she'd known there was a thing to decide"*—girl, you have NO idea how true that is), and that goodbye hug where Vee kisses the hollow of Randi's neck and feels Randi's whole body catch and rise under her mouth for a beat too long before Randi blames it on being ticklish. And Dr. Marsh's lecture on primate grooming—how a fifth of our waking day used to go to putting our hands on each other just to keep from sickening, and asking what modern people spend that budget on now, right before Vee and Randi spend two hours verbally grooming every inch of Vee's sex life over wine. Everything in this book rhymes without ever clanging.
