REACTION

God, that common room scene made my skin crawl in the best possible way. I don't think I've ever read a sorority walk-of-shame where the actual violence is done entirely through bright, polite voices and a spreadsheet. Brooke not standing up. Sloane's *"I love that you don't care about cars."* Kinsey calling Pace *"short-cute"* — after we just spent forty pages watching him take Randi apart on a custom walnut bench! And Randi has to stand there and match Kinsey's pitch and say *"Yeah, he's cute"* because her whole survival system in this house depends on never letting them see she cares about anything.

And then Brooke catches her in the lie about the paper without ever saying she caught her. First she asks where Randi worked on the paper last night, and then seamlessly pivots to Pace's white pickup truck parked behind the house. It's a surgical strike. "Sorry to grill you. Go." Dismissed before she can even use her prepared lines.

What is killing me right now is seeing the origin story of the blurb. When I bought this book, the jacket made Randi and Pace sound like this cool, terrifyingly synchronized *Dangerous Liaisons* couple who pick Vee as a bedroom game. Now I'm watching how Randi becomes that person, and it's not out of boredom or random cruelty — it's because Pace broke through her armor last night, AND Brooke just reminded her what happens to her status when she slips up for five minutes, and she is lying under a cheap cold duvet in her heels completely short-circuiting. That line where she thinks about the duvet — *"It was light and synthetic and adequate. It was not — it was a duvet"* — she won't even let her own brain finish comparing it to the cashmere blanket at Pace's warm house.

I am so hooked on her psychology. She's terrifyingly shut down. How does she get from lying on this bed with her shoes on, refusing to cry, to offering up another girl so she never has to be the one *seen* on that bench again? Bring on Vee.
