Oh, that common room scene made my stomach hurt. I have *been* in that room. That specific, smiling, lethal female policing where nobody raises their voice and every single sentence is a tiny scalpel. *"Oh my god, I love that you don't care about cars."* *"Like — short-cute? And what kills me is watching Randi just... take it. Not even defend him! We literally just watched this man worship her on his knees, build a custom walnut bench for her with his own hands, read every micro-tension in her spine, and hold her while she cried—and upstairs in the sorority house she lets Kinsey call him "short-cute" and matches her pitch! Because if she pushes back, if she says one real thing about who Pace actually is or what his body looks like under that henley, she tips her hand that he *matters* and that he's outside their caste system. Remember in chapter 1 how she looked at his handmade oak cabinet like something whose price tag she couldn't read? In Brooke's world, a white pickup truck and a math PhD are legible, and they read as *beneath us*.

And then the contrast when she gets upstairs to her single room. That tiny interrupted sentence—*"The duvet was light and synthetic and adequate. It was not — it was a duvet."* She couldn't even let her own brain finish comparing it to the cashmere blanket at Pace's warm house! Lying on top of her made bed with her heels still on, in a cold room, next to a closet of bone-to-camel-to-oatmeal clothes with "one disciplined oxblood at the end." You see the entire cage she lives in. In Chapter 1 I was almost scared *of* her at the end, thinking she was some cold mastermind plotting in the bathroom. Now I just ache for her. She's twenty-one, she's exhausted, and the two halves of her life cannot coexist. On the bench she begged; in the common room she got patted on the head by a future CEO's third wife. No wonder she has to do something drastic. If Pace keeps stripping the armor off her on Friday nights, she won't survive Saturday mornings in that house.
