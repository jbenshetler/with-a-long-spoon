REACTION

*"Goodbye, Vee."*

Chills. Actual chills down my arms. Because Cassie saw the whole thing in thirty seconds flat! Look at the chapter title: *See You Later*. Vee is standing on the sidewalk at the end spinning like a girl who just got asked to prom by the homecoming queen because Randi said *"See you later, okay?"*—and meanwhile her actual friend in the ball cap and yesterday's shirt took one look at Randi's rehearsed name-bit, refused to play, watched Vee's social fluency rush in to smooth it over, and said *Goodbye, Vee*. Not see you at home in two hours. *Goodbye*. Like she knows Vee is walking out of that lecture hall into a threshing machine and isn't coming back the same person. I already love Cassie and I am already terrified for Vee.

And getting inside Vee's head for the first time... god, she is so much more interesting and specific than the standard "innocent heroine" I was bracing for. First of all, she's *good at stats*—she actually loves "the structure under the numbers, the pleasure of the problem opening," and she lets it float right past her like scenery because Randi's shoulder is touching hers! Second, that whole sequence in Chi Latte where she scans the room is a masterclass in interiority. The way her mother's voice pops into her head automatically—*Floozies*—at the two girls in sports bras and low waistbands, and then right underneath her mother's shaming voice are *two* secret truths she won't look at straight: one, she's lingering on "the sprinter's cut of the leaner one's stomach, how low the other one's waistband sat" (hello, queer gaze she doesn't even clock as queer!), and two, the private, arrogant little thrill of knowing *she has a better body than all of them* under her cardigan! *"She was the most of what the room was selling and she was the one with the most fabric on, both at once."* That is such a real, complex way to inhabit a lush body when you've been raised by a conservative mother—simultaneously policing yourself, judging other women with your mother's voice, secretly admiring their stomachs, and privately knowing you'd blow them all out of the water if you took the cardigan off.

And then watching Randi work from the *other* side of the glass! It's devastating because we know every single seam of what Randi is doing, and to Vee it looks like magic. Even Randi's voice—Vee hears the slightly long esses and arched a's of Randi's carefully constructed, over-corrected anti-Kentucky voice and thinks *a school in Switzerland, a year in Paris*! That made me gasp and laugh at the same time. Randi erased Kentucky so hard that to an outsider she sounds vaguely European! And the detail that Vee walks away from an hour of conversation feeling completely intoxicated, like a key turned in a lock (the exact phrase from Chapter 1!), and *cannot remember a single actual word Randi said*. Because there was no content! It was pure sorority recruitment hypnosis—high-wattage warmth, the forearm squeeze, "you have the right pen," "you're a total fox," sitting her up in the window on the high stool so she feels chosen and displayed.

Wait—and notice how Vee looks at Randi: *"with a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance... and the want had nowhere to put itself except here, this, with her."* Vee thinks she just wants to be Randi's friend, to be let in under the gold Greek sign, while Randi is literally flirting with her (*"You're a total fox, you know that?"*) to bring her back to Pace's bed and Pace's bench. Both of them are disguising their desire for each other even inside their own heads—Randi dressing it up as a gift for Pace, Vee dressing it up as social longing for a brighter life. This is so layered I want to scream.
