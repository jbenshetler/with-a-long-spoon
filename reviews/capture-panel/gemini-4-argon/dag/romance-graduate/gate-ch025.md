REACTION

Oh, Vee. My stomach is in knots for her, and at the same time I am *so* relieved this book didn't turn a bar misunderstanding into five chapters of artificial sulking. Because for about two pages in the middle of The Rusty Nail, when that blonde came over with her *"He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night"* poison and Vee immediately lashed out and told him she'd get herself home, every romance-reader alarm bell in my head went off. I was bracing myself for the dreaded third-act miscommunication trope—the one where the heroine refuses to listen, the hero stalks off in a brood, and they don't speak for three weeks.

Instead, this felt painfully, humanly earned, and then she *found out the truth the same night*. Look at the timing of it: she is coming directly off Chapter 24, where she lay in his bed thinking she had stumbled into a fairy tale for no reason she deserved, and where he had just told her about Daphne leaving him at seventeen. And she's 21, carrying her mother's voice in her head (*"a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them"* / *"spread her legs like a slut"* / *"Floozies"*), standing in a room full of women in bare midriffs who all know him, all hug him, and all learned to dance from him. Of *course* her deepest insecurity—that Pace is a fantasy she built out of wishing, that she's just next in line—snapped its teeth shut on her the second a territorial woman poked the bruise.

And Pace's reaction! He didn't yell, didn't grab her wrist in the parking lot, didn't do the toxic alpha thing and throw her over his shoulder into his truck. The good mood just drained off his face into that flat, still quiet we saw when he talked about seventh grade and Daphne, and when she said *"I'll handle that myself,"* he respected her no, put his untouched beer down on the bar, and walked out. Because that's who Peter is: if you tell him to leave you alone, he leaves you alone. Which made it hurt ten times worse when the older bartender set Vee straight (*"Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey"*).

Also, the dancing lesson on the concrete pad before the bar was pure Pace magic. The battered boom box with electrical tape on the handle! Jeans with a pressed crease! And the way he taught her frame—*"Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead... Being jelly doesn't work"* and *"The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours."* That is their whole dynamic in miniature: he doesn't want her limp and erased, he wants her meeting his pressure so they can move together.

Two details at the end broke my heart: the cab fare costing money she didn't have (especially right after putting those boutique heels on her emergency credit card!), and her putting on his handmade flannel shirt that she'd finally had to wash because she'd rationed his scent until it went stale. And then that ending: she wakes up when it's light, decides *"She'd been wrong. She was going to fix it,"* and decides to go to *Randi's*. Not waiting until Saturday brunch—going *to Randi's* on a Friday morning! Has Vee ever even been to Randi's place?! And what is Randi going to do when Vee shows up on her doorstep in tears over blowing things up with Pace, right after Monday night's truce?
