Oh, my god. Stop. Just stop. I have my hand over my mouth.

"She had stumbled into her. And not ten days after that she had said out loud, to nobody, that she couldn't do an integral, and a man had crossed a coffee shop for it."

She thinks it's Newton's laws! She thinks the universe just handed her a miracle all of a piece! When she is lying there at the end, wrapped around him, thinking about how six weeks ago she "stumbled" into Randi and then ten days later Pace crossed a coffee shop because she complained about an integral, and how she had never been so fortunate or so happy—I physically felt my stomach drop through the floor. Because we were IN that coffee shop in the very first chapters! We know why he crossed the room! It wasn't the stars and the leaf doing the same thing, it was a trap, and now he is in so deep he's telling her his real name in the dark and holding her hand pressed flat against his scar!

Let me back up because this chapter just wrecked me in six different ways. I have said out loud before that the best scene in a book can be two people just talking, and *this* is what I mean. No sex on the page at all—just his hand resting warm over her bare breast under an open robe like her skin is what's keeping him warm—and I was glued to every single line.

First of all: *Peter*. And he's TWENTY-TWO?! Wait. Hold on. He went to college at fifteen. They assigned him a nineteen-year-old "tutor" named Daphne when he was a fifteen-year-old boy alone in a dorm full of grown men, and she slept with him for two years until she turned twenty-one and could go to bars and left him behind at seventeen?! I had the exact same physical reaction Vee did—doing the math in my head and feeling it land low and heavy in my stomach. Nineteen and fifteen. And the way Vee handles it! Does Randi even know his name is Peter? Does Randi know about the seventh-grade hallway? We know Pace thought about Daphne way back at the beginning—that he forced a door open on her once when he was young—and now we see the other half of what Daphne was to him: the only soft thing he had when he was fifteen and terrified, who walked away when he was seventeen because he was still a kid. No wonder he is so almost religiously careful about never pushing a woman through a door she hasn't chosen! No wonder he started lifting weights at fifteen in a dorm full of men! I am terrified. I am so happy for them inside this bubble and so sick with dread about the needle coming toward it. Because how do you walk back from *"Peter"* whispered into the warm dark with your palms laced over his scar, when the whole foundation of how you met is a lie?
