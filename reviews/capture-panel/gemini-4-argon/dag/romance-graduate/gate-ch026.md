REACTION

Oh, Vee, no! I was physically wincing with my hands over my face when that blonde walked over to her. Usually I have zero patience for jealousy/miscommunication fights in romance—it’s one of my biggest pet peeves when an author manufactures drama out of nothing—but this didn't feel manufactured at all. It felt completely earned and psychologically brutal, because we’ve watched Vee’s mother’s voice (*a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them*, *three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut*) waiting in the wings the whole book. The second Vee was already in her head on the dance floor wondering *"how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him,"* she was primed for a mean girl with too much makeup to stick a pin right in her deepest insecurity. And Pace! He doesn't do the toxic alpha thing and grab her arm or yell or chase her down in the parking lot. Because his whole thing is that she chooses every step, when she tells him she’ll handle getting home herself, he just... sets his untouched beer on the bar and leaves. My stomach dropped when the door let in that wedge of cold behind him.

Also, can we talk about that dance lesson on the concrete pad with the battered boom box wrapped in electrical tape?! *"Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead... Being jelly doesn't work."* That entire scene was pure subtext without the book ever winking at the camera and explaining it. It’s literally the thesis of their dynamic: she can't just go limp, and she can't hold space between them out of fear of getting stepped on—she has to come all the way in and push back against his frame so the lead can travel through his body into hers before her head gets in the way. When it finally clicked and he just said, *"There it is,"* quiet, I had chills.

And then the bartender! *"Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey."* Followed by the awful cab ride that cost money she didn't have, and putting on his flannel shirt that she’d already had to wash because she’d rationed his smell until it went stale... god, the ache of lying on top of the covers in a shirt that just smells like fabric softener while hating yourself for hurting a good man.

And now at dawn she is going straight to *Randi's*. Not Cassie's—she wouldn't even wake Cassie up three feet away—she's going to Randi's house! Do we even know if Vee has ever been to Randi's place before?! And what is Randi going to do when Vee shows up on her doorstep in pieces because she blew it with Pace, especially when Randi probably already knows from Pace (or is about to find out) that Thursday night crashed and burned?! Give me the next chapter immediately.
