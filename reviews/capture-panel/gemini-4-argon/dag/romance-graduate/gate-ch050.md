REACTION

Oh my god. *Pace.*

We just spent three chapters watching Vee and Randi on New Year's Eve—Randi in Gstaad realizing a literal fairytale billionaire means nothing compared to Pace and Vee, Vee in her childhood twin bed in Ohio staring at her ceiling thinking Pace is already cooking fra diavolo for some tan blonde model because he doesn't care enough to text her—and Meanwhile! Pace! Is getting blackout, violently sick drunk alone in his dark house, scrubbing his own vomit off the bathroom floor with bleach in the middle of the night, sleeping under *Randi's* cashmere blanket, and then bending over his boiled "prison food" chicken and broccoli on New Year's Day and *sobbing* because a truck drove past on the highway without turning into his driveway!

I am completely wrecked. For fifty chapters this man has been a rock—steady, exact, unhurried, always in control, the man who never drinks alone and always has the temperature of the room and the timing of the sauce figured out to the second. Watching him come apart like this hit me ten times harder because we have never seen him lose his footing once. The mop bucket in the hall with the crust of bleach foam! Boiling the neat freezer bags of plain chicken and broccoli because when Vee isn't there to cook for, food goes right back to being bare survival fuel (*"everything a body could need and nothing his mouth wanted"*), just like Vee's pizza vs. cotton candy realization in reverse! Remember when Vee watched him make the water buffalo mozzarella pizza and thought *he'd have made the pizza exactly like this for an empty house*? NO HE WOULDN'T, VEE! For an empty house he boils a bag of frozen chicken and broccoli and eats it cold with shaking hands!

And we finally, *finally* get the truth of what happened on the porch in Chapter 44, and why he wouldn't say the words. He *did* understand the crackers-and-meal speech! Every single syllable of it! *"He had known, before he spoke, that it would not give her what she had given him. He had watched the answer reach her and fail. He had let it stand because it was safe, and because he was afraid, and because at the moment she had asked him to be brave he had been weak."*

And Daphne! The girl with brown hair and glasses who laughed with her hand over her mouth—his tutor when he first got to the university as a kid who didn't even know how a meal plan worked after leaving home at fifteen. *"There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told."* That line put a fist right through my chest. He is a mathematician who survived a catastrophic heartbreak as a teenager by drawing a false correlation: he told Daphne he loved her and losing her destroyed him; he didn't tell the women after her and losing them was survivable; therefore *the words were the difference*. It's such a devastatingly human, broken piece of logic for a math prodigy to cling to.

Also, can we talk about Sheri at the double-wide trailer diner?! I love her so much! Five feet tall, bleached-and-permed hair, demolishing a double cheeseburger and stealing his milkshake, clocking Randi at the dance hall (*"She looked at me like I was one more of your harem applicants"*), admiring Vee's backside from the bar (*"the one with the big tits... Bet those long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you"*), and just calling Pace out straight to his face: *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* And her own quiet heartbreak over Paige and not talking to her family at Christmas while Elvis's "I'll Be Home for Christmas" plays on the jukebox (*"People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice"*).

And then the fantasy in Pace's head while he's drinking the whiskey: the three of them in his kitchen on a morning after—him at the stove, Vee in his sheet putting too much honey in her tea, Randi with her feet up on the lacquered chair, the two of them laughing together. He isn't just using Randi to get Vee or replacing Randi with Vee; in his head, the whole dream is *all three of them* under his roof, and he's genuinely wondering *"whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her."*

Now it's January 1st. All three of them are coming back to town for the spring semester. Vee thinks Pace might be done with her; Pace just cried over a passing truck because he wants her back and knows he was a coward. What happens the second she gets back to campus?!
