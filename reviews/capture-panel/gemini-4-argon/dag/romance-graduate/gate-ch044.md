REACTION

Oh my god. I am sitting here with my hand over my mouth. *Three* New Year’s Eves in a row—Randi in Gstaad, Vee in Ohio, and now Pace in the woods—and this one just tore my chest wide open.

First of all, finally getting to really meet Sheri! I loved her within thirty seconds. Five feet tall, bleached-and-grown-out roots, a girlfriend named Paige who couldn't handle that Sheri is estranged from her family too (*"People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice"*—what a line), stealing Pace's double burger and chocolate shake, and calling him straight out on his bullshit across a double-wide diner table. When she leaned in on her forearms and said, *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty,"* I wanted to stand up and applaud. And her clock on Randi from the night they came in dancing before Thanksgiving! *"She looked at me like I was one more of your harem applicants. Never did learn my name."*

And then Pace goes home alone on New Year's Eve and gets black-out, puking-on-the-bathroom-floor drunk on a bottle of whiskey while looking at photos of Vee on his phone. *Pace.* The man who measures everything, who never drinks alone, waking up on January 1st to the smell of bleach and a mop bucket in the hall, boiling plain bags of frozen chicken and broccoli ("prison food"!) in a cold house because he only cooks real food when he's feeding *them*. That contrast—between the man who reduces roma tomatoes and shaves chocolate and warms the house hours before Vee arrives, and the man sitting in a cold kitchen with shaking hands forcing down boiled chicken from a freezer bag—is so devastating I can barely stand it.

And we finally, *finally* get the truth of what happened on the porch in Chapter 39, and why he holds the line on those three words. It wasn't because he didn't understand Vee's "crackers and a meal" confession, and it wasn't even about Randi! He *did* understand. He had the true answer right there in his chest—*he loves her*—and he deliberately gave her the smaller, safer sentence (*"I love how good we are together"*) because when he was fifteen and alone in the world, he said *I love you* to Daphne, and when Daphne left him, there wasn't even anyone to tell that she was gone because Daphne was the only person he had. He literally trained himself to believe, like a math proof, that the *words* were what made the loss unsurvivable, and if he never said the words again, he'd survive the leaving. *"He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him."*

Wait—and tucked inside his drunken spiral the night before: *he finally realized what Tuesday morning in November meant.* Six weeks of silence from Randi since she walked down his hall with Vee's green panties in her hand! He knows now it wasn't "hormones." And look at the fantasy he's been building in the dark: all three of them in his kitchen in the morning, him at the stove, Vee in his sheet putting too much honey in her tea, Randi with her feet up on the lacquered chair, the two of them laughing together. He wants them *both* at the same table in the daylight, and right now he has neither of them, and he's sobbing over a bowl of cold boiled chicken because a truck drove past on the highway without turning into his drive.

When his fork stopped halfway to the bowl at the sound of the engine on the highway, and then he bent over the table and sobbed until the truck was gone... I have read hundreds of romances where the stoic alpha hero "breaks," and it's always some melodramatic roaring and punching a wall. Watching this quiet, hyper-controlled man sit in a cold kitchen with a hangover, cry for the length of a passing truck over the girl he was too scared to say *I love you* to, and then pick up his fork and keep eating his cold boiled chicken anyway? Masterclass. Put the next chapter in my veins right now.
