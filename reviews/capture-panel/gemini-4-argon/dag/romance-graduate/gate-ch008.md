REACTION

Stop it. *Stop it right now.* How is a first date with a single kiss under a tree this erotic AND this heartbreakingly romantic at the exact same time?!

Let me talk about the restaurant first because the way Helen Rivers writes food and appetite in this book should be studied in a lab. Think about the contrast with Randi: in Chapter 1, Pace has to slice a honeycrisp apple razor-thin and lay the cut sides up just to get past the food police in Randi's head; in Chapter 3, Randi walks past a burger and fries on the quad and automatically says, *"I can't eat that."* And now here is Vee, who forgot to eat lunch because she spent the whole afternoon trying on dresses and borrowing shoes down the hall with Cassie delivering verdicts from the bunk (I love them so much), walking into this tiny hole-in-the-wall Arabic place where Christine knows Pace by name.

And when Vee looks at the menu and jokes that she has no map past hummus, Pace doesn't mansplain the menu to her! He just asks, *"May I choose?"*

Look at what happens to Vee when she says *Yes, please* and hands the reins over to him. She literally drops into subspace at the dinner table! I am not even kidding—read that middle section again! That is the exact psychological architecture of D/s written completely without a single piece of leather in sight. She copies his hands tearing the hot bread, she stops watching herself eat, she stops waiting for the bill of the evening to come due, and when he licks a drip of smoky baba ghanoush off the side of his hand without pausing his sentence, she *loses the thread of what he's saying and doesn't scramble to pick it back up*. For a girl who spent a whole semester staying up until 2 a.m. playing a healer to keep some boy alive, who is always doing "the small constant work of keeping up her end," letting a man carry the evening while she drifts in the sound of his voice is the most restful, intoxicating surrender of her life. Until the sharp hot mint tea in the little brass cages brings her back up to the surface! Who writes a dinner date with the exact rhythm of a scene and aftercare?!

(Also: *he's a competitive powerlifter!* That explains why Kinsey and Sloane clocked him as "five-foot-nine / short-cute" while Vee and Randi are both losing their minds over the thick planes of his chest and thighs and forearms under his loose shirts!)

And then the walk back. And the kiss.

Wait—did you catch that he did the **exact** same move with Vee that he does with Randi in Chapter 1?!
In Chapter 1: *"He bent and kissed her, brief, warm, the kiss he gave her when she came in the door. He started to back off — that was what he did, he backed off and let her decide — and she did what she had started doing, which was to lean in and bring her mouth back to his and kiss him harder."*
And here, under the shadow of the tree! First, when Vee panics and throws out her mother's rule (*"I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"*), he doesn't push, doesn't pout, doesn't even do a "patient little wait for her to change her mind"—he actually accepts the no, smiles like she told him something fine about herself, and starts walking her home! So when her feet stay planted and her hand pulls taut in his, it's 100% her choice. He leaves the last inch to her. She gives him a tiny, careful kiss. And then *he eases back a breath of space and waits*, so she gets to stand there in the smell of soap and shave and restaurant smoke, feel the size of her own wanting, and lean in to take the real kiss herself!

I had the biggest, dumbest grin on my face at the end of this chapter—right beside a massive pit in my stomach. Because NOTHING about how Pace was with Vee tonight felt like a guy running a game to bring a third back to his girlfriend. He brought her to *Christine's*, where they know him! He gave her his real life, his real tenderness, his real patience. He is falling into Vee with his whole chest while telling himself this is all for Randi and "for us." And now Vee has to go tell Randi all about this date!
