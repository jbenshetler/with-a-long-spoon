REACTION

Wait—oh my god. I was reading this thinking, okay, we're getting a grounded campus scene with the three of them in stats, seeing how the class divide sits between Cassie and Randi with Vee caught in the middle... and then MIRA popped up on the graph and my stomach dropped straight through the floor.

Not just that her dad took PPP loans and laid everyone off anyway—after Randi sat there two weeks earlier so effortlessly confident that her father was one of the good ones, the bright counterexample who kept people on payroll—but that he named the holding company *MIRANDA*. Her name is literally stamped on the worst, ugliest outlier in the county. That green Mercedes she drives, the white gold on her wrist, the brunch bills she picks up without blinking, the whole polished, untouchable rich-girl ease she walks around in... she just found out in real time, in a glass study room next to a girl whose dad got laid off in 2020, that all of it is sitting on top of fraud with her own birth name on the paperwork.

And what kills me is that Randi *believed* him. For all her ruthless, manipulative games with Vee and Pace, she was completely earnest about her dad. Watching her read that column twice, fast and then slow, hoping the second pass would take it back, and then whispering *"Cheating bastards"* with the color drained out of her face—and Cassie not even seeing the name on the screen and just saying *"That's the whole thing, right there"*! I physically winced for her. First she's losing her footing with Pace on Monday, and now on Wednesday her whole family myth just cracked open in her lap.

And then look at Vee. The whole book has been about Randi and Pace being a step ahead of Vee, watching her, managing her, curating what she knows—and here, for the first time, *Vee* sees something Randi doesn't know she's been seen in. And what does Vee do? She doesn't expose her, doesn't ask a falsely sympathetic question, doesn't let Cassie see; she just slides the laptop over and gives Randi busywork so she can put her hands somewhere and recover her dignity. And she walks out thinking Randi has *"no hand in it, none of it earned"*—completely innocent of the fact that Randi *is* running a con on her in her personal life, while being genuinely innocent of the one her father ran in her name. The layering of who is deceiving whom, and Vee's pure, instinctive tenderness toward the woman who is both loving her and playing her, is unreal.
