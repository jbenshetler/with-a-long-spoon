REACTION

Oh, my chest hurts. That title drop right in the middle of her trying to convince herself everything was fine — *"She had been received warmly"* — felt like a cold hand on the back of my neck. Because the blurb warned us from day one: *A warm reception is also what you call walking into fire.*

What destroys me about this chapter is how painfully, excruciatingly recognizable it is. Every woman who has ever been in a relationship where the man holds all the unspoken power over the "what are we" conversation knows that exact architecture Vee built in her head. You know the rule without ever being told the rule: you cannot say *I love you* first, so you spend weeks workshopping a proxy sentence. Something poetic and true that says *you changed my whole life* while leaving you just enough plausible deniability if he doesn't match it. *"I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal."* She hands him her whole heart disguised as gratitude.

And Pace's physical reaction tells you *he heard what she was really saying*. His hand stops on her shin, presses once, stops again — *"as if he had begun two different responses with his hand and put both down before either had finished"* — and he has to take *two* breaths before he speaks! He knows she's telling him she loves him. And what does this man — who gives her the top bathroom drawer, who wears her flannel over his own shirt all weekend so it'll smell like him, who sews her couture silk dresses — give her back?

*"I love how good we are together."* And then he pivots straight to her body: *"What you do — what your body does — I've never."*

I wanted to scream at him! Using the word *love* right at the front of the sentence just to hook it onto *how good we are together* and what her body does in bed is such a devastating sidestep. Even if he meant it sincerely, even if in his damaged, compartmentalized math-brain he thinks he's giving her something huge while keeping the load-bearing wall around his arrangement with Randi intact, it reduces her soul-deep confession to great sex and good chemistry. And the way Vee's body registers the miss before her mind will let her see it — that little *"wait"* behind her breastbone that gets smoothed away because he pulls her close under the warm quilt — and then two days later on the dorm laundry table when it finally catches up to her: *"She had said her best sentence. He had not said the sentence back."*

And that paragraph about the pile inside her where she puts the things she doesn't know what to do with ("the place was not empty... moments across the fall when what she knew about him had had to do small repair work to keep functioning")—God, the psychological precision of this book is unreal. First Cassie asking her at the cafeteria table *"So what is he... To you,"* and now this. The silence where his word should be isn't romantic anymore; it's starting to bruise.
