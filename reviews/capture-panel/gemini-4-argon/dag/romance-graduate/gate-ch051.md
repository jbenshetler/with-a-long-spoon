REACTION

Oh, my god. I have actual tears in my eyes. *This* is the third prong of New Year's, and it completely shattered me.

While Randi is in Gstaad fucking a literal fairytale lawyer only to realize she's freezing cold everywhere they don't touch, and Vee is lying in her childhood twin bed torturing herself with an imaginary blonde model in Pace's sheet at 11:52 p.m.... Pace is getting so blackout, violently sick on whiskey alone in the dark that he has to mop his own bathroom floor with bleach in the middle of the night, and then sitting over a bowl of boiled freezer chicken and broccoli, sobbing over his kitchen table when a truck drives past on the highway without turning into his driveway!

I have been dying to know what was behind his iron wall around words, and honestly, until this chapter, part of me thought it was mostly the arrangement with Randi—that he couldn't call Vee his girlfriend because he wasn't exclusive. Finding out that it's *Daphne*—the tutor assigned to a fifteen-year-old kid who was brilliant at math and completely alone in the world, who didn't even know how to make a meal plan stretch, who became his first lover and his whole world and then left him with no one to even tell she was gone (*"Daphne was the person he would have told"*)—oh, that gutted me. Look at the superstitious math-brain logic he built out of that trauma: with Daphne he said *I love you* and losing her nearly killed him; with the women after her he kept the words inside and survived the endings; therefore, *the words are what make loss fatal.*

And the fact that he isn't lying to himself anymore! On that morning after the whiskey, he doesn't hide behind "words are cheap." He looks straight at the porch scene—at Vee's crackers-and-a-meal confession—and admits to himself: *"He had let it stand because it was safe, and because he was afraid, and because at the moment she had asked him to be brave he had been weak."* And then: *"He knew he loved her."*

Wait—can we also talk about Sheri?! I LOVE Sheri. Five feet tall, bleached roots, housing a double cheeseburger and stealing his milkshake, immediately clocking Randi at the bar (*"She looked at me like I was one more of your harem applicants. Never did learn my name"*), and looking Pace dead in the eye to say: *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* Every person in this book needs a Sheri or a Cassie to cut through their bullshit! And when Pace tries to explain why he's so gone on Vee—that she understands the *philosophy* of his food, the whole idea underneath it—and Then the contrast between the meals he builds for Vee and the rows of boiled chicken and broccoli in freezer bags ("prison food") that he eats when she's gone. When Vee isn't there to feed, food is just fuel he forces down his throat because he tells his body to.

Also, that vision he has while drinking: the three of them in his kitchen the morning after, him at the stove, Vee in his sheet putting too much honey in her tea, Randi across from her with her feet up on the lacquered chair, the two women laughing together while nobody hurries anywhere. He doesn't just want a threesome; he wants a *home* with both of them in it, and he's actually stops to wonder whether the room would ask more of Vee than she can carry.

When he heard that engine on the highway, knew immediately it wasn't her car, hoped anyway for one second, and then bent over his cold chicken and broccoli and sobbed until the truck was gone... I would text my best friend at 1 a.m. in all caps: *HE LOVES HER. HE BROKE DOWN CRYING AT THE KITCHEN TABLE. THE TERMITES IN MY CHEST ARE SCREAMING.* I need the spring semester to start IMMEDIATELY.
