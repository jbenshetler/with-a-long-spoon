Oh, my god. Stop everything. This chapter just took my breath away—and broke my heart into a million glittering pieces.

First of all, the sheer structural audacity of jumping straight from Vee walking out into the freezing cold in Chapter 50 to New Year's Eve in Gstaad with Randi! For the first two pages I had that delicious disorientation of *Wait, we jumped all of December? What happened after the walkout?!*—and then by the time Randi was in that ski line with John, I realized what the book was doing and I couldn't read fast enough. Because while Vee was in Ohio eating crackers her whole life until Pace fed her a meal, Randi has been eating Michelin-starred foam and venison with shaved white truffle her whole life and starving to death inside it. Look at that opening breakfast table: her father eating rare steak and eggs while she "eats her revulsion" over what she now knows he did with the *MIRANDA* PPP loans, her mother "all edges and points" in a designer belt, and Randi hiding the locked white-gold bracelet on her wrist! (Wait—*the white-gold bracelet that never came off*, locked on her wrist, catching on the heel of her hand when John spins her?! Is that Pace's?! Did Pace lock that bracelet on her wrist?!)

And then *John*. In ninety-nine percent of romance novels, John is the Billionaire Alpha Hero on the back cover. He's tall, tanned, Yale Law, skis black diamonds with effortless quiet reserve, pulls strings for the window table at the hütte, quotes Byron at the bottom of the stairs when she comes down in pale metallic blue, dances like a dream, conjures a literal horse-drawn sleigh in the snow, and takes her upstairs without asking because he assumes the world is his. And normally, when a book introduces an outside guy, I'm immediately annoyed and skimming to get back to the main couple. Here? Watching the book build the absolute pinnacle of the traditional hetero-romance fairytale—not a bad version of it, not a jerk or a clumsy fumbler, but *the best the acceptable world could do for her*—just so Randi could lie down in the middle of it and feel *nothing*?!

That cut to "Afterward" hit me like a physical blow. I didn't even want a sex scene with John, and the fact that the chapter skips the act entirely and just gives us the aftermath—her lipstick ring left around the base of his cock where she'd watched her own mouth take him "from a long way off," and that devastating line: *"The eyeliner had held. Two chapters ago, Vee took off Pace's flannel shirt in her freezing dorm room because she realized the warmth inside it was only her own heat reflected back at her. Now Randi is lying against a warm, sleeping man in Switzerland, warm only along the one seam where her skin touches his, realizing the cold in the room is *hers*—and then putting her makeup back on in the dark only to crawl *back into bed* with "the perfect nothing asleep behind her" to borrow warmth she cannot make!

How on the dance floor she compares John's firm, easy lead to Pace's lightest pressure that made her *listen* and do the work, and how Pace never assumed anything in his life and made her say it out loud every time. I am completely floored by how deep these three people are. Now take me back to January and show me the wreckage when they all get back to campus!
