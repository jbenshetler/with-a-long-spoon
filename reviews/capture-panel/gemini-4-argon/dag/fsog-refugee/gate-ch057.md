REACTION

Oh, I love Sheri so much. And I love what this chapter just did for Vee and Pace.

After how intense and knotted up Chapter 55 and Chapter 56 were—watching Vee in her dorm room trying to reconcile her own wetness and stopping a breath away from her own fingers, and then immediately cutting to Randi and Pace in bed together talking about Vee while Randi licks them both clean—I needed this breath of fresh air. More than that, I needed Vee to finally meet the ghost she'd been torturing herself with all through December.

Watching Vee dress in "battle armor"—Meg's keyhole shirt, the borrowed short skirt, and Randi's four-inch induction heels in the middle of January—to go face the imaginary tall, tan blonde model in her head was so painfully relatable. And then the moment she walks in and hears Pace call out *"Sheri"* with real warmth in his voice, and the woman who turns around is five-foot-nothing with dark roots, a perm, and crooked makeup! The moment Sheri looks Vee up and down—not pricing her or ranking her the way women usually do, and certainly not sizing her up as a rival for Pace, but openly *enjoying* her—and Vee just bursts out laughing in the middle of the taproom as a whole month of 3 a.m. dread evaporates off her shoulders... I was smiling so hard my face hurt.

And Sheri! *"And you get to motorboat them things?"* MAKING PACE BLUSH! All the way up his neck and into his ears! We have never seen Pace blush once in fifty-seven chapters. Seeing this controlled, steady man get completely flustered while sitting "off duty" with a friend who doesn't want anything from him made him feel so human and so warm. And the fact that he told Sheri Vee gets *"the philosophy of his food"*—oh, my heart.

Then the second half of the taproom scene just gutted me in the best way. When Sheri sends Pace to the bar and tells Vee how they actually became friends: losing her whole family, her Marine Corps dad and brother, her church, and her friends inside of a month when she came out last March. Notice how Sheri phrased it: *"People I hardly knew kept telling me it'd be all right. Strangers will say that for free. Easy words, for a hard thing. The ones who owed me words weren't spending any."* That hit right against Vee's December fight with Pace (*"Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me"*), except here we see *why* Pace believes actions are what count when the bottom drops out. He didn't give Sheri false platitudes when she broke down crying during a two-step; he drove her home in silence, showed up the next morning with coffee and donuts, fixed her lock and her dripping faucet, invited her to ride bikes, bought her a bacon cheeseburger, and just *stayed*. Because things went bad with his own daddy and he hasn't been home since he was sixteen.

It also quietly landed on me that Sheri lost her entire world when she came out—which makes Brooke's nasty threat to Randi back in the sorority kitchen (*"You remember Lauren Pike"*) feel even heavier in the background of this town.

And that ending! Pace's arm coming around Vee's waist when she's tipsy on cider ("It read as affection. It worked as ballast. With him, she was coming to understand, those had never been two different things"), Sheri singing filthy parodies in the middle of the bench seat, Pace holding the headlights on Sheri's door until her porch light comes on, and then driving Vee home with both hands at ten and two "like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling." This is the exact tenderness-inside-control that keeps me glued to this book. And now Valentine's Saturday at the dance hall with the Brooks and Dunn cover band is officially set!
