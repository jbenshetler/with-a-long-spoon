REACTION

Oh my god. I am sitting here with my hand over my mouth. *Pace bent over the kitchen table sobbing over a bowl of boiled chicken and broccoli because a truck drove past on the highway and didn't turn in.*

For forty-four chapters, this man has been the steady, unshakable center of the universe—the one who knows how to do everything, who keeps the house warm, who never loses his temper, who Vee pictured in Chapter 41 sleeping "easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." And here he is on New Year's Eve and New Year's Day, drinking himself sick in the dark, scrubbing his own vomit off the bathroom floor with bleach, sleeping under *Randi's* cashmere blanket because both of his women are gone, boiling plain frozen chicken and broccoli in bags ("prison food") because he only cooks real meals for Vee, and finally breaking in half at the kitchen table.

First of all, I love Sheri so much! What a breath of fresh air. After Vee torturing herself on New Year's Eve imagining Pace cooking fra diavolo for some tan blonde model—or worse, for Sheri—we find out Sheri is a five-foot country lesbian who eats double cheeseburgers like she rides, just got dumped by a design student named Paige because Sheri is estranged from her homophobic family ("call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas"), and doesn't want a damn thing from Pace except his fries and his friendship. And the way Sheri cut right through his brick-by-brick defenses! When he tried the exact same math on her that he used on Vee—*I'm there every weekend, I told her she was the most important thing in my life*—Sheri didn't let him get away with an inch of it: *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* Somebody needed to say that to his face! And when she asked what Vee's got and he blushed and said she understands "the philosophy of my food," I laughed out loud right along with him.

And then the truth about why he couldn't say it on the porch. *Daphne.* We knew he went to college at fifteen to escape his parents and lived with his nineteen-year-old tutor, and I always wondered how that scarred him, because fifteen is so young. Seeing it from inside his head broke my heart: he was a brilliant fifteen-year-old boy who didn't even know how a dining hall worked or where to stand, and Daphne was his whole world—his tutor, his best friend, his first love. When she left, "there had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told." So his mathematician's brain drew a straight causal line: he told Daphne he loved her, and losing her destroyed his life; he never said the words to any woman after her, and their endings were survivable. *He thought the words were the switch that gives someone the power to annihilate you.*

And watching him admit it to himself in the cold light of January 1st—*"He knew he loved her... He had let it stand because it was safe, and because he was afraid, and because at the moment she had asked him to be brave he had been weak. He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him"*—that is everything I read this genre for. A man that controlled, that dominant in bed, stripped down to the raw truth that he was a coward when she handed him her heart, and weeping alone when the car on the road isn't hers.

AND YET. Even while my heart is breaking for him, I caught two massive things that made my stomach drop for what's coming:
1. Randi hasn't seen him or called him for *six weeks*—not since the morning of November 17 when she walked down the hall with Vee's green satin panties in her fist! He finally realizes that morning wasn't "hormones," and he misses her terribly, and he still doesn't know she saw Vee's top bathroom drawer first!
2. Even while he is drinking himself sick over Vee, his daydream of the future isn't just him and Vee—it's *all three of them* in his kitchen the morning after, Vee in his sheet with honey in her tea and Randi across from her with her feet up, laughing together while he cooks! He still thinks Vee is going to step into that room with both of them and the only question is "whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her." He has no idea that even if he tells Vee *I love you*, the secret of how he and Randi chose her is waiting right behind it like a landmine.

The term is about to start. All three of them just spent New Year's Eve miserable in three different time zones (well, Ohio and Virginia are the same time zone, plus Switzerland), aching for each other. Bring on January!
