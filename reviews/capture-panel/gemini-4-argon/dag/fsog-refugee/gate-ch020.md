REACTION

That closing line—*And Vee thought, as she had been thinking lately and could not stop, that she had never once in her life had a friend like this*—actually made my stomach hurt. Because half of me was smiling right along with her on that sunny sidewalk, and the other half of me wanted to grab her by the shoulders and tell her to check her bank balance and run.

There is so much going on in this chapter under the surface of a fun Friday/Saturday girls' hangout. First of all, Cassie continues to be the absolute North Star of this book. That forty seconds in stats class where Vee looks up from Simpson's paradox and realizes Cassie hasn't looked at the board once because Cassie doesn't care about a dress, she only cares about *what the dress did to her friend* ("Was it a good night?" / "Yes. Oh, yes.")—that made my throat tight. Cassie loves Vee with zero agenda.

And then you put that right next to Randi. I am so fascinated and conflicted by Randi here. When Vee comes out of the dorm bathroom in the burgundy silk and Randi goes completely still, touches the charmeuse at her hip, runs her fingers up the bias seam to Vee's bare shoulder, and says, *"He didn't make this for you. He made it to you"*—Randi isn't faking that. She sees the exact same thing Vee cried over in the sun porch. And when Vee says, *"He was up all night finishing it. He fell asleep after. I watched him sleep,"* and Randi's hand stays on her shoulder while she goes blank and somewhere else for a long breath... Vee thinks Randi is jealous *of* her for having a man like Pace, when the truth is Randi *has* Pace, and she just found out what Pace does for Vee when Randi isn't in the room. Remember Chapter 18 when Randi left Pace's stew untouched and accused him of taking all weekend with Vee because *he* wanted to? Finding out Pace stayed up all night sewing a gown for Vee—and then actually *slept* next to Vee afterward, when Randi bolted out the door in Chapter 11 and turned her back on him in Chapter 18—had to go into Randi like a knife.

And yet! At the shoe store, when Randi goes down on one knee in front of the bench in her expensive clothes, takes Vee's bare foot in both hands, sees the frosted plum polish from Sunday's brunch conversation, and just says *"Mm"* while stroking her thumb across Vee's big toe... I felt that all the way up my own spine right along with Vee! That was pure seduction, quiet and electric, and Vee grabbing Randi's shoulder because the floor tipped under her was such a real beat. Plus their goofy banter at the discount shoe rack with the yeti snow-tire shoe and the pink rhinestone platforms was genuinely adorable. Randi gives Vee her real laugh there!

What scares me, though, is the credit card. Watching Vee put her debit card back and put a pair of boutique heels she cannot afford on her emergency credit card—while Randi drops multiple times that amount on everyday loafers without even looking at the receipt just to walk out of the store in them—gave me such a sinking feeling. Randi didn't force her to buy them, just like the blurb says (*Every yes was freely given*), but Randi steered her straight out of the store where Vee could afford to live and into a store where Vee had to go into debt just to not feel like an apology next to Randi and the dress. It's the exact same dynamic as the seduction, played out with money, and Vee is already in over her head without realizing how much it's going to cost her next month.
