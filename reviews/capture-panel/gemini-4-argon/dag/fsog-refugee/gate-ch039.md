Oh, my chest hurts. My chest *hurts* for her.

The title of the book just dropped right into the middle of the page — *"She had been received. She had been received warmly"* — and it felt like a physical blow. Because she *was* received warmly! He didn't freeze her out, he didn't pull away, he didn't make her feel stupid. He took two breaths (she'd never heard him take two breaths before!), his hand started two different responses on her shin and put them both down, and then he pulled her in, held his kiss on her hair longer than usual, and said something true. And yet he steered her giant, brave, terrified confession of her whole soul right back into *"I love how good we are together"* and *"what your body does."*

I am sitting here with my hands over my mouth because you can feel every millimeter of what just happened on both sides of that quilt. Vee spent *weeks* building a safe little house around *I love you* so she could hand it to him without breaking the unspoken rule that he has to say it first: *"I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal. I didn't know what a meal was until you fed me one. And now I know."* And Pace *heard* it. He knows she isn't talking about salmon or pizza or even just orgasms. That's why he had to take a second breath. That's why his hand stopped twice on her shin. He felt the size of what she was handing him, and because of what he hasn't told her—because of Randi, because of the arrangement, because of whatever wall is inside him—he couldn't give her the sentence back in the same shape. So he used the word *love* in the one safe place he could (*"I love how good we are together"*) and narrowed the meal down to their bodies.

And her body knew before her head did! That little *"wait"* behind her breastbone while she was still warm under his quilt, and then two days later sitting on the folding table in the dorm laundry room, realizing *"She had said her best sentence. He had not said the sentence back."* And putting it on the pile of small flickers she doesn't look at. Oh, Vivienne.

The first half of the chapter with Cassie at the athletic cafeteria set this up so quietly and devastatingly, too. Vee having to *crop* her own life for her parents—cropping the induction photo at the waist so her mother wouldn't see her bare leg (and her mother still touching her own collarbone at the neckline and putting on her "nurse face" because Vee has lost weight from having so much sex!), hiding the cooking and the house from her dad because "too good is its own kind of suspicious," hiding that Pace *sewed* the dress because her dad would understand a man taking a dress off her better than a man making one. To you,"* Vee realizes she both *can't* say and *doesn't want* to look too closely at why she doesn't have the word.

It's the blurb coming to collect its bill: *He gives her everything but a word for what they are.* Up until now, everything he *does*—the top drawer, the flannel shirt over his own shirt, the slowness, the care—has been so huge that she could live inside the actions and not starve for the word. Now she reached for the word, sideways, as bravely as she knew how, and felt the ceiling of the room. I am aching for her, and I am terrified of what happens when that pile of things she isn't looking at gets too tall to ignore.
