REACTION

Oh my god. I have actual tears on my face right now. *Pace.*

I have spent the last four chapters—ever since the porch, really, and all through that devastating fight at his door in Chapter 42—thinking that the reason he couldn't give Vee a word or say her sentence back was because of Randi and the secret between them. And don't get me wrong, the secret is still a ticking bomb waiting to go off,especially seeing him picture the three of them in his kitchen on a morning after while Vee has no idea. That still knots my stomach right up.

But finding out that the silence around *I love you* wasn't about keeping his options open or protecting the game—that it was about *Daphne*, about being a fifteen-year-old kid who escaped a home he never goes back to, who had no one else in the world when his nineteen-year-old tutor left him ("There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told"), and who mathematically convinced himself that *saying the words* was what made losing someone unsurvivable? That completely broke me in half.

And remember in Chapter 41 when Vee was lying in her dorm bed in the cold, taking off his flannel because she thought he was "across town in his own bed, gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him"? Look at him here! Drinking alone in the dark until he's throwing up and bleaching the bathroom floor in the middle of the night, waking up on New Year's Day in a cold house (because he only heats it for *her*!), boiling plain frozen bags of chicken and broccoli—"prison food"—because all the magic, all the garlic in the oil and the water-buffalo mozzarella and the fra diavolo, was only ever for *her*. Watching him hear a truck on the highway, knowing immediately it's the wrong engine, and still holding onto the hope for one second until it drives past and he bends over his cold bowl of chicken and sobs... I want to reach into this book and shake them both and put them in the same room right now.

Also, I loved finally meeting Sheri! What a breath of fresh air. After Vee spent midnight in Ohio torturing herself by imagining Sheri—or some tan blonde model—in her sheet eating Pace's food, here is five-foot-nothing Sheri in a converted double-wide diner, mourning her ex-girlfriend Paige and stealing Pace's double cheeseburger while calling him straight out on his bullshit: *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* Every hero needs a friend who isn't in awe of him to look him dead in the eye and say that. And the fact that Pace didn't even argue with her, and then admitted cleanly to himself the next morning: *"He knew he loved her... He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him."*

We just had the whole New Year's Eve trifecta—Randi feeling icy cold next to a literal fairytale prince in Gstaad, Vee aching in her childhood twin bed in Ohio, and Pace coming apart over a bowl of boiled chicken in Virginia. Winter break is over. Term is starting. He knows he loves her and he knows he was weak when she asked him to be brave. Go get your girl, Peter!!
