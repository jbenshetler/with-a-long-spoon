REACTION

Oh my god, this chapter just broke me wide open. I am sitting here with a lump in my throat the size of my fist.

First of all, the sheer *tenderness* of this scene from the very first paragraph—her poorly tied robe falling open, and Pace's palm coming up to hold the weight of her bare breast under the quilt, not groping, not pushing for another round, just resting there warm like her skin is what's keeping him warm while they talk until their tea goes cold. And the way he listens to her! When she tells him about her Ohio childhood and the church basement casseroles and Cool Whip containers, and instead of mocking it or making a snobby joke, he says, *"And affectionate."* Because he goes straight to the hand underneath the thing. And when she tells him her mother calls her Vivienne and her dad only calls her Vivienne when she's in trouble, he distills her whole life's ache in one quiet sentence: *"So the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other."* How do you not fall completely, hopelessly in love with a man who listens like that?

And then he opens the doors for her. *Peter.* He's twenty-two! He went to college at *fifteen* because staying home was scarier than leaving! That seventh-grade teacher putting his desk in the hallway for an entire school year to do long-division worksheets because he taught himself trig—and Vee not giving him a pitying "I'm sorry" that would make him manage her feelings, just pressing her shoulder against his arm and naming it: *"They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment."* Look at how she meets him. She isn't just a girl being taken care of by an older, worldly dominant; she sees *him*. And when he talks about math—Gabriel's Horn with the cup of paint that can fill the inside but never cover the outside, the fractal coastline, Newton's three sentences connecting a thrown ball and an October leaf and the stars over the ridge—you can feel why he is the way he is.

Then *Daphne*. My stomach dropped right along with Vee's. Fifteen and nineteen. A fifteen-year-old boy alone in a dorm full of grown men—lifting weights so nobody would mess with him!—and his nineteen-year-old assigned tutor taking him into her bed for two years and then walking away when she turned twenty-one and could go to bars. And the way his voice goes flat and careful, defending it before Vee can even say the ugly grown-up word that's forming under her tongue, handing her the thing that hurt him and calling it luck. It suddenly explains *everything* about why Pace is so fiercely, religiously devoted to consent and never rushing Vee. He knows in his bones what it is to be fifteen and have someone older take what you don't even know you're too young to give, even if he still calls it the best two years he had. And Vee—God, I love Vee so much in this chapter—holding the word behind her teeth because it's not hers to force on him, fitting herself along his side with her palm flat over his chest until his breathing eases.

And that beat with the scar on his inner thigh! When her fingers find it and she asks *"This?"* and he doesn't push her hand away or shut down—he lays his hand over hers, presses her palm *down* onto the scar, and says, *"Not tonight."* A boundary with zero coldness in it, just a door with a time on it that isn't now, and she turns her palm up under his so they're holding hands right over the scar, and whispers *"Peter"* into the dark just to keep it. That is intimacy at a level most books never even touch.

Which makes the ending of this chapter almost unbearable to read. Because she's lying there wrapped around him while he sleeps, thinking about how six weeks ago she "stumbled into" Randi outside the dining hall, and ten days later Pace crossed a coffee shop because she complained about an integral, and how Newton's leaf and the stars and Randi and Pace have all come to her "all of a piece... for no reason she had done anything to deserve." She thinks it was luck! She thinks it was the universe moving like the stars! And meanwhile *he just gave her his real name and his childhood and Daphne and held her hand over his scar*, on a Tuesday night, twenty-four hours after Randi was in that exact bed. This isn't a game for him anymore either—he is falling completely in love with Vee, and Vee is completely in love with him, and the trap under all their feet is set with dynamite.
