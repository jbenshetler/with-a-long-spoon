REACTION

Oh, my chest hurts. Look at how this book earns a fight! If this were a standard KU romance, the blonde at the bar would be a fabricated "miscommunication" trope dropped in from outer space to create third-act drama. Here, every single beat of Vee blowing up that night comes straight out of the marrow of who she is and what she's been carrying since Chapter 4.

Because think about where Vee was at the end of *Rock*: lying in Pace's bed on Tuesday night thinking she had never been so fortunate or so happy, terrified on some subterranean level that it's too good to be true. She literally looks for "the bill of it" right out on the dance floor (*"how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join"*). And the second she walks into The Rusty Nail—visible in Meg's keyhole shirt and borrowed boots, out in a public room where Pace is beloved by dozens of confident, bare-midriff women—her mother's voice is already primed and waiting in her ear: *"a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them."*

Before we even got to the bar, though, that dancing lesson on the concrete pad outside the dark basketball gym had me smiling so hard. The battered boom box with the handle wrapped in electrical tape! The pressed crease in his jeans and the short-sleeved knit shirt in October because he dressed for the heat of dancing! And the physical metaphor of how Pace teaches her the three-step is pure gold: *"Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead... Being jelly doesn't work."* And when she holds herself a few inches away so he won't step on her feet: *"That's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours."* That is their entire dynamic in two sentences. When she finally stops anticipating and gives the frame her weight under the cold field lights, you can feel the exact click in her body.

Which makes the crash at The Rusty Nail hurt ten times worse. That jealous blonde at the rail knew *exactly* where to slide the knife in ("Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to... He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night"), and it goes straight into Vee's deepest wound—that she's too much, too greedy, too easy, that she spread her legs like a "slut" after a few dates because a smooth man worked his routine on her. Watching the ease just drain right off Pace's face when she spits *"Does your service include getting the girls home safe from here, too?"*—and then watching him do the exact thing he did under the tree in Chapter 8 when she set a boundary, except this time in quiet hurt: taking her at her word ("I'll handle that myself"), setting his untouched longneck down on the bar, and walking out into the cold without making a scene. My stomach dropped through the floor.

And the bartender! God, I love the older woman behind the bar so much: *"Tonight he brought one, on a Thursday, when folks come here to dance. Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey."* The excruciating dramatic irony here is that Vee is 100% wrong about the bar girls—Pace really hasn't touched any of them, he really did bring *her* into his Thursday community as his chosen date—and yet her gut instinct that there is another woman in his life, that she isn't the only one who knows what his pillow smells like, is *right*, just pointed in the completely wrong direction!

The detail of Vee paying too much for the smelly, belching cab with money meant for other things, not waking Cassie three feet away because she can't bear to put words to how badly she sabotaged herself, and pulling on Pace's flannel shirt that she finally had to wash until it smelled like "fabric softener and no one"... I wanted to hug her so badly.

AND THAT LAST PARAGRAPH. *"When it was light she knew what she was going to do. Not how — the how was a separate problem — but what, which was: go to Randi's."* SHE IS GOING TO RANDI'S SORORITY HOUSE ON A FRIDAY MORNING UNANNOUNCED. Not a scheduled Saturday brunch at a restaurant—she is walking straight into Randi's world (or into Randi right after Pace has talked to her?!) to ask Randi how to fix things with Pace! I am hitting next chapter immediately.
