REACTION

Oh, Vee. Oh, *Vee*. My heart was in my throat for the entire back half of this chapter, and I am sitting here with my hands over my face because normally—in 99% of romance novels—a miscommunication fight at a bar over a jealous blonde would make me roll my eyes and skim. Here? It hurt so badly I felt sick to my stomach, because every single ounce of it grew directly out of Vee's deepest, ugliest wounds AND the terrible, twisted irony of this whole book!

Let's start at the beginning, though, because the first half of this chapter was pure magic. Meg coming through with the borrowed ropers and the black keyhole shirt (and Vee's hand starting up to cover her cleavage in the mirror and putting it back down!). And then Pace waiting in the common area with his freshly polished boots, a crease pressed into his jeans, a short-sleeved knit shirt in October because he dressed for the dancing, and a battered boom box with electrical tape on the handle! I loved that concrete pad scene by the dark basketball gym *so* much. It is classic Pace: breaking down the three-step with total patience, showing her the woman's part with zero ego, and then the physical metaphor of the frame: *"Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead... Being jelly doesn't work."* And when she keeps a few inches of space because she's afraid he'll step on her foot, and he tells her, *"That's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours."* And *"There's no sorry — unless you take off a foot."* When the dance finally clicked on that empty concrete pad under the cold field lights—when the pattern came through his body before it arrived in her feet—I was swooning.

And then they get to The Rusty Nail, and the trap snaps shut, and the genius of this book is that *it isn't even the trap Pace is actually running*. Vee walks in, she's already bracing because she's visible, and suddenly all these women are hugging Pace. And while they're dancing, right when it feels so good she can't bear it, her brain goes looking for "the bill of it"—*how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join.* So when that jealous blonde in the heavy makeup strolls over from the bar to plant the poison (*"Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to... He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night"*), it hits the exact spot where her mother's voice lives (*"a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them"*, *"spread her legs like a slut"*, *"floozies"*).

Watching the good mood just drain off Pace's face when she spits that venomous line at him (*"Does your service include getting the girls home safe from here, too?"*) was physically painful. He doesn't yell, he doesn't chase her when she tells him she'll handle getting home herself and walks away—because as we know from Chapter 1 and Chapter 18, Pace's absolute rule since Daphne is that he *never* pushes past a closed door or forces a woman who says no! So he just sets his untouched beer on the bar and walks out into the cold.

And then the older bartender woman! God, give that bartender a medal: *"When a girl's had too much, Pace gets her home. Leaves her at the door. Doesn't take advantage — I'd have heard otherwise. Not that plenty haven't tried, hoping he would... Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey."* The excruciating part is that the bartender is 100% right about Pace's character at the dance hall, and Vee's realization in bed—putting on his freshly washed flannel that doesn't even smell like him anymore because she finally had to wash it, realizing *"She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be. She'd done it to a good man"*—is so raw and grown-up and heartbreaking. AND YET, at the exact same time, Vee's dark, paranoid instinct at the bar (*"a few dates and she'd spread her legs like a slut"*, that he knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to) is brushing right up against the actual secret of the pointing game, only she's convicting him of the wrong crime and now feels so guilty that she's going to throw herself even deeper in!

And that ending! She doesn't call Cassie for a ride (wasting money she doesn't have on a gross cab), she doesn't wake Cassie up three feet away in the dark, and when morning comes, where does she decide to go? *To Randi's.* On a Friday morning! Unannounced?! Does she even know where Randi lives, or is she going to the sorority house, or is she going to go to Randi to figure out how to fix things with Pace?! My thumb is already hitting next.
