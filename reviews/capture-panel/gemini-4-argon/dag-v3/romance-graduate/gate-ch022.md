REACTION

I am sitting here with tears in my eyes and my stomach on the floor. Remember when I said a while back that sometimes the best scene in a book is just two people talking? This is that chapter. And then that ending came in like a guillotine.

First—*Tuesday, October 20*! Hold on! Last night was Monday, October 19, when Randi was in this house, in this bed, eating chicken and couscous at this kitchen table! And the very next night—a Tuesday!—Vee is here for dinner, and stays the night, and is wrapped in his robe on the sun porch couch under a quilt with a fire going in the den! Pace has completely abandoned the "weekends are Vee's, weekdays are Randi's" schedule. He just wants Vee here all the time.

And what he gives Vee tonight is everything he has never given Randi. Look at the title: *Rock*. At first I wondered where "Rock" came from until he told her: *"My real name is Peter."* Peter means rock! And she is the one who gets his real name—not "Pace," the name Brooke mocked in the sorority house (*"Pace. That's actually his name?"*), not the campus persona—she gets *Peter*. And notice what happened right before he gave it to her: she asked what his mother calls him, and he answered *"My real name is Peter,"* taking his mother completely out of the answer!

Every single layer that peeled back on Pace in this chapter broke my heart and made him ten times more real:
- HE'S TWENTY-TWO! Only one year older than Vee and Randi! Because he went to college at *fifteen*!
- That story about seventh grade—teaching himself trigonometry to write a fractal graphics program, asking questions his teacher couldn't answer, and getting his desk put out in the hallway to do long-division worksheets for *an entire year*! And look at how Vee responds when he tells her that. She doesn't gush or pity him; she sets her mug down, rests her shoulder against his arm, and says: *"They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment."* Look at his reaction: *"More as if she had put her hand on a place he had not offered her and had, by accident, found the right one."*
- And then the way he opens up and talks to her about why math is beautiful! Gabriel's Horn (the shape you can fill with a cup of paint that has an infinite surface area you can never finish painting—which, hello, is also a metaphor for a person!), the fractal coastline problem ("A country's edge is as long as you have patience for"—which is literally how Pace touches and measures a woman!), and Newton's three sentences connecting a thrown ball, a falling October leaf, and the stars over the ridge! *"Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some."* I was falling in love with him right alongside her.
- Why he started powerlifting: because he was a fifteen-year-old boy living in a dormitory full of grown men! (*"Some of it was practical."* Jesus.) And what lifting became for him—stepping up to a squat bar with enough weight to bend one-inch steel so there can be no future and no past, only this heartbeat—and Vee connecting it in her own body: *"That's how you are with me... It's the lift. It's the same face."*

AND DAPHNE. We finally found out who Daphne was from Chapter 1!
My jaw dropped and my stomach turned right along with Vee's when I did the math. He was *fifteen*—a lonely kid who was less scared of leaving home at fifteen than staying in the house with his parents!—and the university assigned him a nineteen-year-old female tutor "to help the child find his feet." And it went on for two years until she turned twenty-one and dumped a seventeen-year-old boy because he couldn't get into bars with her!
Wait: remember in Chapter 1 when Pace thought, *"Daphne had built a door, once, and he had not respected it. He had been young enough to think a locked door was an invitation to be clever, and when he had gotten it open what was behind it had not thanked him and had not stayed"*?!
Look at how he tells Vee the story here: *"Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong. She was twenty-one and I was seventeen... So she went on. Which was only sensible."* While in Chapter 1, deep inside his own head, he still blames *himself* for pushing on a locked door when he was seventeen! And Vee sees right through his careful, flat defense of Daphne—sees the groomed, lonely fifteen-year-old boy who "handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck"—and keeps the ugly grown-up word behind her teeth while putting her palm flat over his chest until his breathing slows down.

And then her hand finds the scar high on the inside of his thigh—"longer than her hand"—and instead of pulling away or shutting down when she asks *"This?"*, he lays his hand right over hers, presses her palm gently down *onto* the scar, and says, *"Not tonight."* Not a wall, just a door with a time on it that isn't now! And she turns her palm up to hold his hand right over the scar and whispers, *"Peter."*

Compare this to Chapter 21 last night! Last night with Randi, she chatted about sorority flower arrangements and asked a polite five-second question about his Chicago presentation while stepping into Vee's kitchen-table "painting." Tonight with Vee, they aren't even having sex during this scene—they're sitting in the dark after dinner and sex, with his warm hand resting on her bare breast under his open robe, and he is handing her his real name, his childhood hallway, his poetry, his fifteen-year-old heartbreak, and his hand over the scar on his thigh.

And then those last three paragraphs.
*"One piece, he'd said, about the leaf and the stars. It felt like that. It had come to her all of a piece, the one and then the other, in six weeks, for no reason she had done anything to deserve. She had never been so fortunate, or so happy."*

I wanted to scream. She is lying there wrapped around him in the dark realizing that meeting Randi outside the dining hall and Pace crossing the coffee shop ten days later feel like "all one piece," like Newton's law connecting the leaf and the stars—and SHE STILL THINKS IT WAS LUCK. While her own unconscious mind just put the two halves of the plot right next to each other! When she finds out *why* they are all one piece, it is going to shatter her into a million fragments.
