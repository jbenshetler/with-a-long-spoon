Picking her up off the porch under her arms and spinning her around until she makes a "bright stupid sound," sliding her down the front of his body, not even making it to the kitchen or the hall before their coats and clothes are on the floor—and *still*, even when she's clawing at him after a week apart, he brings her off first and more than once before taking a thing for himself. WAIT A SECOND. She is eating until she's completely full at his table every night, never policing herself, and *she's actually losing weight* ("her bras had gone in a hook") because he feeds her real, lovingly cooked food—salmon with dill sauce, warm tomatoes, chicken piccata, water and wine—instead of stressed dorm cafeteria pizza and fries and soda! That detail felt so lived-in and tender I wanted to cry.

That tore me in half, because it's the exact line from the book jacket (*"When she asks what she is to him out in the world, he tells her to believe what he does, and says nothing more"*), AND because right now she's convincing herself she doesn't need the word—until Saturday morning in the bathroom!

Let's talk about Saturday morning in the bathroom!
She finally brought her overnight bag! And her brush, bobby pins, fallen green-dotted scrunchy, open face cream, beige-stained makeup sponge, and foundation bottles have completely colonized his bare, immaculate counter where usually only his razor sits squared to the edge.

Wait—WHY did his eyes hold on the counter for a second without saying a word?!
Two reasons at once! First, because Pace never puts words to things; he just smiles privately and kisses her shoulder. Second—and Vee can't possibly know this, though her gut feels the tiny tail of worry ("*Had he minded?* She couldn't tell. He hadn't said. He never said")—**Randi uses that bathroom**! If Vee leaves her makeup or pins or scrunchies out on that counter, or if Pace has to tell Vee to pack her kit back into her bag before Monday or Tuesday night so Randi doesn't walk into a bathroom colonized by Vee's foundation and face cream... OH GOD. Did he mind because of his neatness, or was he calculating the Randi logistics, or was he genuinely happy to see her things there and just too silent to say *"I like seeing your things here"*?! And then Monday night with Cassie!
First, that adorable story about Vee getting greedy over Pace's herb-and-cheese baked tomatoes, dropping a hot tomato onto the side of her bare breast, and Cassie deadpanning: *"There's only one way that burn happens. Not enough on."*

2. *"Do you love him."*
And why does Vee—who knew plainly in Pace's bed on Friday night that she loved him—hear herself go small and tell Cassie *"Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast"*?!

That Tuesday swim set where she takes *"Do you love him"* and *"Did he mind?"* into the third lane with her was some of the best writing in the entire novel. If you've ever done lap swimming (or any endurance sport where you count and breathe every third stroke against the black line on the bottom), you can feel every single yard of that seventy-five-minute set in your lungs. Watching her body strip the words off her worries one hundred at a time until *"Do you love him was a pressure under her ribs with a dread riding it"*—WHY is there a dread riding it?! Because deep down, for four strokes in the quiet middle of the set, the question from The Rusty Nail comes back up in the water:
*"she could not tell, for about four strokes, whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting, out of two months of being looked at like that."*
Because he never *says* anything! He gives her everything inside that warm house, and yet because he hasn't given her a single word—not about her things on his bathroom counter, not about what she is to him, not about love—there is a subterranean dread under her ribcage that she can only quiet by swimming until her legs go loose and her head empties out.

I loved that ending of her walking up the hill in the dark with wet hair, thinking about a sandwich, her arms feeling "like they belonged to somebody generous." And now it's Tuesday night, November 10—she's actually on campus on a Tuesday evening instead of at Pace's house! Does that mean Tuesday night is Randi's night at Pace's house, with that tower of green-dotted scrunchies sitting by the tap?!
