REACTION

Oh no, Vee. Oh *no, no, no*. My stomach was in my throat from the second that blonde came off the bar rail, and by the last paragraph I was practically yelling at the page. *Do not go to Randi's!* Not when Cassie is literally three feet away from you in the dark!

First, let me just mourn the first half of this chapter because it was *so good* it made what followed hurt ten times worse. Meg coming through with borrowed ropers and a keyhole top ("The keyhole had stopped suggesting" — YES, Meg!). Pace showing up in polished boots, creased jeans, short sleeves for the heat of dancing, freshly shaved, carrying a battered old boom box taped together at the handle! Taking her to an empty concrete pad under the field lights at seven so nobody would watch her learn! And the dancing lesson itself was pure, distilled Pace-and-Vee magic. "You have a job here. Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead... Being jelly doesn't work." That is literally everything I love about his dominance in miniature — he doesn't want a limp rag doll to push around, he wants her present, pushing back, closing the gap so he won't step on her feet, trusting his frame enough to stop guessing ahead. And "There's no sorry — unless you take off a foot" breaking into his real laugh! When something finally gave and the pattern came through his body into her feet before she could think it, I was grinning from ear to ear.

And then they walked into the Rusty Nail, and I watched every single one of Vee's deepest insecurities — and the jacket blurb! ("He gives her everything but a word for what they are") — ambush her in real time. Because she's *already* primed by her mother's flat, joyless voice (*a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them*, and later *spread her legs like a slut* and *floozies* returning like poison now that she isn't safe inside his gaze), and she's already half-afraid she made him up ("how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him"), AND she has no word for what she is to him! He introduces her as "This is Vee." So when that territorial, heavy-makeup blonde slides over with that nasty, plausible insinuation — *"Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to... He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night"* — it hits the exact bruise Vee doesn't have armor for.

Did I cringe so hard my chest hurt when Vee spat *"Does your service include getting the girls home safe from here, too?"* at him while he was standing there smiling with two beers? God, yes. Watching the ease just drain right off Pace's face — not into anger, just "something still and plain" — and then watching him shake off his friends, set his untouched beer on the bar, and walk out the door *killed* me. Because here again is Pace's absolute rule: when a woman says *I don't need you to get me home safely, I'll handle that myself* and walks away from him, he does not chase her down, grab her arm, or argue her out of her no! In fifty other books the hero would haul her out to the parking lot and pin her against his truck until she kissed him, and normally I'd hate that, and here Pace respects her boundary to the letter — and leaves! Wait... did he actually leave the property completely, or was he waiting somewhere, or did he really just drive home? The bartender said "Pace left a while ago," and Vee had to take a sketchy, rattling cab she couldn't even afford (another hit to her dwindling money right on top of the boutique heels!). That hurt in both directions — I hated what Vee said to him, and my heart broke for both of them, though a tiny part of me also flinched that she ended up alone outside a bar at closing time paying fare money meant for groceries/bills. No, she explicitly told him *I don't need you to get me home safely. I'll handle that myself.* After Daphne's closed door, Pace was never going to override a sentence that explicit.

And that older bartender!! Bless that woman forever:
*"When a girl's had too much, Pace gets her home. Leaves her at the door. Doesn't take advantage — I'd have heard otherwise. Not that plenty haven't tried, hoping he would... Tonight he brought one, on a Thursday, when folks come here to dance. Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey."*
Inject that straight into my veins — and straight into Vee's heart like a knife! Laying awake in his freshly washed flannel shirt (rationing his smell until she finally had to wash it and it came back smelling like nobody — what a detail!) realizing *"She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be. She'd done it to a good man."* I really respected Vee so much at the end of this chapter for not wallowing in self-pity or doubling down past sunrise: by morning the hot shame cools into information, she owns "She'd been wrong. She was going to fix it."
