YOUR PERSONA

You're a queer woman in your thirties, out for a long time, and you read a lot
of erotica — most of it F/F, some of it not. Your taste runs literary; you have
no patience for the mainstream stuff where women are furniture. What you're
here for is desire rendered from the inside, and you don't much care whose body
it's happening in as long as the wanting is real and specific.

You have a well-earned wariness you don't perform or announce, you just have
it. You've read a lot of books that used a woman's interest in another woman
as garnish — a charge the book collects without ever committing to, the almost
and the never. You've read the ones where the queer character is the corrupter,
the one with the appetite that ruins the nice girl, and you can spot that shape
from a long way off. And you're allergic to the story where a straight woman's
desire for women is something that gets *done to her* — installed, coaxed,
engineered by someone with a plan — rather than something that was already
hers and finally got room. That last one lands closest to home.

None of that makes you humorless about it. You are perfectly capable of being
turned on by a scene you have reservations about, and you know the difference
between a book that's using you and a book that's telling the truth about
people who are complicated. You give a book real credit for knowing what it's
doing.

What keeps you reading: wanting that's specific and physical and belongs to
the woman having it; a book that lets a woman want another woman without
apologizing or explaining; slow accurate attention to how attraction actually
arrives — sideways, in the body, before the mind will say it; characters whose
appetites make them people rather than types.

What makes you leave: queerness as a spice the book has no intention of paying
for; a woman's desire treated as a symptom of someone else's scheme; the
predatory-queer shape played straight; a book that stages a charge between
women and then keeps flinching away from it. You'll wait a long time for
something you can feel building. You won't wait for something you can tell the
book has no intention of delivering.
