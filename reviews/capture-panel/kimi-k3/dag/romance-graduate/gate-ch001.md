REACTION —

Okay, I did not expect that. I bought this book on Vee — the girl who's being set up, the one I'm supposed to fall for while knowing what she doesn't — and instead the book opens the door on the two people holding the long spoon. Randi. The glamorous friend from the jacket. And Pace. And this is *their* intimacy, fully realized, years or months or whatever deep, and it's gorgeous and it's awful and I am absolutely in.

The scene itself — the bench — is the best-written BDSM scene I've read in I don't know how long. Not because it's elaborate, but because every single beat is doing two jobs at once. The apple slices, cut thin because she'll eat thin and not thick, laid cut-side-up because she eats faster that way — that's devotion and it's also husbandry. That's a man managing a woman's intake with love in his hands, and the prose lets it sit there being both without telling me which one to feel. I kept thinking about the "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" line from the jacket while reading him extract that real, broken, subterranean "please" out of her — because yes, she said every word, and also he *engineered* the room where that was the only word left. The book knows that. It's the whole book, I think.

And then the bathroom. God, the bathroom. The lock clicking over for the first time ever in his house, and then the entire interior monologue of a woman watching her own mask reassemble itself in the mirror. "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." The face as the decoy that takes all the being-wanted so the real self never has to be touched — and tonight the real self got touched, got *found*, and her response is not joy, it's a deadbolt. The one he found is going back behind the face and "she was not coming out again." That's the most psychologically true thing I've read in a romance in years, and it's also terrifying, because I know from the jacket that this woman — this woman who just swore never to be seen again — is about to steer a twenty-one-year-old into this man's path. Is the Vee plan her closing the door? Is Vee the offering she makes so she never has to be the one on the bench again?

Also — small things I clocked: he never takes his own pleasure, and holds that fact like a fact and sets it down, and it's presented as courtly and it also reads as control of the entire chessboard. "Daphne had built a door, once, and he had not respected it" — a whole previous casualty in one sentence, filed under *lessons learned*. He respects doors now. He just builds rooms where you lock yourself in and then hand him the key. And Randi pressing the bottle-cap edge under her thumbnail while she "recovers" — she self-soothes with pain she administers to herself, quietly, where he won't see. These two are so much sicker and sadder than any billionaire-and-intern book has ever let its people be.

The heat was real — the scene is explicit, unhurried, embodied, none of the euphemism drift I can't stand — but honestly what has me is that I just watched a love scene that is also a horror scene and the book never once flinched or picked a side. If this is what the two predators feel from the inside, I need to know what it feels like when Vee walks into it.
