REACTION

Okay, I need a minute. And also I need to talk about the name thing, because I got to "There you are, Randi" and literally put the book down on my chest and stared at the ceiling. The blurb said Vee. The jacket said Vee is the girl, Randi is the friend who steers her. And this whole first chapter — this exquisitely built, devastating scene — is *Randi*. So either we've jumped to some other point in the timeline, or "Vee" is something else entirely, or the woman on that bench is the one who's supposed to be doing the steering, and the game the blurb promised is already older and deeper than I thought. I don't know yet. I love not knowing yet.

And the scene itself. God. This is the thing I've been hunting for. He asks. He asks *constantly* — "tell me your word," "you okay," "you with me," checking her hands and shoulders, and her answer comes back "they're FINE, Pace," half-exasperated, and he smiles. That's the register I want: the dominance is total and the consent is load-bearing, not decoration. He undresses her one garment at a time on one knee and folds each thing. He waits for the moment her actual weight comes forward against him before he does anything else. He notices she eats apple slices faster cut-side-up and preps them that way. That's the attention that undoes me — not the spanking, the *slicing*.

The begging sequence wrecked me in the best way. Him recognizing that her first "please" is still the waiter voice, still performance, and quietly saying "no" and waiting for the real one to come up from below — and then the chapter *going inside her* afterward, the bathroom, the mirror, the face as an apparatus she's been hiding behind since she was thirteen, and the realization that being seen is the thing she can't survive. That flinch at "you're beautiful." The bottle-cap edge under her own thumbnail that she doesn't notice doing. This book is doing something psychological under the heat and I am *here* for it.

But. The blurb's last line — "every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — is sitting on my chest through this whole chapter. Because yes, she says yes, she has a safeword, he checks... and also he *built the bench* and let her think it was a table, and he's clearly running a program on her, peeling her open on a schedule he designed and she didn't. It's consensual and it's also *engineered*, and the book knows it, and that's the tension the jacket sold me. The "not ever" in the bathroom — her deciding he'll never get the real her again, even as he's in the next room tenderly certain he just met her — that's the knife. And Pace's little interior moment about her appetite, "all he had done was find it," is tender and also faintly proprietary in a way I am deliberately not letting myself fully trust.

Also the end, with the bench waiting "for the next time" — I felt that in my spine.
