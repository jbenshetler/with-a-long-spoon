REACTION

Okay, I did not expect the book to open here. The jacket sells me Vee — twenty-one, starving, doesn't know there's a plan — and instead the first chapter drops me into a scene between Pace and *Randi*, the glamorous friend, the one doing the steering. Which means the book just showed me the inside of the machine before it shows me the girl the machine is for. That's a hell of a choice, and I trust it, because it means every later scene with Vee will be sitting on top of what I watched tonight. The bench will exist. The apple slices will exist. I'll know what "courtly, exact, he asks before he takes" costs and how it's engineered.

And the scene itself — God. It's *good*. It's written from inside his attention, and his attention is genuinely erotic: the apple cut thin because she eats thin slices, the cut sides up because she eats them faster that way, the hand that approaches her breasts and turns back three times until she stops pretending to eat. That's desire rendered as *craft*, literally, he's a woodworker, and the bench is his thesis statement. I was pulled all the way in. The edging sequence is patient and specific and the moment her "please" changes register — from the sorority please to the one that comes from below — I felt it land the way the book wanted it to.

But here's the thing that has me sitting up: the book is doing something much more dangerous than a hot spanking scene. Randi — who I met on the jacket as the confident schemer, the one with the plan — is the one who *breaks* on the bench. She's the one who gets seen past the face, who begs, who locks the bathroom door for the first time, who stands at the mirror and decides "not ever," who rebuilds the mask while he sits on the bed quietly happy about her. The corrupter shape I was braced for — Randi as appetite, Randi as predator steering the nice girl — just got complicated to hell in chapter one. She's not the spider. She's someone whose perfection was armor and who just had it taken off without quite consenting to *that part*, even though she consented to everything else. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — yes, and the book knows exactly what's horrifying about that sentence.

Also: she presses the bottle cap into her nail bed and doesn't know she's doing it. That detail scared me more than the bench did.

And I clocked Daphne. He "did not respect a door once" and learned. The book wants me to find that reassuring — and it is, on its face, good consent hygiene — but given the jacket tells me he and Randi are running an unconsented plan on Vee, that little paragraph reads less like reassurance and more like the author showing me the exact seam where his ethics stop. He's scrupulous about everything except the one thing that matters most. That's "psychologically merciless" doing what the jacket promised.

What I'm suspicious of: whether Vee's version of this arc — the queer one, the wanting Randi — will be given the same interiority Randi just got, or whether it'll stay at the level of "never lets herself notice." One chapter in, Randi has more interiority than most female characters in this genre get in a whole book. That's a very good sign. The pacing question doesn't worry me — this was a full meal, not a deferred one.
