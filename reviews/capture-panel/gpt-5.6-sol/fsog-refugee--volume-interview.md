**T1**

Vee leaves me aching for her and fiercely on her side. I love how much desire she discovers in herself—how playful, hungry, intelligent, and brave she becomes when she is not trying to fit somebody else’s idea of her. None of that feels false just because the circumstances were engineered. Her choices are still hers. But she ends the volume having surrendered a question she had every right to ask, because returning to Pace felt safer than holding out for an answer. That hurts. I don’t blame her; I feel protective of her. She has offered both Pace and Randi an extraordinary amount of trust and tenderness without knowing what they have done with it.

Pace leaves me deeply attached and deeply angry. He is so close to the man I have spent years wanting to read: dominant without cruelty, attentive without making a performance of his attentiveness, able to make room for Vee’s desire rather than overwrite it. He asks, waits, notices, feeds her, fixes things quietly, and gives her dignity at the moments when she expects humiliation. I believe his care. That is exactly why his deception feels personal to me. And when Vee finally asks for words, he uses all that beautiful care to avoid giving her the clarity she needs. I don’t think he is secretly empty or that everything between them is a trick. I think he loves her in action and is still choosing his own defenses over her informed choice. Right now, I want to shake him.

Randi is the one I feel most tangled about. I believe she genuinely loves Vee. I also think she understands Vee’s vulnerabilities with frightening precision and repeatedly uses that understanding to steer her while preserving deniability. She comforts Vee, opens her, helps her claim her wanting—and redirects her whenever that wanting threatens the arrangement. Vee protects Randi’s hidden pain while Randi continues harvesting Vee’s confidences for a purpose Vee never agreed to. So I feel tenderness toward Randi’s wound, but very little patience with what she has made that wound permission to do.

**T2**

Yes. I wanted the truth to enter the room.

I did not necessarily need a complete reckoning or a neat resolution in Volume One. I could accept the relationship breaking open at the end and leaving the consequences for Book Two. But I needed the concealed plan to stop functioning merely as dramatic irony for me while Vee remained inside it unknowingly. The whole volume taught me to distinguish consent to an encounter from consent to the structure surrounding it, and then refused to let Vee make that distinction for herself.

The book also still owes me an answer to Vee’s request for relational clarity. “I love how good we are together” is not “I love you.” A drawer, a dress, a meal, a flannel shirt, and exquisite attention are not an answer once she has explicitly said that she needs words. They are meaningful; they may even be how Pace most truthfully loves. But they cannot be used to declare the question obsolete. The ending’s suggestion that nothing needs saying is the one thing I absolutely do not accept. Something needs saying twice over: what she is to him, and what he and Randi have done.

**T3**

It sits badly enough that it changes the quality of my enthusiasm, but not my decision. I still buy Book Two immediately.

Pace does not get absolved for me because he treated Vee beautifully within the encounters he helped arrange. That care is real, and so is the deception. In fact, his sensitivity makes the silence harder to excuse: this is a man who can detect a shift in her breathing, recognize shame before she names it, and understand the importance of giving her back a pencil. He cannot plausibly be unaware that the hidden arrangement would matter to her. He is choosing not to know what he already knows.

And yes, Volume One lets him end in the most comfortable possible position. Vee returns. She trusts him. She gives up the demand for words. He receives all the intimacy without having to risk either confession. Nothing lands on him yet, while the emotional cost lands on Vee—who believes the problem is her need—and on Cassie, who is losing access to her friend’s whole life.

That makes me angry, but it does not yet make me feel the book approves of him. My own record shows the warning running underneath the romance too consistently: the hidden design, the lecture reversal, the repeated evasions, the difference between what Vee asks and what she is given. I am buying Book Two because I believe the book has deliberately incurred that debt.

But my purchase comes with a condition, emotionally. I need the consequences to be real. I need Vee’s pleasure not to be used as retroactive permission. I need Pace’s tenderness not to function as moral immunity. And I need him to understand that being safe with her body while denying her the truth about her own relationship is not enough. If Book Two asks me to call his disregard romantic because he loved her all along, that is where it loses me.
