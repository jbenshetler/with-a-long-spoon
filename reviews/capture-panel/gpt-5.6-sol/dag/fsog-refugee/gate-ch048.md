REACTION

Oh, Vee. This hurt, because she finally asks the right question sideways and then accepts an answer to a different one.

“Is this what we are now?” is not really about Chili’s. It is not even about being seen in public, though she needs that too. She is asking whether she exists in his life outside the beautiful sealed world of his house and his bed, and Pace hears the practical complaint and solves it practically. Of course he does. Then the restaurant goes badly, and because Vee is already frightened she turns every distracted glance into proof that he is ashamed of her. I felt her doing it and wanted to reach across the table and say, no, look again. The fork. The screens. The music. His shoulders coming down in the truck. This is not a man sulking because you made him take you out. This is a man being overwhelmed and trying desperately not to make his discomfort your problem.

And his explanation got me: “In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can’t hear you. Somewhere quieter, I can. And I do.” That is so specifically Pace. It is tender, vulnerable without turning into a performance, and it answers the cruelty she threw at him without punishing her for throwing it. He tells her what happened and then, most importantly, tells her he is listening now. I absolutely believed him.

But I hated—hated—the little warm story she immediately made about having “forgiven” him. He did not need forgiveness for being overloaded in a restaurant. She accused him unfairly, including rewriting the Rusty Nail when some part of her knew perfectly well that she was the one who froze him out. Pace was generous enough not to demand an apology, but that does not make her version true. She keeps taking his refusal to fight as proof that she has correctly understood what happened. She has not.

And then the sex was ferocious in exactly the way I wanted it to be. Her wanting his control to break, his patience winding her tighter because he will not spend himself before he has attended to her, and then that sudden loss of restraint when he comes up to her—I felt all of it. This did not read as him imposing roughness on her. She wanted precisely this, and the contrast only worked because his control has been trustworthy for so long. The moment he finally ran out of patience was a full-body yes for me.

But it also scared me how efficiently the sex drowned the question. Vee knows it is happening. The word rises, orgasm takes it under, and she lets that happen because bodily certainty is easier than risking an answer. That line between sex revealing truth and sex keeping her from it is becoming the whole danger of them. Pace can make her feel completely wanted without ever naming what the wanting means. Worse, he is withholding the fact that would change the meaning of every possible name.

“She belonged to him” should have thrilled me—and part of it did, because chosen belonging is exactly my weakness—but the next question cut straight through it: does he belong to her? Not in the way she thinks. She is lying there believing she has reached the final locked room in him, while Randi is already inside a room Vee does not even know exists. Pace did lose control with her. He does love something here, unmistakably. But Vee’s fear that she cannot prove any of it beyond the edges of his bed is not paranoia. It is her body finding the shape of missing information before her mind knows what is missing.

I am furious with Pace now in a colder way than before. He listened beautifully to the question he could hear and remained silent about the answer only he knows she needs. When she says, “If we’re just sex, I’d like to know,” he cannot honestly answer by taking her to dinner. Not while Randi exists. Not while he knows Vee does not know. He is treating emotional naming as something he may withhold until he is ready, while accepting more and more of Vee’s belonging every week.

And still—I want him. I want them. That is what makes this unbearable. The tenderness is real. The listening is real. The heat is real. Vee’s doubt is real too, and for once the book is not letting the warmth erase it. She is no longer merely waiting for him to say “I love you.” She is beginning to understand that she cannot even name the relationship she is already arranging her life around. Something is finally pushing up from under the rug, and I need it to keep pushing.
