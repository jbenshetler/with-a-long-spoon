He gave her the easiest drawer without announcing the sacrifice, without making her thank him, without treating her presence as clutter he graciously tolerates. That is so intimate it almost hurts to look at.

But I cannot float in any of it, because this chapter has made his silence the love language and the weapon at exactly the same time.

Vee tells him about Randi with this delighted innocence—her new friend, their brunches, the funny mouth kisses—and he knows every piece she does not. He knows that Randi is his lover. Vee is offering him the perfect opening without knowing she is doing it, and he walks over and kisses her instead. Then she decides she can live inside everything he does without needing him to say the word. That is romantic in the smallest frame: the spin at the door, her pleasure first, dinner afterward, returning to her on the couch, making space for her things. Yes, those are ways of saying love. They count. But words are not decorative when another person needs information only words can give. He cannot make a drawer that means *you belong here* and expect that to answer the question he is refusing to let her know she must ask.

“There was no note. There was never a note.” That landed like a warning. Vee reads him beautifully, but she is also learning to treat his silence as depth and supply the safest possible meaning herself. First the counter: he said nothing, so she worried he minded. Then the drawer: he said nothing, so she understood he wanted her there. She is becoming fluent in his acts, and that fluency is going to make the withheld truth so much worse, because she trusts that whatever he does not say can be read in what he lovingly does.

And meanwhile Randi’s pastry sweetness was still on Vee’s mouth a week ago. Vee tells Pace about the kiss as a funny little custom, and his grin says he understands more about that sweetness than she does. I hate that for her. I hate that the two people she loves are each receiving her innocent version of the other and letting her keep it.

I want somebody to notice her as a whole person, not simply delight that she eats everything Pace puts down.

This is precisely the warmth that makes the betrayal dangerous. If Pace were merely using her, I could leave without pain. He loves her. I believe that completely. He is building her a home while withholding the fact that someone else already lives inside the relationship.
