Oh, she called him back.

That whole truck scene did exactly what I’ve been waiting for this book to do: it made her the author of the moment. He gave her privacy without turning it into a display of his virtue, and then she looked at his back in the rain and decided privacy was not what she wanted. Not because he persuaded her. Not because he read her body and declared he knew better. She had to knock. She had to beckon him in. She had to hold his eyes and show him, deliberately, what she wanted him to see. I could feel every button.

And he did not touch her. God. That was the hot part. He looked because she asked him to look, wanted her openly, and stayed still enough that the entire thing remained hers. “His face was want and it was gladness” nearly undid me. Gladness is the word. Not conquest, not smugness, not the satisfaction of having maneuvered her there. He was glad to receive what she chose to give him. This is the intensity I want: a man wholly focused on her, with enough control not to grab the thing being offered.

Then she discovered his hand was shaking and changed the moment. That made it mean something. She wanted more—she was already reaching for more—and then she actually noticed him. He had stood outside freezing so she could choose without being watched, and now she brought his cold hand under the shirt and held it against her warmth. That is tenderness passing both ways. She isn’t merely the object of his exquisite care anymore. She can see him, stop, and care for him too. I melted.

Even before that, the chapter kept finding the exact point where care could have become humiliation and refusing to humiliate her. Her bare foot in his hand could have been mortifying; he simply fixed what hurt. The cherry pit could have made her feel ignorant; he quietly put his own beside hers and let her learn without shrinking. And with the dress, he did not offer to buy it, rescue her, interrogate the wanting, or turn her confession into leverage. “That sounds like a beautiful dress.” I am now horribly suspicious that the russet dress will appear later, but in that moment he let the desire exist without taking possession of it.

The hike itself also got under my skin because Vee’s appetite was allowed to be so bodily and so innocent at once. Tired legs, sharp mustard, cold water, dark cherries, wanting the next bite before she had swallowed this one—she is learning that need can rise in her and be answered without shame. “The plain animal rightness of needing something and being handed it” is her whole awakening, sexual and otherwise. Pace keeps feeding her, but more importantly, he keeps leaving the wanting recognizably hers.

That complicates the gorgeousness because Vee’s desire is real, absolutely real, but the secret plan is still shaping the space in which she discovers it. And that ending—no. She had something to say, and the siren physically tore it away. Now she is walking into the dorm in his enormous flannel, soaked to the skin, carrying the evidence of what she chose, and Cassie is presumably somewhere inside. I need that encounter immediately. I also need to know what Vee was going to say, because she knew the small words would be standing in for something enormous. I am past casually interested. I am following her through the doors.
