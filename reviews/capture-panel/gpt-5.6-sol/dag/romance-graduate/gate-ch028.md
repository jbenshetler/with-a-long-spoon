REACTION — Oh, Vee. Oh, no. The jealousy itself didn’t bother me—I believed every ugly, instantaneous inch of it. She has only just begun to trust that being wanted might not be a clerical error, and then she walks into a room full of women who already know his body in motion. Of course one poisoned little sentence gets straight through her. Of course her mother’s voice is waiting to turn fear into *you spread your legs like a slut*. That isn’t manufactured conflict by unsent text; it’s an old wound finding exactly the right knife.

And Pace leaving absolutely hurt. He didn’t chase her, didn’t overpower the boundary she had just thrown up, didn’t stage some public demonstration of innocence. He took her seriously and went. That is consistent with everything I love about him, and it made the whole thing worse in the best way.

The dancing was hot. Not generically “big man leads” hot—the lesson understood why this particular woman would find a firm frame erotic. That is exactly the question I need this book to know exists. I don’t need it answered yet, but I need Vee capable of asking it. Her desire isn’t making her stupid; it’s making the stakes terrifying.

The bartender’s correction was almost painfully satisfying. He takes drunk women home because he is safe. He doesn’t sleep with them. He doesn’t normally bring dates. The women weren’t warning Vee that she was interchangeable—they were jealous because she plainly wasn’t. And Vee gets all the way to the hardest truth without Pace having to explain himself: she punished him for the very goodness she loves in him. That is adult emotional movement. She behaved badly, but the book doesn’t infantilize her, excuse her, or keep her wrong for six chapters to manufacture suspense.

But that ending has me making an actual distressed noise. She could call Cassie, the person who would come without arranging her, and again she doesn’t. She goes to Randi—the woman who knows Pace’s entire history with that room, who can soothe Vee with privileged information while pretending it is intuition. Randi is about to be handed Vee’s guilt, jealousy, and desire to repair things, and I do not trust what she will do with any of it. Her love is real. So is her agenda. This is exactly how Cassie gets displaced: not through one dramatic betrayal, but because Vee increasingly takes the most vulnerable thing to the person best equipped to shape it.

Also: Vee still doesn’t know that while she was awake in Pace’s bed thinking she had stumbled by chance into two miracles, both miracles had selected her beforehand. Now she is going to one secret architect for help repairing things with the other. I am already turning the page.
