Oh, she did it. And Pace wasn’t there, and Randi didn’t tell her how, and her mother’s voice wasn’t the one that got to decide what it meant. She made the room turn by choosing, in the actual moment, to lengthen her stride. That distinction matters so much to me. The dress may be Pace’s work and the shoes may carry Randi’s fingerprints, but the second step was Vee’s.

And yes, it was hot. Not because everyone could literally see anything—they couldn’t—but because she knew exactly what the open slit meant, felt the room understand without being allowed to say it, and kept her face composed while her body lit up beneath the silk. “A girl with a certificate” absolutely murdered me. Outwardly decorous, inwardly running hot from the fact that she had deliberately given a roomful of strangers the sight of her body. This is exhibitionism arriving as self-possession, before she has the language to file it under kink. I love that the chapter lets pride and arousal become impossible for her to separate. Why should she have to separate them yet?

The mother-shaped absence hurt. Her relief isn’t that her mother couldn’t afford the flight; it’s that Vee got to cross that stage without monitoring the face of the woman who taught her shame. Cassie understood enough not to force an explanation, even when Vee couldn’t tell her the real one. God, Cassie. She takes the picture, gets bright-eyed over the achievement, calls her “streetlight,” and offers to let Vee “be famous at me.” She gives Vee witness without possession. And sending Pace the uncropped photograph while keeping the heat of the walk for herself felt like a tiny, necessary reclamation. I’m still desperate for the secret to break, but this chapter earned the delay. It gave me the entrance I’d been waiting for and made it belong to Vee—not to the conspiracy arranged around her.
