REACTION — Oh, they rehearsed this. Randi was texting him while Vee fought the problem, Pace came in on cue, Cassie unknowingly supplied the line that made his approach look spontaneous, and then Randi sat there with both hands around an untouched coffee while her lover looked through her at the woman they had chosen. This was not a meet-cute. It was theater staged for one person who did not know she was the audience, the lead, and the prize.

And it worked on me anyway. That is the awful part.

Pace giving Vee the pencil back at exactly the point where she could solve the problem herself was devastatingly attractive. Of course that is how he courts. He does not perform intelligence at her; he uses his intelligence to find the place where hers has been trapped and opens a door there. He asks what her professor taught her. He reads her abandoned work and tells her it was right. He listens to the research idea nobody has invited her to articulate. Then he asks what draws her to economics, and suddenly she is not merely a beautiful woman being selected—she is a mind he wants to watch move. I completely understand why her body registered what he had done before she could name it. Mine did too.

The cuffs were hot. The forearms were hot. “Asked for it with his eyes first” was very hot. But the real charge was him returning the pencil. I have read approximately nine million scenes in which the brilliant alpha solves the heroine’s problem to establish that he is the most competent man in the room. Pace’s competence is in knowing where to stop. That is so much sexier, and so much more dangerous, because it makes Vee feel more fully herself in his presence. He does not overwhelm her; he gives her access to herself. How is she supposed to defend against that?

And Vee borrowing Randi’s introduction—then abruptly wanting her own name back—hurt. She is already substituting Randi’s social machinery for her own, trying on the little practiced swerve because Randi made it look like belonging. But “Vee” comes after because she still exists under the borrowed poise. I wanted to grab hold of that moment and keep her there. She can imitate Randi without vanishing into her. Maybe.

Randi, meanwhile. God. Watching her give Pace the bare “Randi” absolutely lit me up. No bit, no social flourish, no pretending they have never met. It is the tiniest possible private acknowledgment: *I know you; you know me; we are doing this.* And then she has to sit beside him while he deploys all that exact attention on Vee and treats her like somebody’s great-aunt. She asked for this. She chose Vee. She is getting precisely what delighted her on the lawn. Yet the cup keeps rising without a sip. Something in her is not enjoying the substitution as cleanly as the fantasy promised.

I cannot decide whether she is jealous of Vee for receiving Pace’s focused attention, jealous of Pace for being allowed to want Vee directly, or aroused and frightened by watching the two people she wants begin to form a bond that does not need her. Probably all three. Whatever it is, she is already learning the problem with engineering a connection between two people: once it becomes real, you are no longer the author of it.

Cassie sees far too much. She noticed Randi texting. She noticed the difference in Pace’s looks. She identified the whole emotional geometry without knowing the hidden relationship, and because she never fills silence just to be agreeable, every observation lands clean. The healer joke also told me exactly what kind of scraps Vee has accepted before: staying up until two doing the sustaining work so some mediocre man can continue having fun. Pace’s attention feels miraculous partly because he lets her do her own work without making her carry his. That is a brutally low bar, and he clears it with such grace that it looks like the moon.

Then Randi takes Vee’s phone. That bothered me. Not enough to rupture the scene, because Vee lets her and their new intimacy clearly includes this kind of excited friend interference, but Randi is not a neutral girlfriend helping decode a text. She is reading a message from her lover inside a courtship she secretly arranged, then telling Vee what his desire means and pushing her to answer. “You’re not unsure” is true—and Randi has spent nine days manufacturing the conditions in which that certainty could bloom. She is not merely opening a door anymore. She is standing behind Vee with a hand between her shoulder blades.

The right-angle explanation came perilously close to making me roll my eyes. Yes, I see the triad metaphor: two difficult things become plain when joined in another dimension; an old truth that seemed useless turns out to be the key; substitution solves the circle. It is elegant, but the book put a fluorescent arrow over it and made Pace explain the relationship before Vee knows there is a relationship. Trust the image. I was already there.

But then the froyo date softened me completely. Her cup getting too big because her mother’s voice simply fails to arrive—oh. Pace does not tell her to eat. He does not praise her for eating. He just makes a world for an hour in which appetite is not being policed, and she discovers she can want too much and carry napkins and let it drip. That is the feeding I loved in chapter one, except here Vee has built her own cup.

“This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time” should not be romantic; it should be an alarm. It was both. His answer was perfect because he neither denied the loneliness nor made her disclosure serve his ego. He gave her a minute of dignity and then handed her the tart as a private joke. “Your tart’s holding up” made my chest ache. So did the fact that Randi handed him the opportunity to be this lovely to Vee without giving Vee the one fact that would let her understand what she is entering.

At one in the morning I would be texting: the setup is appalling, the date is adorable, and that is exactly why I cannot put it down. They have not fabricated Vee’s attraction. They have found the precise conditions in which something real can grow, then hidden the greenhouse.
