Cassie watching Vee instead of the dress nearly undid me. That is love without appetite in it—she doesn’t need the details, doesn’t need to steer the story, doesn’t even care about the object making Vee glow. She only needs to know whether her friend had a good night. Meanwhile Randi turns everything into more: the dress, the shoes, the fantasy of entering late, Vee herself. That little disappearance behind her face when Vee says she watched him sleep hurt. She is putting herself into it one buckle at a time.

But the money made my stomach drop. Vee didn’t choose those shoes because she could afford them; she turned them into a debt the dress was “owed” so she wouldn’t have to admit they were a want she could not pay for. That is her mother’s shame wearing a glamorous new outfit: still telling her desire must justify itself as necessity. And Randi stood beside her, bought shoes costing multiples more without looking, and apparently saw none of the white-knuckling. Maybe she truly doesn’t understand money at this scale. Maybe she understands Vee less than she thinks. Either way, this friendship is now literally costing Vee money she does not have while making her feel richer than she has ever felt.

The Simpson’s paradox lecture was almost comically ominous, but it worked on me: Pace alone, Randi alone, each relationship looking like a gift; put the same facts together and the answer reverses. Nobody touched one number. That is exactly what the eventual reveal threatens to do to Vee’s memories. Nothing she chose will become unchosen, nothing tender will become unfelt—and the total may still mean something radically different.

I want the induction immediately. Vee is going to enter believing the dress and shoes make her visible, while Pace and Randi already know every hidden structure holding the picture up. I am thrilled for her, furious for her, and extremely attracted to Randi against my better judgment. So, yes, the book has me exactly where it wants me.
