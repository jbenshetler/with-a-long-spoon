REACTION

Oh, this got me. The dinner itself more than the kiss, honestly: hot bread, too many little dishes, her hunger arriving all at once, and then the almost indecent relief of letting somebody else choose. “May I choose?” is doing an enormous amount to me because it is genuinely courteous, genuinely attentive, and—because I know there is a plan—quietly terrifying. He isn’t forcing her anywhere. He is making surrender feel like rest, and for her it *is* rest. That is so much more dangerous than pressure would be.

The food hurt. Watching her eat without watching herself eat felt tender enough that I almost forgot Pace absolutely notices how women eat; I have not forgotten the sliced apple. Vee thinks he doesn’t notice because he gives her the dignity of not being observed, while observation is practically his governing appetite. That discrepancy is erotic and unnerving in exactly equal measure.

I also noticed that she loses the transcript again. It happened with Randi at Chi Latte, and now it happens with Pace: the words disappear, but the feeling of being attended to remains. That repetition doesn’t feel accidental or decorative. These two are each making her feel so chosen that she stops keeping records. Given what I know, I want to reach into the book and tell her to keep the words—while also understanding completely why she can’t.

And the kiss: yes. The rule coming out of her mouth, his immediate acceptance, and then her choosing to cross her own line after he has genuinely left it alone—that worked on me. The little space he leaves is hotter than a grab would have been. Her grin inside the kiss made me absurdly happy for her, which is exactly how this book is going to murder me, because I know the conditions under which this happiness was arranged.

Also: the repeated emphasis on his right hand has put a pin in my brain. I don’t know what it means yet, but the book wants me to notice it, and I do.
