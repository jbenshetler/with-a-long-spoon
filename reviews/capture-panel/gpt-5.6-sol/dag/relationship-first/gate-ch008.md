REACTION

Oh, Cassie. I love her now. “They will never know the love of a woman” made me laugh, but it was her keeping the joke just for Vee that got me—the instinct not to make Vee’s humiliation into another public event, even in the act of defending her. And then she sees Vee surface with something wrong in her face and doesn’t demand an explanation, doesn’t make her narrate her pain so Cassie can prove she cares. She just shoots water at her. That is such precise friendship. That is knowing someone.

The underwater passage hurt. “She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem” is the whole wound, really. Not that she hates her body, exactly; that everyone else keeps turning it into something she has to manage. I felt the enormous relief of her being beneath visibility, and then the grief of remembering she once inhabited herself without all this surveillance. The chapter let me feel that without Pace or Randi standing nearby to interpret her, which I badly needed. Vee has a life and a history that aren’t merely raw material for their plan.

And the ugly shriek-laugh! I wanted that for her so much. It may be the freest she has seemed yet, and Cassie brought it out without seducing her, studying her, or deciding what she secretly needs. Given how ominous Cassie’s “Goodbye” felt, this made me frightened for the friendship. I can already feel Vee being pulled toward people who make her feel newly chosen and away from the person who has apparently been choosing her quietly for years.

I’m also newly wary of the book’s repeated language about faces, seeing, and bodies getting ahead of minds. It still works on me, but I’m noticing the machinery now. This chapter mostly escaped that feeling because the friendship had room to breathe and Cassie’s care remained beautifully unannounced.
