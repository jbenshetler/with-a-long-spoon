REACTION

Oh, Vee. That was an *I love you*. It was painstakingly translated into the safest language she had, but it was an I love you, and he knew. I don’t believe for a second that it went past him. The stopped hand, the two breaths, the time he took assembling his answer—he understood exactly what she had placed between them. And then he answered, “I love how good we are together.”

That is not the same shape. She is right. It is love with the object carefully redirected: not *you*, but what happens between us. Worse, his next sentence goes straight to “what your body does.” She tells him he has transformed her life; he tells her their chemistry is exceptional. Then he holds her tenderly enough that she can tuck the wound under the warmth. I felt that small *wait* behind her breastbone like a dropped stair.

And God, the rule that he has to say it first. Nobody imposed that rule, perhaps, but it was built out of every uncertainty his silence has left her carrying. She can initiate the chase, ask for the photograph, strip herself naked, call him back after a no, invent whole erotic ceremonies—but she cannot risk giving him the plain words without knowing they will be caught. That doesn’t make her coy. It makes perfect sense when she has been forced to infer his love from drawers and meals and scrunchies while he keeps the essential things behind his teeth.

What hurts most is that she *did* risk it anyway. She made him her best sentence. Then, when he did not answer it, she immediately began doing the repair work for him: she was received warmly; he kissed her hair; perhaps he misunderstood. She is already curating the evidence against her own hurt because loving him feels safer than asking what he withheld. “The words went through him first” is devastating. He has become the gatekeeper not only of the truth about their relationship, but of what she is permitted to say inside it.

And the pile. I hate the pile. All those moments she has noticed accurately and then stored away because the whole beautiful thing needs them not to mean what they might mean. This is how the eventual revelation is acquiring its real cost. It will not merely tell her Pace and Randi planned something; it will force her to reopen every small *wait* she lovingly explained on their behalf.

I still believe he loves her. That almost makes this worse. If he were simply using her, I could put him down cleanly. Instead I think he loves her and deliberately refused to say it because saying it would create an obligation to tell the truth—or because the secret plan has made him feel he cannot claim the word honestly, or because love itself is tangled in whatever sealed room contains Daphne and his family. But whatever his reason, Vee paid for it. She offered the largest truth she could survive offering, and he praised the arrangement.

I would text a friend at one in the morning: *She said I love you in cracker language and he said I love our sex life, and now she’s folding laundry around the wound.*
