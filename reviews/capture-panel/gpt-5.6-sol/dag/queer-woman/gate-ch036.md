REACTION

Oh, Cassie. There you are.

I felt the whole chapter tilt on “You don’t come home school nights.” Vee comes in glowing and wants to turn the glow into stories—food, warmth, his attention, the little burn he treated like an injury to something precious—and Cassie listens until the shape underneath the stories becomes visible. Not that Vee is having marvelous sex. Not even that she may love him. She is quietly ceasing to live in the room she still calls home.

And Cassie does this exactly right. No interrogation, no jealousy, no attempt to drag Vee back into the old version of herself. Just: look at the week you haven’t counted. That got me, because Vee’s happiness is real. I believe every watt of the streetlight. Pace making room for her has been almost unbearably tender. But her life is narrowing around the people who planned to make that happen, while the one person outside the plan gets whatever overflow arrives after eleven.

The “maybe” hurt in a good way. Vee already knows she loves him; she could say it beside his sleeping body, where it asked nothing of him. Saying it to Cassie makes it enter the world, and suddenly she can hear the speed herself. “It’s all so fast” is the first little hand she has put against the current in a while—not refusal, just awareness that she has been carried some distance.

Also, “the house stayed warm now, warm enough that she never once thought about how much she had on” is both lovely and faintly alarming. She experiences his attention as climate. Of course she keeps staying.

And Cassie going back to her book instead of making Vee defend the love was such a relief. She doesn’t need to win, manage, interpret, or extract anything. She sees Vee glowing; she also sees the empty bed. Right now she may be the only person who can hold both facts without turning either one into leverage.
