Oh, Vee. The instant she decided she had “forgiven him,” I wanted to reach into the book and turn her gently but firmly toward the actual direction of the harm. He had nothing to be forgiven for at Chili’s. She chose the loudest, most overstimulating possible place as an emergency test of whether he would be seen with her, watched him struggle to process the room, translated every sign of that struggle into rejection, accused him of punishment, and then accepted his explanation as though it were an apology. That is such a painfully convincing piece of self-protection: if he did something wrong and she forgave him, then she has regained control. She doesn’t have to face the much scarier truth that he may be tender, attentive, sexually helpless for her, and still unwilling to name what she is to him.

And he heard the complaint she could say, not the one she meant. “Where do you want to go tonight?” is a decent, responsive answer to *we never go anywhere*.
