REACTION — Oh, Vee. The way she understands him is so generous it almost hurts to watch, because she takes every carefully flattened fact he gives her and restores the human cost without making him perform that cost for her. Crossing the counter instead of saying sorry was exactly right. She doesn’t demand grief from him so she can soothe it; she simply commits her weight. I felt that.

But I do not believe him. “My one friend. There isn’t anyone else” is a staggering sentence when Randi exists. Whatever private category he has put Randi in—lover, collaborator, co-conspirator, something older and stranger than friend—it lets him give Vee a technically defensible answer that is emotionally false. The tenderness of Vee’s interpretation makes the concealment feel worse. She is spending her extraordinary empathy on a picture he knows is missing its central figure.

Meanwhile Randi wants him, has him, and is being kept perfectly well—just invisibly. I wanted to reach into the kitchen and say, sweetheart, the exception is already inside your life.

He makes safety tangible. He also controls the architecture of what Vee knows. Those two things are both real, and this chapter made them ache against each other.
