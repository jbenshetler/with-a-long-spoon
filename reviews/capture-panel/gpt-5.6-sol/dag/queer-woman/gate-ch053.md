REACTION

Oh, this got me. Not with sex—with him remembering Meg’s boots. Kayla talking the paint off a wall. Theo, without one flicker of territorial nonsense. All the apparently disposable scraps of Vee’s life have been kept. She wanted proof that she exists to him outside his house, and here he is asking for the whole untidy social world she comes from, not merely agreeing to be displayed as her boyfriend but wanting to enter her life and know the people in it.

And I love that he immediately widened the invitation beyond Randi. Vee hears “your people” and thinks Randi first, which is its own enormous blinking sign, but Pace means Cassie, Meg, Kayla, even the man who once asked her out. He isn’t choosing the glamorous friend who fits his table. He wants the full roster because they belong to Vee. “Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one” nearly undid me. That is such a Pace sentence: practical on the surface, tender all the way down. Nobody is to be peripheral.

There’s a delicious, awful irony in Vee imagining Pace and Randi finally getting to know each other. I felt the secret sitting there between every word. “Properly” is almost unbearable when those two have known each other—and known things about Vee together—since before Vee understood there was anything to know. Her happiness is genuine, and so is the future she is building, but the reader can see the trapdoor under that imagined first meeting.

Still, I believe Pace here. This does not feel like a calculated social performance. The detail about the boot heel is too particular, too quietly loved. He has been keeping her world because it is hers. This is the public answer she asked for beginning to take material form, even though he still owes her the actual words and the truth about why he withheld them.

And Vee remembering Chili’s correctly, finally—seeing the fork, hearing his explanation again, and simply asking him to choose a place he can bear—made me ridiculously tender toward her. She doesn’t turn his limits into shame or make a ceremony of accommodating him. She understands and adjusts. “Let me handle it” lands differently now: not as enclosure, but as trust given with the relevant knowledge in hand.

I’m happy for her. I’m also braced for the gathering, because putting all her people in one room means putting Cassie beside Randi, and Pace beside Randi, while Vee watches them supposedly meet. That secret is going to have to perform innocence in public. I want to see who slips.
