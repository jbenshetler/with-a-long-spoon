REACTION — Oh, that first scene gave me exactly the ownership I wanted for Vee, and then Randi spent the rest of the chapter gently taking it away from her.

“I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.” Yes. That is the sentence. Vee knows why the kiss mattered: not because Pace was irresistible, not because his technique finally overcame her rule, but because he accepted her no and she discovered she wanted to cross the distance herself. Her ridiculous little spin nearly into the closet door made me so happy. That is desire becoming delight inside her own body. And Cassie’s question about alcohol did not bother me; it felt like the practical check of someone who loves Vee and knows how unlike her this is. Once Vee answers, Cassie believes her. “Look at you” means *I see what you chose*, not *look what he made you do*.

Which is why I wanted to hiss when Randi translated the kiss into “Because you couldn’t not. Because of him.” No. Vee had just told Cassie, with such pride, that she could have not—that Pace had already accepted the no—and she deliberately called him back. Randi takes the exact part of the story that belongs most clearly to Vee and recasts it as evidence of his irresistible power. Then Vee gives up “I made an exception” and agrees with the smaller version. There is the pattern I was afraid of: Randi offering Vee the same escape from ownership she uses herself. It is seductive because she offers it as tenderness.

And Randi is concealing herself all over this conversation. She apparently forgets Pace’s name, invents or at least foregrounds some disastrous glamorous Friday date, and positions herself as the hungry friend who must live on Vee’s good romance secondhand. Maybe the French date happened; Randi could be seeing other people. But the performance is doing extremely convenient work. Vee cannot compare notes about the man they are both sleeping with because Randi has made herself the woman whose own date was everything Pace is not. She gets to ask for every intimate detail while pretending she has no stake in the answer.

Still, Randi’s delight in Vee felt real enough to hurt. “You let him choose for you. And you liked it” is reconnaissance, yes, but it is also Randi hearing the map of what Vee wants and recognizing the terrain. Then “ask and then ravish” drops into Vee’s low register, and suddenly they are together inside the fantasy rather than Randi merely directing it from outside. That beat was hot. Vee feels Randi beside her there. I did too. But even their first shared erotic frequency is still organized around what Pace will do to Vee. Randi remains close enough to feel the desire and safely absent from its grammar.

The Cassie drift frightened me more quietly. First Cassie watches Randi reach for Vee and folds something away. Then Vee decides the smoothie place is not really Cassie’s place and supplies the cheerful healthy-friendship explanation herself. That is how a person gets displaced without anybody ever excluding her aloud. Randi even asks whether they could have brought Cassie, so Vee gets to experience the separation as her own sensible observation. Meanwhile Cassie is the one who celebrated Vee’s agency, and Randi is the one rewriting it. I do not want Vee to lose the friend who can tell the difference.

And then the hand. Goddammit, the hand got me.

“Vee’s my dear friend” is absolutely a public claiming. Randi spends social capital on her in front of Brooke, holds her hand where the whole hierarchy can see it, and does not retreat when the air changes. After watching Randi reduce Pace to “cute” under Brooke’s smile, I felt the cost of that. She protects Vee from being reduced to a stats-class acquaintance in the exact room where Vee is already making herself smaller to protect Randi. That is a real turn in the bond, even if Vee experiences it primarily as being elevated by someone powerful. Brooke understands the declaration. The silent taller woman understands it. I think Randi understands more of it than she can admit.

But the charge is still braided disastrously with status. Vee looks at Randi’s face, feels the room rearrange around their joined hands, and leaves feeling picked. I still cannot isolate *I want this woman* from *this woman publicly chose me*. The chapter brings them closer than ever and makes the distinction harder, not easier.

Also, Brooke’s “We should all get dinner sometime” made my blood run cold. I know exactly what her lovely nothing sounds like now. Randi has just put Vee on Brooke’s map, and Brooke’s maps are not benign.

I am thrilled, turned on, and furious. Randi’s public claim is the most direct thing she has done toward Vee, but she still cannot say desire in her own name; she can only call Vee dear, hold her hand, and eroticize what Pace will do. Meanwhile she is teaching Vee that the most self-authored choice of her life happened because of him. This book knows precisely where to put the knife.
