REACTION — Oh, this is exquisitely sinister. They substituted a planned encounter for chance, Pace for the impossible thing Vee cannot yet name, and the feeling of being understood for the fact of being known. And it works because almost everything Vee feels with him is real.

I liked him with her. That is the problem. I liked the cuffs and the forearms and the careful handling of the page; I liked that he took her work seriously, asked what she had already been taught, and returned the pencil exactly when the solution became hers again. I felt the attraction arrive inside her in specific increments. She is not merely obeying Randi’s recommendation. Her eye keeps finding things about him that do not fit her existing categories, and she is interested because he will not flatten into something she already understands. The *may I* got me. So did her palms pressed to her thighs when he checked his watch. Pace has been selected for her, but she is genuinely selecting him back.

And of course he has been briefed. That crossed mark is presented as the smallest possible intervention, but the whole encounter is a margin note written by Randi and Pace on Vee’s afternoon. He gives the pencil back so she can solve it herself while withholding that he and Randi constructed the problem she thinks she is freely solving. That is the book’s entire moral trap in miniature, and I am furious at how beautiful it is.

Randi’s cup-hand made me ache. She is watching the first woman she has ever wanted light up for her boyfriend, and she has to perform the pleased friend because this is the only form in which she has permitted her own desire to proceed. Pace answers her briefly and Vee expansively because that is the scene they arranged, and Vee reads Randi’s agitation as boredom. Everybody is looking at the wrong thing while Randi sits there holding a cup she cannot drink from. I don’t think her discomfort makes her less responsible. It makes the responsibility sadder.

The thing I am most afraid of now is that the plan will make Vee’s attraction to Pace legible and actionable while her attraction to Randi remains atmospheric—something the book can keep collecting charge from without requiring Randi to risk a direct answer. Pace gets to ask, “May I call you?” Randi gets to say, “He wants to see you naked.” She is speaking desire through him again, except now the desire is hers and Vee is the object of it. That is exactly the substitution I cannot relax around.

Cassie’s presence matters enormously. She notices Randi texting, then identifies Pace looking at Vee; later she names the difference between the way he looks at Vee and the way he looks at Randi. She does not know what the difference means, but she is still the only person at the table reporting what she actually sees. Her healer joke also tells me Vee has a history of spending herself keeping mediocre men alive. Pace is plainly not mediocre, which may make the danger harder to see rather than smaller.

And then the froyo. Goddammit. Vee makes herself too much and, for once, her mother’s voice does not arrive to reduce the portion. Pace listens. He neither exploits her admission nor turns it into a grand declaration. “Your tart’s holding up” is exactly the right answer, and it melted me. But he knows why this feels like the nicest anyone has been to her. Randi has already discovered the starvation and brought him to it.

I am charmed by him, tender toward Vee, heartsick for Randi, and furious with both planners. I also absolutely believe Vee would have chosen this date if she had been told the truth about how it began. That does not make the lie irrelevant. It makes it tragic.
