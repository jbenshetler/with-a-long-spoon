REACTION — Thank God she went swimming.

Cassie’s “You don’t come home school nights” landed harder than any accusation could have, because she didn’t dress it up as concern or jealousy or try to tell Vee what the pattern meant. She just gave Vee the count. More nights away than home. Vee genuinely had not noticed. That frightened me—not because staying with Pace is inherently alarming, but because the brightest thing in her life is consuming her ordinary life so smoothly she needs someone outside it to tell her she is disappearing.

And Cassie remains the safest person in this book. She asks whether Vee loves him, receives the tiny frightened “maybe,” and does not pry, interpret, celebrate, or warn. She lets the answer be unfinished. Randi would have known what Vee meant before Vee finished saying it and guided her toward the meaning Randi wanted. Pace would have made the room so tender Vee might decide the question had been answered without words. Cassie closes her book, asks plainly, then opens it again. I love her.

The burn story unsettled me more than Vee understood. Pace keeps the house warm enough that she no longer thinks about clothes; she is apparently half-dressed often enough to catch food on her breast; he tends the little injury for days. All of that is affectionate and sexy and domestic. But Cassie hears the story and immediately sees the degree of undress Vee skips over as ordinary now. Vee is becoming profoundly at home in Pace’s house while barely returning to her own bed, and she still has no idea another woman already belongs inside that warmth.

Then the pool gave Vee back to herself without pretending to solve her. I loved that so much. No Pace, no Randi, no gaze turning her body into evidence of anything. Her body has “opinions all day long,” and in the lane it simply works. She is strong without being admired, directed, fed, dressed, interpreted, or desired. After so many chapters in which bodily truth has been treated as the deepest truth—wetness as proof, appetite as revelation, blushing as confession—it felt almost medicinal to watch her body do something honest that meant nothing beyond itself.

And those four strokes scared me: is Pace truly this good, or has she built him out of wanting and two months of being looked at? That is the first time Vee has allowed even the shape of doubt to touch the romance without immediately turning the doubt into shame. She does not pursue it, but she does not punish herself for having it either. The wall comes. She turns. The thought is allowed to exist and pass without Randi naming it for her or Pace answering it with another beautiful act.

Swimming does not tell her whether she loves him, whether he minded her things, or what she is to him. It only removes the frantic need to settle those questions immediately. That distinction felt enormous. Vee has been living inside other people’s exquisitely arranged attention; here she makes her own narrow corridor, keeps her own count, spends what remains, and leaves with her head quiet.

This was a quiet chapter, but not a stalled one. It restored the part of Vee that existed before being chosen became the organizing fact of her life. I’m not desperate in the same way I am after a kiss or a betrayal, but I badly want to know whether she can carry that quiet back into Pace’s house and ask for an answer no drawer can provide.
