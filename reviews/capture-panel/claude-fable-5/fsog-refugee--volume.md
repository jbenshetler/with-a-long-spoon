VERDICT

BOOK TWO: Yes. Today. The volume ends with everything I need resolved still standing — the word he can't say, the plan she can't see, the woman holding both threads — and I have never wanted a reckoning this badly. If Book Two lets Vee find out and doesn't flinch from what it costs all three of them, this series is something genuinely new. If it lets the con dissolve unexamined into a happy throuple, I will throw it across the room. Either way I'm buying it, which tells you something.

MAILING LIST: Yes. This author writes consent as an erotic engine, not a liability waiver — I've been waiting years for that and I want to know the minute she publishes anything.

TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — you know how I'm always saying I want the Fifty Shades intensity but with a man who actually asks, and actually waits for the answer? Someone wrote it. The scenes between Vee and Pace are the best consent-forward heat I have ever read — a safeword offered like a gift, a spanking bet where she nods before his hand moves, a dress fitting that made me cry over a *hem* — and the heroine's desire is written from so far inside her own skin that her shame and her wanting run on the same wire, which is the truest thing about being raised the way we were raised that I've ever seen in print. Now the catch, and it's a big one: the whole relationship is a setup. Her best friend and her boyfriend chose her together, before she ever met either of them, and you know from the jacket, so every gorgeous scene has this cold thread running under it, and half the time I was turned on and sick to my stomach *simultaneously* and I think that's the point — the book keeps asking whether a yes can be free when the room was built around you, and it never once winks or lets anyone off. It's not a comfort read. The heroine doesn't find out by the end of Volume One, so you'll finish it furious. Read it anyway. I need someone to lose their mind about Chapter 46 with me.
