MAILING LIST: yes. An author who can write chapter 46 and chapter 34 in the same book is one I want to hear from the moment she publishes anything.
TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — you know how we're always saying we want dark that knows it's dark? This is the most consent-literate grooming novel I have ever read, and I mean both halves of that. On the page, every single yes is real: safewords, aftercare, a hero who backs off so scrupulously it's almost a fetish of its own. And the entire year is rigged — the meet-cute is staged, the best friend drawing her open is the boyfriend's secret lover, the kinks are seeded over mimosas — and the book never once pretends otherwise; the jacket tells you up front and then dares you to fall for it anyway, exactly as she does. And you do. That's the trick and the ache of it. Fair warnings: it's slow — whole chapters are a man with a tape measure, though that one's the hottest thing in it; the hero has a backstory (nineteen and fifteen, and the book names it, thank God, through her flinch and not a lecture) that reframes him as wound-shaped rather than villain-shaped, and you'll argue with yourself about whether that's enough; and nothing resolves — no reveal, no reckoning, fifty chapters and the girl still doesn't know. If you need the trap sprung in one volume, wait and binge. But the redhead is one of the best-drawn heroines I've read in years — the shame wiring, the credit card at the boutique, the shirt she rations in the dark — and the villainess is in love with her and coaching her own replacement into bed and it's the saddest thing, it's *Gatsby* with mimosas and a spanking bench. Read the first chapter. If the last three words of it don't get you, we can't be friends anymore anyway.
