TELL A FRIEND: Okay, listen, because I need you to trust me past the premise: it's about a college girl being secretly set up — groomed, honestly, there's no softer word — by her new best friend and the friend's secret lover, and the jacket TELLS you, so you spend fifty chapters watching the best year of this girl's life get built by two people who are lying to her, and the awful part is it's also genuinely the best year of her life, and the book never lets you put either fact down. The heat is the real thing — explicit, on the page, no euphemisms, and every scene is ABOUT something: shame turning into appetite, a girl learning her body was never the problem. Chapter one alone is better than the last thirty books I've KU'd combined. The man is the fantasy done honest — he cooks, he sews, he asks first — except he cannot say one specific sentence, and the book makes that withholding hurt worse than any dark-romance cruelty I've read. Fair warnings: the middle has too many she-tells-her-roommate-about-it chapters, the brunch scenes run on a template you'll start to see, and it ends with essentially nothing detonated — it's Volume One of a fuse, and if that makes you feel farmed, wait and binge. But Randi. RANDI. The predator who's accidentally in love with her prey and can't run that one scenario to the end — she's the best character in romance this year and I'd read three hundred pages of just her. I haven't underlined a book since college. I underlined this one.
