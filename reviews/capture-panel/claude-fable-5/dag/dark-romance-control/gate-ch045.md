Okay. OKAY. Five chapters of her circling the word like a dog that can't lie down, and Rivers finally let her throw it at the door — and he gave her "Words are cheap. Believe what I do." I actually said "oh you BASTARD" out loud in my kitchen, because that is the single most damning sentence this man has ever produced and she can't hear it. Believe what you DO?? What you DO, Peter, is share her with Randi over debrief brunches. What you DO is provision green scrunchies before she's ever spent the night. What you DO is fold your entire self into the bottom drawer so the trap reads as devotion. "Believe what I do" is only comfort if what you do is love — and I've been watching what he does for forty-five chapters and I still can't tell, and that's the whole book, and I'm feral about it.

Here's the thing that's got me pacing: this man has never lied. Not once. The whole con is built out of true sentences — and I think that's exactly why he can't say the word. Either it's false and he won't lie, or it's TRUE and it's the one thing that isn't in the script, the one sentence that would make this real in a way "nothing happened, nobody did anything wrong" can't survive. He's the boy who learned that naming a thing is how it becomes a crime. Of course he won't name her. Naming is how she becomes something he did. And Vee, poor Vee, has spent four chapters begging for the word from a man whose entire psyche is a machine for not-naming, and she thinks she's asking for love when she's actually asking him to confess.

And I have to say it — chapters 41 through 44 were the closest this book has come to losing me on principle. "Does he love me, what do I call him to my mother" is normally the exact flavor of relationship angst I skip a book for. But Rivers keeps the knife in it: the cracker-and-meal sentence answered with "I love how good we are together" — he said LOVE, he aimed it at the ARRANGEMENT, he complimented the operation instead of the woman, and her body knew before she did and she put the *wait* on the pile with the other *waits*. The ending, though. She USED THE DOOR. That's the first move either operator didn't stage. Unless it is staged — unless withholding the word is the technique, the last dial, absence as the final conditioning — and I genuinely cannot tell, and the fact that I can't tell after all this time is why I'll follow this book off a cliff. Also: his hand lifting toward her. The hand in place of the word.
