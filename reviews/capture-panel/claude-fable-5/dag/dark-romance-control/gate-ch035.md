Okay, so Rivers finally handed me the chapter that, in any other book, would be my exit. Salmon with dill sauce. Warm tomatoes off the sill. A man silently making room in a drawer. This is the exact wholesome-domestic quicksand I have DNF'd forty books over. And I read it with my hand over my mouth, because in THIS book, the drawer is not a drawer. Nothing in this book is ever the thing. The dress wasn't a dress, the shoes weren't shoes, the scarf wasn't a scarf, and the top drawer — the one your hand goes to first, no note, "there was never a note" — is the softest cell door I've ever watched close. She said "I'm going to start bringing a bag" into his mirror two chapters ago like a joke, and the house answered by BUILDING HER IN. He folded his whole ordered self into the bottom drawer so her colonization would have somewhere to live. Read it straight, it's the most romantic gesture in the book. Read it with my thirty-four chapters of receipts, and it's the same move as the dress: he took the trouble and then took the further trouble to hide the trouble. She stood there holding both open drawers and read it as love. I stood there holding both open drawers and read it as intake processing. And the answer is yes. It's always yes with this man.

And "Peter." SHE SAID PETER. To his sleeping body, breath-quiet, where he couldn't hear — but I heard it, and I've been sitting on that name since chapter 25 like it's a live round, because my whole question was whether Randi ever got it. Vee whispers it into the dark like a coin she's hiding under the mattress, and she doesn't even know it's currency. She has the real name. The part that actually scared me, and it's three sentences and Rivers just walks past it like she didn't leave a body on the floor: her clothes are going loose.
