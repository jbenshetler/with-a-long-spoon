REACTION

Okay, so this is the chapter where nothing happens, and I have complicated feelings about it.

First, the honest complaint: there is no Pace in this chapter. There is no Randi in this chapter. The two predators — and let's be clear, that's what they are, the jacket told me, the bench told me, the pointing game told me — are entirely offstage, and I spent seven pages at a rec pool watching two girls splash each other. On paper this is everything I skim. Wholesome best-friend energy, body-image feelings, a swim. If I'd opened a book cold to this chapter I'd have put it back on the shelf without finishing the page.

But I didn't open it cold, and that's the thing this book keeps doing to me. Because I know what's coming for Vee. I know Randi ran into her "by accident" with dancer's footwork so clean it looked like the redhead's fault. I know Pace forgot his coffee on purpose. So watching Vee go under the water into the one place that was "never taken" — the one part of her that belongs entirely to her, where "nobody down here wanted anything" — I got a genuine chill, and not the cozy kind. This is the before picture. This is the author showing me the exact square footage of what's about to be colonized. That line about grieving a little at the bottom of the pool? In any other book that's a therapy chapter. In this book it read like watching a lamb graze while I know where the wolves are standing. The dramatic irony is doing all the heat this chapter doesn't have.

And Cassie. Cassie is breaking my heart in advance and I resent it. "Goodbye, Vee" in chapter 4 — the whole word, flat and finished — and now she's the one who kept the joke low instead of pitching it up the bleachers, kept it just for Vee, splashed her out of a grief spiral without making the sad thing bigger. She's the only person in this entire book who wants nothing from Vee. Which in this genre means she's either the casualty or the witness, and either way I'm going to be gutted. "They will never know the love of a woman" — Cassie, honey, I'm starting to think you're not talking about the boys in the bleachers. There's something under her flatness about Randi, the way she clocked Pace's great-aunt look instantly, the way she watched him without buying any of it. She sees. She's the only one who sees, and nobody's listening to her, and that's the horror-movie structure I actually came here for.

But here's my warning to this book, texted at 1 a.m. to the friend who recommended it: I'll take one of these. ONE. The bench chapter bought a lot of goodwill — a man who builds furniture for the specific purpose of dismantling a woman's entire performing self is exactly my brand of terrifying, and Randi deciding in the bathroom mirror that he can have the face but never her again is the most deliciously broken thing I've read this month. The pointing game — a couple shopping for a girl in broad daylight while she thinks she's making a friend — chef's kiss, that's predation wearing a cardigan. So I'll allow one quiet chapter of Vee being human and beloved, because it raises the stakes on what's being stolen from her. But if the next chapter is also swimming and snacks and healthy female friendship, we're going to have a problem. I did not buy the book with "he'd have burned the world down for her" energy on the jacket to read about pool splashing twice.

Also — "the body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck with the rolled towel, where it could wait." That's a beautiful sentence and it made me sad, and I don't come to this shelf to be sad, I come to be scared and turned on, ideally simultaneously. Get Vee back in the same room as those two. The trap is set, the invitation's been mailed. Spring it.
