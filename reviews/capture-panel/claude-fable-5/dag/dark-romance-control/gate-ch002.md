REACTION

Okay so first — I'm still not over Chapter 1 and I need to say that before I say anything about this one. The bench chapter rewired me a little. "There you are, Randi" — the NAME DROP at the end, when the whole jacket had me primed for Vee, and it's RANDI on that bench? Randi, the one who's supposed to be running the game? I actually flipped back to the blurb to check. So the puppetmaster got taken apart first, and then in the bathroom she made the coldest decision I've read in months — *he can have the face, the real one is never coming out again* — and THAT, that quiet deadbolt of a sentence, is the whole engine of this book, I can feel it. That's the knife under the silk. It's not the man who's the threat here. It's her. It might be both of them, pointed at a girl who doesn't know she's been chosen yet. I am SEATED.

So Chapter 2 is the comedown chapter, and I knew it would be, and I read it anyway with my chest a little tight. No Pace. No heat. Just Randi doing the walk of shame that isn't allowed to be a walk of shame, and Brooke — okay, Brooke. Brooke conducting an interrogation entirely made of warmth, never standing up, deciding when the conversation is over. I know that girl. Everyone knows that girl. "I was about to be scandalized" with the relief exhale — she wasn't relieved, she was filing it. The chapter is called *Standards* and Randi SITS ON THE STANDARDS BOARD. She polices other girls for a living and she just got softly frisked in her own common room and couldn't deploy a single one of her three prepared lines. The predator getting handled by a bigger predator, twelve hours after begging facedown on a bench. That's delicious in a bone-dry way.

And the back half — her alone in the room, unable to sit down, unable to pick a hanger, lying on the bed in her shoes under the "adequate" duvet. Because HIS house is warm. His blanket is cashmere. Her whole life is the good duvet on paper and it's synthetic to the touch, and she felt the real thing last night and now everything in her room is exposed as a prop. "He's hard to say no to" repeating in a different key — she said it as a joke and it's actually the truest sentence she owns and she can't afford to hear it. The not-crying that "did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." Girl. GIRL.

Here's my honesty though: this chapter has zero teeth of its own. No Pace, no danger, no want. It's all aftermath and social micro-fencing, and if I hadn't had Chapter 1 in my bloodstream I'd have skimmed the Sloane-and-Kinsey filler — "short-cute?" nearly lost me, I do not care about cereal girls. What's holding me is that I know what this book is doing: it's showing me the exact wound that's going to make Randi do something terrible to Vee. She got seen, she couldn't survive being seen, so she's going to go find a girl and do the seeing TO her. Give her the power side of the table since she can't bear the bench side. That's the theory I'd text my friend at 1 a.m., all caps, DO NOT SPOIL ME. If the next chapter is Vee, I'm hers. If it's more sorority brunch logistics, we're going to have words.
