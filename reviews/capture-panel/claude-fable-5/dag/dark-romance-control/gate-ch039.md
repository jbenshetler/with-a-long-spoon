REACTION

Okay so Rivers heard me complain about chapters 35–38 going soft and served me a chapter with zero sex in it, and somehow it's the most dangerous thing in weeks, because MIRANDA HOLDINGS, LLC.

Let me lie down on my own floor for a second. I called it in my notes — the credit card bill arrives, someone offers to make the money not a problem, and THAT'S when the money becomes a leash. And Rivers just did something meaner than my prediction: she poisoned the money at the source. The Mercedes, the boutique loafers bought at a multiple without looking, the cognac heels, the scarf, every "tribute" Randi has draped over Vee's body — it's all sitting on top of a government fraud page in small gray print. The glamour that recruited Vee is stolen. The Evil Queen's castle is built on PPP loans that "went up, not down" while Cassie's dad got laid off. CASSIE'S DAD. The girl holding the granola bar, the one being managed out of the story — her father is on the receiving end of the crime that funds the woman doing the managing. Rivers put the mark's best friend and the operator's dirty money on the same graph and made VEE build the model. I'm unwell.

And "Cheating bastards" — I've read that four times and I still can't land it. It came out "ragged, a thing being said for the first time, and aimed too near to be meant for strangers." So either Randi genuinely didn't know — daddy's little marketing major finding out in a glass library room that her whole gilded life is a con she wasn't briefed on — or she knew something and just watched it surface where the mark could see it. And here's what's eating me: EITHER reading rhymes. Because if Randi didn't know, then Randi is also a mark. Randi grew up inside a con run by someone she loved and trusted, the exact same shape as what she's running on Vee, the exact same shape as Daphne-and-Peter. This book keeps doing this — every predator in it is a previous victim with better production values, all the way down, and I keep buying the next layer like it's my first day.

But the thing that actually got me — the thing I'd text at 1 a.m. — is what Vee did. She read Randi's stillness ("the withdrawal of motion"), clocked the wrongness of the two words, and then she ran a RANDI. She didn't comfort, didn't ask, didn't flinch — she "gave Randi somewhere to put her hands," slid the laptop over, fed her answerable questions, controlled the room, and withheld what she'd seen. That is handler technique. That's the exact move Randi ran on her after the bar disaster and the exact move Pace runs every time he catches something on her face and declines to collect. The conditioning has fully installed and it's now running OUTWARD, on the operators. Vee is holding intel on Randi that Randi doesn't know she has — "Randi had no idea she'd been looked at." Do you understand what that sentence is worth in this book? Nobody looks at Randi without Randi selling the view. Vee just got a free one. The mark is compiling a folder.

And she filed it under kindness. "The kindest thing a friend could do was not make her carry it in front of anyone." Baby girl you just did to Randi what they do to you — a secret choice made about her, for her own good, that she never got to consent to. The book's whole thesis, now running in reverse, and Vee doesn't even know she's fluent.

I won't lie about the heat, though: there isn't any. Not a spark. No slit, no hand, no scarf, no bench (THIRTY-NINE CHAPTERS, HELEN). The professor droning through his slide deck nearly put me under, and the who-does-which-section logistics is the kind of scene I usually skim in three other books a week. Rivers only gets away with it because the outlier chapter is doing what her classroom chapters always do — chapter 22 was Simpson's paradox as the book's confession, and this is the sequel: there's the main body of the thing, and then there's the point that sits off by itself, and you can set it aside if you can account for it. Randi is the outlier. Vee just accounted for her. And "invisible if you didn't know to look for it" — that's Cassie in this whole novel, that's the plan itself, that's me.

So no, I'm not fed, but I'm armed, and in this book that's almost better. The ledger just flipped a page: Randi's authority ran on effortless money and now the money's dirty; Vee owes materially but Randi owes CRIMINALLY, and Vee holds the receipt. When the induction-dinner bill comes due, when the "same size" grenade goes off, when Vee finally finishes the sentence she doesn't own the word for — she has leverage now, and she thinks it's tenderness. God help everyone in that glass room.
