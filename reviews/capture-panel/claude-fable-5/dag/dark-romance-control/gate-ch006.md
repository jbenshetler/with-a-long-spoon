REACTION —

Okay, so on paper this chapter is everything I skip. Frozen yogurt. A boy with a topping *system*. Walking the long way at blue hour while he listens like her grandfather. If I'd opened a book cold to this chapter I'd have DNF'd before the plastic ivy. This is the wholesome dinner-cooking chapter I have left a hundred books over.

Except.

Except I know what I know, and that's the whole trick this book is running on me, isn't it. I watched him build a bench with his hands and take Randi apart on it until she begged in a voice she hadn't used since childhood. I watched him play the pointing game on the quad like he was picking out produce. I watched Randi *engineer* that shoulder-bump so cleanly a decade of dance folded into three seconds. So when Vee stands there under the neon saying "this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time" — girl, I felt sick. Good sick. That line is the saddest, most dangerous sentence in the chapter and she thinks it's just embarrassing. The niceness IS the trap. The niceness was scheduled. Two people who are sleeping with each other picked you out of a crowd like the redheaded girl you literally were, and now the kindness landing on your starving little heart like rain — that's the manipulation, it's not adjacent to the manipulation.

And I'll admit the food thing got me somewhere I didn't expect. Randi eats thin apple slices under her mother's knife-voice; Vee builds a leaning tower of froyo and waits for the mother-voice and it *doesn't come*. He silences the voice. That's the seduction — not his forearms, not the *may I call you* — the fact that around him her appetite is allowed. He fed Randi and now he's feeding Vee, and "let someone feed you" is doing the work "let someone tie you up" does in lesser books. I see you, Helen Rivers.

But I have to be honest with myself: this chapter has no teeth ON the page. All the teeth are in my memory of chapters 1 and 3. "Your tart's holding up" is a genuinely lovely beat — he doesn't pretend, doesn't make it large, gives the true thing space — and if I didn't know he was a predator with a co-conspirator, it would be exactly the beige tenderness I flee from. The chapter is coasting entirely on dramatic irony, and it's SHORT, and there's zero Randi in the back half, zero heat, zero danger you can touch. I'm continuing on credit. The book knows I'm ahead of Vee — the jacket literally promised me that — but I need the knife to glint again soon. Where was Randi tonight, while her boyfriend was on a date with the girl she picked? That's the scene I wanted. That's the scene I'd better get.

Also, small thing, 1 a.m. text material: Cassie is the only person in this book with functioning smoke detectors and I am terrified for her. "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter, and now she's making jokes while the trap closes. Someone is going to have to deal with Cassie eventually and I don't think it'll be polite.
