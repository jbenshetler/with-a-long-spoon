REACTION

Okay so I need to be honest about the first half of this chapter, because for a solid stretch I was tapping my foot. Apple slices arranged cut-side up? A man who keeps the house warm so she's never cold? He *folds her cardigan*? Babe. I bought this book because the jacket promised me a conspiracy — "a secret choice made about her," a girl walking into a trap two people built for her — and instead I'm getting the most emotionally attuned man in fiction doing safeword check-ins like a TSA agent. "How's your back?" "Hands and shoulders?" "Tell me your word. I won't be upset." I literally said out loud, if he asks her one more wellness question I'm going back to my mafia boys.

BUT.

"There you are, Randi."

I GASPED. Actually gasped. Because I've read the jacket — I read jackets like scripture — and the whole time I thought I was watching Pace and *Vee*, watching the seduction the blurb promised. And it's RANDI. It's the architect. It's the woman who's going to hand-pick a starving girl and feed her to this exact man, and I just watched the moment she gets broken open on that bench and decides — cold, deliberate, "quiet as a deadbolt going over" — that she is never coming out from behind the face again. That bathroom mirror scene is the darkest thing in the chapter and there isn't a hand laid on her in it. She got seen, truly seen, for the first time in her life, it was the most alive she's ever been, and her response is: never again, he can have the mask, and — this is the part that has me feral — she's going to go find him a *replacement*. That's what Vee is. Vee is the body Randi throws into the searchlight so it stops pointing at her. Tell me that isn't the coldest origin story for a villainess you've ever read. And she's going to fall for Vee anyway, per the jacket, WHILE running the con. I'm unwell.

And the bench itself — okay, fine, I'll admit it. "You said it was a table. I didn't correct you." That line has teeth. He built a spanking bench, let her drape her underwear over it for weeks like furniture, and said nothing. That's not healthy communication, that's a man who plans in silence and lets you walk into the thing he made for you, and that IS my genre, it's just wearing a henley about it. The detail of her pressing the bottle cap into her own nail bed, hurting herself quietly where he can't see, while he sits there feeling *proud* of his beautiful evening — the dramatic irony is vicious. He thinks he found her. He did find her. He just doesn't know that finding her is the thing that lost her, and he's about to be handed a decoy and he'll never know the real one slipped out the back.

My one real worry going forward: Pace himself. He's exact and patient and I like that he's a builder of traps, but he's tender-first, and the jacket keeps promising "asks before he takes." If the whole book is him being courtly while women unravel around him, I need Randi's knife to stay sharp on every page, because she's the danger here. She's the villain love interest. She's the one who'd burn the world down — or at least burn a girl down — to keep her lock turned. I didn't expect the woman to be my morally black lead but Helen Rivers, I see what you did, and I'm in.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: HE CALLED HER RANDI. IT WAS RANDI THE WHOLE TIME. THE FRIEND IS THE ONE WHO BROKE FIRST AND NOW SHE'S GOING TO DO IT TO VEE ON PURPOSE.
