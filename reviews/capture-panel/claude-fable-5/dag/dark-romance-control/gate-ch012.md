Okay, so on paper this chapter is everything I don't buy books for. Two roommates in a cold dorm room, banter, a girl glowing about a nice day, keep-the-shirt cuteness. If you'd shown me this scene out of context I'd have shelved the whole book under "wholesome" and walked. I need to say that up front so you understand what it means that I read it with my stomach in a knot the whole time.

Because here's what this chapter actually is, underneath the flannel: it's the smoke detector getting its battery pulled out, gently, by the person it's trying to protect.

"No van." Cassie came into this with a witness statement ready. She said "Goodbye, Vee" — the funeral word — the day Randi collected her. She clocked Pace's fake great-aunt look. She is the ONLY person in this book with working eyes. And Vee just sat there in his shirt and handed her, one by one, every piece of evidence for the defense. He fixed my blister and didn't make it weird. He stood in the rain so nobody could look at me. He didn't touch me. He was shaking and he just looked. And I watched Cassie go from file-revising to rapt to "Vee," soft, setting her name down where it wouldn't break — and that's it, that's the alarm going quiet. Not because Cassie's wrong to be moved. Because everything Vee reported is TRUE. That's the sick genius of this book. Pace doesn't need to fool Cassie. Vee's real experience does it for him, because the experience was engineered to be real. The trap doesn't have a false bottom. That's why nobody can find it.

Except one listener is running her through a funnel and the other one loves her. Vee can't tell the difference. VEE CANNOT TELL THE DIFFERENCE. She gives the same open face to the viper and the smoke detector. I want to grab her by the flannel collar.
