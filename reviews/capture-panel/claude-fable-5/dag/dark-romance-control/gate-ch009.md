REACTION

Okay so on its face this is the most wholesome chapter in the book — girl comes home glowing, tells her roommate about the kiss, spins in a circle, falls in a bean bag. If I'd opened to this page cold I'd have put the book back on the shelf. This is exactly the shelf I don't buy from: two friends being cute, sock feet, highlighters, warm feelings, a pillow thrown and caught. Nobody's in danger. Nobody's lying. It's *dinner-and-they-like-each-other* energy and normally I am GONE.

Except I'm not cold. I know what Vee doesn't. And that's the only thing keeping this chapter alive for me — the dramatic irony is doing all the work the chapter itself refuses to do.

"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." GIRL. That is the entire thesis of the jacket copy — *every yes was freely given, that was the problem* — coming out of her mouth like a victory lap, and she's SO proud of it, and I actually got a little chill because she's right and she's completely wrong at the same time. Randi walked into her on that lawn like a choreographed dance move. Pace "happened" to be in that coffee shop. Her wanting is real AND it was manufactured, and watching her hold it up to Cassie like a trophy — "Me. I did it. Me." — is genuinely a little sick, in the way I like. Helen Rivers knows exactly what she's doing with that line.

And Cassie. Cassie is the smoke detector in this book and I love her and I'm scared for her. "What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop" — it's a joke, and it is NOT a joke, because the answer to "where does he live" is literally "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel," which is the address of every cabin in every book where a girl doesn't come back the same. And I already know what's in that bedroom off six-fourteen. I've SEEN the bench. Vee's going to go hiking in borrowed shoes to a house with a table that isn't only a table, and she thinks the mysterious pile of things she doesn't know about him is "the best pile there is." Baby. Also I have not forgotten Cassie's flat little "Goodbye, Vee" from the lecture hall — the whole word, the word for a longer parting — and every scene of Cassie being the last honest person in Vee's life reads like a countdown to me now.

My real complaint: I'm four chapters past the bench and nobody has been dangerous SINCE the bench. Chapter 1 had Randi begging with her face in a cradle and then deciding, cold as a deadbolt, that Pace gets the mask forever — that's my girl, that's my villainess, and she's been reduced to clutching her coffee cup and not sipping while her boyfriend flirts with her target. I need Randi's teeth back. I need to know what she's actually feeling watching this — jealousy? appetite? both at once? The book keeps promising me a predator in cashmere and keeps handing me froyo. The gap between what I know and what Vee knows is a good knife, but a knife has to eventually cut something, and we're nine chapters in.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: "the lamb doesn't just walk to the slaughter she THANKS the road. also the roommate knows. the roommate always knows."
