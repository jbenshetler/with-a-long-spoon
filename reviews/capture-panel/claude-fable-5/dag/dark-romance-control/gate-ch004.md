REACTION

Okay so first — I know what this chapter is doing and I know it worked on me anyway, which is honestly the sign of a good one.

Because here's the thing: on paper this is a girl getting a latte. This is the exact chapter that should lose me. Coffee shop, coffee date, warm feelings, a girl feeling chosen. If I'd read this cold, chapter one of some other book, I'd be gone before the lavender thing arrived. But I've SEEN the bench. I've watched Randi get taken apart on it and go into that bathroom and turn the deadbolt on her own soul, and then I watched Pace point at Vee across a quad like he was picking out produce, and Randi walk a collision so clean it looked like the mark walked into HER. So every single warm beat in this chapter reads double, and the doubling is the whole meal. "Love you, girl." "See you later." The hand flat between her shoulder blades for one second. I was reading a seduction the whole time and Vee was reading a friendship and that gap is doing exactly what the jacket promised — I'm ahead of her from the start and I'm falling for it exactly as she does. It's stalking-as-devotion with a cardigan on and I am, unfortunately, extremely into it.

The line that actually got me — got me got me — was "Goodbye, Vee." Not bye, not see you later. Goodbye. Cassie CLOCKED it. Cassie, four words a day, flat cap, wrong textbook Cassie looked at the shiniest girl on campus love-bombing her friend and said the word you say at a funeral. And Vee's face "did the thing it did with wrongness, which was to smooth it" — that sentence is a knife and I felt it go in. That's the horror movie beat. That's the friend in the passenger seat saying don't get in the van, and Vee laughing "it's coffee, weirdo," and getting in the van. I would text a friend that line at 1 a.m. I might actually do it.

And the "latent variables" thing — the professor literally announcing the theme, the factors you can't measure directly, and Vee writing it down and underlining it and letting it go by like scenery because the latent variable is sitting at her elbow smelling like cold citrus? Cute. A little pleased with itself. I'll allow it because the transcript-blank thing after the coffee is genuinely eerie — she can't remember one word Randi said, only the feeling. That's not friendship, that's a spell. That's how you write grooming from the inside and it made my skin crawl in the good way.

What I'm suspicious of, and what I need this book to not chicken out on: Randi. Because right now I can't tell how much of the warmth is technique and how much leaked through, and I don't WANT to be able to tell, and neither does Randi, I think — this is the girl who put herself back behind the face forever and is now doing to Vee what Pace did to her, except she's on the other side of the bench this time. The queen went back to her tower and picked up the crossbow. That's my thing. That's exactly my thing. The mother's voice living in Vee's mouth — floozies, the cardigan over the better shirt on purpose — she's Randi five weeks ago. It's a pipeline and I can see the whole length of it and Vee can't and I feel genuinely a little sick and I want the next chapter.

My one flag: no teeth in this chapter. No Pace at all, no danger in the room, just danger in the subtext. I can live on subtext for exactly one chapter. The book has banked enough menace that this one rides free — but if chapter five is also lattes, we're going to have words.
