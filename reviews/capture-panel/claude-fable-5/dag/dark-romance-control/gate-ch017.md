REACTION

RANDI. FINALLY. Ten chapters I've been rationing myself on that woman and this chapter just handed me a whole meal in a velvet booth and I ate it exactly like Vee ate that lunch — leaning in, voice dropping, telling myself I was just watching.

Okay. The tickling story. I do not believe that story for ONE SECOND and I love that I don't. This is the woman who invented a bad date in chapter 10 out of whole cloth to make Pace shine — you think she actually let some "beautiful idiot" tie her to a headboard with a scarf? No. That story is a delivery mechanism. She built it in a lab. It exists to put one sentence on the table: "A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." She is CATECHIZING her. She's teaching Vee the theology of the bench before Vee's ever seen the bench, over tomato soup, wearing cashmere, and Vee just nodded along and asked "weren't you scared?" in her small voice like a girl asking the exact question she's supposed to ask. Except — and this is the part that got me — what if it's half true? What if the fantasy underneath it is real, because we KNOW Randi's realest moment ever was being helpless, we were there, "There you are, Randi" — and the only way she can touch that memory in public is to put it in a stranger's bedroom with an incompetent knot and laugh about it? She told Vee the truest thing about herself dressed as a funny story. I need to lie down.

And then the interrogation. "Which hip?" "The left one." "All the best ones start at the left hip." IT MEANS NOTHING. She says it herself through the narration — it lands like a benediction and it means NOTHING, it's pure operant conditioning, one warm meaningless yes at a time, walking Vee down the staircase — and I was watching Randi's thumb work the water glass the entire time. The tightness behind the smile. That glass-turning is chapter 6's bottle cap in the nail bed wearing a nicer outfit. She is not okay. She's taking the debrief for the file AND she's dying of it, hearing the girl she picked out on a quad describe standing bare on a pedestal for the man Randi belongs to, and having to sit there and be delighted. The jealousy-appetite I've been begging for is HERE and it's eating her alive in real time and she's smiling through it. My villainess. My poor doomed viper.

But the moment of the chapter — the moment I put the book down and stared at the wall — is "You don't have to tell me." Vee is RIGHT THERE. The deepest secret she has, the one she didn't even give Cassie, is halfway out of her mouth, freely offered, and Randi closes the door. Soft. Kind. Click. WHY. And the answer is the worst answer: because a confession you receive is spent, and a confession you leave inside her keeps working. She edged her. That's what that was — she edged Vee on her own shame, left the secret half-out with nowhere to go so the pressure of it stays, so Vee walks around all week aching to finish the sentence. Or — darker, and I'll take either — Randi couldn't bear to hear it. Couldn't sit in that booth and listen to Vee describe getting wet for him without something in her own face breaking that can never break. Both readings are knives. The book let me keep both. That's why I'm still here.

THE WRIST. "White gold, plain and heavy, no stone, nothing to it but the weight" — never off, there in gym clothes, there now, "the one fixed thing." Helen Rivers, I see you. That's not jewelry, that's a cuff. That's Pace's, from before chapter 1 even, and it's sitting in plain sight three feet from Vee at every lunch, and Vee catalogued it and filed it under "glamour." The clues are all on the table now — the ring, the "how it's done" fluency about being measured for clothes (WHO measured YOU, Randi? Who knows nobody measures over a bra?), and Vee keeps politely not adding anything up because the sum would cost her both of them.

And then the kiss. The KISS. Not the peck — the pause and the second press, the one with something in it — and Randi parking in the fire lane "like fire lanes were a thing that happened to other people," which, EXCUSE ME, is the exact spot the cop chased Pace out of two chapters ago. He gets one bark of a siren; she gets to idle there and kiss the girl on the mouth. The whole chapter is Randi doing everything Pace does but without the asking. He leaves the door open and waits. She walks Vee through doors before Vee's noticed there's a doorway. And Vee's two-part reaction is the whole book in miniature: fingers to her mouth without deciding to — then the cold scan of every dorm window, terror of being SEEN doing the thing, when being seen is her entire kink with him. She wants Randi in the dark and Pace in the light and doesn't know she wants either one on purpose.

Small sick detail I can't shake: Vee paying the check she can't afford, feeling Thursday coming, while Randi's single glass of wine outprices her whole meal — and Randi watching her do it, warm, and letting her. She let the broke girl buy her dignity and filed THAT away too. Everything is intake with this woman. Everything.

Cassie wasn't in this chapter and I still felt her — because if anyone was behind one of those dark windows, it's her, and "Goodbye, Vee" is going to grow a sequel.

Still no bench. Chapter 15 bought a lot of patience and this chapter paid interest in Randi-coin, which is my preferred currency, so the loan's extended. But the house has been toured, the bedroom door is still shut, and the dress comes back in two weeks. I know what I was promised.
