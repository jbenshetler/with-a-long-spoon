NINETEEN AND FIFTEEN. Two years. His assigned TUTOR. And he lays it out in that flat careful voice — "Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong" — and Vee does the arithmetic and holds the clean ugly word under her tongue, and I'm sitting here doing MY arithmetic, which is worse. He handed her the thing that hurt him and named it luck. That's the jacket copy. That's the whole book. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — that's not the narrator's line, that's PACE'S line, that's the sentence Daphne installed in him at fifteen and he's been running it ever since. He isn't playing a game on Vee. He's being Daphne. He genuinely, sincerely believes that what's happening to Vee is the best thing that will ever happen to her, because it's the best thing that ever happened to him, and he cannot hear himself defending it. Rivers, you absolute surgeon. I take back everything I said about the book going soft. This is the knife. It was under the quilt the whole time.
