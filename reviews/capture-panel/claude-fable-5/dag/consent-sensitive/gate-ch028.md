REACTION

Oh, so we're doing outside witnesses now. Twenty-seven chapters inside the terrarium and then Helen Rivers just hands me a woman on a bicycle who can see the whole thing from the road. Sheri is four pages of person and I already trust her more than anyone in this book except Cassie, and for the same reason: she has no stake. She "remarks on women the way other people remark on the weather," she's faster than him uphill and says so, and when she runs the arithmetic — "Two of them at once. If that's not already complicated, it will be" — she's the first character in this entire novel to say the plain thing in the plain register. Not the jacket being sly, not the narrator dropping a knife into a warm sentence. Just a friend on a Saturday going: anybody can count to two, Pace. And he doesn't answer. He doesn't answer! The man who answers every question with the actual answer goes silent for the one question that is the book.

And "Listen to you." God. That's chapter 24 happening to HIM. Randi went still on his chest when he described Vee lighting up a room; now Pace is narrating a thumb on a walnut joint and a math book put back wrong, and "he heard himself going and didn't stop," and — "he didn't know what there was to listen to." He can hear a wrong note in a room like Vee's father can hear it in an engine, and he cannot hear himself. The jacket promised the plan would get as real for the planners as for the girl, and this is what that looks like from inside his skull: helpless inventory. He kept the chair-touch. He kept the book. He harvests her gestures the way he harvested the cherry pit, except now I genuinely can't tell if it's harvesting or just love with excellent filing. That's the whole book in one sentence and it's still working on me.

Here's the thing that actually chilled me, though, and I want credit for catching it: "After you left Thursday, I kept an eye on her." Vee spent that whole night believing she was alone — the wall, the stool, the one nursed drink, the sad cab. She was observed the entire time. The bar was full of his people; the bartender who rehabilitated him to her; now Sheri filing a report on the ride. I don't think he asked. I actually don't think he asked, and that's worse, because it means his world does this for him ambiently — the man doesn't need to surveil, his ecosystem just delivers. Vee's lowest, most private hour of the book turns out to have had an audience with a "hell of a backside on her, though" attached. The book knows this is eerie. It hands it to me flat, no comment, and lets me do the shudder myself. That's the Rivers move and the eyebrow is still up.

And then: "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" "Randi. That's not finished." Not finished. Not "we're still together," not "it's complicated" — *not finished*, like a chair, like a dress, like a project on the long table with the pins still in it. After chapter 24 gave me Randi undefended in his bed — "she got past me before I could help it," her going still when he described what Vee does to a room — hearing him file her under unfinished business, in five syllables, to a third party, made my stomach drop on Randi's behalf. I have officially arrived at the place the book has been walking me toward for ten chapters: I am frightened FOR the spider. Both spiders. Everyone in this web is stuck to it.

It's a small chapter — an interstitial, one ride, one conversation, no Vee at all — and honestly after the density of 25 through 27 I didn't mind the breather. But I notice Sheri got a full physical loadout (the small frame, the ponytail, the two years of handlebar) which means she's not a cameo. She "could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years... which was everything" — that's a loyalty being banked for later, and I'm noting it the way I noted the second towel.
