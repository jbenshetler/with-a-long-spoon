Okay. So the book did the thing where it hands me the most romantic chapter so far and salts every inch of it, and I need to talk about the salt.

Start with the line that stopped me cold, mid-cherry, mid-swoon: "The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." The narrator just SAID it. That's the whole Pace operating system in one sentence — engineer the appetite, then be the hand that feeds it. The apple sliced thin, the Middle Eastern dinner, and now a literal mountain deployed as an appetite-generator. And Vee half-registers it as pleasant outdoorsy wisdom while I'm sitting here going: that is a description of everything he has done to you since the coffee shop. The book keeps doing this — putting the confession in plain sight, in the free indirect voice, where she can hear it as charming and I can hear it as a schematic. That's the eyebrow. The eyebrow is still up. Thank god.

The moleskin scene wrecked me a little, honestly, and I resent how well it worked. He's not fighting her mother's voice. He's outrunning it. He gets to her wounds before the wound-voice does, every single time, and each time he does it, her mother's operating system gets one update overwritten with his.
