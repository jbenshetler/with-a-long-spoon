REACTION

Okay, so this book is doing the thing to me that it's doing to Vee, and I know it's doing it, and I let it anyway. That's the review in one sentence.

This chapter with the jacket in my head is almost unbearable, in the good way. On its surface it's the gentlest first-date chapter I've read in years — the shaved jaw still pink, the boots that are polished because he keeps his things and not because he bought them to perform having lived, the "May I choose?", the mint tea in the little brass cages. If I'd wandered into this with no jacket copy I'd be texting my friend THIS MAN, in caps, and meaning it. But I know what she doesn't. I know the coffee-shop meeting was choreography. I know her best friend picked her off a quad like fruit. So every warm line in this chapter is playing in two keys at once, and the book knows it's playing in two keys — that line "she was already learning she could trust where he was taking her" is not an accident, that's the author looking at me over Vee's head. It should feel smug. It mostly feels like a hand tightening.

The thing that actually got me — and got me somewhere low — is the food. She hasn't eaten since breakfast and doesn't notice, "the hollow had been opening in her since midday," and then this hollow that's been empty "a lot longer than since noon," and she eats without watching herself eat, mother's voice gone quiet, for maybe the first time in the book. And I sat there torn exactly in half, because that's real. That's the most tender thing anyone has done for this girl. He fed a starving woman and didn't make a single thing of it. And then I remembered the apple. Chapter one, the honeycrisp — sliced thin because she'll eat thin slices and not thick ones, cut sides up because he noticed she eats faster that way. This man studies women's appetites the way other men study game film. Feeding her isn't spontaneous grace; it's his signature move, it's a *technique*, and the book put the apple sixty pages before the flatbread precisely so I'd feel the floor shift under the sweetest scene in the chapter. That's the difference between a book that knows and a book that doesn't. This one knows. It keeps proving it knows, and that's the only reason I'm still here, because the material itself is radioactive: a girl with a starved-down appetite and a body her mother made into a problem, being taught to want by a man who selected her, and the wanting feels like healing because parts of it *are* healing. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — the chapter is that sentence, dramatized for twenty pages. He never pushes. He never once pushes. He gives up the kiss gracefully and lets HER close the distance, and it's the hottest beat in the chapter, and it's also the trap functioning exactly as designed. Choice architecture with candlelight. I want to scream at her and I also fully understand why her hand felt like it had been wrong all its life until his was around it. I hate that I understand. I understand.
