Oh, this one's small and it *hurt*, and it hurt in the specific way this book has trained me to expect: the softest chapters are the ones doing the knife work.

Because here's what Chapter 19 actually is. It's Vee learning to ladle. "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness." Girl. GIRL. That is Randi's entire technology. That is what's been done to YOU since the shoulder-bump — the smooth surface, the edited version, the true story with the middle removed — and now you're doing it to Cassie, the one person in this book who has never once curated herself at you. Vee is being taught to handle people the way she's being handled, and she doesn't know she's in school. The book didn't underline it. It didn't have to. I sat there with my coffee going cold.

And the zipper. The zipper half down and then back up — that little moment where taking off the skirt three feet from Cassie would be the unedited truth, "on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." She waited. She waited for Cassie to leave before she'd be bare in her own room. There is now a room in Vee's life that Cassie doesn't get into, and Vee installed the lock herself this morning, humming. That's the cost of the trade. The panties went to Pace and the door went up between the roommates in the same gesture, and Vee thinks the only thing she gave away was green satin.

Cassie broke my heart in about four lines, which is her specialty. That flash across her face "before it found a name" — she knows. Some animal part of her read the whole night off Vee's grin before the story started, and then she sat there and took the ladled version and "did not pull at the seam." She let Vee keep her edit. And then "Don't wait up, lover" — kindly, certainly, wrong — and out the door with her cleats, and I wanted to run down the hall after her. Also "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend": she's wrong about what Vee forgot, wrong in the most innocent direction possible, and the word *boyfriend* just sits there being the thing nobody's actually confirmed. Cassie's working from the public file. She doesn't know there's a classified one now.

And the last line — "She had never in her life been so happy" — is doing exactly what this book does to me every time, which is hand me something true and warm and make me hold it while knowing where it came from. Her happiness is REAL. It has been real the whole time, that's the whole cruelty of the architecture, and this morning it's at its realest: bare under the skirt, smiling at red lights, not minding her mother's word for once. My one complaint: after eighteen, this is a sorbet course, and I noticed. It's short, it's quiet, nothing charged happens *in* it — its whole job is aftermath and one thematic turn. But the turn is real, so I'm not mad. What I want next: Randi hearing about Friday night. Because Vee ladled for Cassie, but she does NOT ladle for Randi — Randi has the keys to the pot — and I need to see Randi's face when the girl reports back that the plan worked, completely, and it was wonderful. I need to know which of the two people in that booth it wrecks.
