REACTION

Okay so nothing explicit happens in this chapter and I did not care, which is how I know this book has me, because I am the woman who DNFs at the first whiff of a chaste dinner date. But this wasn't chaste. This was the most predatory chapter so far and it's all bread and hummus.

Because I've been ahead of her since Chapter 1 — the jacket promised me that and the book is actually delivering it — and so I watched this whole gorgeous date through double vision. She's having the best night of her life. I'm watching a man run a protocol. "May I choose?" — and I actually said "oh no" out loud, because that's the apple slices. That's the honeycrisp cut thin with the cut sides up because she eats them faster that way. He fed Randi on his lap and now he's feeding Vee across a table, and the feeding IS the sex, it's the same act, he finds the hunger a woman has been starving down her whole life and he answers it, and the answering feels like love and functions like a leash. "The hollow only opened wider, asked for more before she'd swallowed... something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line gutted me. That's Vee's whole life in a sentence, and he KNOWS it's her whole life, that's why he picked the restaurant.

And the detail that actually made me put the book down for a second: she can't remember what they talked about. "She couldn't, later, have laid out what they talked about." The transcript goes blank and the feeling stays. Which is EXACTLY what happened at Chi Latte with Randi. Word for word the same effect. Two different people are doing the identical thing to this girl — flooding her so pleasantly that she keeps the warmth and loses the words — and she reads it as the two best relationships of her life. That parallel is so quietly evil I want to text someone about it at 1 a.m. and I have no one to text because nobody I know is reading this yet.

Here's what's messing me up, though, and it's the good kind of messed up: everything he does is genuinely kind. He leaves the text so she doesn't have to answer that night. He doesn't lean in when she says no to the kiss. He walks street-side. Christine's face softens when he walks in — the man is beloved by middle-aged restaurant women, the most reliable character reference in fiction. If I didn't know about the pointing game I would be feral for him. I DO know about the pointing game and I'm still a little feral for him, which I think is the whole design of this book and I resent it and I'm staying.

The kiss reversal at the end — her stating the rule, him accepting it instantly and turning to go, her hand pulling taut and stopping him — "I'm making an exception." Perfect. That's the thesis of the entire series in miniature, right? He gives up the ground, and she closes the distance herself, and every yes was freely given, that was the problem. She chose it. She'll always have chosen it. I feel sick and warm at the same time, like the smoky dip.

Small suspicious things I'm filing: the right hand, always the right hand, "some consistent rule he kept" — the book pointed at it twice so it means something and I don't know what yet and it's bothering me. And Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" from a few chapters back is still ringing under everything. Cassie sees it. Cassie's the only one who sees any of it, and Cassie went to her lab, and I have a bad feeling about how alone that leaves Vee.

Honest note: the middle of the date goes very soft-focus — "the talk came easy and she couldn't have said about what" — and for about a page I felt the book drifting toward that literary haze I usually bail on. Then I realized the haze is diegetic, it's her going under, it's the same blur both her handlers produce in her, and I forgave it completely. But it flirted with losing me for a paragraph.

I want the next chapter to hurt or burn, though. Preferably both. The book has banked enough trust with the bench that I'll take one slow chapter. I won't take two.
