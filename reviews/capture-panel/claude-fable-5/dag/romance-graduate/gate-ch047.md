REACTION

Randi POV. Randi POV. Forty-six chapters locked inside Vee's skull and Rivers finally cracks the door — and she does it *right after* Vee walks out of that house crying, which is the cruelest cut in the book so far, because I wanted the fallout, I wanted the Sunday shirt never returned, I wanted to know if Vee went back, and instead the book hands me snow and champagne and a woman I've been trying to read through frosted glass for three months. And I forgave it in about four sentences, because this is the chapter I've been begging for since brunch one. Interiority for the Evil Queen. It's here and it's worse than I hoped.

"Eating her revulsion." At her own father's table. The invented boys "safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table" — that's a whole childhood in eleven words. This is where the handler was built: a house where you perform your own romantic life as light entertainment for a man pricing you for eligible bachelors, where your mother hugs you with edges and points. Randi wasn't born running protocol. She was raised at a breakfast table that ran it on her. And the MIRA thing is sitting right there under the tablecloth — "they spoke of her father's business," the chalet, the vacation steak — while somewhere in a county dataset Cassie's dad's layoff is a dot with this family's name near it. Rivers didn't mention the scatterplot once and I thought about it the entire scene. That's how good the plant was.

THE BRACELET. Plain white gold, *locked* on her wrist, never comes off, and she keeps it out of the light at her parents' table — hides it — and it catches on the heel of her hand when John spins her. Rivers put that word in — locked — and then never looked at it again, which is how this book tells you something is a bomb. That's a day collar or I'll eat my Kindle. And whose? Because this chapter also quietly confirmed what the jacket promised and the text kept coy: "Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." *Made.* Past habitual. Randi has been in Pace's bed, under Pace's protocol, and — here's the part that actually rearranged my organs — the courtly asking that Vee experiences as being seen, as the most erotic thing she's ever done? Randi experienced it as *work.* "That was more work, and tonight, gliding, she thought she was done with the work." The same lead, the lightest pressure, the room left to you to choose — one woman calls it love and the other calls it labor. I don't know which of them is right and I think that ambiguity is the entire series, and I want to underline the dancing paragraph and mail it to everyone I know.

John. Oh, John. He opens with "play along" — a stranger installing a fiction and inviting her into it before she's consented to the game — and I sat up so fast, because that's *Randi's own move.* That's the polite invitation, run on the runner. She gets recruited into a fake marriage in a lift line by a beautiful competent man who assumes everything, never asks, conjures sleighs — and she has the best night of her performed life and lies down in the middle of it and feels *nothing.* "It had meant no more than a good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." The lipstick ring on him — she watched her own mouth do it "from a long way off." Warm only along the one seam where she touched him, and the cold was hers, carried in and carried out. Rivers compressed the sex to almost nothing and for once I'm not filing an IOU, because the nothing IS the sex scene. The absence is the content. "Nothing had happened to her eyes" — the eyeliner held — I put the phone down at that one.

And then the turn I've been waiting for since the glass room, stated plainly at last: Vee. "Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of, and her whole body knew it, and she knew exactly what it was." She's in love with her. Says it to herself in full. And then — this is the psychologically merciless part the jacket promised — she runs the confession scenario, hits the one frame she can't render (Vee's face), and chooses instead to *keep grooming.* "Let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were." She takes the truest feeling she's ever had and immediately converts it back into handling, because handling is the only container she owns. "She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be." I have never felt so much tenderness and so much alarm for a character in the same breath. And meanwhile — Vee's midnight text. "i'm here if you want to talk. or not." Vee, freshly wrecked by a man who won't spend a word on her, still finds words to spend on Randi. And Randi answers with the fairytale — "every word of it true. None of it the thing" — which is EXACTLY Pace's "I'd never share your photograph." These two people speak the same lawyer's dialect of true-and-worthless, they learned it somewhere (each other? Daphne's world?), and Vee is fluent only in the plain kind. That asymmetry is the machine, right there, in two text bubbles.

What I'm carrying out of this one: Pace and Randi are both people who can only be reached by someone who "goes straight to her," and both of them found that in each other and it *terrified them into running a curriculum on a third person instead.* That's my working theory as of tonight. The game isn't cruelty. It's two cowards outsourcing intimacy to a girl brave enough to feel it out loud. And Vee — from her warm Ohio kitchen, parents who love her out loud, dad burning rolls on purpose — is richer than both of them and nobody in the book knows it yet except me.

One small honest note: the opening paragraph reads weirdly flat — "Her father was tall, with a strong jaw," brochure sentences — and for half a page I worried the POV switch had cost Rivers her prose. Then I decided it's Randi's interior register: glossy, staged, camera-ready, everything at its label-out best, and it only goes ragged when the chapter does. I'm choosing to believe it's deliberate because the back half earns it. But I noticed.

Also: still no resolution on Vee and Pace, the shirt is presumably still on his body or hers, "words are cheap" is still ringing in that front room, and the book just made me spend New Year's with the other woman. The nerve. I'm reading on immediately.
