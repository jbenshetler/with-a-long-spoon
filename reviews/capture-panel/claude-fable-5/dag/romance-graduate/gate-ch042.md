Her dad would start looking for the catch. HER DAD IS RIGHT. There IS a catch. He's pricing the dress and going quiet and he has, from a folding chair in another state, run the exact scan Vee stopped running in October. And she's managing him out of it. The mark is now handling her own family, and forty-two chapters of curriculum are why she's good at it, and she thinks she invented that too.

And the dad exists! A whole warm dad who burns the rolls on purpose. Forty chapters of "the mother" as an offstage frost — Cassie's "go make your mother cry again" — and now Mom cries twice out of pride and touches her collarbone at the shoulders of the dress and doesn't say the thing. This family is warmer than I'd been led to believe, which somehow makes it worse — she's not fleeing a wasteland, she's cropping perfectly decent people out of her actual life because there's no version of the truth that stands up in their frame.

Boyfriend-with-a-question-mark-said-out-loud for the parents, and for her best friend: "both, probably both." Can't say AND doesn't want to. That flicker is the whole chapter. Randi taught her that. I want to put my head down on the table.
