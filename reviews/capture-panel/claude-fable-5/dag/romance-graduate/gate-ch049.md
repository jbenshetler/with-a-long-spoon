REACTION

Forty-eight chapters, Rivers. Forty-eight chapters and you finally let me inside his head — and the first sentence of his interiority is him deciding to conceal something. "He had long practice at deciding a thing could keep." I actually laughed out loud, alone, at the audacity. I've been begging for Pace's POV since the moleskin, certain it would rule on the only question that matters — con or love — and the book hands me his skull and *the narration itself keeps things from me*. What was in the truck? What did he decide not to bring her down with? The chapter opens on a withholding and never pays it, and that's not sloppy, that's the thesis. Even in his own head, this man is a corridor of decorated locks.

And here's what's wrecking me: the inside of him is *clean*. That's what I can't get over. I went in braced for protocol — for one clinical Randi-flavored sentence, one "she's nearly ready," one debrief scheduled in his mental calendar — and instead I got a man in a diner missing his girl. "I want Vee. I miss her." No hedge, no machinery, said out loud to the one person who wants nothing off him. Either this is the exoneration I've been afraid to hope for, or Rivers has built a narrator disciplined enough to not think about the conspiracy even in free indirect, and I genuinely cannot tell which, and I have never respected a book more for refusing to let me off the hook. Because the conspiracy IS in the chapter — it's just wearing Sheri's voice. "She find out about the brunette, then?" *"No. Not like that."* Not like that. NOT LIKE THAT. That is the closest the book has ever come to confessing from inside the house, and it goes by in four words and a subject change. Sheri met Randi. Pace brought Randi *dancing*, before Thanksgiving, while Vee was cropping photos for her mother. There is a "brunette" for Vee to "find out about" and Pace knows it and the chapter just... orders the usual. I had to put my phone down and walk to the kitchen.

"I can't." Not "I won't." I've read a thousand emotionally unavailable heroes say some version of this and it's always a pose the third-act grovel dissolves. This didn't read like a pose. It read like a wall with a body buried in it. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice" — he said that about Sheri's estrangement and it landed like a man quoting his own file. Daphne. The scar. "I don't go back." Someone died, or something did, and the word Vee is starving for is buried with it, and I'd bet the Kindle that Sunday-shaped dread I've been carrying is standing right on top of the grave. And Sheri — God, Sheri — "then not saying it matters more to you than having her... and from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." She said the thing. The one clean person looked at him and said the true ugly thing to his face and stole his fries after, and he took it, because he came there to take it. That's the friendship I've been promised since the pie. Also she's queer, also she's estranged and grieving, also she clocked Randi as a "harem applicant" in four seconds flat — Sheri has the best eyes in this novel and nobody's paying her.

And the shape of it — I see what you did, Rivers. Three New Year chapters, three POVs, a triptych of people who cannot say one word: Randi cold in a perfect man's bed, Vee cold in a bed she's outgrown, Pace warm in a diner laying up bricks — "I said it, out loud, in words" — and genuinely not understanding why "most important person in my life" landed as half of something. It landed as half because it IS half, Peter. She didn't ask for reasons. Sheri knows it, I know it, the waitress probably knows it. The Chili's callback gutted me from his side — "I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you" was true, he wasn't lying, and it still wasn't the word. Everyone in this book keeps handing over everything except the one thing, and calling the everything payment.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: he misses her and he can't say it and Sheri knows about Randi and said "the brunette" like it was nothing and I have to know what was in the truck.
