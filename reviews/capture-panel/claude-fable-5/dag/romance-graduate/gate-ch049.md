REACTION

Oh, this chapter is a magic trick performed in front of an audience that's been handed the secret, and it STILL works. That's the thing I keep having to sit with. I read chapters 46 and 48. I know what actually happened in Gstaad. I know who Sheri actually is. And I still spent this whole chapter half-melted by how good Randi is at loving her, because the awful genius of this book is that the comfort is real comfort. Vee showed up wrecked off a red-eye and Randi swept her Gstaad dresses onto the floor — the fairytale wardrobe, tissue paper and all, dumped without a look — to make room for the crying girl. That detail nearly took me out. The stuff she bought to armor herself against her parents' world matters less to her than Vee's raccoon face. And it's TRUE, and it's also exactly what a handler would do, and I have given up untangling those two threads because Rivers has spent forty-nine chapters teaching me they're the same thread.

But the Sheri thing. THE SHERI THING. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." Randi has SEEN her face. She hugged him hello at the dance hall and looked at Sheri like a harem applicant — Sheri told us so, one chapter ago, around a mouthful of Pace's fries. Randi knows Sheri exists, knows she's five feet of country dyke who thinks about Vee's backside "more than's decent," knows she's the least romantic threat in the county — and instead of saying the one true sentence that would end Vee's whole spiral ("I've met her, honey, she's a friend, she's gay, breathe"), she lets Vee believe Sheri is a 3 a.m. phantom Vee invented out of fear. Which dissolves the jealousy just as effectively AND leaves Randi as the only person Vee trusts to tell her what's real. She fixed the pain and kept the dependency. It's the photograph vow all over again: not one lie in it, and rotten at the joint. I actually said "you bitch" out loud in my kitchen, with something horribly close to admiration.

And then the pivot that scared me most, because it's structural: Vee walked in with the same question she carried out of Pace's house — *what am I to you, give me the word* — and Randi's solution is to take the question away from her. "Words are the whole war." Don't ask for the name; show up naked under a coat instead. Look at what just happened. Vee's one demand, the one thing she walked out into the cold for, gets converted — warmly, lovingly, with her own participation — into a gift for HIM. Her grievance goes in one end of the machine and her body comes out the other end, wrapped, on his doorstep. And Randi is objectively right that it'll work, and Pace objectively misses her (the diner chapter broke my heart, "I want Vee, I miss her," Sheri stealing his fries and telling him he's being shitty), so the reconciliation everyone wants is coming — and the price of it is that Vee stops asking. The book let Sheri say the true thing to Pace ("not saying it matters more to you than having her") and let Randi unsay it to Vee, in the same weekend, and nobody on the page can compare notes except me. That's the loneliest privilege I've ever had as a reader.

The shame mechanics — Rivers just NAMED it this time. "The wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." Vee making herself say "slutty," flushing to her ears, and Randi walking her down word by word into "the bravest thing a woman can do." It's grooming and it's the truest sex-positive pep talk anyone's ever given this girl and it made me hot, and I noticed it making me hot, which is the whole "ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does" promise on the jacket cashing itself. And the heels. Randi's heels, pressed into her hands, "Randi always knew the shoes." She buckled the induction heels onto Vee's foot herself and now she's dressing her for the doorstep — Randi will be standing in Pace's doorway tonight, four inches tall, exactly like last time. Vee is going to walk to him naked in Randi's shoes and neither man nor girl will hear it.

And the hug. Past the end of a hug, the beat longer and the beat after that, Vee breathing bitter orange off her neck and not wanting to stop, "neither of them let go." After chapter 46 — after "she was not brave enough to lose her," after "let Vee arrive at it on her own" — I read that hug knowing both women were taking something from it and neither would name it, and the wet patch on Randi's shoulder felt more intimate than the ski-lodge sex scene did. Then Vee asks for the New Year story and Randi performs the redaction LIVE, and Vee mistakes emptiness for discretion, and "the bedroom's the room I actually live in" gets a laugh out of her when it's the saddest sentence Randi has ever said — a confession wearing a punchline, and it worked, the whole room believed it, I'm the only one who flinched. The chapter title is "My Friend Randi" and I want to shake Vee and I want to shake Randi harder and I mostly want the next chapter, because a naked girl in borrowed heels is about to knock on a door held shut by a man who told a diner he can't say the word, and Rivers owes me the long scene, and this doorstep had better be where some of that debt comes due.
