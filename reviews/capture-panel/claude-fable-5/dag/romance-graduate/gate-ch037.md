Okay, so this is quiet chapter number three, and I clocked it by the second paragraph — bleachers folded back, lane lines, chlorine, no Randi, no Pace, nobody. My thumb did the thing my thumb does. I'll be honest: somewhere in "Breathe every third. Hand in front of the shoulder, not past it," my eyes started sliding down the page looking for a voice that wasn't the pool.

And then the book did the one thing that earns a swim chapter, which is that it put MY question in her head. "Whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting" — that's the thing I've been carrying for thirty chapters, the fixing-hands/using-hands problem, and Vee touched it for exactly four strokes and the wall came and took it away from her. Four strokes! Rivers let her graze the truth and then let the flip turn eat it, and that's honest — that IS what exercise does with a thought you can't afford — but I wanted to grab her by the cap and hold her head under until she finished the sentence. Also very sneaky: "did he mind" weighed nothing in the water and "do you love him" was a pressure under her ribs with dread riding it. Her body sorted her mail for her. The drawer question is answered; the love question has DREAD on it, and she doesn't ask why there's dread on it, and I know why there's dread on it, and it's a Mercedes.

Here's what actually got me, though, and it took me until the shower to name it: this is the first chapter in the entire book where nobody is watching her. No Randi reading her face across a tablecloth, no Pace filing her reactions, no Brooke with a folder, no blonde I have to frisk for a wire. A butterfly man and a flip-turn woman who've never spoken to her and never will. Her body — which has been the machine's instrument panel for two hundred pages — "had opinions all day long every day of her life, and none at all down here. It only worked." I nearly cried a little at that and I don't fully know why. She has one room left where she's not a project. Swimming since she was twelve — this predates all of them, the mother included maybe. The book gave her a sanctuary and gave it to me for exactly one chapter, and now I'm terrified of the day Randi says "oh, you swim? I've been meaning to get back in the water."

But — and this is the gate talking — nothing MOVED. The pastry kiss is still sitting there unmetabolized; she got kissed on the mouth with stolen glaze three chapters ago and the book has gone salmon, scrunchies, bean bag, backstroke since. "Sticky fingers" is still unexploded ordnance. The credit card is still empty. Cassie's still holding. I know a breather when I'm in one, and after the induction and the bet and the bathroom stall I probably owed the book a breather, but three quiet chapters is my documented limit and this was the third. It's a beautifully written lap swim. It's still a lap swim.
