REACTION

Okay, the photo scene. THE PHOTO SCENE. I have read a hundred "send him a nude" beats and they are always about the sending — the risk, the thrill, delete-it-if-we-break-up. Rivers made it about the *posing*, and specifically about who does the posing. "No, not like that. You'll have to wait" — and then he arranges her limb by limb and kisses each place his hands set her, and hands her back a version of herself she can only find beautiful *because it's his*. That is gorgeous and it made my stomach drop in two directions at once, because in any other book that's the swooniest thing a man has ever done, and in THIS book I'm watching a man who was curated at fifteen curate his girlfriend into a keepsake, and she says out loud — out LOUD — "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone."

And he says: "Never. I'd never share your photograph."

HELEN. RIVERS. Read that sentence again. She asked him not to share HER. He promised not to share the PHOTOGRAPH. That is a lawyer's answer. That is a man who has already shared her — every inch of her, weekly, in bed, to the woman running the shopping trips — parsing his vow down to the one object he can keep clean. And Vee doesn't hear the swap because she's still glowing from "Delivered." I heard it. I put the book down and said "oh you bastard" to my empty kitchen. This is exactly the thing this book does that nothing on my Kindle does: the tenderness is real AND the sentence is a trapdoor, at the same time, in the same breath. He probably doesn't even hear himself doing it. That's the worst part. Sheri proved he can't hear himself.

Also — small, but it got me — "she wondered whose doing it was that she could taste now." The book keeps insisting he's improving her. He IS improving her. She eats, she tastes, she takes up a drawer, she's braver in her own skin than she's ever been. Every single upgrade is genuine and every single one deepens the dependency, and I still cannot pull those two things apart and I've stopped believing I'm supposed to. The drawer chapter last week did it with a bathroom counter; this one does it with a lemon.

Now the part that actually chilled me: "I don't go back." Three words and the whole kitchen changed temperature. After the fireside chapter I know what's on the other side of that door — Ohio, the hallway desk, Daphne, "nobody did anything wrong" — and watching Vee touch the handle and choose to take her hand off it was so tender and so sad. She thinks she's respecting his privacy. She's actually letting the sealed rooms stay sealed, the way she lets all the sealed rooms stay sealed, because unsealing anything in this book collapses the whole house. And notice what he did with his hands after: moved her wine glass an inch. Adjusted an object. That's what this man does with feeling — he relocates it into things. The dress, the drawer, the scrunchie form, the pie. The scrunchies came back in this chapter like a warm little callback and I don't trust a single soft prop in this house anymore.

And then Thanksgiving. Sheri at his table — fine, I love Sheri, I exhaled. But watch what the book just did: it quietly established that when Vee flies home to her mother (THE MOTHER, on-page at last?? please, I've been waiting), Pace will be here, and Randi will be — where? Randi has a husband-shaped white gold band and a bedroom with a Sade playlist and no mentioned plans. The board is being set for the holidays and I can feel it. Also Vee's jealousy flicker over Sheri — "another woman at his table" — extinguished in one line because Sheri's alone and harmless. Sweetheart. The other woman at his table doesn't bring pie. She brings a curriculum.

Heat check, because that's why I'm here: the opening — her folded over him inside the curtain of her own hair, the "deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of" — is exactly the register I want, lived-in and explicit-adjacent and *felt*. But I have to say it: three times in one evening and all three happened between chapters or in summary. "You've used me terribly. Three times." Ma'am, I was PRESENT and I got none of them. That's IOU number four. The spanking chapter paid real principal, so I know Rivers can, and the bathroom-stall scene in 34 proved she'll go all the way inside Vee's body when it counts — but this chapter banked its sex and spent its pages on lemon zest. The lemon zest was excellent. I still noticed.

One more thing I can't shake: she whispered "Peter" to a sleeping man two chapters ago, and this chapter he told her "this is my home" — and both of those are the truest things either of them has said, and neither was said to a conscious person or in a complete sentence. These two are in love in the negative space and the plot is a machine specifically built to monetize negative space. Randi is going to hear about that photograph at brunch. Vee will tell her herself, glowing, and I will read it through my fingers.
