REACTION

Okay, so — no sex, no Pace, no Randi, and I didn't even flinch. That's how I know this book has me, because six chapters ago a heat-free pool chapter would've been exactly where I bailed. Instead I read the underwater section twice.

"She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem" — that line got me somewhere I wasn't guarding. I've read four hundred romance heroines who "struggle with body image" and it's always cellulite mentioned once and then a man kisses it away in chapter twelve. This is not that. This is the old synchro suit that fit at sixteen, the towel carried down at her side instead of up against her — *not up against her*, because holding it in front of you admits you heard the bleachers before they said anything — and the entrance-walk as armor. That's a woman writing a body that's been managed, not a body that's a plot device. And then the water: the room switching off, the scull her hands remember before she asks them, grieving *a little* — a little! the restraint of that! — for the one place that was never taken from her. I sat with that longer than I want to admit.

And Cassie. CASSIE. I need to talk about Cassie at 1 a.m. with somebody. "They will never know the love of a woman" — pitched low, kept just for Vee, when she has the lungs to make it a performance and doesn't. That "Goodbye, Vee" back in chapter 4 has been sitting in my chest like a stone and this chapter just confirmed it: Cassie is in love with her. Cassie is the only person in this entire book who loves Vee without a plan. Randi's warmth is choreography — I've seen the choreography from the inside now, I watched her get built on that bench and then watched her go deadbolt-quiet behind the face — but Cassie's love is a splash fight calibrated to outrage-per-ounce-of-water. Cassie never asks because asking makes the sad version bigger. Cassie stops at the middle of her ribs, planted like a fence post, and says *go on, I'll be right here.* That is the thesis of this whole series in a swimsuit, isn't it. One person on the deck saying I'll be standing right here, and two people offstage who've already decided how far into the deep end Vee is going.

Because that's the other thing — the jacket told me, and the pointing game showed me, and now every soft chapter like this one reads double. Vee under the surface thinking *nobody down here wants anything* and I'm up on the bleachers with Randi and Pace knowing exactly how much is wanted. The book is doing the thing the blurb promised: letting me fall for it ahead of her. The bleacher boys catcalling her chest are the crude version of what the two charming people in her life are doing with better manners, and I don't think Vee's body caught the rhyme but mine did. It's making me a little sick in the way I signed up for.

Is there heat in this chapter? None. Zero. And here's the honest ledger: the book has banked enough — the bench, Pace's mouth still wet from her in chapter 3 — that I'll take a breather chapter, especially one this emotionally load-bearing. But I'm noticing it's been two chapters now running on charm and ache instead of charge. Froyo, then pool. The book knows Vee's body better than Vee does; I want Pace's hands to start knowing it soon, because that's the collision I bought the ticket for. One more gentle chapter after this and I start tapping my foot.

Also — the body staying up on the deck with the rolled towel, "where it could wait." Last line like a held note. This author does not miss on endings.
