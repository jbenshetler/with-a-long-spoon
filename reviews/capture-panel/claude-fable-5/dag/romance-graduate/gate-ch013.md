REACTION

Okay, so here's my honest, slightly cranky truth: this is the third time I've heard the hike. I lived it in eleven, I got the Cassie debrief in twelve — which I loved, Cassie's "I had your blood type" is the best line in three chapters — and now I get the girls'-table remix with cheese-plate Meg and Kayla the human group chat. And I was ready to be annoyed. I felt myself starting to be annoyed. And then the book did the thing this book keeps doing, which is that the retelling isn't a retelling, it's evidence. "She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care." That's Vee polishing the myth herself now. Nobody has to sell her Pace anymore — she's the sales team. Randi doesn't even appear in this chapter and the machine runs fine without her, which is honestly the scariest thing in it.

Because listen to what Vee actually says, laughing, into the table: "I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." She SAYS IT. She describes the mechanism out loud — he asks, he waits for the real answer, and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it — and it sounds like devotion to her and it sounds like a training log to me. And then Meg — level, boyfriend-having, follows-this-in-installments Meg — says the one sane sentence anyone has said to Vee in thirteen chapters: "Everybody's different at the start. Sometimes they even are." And it "didn't catch on anything." It slid right off her. That's the smoke detector going off in a different room again. This book keeps handing Vee exits with the doors propped open and letting her walk past them, and I am chewing my own knuckles.

Powerlifter. POWERLIFTER. The book has been feeding me his forearms and his hand-built furniture and "I think there's a whole situation under there" for chapters, and now: he lifts first thing, before anything, then writes for six hours and stops. I hate how much that worked on me. It worked on me exactly the way it works on Vee, which is the whole design of this novel — the jacket promised I'd fall for it ahead of her and I'd like to report the jacket was not lying.

And then the text. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." Kayla calling it a ransom note is funny, but Kayla also nails the thing that made my stomach drop: he didn't say what to wear. This man chooses her dinner down to the dish. He laced her boot a different way. He does not leave details unspecified — so the blankness IS the instruction. The open door is the door. That's his whole method and the title of the chapter is "What to Wear" and I know — I KNOW — that russet silk dress from the summit is coming back. He heard her set it down on the rock and not pick it up again, and that man does not forget a hunger once she's shown it to him. Whether it appears Saturday or at the induction, it's coming, and when it does I'm going to feel four things at once, only two of them good.

Saturday. Two o'clock. His house. THE house. The house with the bench in it that used to be Randi. Vee is going to walk through that door with her hair done by committee and I've been waiting for this since chapter one, and my capture score would be higher if this chapter had done anything itself instead of pointing very charmingly at next chapter. It's a lobby. A warm, well-written lobby with good banter and one genuinely chilling beat — but I've now gone SIX chapters since the last real heat unless we count the truck (I count the truck, half-credit, Vee's fingers on those wet buttons carried me a while). If Saturday is another chaste lunch I'm going to throw my Kindle into a lake that holds the sky flat as a poured thing.
