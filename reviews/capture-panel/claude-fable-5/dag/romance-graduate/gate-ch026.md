REACTION

Okay. So the book took the one trope I would have sworn I never needed to see again — the jealous-misunderstanding bar fight, the blonde with an agenda, the storming off — and it made me eat it like it was new, and I'm a little mad about how well it worked.

First, the lesson. The concrete pad, the field lights, the boom box with the electrical tape handle — I was gone before they even got to the bar. And I see what you're doing, Helen Rivers, I see it with both eyes: "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead. Being jelly doesn't work." That is the entire book's erotic ethics delivered as a dance note. He doesn't want compliance. He wants resistance he can read. It's why the consent in this book actually crackles instead of reading like a liability waiver — her pushing back IS the connection. And then the sentence that stopped my breath, the one she thinks on the floor like it's a nice thing: the frame "held her in the one place the next step could come from, so there was nothing to do but take it." Honey. HONEY. That is the whole conspiracy in nineteen words. Every step is genuinely hers — and the frame decides where steps can land. She said the jacket copy out loud, mid-twirl, happy. I had to put the book down for a second.

And then the blonde detonates her, and here's why this isn't the KU version of this chapter: the blonde isn't the cause. The mother is. "A man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them" arrives BEFORE the blonde does — Vee's already got a count going of the hugs, the shame is pre-loaded, the blonde just pulls the pin. And Vee knows it. That's the part that gutted me: "She knew what she was doing, and she did it anyway." "Some part of her wanted to stay that way." In a lesser book she'd be a victim of a misunderstanding. Here she's a woman watching herself pour poison and drinking it because the shame FEELS like the truth — "three dates and she'd spread her legs like a slut" is her mother's voice wearing Vee's mouth. Every gorgeous thing Pace un-taught her this whole book, undone in one sentence she chose to say with venom in it. And his face just... draining. He set the beer down untouched and walked into the cold and I made an actual sound.

The bartender is my favorite kind of scene — the world confirming he's exactly what he appears to be, which in THIS book is the most frightening possible information. "Leaves her at the door. Doesn't take advantage. Not that plenty haven't tried." "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." A whole roomful of women wanted him and he waited for the one Randi picked out and hand-delivered. The bartender thinks she's telling Vee a comforting story about a gentleman. I'm reading a background check on a predator's cover, and it's airtight, and it's also — this is the sick part — probably all true. He probably IS good. Both things. This book keeps making me hold both things and my arms are tired.

The washed shirt. "Rationing the smell of him, until what was left of him went stale and wrong" — and it comes back "smelling of fabric softener and no one," and she puts it on ANYWAY. That's the most accurate sentence about longing I've read in years and it cost me something.

And then the last line walks in wearing a knife. "Go to Randi's." Of course. Of COURSE. She just had her first real rupture with Pace — the first time her own damage put a crack in the machine — and her instinct is to carry it straight to the other conspirator to get it repaired. Fresh off chapter 24, where I watched Randi debrief the shoe trip IN HIS BED, I know exactly what happens next: the fight gets metabolized. Randi will be warm and wise and completely right, and Vee will go back to him repaired and more open than before, and the repair itself will be the plan working. Even her mistakes feed the machine now. Also — quietly, in the back of my skull — "she's not ready yet" and "you'll want to take your time" are still sitting there from Randi's mouth, and Vee is about to show up at her door raw and ashamed and needing to be held. I don't love where my mind went. The book put it there on purpose.

No sex this chapter and I didn't miss it, which for me is basically a miracle — the hand at her shoulder blade did more than most full scenes in my Kindle graveyard. But I'm keeping the tab open: the book still owes me the long version, and now it owes me the reconciliation too.

One more thing I can't shake: he taught the class. Two semesters. Half the women in that bar can dance because of him. He makes women light on their feet and safe and seen, at scale, as a hobby. Vee lay awake realizing that means he's good. I lay awake realizing that means he's practiced.
