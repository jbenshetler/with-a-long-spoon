Okay so I need to talk about the grin first. He breaks a kiss — a *good* kiss, door-still-open, fistful-of-shirt kiss — because he cannot wait one more second to show her the thing he made. Helpless with it. Like a kid. And here's my problem: I know what this man is. I have twenty chapters of dossier on the technique, the debriefs, the two towels hung before the yes. And that grin still got me. I sat there going *oh no, he's real*, which is EXACTLY what the book has trained me to distrust, and I felt it anyway. That's the whole engine of this series in one doorway — I am Vee. I keep saying that and it keeps getting truer.

The dress. He hid the trouble. That line — "taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble" — that's the thesis of the entire book wearing a bias cut. That's what Pace and Randi have been DOING to her for four hundred pages: enormous, meticulous labor, sanded down until the only visible surface is effortlessness, no seam of effort showing, *no closure she could even find*. Helen Rivers put the conspiracy in a window and lit it with afternoon sun and made Vee cry happy tears at it, and made ME feel warm about it, and I want to scream. The lining, too — a soft wall set into "the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away." The dress makes decisions. The dress decides what's given away. Do you hear yourself, book. I hear you.

And then the mirror scene, which is Vee's whole arc in miniature and I'd teach it if I taught anything: *Floozy* flares up — mother's voice, automatic, right on schedule — and she stays. She makes herself look. And then the underwear. First thought: small steps, hide it. Second thought comes up "hot behind the first and didn't care what showed." She strips herself, alone, on purpose, to be looked at, and the shame crosses into wanting "almost before it was shame at all." Nobody in the room. Nobody asking. This is her doing it to herself now — chapter 20 me wrote "the corruption isn't being done to her anymore; it's teaching her its own hands" and here it is, bare leg to the hip, and the horrible beautiful thing is it reads as pure victory. It IS victory. Both things. Always both things in this book.

The heat this chapter is quicker than the Friday-night showcase but it's right — the leg hooked around him, "nothing left for either of them to decide," and honestly the sexiest beat isn't the sex, it's WAIT, THE DRESS, both of them stopping mid-tear-off to hang it like something alive.
