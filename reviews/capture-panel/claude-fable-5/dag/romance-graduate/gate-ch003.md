REACTION —

Okay, so I've been waiting to see if this book could keep doing the thing, and chapter three just did a different thing, and I'm a little rattled by how well it worked on me.

First: the opening. He asks her "would you like to taste another woman" while he's *inside* her and she comes so hard from the question alone that her body outs her before her brain can lawyer it. That is the hottest thing in the book so far and there's no spanking in it, no bench, no hardware — just a question landing in an undefended body. This author understands that the actual erogenous zone is the secret. Every KU book I've read would have staged the threesome offer as banter over brunch. Here it's a detonation, and then — this is the part that got me — we watch Randi *catch* the wanting mid-air and re-gift it. "Only in a threesome. With you." Same act, different owner. The book literally says it: not a woman who hungers for another woman, which is unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which is merely sexy. I had to put my phone down. I have known that girl. I have maybe *been* that girl. The precision of that maneuver — the way she launders her own desire through his — that's the interiority I keep begging literary fiction for, and it's happening in a chapter that opens with his mouth still wet from her.

The pointing game itself: I went back and forth. There's a stretch on the quad where I felt the author enjoying her own scenery — the maple that's "gone over all at once," the colonnade shadows — and I thought, careful, don't turn into one of those books. But then I realized the catalog of rejected girls is doing real work. Every "no" is Randi telling us who she is. Not the mirror of herself. Not the sealed pairs. Not the closed project. Not the girl who performs ease *at* people — and God, the irony of Randi rejecting a girl for performed warmth, when Randi is a performance in a cardigan. She doesn't hear herself. Pace maybe does. I did. That's the book trusting me, and I'll follow a book that trusts me a long way.

And then Vee. This is where the jacket copy finally clicked into place and my stomach dropped in the good-bad way. The blurb told me Vee is the protagonist — Vee, twenty-one, quietly starving, steered by her glamorous new friend Randi "as if by accident." And now I've just watched the accident get *manufactured*. I watched Randi execute a shoulder-check so clean the victim apologized. The jacket promised I'd be ahead of Vee from the start and I am so far ahead of her it hurts. She came out of that dining hall laughing with her whole throat bared to the sky — the only person on that quad not holding the wheel — and the two most controlled people in the book just picked her like fruit. "Let's go feed you," he says at the end, to Randi, and I actually said "oh no" out loud, because feeding is what he does. He fed Randi thin apple slices. He's going to feed Vee to Randi, or Randi to Vee, or both of them to something, and everyone will say yes the whole way down. Every yes was freely given. That was the problem. I get it now.

Things I'm holding with suspicion: Pace. Two chapters ago I found his attention swoony — the folded cardigan, the water bottle left in advance. Now I've watched him decide, mid-sex, to ask a destabilizing question at the exact moment her defenses were down, and then congratulate himself on his own gentleness about it. He keeps narrating his restraint like it's a gift he's giving, and the book keeps letting me feel how good his attention feels while showing me it's also *surveillance*. He read her flinch. He reads everything. The Daphne backstory in chapter one said he learned not to open closed doors — but pointing at strangers on a quad to surface a woman's buried queerness is just opening the door from the inside, isn't it? I don't trust him and I don't want to stop watching him, which I assume is exactly where Helen Rivers wants me.

Also — Randi's happy footwork on the brick after meeting Vee, the un-decided half-second of it. That's the realest thing she's done in three chapters, and it happened because of a girl, not because of Pace. The book is keeping a ledger and I'm keeping one too.

One small ding: no heat in the back half, and I felt its absence a little — the quad walk is charged but it's cerebral charge. I'll allow it because the front half paid the toll in full, and because the pointing game *is* foreplay, just foreplay for something two chapters away.
