The debrief chapter. Of course there's a debrief chapter — the jacket promised me "lunches too intimate to name" and now I finally get to sit in one KNOWING what I know, and it's so much worse and so much better than I wanted.

Let me start with the thing that made my skin crawl in the best way: "How was he?" Randi. RANDI. Asking how Pace was in bed. She knows how he is. She's had him on a bench he built for her, she begged in her real voice, she cried, she locked the whole thing behind a mirror — and now she's leaning across a brunch table with her chin on her hands asking her recruit to describe him, "patient, gentle, hungry, like there was no clock anywhere in the world," and she has to sit there and receive it with her face open and warm and glad. What is that costing her? Or — and this is the part I can't decide and it's eating me — what is it *feeding* her? The chapter is called "Fed" and Vee thinks she's the one who got the meal. Randi is the one being fed. She's living on this. "I'm running on fumes here" — she said it as a joke and I don't think it was a joke. Her own story, the beautiful ex, the boy in a hurry — that's the second fabricated-or-curated bad-man story she's installed (I clocked the first one, the "Paul?" chapter), and it does the same work: every other man is nervous and rushed, Pace is the only one on earth with no clock in him. She's building the cathedral and lowering the surrounding skyline at the same time.

And the interrogation technique — I've now watched it twice and I could teach a seminar. Chapter 17 she pressed until Vee's secret was half out and then closed the door — "You don't have to tell me" — left her holding it, unspent. This time she doesn't close the door. This time she works the toes, the plum, the underwear, walks Vee right up to the middle and then — pivot — doesn't take the confession, *reframes* it. "You knew how that night was going to end before you ever got in the car. So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" And Vee says "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." And Randi says "Good" and puts her hand over hers.

Here's what I keep turning over at 1 a.m.: that's the exact inverse of the Chapter 10 move. Back then Randi took Vee's proudest "I did it on purpose" and rewrote it into "you couldn't help it, because of him." Now she takes Vee's shame and rewrites it into agency. And the horrible thing is — the second rewrite is TRUE. Vee did want it, did take it, and that sentence is the healthiest thing this girl has ever said out loud. The book keeps doing this to me: the manipulation and the medicine come out of the same bottle. Randi is genuinely giving Vee something her mother spent twenty years taking away, and she's doing it because a confident Vee is a more useful Vee, and I cannot get the two motives to separate, and I've started to suspect Randi can't either. The stillness at "he looked at me and I was fine" last lunch, the fumes line, the way her attention is "wound a notch tighter" — the leak is widening. She wanted this girl before there was a plan; the plan is just the plumbing the want runs through.

The kiss at the curb. Vee *met* it this time. Met it, and didn't scan the windows after. Two lunches ago she stood on that curb doing paranoid arithmetic on every dark window; now she just drives home with the radio up. The book is moving her toward Randi in these tiny load-bearing increments and Vee's filing every one under "French thing" and I want to shake her and I also want to watch every second of where this goes, because the jacket told me she wants Randi too and I'm watching her not-know it in real time, which is exactly the merciless thing I was sold.

Randi has made herself the only room where Vee's real life can be spoken. That's the whole apparatus in one sentence. Cassie gets the ladled-out soup; Randi gets the pot.
