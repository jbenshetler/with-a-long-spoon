REACTION

Two pages. I've never read a two-page chapter in my life and I just found out what one is for: it's for a sleepless night. You don't get fifteen pages of a girl not sleeping — you get exactly as long as it takes her to put the shirt on skin, breathe him, realize the heat is her own given back, and take it off into the cold. The chapter is the length of the wound. Okay, Rivers. Fine. It's a technique. I'm annoyed at how well it works.

Because here's what she did to me. Chapter 41, the shirt was the most romantic thing in the book — "wear it all weekend, give it back Sunday smelling like you," him pulling it on over the shirt he already wore, no argument, and I cried a little, and per my own standing policy I checked it for wires and couldn't find any. Four chapters later she hands me the wire. *The heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it.* That is the whole novel in one sentence about a flannel. Everything he gives her is her own warmth returned — the meals built from her mentioned magazine, the drawer, the dress, the attention — and she's across town in the dark discovering that a smell is not a person and a curriculum is not a name. And the shirt down in a heap, not folded, not set back on the pillow — "where a dropped thing lands." She has never once left one of his objects uncared-for. That heap is the loudest thing in forty-five chapters.

And I have to talk about the run-up, because this little chapter is the third beat of something that's been flaying me since the porch. She built a sentence for weeks — crackers and a meal, the best sentence she owns, her one attempt — and he came back with "I love how good we are together," and I actually said *no* out loud in my bed. Not cold. Worse than cold. Warm, adjacent, and precisely one degree off true, and she felt the *not the same shape* of it and filed it in the pile she doesn't look at. That's the most emotionally intelligent rendering of a deflected almost-I-love-you I've read anywhere, lit fic included. And now I'm doing the thing the book has trained me to do, which is hold two readings at once and hate both: either Peter is a man with a Daphne-shaped hole where the word lives, genuinely unable — the flinch, the scar, "I don't go back" — or the word is being *withheld on schedule*, because a girl kept reaching is a girl kept climbing, and "she's nearly ready" needs her exactly this hungry. The gentle spanking made me believe in Peter. The porch made me afraid of the machine again. Rivers will not rule, and I've stopped expecting her to, and I resent how much that non-ruling is the reason I can't quit.

Genre me has to note: that's now three quiet chapters in a row by my usual count — cafeteria, porch, and this — with 44's real, paid-in-full sex scene sitting in the middle of them like a hinge, and normally three quiet ones means I'm skimming for dialogue. I'm not skimming. But I can feel the ledger: no Randi in five chapters, the debrief still owed, the nude still unaccounted for, MIRA sitting there like an unexploded shell, and the book cashing check after check on "he won't say it." That well is deep but it is not bottomless. One more chapter of her aching alone at 3 a.m. without the plot's other hand moving and I'll start feeling managed instead of moved.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: she slept naked in the cold on purpose rather than sleep in a lie, and Cassie breathed on three feet below her the whole time, the one person who'd say the word first and mean it, and Vee would rather freeze. That's the book. That's the whole cruel tender book.
