REACTION

Okay, so this is the afterglow chapter — third quiet-ish chapter counting the swim and the roommate scene, except it isn't quiet, it's three rounds and a photo shoot, and I read every word anyway because Rivers has trained me to check the wallpaper for wires. And there's a wire. There's a big fat wire and it's the phone.

Let me have the good stuff first, because there's real good stuff. The way he poses her — moves her body himself, kiss to the shoulder, kiss to the hip, kiss to the knee — that's the exact grammar this book has been building since the fitting: his hands set her, his mouth follows, she stops arguing. And "he reached across her to the nightstand and took up her phone, not his, because hers took the better picture" — Rivers, you sly thing, that's a fig leaf and a chain of custody in one sentence. The photo lives on HER phone. She sent it TO him. Every single yes was freely given. That is the jacket copy operating at the level of a text message and I felt genuinely cold reading "watched the bar cross and the word under it go to Delivered." Delivered. To a man who debriefs in bed with the woman who described Vee's body to him by dragging her own across his chest. "I'd never share your photograph." I read that promise four times. He didn't say I'd never *describe* it. He didn't say Randi won't be lying next to me when I open it. And Vee is the one who set the terms — "only if you promise" — so when this goes wrong she'll be able to say, correctly, that he kept his word to the letter, which is this book's whole horror in one clause. Also: she already sent him the induction photo, uncropped, "that part was hers." She's building him an archive. She thinks she's the curator.

And the thing that actually got me under the ribs: she went to the bathroom and PUT HER FACE BACK ON first. He said "I like you exactly like this," and she went anyway — she manufactured the version of herself she wanted kept. That's not the machine, that's Vee, that's the mother's daughter, doing self-editing that nobody installed. Except then the picture comes back and she loves it *because it's his* — "her as his eyes had her" — and there it is, the transfer complete: she can only find herself beautiful through his lens now. That's tender and it's terrifying and Rivers refuses to pick one, which is why I'm still here at chapter 38.

The heat itself — honest accounting — was warm rather than scorching. Round one happens in the first paragraph, rounds two and three happen in white space, and I noticed. That's four IOUs now on the long scene? Five? The book keeps writing checks against sex it's already had off-page. Chapter 32 finally paid real principal so I'm not enraged, but the pattern is back and my trigger finger knows it. The chicken piccata got more granular choreography than the sex did. (The chicken piccata was, admittedly, gorgeous. I could smell the lemon. I'm going to gain weight reading this book.)

And then the last page, which is where the chapter earned its keep: "I don't go back." Each word set down clean and apart. That's the desk in the hallway, that's Daphne, that's Ohio-and-casseroles slamming shut — and Vee, bless her, takes her hand off it and *lets the quiet be kind*, which is the most emotionally intelligent thing anyone's done in this book that wasn't a technique. Then Sheri at Thanksgiving. SHERI. The one clean person in the novel is coming to his table with a pie, and Vee's flicker of jealousy went to the wrong woman and died in five seconds — jealous of the cyclist, serene about the Evil Queen, still tasting pastry glaze she never touched. Girl. The wire isn't in the pie.
