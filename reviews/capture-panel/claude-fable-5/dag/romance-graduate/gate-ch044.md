The shirt. God, the shirt. She built that ritual herself back in the warm chapter — washed it, made him wear it all weekend to refill it with himself, moved in by accessories — and I wrote in my own notes that nobody installed it, or everything did. And now here's the bill. She takes off her own shirt to get his against her bare skin, and it works, it always works, and then the one clean true perception this poor girl has had in weeks arrives like ice water: *the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it.* That is the whole book in one sentence. That is the whole BOOK. She leaves it in a heap — not folded, and in this book, in HIS grammar, where feeling lives in objects and everything is folded and placed and provisioned, an unfolded shirt in a heap is a scream.
