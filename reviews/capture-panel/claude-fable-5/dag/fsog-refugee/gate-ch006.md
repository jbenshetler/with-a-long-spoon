REACTION

Oh, this chapter is small and I loved it anyway, and I'm annoyed at myself for how easily it got me.

"May I call you" and then he actually texts within twenty minutes — no games, no three-day rule, no manufactured scarcity. Randi's "the rule's for boys you're not sure about" is exactly right and I hate that it's *her* saying it, because she's right about everything and she's right about it for reasons Vee can't see. That's the thing this book keeps doing to me. Every individual beat is the thing I've been hunting for in this genre for years — a man who asks, who waits out the whole sentence, who lets a true thing have its space without making it large — and the whole time I'm reading it with the jacket copy sitting in my stomach like a stone. *She doesn't know there's a plan.* The froyo date is genuinely one of the sweetest first dates I've read in ages, and it exists because two people played a pointing game on a quad and steered a collision.

The overfilled cup wrecked me a little. "Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." That's the blurb's "quietly starving" made literal, and it's so unshowy about it. A girl who portions herself down to nothing, letting the cup lean, biting her lip, deciding she doesn't care if it drips. And then "Your tart's holding up" as his answer to *this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time* — he didn't pretend, he didn't inflate it, he just pointed at the thing still standing. That's the tenderness I read for. It's also, and I can't stop clocking this, exactly the move he ran on the calculus and exactly the move he ran on Randi on the bench: find the hunger, feed it precisely, watch it land. He's *good*. He's good the way a craftsman is good, and Vee is now on the workbench, and I don't fully know yet whether the book knows that's chilling or thinks it's only beautiful. The jacket says it knows — "psychologically merciless in its tenderness," "that was the problem" — so I'm extending credit. But I'm watching.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: the scariest thing in this book so far isn't the bench, it's that Randi took the phone out of Vee's hand. Just took it. Read the text herself. Vee didn't blink. And Cassie — Cassie with her "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter, Cassie who rolled a healer for her for a semester, Cassie who names what she sees in four flat words — Cassie is the only person at that table who loves Vee with no plan attached, and I can feel the book slowly walking her out of the frame, and it's making me genuinely sad in advance.

Also: "He looked like a man who wants you" — Randi saying that about a man she was begging facedown five chapters ago, grinning while she hands the phone back. The layers on that woman. I don't know if I want her caught or saved and apparently neither does she.
