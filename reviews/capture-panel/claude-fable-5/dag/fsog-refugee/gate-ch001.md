REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I went into this braced — the jacket practically dares you, "every yes was freely given, that was the problem," which is exactly the kind of line that usually means the book is about to sell me coercion in a nicer dress. And then the first chapter is... this.

Let me say the thing first: this is what I've been hunting for. Years. The apple sliced thin because he noticed she eats thin slices faster when the cut sides show — that's the whole genre I want, in one bowl. He's not dominant AT her. He built a room where her wanting fits. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." I actually put the book down for a second there. Do you know how many doms in these books treat the safeword like paperwork? He treats it like a gift he's asking her to keep in her hand. And he took her heels off in the middle of the spanking. Crouched at her feet and unbuckled them one at a time, mid-scene, because he was thinking about her ankles. I'm not okay.

And the edging scene — the "please" thing. When she begs the first time and he says no, I got nervous. That's the exact hinge where these books usually tip into cruelty, where "not yet" becomes about his power instead of her arrival. But he wasn't withholding to break her. He was waiting for her to be there. "There you are." I felt that in my sternum. He wanted the real one, not the performance, and the book let me feel the difference between the waiter-please and the please that comes from below. Rendered from inside her body, mostly through his attention, which is a trick I didn't know could work like that.

Then the name. RANDI. I gasped out loud. The whole chapter I'm thinking this is Vee, this is our girl, and then — no. This is the glamorous friend. This is the woman who, per the jacket, is going to help steer a twenty-one-year-old into this man's path as a game. And suddenly the whole gorgeous chapter recolors itself. Because now I've watched what Pace can do — he can find you behind your own face — and I've watched Randi decide, at the sink, "not ever," deadbolt going over, the face going back up permanently. She got seen once, it nearly killed her, and she's never letting it happen again. So what does a woman like that do? She finds him a girl who CAN be seen. She feeds him Vee the way he fed her the apple. Oh my god. The plan isn't a game, it's a deflection. It's Randi outsourcing her own annihilation to someone with less armor. I would text my friend at 1 a.m.: THE FRIEND IS THE ONE WHO COULDN'T SURVIVE HIM.

The details that are going to haunt me: her pressing the bottle cap edge under her thumbnail, slow, where it would hurt, not knowing she's doing it. Her flinching at "you're beautiful" — not at the spanking, not at the begging, at the tenderness. The tenderness is the thing she can't take. That's psychologically exact in a way that scared me a little, because I know that girl. A lot of us know that girl. And the mother with the egg whites and the knife in her voice, and the appetite trimmed to almost nothing — the food stuff and the sex stuff are the same wound and the book knows it without underlining it.

What I'm suspicious of, and I want to be honest: Pace ends the chapter wrong about everything. "She had finally let someone feed her." No, baby, she went into the bathroom and re-armored for life and you smiled at the armor. His care is real AND his read is catastrophically off, and the book is letting me see both. Also — Daphne. A door he didn't respect, that cost him. So he's overcorrected into never knocking, which sounds like respect and functioned, tonight, as the exact gap Randi escaped through. That's the kind of moral complication I trust. Nobody is villained. Everybody is precise. And the bench sitting there at the end, "full of what it was, waiting for the next time" — for VEE, presumably, and I already ache for a girl I haven't met.

I'm not fully at ease. The jacket promises this all gets done TO someone who doesn't know there's a plan, and I reserve the right to bolt if the book starts winking at that instead of hurting about it. But this chapter earned my nervousness instead of my disgust. The consent was airtight and the heat came THROUGH the consent, not around it. That's the unicorn. I'm in.
