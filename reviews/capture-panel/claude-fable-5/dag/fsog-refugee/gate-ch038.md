REACTION

Okay, the photo. I need to talk about the photo, because I read that whole sequence with my stomach doing two different things at once.

The surface of it is the best version of a scene I've read botched a hundred times. In every other book on my shelf, the naked-picture beat is the man wheedling, the woman relenting, the camera as a little theft. Here she offers it — taps her tooth three times, her tell, her idea — and he doesn't grab the phone. He says "you'd do that?" like she's handed him something. And then the detail that got me: he uses HER phone. The picture lives on her device. She looks at it, she decides, she presses send herself and watches it go to Delivered. "Gave him herself to keep" — her verb, her hand. And the posing — moving her limb by limb and kissing each place his hands set her, shoulder, hip, knee — that's the tenderness-and-dominance-in-one-man thing I have been chasing since 2012 and almost never get. Nobody's ever going to convince me "he looked at her and not the picture of her and grinned like a boy" isn't the sexiest sentence in the chapter, and there's no sex in it.

But. I know what book I'm in. "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." "Never. I'd never share your photograph." Read that answer again. She asked him not to share HER. He promised not to share the photograph. He's a mathematician; he doesn't misplace a word. And even if he keeps that promise to the letter forever — he already shares her. He's been sharing her since before she knew there was anything to share. Every lunch Vee narrates to Randi is her being circulated; there's a whole chapter called "One Bite" about it. So the promise is technically keepable and spiritually already broken, and Vee sent the picture on the strength of it, and the book knew exactly what it was doing putting "believing him was the brave part" right there. There is now a nude in the world that exists inside a lie about who's in the room. That's the plan's second piece of hard currency, after the credit card. I wrote it in the ledger before I finished the paragraph.

And then the chapter did the thing this book does that keeps me up at night: it followed the hottest scene with the realest one. "I don't go back." Three words, the ease going out of his face, each word set down clean and apart — and she takes her hand off it, doesn't push, lets the quiet be kind. She's learning his grammar. She did for him exactly what he's always done for her: left the door shut until he opens it. I felt that more than the three rounds of sex, honestly. This boy — twenty-two, and I keep having to re-say that to myself — has parents alive enough to not-go-back-to, a scar with a surgeon's line she's decided to keep without asking about, a tutor he calls luck, and a Thanksgiving table set for himself and one other orphan. "This is my home." He built the home the way he built the dress: because nobody was going to hand him one.

The Sheri flicker was well done too — the jealousy arriving and then dying of natural causes when he says "she's alone," because his fondness is so plainly the feed-a-friend kind. Though I notice: Sheri gets Thanksgiving and Vee flies home. Sheri, who said "two of them at once, if that's not already complicated, it will be," is going to sit at his table for a whole day with a pie and her plain sentences. I'm looking forward to that offscreen conversation the way you look forward to weather.

The cooking I could read forever. She used to just eat and now she tastes and she knows whose doing that is — that's this whole book in one line, actually, and it's the line I can't decide is beautiful or terrifying. Everything she is now, she's better and freer and hungrier and it's all downstream of two people who chose her like a project. The chapter keeps handing me things that are purely good — the water before the wine, the drawer last chapter, the kiss on the knee — and I keep loving them, and I keep hearing the jacket in my other ear: *ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does.* Fine. I am. I know.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "he promised never to share her PHOTOGRAPH. the photograph. she asked about HER. I'm going to be sick and also I've never been more into him, this book is doing it to me on purpose."
