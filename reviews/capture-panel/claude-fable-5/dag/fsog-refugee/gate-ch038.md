REACTION

I read this whole chapter with my stomach slowly dropping, because I know exactly what I just watched and Vee doesn't, and the book knows I know, and that's the deal I signed up for on the jacket — but God, this one hurt.

The photo. THE PHOTO. Last chapter I sat there watching her make him promise — "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone," and him, instantly, "I'd never share your photograph" — and I thought, okay, that's the vault, that's safe, that's the one thing in this book with a lock on it. And then Randi doesn't ask. Randi never asks — thirty-eight chapters, ask count still ZERO — she does the thing that's so much worse than asking: "You don't have to show me," and slides the phone back. And the book, bless it and damn it, shows me the seam in plain text: *And that was the thing that changed her mind.* That's the whole con in one sentence. Pace's no leaves the room the second you say it — hand off her knee, palm up, empty hands. Randi's "you don't have to" is a hook shaped like a door. She engineered the flinch ("you only gave him *half*?" — that little shame-lever, filed to fit Vee exactly, because Randi has spent months learning where Vee keeps her shame) and then let Vee's own pride carry the uncropped photo across the table. Every yes was freely given. That was the problem. I've had that tagline in my head since the bookstore and this is the first chapter where I felt it in my body like a cold coin swallowed.

And here's what I keep circling at 1 a.m.: she put the phone IN Randi's hand. Randi held it. Randi went quiet over it, "past a glance, past what a verdict needed." I don't know that she sent it to herself. I have no evidence. But I sat there watching Randi hold that phone the way you watch a stranger hold your baby, and the book let the moment run just long enough that I'll be sick about it for chapters. That photo was HIS — "her as his eyes had her," she said she could only think herself beautiful in it because the looking was his — and now it's been spent at a brunch table to win an argument nobody was having. Vee broke the exact promise she extracted from him, and she doesn't even know she did it, because Randi built the room where it wouldn't feel like breaking anything.

And the thing that makes me crazy — makes me stay — is that half of what Randi said was TRUE. "You're just finally hungry out loud. You've been starving your whole life and calling it fine." That's the truest, kindest thing anyone has said to Vee in this entire book, including Pace, including Cassie. It's the absolution Vee has needed since girlhood. And it came out of the mouth of the woman who is farming her. That's the book's whole cruel gift: the food is real and the feeder has a plan. Randi took one bite of the pastry last chapter and said "Delicious" and closed the subject, and this chapter she's drinking Vee's Saturdays like they're the only meal she gets — the bright unfixed eyes, the catch in her breath she didn't smooth over, "Just us" with that roughness under it. She's not acting anymore. She slipped three separate times at that table and Vee filed every slip under "I made a story land on a glamorous woman, aren't I lucky." Randi is starving in front of a girl describing a feast, and I felt sorry for her for about four seconds before I remembered she is currently holding my girl's nude.

And the kiss. Vee leaned in. Vee took the extra half-inch FOR HERSELF, "noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again" — which is the most Vee sentence alive, the noticing and the immediate filing-away. The jacket promised me this: wanting the woman beside her without letting herself know it. It's arriving, and it's arriving as HER want, from inside her, which is the only way I'd tolerate it — except every inch of the road it's traveling on was paved by the person she's traveling toward. That's the whole book in one goodbye kiss on a sidewalk. I don't know what to do with myself about it. I want it and I want to throw the book across the room, usually in the same paragraph.

Small knife-turns I clocked: the hostess knowing "Randi?" at the door — Vee reads it as being thought of; I read it as Randi owning another room Vee walks into. Vee's card down on the check before Randi's hand could drift — the debt is compounding and she's proud of it now, "said nothing about the number," and I know what's behind that card: nothing. The anthro class — Randi saying "Done" like it costs nothing, and then, so casually, "Is Cassie in?" She's checking the perimeter. Cassie's out; "just us"; another six hours a week annexed. Cassie is being disinherited on a schedule and this chapter Vee did the paperwork herself, smiling. Her whole life is narrowing to the two people running her and it feels to her like the world finally getting saturated.

The book still hasn't winked.
