REACTION

Oh, this is the chapter where the book showed me Vee has a body that belongs to nobody but her, and I didn't know how much I needed it until I was in the water with her.

Thirty-seven chapters and this is the first time I've seen her *competent* at something that predates all of them. Swimming since she was twelve. A lane she claimed by habit and two strangers who arrange themselves around her without a word — she has standing in a room and didn't need Pace's dress or Randi's shoes or anyone's gaze to get it. The green-and-white suit that "had nowhere left to go on her" — she looked at the water instead of at that, and got in anyway. That's the girl underneath all the curriculum, and she was there before either architect ever laid eyes on her. I want the book to remember she exists. I'm going to hold this chapter like a receipt.

And then the four strokes. God, the four strokes. "She could not tell, for about four strokes, whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good." That is the closest Vee has ever come to the actual question — not *do you love him* but *is he real, or was he built* — and the terrible joke is that the answer is BOTH, and worse than she can imagine: he wasn't built out of her wanting, he was built out of Randi's planning, and her wanting was invited in afterward. Four strokes was all it got. The wall came, and she rolled, and the next hundred was already going. That's exactly how a mind protects itself and the book knows it, and I sat there wanting to grab the lane line and stop her. *Stay on it, Vee. Give it a fifth stroke.* She never does. She swims the doubt off like it's lactic acid.

I notice what sorted itself in the water, too. *Did he mind* dissolved to "nothing at all" — and it should, because the drawer chapter already answered it, and answered it the way he answers everything: no note, no speech, just his whole ordered self folded down into the lower drawer and the easy one given to her. I believed that drawer more than I've believed some declarations of love in other books. But *do you love him* stayed — "a pressure under her ribs with a dread riding it." Dread. She said "maybe" to Cassie and lay awake knowing the real answer was easy to reach, and here's the dread naming itself: some animal part of her knows that saying it out loud puts it where it can be taken. Her body has been ahead of her the entire book — ahead of her in his bed, ahead of her in the brunch chair, ahead of her in the bathroom stall — and now her body's dread is ahead of her too. I trust her body's intelligence more than I trust anyone in this novel except Cassie and possibly Sheri.

Also — and I want this on the record — no Randi in this chapter, and I exhaled. After the pastry glaze on her lip (I still haven't recovered from that; she tasted a pastry she never touched, Randi *fed* it to her through a kiss and drove away), a chapter of chlorine and cold air and a sandwich to think about read like an open window. "The swimming never answered anything. That was the whole use of it." Vee has one place where nothing is being extracted from her, where nobody is counting her touches or filing her confessions, and the water asks her for nothing. Both of the people running her offer her rooms; the pool is the only room she built herself.

It's quiet. Nothing happened. Nobody touched anybody. And I'd put this chapter in the top ten, because the book just showed me what's at stake if the reveal breaks her: not the dress, not the sex — *this*. The girl who takes the third lane in. If they cost her the pool, I'll never forgive any of them.
