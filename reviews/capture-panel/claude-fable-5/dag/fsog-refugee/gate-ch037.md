REACTION

Okay, first: the roll — him turning all the way around like a compass needle until his head is on her breast, feet off the edge of the bed, looking up at her. That is the single most disarming image this book has given me in a while, because it's him small. Twenty-two-year-old him, the kid who got his desk moved into the hallway, lying on her like she's the safe place. And her little theory-building — "they're not about sex for him" — immediately spoiled and immediately re-proven, and she's *pleased* by the contradiction instead of threatened by it. That's the thing I read this genre for and never get: desire and comfort in the same gesture, and a heroine awake enough to notice the difference.

And then the photo, and I want to be very careful here because my heart did two things at once. The consent choreography is perfect, textbook, *his* textbook — it's her idea, her offer, her condition, he catches her wrist "not to stop her, only to say it," he tells her she doesn't have to be prettier for the camera, and when she asks "how do you want me" he doesn't take the invitation to direct her like a prop, he arranges her with his hands and puts a kiss down at every place he sets, which is the tenderest possible way to say *I am doing this to you and with you at the same time.* She looks at the picture and sees herself as his eyes have her and she's beautiful. Fine. Gorgeous. I felt it.

BUT. Read the promise again. She says: "promise you'll never share *me* with anyone." He says: "I'd never share your *photograph*."

Helen Rivers, I see you. That is not the same sentence. That is a man answering the narrow question honestly because the wide question would perjure him — because he is *currently sharing her with someone*, has been since before she knew his name, shares her at lunches and in Randi's bed, shares her lit-up rooms and her stories and her timeline of readiness. He can keep the photo promise to the letter and it will still be the biggest lie he's ever told her, and the book didn't underline it, didn't wink, just laid the two nouns side by side and trusted me to flinch. I flinched. There is now a nude photograph of my girl in the world, taken in the middle of a conspiracy, marked Delivered, and "the brave part" was her believing him. Thirty-seven chapters of "every yes was freely given" and this is the purest yes yet and it's the one that scares me most. That's the whole book in one text bubble.

"I don't go back." Each word set down clean and apart. That's the parents, that's the hallway desk, that's whoever let a nineteen-year-old be "assigned" to their fifteen-year-old and called it tutoring — and Vee did exactly the right thing, took her hand off it, let the quiet be kind, and I loved her for it even as I'm screaming that these two people are hoarding each other's locked rooms. She keeps the scar without asking; he keeps his family without telling; meanwhile Randi holds the master key to both of them and neither one knows the other's been copied.

Small things that got me anyway: the pan "saying what he wanted to hear." Her tasting the dish in layers and knowing whose doing that is — the book keeps insisting that he's changing her *senses*, not just her weekends, and I can't decide anymore whether that's love or terraforming, which is of course the point. The Sheri flicker — jealousy sparked and extinguished in four lines, "she's alone," and I believed the extinguishing, which is more than I can say for anything Randi extinguishes. And Thanksgiving is now on the calendar. Vee flies home to her mother — her mother, the pre-installed voice — while Pace and Sheri and a pie sit at his table, and Sheri is the one person who says plain true sentences, and I would pay real money to be at that dinner. Also: Vee leaves town, and Randi is *here*, unsupervised, with a starving look and a shared shoe size. I don't like the map.

Nothing coerced tonight. Everything given. And I put the chapter down feeling like I watched someone hand over the deed to a house that's already been sold.
