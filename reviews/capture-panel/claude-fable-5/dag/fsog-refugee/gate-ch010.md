REACTION

Okay. So this is the chapter where the book showed me the knife and dared me to keep watching it get used.

"Paul?" She *snapped her fingers* like she couldn't retrieve his name. Randi. Who begged that man on a bench he built with his hands. Who was texting him under the table in the coffee shop. The performance is so casual, so cheap, so *effective*, and I actually put the book down for a second because I felt it in my stomach — not the manipulation itself, but how good it felt to Vee. That's the thing this book keeps doing to me. It won't let the manipulation be cold. It makes every move land as warmth on the receiving end, and I know what the warmth is made of, and Vee doesn't, and I sat there in the smoothie shop with her feeling picked and chosen and sick.

The date-disaster story. Was there even a date? I don't believe there was a date. "A man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else" — that's not girl talk, that's a *frame*, installed right before Vee tells her story, so that Pace's listening gets read as care and filed as rare. Randi is teaching Vee how to interpret Pace. She's doing the exact thing Pace did with the pencil — staying out of it so the conclusion feels like Vee's own — except Pace was handing her a math problem she got to solve herself, and Randi is handing her a *self*, pre-solved.

And here's where I nearly walked. Vee had the sentence. She HAD it. "I made an exception." I loved that sentence in chapter 8 — I loved her spinning into the beanbag telling Cassie "nobody did anything to me, I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That was the best thing in this whole book, a girl claiming her own yes like a flag. And Randi leans in, soft, "near tender," and swaps it: *because you couldn't not. Because of him.* And Vee — who owned it two chapters ago — gives it up. "…Yeah. Because of him." Randi took the agency version of the kiss out of Vee's mouth and replaced it with the helpless version, and Vee thanked her for it by feeling interesting. I want to scream. That is the precise thing I read this genre hoping someone will finally NOT do, and the book did it on purpose, with full knowledge, and made me watch the substitution happen word by word. The chapter before this one was literally called "Substitution." I see you, Helen Rivers. I don't know whether to be impressed or to throw the book across the room.

And Cassie. Oh, Cassie. "I'll see you at the dorm," easy, already going, whatever was in her eyes *folded away* — and then Vee does the isolating herself. "It isn't really her place." Randi didn't even have to say it. That's the part that scares me most, because that's how it actually works — nobody cuts your people off, they just build a room your people wouldn't like, and you do the cutting and call it healthy, and "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." Cassie said *Goodbye, Vee* in chapter 4 and I didn't want it to mean what it meant and it means it.

What's keeping me here, and I want to be honest about this: the book is not winking. That's my line — coercion played sexy with the author elbowing me in the ribs — and this isn't that. The prose is cold-eyed about every move. It flags Vee's flinches, it shows me the turn-over-and-let-it-lie, it names the cost. And "dear friend," the cool damp hand on the white table in front of Brooke — I genuinely cannot tell how much of that was chess and how much was real, and neither can Randi, I suspect, and the jacket promised me that's the whole engine: it gets real for the ones who started it. Randi spending status on Vee in front of her own president reads like a move AND like the first thing Randi's done that wasn't in anyone's script. That ambiguity is the hook in my cheek.

But I'm putting the book on notice, in my head at least. Pace's consent practice is still the realest I've read — the *may I*, the water bottle, the door he wouldn't knock on. And it coexists with him being half of a plan Vee doesn't know exists, and the book is asking me to hold both, and right now I can, because Vee's yeses are still hers even if the room they happen in was staged. The day the staging reaches into the yes itself — the day one of them takes something Vee couldn't have said no to because the no was engineered out of her — I'm done. This chapter walked right up to that line when Randi rewrote the kiss. Right up to it.

Also, 1 a.m. text to my best friend: Brooke showing up at the Smoothie Bird. Was that a coincidence? I don't believe in coincidences in this book anymore. That's what it's done to me in ten chapters — I read a warm hug on a sidewalk and I check its pockets.
