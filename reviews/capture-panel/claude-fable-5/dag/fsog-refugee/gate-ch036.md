REACTION

Oh, Cassie. Cassie opened the door and Vee walked through it, and I've been waiting eleven chapters for that.

Look, this is a small chapter — a school-night landing, a bean bag, a book turned facedown — and I don't care, because it's the chapter I flagged as my hill. Vee came *home*. She sat backward in the chair and gave Cassie the shape of her weeks, unfiltered, no HUMBLE PIE choreography, no Randi-issued vocabulary. And Cassie did the thing Cassie does that nobody else in this book does: she asked about *Vee*. Not the mechanics, not the spice level, not "walk me through the good part." Two questions. "You don't come home school nights." "Do you love him." That's it. That's the whole interrogation, and it did more honest work than thirty chapters of Randi's brunches.

Because here's what gutted me, reading it fresh off chapter 34: put these two listeners side by side. Randi extracts. She narrows her eyes, fond, relentless, and pulls the confession out one thread at a time and feeds on it — and Vee leaves those lunches soaked and ashamed and more tangled than she came. Cassie listens with *no face on at all* and lets Vee talk herself out, and then names the two true things in the room — you're not sleeping in your bed, and you might be in love — and then lets the maybe *stand*. Doesn't push it, doesn't collect it, doesn't file it for later use. "Go to sleep, streetlight." That's what a friend who isn't running a project on you sounds like, and the book knows it, and it put this chapter right here so I'd feel the difference in my teeth.

And "maybe. I think so. It's all so fast" — oh, honey. She said "Peter" into the dark last chapter and decided she could live inside the everything-he-does without the saying. But out loud, to Cassie, it shrinks to a maybe. She can be surer of him in his bed than in her own room, and I don't know if that's romance or a symptom. Probably both. That's this book's whole trick, isn't it — everything is both.

The tomato burn detail — Cassie's "not enough on," dry as toast — she *clocked* it. She clocks everything. She knows exactly how her friend is spending her school nights and she's not judging it, she's just... watching Vee run out of words, which Vee never does, and letting it sit. There's a Hrm under this whole chapter that never got said, and I felt it anyway.

What worries me, because something always worries me now: Vee gave Cassie the cooking and the watching and the warmth. She did not give her Randi. Not the brunch, not the kiss at the curb, not the glaze on her lip that came from a pastry she never touched — I'm still not over that, by the way, that's the most quietly deranged image this book has produced and Vee has told NO ONE. So this was real, and it was warm, and it was still curated. The Pace half of her life is now open to Cassie. The Randi half is a locked room even Vee won't look into. The edging-out isn't reversed; it's just that one channel reopened.

But I'll take it. After the brunch chapter left me needing a shower in the good light, this was a glass of juice. The book keeps doing that — Pace hands her water after, Cassie hands her plainness after — the people who actually care about Vee are the ones who make her drink something before anything else.

Thin chapter, honest chapter. Two pages of my favorite character doing her one job perfectly.
