She walked out. She actually walked out, into the cold, coat buttoned to the throat, and he let her go with one "Vee" — and I'm sitting here with my heart pounding like I'm the one standing on that mat.

I've been waiting for this fight since chapter 43, since the cracker-and-meal sentence went out into the air and came back as "I love how good we are together." I watched her build to it — the dryer, the shirt dropped in a heap, two nights of not-sleeping — and when she pulled in and left the bag in the car, I said *oh*, out loud. She carried nothing in. She wasn't going to let the house do its work on her. The house tried anyway — the heat brought up hours early so she'd never once be cold, and "it went through her now, and she hated that it did" — God, that line. That's the whole book. Every kindness in that house is real AND is climate control, and she's finally standing in the doorway feeling both at once.

And here's what's tearing me in half: he didn't do anything wrong by my old rules and he still failed her completely. He didn't touch her without leave. He didn't block the door. He didn't punish. He stood there and took her question seriously and gave her the truest things he had — "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?" — and I wanted to throw the book, because it's TRUE and it's a dodge, and she saw it was both, "he'd made the true thing pull against the point." That's not the evasion of a boy who doesn't know. That's the evasion of a man who *can't answer*. And I know why he can't, and she doesn't, and this is the first chapter where the dramatic irony physically hurt me instead of thrilling me. "What am I to you *out there*?" Vee, sweetheart, out there you are a project two people started over a game. He cannot name you in public because the name would be perjury. "Words are cheap" from a mathematician — a man who doesn't misplace a word, who put a loophole in a photograph promise — words are not cheap to him. Words are the one currency he refuses to counterfeit. He'd rather starve her of the sentence than say it inside the lie. Some awful corner of me respects that and the rest of me wants to shake him until the truth falls out.

The arms folding killed me. "The still of a man holding a line at cost." What line, Peter? Is it "I don't go back" — the parents, whatever words got spent and broken in that house he doesn't return to? Is it the plan — can't say *I love you* to a woman who doesn't know what she said yes to? Both tickets I've been holding just got warmer in my fist. And then the crying, and the cold going out of him instantly, the hand lifting — and the book, bless it and damn it, names the move itself: "Not the hand in place of the word." Forty-five chapters of his objects-as-language, the dress, the drawer, the shirt worn double in his own kitchen, and this is the chapter where the book turns and says: the objects were never going to be enough, and he knew it, and he kept paying in that currency anyway because it's the only one he'd spend.

And she refused it. She refused the hand. She took her crying out the door rather than let him have it "on top of everything else he had of her and would not name." That's the strongest thing she's done in the whole book — stronger than the long step, stronger than the flat hand on the recliner — because this time she wasn't performing for a room or managing anyone. She priced the comfort and declined it. Fifty Shades' Ana walked out at the end of book one too, and I remember how that felt like the first honest beat in the whole thing. This felt like that, except I believe this one, because this book earned every inch of the doorway.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: he let her go. He didn't follow her into the cold, didn't grab, didn't stand in front of the car. Is that respect or is that the same withholding wearing respect's coat? I genuinely can't tell and I think that's the point. And — where's Randi? Vee walked out of that house with nowhere warm to go and a woman across town whose entire craft is being the warm place. If the next chapter is Vee crying in Randi's kitchen I am going to be terrified and I am going to read every word of it.
