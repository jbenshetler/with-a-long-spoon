Oh, this chapter was for *me*. Twelve chapters in, Helen Rivers knew exactly which of my worries had been sitting heaviest and handed me Cassie — warm, dry, in her sweats, with Vee's blood type memorized. I actually laughed out loud at "I had your blood type" and then got a lump in my throat about it two lines later, because that's what love with no plan attached sounds like. Cassie has been getting edged out of frame for chapters and I was braced for the book to just quietly disappear her, and instead: Vee comes home soaked and wrecked and glowing, and it's *Cassie's* chair she falls into. Not a text to Randi. Not the lunch too intimate to name. Cassie gets the story first, and she gets it raw and complete and un-curated — the blister, the second toe, the "I think there's a whole situation under there." That's the real friendship. The book knows it's the real friendship. That's what scares me.

Because here's the thing I keep circling at — Vee told this story in her own words. "And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." That's her sentence. That's the sentence structure Randi stole at the Smoothie Bird — "Because you couldn't not. Because of him" — and here, with Cassie, Vee gets it back. Nobody rewrites it. Cassie doesn't reframe, doesn't draw her further open, doesn't take the agency and hand back a prettier version. She just goes still and rapt and then says "Vee." Soft. Like setting something down where it wouldn't break. I had to put the book on my chest for a second. THAT is what listening looks like when it isn't harvesting. The book just showed me, side by side, the difference between the friend who receives Vee's story and the friend who edits it — and Vee can't see the difference yet, and I can, and that's the whole machine of this novel working on me again.

And the truck scene, retold — it holds up secondhand, which is its own test. When Vee says "he got out, into the rain, so I wouldn't have anybody looking at me," and then "I called him back in" — the sequence matters so much. He removed his own gaze first. She *summoned* it. Even filtered through a giddy debrief, the consent architecture is intact, and Cassie — the smoke detector, the girl who never took to Randi — doesn't beep. Cassie hears the whole thing and lands on "keep the shirt." If Cassie's instincts are the book's early-warning system, and Cassie signs off on Pace-as-experienced-by-Vee, then the book is telling me what I already believe: what's rotten isn't in the treatment. It's in the provenance. Every gesture is real and the frame is a lie, and Cassie can't smell the frame because Vee doesn't know there is one.

"He looked at me like I'd handed him something." God. She did hand him something. And he received it like it was hers to give, which it was — and also he's half of a plan that decided months ago she'd be handing things over, which she doesn't know. I hold both of those in my hands every chapter and they never get easier to hold.

Small things I'm tucking away: Vee's "I got up a mountain. Me. I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life" — that's the pool girl, the body that was a problem, getting somewhere *on her own legs*, and it's not about Pace at all, and I loved her so much right there. The rye and horseradish getting the chapter title — the meal as sacrament again, "we didn't leave a pit," she's already speaking his liturgy. That's tender and it's also colonization of her vocabulary and I noticed. And "I'm keeping the shirt" / "Keep the shirt" — the flannel as the first thing in this whole story Vee took without asking, kept without permission, and the book let her have it clean.
