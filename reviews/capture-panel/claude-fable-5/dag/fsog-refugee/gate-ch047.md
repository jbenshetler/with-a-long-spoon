Two women each convinced the other one is living. That's the loneliest structure this book has built yet and it built it out of two texts I watched get composed.

And this run — 42 through 47 — has quietly become the hardest stretch of the book for me, because the thing being withheld isn't sex or safety, it's a *word*, and Pace's withholding of it is starting to rhyme with things I don't like. "I love how good we are together." I've been sitting with that sentence since the porch and it's a sibling to "I'd never share your photograph" — technically warm, structurally evasive, answering a narrower question than the one asked. She fed him her best sentence, the crackers and the meal, memorized like a poem, and he returned a compliment about *compatibility*. About what her body does. He didn't just decline to say the word — he left her fantasy life undefended, and the intruder walked into her *best memory* and sat down in her chair. The fra diavolo night, the one I've been carrying since it was rumors, and she can't keep it. That gutted me more than the fight did.

He is *sharing her with Randi right now*.
