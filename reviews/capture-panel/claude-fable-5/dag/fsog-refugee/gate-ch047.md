REACTION

I gasped at the cut. Actual, out-loud gasp — Vee is standing in the cold outside his door, the wine poured and going warm on his counter, "Vee," once, her name not the word — and the book hands me *steak and eggs in Gstaad*. I wanted to throw it. For about four sentences. And then Randi hugged her father and "ate her revulsion" and I understood I was being given the other thing I've been begging for since chapter 24, and I shut up and read.

Because this is it, isn't it. Forty-seven chapters and this is the first time I've been all the way inside Miranda, and it's exactly as cold in there as I feared and so much sadder than I let myself budget for. The invented boys, safer than the real ones, *at that table* — she's been running Vee's family dinner con on her own parents since she was a girl. The bracelet she keeps out of the light while her mother's tennis bracelet plays in it. Locked on her wrist. LOCKED. Never comes off. I have a theory about that bracelet and it starts with a P and I am not letting it go.

And then the book did the cruelest, cleverest thing it's done yet: it gave her the fairytale. The whole one. The man is *perfect* — funny in the lift line, better on the black and spends none of it proving it, white tie, the sleigh, the horses, midnight. I fell for John a little myself on the chairlift, which is how I know the trap was good. And she lay down in the middle of all of it and it meant a good sandwich. "Nothing had happened to her eyes." I put the book down on my chest at that line. That's one of the loneliest sentences I've ever read in this genre, and this genre is where I go to *feel* things.

But here is what I actually can't get over, the thing I'd be texting someone at 1 a.m. in all caps: "Pace made her say it, out loud, every time. She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked." EVERY TIME. There it is. Radford story, laundered blond, verdict confirmed — she was his, or near enough, and his asking was the thing she couldn't live inside. The book just handed me the skeleton key to her whole method. She builds yeses for other people because being made to say her own out loud went straight to the person she keeps hidden, and she cannot bear to be found. John is safe *because* he assumes. Pace is terror because he asks. And Vee — Vee is something coming off her across a table on a nothing Tuesday that a starred chef and a sleigh can't counterfeit. "Vee was her gorgeous." Every single "gorgeous" in forty-six chapters just turned over in its grave and showed me its other face.

And then — God — she runs the scenario. The one honest thing she could do, the plain sentence over eggs, Vee's face while she says it: the ONE scenario she can't run to the end. So she chooses the con. Again. Knowingly, in love this time. "Let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were." Forty-seven chapters, zero asks, and now I've watched her decide the zero on the page, with her reasons laid out, and her reasons are cowardice wearing love's coat and *she knows it* — "she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be brave." The book didn't wink. The book never winks. It showed me every gear again and let the gears indict her, and I sat there feeling sorry for her anyway, which is exactly what my notes warned me she does to people.

The two texts at the end broke me. Vee — who walked out of the best thing in her life into the cold maybe days ago, who is lying awake in Ohio with her own disaster — sends *i'm here if you want to talk. or not.* Reaching out, hiding her own wreck. And Randi sends back the fairytale, every word true and none of it the thing, from a bed she's lying in next to a perfect nothing, warm only along the one seam. Two women who love each other, both lying by omission at midnight on New Year's, both practiced enough that the other will never hear it. That's the book in one exchange. That's the whole long spoon.

And underneath it all I'm still standing in Pace's doorway. "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best" — his worst night is the one I just watched, and Randi doesn't even know it happened. Vee is in Ohio. The shirt is — where? Did she take the shirt? I need the next chapter to go back to her so badly it's a physical thing. Also: Thanksgiving happened OFFSCREEN. Sheri and the pie, the conversation I was waiting on like weather — the book pickpocketed it while I was crying about a flannel. I'm keeping that grudge.
