REACTION

"Cropped." I saw the title and my stomach did the thing before the chapter even started, because I knew — I *knew* — the book was going to take the word she used on that photo and show me it's not a photo skill, it's who she's becoming. And it did. The version her mother carries in her purse stops at the waist. The version her father gets stops at "hiking and dancing." The version Cassie gets stops at — well. Cassie doesn't even get a waist. Cassie gets "both, probably both."

Here's what's eating me: Vee has become fluent in exactly the kind of editing Randi taught her by demonstration, and the book is letting her do it in the gentlest possible register — protecting her sweet burned-rolls dad from the sewing machine, protecting her mom from the bare back of that dress — so it reads as kindness, as tact, as growing up. And it IS those things. I'd have cropped the photo too; every daughter in America has cropped that photo. But I watched her run a Randi on Cassie two chapters ago and file it under kindness, and now I'm watching her run one on everybody she came from, and the terrible arithmetic is this: the only two people on earth holding an uncropped Vee are the two who took her by con. Her parents get the waist-up girl. Cassie gets the locked door. Pace and Randi get everything — the nude, the nights, the tally her body kept — and they got it inside a frame she never knew was a frame. The book didn't say one word of that out loud. It just set the dinner trays down and let me do the math myself, which is this author's whole method, and it works on me every single time.

The line that actually got me, though — got me laughing and then got me somewhere else — is the sex/sewing line. "My dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it." That is the truest thing anyone has said about fathers in forty-two chapters, and it's also Vee understanding, maybe for the first time consciously, that what she has is *unexplainable* — not shameful, she says so, "the opposite, if anything," and I believed her — but outside every frame she was raised in. Sex fits the frame. Devotion rendered as labor, a man at a machine on a sun porch because she mentioned a magazine once — that doesn't fit anywhere, and "weird is worse than wrong" because wrong can at least be rejected. She's right. And the cold finger on my neck is: a plan doesn't fit anywhere in her frame either. She has no slot for "two people chose you as a game." When the reveal comes, it won't land as *wrong* for her. It'll land as *weird* — the kind that unmakes how she sees the world — and this chapter just told me so while pretending to be about burned rolls.

And Cassie. God, Cassie, my hill. "So what is he. Not to your dad. To you." Careful, exact, not prying — she asks the real question the way she always asks the real question, one clean cut, and then she *lets the maybe stand again* and goes back to her plate. She's now let Vee off the hook twice, and both times the book gives me that little tail — Vee grateful, "also, a little, not." Some part of Vee is standing at that lunch table WANTING to be pushed, wanting one person who loves her for free to refuse the cropped version, and Cassie's respect keeps handing her back the crop. That's the tragedy in miniature: the honest friend honors her no, and the dishonest one never needed to hear it. The consent structure of her whole life is inverted and nobody in it can see the diagram but me.

Ledger notes, because I keep them: the mother collision I've been bracing for since chapter one got paid off as a collarbone touch and two rounds of crying — offscreen, softened, and honestly? Fine. The proud crying mother hurt worse than a fight would have; those parents are so ordinary and so easy to protect, and every warm fact about them raises the price of reveal day another notch. But the book still owes me Sheri. She spent a whole Thanksgiving alone with Pace and a pie — the one adult who called the shape of this thing out loud — and I got Cassie's sister's dork husband instead. I'm not angry, Cassie earned her chapter, but that conversation is now the biggest uncollected debt in my fist and the book knows I'm holding it.

Quiet chapter, no Pace in the room, no Randi in the room — and Randi's absence from Vee's telling is its own data point, isn't it? A whole dinner about "who matters at home and school" and Miranda's name never surfaces. Even in the cropped version for Cassie, Randi isn't waist-up. She's cut out entirely. That's not tact. That's the con protecting itself with Vee's own hands.
