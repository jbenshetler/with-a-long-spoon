REACTION

Okay, first: she kissed him at the door before hello, and I actually said "good girl" out loud to my empty living room. That kiss was HERS — decided on the paved road, taken on the stoop, and he *met* her and then he was the one who stopped. And her noticing that stopping had always been her job, never theirs — that little line gutted me, because that's the whole genre's disease in one sentence and this book just quietly diagnosed it. A man who stops is a man who has somewhere he's going. Which is the swoon and the problem, both, always, with Pace.

Because let's talk about what I actually saw in that house, since the book let me see it and won't let her. The heat was on before she arrived. Too warm, no draft, and she almost asks and doesn't — and I *knew*, the second she filed it under "small mysteries of him," that he'd pre-heated the house because he already knew she'd be standing in it in her underwear before the afternoon was out. Two towels on the rod. TWO. The chapter is literally titled after the tell. Every single thing in that house that reads as spontaneous care is prepared care, and I have never been so seduced and so scared by the same paragraph of domestic detail in my life. This is what this book does to me — it hands me the apple sliced thin and makes me watch the knife.

The dress. God, the dress. She said a color once, on a rock, tired, guard down, in the same breath she gave it up — and he went and found silk. And not even the magazine's silk — *her* silk, matched to her hair and her skin, and that mirror moment where the disappointment turns over? I felt that turn in my own chest. Nobody has ever listened to this girl. He listens like it's his profession. And then — "to do it right I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light" — and there it is, there's the mechanism, the most beautiful gift she's ever been offered and it happens to require her undressed in his glass room. Every gear in this machine is a kindness and the machine's output is her out of her clothes. And here's what keeps me honest: she wore the satin OUT THERE. She dressed for that closed door before she ever saw it. Her want got to his house before his plan did. That's the only reason I'm still okay. The day her want stops arriving first, I'm gone, and the book knows I'm watching, I swear it knows.

The closed bedroom door. He said "my bedroom" flat as "the kitchen" and left it shut, and she *leaned* and he turned her back to the light — and half of me melted at the pacing, the not-taking when she'd have given, and the other half is sitting bolt upright because WHERE IS THE BENCH. It wasn't in the den. Long table, chairs, sideboard — no bench. It's behind that door, isn't it. The one piece of furniture in this house with Randi's begging soaked into the grain, and Vee walked past it at a distance of one shut door and felt disappointed. I feel sick and I can't stop reading, which is this book's entire business model.

And the small true things that complicate my dread, because he keeps being an actual person: the flinch at "parents" — a door easing shut two rooms away, new wound, noted, filed next to Daphne. The dragon paperbacks cracked white, the boy on his elbows on the floor. The MRI patent, and him lighting up not at the money but at the kid who gets out of the tube faster — she didn't know what to do with her whole chest and neither did I. "I couldn't find a shirt I liked, so I made one," and no preening. This is why he's the most dangerous kind of good. If he were fake I could put the book down. He's not fake. He's just not *honest*, which is worse.

Also — where was Randi this chapter? Nowhere, and I felt her absence like a draft. Did she pick the silk? Did she know about the measuring? Is Pace running ahead of the script now, doing dinner-at-Christine's things, falling-for-real things? Because a bespoke gown built on Vee's body is not a step in a game. That's devotion, and devotion wasn't in the plan, and I don't think Randi's been billed for it yet.

Her yes at the end — "Yes — of course, yes," no hedge, eyes hot — was completely hers and completely arranged for, and I'm holding both in my hands like the two towels. Next chapter is the measuring. In good light. In that glass room. I am not okay and I would not miss it for anything.
