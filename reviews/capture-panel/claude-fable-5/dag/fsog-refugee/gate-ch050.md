REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I have to start with the mirror, because the mirror is where this book keeps winning me back every time I get suspicious, and it knows it. A woman alone in a dorm room, doing her own eyeliner as an act of courage, standing in front of the glass in nothing but heels and taking stock of the body she's spent since fourteen apologizing for — and it's all from inside. Her hands, her nipples, her pacing, her cruelty to herself, HER choice to stop. "It was not hers to finish." She decided her own orgasm was a gift and she wrapped it and carried it out into January unspent. That's the exact thing I read this genre for and almost never get: desire that belongs to the woman having it, dominance she performs on herself, on purpose, for a reason she articulates. She walked away from her own edge and felt *powerful.* I felt it too. I put the book down and breathed.

And the doorstep. God, the doorstep. She doesn't say anything — after fifty chapters of words failing both of them, she brings the one text he's fluent in — and he opens the coat and the wall just... isn't there. "He looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look." I've read a hundred doorstep-in-a-coat scenes and they're always about the man consuming; this one was about her watching what SHE did to HIM, and then he goes to his knees on freezing boards before he takes one thing for himself. First. Before anything is his. Pace's whole grammar, even wrecked: her pleasure is the down payment, always. The open door banging in the wind, neither of them shutting it — that's the most honest either of these two control freaks has ever been.

But here is what I texted my imaginary friend at 1 a.m., in all caps: THOSE STAY ON. The heels. Randi's heels. Randi, who couldn't run the one scenario where she tells Vee the truth, who decided in a hotel bed in Gstaad to "let Vee arrive at it on her own" — Randi couldn't walk up to that door herself, so she sent her shoes. She picked the plan, she picked the script, she talked Vee down off the word "slutty" (and made her say it out loud first — she always makes her say it, that's her whole technology, shame lit on purpose so the wanting comes up hand in hand with it), and then she buckled herself onto Vee's feet. And Pace — Pace, who doesn't know, or GOD, does he know? — says "those stay on," and Randi is in the room for all of it. Vee wore his sweats and Randi's heels home and Cassie read the outfit in one pass, and I sat there thinking: that outfit is the entire book. Half his, half hers, and Vee thinks it's all Vee.

And the ledger. I keep the ledger now, it's my job. She drove through the cold bare and stained her good coat and handed him everything, no words, nothing kept back — her phrase, Randi's phrase really — and what did he hand back? Pancakes, made and not poured, and one knee, and "I missed you so much" with his voice cracking and not coming back. Which destroyed me, I'm not made of stone, the man on his knee under her chin with his face cracked open is the realest he's been since the diner. But count it. She paid in body. He paid in objects and a broken voice. The word is still unspent. And the chapter closes with "there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given" — which is warm and true tonight and is ALSO exactly the sentence the terrarium is designed to produce. She asked "what am I to you" in December and the answer she got in January was: a wordless reunion so overwhelming that she stopped asking. The fight didn't get answered. It got fed.

Because I was just in that diner. I heard "I can't" — not I won't, CAN'T — and I heard Sheri say the thing straight to his face: then not saying it matters more to you than having her, and from this seat that feels pretty shitty. The book let a witness say my line out loud, which is why I trust it. He can't say it, and I have my theories, and every one of them runs through Randi and whatever's "not finished," and through a man whose words are contracts and who will not sign one he knows contains a lie or a landmine. "Words are cheap" from the man who lawyered "I'd never share your photograph" — no. Words are the most expensive thing he owns, that's why he won't spend them. She's dating a man whose sentences are all under oath and she doesn't know why.

Small things I loved: Kayla back from the dead with a laundry basket, mourning a week of underwear at a woman wearing none — that's the funniest the book has ever been and the cruelest, Vee dying by inches through a sock inventory. The cop lights — my whole body clenched with hers, and then the cruiser went past and the fright drained off and left the want sitting right where it was, "denied a second time and no nearer anything that was hers to take." So: the hottest chapter in the book, and every degree of the heat is real, and her yes was so free she manufactured it herself in front of a mirror — and the whole night was choreographed by a woman in Switzerland who loves her and can't say it either. Nobody in this book can say it. Vee can't say it first, Pace can't say it at all, Randi says it constantly and hides the meaning in plain sight. Three people starving at a full table. Every yes was freely given. That was the problem. I know, book. I know.
