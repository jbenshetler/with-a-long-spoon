WHO'S WHO

**Pace** — the mathematician, and now I've been through his door. Late twenties, math PhD nearly done, house off 614 up a gravel driveway the hill almost hides: small, post-war, a two-car garage that's actually a woodshop cleaner than my kitchen, a glassed-in sun porch with the mountains behind it. Powerlifter mornings, six hours of writing, stops when it goes bad. Licenses a patent — MRI signal-from-noise, faster scans, and he lit up about the scared kid getting out of the tube, not the money. Makes furniture with dowel joints you find only by the grain change. Makes *clothes* — the tagless flannel Vee's been sleeping in was his own work, and now the burgundy silk dress, cut and pinned on her body. His whole method is prepared care: heat turned up hours early, water already hot for tea, kettle, blanket, wax paper for cherry pits, moleskin warmed on his thumb before it touches her heel. Asks before everything; when he doesn't use the word he leaves the door ("the bra too — it changes the line," and then he *waited*). Flinched at the word "parents." Has a large scar high on the inside of his thigh — her hand stopped on it, he drew her past. I'm betting that's Daphne, or Daphne's price. Still half of the plan. Increasingly, I think, off-script.

**Randi** — the other half. Pace's secret girlfriend; her sisters don't know his last name. Green Mercedes convertible, cream cashmere, champagne silk, and one fixed thing: a plain heavy white-gold band at her wrist, never off, no stone. I've decided it's a cuff. She wears him where no one can read it. Queer and can't survive knowing it. Runs Vee's debriefs like surgery conducted in warmth — "nobody measures over a bra," "which hip?", "all the best ones start at the left hip" — and has now kissed Vee on the mouth twice at the curb, no ask, the second press with more in it than the first. Tells laundered stories (the scarf, the tickling, the "old boyfriend") to set the exchange rate before harvesting Vee's real ones. Said "I'm running on fumes here" and I believed her more than anything else she's said.

**Vee** — the mark, and the happiest she's ever been, which is the problem. Slept with Pace (ch. 18) — her initiation, every joint of it hers: the green satin set bought "for the color," frosted plum toenails, "Famished," the apron strap in her fist, "Will you show me your bedroom?" Burned through the shame instead of being closed by it — first time in her life. Traded him her panties, folded in thirds, spot centered, not knowing she was reenacting Randi's ritual. Reads the right side of the menu first, does arithmetic that ends in Thursday. Starting to curate Cassie the way Randi curates her. Wants Randi too and has no alarm installed for it.

**Cassie** — roommate, real friend, the smoke detector. Back on the page and I could cry. "I had your blood type." Gets the raw versions — got the truck story before Randi did — but Vee ladled the middle out of the sex story clean, and zipped her skirt back *up* rather than be bare-fact in front of her. Cleats, brown cereal milk, "Don't wait up, lover." Loves with no plan attached. Still can't smell the frame because Vee doesn't know there is one.

**Kayla and Meg** — dining-hall friends, normal-volume life. Kayla: blonde curls, unbroken stream, "there's no meal at two, Vee." Meg: level, follows the Pace situation like her shows, lent the wrong boots, and said the thing that bounced off: "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word."

**Brooke** — chapter president, deep water, offstage these ten chapters. Not forgotten.

WHERE THINGS STAND

The hike (ch. 11): not at his house after all — trailhead, summit, the lake holding the silver sky. Moleskin. Cherry pits and "we take nothing and leave nothing" — which is also the plan's motto, and the chapter knew it. She named the russet dress she couldn't afford and set it down; he said "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and logged it. Then the rain, his only dry shirt handed over, him standing in the downpour with his back turned — and she *called him back in* and stripped her wet shirt with her eyes on his, her show, every button. Then found his hand shaking with cold and took care of *him* — first time the care ran backwards. A campus cop's siren ate whatever big thing she was about to say.

He'd bought burgundy silk — deeper than her magazine russet, matched to *her*, not the dress — built a riser, measured her down to her satin, and when her body soaked through and she shut her eyes bracing for the lifetime grin, he had never looked down once. Sorry" — and he went red and chose the hem over the easy yes and made her hear what it cost. He kissed the pinprick before deciding to. Reflexes don't run plays.

The night (ch. 18): everything. His question — "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — his want asking permission too. Fra diavolo after, her in the sheet, midnight, nothing missing. The trade. Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be.

Ch. 19–20: the edited version to Cassie; the fuller one to Randi at brunch, ending in Vee's own earned sentence — "I wanted it. And I took it. For once" — which Randi, for once, did not rewrite. HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace: I've stopped being able to separate the play from the man because I don't think there's a play left — the warmed moleskin, the never-looking-down, the kissed pinprick, the door left shut until it was hers to ask for. His consent practice hasn't blinked in twenty chapters, including at the exact moments breaking it would've been cheapest. His flagged flaw — taking the body's answer for the true one — got tested at the fitting and he passed, on his knees, at the worst height. And he is still standing in his kitchen holding the second woman's folded token, letting Vee's grace pay for his guilt. The most dangerous kind of good, and the bill's in his name too.

Randi: villain or the most damaged person in the book — the answer is yes. She's grooming Vee with the softest machinery I've ever watched (make the secret ordinary until it walks out on its own) and she's also starving — "running on fumes," asking a girl to rate the lover she lent her. She's coaching Vee toward the surrender she couldn't survive herself, selling tickets to a door she locked. Two kisses, zero asks — the one thing Pace never does, she does every time. And "You don't have to tell me" I still can't parse: technique, mercy, or flinch. I love her and I'm frightened of her and I ache for her, all worse than before.

Vee: mine. Her yeses are hers at every joint — she initiated the consummation start to finish — and she has never been happier and the room she's happy in has architects. What worries me newly: she's learning the con's grammar. Curating Cassie. Telling secrets in the wrong direction. And wanting Randi with no alarm installed.

Cassie: still my hill to die on. Being armored *against* now, by Vee herself, which is the cruelest development. She exits every chapter smiling and I dread her re-entry.

The ledger: Pace and Randi owe Vee the truth and the bill just changed currencies — before ch. 18 the truth would have humiliated her; now it will unmake the first night her body was ever fully hers. Randi owes Vee an ask she's never given. Vee now owes Cassie the middle of a story. Pace owes Randi — something; the fumes are real.
