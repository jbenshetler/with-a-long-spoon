REACTION

The chapter is called "Fed" and I sat with that title for a full minute after I finished, because everybody at that table got fed and only one of them knew what the meal was.

Randi's opening story. "I ran into an old boyfriend on campus." I read that three times. Is it even true? Is it Pace-adjacent? Is it invented whole? Because I've watched her do this now — the scarf-and-headboard story last lunch, the sad-nervous-boy story this one — she never tells a story for the story. She tells one to set the exchange rate. *I'll go first, I'll be vulnerable, now you owe me yours.* She primes the pump with "at least I got dinner ahead of time" and "he lowered my expectations" and then Vee — sweet, glowing, generous Vee — hands over her boyfriend's patience, his hunger, his not-being-in-a-hurry, to the woman who is *sleeping with him*. Randi sat there and listened to how her own man made another girl feel singular, and her face did "open and warm and glad for her friend." I don't know if she's a genius or if she's bleeding out under the table and nobody can see it, including her. "I'm running on fumes here" — Randi, honey, I believe you. That might be the truest sentence you've said in twenty chapters. What are you actually starving for? Because I don't think it's gossip.

And here's what's tearing me in half: the debrief is grooming — it's the same architecture as the smoothie place, the leading questions, the "which hip" method scaled up — and *this time the words were Vee's.* At the smoothie place Randi rewrote Vee's sentence and Vee signed it. This time Vee got there herself: "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." That's hers. That's the sentence I've been waiting for her to earn since chapter three, and she earned it in his kitchen and said it out loud at brunch, and Randi didn't substitute a word. She just said "Good" and put her hand over the painted one. And the horrible thing — the thing this book keeps doing to me — is that everything Randi said at that table is what a genuinely good friend would say. "You're not caught, you're lucky." "That's not a story, that's a painting. Keep that one." If I didn't have the ledger, I'd want a Randi. I *do* have the ledger and I still kind of want a Randi, and that's the trap working on me, the reader, exactly like the jacket promised it would. Falling for it exactly as she does. I hate how well it's going.

The small stuff got me the way the small stuff always does in this book. Vee reading the right side of the menu first, doing the arithmetic that ends in Thursday, finding the mimosa she could hold — and the second one she didn't price at all. That's the whole novel in two drinks. Her whole life is right-side-first and these two people keep handing her the left side of the menu, and every step toward not-pricing-things is real growth and real capture at the same time and I cannot tell them apart anymore, which I'm starting to believe is the point.

The kiss at the curb. She met it this time. She didn't scan the windows. Two lunches, and the thing that sent cold through her last chapter is now just how they say goodbye. That's how it works — not a seduction, an *acclimation* — and Vee still hasn't let herself put one word to what Randi is to her. "The friend she'd wanted at fifteen." Girl. The frosted plum answer she couldn't say out loud — she could feel that she was too far gone on Pace to name it. She has no such alarm installed for Randi, and that's the unguarded door.

What I'm carrying out of this one: Pace has the green panties, folded in thirds, the spot centered. Does Randi know? Is that what "running on fumes" is? And the queasiest beat in the chapter, the one I had to put the book down on: "How was he?" A woman asking a girl to rate the lover she lent her without the girl knowing there was a loan. The book didn't wink at it. The book let it be exactly as tender and exactly as monstrous as it is, in the same sentence, and that's why I'm still here.

One worry, said plainly: it's been three chapters of glow. The bill is accruing and nobody's paid anything yet. I trust "psychologically merciless" to come due. But I'm watching the meter.
