REACTION

A breather chapter, and I inhaled it anyway, because I know what's at the end of it and Vee doesn't.

First — I liked being in a room without Pace or Randi in it. Kayla and Meg are cheap dates as characters go, but they're *normal*, and normal is exactly what this book has been quietly draining out of Vee's life, so it startled me how relieved I was to see her at a corner table stealing fries and laughing loud enough to make the next table want in. This is what she's like when nobody's running a play on her. Loud. Easy. Whole-body. And I noticed — the book made sure I'd notice — Randi is nowhere in this chapter. Vee's got a whole social life Randi hasn't colonized, girls who sneak into the good dining hall and heckle her about Theo, and it made Cassie's edging-out hurt differently, because it turns out Vee *can* keep people. She just keeps the ones who don't have plans for her at a normal volume, in the daylight, and gives the plan-havers the deep parts.

Meg's line got me: "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word." That's the closest anyone's come to Cassie's smoke-detector job since Cassie herself, and it's the awful joke of this book that Meg is *wrong to be right* — Pace genuinely is different, that's the whole horror, he's different in a way Meg's Danny-calibrated radar can't detect. The book keeps doing this: giving Vee sensible friends whose sensible warnings bounce off because the trap isn't shaped like any trap they've seen. "Vee let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." Of course it didn't. Nothing catches. That's the design.

And Vee trying to explain him — "climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner" — I felt that in my teeth, because that's MY kink she's describing, that's the exact thing I've been hunting through this genre for years, a man whose asking is the seduction. Hearing her say "I can hear it sounding like nothing" — yes. It sounds like nothing to people who've never been starved of it. She knows how many days since the kiss without counting. Twelve. God.

Then the text. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." Kayla calls it a ransom note and shrieks about what to wear and the whole table treats it like the fun kind of mystery, and my stomach dropped through the floor, because I know what's in that house. The bench is in that house. Randi's chapter-one bench, the one her underwear lived on, the one she begged on — and the book just let three girls plan Vee's *hair* for it. The title of the whole novel is A Polite Invitation and this is it, isn't it — nine words, no explanation, all the choosing left to her, which is his method down to the bone: the least information a human being has ever sent, and she's read it a thousand times, and every step through that door will be hers. That's the machine working exactly as built. Freely given. That was the problem.

One small thing I'm holding onto: the powerlifting detail, the six hours of writing and then stop — Vee's assembling him for her friends, proud, and every piece is true, and she still doesn't have the one piece that matters. She's never been through his door. Neither have I, really — chapter one was Randi's version of that house. Saturday I get Vee's. Kayla said bring a change of clothes as a joke. I'm not laughing, I'm turning the page.
