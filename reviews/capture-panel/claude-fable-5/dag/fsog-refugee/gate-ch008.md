REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. This chapter is the trap, and I walked into it smiling, and I knew it was a trap because I read the jacket, and I still walked in smiling. That's the thing I'd be texting someone at 1 a.m. about — not the date itself, but what the date did to me *while I knew.*

Because taken on its own, this is the chapter I have been hunting through this whole genre for years to find. The text that doesn't demand an answer that night — that "he'd built the quiet into it without being asked" — I actually put the book down for a second there. Every romance hero I've ever side-eyed makes her answer *right then, so they'd know where they stood,* and Vee names that, out loud, and I felt seen in a way that was almost embarrassing. And then "May I choose?" at the table. Not "I'll order for us." Not the alpha grabbing the menu. He asks for the surrender, and she gets to feel the relief of handing it over *as a gift she gave,* and that is — that is the entire thing. That is what I keep telling people I want and they keep recommending me books about billionaires who confiscate a woman's phone.

And the kiss. God, the kiss. She says her rule — a rule that has "never been breached," which tells you every man before him treated her no as an opening bid — and he doesn't lean, doesn't wait her out, doesn't do the patient predator hover. He smiles like she told him something true about herself and *starts walking her home.* He genuinely lets it go. And THAT is what makes her stay planted. Her no costing nothing is what makes her yes possible. "I'm making an exception" — hers, initiated, closing a distance he'd already given up. I want to frame that beat. Most authors do not understand that the hottest thing a man can do with a no is believe it.

But.

I know what I know. I read the pointing game. I watched Randi run the shoulder-bump like choreography, because it *was* choreography. So the whole time Vee is dissolving into this warm yellow room — not watching herself eat for maybe the first time in her life, that hollow "empty a lot longer than since noon," which wrecked me, this girl who's been starving on purpose the way Randi starves on purpose, the way apparently every woman in this book has been portioning herself — the whole time, I'm sitting up here in dramatic irony's cheap seats going *she doesn't know she was picked out of a lineup on a quad.* He didn't stumble into that coffee shop. "It sounds like you're having trouble with math" — she'd said the word *integral* out loud right as the bell tinkled, and now I'm wondering if Randi texted him her order. That "some boy, Vee thought, amused" from chapter 5 — Randi was texting HIM. I went back and checked. I'm sick about it and I'm impressed and I'm sick about being impressed.

Here's what the book is doing to me and I want to say it plainly: it is giving me perfect consent at the level of every individual interaction, and total manipulation at the level of the frame. Every yes freely given. That was the problem — the jacket TOLD me, it's the tagline, and I still can't locate where the crime is when I look at any single scene. He asked. He waited. He honored the no. She chose. And also two people decided who she'd fall in love with before she knew they existed. Is that seduction or is it a con with impeccable manners? I genuinely don't know yet, and the fact that I don't know is why I can't stop.

The thing that scares me — the good scary, mostly — is Pace's noticing. In chapter 1 the noticing was devotion: apple slices thin, cut-side up, water bottle waiting. Now I've seen him aim the same instrument at a girl who doesn't know she's being read. The right hand, always the right hand, the mezze chosen "one to wake you and one to fold you under" — she registers it as one more thing he knows that she doesn't, and there is SO much he knows that she doesn't. The chapter kept whispering that under all its warmth. She stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong. Baby. The bill is absolutely coming.

And Cassie. Cassie saying "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, flat and finished — back in chapter 4 is still ringing in my ears like a bell in an empty church. Cassie is the only person in this book with no agenda and the book keeps writing her out of rooms and I am watching that like a hawk.

Also, quietly: Randi locked in a bathroom deciding never to come out from behind the face again, and Vee at this dinner coming out from behind hers so easily, fed and warm and unwatched. He got in one evening from Vee what he'll maybe never get again from Randi. That's going to detonate. Randi wanted Vee before Pace ever pointed — "Yes," bare, before the costume arrived — and now she's handed her own hunger to her boyfriend to hold, and he's at dinner falling for it for real. I don't know who I'm afraid for most. Vee, probably. But it might be Randi.
