Okay. I need to talk about the flannel and the rain, because that's where this chapter got me, but let me go in order because the whole thing was built in order and it *worked* on me in order.

The moleskin scene. I knew what he was going to do the second she said "my boot," and I still held my breath through it — because I was inside *her* head, and her head went straight to her mother's voice, *what kind of girl lets a man*, the sock, the shame, forty minutes of foot in a boot — and he just... did it. No ceremony, no lingering, no making it a Moment. Peeled the sock, found the hot spot, warmed the moleskin with his thumb before pressing it. Warmed it *first*. And then apologized — to HER — for not asking about the boots at the lot. He took the failure onto himself so she didn't have to carry the wrongness of Meg's boots. That's his whole method in miniature and I melted and then I did my compromised-melt check, the one I do every chapter now: is this the play or the man? And honestly — I think this one is just the man. There's no camera on this trail. Randi isn't watching. Nobody's grading this. He warmed the moleskin because that's who he is, and that's exactly what makes this book so dangerous to me.

The cherry pits. Oh, this one hurt in the good way. She spits the pit, he says "we take nothing and leave nothing" — *gentle* — and then eats his own cherry and puts his own pit on the wax paper beside hers *without looking at her doing it*. He taught her a thing her mother's voice was already calling her *common* for not knowing, and he did it in a way that left her dignity completely intact. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." That sentence is why I'm still reading this book. That is the thing I have been hunting for across a hundred novels where the dominant man's "teaching" is humiliation with a bow on it. And the chapter title is the lesson: Leave No Trace. Except — and here's where my stomach turned over even as my heart was doing its thing — that's ALSO the plan's motto, isn't it. Take nothing and leave nothing. No fingerprints. Every yes freely given, no trace of the hands that arranged it. Helen Rivers, I see you. I see what you named this chapter.

The dress. THE DRESS. Russet silk, "like the red of a brick," and she says the wanting out loud and then puts it back on the shelf herself — "you put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking," god, that line is her whole childhood in one sentence — and he says "That sounds like a beautiful dress" with *no weight on it at all* and then leaves it alone. Reader, I know what that means. That dress is going to show up. Wrapped, or hanging on a door, or delivered by Randi with a story attached. It's been logged the way the apple slices were logged, the way her coffee order maybe was. And I want her to have it so badly, and I already know that when it arrives I'm going to cry and be suspicious at the same time, which is the exact double-state this book keeps me in on purpose.

Now the truck. The flannel.

He gave her his only dry shirt — the one he packed for himself because he has no house to go home to up here — and then he *got out of the truck and stood in the rain with his back to her* so she could change without being seen. Didn't announce it. Didn't say "I'll give you privacy" in the voice men use when they want credit for privacy. Just took the rain. And she sat there and worked it out — his shirt, and then the truck, both — and this is the part where I put the book down for a second, because what happened next is the whole reason I read this genre and almost never get it:

*She called him back in to watch.*

His restraint didn't shut the door on her desire. It handed her the door. His absence, his turned back, his not-taking — that's what made her want to be seen. She knelt up on that bench and knocked on the window and crooked her finger, and then she unbuttoned her shirt with her eyes ON HIS, and he *stayed where she put them*. "Because she'd put them there." He didn't lean in. He didn't lift a hand. He received it. The want and the gladness and the stillness — and it was HER show, every button of it, her mother's voice turning to heat in her own hands, *floozy* becoming fuel instead of a leash. This is the most erotic scene in the book so far and nobody touched anybody. I'm a little undone about it, honestly. This is what I meant, all those years of saying "I want the heat WITH the consent" and being told those are opposites. They're not opposites. They're this truck.

And then — the turn that made me actually put my hand over my mouth — she reaches for his hand to start the next thing and *it's shaking.* He's freezing. He's been freezing the whole time and he was so far inside the looking, so wholly given over to what she was giving him, that he'd left his own body behind. And SHE stops. She takes care of HIM. She pulls his hand against her warm belly and says "you're freezing" and "we should go, please" — and he leaves his hand there a beat longer than he had to. The care ran backwards for the first time. She noticed his body before he did. That's not a mark tending her handler. That's two people. That's the thing getting real, exactly like the jacket promised it would, and I felt the ground shift under the whole plan when her fingers closed around that shaking hand.

The ending unsettled me in a way I want to flag for future-me: the cop, the siren, the sentence she never got to finish. "The thing she wanted to say was bigger than any words she had for it" — she was about to say something enormous, maybe the biggest thing, and one electronic bark took it. Her body "had already heard the siren and gone." Trained to flee at authority's first syllable, three steps and out, and then standing under the overhang watching his taillights get escorted off her campus like something that doesn't belong there. There's a little omen in that image and I don't like it. The world outside the cab has rules, and the thing in the cab doesn't survive contact with them yet.

What the chapter did NOT have: Randi. Not one mention, not one text under a table, and I noticed the absence like a missing tooth. This was the first chapter that felt entirely unstaged — no meet-cute choreography, no debrief lunch looming that I know of — and it was the best chapter, and that's the trap closing on ME now, not just on Vee. Because the debrief IS coming. She's going to sit across from Randi and hand over the truck, the buttons, the shaking hand, and Randi is going to hold it and turn it and want it — want *her* — and I'm already bracing. Also still carrying: he thinks the body's answer is the true one, and in that truck her body's answer WAS the true one, so the flaw didn't get tested. It got fed. Watching.

One more thing at 1 a.m.: he never told her the hike ends at his house. Either that's patience or that's the longest game of all, and God help me, I can no longer tell the difference, and neither can she, and I think that's the point of the whole book.
