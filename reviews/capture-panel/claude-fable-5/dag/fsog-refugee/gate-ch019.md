REACTION

Okay, small chapter, and I read it with my chest a little tight anyway, because after eighteen and that lunch from hell-disguised-as-heaven in seventeen, I needed to see what the morning after looked like — and the book gave me exactly the morning after and nothing else, which is honest of it.

The no-bag thing got me first. She couldn't pack a bag because packing a bag would have meant *saying it to herself* — that's so precisely this girl, the girl who wore the cardigan over the better shirt, the girl who has to keep her own knowing one step behind her own doing so the voice can't get a clean shot. She drove out there with nothing, deniable to the last, and woke up with her hair a wild country and laughed at herself in his mirror. That laugh is the whole book's case for Pace, honestly. Whatever else he is — and I have not forgotten what else he is — the girl in that mirror is happier and freer than the girl in the carry-forward notes I wrote ten chapters ago, and she got there yes by yes.

But here's what actually made me sit up: Vee edited Cassie. She ladled the middle out of the story "clean as a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole" — and told herself it was a kindness, and *believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep.* Helen Rivers, you absolute knife. Because that is Randi's move. That is the exact operation Randi runs on Vee every single lunch — the curated version, the seam nobody pulls at — and now Vee is running it on the one person in this book who loves her with no plan attached. The con is teaching her its grammar and she doesn't know she's learning it. She kept the middle from Cassie the same week she almost handed the middle to Randi over tomato soup. The wrong person is getting the withheld version and the wrong person nearly got the real one. That's the sentence I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she's telling her secrets in the wrong direction and the book knows it.*

And the zipper. She gets the skirt half down and stops because the next thing off wouldn't be a story she could edit — it would be a fact on the floor, bare, plum toenails and no green satin, nothing left to narrate. She zips back up. In front of Cassie. Cassie, who has seen her in every state of laundry for two years. The night made her freer with him and more armored with her — that's not nothing, and it's not good, and the book put it there quietly and didn't underline it. Also: "Was he worth painting your toenails?" — "In October. I have eyes." God, I love Cassie. She reads Vee fast and whole and doesn't pull the seam even when she feels it there. "Don't wait up, lover." She's being written toward the edge of the frame with her cleats and her brown milk, and every chapter she exits smiling I get more afraid of how she comes back in.

What's conspicuously absent: Randi. Not one thought of her. The kiss on the mouth in the fire lane, the two fingers Vee put to her own lips, the cold scan of the dorm windows — none of it surfaces this morning. Vee is running on the Pace channel at full volume and the Randi channel is just… muted, and I don't believe for a second it's off. The book let her have one clean happy morning and I notice it had to keep Randi entirely off the page to do it.

"She had never in her life been so happy." Last line, flat declarative, and the tagline sat right on top of it in my stomach: *every yes was freely given.* She is so happy. It is so hers. And two people built the room she's happy in and she still doesn't know the room has architects. Eight chapters till the end of this volume, probably, and the bill is still growing.

Thin chapter, honestly — a landing, a breath — but the ladle image and the zipper are doing real work, and after eighteen I'll take the breath.
