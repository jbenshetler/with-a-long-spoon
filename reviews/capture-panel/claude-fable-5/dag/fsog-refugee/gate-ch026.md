Oh, the book finally sent the bill. Twenty-five chapters of glow and I wrote in my own notes that I was watching the meter, and here it is — except it's not the con that costs her first, it's her own mother's voice, and that's so much smarter and so much worse. "A man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them." She didn't need Randi to sabotage her. She came pre-installed. The blonde just pressed the button that was already wired.

Let me back up, because the front half of this chapter is one of the best things this book has done, and it's a dance lesson. "If you want to, I'll teach you" — she repeats it to Meg twice like she's checking it's still real, and of course she does, because a man leading with *if you want to* is the whole reason I'm twenty-six chapters into this book. And then the frame instruction. "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead. Being jelly doesn't work." I actually put the book down for a second. That is the thesis. That is the entire argument this book has been making against every book I've thrown across a room — his lead *doesn't function* without her active pressure. She has a job. Her resistance isn't an obstacle to his control, it's the medium of it. And "come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours" — the closeness is the safety, the distance is what gets you hurt. I know what the book is doing, laying his erotic grammar out on a concrete pad in cold field light, and I don't care that I can see it, because it's *right*. Twelve years I've been waiting for someone to write dominance as a two-body physics problem instead of one person's will erasing another's.

The count coming back into her head the moment she notices she's being watched — that's exactly how it works, being good at something in public. The blonde with the daylight-wrong makeup doing her little poisoned welcome — Vee never questions the source, that's the thing, she takes a stranger's obvious jealousy as *evidence* because it agrees with her mother. That's the shame she burned through in chapter 18 coming back to collect, because shame doesn't die from one good night, it just waits for a crowded room.

When it's required." No defense, no charm offensive, no grabbing her wrist, no following her across the bar to manage her. He set down the beer and left.
