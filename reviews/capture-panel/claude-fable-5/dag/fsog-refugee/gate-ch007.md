REACTION —

Okay, so no Pace, no Randi, no sex, and I didn't miss any of it. That's how I know this book has me.

Cassie. CASSIE. "They will never know the love of a woman" — pitched low, just for Vee, when she could've made the whole bleachers hear it. That detail wrecked me a little, honestly. This author keeps doing this thing where love is shown as *calibration* — Pace slicing the apple thin with the cut sides up, and now Cassie choosing the volume of a joke. Same move, totally different relationship, and it's making me realize the book's actual thesis isn't dominance at all, it's attention. Who's watching you closely enough to know what you need, and what are they doing with what they see. Which is also why the Randi/Pace plot scares me, because that same attention is what makes their game work on Vee, and I know from the jacket it IS a game.

The underwater sequence — "she had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." Oh. That line is going to stay with me. The whole walk past the bleachers, the rolled towel carried down at her side, *not up against her* — the discipline of that, the entrance-face, and then the water swallowing all of it. I was a girl in a swimsuit walking past boys once. Everyone was. The book knows exactly what that costs and it didn't melodrama it, it just let her go under where nobody wants anything from her, and let her grieve for one beat past her lungs. And then didn't let it curdle — the splash fight breaking it up, the shriek-laugh, "the ugly one." That's the kind of thing I hunt this genre for and almost never get: a heroine whose interior is real enough that when the sex eventually arrives it'll mean something, because I'll know exactly what body she's bringing to it and what that body has survived.

Here's what I'm chewing on at 1 a.m., though. The last line. "The body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck with the rolled towel, where it could wait." Wait for WHAT. Because I know what it's waiting for — it's waiting for Pace, whose whole method is looking at exactly the thing a woman has been hiding. He did it to Randi in Chapter 1 and she bolted behind her face forever. He's going to do it to Vee, and Vee — quietly starving, "fine has never once been enough" — is going to open like a door, because she's been waiting her whole life for someone to look. And the difference between those two women, that Randi couldn't survive being seen and Vee is starving to be — the book is building that comparison so deliberately I can feel the architecture, and I don't know yet if it's going to break my heart or make me furious. Both, probably. That's fine. I signed up for both.

Also: Vee's suits-versus-Cassie's-suit paragraph, Cassie planted in the middle of her ribs "like a woman leaning on a fence" — this writer loves her secondary characters and it shows and I'm grateful. Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" from Chapter 4 is still sitting in my stomach like a stone, though. She said the word for a longer parting. Cassie sees what's coming. Cassie is the reader in the book and I'm worried about what the book does to its reader.

One small honest note: I noticed there's no Randi here at all, and I noticed I was relieved, and then I noticed the relief itself is data. The book made me love her in Chapter 1 and fear her by Chapter 4 without her doing one cruel thing on the page. Every yes was freely given. That was the problem. Yeah. I see it coming. I'm still walking toward it, same as Vee.
