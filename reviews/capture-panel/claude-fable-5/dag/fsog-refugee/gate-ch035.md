REACTION

Okay, so after the brunch chapter left me feeling like I'd swallowed something with a hook in it — the pastry glaze on Vee's lip, I still haven't recovered — the book hands me this. A quiet one. And I want to be clear about what it did to me, because it's sneaky: it made me happy, and then it made me watch myself being happy the way Vee watches herself being happy, which in this book is never free.

The door swinging open before she reaches it and her feet leaving the ground — that's the stuff. That's what I read this genre for and almost never get: a man whose want is enormous and whose hands still don't take anything she isn't throwing at him. "He never went straight at his own finish." "She hadn't known a man would want to." Thirty-five chapters in and the book is still cashing checks on the consent it set up in chapter one, and they still clear. I felt the whole week of hunger in one paragraph.

"Peter," to the sleeping shape of him. She's done it twice now, and both times it's wrecked me the same way — she keeps the real name for the dark, like contraband. She loves him. She let it be true where she can't take it back, and I sat there doing the math she isn't doing: he has said it in salmon and warm tomatoes and a spin off the porch, and in exactly zero words, and she's decided she can live inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying. Sweetheart. You are describing a diet. You are describing the exact shape of a man who has something he cannot say, and you've made a virtue of the silence because the alternative is asking a question he'd have to answer. "He never said." She notices it herself — *had he minded, she couldn't tell, he never said* — and the book put that sentence in a chapter about a bathroom counter so I'd swallow it easy, but I saw it. His wordlessness is his tenderness AND it's the con's whole climate. The same silence that gives her a drawer is the silence sitting on top of Randi, and Daphne, and the plan. One instrument, two songs.

The drawer, though. God, the drawer. That beat is built like a tiny thriller — the scrunchies gone, the counter bare, her whole morning bottoming out because she reads it as *I was too much, my sprawl offended the clean man* — and then she opens the drawer and he's folded his entire ordered self into the lower one and given her the top. The one your hand goes to first. No note, there's never a note. I actually put my hand over my mouth. That is the love language of a man who was taught early that his words get used against him — they moved his desk into the hall, remember, they took the thing he loved and made it the punishment — so he says everything in objects. The dress. The scrunchies. The drawer. And here's what scares me: when the reveal comes, every one of these objects converts. The drawer becomes evidence. The book is furnishing her whole life with things that will have to be re-appraised, and it knows it, and it made me love the drawer anyway.

Also — and I noticed this the way you notice a draft — her body is being quietly remade. Eating like a field hand, clothes going loose, "her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, and she'd never asked it to." The book presents it as flourishing and I mostly believe it, but there's that phrase again — *she'd never asked it to* — Vee's body deciding things without her permission is a running motif and it's never once been neutral in this book. Her body decided in the bedroom, decided in the bathroom stall, and is now deciding at the dinner table. Everything about her keeps getting handed over before she's consulted, and the book keeps calling it ease.

And Randi is nowhere in this chapter and everywhere in it. Vee whispered "Peter" into the dark and last week she couldn't finish in a bathroom stall because Randi's face arrived. Both of those are true in the same girl. The chapter didn't mention brunch once and I felt it under the floorboards the entire time.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: he gave her the top drawer and I cried a little and I do not trust a single object in that house and I can't stop reading.
