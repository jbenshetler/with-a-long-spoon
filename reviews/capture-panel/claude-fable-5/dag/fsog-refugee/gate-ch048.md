REACTION

The mirror of it got me before anything else did. I read chapter 47 from inside Randi's cold — the text arriving on her nightstand, "thinking about you 💛" — and now the book walks me around to the other side of the glass and makes me watch Vee compose it, read it twice for anything that gives her away, turn the phone back over to be sure it sent. Both of them awake at midnight. Both of them cold in rooms that don't know them. Both of them lying by omission to the one person they're honest with. And Randi's "fairytale" text — which I watched her write standing at a mirror putting her face back on after a man who never found her — lands on Vee as *envy*. Vee lies in a twin bed her legs outgrew and aches for a poisoned Gstaad, and I know it's poisoned twice over, the money and the man, and she only knows about the money. The book keeps letting me be ahead of her and it keeps hurting instead of feeling clever. That's the jacket's promise doing its work: *lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does.* I resent how well it's holding up its end.

But here's the sentence I sat straight up for: "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That's the book turning its own crown jewel over to show me the underside. I have spent forty-eight chapters loving Pace's room-leaving — it's the thing I came to this genre starving for, the man who doesn't crowd the doorway — and now the book asks, quietly, at midnight, in Ohio: what does the room feel like when you're standing in it alone and he won't cross it either? She walked out of his house crying and he said her name once and then — nothing. A whole break, not a word. And I genuinely cannot decide if that's respect or a wall, and neither can she, and I think the book has engineered it so that his best quality and his worst are the same behavior viewed from different temperatures. "Believe what I do" was almost enough in his warm kitchen. From a cold flannel bed six hundred miles away, what he's *doing* is silence. Words are cheap, Peter, and you still won't spend them, and now the doing has gone quiet too. My second ticket — "I don't go back" — is starting to feel less like a mystery and more like a diagnosis. A man who doesn't go back also, maybe, doesn't come after.

The blonde she built from spite gutted me, because of what the book did with her. It wasn't just jealousy — it was Vee losing custody of her own best memory. The fra diavolo night, the sheet slipping, the not-fixing-it — that's *her* scene, one of the ones I keep in my own fist — and she reached for it to get warm and the girl at the table turned gold and tan while she was inside it. She can't even keep what happened to her. That's what having no name for a thing costs: it can't be defended, it can't be proved, and at midnight it can't even be remembered safely. He gave her everything and no name, and the no-name turns out to be a hole other women can climb through.

And I have to say the quiet, awful thing: Vee ran a Randi again. On Randi this time. That text was *engineered* — "made sure it demanded nothing," read twice for leaks, the folded-away glass-room knowledge held back with perfect discipline. She's fluent now. She learned it across a hundred brunches and she uses it for love instead of extraction, and I don't know whether that makes it better or means the curriculum took. Meanwhile the honest ledger stands like this: the only unguarded sentence in the whole exchange was Randi's "miss your face most though, gorgeous" — the one line Randi meant, wrapped in a lie — and Vee received it as proof the fairytale girl spared her a thought. Two women in love with each other's counterfeit. The jacket told me this would happen and I bought the book anyway. Fine. Fine.

What I'm holding onto, and it's small but it's real: nobody did anything cruel in this chapter. Nobody coerced anyone. It's forty-eight chapters in and the pain on the page is the pain of two words withheld — *I love you* from him, *Miranda* from her — and the book is being ruthlessly honest that withholding is its own act with its own bill. Also, practically: it has now been four chapters since Pace and Vee were warm in a room together, and my body knows it the way she knows the shirt went stale. The term starts. She can circle a day for Randi and nothing for Pace, and that asymmetry is a loaded gun the book placed very deliberately. I need him at a door soon — hers, this time. Let him cross a distance for once. Let him spend one word.
