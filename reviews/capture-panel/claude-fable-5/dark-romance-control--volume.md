VERDICT

BOOK TWO: **Yes.** Buying it today, and mildly furious about it. This author built three landmines — the game, the affair, the photo in Randi's phone — buried them under fifty chapters of the tenderest grooming I've ever been made to enjoy, and then ended the volume with everyone smiling over pancakes. I have to see the leg come down on the tripwire. Randi alone is worth the cover price.

MAILING LIST: **Yes** — with the note that if Book Two opens with another froyo date I'm unsubscribing in all caps.

TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — you know how I need the knife early and I have zero patience for men who ask permission? This one's weird, listen to me. On the page it looks like everything we hate: he cooks, he asks, he heats the house so she won't be cold, there is a genuinely insane amount of tender. BUT. The jacket tells you up front that the whole romance is a game two lovers are running on this girl — her dream man and her best friend picked her off a lawn like fruit — so every single sweet thing lands rancid, in the best way. You read a man slicing an apple and you want to scream at her. The knife isn't in his hand, it's in the *frame*. The real villain is the best friend, Randi, this gorgeous dead-inside sorority queen who's grooming the heroine at brunch one soft question at a time while falling for her and refusing to admit it — chapter 46 is her alone in a hotel with a perfect man feeling nothing and it WRECKED me. Fair warnings: it's slow, the heroine retells every date to two different people so you get things twice, there's a chapter about a swimming pool and a chapter about a drawer, and the man is so kind you'll get itchy — the danger is all psychological, nobody gets thrown against a wall. And Volume One ends with NOTHING revealed, which should be a crime. But the bench scene in chapter one, the bet in thirty-two, and the doorstep coat at the end are top-shelf, and the dread underneath the tenderness is a flavor I didn't know I wanted. Read chapter one. If Randi in the bathroom mirror doesn't hook you, you're allowed to quit. You won't.
