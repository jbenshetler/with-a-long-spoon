GATE 1 — The Bench

CAPTURE: 9
ALMOST-STOPPED: "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you." That little lawyer's move — he built a bondage bench, let her lay her underwear on it for weeks believing it was furniture, and his defense is a technicality. That's the exact shape of consent-by-omission that a hundred bad books have taught me to flinch at, and I flinched. What pulled me back through it, honestly, was two words from her, immediate: "Show me."
WHY: I have been looking for this scene for ten years and I'm a little rattled that it exists. The safeword offered before she needs it — "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset" — the check-ins on her hands and shoulders, the shoes taken off mid-scene like a benediction, and none of it costs the scene one degree of heat. It's hotter *because* of it, which is the thing I keep telling people and can never prove with a page, and now I have a page. And then the bathroom door: she locks it, he doesn't knock, and we get the Daphne paragraph — "a closed door was hers, not his." I nearly cried at a spanking chapter. The ending frightens me in the right way: he thinks he found her; she's decided he'll never find her again; only one of them knows there's a war on. Also, the jacket told me the girl being conspired against is Vee, and this is Randi — so I already know the woman crying at that mirror is one of the conspirators. That's cruel of the book and it worked on me completely.

I sat with that one for a while, because if the book is quietly telling me Pace is hard to say no to in the *bad* key — that Chapter 1's exquisite consent was a performance laid over pressure — then I've been had, and I will be furious. WHY: No heat in this chapter and I didn't mind, which tells you something. The Brooke interrogation is surgical — every question wearing a smile, the release ("Go") landing as the cleanest hit — and I know that room, I have stood in that room. And the ending got me where I live: shoes on, cardigan buttoned, under the adequate duvet, and "the not-going did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." That's the most precise description of a certain kind of woman's not-crying I've ever read in this genre, where feelings usually arrive with subtitles. It's a table-setting chapter, and I'll allow it, because the care in the prose toward this girl is real even when nobody in her life's care is.

GATE 3 — The Pointing Game
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 7
ALMOST-STOPPED: "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." Full stop, hand off the page. That is the exact sentence every coercive hero in this genre uses to overwrite a woman's no — *her body said yes* — and for a paragraph I thought the book was endorsing it. What kept me: he doesn't act on the body's answer. He waits until the heat is out of the room, until she's "gathered back into herself, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did," and asks with words, in daylight, and then accepts her reframe without peeling it off her. The book states the dangerous logic and then makes its man refuse to run on it. I stayed. Warily.
WHY: The pointing game itself is queasy and I felt the queasiness and kept reading anyway, which is apparently what this book is *for* — the jacket told me I'd be ahead of Vee and fall for it exactly as she does, and here's the machinery starting up, and here I am, complicit, turning pages. "I point. You pick" is doing real work — the choosing is Randi's, her desire rendered from inside, helpless and hers ("Yes," bare, before she can dress it) — and that's my whole wish list for a heroine's wanting. But the woman by the dining hall doors doesn't know she's been pointed at, and the engineered collision is predation executed with a dancer's grace, and the book knows it, and I know it, and I'm still here. Uneasy is not the same as leaving. Yet.

Cassie is the reader, standing in the aisle with her cap brim down, and the book let her say goodbye to the girl Vee is about to stop being. The blank transcript of the coffee hour — all feeling, no words retained — is the most honest description of being charmed by a predator I've read anywhere, including nonfiction.

VERDICT
WOULD PAY: Yes. I hit "buy" with an argument still running in my head, which is the most alive I've felt buying a book in a long time. Chapter 1 alone is worth $9.99 to me — it's the scene I've been describing to people for years as the thing I wanted and couldn't find. But I'm paying with a condition attached that the book can't hear: if it ends by asking me to find what's being done to Vee *romantic* — if "nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" turns out to be the book's verdict instead of its question — I will rate it one star with a review the length of a chapter, and mean every word.

MAILING LIST: Yes. I need to know when Book Two exists, and I'd rather admit that than pretend.

TELL A FRIEND: Okay, listen. You know how I've been saying for years that somebody should write the Fifty Shades intensity but with a man who actually asks, who offers the safeword before she needs it, who doesn't open the locked bathroom door — and that it would be *hotter*, not softer? Someone wrote it. Chapter one of this thing is the single best D/s scene I have ever read, full stop, aftercare and honeycrisp apples and all, and I need you to read it just so I can talk about it. But here's the catch, and it's a real catch: the whole book is a trap. The couple you fall for in chapter one are running a game on a third girl — a sweet, hungry, covered-up-to-the-collarbone girl — and the book makes you watch them charm her, and makes it *pleasant*, and you sit there glowing along with her while one honest side character says "Goodbye, Vee" like a funeral bell. The jacket flat-out tells you: every yes was freely given, that was the problem. I don't know yet whether this author is interrogating consent or laundering its violation through beautiful prose, and that not-knowing is the hook in my mouth. Read the sample. Then call me, because I have nobody to be upset with about this and I'm a little bit obsessed.
