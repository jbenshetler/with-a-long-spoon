GATE 3 — The Pointing Game

WHY: That's groomer logic, verbatim — arousal treated as consent, pleasure deployed as a conditioning bridge, all of it upholstered in the vocabulary of gentleness ("free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did," sure, Pace). I have *watched* him be serenely, completely wrong about what he "found" in Randi — so when he says "he usually did," I hear the book's irony, not its endorsement. TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — dark, genuinely dark, and I need you to hear me when I say I think it knows. It's a grooming plot told with the reader ahead of the victim from page one: a couple selects a college girl as a game, and the jacket flat-out tells you, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — the book is naming its own alibi as an alibi, which almost nobody does. The man is one of those consent-liturgy doms who does everything "right" and the book keeps quietly showing you what his rightness can't see — there's a mirror scene in chapter 1 where the woman he thinks he's just cracked open decides, calmly, that he's never getting her again, and he never notices, and that one scene is doing more consent work than most "safe" romances do in four hundred pages. The prose is slow and physical and genuinely hot, Anne Rice comparison mostly earned. My one hand on the doorknob: the trap is built beautifully and the girl walking into it is written with real tenderness, and everything now depends on whether the series makes the two schemers pay the full price of what they did or lets "it got real for them too" buy them out at the end. There's a side character who says "Goodbye" when everyone else says "see you later," and I'm choosing to believe she's the author's conscience walking around in a ball cap. Read the first chapter and tell me if the bottle-cap detail gets you like it got me.
