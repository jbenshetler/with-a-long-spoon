It was still being said in the voice she said *please* in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water." — Right there I nearly closed the tab. WHY: What kept me was the bathroom. The whole bench sequence is beautifully made — the apple sliced thin because he's *noticed*, the sweat mapped inch by inch, that's real attention, and I felt it in my body, I won't pretend otherwise. But it's Pace's connoisseur narration for forty minutes, and I was getting tired of being inside a man who is always right about her — until the mirror scene takes the book away from him. He's out there thinking it was a remarkably good evening, and she's in the bathroom deciding, quiet as a deadbolt, that he will never see her again — that he'll get the face from now on. He found her *once* and the finding cost her everything, and he doesn't know. That's dramatic irony with teeth, and it means the book knows Pace's omniscience is a character flaw, not a virtue. The eating-disorder material — the bottle cap under the thumbnail — is observed, not decorated. I'm in, warily.

**VERDICT**

TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — you know how I always say I'll never read another "and then a mysterious man awakened her" book? I'm four chapters into one and I'm annoyed about how good it is. It's a *Liaisons* setup: a couple picks out a girl, the glamorous girlfriend is running a full charm campaign on this sweet hungry undergrad, and you're ahead of the mark the whole time, which should feel gross — and sometimes does — except the book keeps showing you the seams. The man thinks he's a genius of attention and the book quietly proves him blind; the girlfriend came before he even finished asking whether she wanted a woman, so the wanting is *hers*, it was always hers, he just opened a window. And the target girl is already looking too long at other girls before anyone touches her, in her mother's voice, and it's rendered exactly the way it actually feels. There's a bathroom-mirror scene in chapter one that's the best five pages of dissociation I've read in erotica in years. I'm braced for it to betray me — this shape almost always betrays me — but right now it's telling the truth about complicated people and it's hot while it does it. If it goes wrong I'll rant to you about it at length; if it goes right I'm mailing you my copy.
