REACTION

Oh, Vee. Honey. The third minute of *not looking at the door* nearly finished me.

I finally get inside her head, and now I have to admit I believed Randi and Pace’s version of her a little too much. The laugh was real. The unselfconscious, lavish ease they projected onto her was not the whole woman. Vee measures rooms. Vee folds her eagerness down before letting anyone see its size. Vee chooses the cardigan over the better shirt. She forgot she could be seen for the length of one laugh, not for a whole life. Somehow that makes me love her more and makes what is coming feel much worse.

She is also *kind* in motion, not just in the abstract way her apology on the lawn suggested. When Cassie refuses the little door Randi’s name-bit opens, Vee immediately steps into the awkwardness and turns Cassie’s flatness into something charming without making her the butt of it. That is social intelligence being used generously. And Randi takes it, delighted, and keeps including Cassie without ever surrendering the current running between herself and Vee. I kept trying to catch Randi being obviously false, and I couldn’t. That is the horrible part. She does not have to sneer or punish resistance. She metabolizes it.

Cassie frightened me, though. Not in a somebody-is-about-to-die way. In the infinitely more recognizable way of watching your friend get dazzled and understanding, before she does, that she has started moving away from you. *Have fun. Goodbye, Vee.* That whole flat, finished word made my stomach drop. Vee hears the wrongness and smooths it because Randi is already holding the row open and calling her toward coffee. Then Randi gives her *See you later* at the end. Cassie offers goodbye; Randi offers a future. Vee goes home warm with the future. I could scream.

And Chi Latte. God, the stupid beautiful sign resolving into a joke only once she gets to walk under it with Randi. I know exactly that pathetic, exquisite feeling of discovering that a place was never actually locked, only that you had never had the person who made entering it feel permissible. Randi gives her the high table in the window, the lavender drink, the little office of being *the one who knows things*, and Vee is so starved for chosenness that every small offering lands like a sacrament.

But she is not some blank little ugly duckling, thank God. That roomful of girls brought out such an interesting, uncomfortable tangle in her. Her mother saying *floozies* through Vee’s mouth; Vee looking longer than the word can explain; Vee knowing she has more body than they do and that the clothes would look better on her; Vee covering all of it anyway. She has appetite, vanity, desire, pride, shame, and absolutely no safe language yet for the fact that some of her looking is *looking*. Randi can only want a woman by making her a gift for Pace. Vee can only want Randi by calling it admiration and belonging. Neither of them can say the plain sentence, and now I am afraid Pace is going to be the one who names Vee’s desire for her before she gets the chance to own it.

The blank transcript was the part that actually made me feel sick. Not because I think nothing real passed between them, but because Vee was not keeping Randi. She was keeping herself as Randi’s attention made her feel. She invents Switzerland and Paris and some elegantly foreign mother for that voice because the actual woman is still a blank she is decorating. She knows the parking-ticket story was funny, knows something flattering went into her like a key, and cannot retrieve a sentence. Meanwhile she has surrendered the clean pleasure she usually takes in statistics because Randi’s shoulder is against hers and occasionally her breath reaches Vee’s ear. That tiny abandonment of the thing she is good at chilled me more than the note-taking did.

And the worst, most seductive thing: I believe it was the best hour she’d had in months. I believe Randi’s delight in her may be real even while the approach was designed. Vee standing there with an empty cup, thinking she has been chosen for reasons she does not need to understand, by exactly the person she would have chosen to be chosen by—there is the whole tragedy warming its hands. Pace has already imagined her mouth on Randi, and Vee is spinning on a sidewalk over *the right pen*. There was a third person at that coffee table, and she has no idea.

I did not miss the sex for one second. This was dread with a lavender latte, and I need the next chapter immediately.
