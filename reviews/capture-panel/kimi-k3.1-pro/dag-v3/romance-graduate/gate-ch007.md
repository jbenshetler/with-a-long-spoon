REACTION

Oh, I needed this. Not a breather, exactly—my chest hurt through most of it—but Vee got to have one place and one person that Randi and Pace haven’t touched.

The walk past the bleachers made me so angry I could feel it in my jaw. She has perfected looking like she *meant* to arrive, and they still get to turn her body into a little public joke for each other. The floaties comment wasn’t clever; it wasn’t even really about wanting her. It was about making her carry their looking while they congratulated themselves. And Cassie’s *They will never know the love of a woman* made me laugh, but the part I loved was that she kept it for Vee. She could have defended her loudly and made Vee’s humiliation into a bigger event. Instead she put exactly enough warmth under her to get her to the water.

And then the underwater section just undid me. *Nobody down here wanted anything.* God. Of course that is the only place she can put something down. Randi and Pace saw a lavish body and a whole-body laugh and thought they understood what Vee offered the world. They have no idea she has a history of going underneath everything, somewhere her hands remember what to do and nobody can grade the shape of her. She loved this before her body became a problem. That little grief she feels down there isn’t self-pity; it’s homesickness.

It also made the contrast with Randi almost unbearable. Randi arranges her face in a bathroom mirror and cannot find a door back to herself. Vee walks into the rec center with her face arranged too—but she has somewhere beneath the surface where she still belongs, and she has Cassie noticing when she comes back with her face on wrong. Vee is vulnerable here, desperately so, but she is not hollow. She is not an empty cup waiting for these two people to fill her with aliveness.

Cassie’s water trick might be my favorite thing anyone has done in this book. She doesn’t ask Vee to produce the sad story so Cassie can prove what a good friend she is. She just fires a completely inadequate sheet of water at her head and gives her something better to do with her body. The ugly shriek-laugh! I loved that laugh more than the beautiful laugh on the lawn, because this one belongs entirely to Vee and Cassie. Nobody selected it. Nobody is watching it and imagining what it could be made to mean. *The body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck* hurt like hell, though. If this story takes Cassie away from her, I am going to take it extremely personally.

Then the shower got me in a much less innocent way. Vee sitting there in her towel, thinking about his hands again and then getting her sweats on quickly—yes, all right, that got a little heat under my skin. But what I really liked was how specific her attraction is becoming. She isn’t simply dizzy because a grad student noticed her. She has decided he isn’t gorgeous. She knows he could disappear into a dorm laundry room in a hoodie. And then she remembers the chair, the pencil, the question that made her research sound real, the door held with his back. Her wanting is being built out of things she actually noticed. It feels like hers.

Which brings me to *Don’t answer tonight*, because I think I made an actual noise.

That is infuriatingly attractive. He can feel the yes already in her mouth and instead of snatching it, he gives her an evening in which she does not have to be carried by her own momentum. No explanation, no demand that she admire him for it. Just room. After Randi removed Vee’s little wait-a-day rule, Pace is the one handing time back to her.

And yet. He can give her time to choose while still withholding the one piece of information that might change the whole choice. He is being exquisitely careful with her answer and not honest about its premise. I don’t think the care is fake—that’s what makes this so much worse. The better he treats her, the more she will trust what she is discovering about herself with him, and the more violently the design underneath it is going to matter when she finds out.

So now she’s standing at the crosswalk with her thumb over the keys, and I want the next chapter immediately. I want to know whether she answers tonight. I want to know whether she tells Cassie or runs straight to Randi and lets this private, tentative wanting become another item in the plan. And I want Saturday. God help me, I want Saturday very much.
