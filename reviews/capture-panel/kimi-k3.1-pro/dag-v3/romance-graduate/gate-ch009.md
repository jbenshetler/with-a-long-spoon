REACTION

Oh, the two debriefs. That’s the whole knife.

With Cassie, Vee says, **“I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.”** With Randi, six days later, the same kiss becomes **“Because you couldn’t not. Because of him.”** And Vee accepts the revision. She actually has the dignified, accurate version—I made an exception—sitting right there, and lets Randi replace it with something smaller and more helpless. That frightened me more than any overt steering has. Randi isn’t just collecting details anymore. She’s teaching Vee what her own experiences mean.

The opening with Cassie made me so happy I could hardly stand knowing where we were going. The clumsy little spin that nearly takes out the closet! Vee dropping into the bean bag, laughing at herself, suddenly a person who says yes to hiking before she’s ever owned the shoes for it. Cassie’s state-trooper question and “rich people hike, poor people walk” kept it from becoming some solemn celebration of feminine awakening. She lets Vee be ridiculous and radiant without trying to take any of it away from her. Even the alcohol question, which briefly made me bristle, ends with Cassie believing her immediately: *Look at you.* Not *look what he’s done to you.* Look at **you**.

And then that tiny movement at stats—Cassie’s look folding itself away, Vee not watching her leave—hurt like hell. The worst part is that Vee now does some of the excluding herself. *It isn’t really her place.* The matched leggings, the hair, the whole bright sorority of it. Then the eminently reasonable thought about everyone having their own people, which is true, and is also exactly how she makes the first little abandonment feel healthy. Randi doesn’t have to lock Cassie out. Vee has started explaining why Cassie wouldn’t want to come.

I didn’t believe “Paul?” for one second. Randi knows Pace’s name down to the social danger of it. And that miraculously useful catastrophe date—the dreamy rich man who talks about himself all night and therefore won’t take care of her anywhere else—felt less like a confession than a comparison exhibit wheeled in before Vee’s story. Whether it happened or not, she is producing the exact foil that makes Pace’s listening seem revelatory. She has forty minutes and she uses every one of them.

Because when Vee gives her the real thing—not the kiss, but *I stopped keeping track of myself*—Randi finds the mechanism instantly. **You let him choose for you. And you liked it.** I could practically hear the little drawer sliding shut. Then she lets Vee retreat into “that’s all it was,” and Vee experiences the retreat as kindness. No. She has just learned something enormously useful about where Vee’s surrender opens, and she doesn’t need to make Vee embarrassed by pursuing it. She can save it.

And yet, God, some of their happiness together is real. The no-bra practicality, Vee’s flat little demonstration, Randi’s bark of laughter turning a head at the counter—I laughed too. This is why I can’t simply sit here hissing whenever Randi appears. She is fun with Vee. She makes room for Vee’s body to be funny instead of graded. Then Vee’s voice drops over *He’d ask, though,* and Randi answers, a beat late and low, **“Ask and then ravish,”** and there I was, pressing my Kindle a little too hard. The charge between them is not invented by the premise. Randi can meet Vee inside the fantasy in a way that feels far more intimate than her cheerful *your man*. Which also makes the whole thing more dangerous: Vee is learning erotic language with Randi while believing Randi is merely narrating Pace.

The Smoothie Bird made my skin crawl before Brooke arrived. Vee’s cardigan turning into an apology, the oat milk she orders because Randi is beside her, Randi’s bruise-colored punishment in a cup, and Vee stuck up on that high chair like something displayed behind glass. She keeps deciding to be happy over the evidence of her own discomfort. Then Brooke walks in and Vee makes herself small **for Randi**—not even to protect herself now, but to leave nothing that could be held against her. That is such an exquisitely awful little surrender.

And Randi answers it by taking her hand in public and calling her **my dear friend**.

I can’t dismiss that. I won’t. She spends something real there. Brooke sees it; the silent taller woman sees it; Vee feels the charge go through the table. Randi, who couldn’t defend Pace in her own common room, chooses Vee out loud in front of the same social machinery. Female friendship gives her cover, certainly, but the claiming still costs something, and Vee receives every bit of it. No one has chosen her like that in a long time. Of course the wrong-size feeling stops mattering. I would have taken the hand too.

Then Vee apologizes for her own fright, and Randi tells her, **“You were perfect.”** That is the sentence I wanted to scrape off the page. Even the reassurance is a grade. Vee doesn’t need to understand what happened because she performed acceptably through it.

So she walks home carrying her empty pink cup, feeling *picked*, and the title is sitting on my chest. She is picked. That is precisely the problem. I want Sunday’s hike immediately, I want her alone with Pace somewhere there isn’t a room of girls grading her, and I am terrified that she is going to bring Randi’s version of herself up the mountain instead of the woman who spun around Cassie’s dorm room because she had kissed a man on purpose.
