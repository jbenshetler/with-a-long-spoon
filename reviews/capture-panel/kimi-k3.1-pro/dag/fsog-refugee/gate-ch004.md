REACTION

Oh, Vee. Getting inside her head made the setup worse, because I understood every single bit of it. Not looking at the door until not-looking becomes its own obsession. Folding the yes down so it won’t come out the size it actually is. The right pen warming her like praise she’d worked for. She isn’t empty or stupid or waiting to be acted upon; she has a mind that likes the structure under numbers, friends she knows how to care for socially, a body she privately takes pleasure in, and this enormous unexamined hunger to be admitted. Randi doesn’t have to drag her anywhere. She just has to hold the door open.

And **“There you are”** nearly stopped my heart. Pace said that when he found the woman behind Randi’s face, and Randi experienced it as something almost unsurvivable. Now Randi says it to Vee, and Vee receives being found as pure joy. That echo feels much too exact to be comfortable. Randi may be giving Vee the approach she needed herself—the lit face, the certainty, the sense that someone has been looking specifically for you—without yet risking any of the exposure that came afterward for her.

The most devastating sentence for me was Vee deciding Randi wasn’t performing for the room like the other girls. Vee thinks she has finally found the one person who is simply herself. I know Randi is performing *specifically for her*. Not necessarily that every scrap of warmth is false—that’s what makes this so hard—but the bit has a shape, the openings are placed where people will walk into them, the flattery goes in like a key in a lock, and Vee cannot remember one complete thing they talked about afterward. She keeps the feeling and loses the transcript. That frightened me more than an overt threat would have. Whatever Randi is offering, Vee is already too dazzled to examine its actual contents.

Cassie. I loved Cassie immediately. Four words a day, no sulk about not wanting the coffee, no performance of being unimpressed either—just *that isn’t mine, you go*. And then **“Goodbye, Vee.”** My stomach dropped. I don’t think Cassie magically knows about Pace or the plan, but I think some accurate, unglamorous part of her registers that Vee is already being carried away from something. Vee smooths it because smoothing wrongness is what she does, then half forgets it because Randi is moving and the better thing is moving with her. I wanted to grab Vee by the cardigan and make her stay with that one strange word for another minute.

The café itself got under my skin: the joke she can finally understand because she’s been invited under the sign, the high table where belonging is visible, all those spoons ringing as everyone stirs the sweetness up from the bottom. Vee has spent two years reading rooms to find where she can safely fit. Now somebody simply pats the stool beside her. Of course she goes home warm. I would have gone home warm. That is the horrible, effective thing about this chapter—I can’t stand outside it sneering at her for falling for status and attention when the book has made me feel exactly how nourishing it seems.

I also loved that she isn’t spotless. *Floozies* arrives in her mother’s voice, but she looks longer than the judgment explains; she knows she has more than the other girls and could wear the crop top better, and then immediately covers that knowledge with the cardigan and the rules about nice girls. Her wanting has so many locks on it already. Her flat, helpless want for Randi has nowhere to go except *here, this, with her*, and she calls it warmth and chosenness because those are the permissions she currently has. I don’t think she is remotely ready to understand how much of this is Randi herself rather than Randi’s world.

“Love you, girl” after one coffee made me wary; **“See you later”** made me more so. Vee hears the queen promising there will be a later, and I hear Randi keeping the next appointment in a calendar Vee can’t see. Yet Vee’s happiness is real. I refuse to pretend it isn’t. She had the best hour she’d had in months with a woman who may genuinely delight in her and is also steering her. Both things are sitting in that empty paper cup.

My 1 a.m. text: *Vee is in so much trouble and I completely get why she’s walking into it smiling. Cassie said GOODBYE, Randi said SEE YOU LATER, and somehow I believe both of them.*
