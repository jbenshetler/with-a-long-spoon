REACTION

That common room made my skin crawl more than the bench did. Brooke’s warmth is horrible—the not-standing, the little glance at the laptop, the way she pretends each question is an afterthought while she checks his name, his truck, his subject, his status, whether there’s a scandal she can enjoy. And “Sorry to grill you. Go.” just as Randi finally has something to say. I hated that. Randi knows exactly what is being done to her and still has to match Kinsey’s pitch and call Pace cute, as though this man who took off her shoes and waited outside a locked door can be filed under *short-cute* and made harmless.

Then the shoes on the bed undid me. The cardigan still buttoned, the phone facedown, opening the closet and forgetting why. She has this private room she fought for, and she can’t even let herself cry in it. After Pace’s too-warm house, the blanket, the water, all that attention, she’s lying under a duvet that is merely adequate in a cold room. It isn’t that I think she should have stayed with him forever because he gave good aftercare. It’s that she has somewhere she was intensely cared for, and she has come back to the place where she knows how to disappear, and disappearing feels less like a choice than the absence of any door she could open.

“He’s hard to say no to” gave me a genuine jolt, because that is not a phrase I will ever find cute or romantic. But I don’t feel the chapter telling me her yes last night was secretly false. She said *Show me*. She stayed. What feels awful is that even her desire has always needed a socially usable explanation, and that was the one she gave the girls: he was persistent, she went along, what could she do. Now she hears how inadequate it is, and she still can’t get anywhere nearer the truth. She wanted him. She wanted what happened. Being wanted and being seen were not the same thing, and the second one has left her unable to recognize her own life.

I’m also frightened by how little Pace can actually know from where he’s sitting. He notices apple slices and held breath and the exact instant her weight becomes real, and now she can put herself back together so thoroughly that he may never get a truthful signal from her at all. Those two unread notifications made me lean toward the book. If one is his, I want her to read it. I’m afraid she won’t, or that she’ll answer in the bright voice and make everything fine.

And Vee’s absence feels more ominous now, not less. Randi knows exactly what it is to be quietly starving inside an immaculate life. If she can’t bear to let Pace find her again, I can imagine her trying to make that discovery happen for somebody else—offering Vee the aliveness she wants and fears, at a safer distance. My 1 a.m. text is: *she came home and lay down with her shoes on and couldn’t cry, and Brooke was downstairs filing his name. I’m officially more scared of the sorority house than the sex bench.*
