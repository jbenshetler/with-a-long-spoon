REACTION

Oh, **Peter**. *Rock.* I finished with my hand over my mouth, and not in the cozy way the first half had me expecting.

First, the timing made it impossible simply to melt. Randi was in that house Monday night, wearing his undershirt, eating his cooking, sleeping on his chest. Vee is there Tuesday night in his robe, wrapped around him in the same warm bed. He cooks, he keeps the cold on the other side of the glass, he holds whichever woman is there. Both intimacies are real. Only one of the women knows she is inside a triangle. I couldn’t let the domestic loveliness wash that away.

And yet I did melt. The robe falling open and Vee deciding she doesn’t mind, his hand resting warm over her breast while he pulls the quilt up with the other—this book can make an open robe more intimate than entire sex scenes on my Kindle. It isn’t just nudity now. It’s the ease of remaining uncovered without the evening immediately having to become about it.

This was the second quiet chapter in a row, and I did not skim a line. Pace finally got to be somebody other than the man administering exquisite care. **“And affectionate”** was the first thing that got me. Vee defends the casseroles before she criticizes them; her mother is not suddenly a cardboard villain because *floozie* lives in her head. The food was love. It was also beige and sealed and never allowed to surprise anybody. Pace receives both without using her childhood as another opportunity to present himself as the colorful rescue. Even *the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other* is offered lightly, not turned into a diagnosis she has to thank him for.

Then **“Less than staying.”**

That took the air out of me. Whatever was wrong at home, he has built a whole life where the heat is even, the tools have outlines, the questions get actual answers, and nobody can arbitrarily send him into the hall. Vee’s **“They took the thing you loved, and made it the punishment”** was so exactly right that I felt his surprise at being found there. She doesn’t swamp him with pity. She presses her shoulder against him once and lets the accuracy do the holding.

The math nearly lost me for half a paragraph—yes, all right, coastlines—and then he got to the first year of his life when he wasn’t in the hall, and I was gone. He was more naked talking about Newton than he was sitting there in his boxers. *Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some.* That is not a hot mathematician’s decorative genius. That is the interior of a lonely boy who found a place where wanting more did not get him punished. When Vee looks at the stars and the world shifts because he has turned his understanding toward her, I believed her completely.

And then **Daphne**.

I know that name. Daphne built a door once, and Pace did not respect it. The book put that fact in my hands forty chapters of emotional weather ago, and now here she is: nineteen to his fifteen, assigned to him, the soft middle of his two best years.

I am not going to pretend that arithmetic felt clean. Nor do I need Daphne to have been some cartoon predator; nineteen is young too, and that is part of the mess. But he was a child, sent away into a building full of adults, and one older student became his entire tender world. He can remember those years as the best he’d had **and** the arrangement can have failed him. Those truths do not cancel each other. Vee feels the ugly word forming and keeps it behind her teeth because she understands that he is defending the memory, not inviting her to prosecute it.

What unsettles me most is **“Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong.”** He sets every reason down in its proper slot until the whole thing looks structurally sound. Sensible ending. Natural distance. No villain. No damage anyone need claim. I don’t think he’s consciously lying to Vee. I think he has lived inside that account so long he cannot see the load-bearing lie in it.

And now I have questions about his consent scrupulousness that I did not want. Is his absolute respect for Vee’s no simply who he is, or something he built after Daphne’s door? Is *she chooses every step* an ethic, a penance, or both? Whatever the answer, it does not excuse his current trick of honoring every door he shows Vee while keeping the most important one concealed.

Which made **“Not tonight”** over the scar almost unbearably intimate. He doesn’t take her hand away. He holds it against the place he cannot discuss yet, and Vee turns her palm into his and leaves it there. After remembering Daphne’s door, Vee’s restraint felt like an answer. She doesn’t try the handle because she loves him. She doesn’t mistake intimacy for entitlement. She lets *not tonight* be complete.

Then that ending put the knife in.

**She had stumbled into her.** No, she hadn’t. **A man had crossed a coffee shop for it. That was all.** No, it wasn’t. Randi is still filed safely under *glamorous friend*, even after the kisses, and both arrivals become one seamless piece of undeserved fortune. Vee can see that Pace has handed her something painful and named it luck; pages later, she does the same thing with her whole transformed life because she lacks the facts that would let her name it accurately.

**For no reason she had done anything to deserve** is the splinter underneath all of it. There was a reason. Randi saw her laugh and wanted her. Pace found her easy to listen to and then found her extraordinary. Her lovability was not imaginary. Their deception was real too. When the truth comes, I am terrified she will keep the deception and throw away the evidence of her own worth.

My 1 a.m. text is: **Pace is Peter, college scared him less than staying home, Daphne was nineteen when he was fifteen AND REMEMBER THE DOOR, and Vee just called meeting Randi and him six weeks of undeserved luck. Monday Randi slept there; Tuesday Vee did. I love him and I want to shake him until the truth falls out.**

I need the induction now. I need something to happen in public, and I need one of these people to give Vee a plain fact before she wraps any more of herself around this beautiful missing premise.
