REACTION

Oh, thank God, Vee has a whole **table**. Stolen fries, Meg working steadily through too much cheese, Kayla ricocheting from Theo to Pace to hair, and Vee laughing so loudly the next table wants in. I hadn’t realized how afraid I was that her world would narrow until it was only Cassie, Randi, and Pace. It hasn’t. She has friends, a history with them, a boy she turned down who can still wave at her without making either of them miserable. Randi and Pace did not invent her radiance. They saw something real and decided they could arrange ownership of it, which is not remotely the same thing.

The truck retelling fascinated me. For a second I was indignant—where is her taking off the wet shirt? Where is her wanting him to **see** her? Where is his freezing hand against her stomach? Then I understood: Cassie got *On purpose. Me.* Meg and Kayla get the gentleman standing in the rain. And Pace kept the truck from Randi; now Vee keeps it from this audience too. She knows she is improving the story in the telling. That feels completely different from Randi revising *I wanted to* into *you couldn’t not*. Vee isn’t surrendering the meaning here. She is deciding who gets through which door. She can be climbing the walls with wanting and still decline to make her bravest, most private exposure into dinner-hall entertainment.

And she is so far gone on him. The pride when she says *doctorate*, the imitation of his flat little writing rule, knowing about the morning lifting, the shirt folded on her pillow, the **twelve days** available without counting. This isn’t merely sexual frustration, and it isn’t merely gratitude for being treated decently. She is collecting the ordinary machinery of his life and carrying it around affectionately. Meg’s *Everybody’s different at the start* was exactly the small, sane caution Vee needed, and I loved that it didn’t catch on anything. She didn’t have to defend him or insist her friends ratify the romance. She just kept feeling what she felt.

Kayla calling one kiss *insane* made me laugh and bristle at once. Vee is trying to explain that the waiting is not an absence of eroticism. It is twelve days of wanting with nowhere she has to put it down prematurely. He asks, waits for the true answer, and then reality actually matches what she said. No wonder she can’t think straight. She isn’t starved because he hasn’t taken enough; she is lit up because nothing has been taken from her at all. Kayla hears the number of kisses. Vee is describing the entire charge living in between them.

Then the phone buzzed, and *his house* changed the temperature of the whole chapter for me.

Because **I** know that house. Vee has never been through the door, but I know the deadbolt Randi always turns, the chair where her overnight bag does or doesn’t appear, the heat, the music, the bedroom lamp. I know the long table is not always a table. Vee is thinking there is finally a door and Saturday behind it, and I am suddenly seeing every object in that house lying in wait for her. Even if Pace’s invitation is entirely his own, even if all he wants is one private afternoon with a woman he likes, she would still be walking into the physical center of a life she doesn’t know exists.

And I do not like the timing. Sunday he protects the truck while feeding Randi selected pieces of Vee; Randi tears *I want us* out of herself and flees; Thursday Vee receives the barest possible invitation to his house. Perhaps Randi knows nothing about it. Perhaps Pace is simply ready. Perhaps this is him choosing Vee more plainly. But I cannot make myself trust the clean version while that tabletop is still sitting there in my mind.

Kayla’s *There’s no meal at two* made me laugh out loud, and then immediately feel a little ill. Yes, probably. Probably he wants her for the afternoon. I want that for Vee too—the anticipation, the privacy, the chance to want him without a trail or a restaurant or a campus policeman arranging the boundaries. But the book has made *two o’clock, no explanation* feel exquisitely exciting and exquisitely unsafe at the same time. Her yes can be completely eager and still be missing the premise. Again.

By the end I was letting my heart do the quick, light, stupid thing right along with hers. She looked so happy, with Meg asking about clothes and Kayla already taking possession of Saturday morning, and none of them getting up as the light died. I want her to go. I want her to have the afternoon she is imagining. I also want to sit outside that house with a hand-painted sign until somebody tells her the truth. I texted no one, but the 1 a.m. message would be: **she’s going to the house and she doesn’t know what the table is. I’m unwell.**
