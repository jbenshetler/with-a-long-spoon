REACTION

Oh, I loved this. I resented how safe it made me feel, because I know what’s underneath it, but for two hours I got to sit at that table with Vee and put my own shoulders down.

And first: thank God, she told **Cassie**. Not Randi. The phone pressed to her chest, the grin she would die to have anyone see—anyone but Cassie—and then Cassie sees the whole face anyway and receives it without making Vee pay for being happy. That felt like such a relief after watching Randi turn every private thing into material. Vee’s *So they’d know where they stood* told me so much about the boys before Pace. They needed her answer immediately because her uncertainty made them uncomfortable. Pace could leave the question with her and tolerate not knowing. *Like it could keep.* Yes. That is the difference.

Cassie’s *That’s not a boy. That’s a man* is normally a line I’d expect embroidered on a pillow in exactly the kind of book I’ve been DNFing, but she earned it. Also, Cassie knowing when to tease and when to put the teasing down may be one of the sexiest forms of emotional intelligence in this book, and she isn’t even the love interest.

Pace in the lobby got me too. Not the shirt, particularly, or even the shaved jaw—though Vee noticing that he had done all of it *for this* made me warm—but that he had been somewhere in himself when she arrived. He wasn’t posed there waiting to be the perfect man for her. He had an interior, and then he came back to the room and to her. The polished old boots, Christine, the six hours of writing, competing badly at powerlifting: he keeps becoming more of a person instead of more of a fantasy. I like that he has a whole life in which Vee is not currently required to be useful, soothing, or entertaining.

*May I choose?* went straight through me. Usually a man ordering for a woman makes me want to reach into the book and order separately out of spite. But Vee had asked for rescue without quite asking, and he heard that, made it a question, asked what she wouldn’t eat, and then took the responsibility seriously. The relief she feels at handing it over is not helplessness. It is being allowed to stop making one more decision correctly.

And then the meal was genuinely sensual. I lost the thread when he licked the dip off his hand too. The bread steam, the bright thing and the smoke-dark thing, the mint arriving just as she surfaced—my whole body was paying attention. But what undid me was that Vee was not *being good* at dinner. She wasn’t picking delicately, performing the cool girl who forgets to eat, or monitoring what her appetite might tell him. She ate until the hollow went quiet, and Pace did not congratulate her for it or turn her fullness into proof that he had healed her. He simply kept her company while she fed herself.

Randi’s *Did she eat all of it?* was in my head the entire time. Randi wanted Vee’s appetite as something she could get wet over and offer to Pace. This chapter gave the appetite back to Vee as a lived experience. Nobody was watching her from across a lawn. Nobody was grading what she took. She got to have it.

The *her hand had been wrong all its life* thought was one spoonful too sweet for me, but then the kiss happened and I stopped caring. Because he takes her no as **complete**. Not as a challenge, not as something to wait out with patient masculine superiority. He smiles and starts walking her home. That was the hottest moment in the chapter. Vee has to stop him. She has to remain still with her own wanting instead of letting his momentum carry her past it. And when she makes the exception, he still doesn’t treat that as an open door to everything else. He answers only the kiss she gives him, eases back, and leaves her another choice. The second kiss is hers too.

Which is exactly why the title is currently sitting in my chest like a splinter. Pace asks whether he may choose her **dinner**. He and Randi never asked whether they could choose *her*. He will honor her answer about Saturday, her rule about kissing, the last inch between their mouths—every choice except the one that would let her decide whether she wants to be inside their arrangement at all. The care is not fake. That no longer lets him off anything.

Vee has now stopped waiting for the bill to come due with him. She trusts where he is taking her. She can drop a conversational thread and nothing bad happens. God, discovering the design after *that* is going to be so much worse than discovering that a hot man lied. I’m afraid she’ll decide the restful, unguarded woman at that table was something Pace manufactured, rather than someone he genuinely had the good fortune to meet.

Now I need Randi to hear about this date. Not necessarily more sex yet—I need the accounting. Randi could manage *she’s hot* and even Vee’s appetite. I don’t think she can manage *she let me stop performing*. And I want Pace to keep some piece of this private for Vee again, which is an insane thing to be rooting for inside the relationship that started all the secrecy, but here I am.
