That is exactly why he decides not to give her the chance.

And Pace knows. He literally knows what she is doing. He feels her go dead still at disclosure and come alive at seduction; he tells himself he will not be talked around by her body; then he talks himself around for her. His little theory about gifts made me want to put my thumb through the screen. A silk dress can be a surprise. A concealed relationship and an engineered courtship are not a gift whose shape would be spoiled by description. He is treating Vee’s informed refusal as the disappointing outcome to be designed away.

The worst part is that his desire for Vee is real. That opening, with the shirts rolled and unrolled and the memory of her opening her eyes, was not a man simply executing Randi’s plan. He knows a mathematical bound will hold, so he thinks he knows what Vee will want before she does. That certainty is becoming more frightening to me than Randi’s overt steering.

And Randi.

**Me. She’ll remember me.**

Randi wants to be the woman Vee remembers when she is eighty. She wants a permanent place inside her. There is something so nakedly hungry in that—and so possessive. She cannot bear to be fully known by Pace, cannot stay for dinner, cannot accept warmth without eventually reaching for some exit, but she wants to be unforgettable to Vee. I loved her for one dangerous second and trusted her even less.

Also: **Never even kissed one. Not really.**

Excuse me, Randi? You kissed her on the mouth Sunday. You held her jaw and kissed her and left her scanning dorm windows in fear. Now you have erased it because it does not count until you decide it counts, and Pace is letting **girl things** stand as a closed door. They are building an “us” out of private Vee-shaped compartments while Vee is not permitted to know there is an us at all.

Their wanting is not counterfeit. It never is. That is what makes this whole thing so exhausting in the best and worst way. They can give each other something real and still use it to make the truth impossible.

Then she stayed.

Just for those few minutes, she gave him the loose-haired afterward he had missed so plainly, and his little *there* hurt me. He is so easy to feed in some ways. One woman lying on his chest and he stops thinking past it. But Randi’s soft, young, open face went straight into teeth, and Pace’s **Don’t** made me sit up. Hardest thing he has ever said to her—and **Friday** is already in his head. He will not let her mark him where Vee might see it. He is carrying a whole different evening into it. *Remembrance* suddenly feels less tender than acquisitive: Pace hoarding memories, Randi wanting to become one Vee can never escape, and Vee not yet allowed to remember anything accurately.
