REACTION

Oh, I would have been in such trouble at the pencil. The forearms, yes, fine, the eyelashes, the birthmark, the mysterious shirt—but *he set the pencil back in her hand*. That is the moment I would have been mentally naming our children.

Because Vee did not want to be rescued. She wanted to solve the optional problem nobody required her to solve, and Pace understood that immediately. Her work was right. She was not stupid or inadequate or in need of a man to perform brilliance at her. She needed one small turn of the lens, and then he gave the problem back to her at precisely the point where she could finish it herself. In the stats lecture, Randi’s presence made the clean pleasure Vee takes in math recede into scenery. Here, Pace’s attention gives that pleasure back to her, sharper than before. That is so much worse than if he had simply dazzled her. He is actually *good* for her in this scene.

The right-angles explanation was the one place I felt the book leaning over my shoulder a little—yes, I see the shape, two difficult things becoming intelligible together—but then Vee produced those clean lines herself and I stopped caring. She was sitting there feeling chosen while also being treated as intellectually capable. Nobody has apparently done both for her before. I believe completely that her body understood what had happened before she could articulate it.

And God, the awful intimacy of Vee borrowing Randi’s little introduction routine to charm Pace. She is trying on Randi’s ease because she wants to be more than herself, while Randi—the woman who invented the ease—can only stand there and give Pace her name as one bare word. *Randi.* No adjective, no parents, no practiced door held open for the other person. She is introducing herself as a stranger to the man who has seen her beg. I felt that all the way down.

Her coffee cup frightened me. Vee reads boredom because Vee is decent and because the alternative is unavailable to her. But twice the cup goes up and comes down without a sip, and Randi is sitting there watching Pace give Vee the exclusive attention she knows from his bed. Is she jealous? Panicking? Discovering that facilitating something in theory is very different from watching it acquire its own weather? Or is she angry that Vee gets to receive Pace openly, without having to turn the whole experience into a shameful amputation afterward? I don’t know yet, and I love that I don’t. The plan is working beautifully and Randi does not look like a woman having a beautiful time.

Cassie’s great-aunt line made me bark. She sees so much and makes so little fuss about seeing it. Of course she can identify the look that slides off her every day; of course she can identify its opposite without needing to make either one a tragedy. And the healer teasing gave me another little piece of Vee I adore—a whole semester up until two keeping some business major alive in a video game. She is competent and loyal and probably much more accustomed to being useful than being courted. Cassie knows her. Pace is learning her. Randi has selected her. Those are three very different kinds of being known, and Vee currently cannot distinguish them.

Randi taking the phone and translating everything into *he wants you* and *he wants to see you naked* felt intensely self-protective. Pace has to become ordinary male appetite because that is a language Randi can manage. If he is tender, curious, and specifically interested in Vee’s mind, then something much less containable is happening. And *he was cute*—there she goes with *cute* again, the word she reaches for when Pace needs to be made smaller.

The coffee nearly made me yell. Of course he left without his coffee. **He did not come for coffee.** Randi knows that better than anyone alive, and she turns the clearest evidence of staging into proof of romantic inevitability: *A man doesn’t forget his coffee over a girl he isn’t going to call.* That is elegant and vile. Then Vee offers the tiny self-protective rule she has—make him wait a day—and Randi removes that too. Not cruelly. Warmly. As a friend helping her not ruin something good. Vee’s yes is entirely real, and she is being denied every piece of information that might have changed how she wanted to use it.

And then the froyo date was so gentle I couldn’t even hold onto my anger cleanly. The leaning, excessive cup and no mother-voice arriving to tell her *that’s enough*. Pace not commenting on how much she took. Vee saying the embarrassing true thing and offering him an escape hatch, and Pace refusing both available bad responses: he doesn’t pretend she didn’t say it, and he doesn’t turn it into a solemn vow to rescue her from everyone who came before. *Your tart’s holding up.* It gives her somewhere safe to put her eyes. It lets the confession be received without making her pay for having made it.

That is the emotional intelligence I am here for, and it is currently inside a lie. Vee is not being flattened by this courtship. She is expanding—research ambitions, appetite, humor, the full size of her own wanting. Discovering the design won’t merely tell her that Randi and Pace lied. It will threaten to make all that expansion feel like something they manufactured rather than something she genuinely found in herself.

I need the next date, obviously. But I need just as badly the moment Randi and Pace are alone afterward and she asks how it went. I want to see whether he tells her the truth about what it felt like, and whether Randi can survive hearing it.
