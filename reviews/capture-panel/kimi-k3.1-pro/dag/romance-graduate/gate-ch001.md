REACTION

Well, *fuck*. Okay. I’m in.

The apple slices did me in before the bench even had a chance. Not the spanking—the thin slices, cut sides up, because he noticed she eats them faster that way. That is the sort of specific attention that makes me put a hand over my Kindle like somebody else in the room might read it. And then she straddles him and puts his hands exactly where she wants them, and he lets her, and the whole thing has heat without either of them turning into a stock character. I believed her appetite. I believed his patience. I believed how badly she wanted to be unraveled while still believing she was directing the evening.

The bench scene was genuinely, almost uncomfortably hot. Not because of the furniture—though, good God, the furniture—but because he keeps refusing the moment she thinks she can manage. The waiter voice. The “please” that still belongs to the woman performing desirability. Him waiting until that voice is gone. When she finally begs, it isn’t cute, and that’s what makes it work. The crying afterward didn’t cool it down for me; it made the whole thing feel dangerous in the way I want this kind of book to be dangerous.

And then: “There you are, Randi.”

Randi. I actually stopped. I had been reading her as Vee because the jacket had put Vee in my hands, and suddenly this is the woman who is supposed to be drawing Vee open while never admitting how much she wants her. So Pace gets *there first*. He finds the woman behind Randi’s face, and her response is not surrender but terror. That bathroom mirror sequence hurt. Her trying the photograph smile over the face that had begged—the face obeying and the eyes not going back—that was awful and excellent. The bottle cap pressing under her thumbnail made my skin prickle.

Pace’s tenderness after is real, I think, and that’s what makes his final contentment so unbearable. He respects the locked door. He doesn’t demand an interpretation. He notices the flinch when he calls her beautiful, but he still ends the night quietly happy, thinking she has met more of herself and that he can feed this hunger again. Meanwhile she has decided *not ever*. She says yes to staying and means something closer to *I have to contain what you just did to me.* He thinks the lock has opened; she’s turning a different key.

So now the game with Vee doesn’t feel like a decadent premise. It feels like something Randi might use to regain the upper hand, and that scares me. I want Vee to arrive immediately. I also want to grab her by the shoulders before she does.
