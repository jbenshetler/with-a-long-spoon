DECADE JOURNAL 4
TRUST: I now read Randi as the book's tragic center as much as Vee — the fraud money (Ch. 39) makes sense of the girl from Chapter 1 who can only be touched behind a locked bathroom door, who built a "face" because her whole life is bought and false. She's operating Vee and dissolving into loving her and cannot say it any more than Pace can. Pace: the withheld word is not cruelty exactly, it's the Daphne wound — he substitutes deeds for declarations because the declaration is where he was destroyed. The book has made both predators legible without excusing either, which is the whole tightrope. Who owes whom: everyone owes Vee the plan; Vee owes Randi a mercy she's already quietly paid (the data); Randi owes Vee a truth (I love you, two ways) she's too cowardly to risk; Pace owes Vee a word he can't afford. The debts are now emotional, not just strategic, and that's the trap the jacket promised — "as true for the two who started it as for the girl." Cassie and Sheri remain the only solvent parties.
APPETITE: 10 — the back stretch is clearly the reckoning and I would claw back to it.
RETURN RISK: Genuinely low now. The only thing that could sour me is the very last page — I'm braced for Chapter 50 to close on the reunion, and if it closes as *pure* triumphant reunion with the withheld word papered over by great sex and pancakes, I'll feel the book flinched at its own thesis. But it's earned the benefit of the doubt forty chapters running.

VERDICT

BOOK TWO: **yes.** I'd buy it right now, walking out of the store, no hesitation. This book did the one thing I most distrust the genre to do and did it with total control: it wrote a grooming-and-manipulation operation from *inside* the enchantment, made me feel the charm exactly as the mark feels it, and never once lost the frame — never asked me to clap for Pace, never pretended shame was spice, never let "it got real for them too" launder the harm. The jacket's "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over. That was the problem" is not a tagline the book aestheticizes and pockets; it's a wound the book keeps its thumb pressed on for fifty chapters. And Gstaad (46) is the proof of authorial intent — the predators are the most trapped, most hollowed-out people in the frame, and the book *knows* the machine cost the operators their own capacity to be known. I need to see whether Vee ever learns there was a plan, whether Randi ever gets brave, whether the word ever gets spent. I'm not owed a happy ending and I don't want one. I want the reckoning, and this author has earned my trust that there'll be one.

MAILING LIST: **yes.** Anyone who can hold consent, arousal, shame, class, and manipulation in the same hand this steadily — and write an infinite-surface/error-correcting-code metaphor that's *also* a description of the seduction — gets my email. I want to know what else she's written and what's coming.

TELL A FRIEND: Okay — so this is the one I've been hunting for and half-afraid to find. It's *literary erotica* about a 21-year-old who gets deliberately steered into a relationship by a couple who planned the whole thing as a game, and I know how that sounds, I know exactly the awful book that premise usually is. This is the opposite of that book. The author *sees it* — every single page, it knows the man is a predator who was groomed himself and calls his own grooming "luck" and therefore can't recognize what he's doing; it knows the glamorous best friend is running a second seduction and drowning in real love she's too much of a coward to risk. The consent is written as an actual live event in the girl's head — her arousal is braided to inherited shame and the book *names the braid* instead of getting off on it. And it's genuinely, meltingly hot, and the food writing will make you insane, and there's a chapter set at a New Year's ball in the Alps that is one of the best single things I've read all year — the predator sleeping with a "perfect" man and feeling absolutely nothing and understanding she's built her whole life to never be brave. The catch: the middle sags a little — there's a scene/debrief rhythm you'll clock, and the two dorm friends are thin next to Cassie. And you have to be okay sitting inside a manipulation for four hundred pages without the book handing you the release of everyone getting caught — it's Volume One, it *ends unresolved on purpose*, the girl still doesn't know there was a plan. But if you read the way I read — if you want the dark stuff and you need the book to *know* it's dark — this one's in good hands. Bring it to lunch. I want to talk about Randi.
