TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — this is the dark one I've been waiting for someone to write *awake*. It's about a girl, Vee, who's starving for kindness because her mother taught her to be ashamed of her own body, and two glamorous people who pick her off a college quad like they're shopping and build the best year of her life around her — and she never knows there's a plan. And I know how that sounds, I *know*, that's the exact premise I usually throw across the room, because ninety percent of these books don't notice the grooming is grooming and want you to swoon at the predator. This one notices *everything*. Every "may I," every heat-turned-up-early kindness is shown to you as tenderness and as technique in the same breath, and the book never once resolves the tension for you — it just makes you hold it. The interiority is unreal: the woman being run, Randi, gets a dissociation scene in chapter one that I'd hand to people as a craft example, and the man, Pace, turns out to have been groomed himself at fifteen and calls it the best years of his life while the girl he's deceiving quietly does the math and holds the ugly word under her tongue and doesn't say it. That chapter wrecked me. It's wickedly hot when it wants to be and the consent mechanics are scrupulous, which is the *point* — "every yes was freely given, that was the problem." My two gripes: the girls'-debrief-over-drinks structure gets used one or two times too many in the middle and you start seeing the seams, and it's Volume One, so it just *stops* — no resolution, she still hasn't shown a soul the dress, the second door (she's falling for Randi too and won't let herself know it) is barely cracked. So don't start it expecting a whole story. Start it if you want to be in genuinely good, genuinely unsettling hands, and be okay that Book Two is where the bill comes due. I already pre-decided I'm buying it.
