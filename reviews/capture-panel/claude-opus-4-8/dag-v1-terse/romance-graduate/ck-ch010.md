WHO'S WHO

Pace — the mathematician. Grad student, finishing a PhD, lives out west of town off six-fourteen past where it goes to gravel, drives a white pickup, builds his own furniture (the bench, the oak cabinet, the table). Older-seeming than his years. Courtly in a way that isn't affected — "may I," "may I choose," asks before he takes, gives the pencil back at the exact line where the solving stays hers. That's his whole move: he waits, he listens, he leaves the choosing to you until choosing is the erotic thing. He did it to Randi and unmade her on the bench. Now he's doing the identical thing to Vee in daylight. There's a woman named Daphne in his past — a door he didn't respect once, and it cost him; he's learned not to go in where he's not invited. He is not a villain the way I keep waiting for him to be, and that's what unsettles me.

Randi — the one I met first, facedown and begging on the bench, coming apart against her own will and then locking the bathroom door to put the face back on. Twenty-one, sits on the sorority Standards board, has the single room, blue eyes she's built her whole life around, eats apples in thin slices, polices every want. Vee (Vivienne Thorne, "V.T., like the university") — the target who doesn't know there's a game.
