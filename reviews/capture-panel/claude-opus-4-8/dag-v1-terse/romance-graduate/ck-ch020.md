WHO'S WHO

Pace — the mathematician, and I've now been all the way inside his house and his bed. Powerlifter (lifts first thing, writes six hours, stops — "my writing's no good after that"), builds his own furniture in a converted-garage shop cleaner than my kitchen, sews his own clothes because he couldn't find a shirt he liked, licenses an MRI patent that pays for the good quiet order of the place. Lives twenty minutes past the pavement, up a driveway you drive past the first time. His whole move is unchanged and it works on me every time: he asks, he waits, he leaves the door open and lets her walk through it — the bra "changes the line, it'll lie to me," "would you be bothered if I was too hard," the summit dress handed back "made real." A scar high on the inside of his thigh she didn't ask about. Something closes in him at the word "parents." He is not a villain the way I keep waiting and that is still the worst thing about him.

Randi — the co-architect, and this stretch is hers as much as anyone's. The brunches are the machine running at full power: she mines Vee for the exact intimacy Vee guarded from Cassie, names Vee's own wanting back to her "clean" (the frosted plum, "you're not caught, you're lucky, done right"), opens the door with soft yeahs and then shuts it at the cruelest possible instant — "You don't have to tell me" — leaving the secret half out with nowhere to go. Plants "a little helpless, done right" as bait via the tickling/scarf story. The Mercedes, the white-gold band she's never without, the green scent. And now she kisses Vee on the mouth at the curb — twice, the drawing-back and the second press — and it's landing as ordinary now, no more window-scanning. Everything she does is an instrument and I still can't find the bottom of what she actually feels.

Vee — no longer the girl who hasn't crossed his threshold. She's climbed his mountain, stripped in his truck, stood bare on a riser to be measured, and slept with him. The book's truest line about her: shame and heat on the same wire, no seam, and she's stopped looking for one. She curates — hands Cassie the clean version, keeps the middle, tells herself the omission is kindness. She invented the "Trade" panty-fold as her own free idea. She's happy in a way that scares me because I know the architecture under it.

Cassie — still the load-bearing thing, still the only one who could catch it, and she is being fed the edited cut. Went rapt at the flannel, "keep the shirt," clocked the turned-up heat as tenderness. Athlete, dry, loving, real. She "did not pull at the seam. Whether she felt it there, Vee couldn't have said." That's my open wire.

Kayla and Meg — Vee's dining-hall friends, the audience for the shirt story. Kayla: blonde curls, never stops talking, "bring a change of clothes." Meg: dark, level, calm, follows the Pace saga in installments, "everybody's different at the start." Theo — a nice boy Vee friend-zoned in September; waves. Peripheral.

Cassie — please, please see it. She's assembling the same file I am and reading it as a love story.
