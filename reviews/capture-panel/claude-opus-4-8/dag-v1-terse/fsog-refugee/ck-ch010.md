WHO'S WHO

Pace — the man. Grad student, math PhD, "more or less" finishing. Lives out west of town off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel. Builds his own furniture — quartersawn oak, walnut, the bench. Small, ordinary-faced, moves like he's never once had to think about where his body is. Keeps his house too warm. Steely Dan. The one who asks — *may I choose, may I call you* — and then actually waits for the answer. Rolls his right cuff, eats with his right hand, some private rule. There's a Daphne in his past: a door she built that he didn't respect when he was young, and it cost him, and he learned. That's the origin of the closed-bathroom-door restraint in ch1.

Randi — not who I thought. The "she" on the bench in chapter one is *Randi*, not Vee. Sorority, near the top of it, Standards board, her own single room. Black hair, startling blue eyes, the flat oval face, builds her whole life around being looked at. Twenty-one. She's Pace's — girlfriend, whatever it is — and she's the one who runs the daylight operation. The one who says *dear friend*, holds Vee's hand in public, calls her *girl*.

Vee — Vivienne Thorne. The redhead they chose. Tall, lush, freckled, copper hair, an athlete's body she treats as a problem. Econ, stats. Grew up under a mother's voice policing every bite. Best hour of her life keeps happening to her lately. Doesn't know any of it was arranged.

Cassie — Vee's roommate and real friend. Plain-spoken, ball cap, lab and rec team, boxy navy suit. Not enchanted by Randi. The only clear-eyed person in the book. "Goodbye, Vee." "Off six-fourteen." "That's not a boy. That's a man."

Brooke — sorority chapter president, warm-voiced, stays seated to stop a room. Sloane, Kinsey — the other sisters. Meg — Vee's floormate at the collision.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Chapter one was a lie I told myself — I read that whole bench scene sure it was the jacket's Vee, and it was Randi. Pace took Randi further than she'd ever gone, made her beg in a voice that was actually hers, made her cry — and she fled to the bathroom, looked in the mirror, and decided: he can have the face, never the one he found. Not ever. Then chapter two: she goes home and lies to the sorority, portions herself, presses the bottle cap into her nail bed like she did on the bench.

Then chapter three flips everything open. Pace and Randi walk campus playing the *pointing game* — he points at women, she picks — and it lands on Vee coming out of the dining hall, laughing with her throat bare. Randi collides with her on purpose, cleanly, so it looks like Vee's fault. "It began as a game between the two people now closest to her." Now I know.

Everything from four to ten is the trap closing, all sweetness, mostly from inside Vee: Randi befriends her (Chi Latte, the high table, *love you girl*). Pace "happens" into the coffee shop, solves her calculus by *giving her back the pencil*, asks *may I call you*. Froyo. Then the real dinner — *may I choose*, the whole evening going soft, and she kisses *him*, on purpose, breaking her own rule. Hiking planned. Randi calls her *dear friend* in front of Brooke and holds her hand where the room can see.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace — this is the knife of the book for me. Everything he does is the exact thing I've hunted for years: he asks, he waits, he leaves the safeword in her mouth, he honors the locked door because of what Daphne cost him, he keeps himself out of the math so the solving stays hers. If I didn't know about the game I'd be in love. But I do know. So the courtliness reads double — real tenderness *and* a machine, the same *may I* aimed at Randi on the bench and at a hungry stranger by accident-on-purpose. I don't trust him and I can't stop watching him, and I resent a little how well he's made.

Randi — I'm frightened for her and frightened of her at once. Ch1 and ch2 made her a person to me — the begging, the nail bed, the girl who decided never to be seen again — so when she runs the daylight con on Vee I can't just hate her. She's spending something real on Vee (*dear friend* cost her something in front of Brooke) while harvesting her. Both true. She owes Vee honesty she'll never give.

Vee — I ache for her. Her wanting is hers, rendered from the inside, not done to her — the pencil landing like earned praise, the mother's voice going quiet at the table, *I let him, I just handed it over.* That's the thing I read for. And she's walking straight into a thing two people built. I want to protect her and I can't.

Cassie — the one I trust completely. Splashes Vee instead of asking. Names the look that slides off her. The dread said out loud.

The mirror monologue: he can have the face, not ever the one he found.
The pointing game, and Vee laughing with her throat bare, already chosen, not knowing.
The redhead's own hand flying to her mouth to apologize for a collision Randi engineered.
*May I choose?* — and the relief of handing it across the table.
Giving her back the pencil at the exact line she could finish it herself.
Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee," and "off six-fourteen," and the cold thin line off the sill Vee doesn't feel.
The bench going back to being a table, waiting.

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

Whether the care stays real or curdles into the disregard I won't forgive — that's the whole knife-edge I'm reading on. The jacket says it got real "as true for the two who started it," so I'm watching for the game to catch its makers: does Randi fall for Vee, does the thing she decided never to feel come back through this girl? What is Randi's actual wound (mother, food, the being-looked-at)? What happens the first time Vee finds out — and she will. Does Pace's *may I* survive the moment Vee learns it was aimed, not found. And I'm bracing for the three of them: the jacket's woman-beside-her Vee doesn't let herself want yet. If the book ever winks — asks me to find the harvesting romantic instead of dreadful — I'm gone. So far it hasn't winked; it lays the machinery bare. That honesty is why I trust it enough to keep reading a thing that scares me.

WHAT'S FADING

The sorority sisters blur — Sloane and Kinsey are barely more than names, and I've half-lost which lines were Brooke's grilling in ch2. The actual content of every Randi-and-Vee conversation is gone the same way it's gone for Vee — I kept the feeling, not the words; I couldn't reconstruct a single thing Randi said at Chi Latte. The pool chapter and the froyo chapter have melted into "sweet, warm, no heat." The specifics of the calculus (Euler's, the crossed marks, right angles) I've let go — I kept only that he gave the pencil back. And I keep having to remind myself Pace and Randi are already a couple with a whole history, because the Vee chapters are so fully from inside her that the two schemers recede when I'm in her warmth — which is, I suspect, exactly the trick being played on me too.
