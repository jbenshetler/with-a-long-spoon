WHY: This is the chapter I've been circling since 39, and it doesn't flinch — the book takes the entire apparatus of romance it's been feeding Vee and runs it flawlessly on Randi, then shows it dead in her hands: "the pleasure real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared," the cold she brought in and carries out, the lipstick left on a man who never found her. That she names both wants plainly, that she chooses cover for Vee's sake dressed as tenderness, that she reads "easier was better" about John's lead and hears it as her own surrender to comfort — the book still sees every wire it's laying. It knows exactly how alarming its own softness is.

WHAT I'M CARRYING: "She had put nothing on the table and had won exactly that." The band kept out of the light at her mother's table — still locked, still unexplained, and now clearly a leash she wears at home. "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best. She wished it wasn't true." And the two texts: the fairytale she gives Vee, every word true and none of it the thing, "miss your face most though, gorgeous" the one line she meant. She loves Vee without a private qualifier for the first time in her life and is too cowardly to risk it — the maker, unmade.
