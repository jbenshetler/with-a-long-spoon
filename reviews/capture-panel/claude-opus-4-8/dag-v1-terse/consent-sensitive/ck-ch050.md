WHO'S WHO

Randi — the maker, and by now the most unmade person in the book. Ch 46 laid her all the way open: New Year in Gstaad, a flawless man named John who skied better and led better and conjured sleighs, and she felt nothing — "the pleasure real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared," the cold she brought in and carried out, her lipstick left on a man who never found her. She loves Vee without a private qualifier — the first time in her life she's said "I love you" to anyone and meant it as no kind of currency — and she is too cowardly to risk telling her, because Vee's face is the one scenario she can't run to the end. So she keeps the brunches, keeps the cover, lets Vee arrive at it alone. And then in ch 49 she runs the nakedest con of the whole book: takes a red-eye-wrecked, cried-out Vee apart perception by perception ("the fear's a liar, and you haven't slept"), reframes Pace's month of silence as a boy dug in, dissolves the spite-blonde, converts Vee's shame to heat in real time, and sends her out bare under a coat in her, Randi's, heels — protecting her own access, dressed as sisterhood. The band on her wrist: still locked, plain white gold, and ch 46 showed her keeping it out of the light at her mother's table — a leash she wears at home, still unexplained. Her father's fraud from 39 sits under all of it, unspoken.

Pace — got his own head this stretch (ch 48) and it didn't flip me to his side, which is the point. He sewed the burgundy dress himself (ch 41) — the trap as devotion, again. He declines every naming: engineers-back "I love how good we are together" against her smuggled sentence (42), "you know what happened after the dance" (43), "believe what I do," "words are cheap" (45). In front of Sheri he lays brick on brick of everything he does — there every weekend, phone in the other room, "I told her she was the most important thing in my life" — and can't say the word, and admits not-saying it "matters more to you than having her." Genuinely gone on Vee ("the air crackles," "the philosophy of her food," deflated same breath). Real name Peter, still unspoken between them. The scar high in the thigh, still unasked.

Vee — walked out on him crying in ch 45, the farthest she's ever gotten from the warm, having named the withholding herself. And then the break broke her: alone in Ohio (47), building a blonde rival from spite, no name for him or herself. Ch 49–50 is the undoing of the walk-out: Randi rebuilds her nerve as "her own bravery" — "you've got the goods, gorgeous girl" repeated "first in Randi's warm certainty and then in her own less certain voice" — and she drives out bare under the coat and gives him the whole of herself in the doorway, and the naming crisis resolves by her abandoning the ask entirely. "She had come to the end of asking." Reconciled, fed pancakes at midnight, called "Vee" warm and cracked-open — and no closer to a word than in December.

Cassie — still my anchor, still flat and dry and kind. Ch 41: "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to." / "Both." Ch 50: takes in the coat and the man's sweats and the going-out heels in one pass, "Hmm," back to her book. Holding the frame.

Sheri — finally onstage, and not what I feared. Not a rival — Pace's friend of two years, small, permed, too much makeup worn like a dare, queer (lost Paige over the holidays, her own family estrangement the grief she won't perform). She's Cassie from the man's side: won't be charmed, takes his true bricks and refuses to be moved — "that's a hell of a lot of reasons for a man who's already got the girl," "not saying it matters more to you than having her," "that feels pretty shitty." She's the one who confirms Randi ("the sorority one") never learned her name. The blonde Vee built from spite was never real.

John — the New Year corpse. The whole safe approved life in one tailcoat, everything done well and nothing landing. Exists to be the photo-negative of Pace.

Meg, Kayla, Brooke — background. Kayla did the laundry-room bit in 50.

WHERE THINGS STAND

The break split the book in two and I got both halves of the same midnight. Before it: the Chili's disaster (43) — she asks to be taken out, he can't hold his eyes on her under the noise, "I'm listening now" dissolves the fight into sex and a forgiveness that won't hold. The shirt night (44) — she wears his shirt bare and feels it go empty, "warm because she was warm, he wasn't in it," takes it off and lies in the cold. The doorstep (45) — drives out, "what are we," "words are cheap and you still won't spend them on me," refuses the settling hand, leaves crying. Then a month of mutual silence, neither breaking. Over the break, the two mirrored New Year midnights: Randi in Gstaad feeling nothing beside a perfect man (46), Vee alone in her outgrown childhood bed feeling everything and building a rival from spite (47); each texts the other an edited self. Pace at the diner with Sheri, unable to say the word out loud even to a friend who calls him on it (48). Then term starts: Vee arrives wrecked at Randi's, gets rebuilt and armed (49), and drives out bare under the coat to reconcile without words — the big doorstep reveal, sex on the couch with the door banging open, pancakes after, "Vee" said warm, both of them cracked open (50). Cassie's "Hmm" closes it.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Randi — I ache for her and I'm frightened of her at once, and ch 49 made me hold both harder than ever. The Gstaad chapter earned every bit of the ache: a woman who arranged her whole life so she'd never have to be brave, now loving someone she's too cowardly to risk. But the very next time I see her she's running the cleanest, most surgical piece of grooming in the book — on a sleep-deprived girl who came to her for comfort — and calling it courage. The book knows both things are true at once and won't let me off either. That's still the best thing in it.

Pace — don't trust, and now I've been inside his head and still don't. The tenderness is real and it's the delivery system both; ch 48 didn't redeem him, it just showed the withholding costs him too. He owes her the one free thing and won't pay it, and the book keeps that a debt, not a quirk.

Vee — frightened for her, and more than before, because the seam I keep checking got walked across. In 45 she found the line herself and left on it. By 50 that walk-out has been reprocessed into her own idea of bravery and she's abandoned the ask. She owes no one and keeps paying — now in shame converted to heat, handed over as a gift she thinks she chose.

Cassie, Sheri — my two anchors, one per lover, both dry and unbuyable. As long as they're in their rooms I have footing.

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

The word — whether he ever pays it, and what it costs when he does, now that she's stopped asking. Whether Randi ever tells Vee, or whether the cover holds to the end of the volume; that unrun scenario, Vee's face, is the loaded thing now, more than the photo. Speaking of which — the nude photo from way back is still a gun on the mantel I haven't heard fire; I've half-lost track of it and I want to know if the book has too. Randi's father's fraud and whether it ever surfaces between them. The band, still. Vee's want for Randi — it went quiet under the Pace-reconciliation and I want to see whether the book brings it back with the same awareness or lets it drift. And the standing test, which shifted this stretch: the danger isn't the dark content going naive, it's the comfort. Ch 50 was the longest applause-stretch yet and it kept the dread packed under it (the wall named, the word unpaid, the ask abandoned as loss) — but only just. I'm watching for the chapter where the reunion glow has nothing under it and the book forgets it engineered the coat.

WHAT'S FADING

The order of the pre-break fights is smearing — Chili's (43) vs the shirt (44) vs the doorstep walk-out (45) want to merge into one long "what are we / he won't say it" loop, and I'm holding them apart mostly by their exits: 43 = they go out and it fails, 44 = she's alone with the shirt, 45 = she drives to him and leaves. The Gstaad specifics are going the way the earlier food went — I keep "flawless man, sleigh, dress bought for a daughter she'll tell someday, felt nothing," not the star-chef foams or which run they skied. John's name I have; his face is gone already. Sheri's details (Paige, the birds, the Brooks & Dunn cover band in February) are thin — I keep "queer, estranged from her family, won't be charmed, calls him on it." The mechanics of Vee's mirror-scene in 50 (the denied orgasm, the near-miss with the cop) I keep as "she wound herself up and left it unfinished on purpose, drove out charged," not the beat-by-beat. Pace's real name Peter is barely held — it hasn't mattered in chapters. And the specific dishes are gone again except as pattern; the midnight pancakes I'll keep only because they were the reunion.
