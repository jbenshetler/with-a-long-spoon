REACTION

Oh, this is the one that turned my stomach in the good way — the way a book does when it finally puts you inside the person you've been watching get walked toward a cliff. Three chapters of Pace's hands and Randi's held breath and the *pointing game*, all of it from the side of the two people running it, and now here's Vee, and she's just… a girl. A nice girl who got there early and wrote the date at the top of a clean page "so that her hand would have somewhere to be." That killed me. I know that. Everyone knows that. And the book knows I know it, and it hands me this open, hungry, un-lonely-wanting-to-be-less-lonely twenty-one-year-old and lets me feel exactly how good the warmth feels while I'm screaming inside because I *watched them pick her off the dining hall steps like produce.*

The thing I can't get out of my head is that the transcript is blank. "She had been keeping the feeling, which was the feeling of being a person who got to sit at this table with this girl, and the feeling was total and the transcript was blank." That is the most honest and most frightening sentence in the chapter, because it's true to how being love-bombed actually works — you can't reconstruct a single word, you only keep the glow — and because it's *exactly* the mechanism. Randi doesn't have to say anything real. Vee will supply the meaning. The book already told us in Chapter 3 that Randi says four hundred words a day and nine of them land; here we watch her land all of them on a girl who won't remember any. And Vee thinks *she's* the lucky one.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. The four-words-a-day girl who won't take the bit's open door, who puts her name down flat — "Cassie" — and won't perform back. She's the only person in this book so far who isn't running a routine, and she's the one who says *"Goodbye, Vee."* The whole word. The word for a longer parting than a Wednesday. Vee smooths it over — "It's *coffee*, weirdo" — and I did NOT smooth it over. That landed like a hand on my arm. Cassie sees something. Cassie's known Vee long enough to feel the temperature drop when a certain kind of person turns the full sun on. That "something passed over her face, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to" — the book is trusting me to name it, and I did, and I hate it.

What I keep circling: I'm being *asked* to fall for Randi exactly as Vee does, and the jacket told me that's the design, and I can feel it working even knowing. The right pen. *Love you, girl.* The hand flat between the shoulder blades for one second. It's warm! It reads as warm! That's the horror of it — there's no seam I can point to and say *there, that's the con,* because a real friendship would look identical from inside Vee's chest. The book isn't telling me how to feel about Randi here. It's making me feel the good thing and holding the bad thing in my other hand, and the two won't resolve, and I don't want them to.

I noticed the floozies beat — Vee reading the Chi Latte room, the sports-bra girls, and the double voice: her own eye lingering ("a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for") under her mother's word *floozies,* the cardigan worn over the better shirt on purpose so the room won't know it's a choice. That's the shame the jacket promised, quietly seeded, and it rhymes with Randi's own trimmed appetite from Chapter 1 — the apple slices. These are two women who were both taught to portion themselves. One of them's now setting the table for the other. That's the part that's merciless, and it's earning its "merciless."

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she can't remember a single thing the girl said and she thinks that means it was the best hour of her life. i need to lie down.*
