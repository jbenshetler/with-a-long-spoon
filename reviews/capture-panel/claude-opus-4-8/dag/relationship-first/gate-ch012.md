REACTION

Okay, so the hike. I've been waiting for this since chapter 10 and here's the thing — I was braced for it to be the chapter where the machine clicks another gear, and instead it disarmed me almost the whole way up. That's the trouble with this book. It keeps being *good* right where I want to stay suspicious.

The boots. Meg's boots, half a size big, the heel lifting — that's the first true thing, because it's Vee's, not the plan's. Nobody engineered a blister. And when he crouches and takes the boot off and her stomach clenches with the mother's voice already loading up *what kind of girl lets a man* — and then he just... fixes it. Moleskin, warms it with his thumb, laces it different, pats the toe, *done*. "Should've done that at the lot. I'm sorry. I didn't think to ask about the boots." And I sat there and felt the exact thing Vee felt, which is the problem, which is that he is *good at this* and I can't always tell the book from the man. The apology is real competence. It's also the play — the same play, back off, take the fright out of the room, leave her bigger than she was. I know it and it worked anyway.

But then the summit, and I have to be honest, the summit is the best writing I've gotten from this book and it has almost nothing to do with either of them. The valley opening, the lake holding the silver sky flat as a poured thing, and the world "did not care that she was standing on the edge of it" — and *that not-caring was the most beautiful part.* Because that's Vee's whole ache, isn't it. The pool where nobody wants anything from her. Up here is a bigger pool. The one place all day nothing is being *done* to her, and the book knows it, and it puts his blue shirt in it and lets the world swallow the blue whole. That gutted me clean. That's the book knowing more than it's saying. That's the withholding being a shape.

And then he's looking at her looking. "Not at the valley, at her face in front of the valley." Of course he is. He can't let her have even the mountain unwatched. He lets her *catch* him — that's the tell, the catching-you-do-it is the move, chapter after chapter, the pencil-back-at-the-line thing — and then "gave her the rest of the looking alone," which is a kindness that is also a leash he chose to drop and can pick back up. I clocked it. I felt it as tender anyway. I hate that a little.

The food I could eat off the page — the horseradish climbing into her sinuses, the cherries, the pits. And the pit. Oh, the pit. She spits it and "we take nothing and leave nothing" and her face goes hot with the *no one taught you* voice, and he lays his own pit down beside hers *without looking at her doing it.* That's the whole engine of him in one gesture: he corrects her and covers the correction in the same motion so she never feels corrected, she feels *joined.* By the third cherry she's caught the pit in her own hand "sometime in between," not looking. He's teaching her to portion herself to him and calling it Leave No Trace. The chapter title. Leave no trace. Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over. The book is *saying* it, right in the title, and Vee can't read it and I can and that gap is the thing I keep coming back for.

The russet dress. God, the dress. She wants it, she names the wanting — "that one, I want that one" — and then puts it back on the shelf herself, the black dress in the back of the closet, "you keep walking." And he says "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and *doesn't pick it up again.* And every nerve in me went: he's going to buy her that dress. He filed it. He does the thing where he doesn't reach — same as the pencil, same as the door — and it lands harder than reaching would. He collects her wants and gives them back later as gifts she didn't ask for so she owes him without a transaction ever having happened. I'm almost certain that dress arrives. I'm scared of how good it'll feel when it does.

And then the truck. This is where the chapter earned its heat and where I got genuinely conflicted, in a way I want to sit in. The flannel — his only dry shirt — and then he *gets back out into the rain.* Both. The shirt and the seat. And here's what matters to me: **she runs the whole scene.** She knocks on the glass. She crooks the finger. She unbuttons with her eyes on his the whole way — she doesn't have to look down for the fourth button and she *looks down anyway* so his eyes go with hers, she gives it to him to see. That's Vee taking it. That's her yes, on purpose, the thing she told Cassie she was sure of. The book keeps its promise: every step is hers.

And yet. And *yet.* Look at what he built to get her there. He gave her his only shirt and then stood in the cold with his back turned taking rain for nothing she could see — a display of not-taking so total that the only thing left for her to do was invite him. He didn't make the move. He made a *situation in which she had to make the move,* and stood in the rain being magnificent until she did. He was shivering. He'd been shivering the whole time. And when she feels it — "you're freezing" — and stops the seduction to warm his hand against her belly, that's her tenderness rising, real, and it's also him having spent his own body as the last unanswerable proof of how little he takes. The martyrdom *is* the seduction. He got undressed by getting her to undress. I don't know if the book knows that. Some nights I think it does and it's the whole cold point; some nights I think it just finds him swoony. Tonight I think it knows, because of that shivering hand — because the book let her be the one taking care of *him*, let the current reverse for a second, and that only works if the book sees the whole apparatus.

The cop. That squawk killing her sentence — "the siren took the rest of her sentence with it" — I actually laughed, because the book wouldn't let her *say* the big thing, and that's right, that's the withholding-as-shape again, she doesn't get the declaration and neither do I. And it plants the white pickup back in front of the sorority-net surveillance, the fire lane, the lights. Somebody's watching the watcher. Good.

What I missed: Cassie. That opening — Cassie already up, cap on, "somewhere to be and glad of it," and the look going *past* Vee to Randi and then folding away before it comes back. Vee explaining Cassie out of the smoothie place, "not her thing," and the something that "turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the knife of the whole chapter for me and it's four sentences long. Vee is choosing the bright matched room over the one person with no angle, and doing it *comfortably.* "Nobody ever called that anything but healthy." The book let me watch her lose Cassie without noticing, which is exactly how you lose a Cassie. I'm more scared for that friendship than for anything that happens in the truck.

At 1 a.m. I'd text: *she gave away the person who was actually paying attention and felt GOOD about it and I want to reach into the book.* And also: *the shirt-in-the-rain thing is the most manipulative romantic gesture I've ever fallen for.*

On the pattern-watch I promised myself: the "her body knew / the voice and the heat braided" beat got struck hard again in the truck, third or fourth time now, and I'll admit the "voice says floozy / thighs press once" mechanism is starting to feel like a switch the book flips rather than a thing I discover. First time it opened something in me. Now I see the gears. But the pit-on-the-wax-paper and the not-caring valley and the shivering hand were all new, all earned, so the ledger's still well in the black. Barely watching the repetition instead of feeling it — but the fresh stuff outran it this chapter.
