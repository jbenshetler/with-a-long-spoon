BUT. The shoe store. God, the shoe store. This is what the book can do when it isn't narrating its own seduction technique.

And then the two stores are the whole thesis and I saw it land in *dollars*, which is new. Store one: Vee's world, the sensible tan heel, goes with everything, the debit card that only spends what's behind it. "The shoes were what the dress was owed." That's the horror sentence of the chapter and it's dressed as a lovely one. She's learned to call a thing she can't afford "owed." That's Randi's grammar in Vee's head now.

The foot.
