REACTION

Oh, this one worked on me and then made me sick about the fact that it worked, which is the trick this book keeps landing better than I want it to.

The opening — Cassie's eyes going past Vee to Randi and then folding away before Vee can read them. That's the whole chapter in the first paragraph and I clocked it immediately. Cassie *sees* something and files it, and "I'll see you at the dorm" is a woman stepping back on purpose. And then the part that actually made me put the book down for a second: Vee explaining Cassie's absence to Randi, unprompted — "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one" — doing the little hand-wave at the leggings and the matched hair. That's the sound of a friendship being quietly deprioritized, and the book knows exactly what it's doing having Vee tell herself "it was fine to have your own people" while "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." She's choosing the shiny people over the person who keeps her jokes for her, and she's narrating it to herself as health. I wanted to reach into the page. That's my Cassie.

The Smoothie Bird itself — the shelf. "She had been set up on a shelf." A thing the room could turn and look at. That's the same anxiety as the pool where nobody wants anything from her, inverted: up on the high chair she's all surface, on display, and Randi is the one who makes the display bearable. And I noticed the cardigan-over-the-better-shirt is *back* — "covered her like an apology." That image has done real work before and here it's doing it in a room engineered to make her feel underdressed. I don't mind that repetition; it's a motif with a function, the mother's portioning made visible on her body.

The confession is the meat. Watching Vee hand the whole dinner over to Randi — "how much she wanted to put all of it into Randi's hands" — and Randi doing the thing she does, holding each piece up to the light and handing back the *truer, smaller* version. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Every time Vee has a dignified version ready — *I made an exception* — Randi extracts the real one instead. This is exactly the froyo/glass-chapter move from Randi's side, except now I'm watching the harvest from inside the girl being harvested, and she thinks she's just *telling her friend about a nice date*. The reader is ahead of her and the jacket promised me that and it still stings.

And then — the thing I've been waiting ten chapters for finally cracked open, just a hair. "Ask and then ravish," Randi said, "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That's it. That's the appetite between the *women* coming into the room. Vee's got her hands running down her own thighs picturing Pace on the trail, and Randi drops into her register, matches her pitch, and is "there beside her in it." The book didn't announce it. It just let Randi's voice fall. I felt that more than half the engineered Pace tenderness so far, honestly, because it's the first thing Randi's done that read as *want* and not *management*. And the "dear friend" with the cool damp hand held on the white table in front of Brooke — Vee reads it as being chosen out loud, and it lands as tender, and I believe it cost Randi something. Both women wanting her and one of them wanting the other and nobody at that table able to say so.

Brooke and the silent tall one, though — that's the audit again. They came to look at Vee, the tall one runs "a slow, easy circuit" of her and hands back "nothing but more smile," takes her measure top to bottom and stays warm the whole way. Vee's the specimen. And "We missed you Thursday" — the whole private life of the chapter Vee isn't part of, set down on the table. The net is still tracking. I like that the book won't let the sorority be only decoration.

What I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: I'd text a friend that the scariest line in the book so far is Vee *choosing* to be smaller "to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi." She's learning to disappear behind a face — the exact thing Randi did at the mirror in chapter one. The victim is becoming fluent in the huntress's own survival move and calling it love. That rhyme is the book being smart on purpose.

One honest note: the "her hands went to her thighs / her voice went down after it / she let it too" cluster is the body-knows-before-she-does beat again, and I recognized it this time instead of feeling it all the way. It's earning its keep because it's braided with the Randi voice-drop, but it's the fourth or fifth time and I'm watching the meter.
