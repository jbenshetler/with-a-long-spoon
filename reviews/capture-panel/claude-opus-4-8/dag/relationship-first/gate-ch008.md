REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been waiting for without knowing it. Four chapters of Randi's warmth and Pace's *may I* and the bench and the wine kisses, and now the man isn't in the room and neither is Randi, and I finally get to see Vee when nobody's building anything on her. That's the chapter I trust. And it's Cassie — thank god it's Cassie.

The thing that got me is the water. That whole submerged paragraph, the room switching off, the scull her hands find before she asks them to, "she had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." That's the first time this book has let Vee have something that isn't being-looked-at. Everything else in her life is an entrance — she literally walks the bleachers "like an entrance," puts her face on, chin level — and the pool is the one place the face comes off and nobody wants anything from her. And she *grieves* a little down there. That's the whole book in one word. She goes to the one place she's ungrabbed and mourns. I don't even fully know what she's mourning and I don't want it spelled out, and blessedly it isn't.

And Cassie. Cassie is the person I've been starving for in this book. "They will never know the love of a woman." "Tossers." The splash that barely gets her — "outrage out of all proportion to the actual amount of water" — she reads that Vee came up from the bottom "with her face on wrong" and she doesn't ask, because asking is for people who want the sad version to get bigger, she just *shoots water at her.* That is love. That is a person who knows Vee and wants nothing. She could have pitched it up into the bleachers, she had the lungs, and she kept it for Vee. God. After chapters of Randi's warmth that I keep clocking as *aimed* — the hand on the arm, the wattage, "love you, girl" said walking backward — Cassie's affection has no angle to it and it made my chest hurt by contrast.

Because here's what I'm sitting with, and what I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: the book has told me on the jacket that Vee is being played, that Randi and Pace chose her like a dish off a menu — I read the pointing game, I watched Randi shop the quad, I know. So every warm thing Randi does now I read twice. And this chapter, by pulling both of them out of the room, quietly shows me the difference between warmth that's a plan and warmth that's just a person. Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" from a couple chapters back — the whole flat word, the wrong-length goodbye — is sitting under this pool scene like a stone. Cassie sees it coming. Cassie is watching her friend get taken and can't say it in a way Vee will hear, so she keeps a mouthful of pool for her instead.

What I love craft-wise, though I'm trying not to review: the body-catcalls are handled without a single lingering beat. "You won't need floaties with those." The bleacher-laugh that's really guys applauding each other. Vee's long blink, half her mouth. It doesn't wallow. And then the pool washes it off — you can't grieve and fight your best friend for a mouthful of pool at the same time, the splashing won, it always did.

This is the chapter that made me believe the book knows what it's doing to Vee. I'm scared for her in a good way now. And I want the next one — but I notice what I want is *more Cassie*, more Vee-without-them, not more bench.
