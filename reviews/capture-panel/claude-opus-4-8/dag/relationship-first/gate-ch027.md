REACTION

Oh, this is the one. This is the chapter I've been waiting for since I started writing "the trap's got her foot in it too" in my own notes and half-not-believing it. The whole book has been Randi running the machine, and tonight the book quietly, without one word of narration telling me it's happening, lets me watch the machine run *her*.

Look at the inventory of firsts. She stays down after — she never stays down; the chill always drives her off him and into the blanket with her back turned, and I have that logged twice. She asks *is there anything to eat in this house* — a thing my notes literally do not have her ever doing. She gets up and goes to his drawer, second one down, no hunting, and pulls on his undershirt, which means she knows exactly where his shirts live, which means she's done this in her head. And then she *eats*. All of it. The couscous to a line he could draw with a ruler. And I nearly stood up, because three chapters ago (Portion) she drew one single line through the top of the sauce with the ladle and left the whole bowl to skin over while she made her point, and he covered it with a plate and put it in the fridge for her. That bowl was her withholding. This plate, scraped clean, is her surrender, and the book had the patience to make me wait three chapters for the rhyme and then not say a word about it. That's the withholding I keep saying is the only thing outrunning the repetition — here it paid me back.

And the crack. God, the crack. "I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come. She got past me before I could help it." That's the huntress admitting the mark got inside her guard. And then Pace does the thing — the room going brighter, somebody found the lamp, she's the most alive person standing there and never knows it — and Randi goes *still*. Not the loose after-stillness. The kind you have to hold. Her shoulders tightening under his shirt. "Yeah," she said, when she'd been quiet a beat too long. "She is." That beat too long is the whole book. She's jealous, and I can't even tell you jealous of whom — of Vee for having Pace, of Pace for having Vee, of both of them for the thing they're falling into that she engineered and can't get inside of. And she covers it exactly the way she always covers, by turning it into sex, taking him "so soon," pulling the shirt off and describing Vee's body *with her own body*, dragging herself down his chest to show him — "it's criminal, Pace." She's using Vee's body as the thing that turns her on out loud in his kitchen, and Pace clocks it — "not the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi, who keeps a hand on every ounce of herself, wanting something out loud and not able to stop it." So he sees it too. They both see it and neither says it. The pencil-back-at-the-line, except this time it's *her* line and *she's* the one who can't hold it.

"It was so cute. She was so embarrassed. That was kind of hot." That's Randi eroticizing Vee's shame to the man they're both handling. The jacket's been promising me Randi all along and this is the closest the book's come to putting it on the table without letting anyone name it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she ate the whole plate. do you understand. she wouldn't touch the bowl and now she ate the whole plate and asked for it. the shark is in love and she doesn't have a word for it either.*

The one thing that made me sigh: I had to sit through the yeti-shoe story again, told to Pace, when I *just* lived it in the last chapter from Vee's side. The canoe, the snow-tire sole, the older couple watching — I got all of it in 26 and got it again here. I understand why it's here (it's Randi giving him the tender version, and "that was kind of hot" is doing work), but for a page I was being recapped a scene still warm in my hands. That's my exact fatigue. The chapter earned it back by the time she went still, but it spent a little of my patience getting there.

Still no panties. "Trade" is still sitting unaddressed and I'm still watching for it. But I don't mind waiting tonight, because I got something better.
