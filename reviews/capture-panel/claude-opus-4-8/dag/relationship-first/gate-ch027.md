REACTION

Oh, this is the one I came to this book for. Two people under a quilt, tea gone cold on purpose, talking — and I couldn't have put it down. After a run of chapters that had started narrating their own machinery at me, this chapter just let two people be in a room, and it turns out the book still can.

The Daphne reveal gutted me, and it gutted me in the exact way I've been begging for: sideways, held, unsaid. Nineteen and fifteen. A *tutor*, assigned to him. Two years. And he hands it to her as the best two years he'd had, as *luck* — "It would have been stranger if she'd stayed" — and Vee does the arithmetic in her stomach before it's even a thought, and the clean ugly grown-up word comes up under her tongue and she *holds it down*, because it isn't hers to say. That's the whole novel in one gesture. The man who runs ask-wait-choose on Vee learned it in a room where a grown woman ran it on a fourteen-year-old who'd been shipped off from home too early. He's not a technique. He's a reenactment. The coldness I've been writing down about him for twenty chapters just turned into the saddest thing in the book, and the book didn't tell me to feel that — it let Vee feel it with her palm flat on his chest and looked away. That's the book knowing more than it's saying, finally, out loud and still withheld at the same time.

And it gave me the *scar* again and took it away again — "Not tonight," her palm turned under his over the old seam. God, I want that one. Two deferrals stacked: the scar, and the word Vee won't say. I'll turn pages for both.

The math monologue I could feel the book leaning on a little — Gabriel's horn, the coastline, Newton's three sentences, "the language the world is written in." One degree too pleased with itself, that last line. But it earned its place, because it's the boy who got his desk carried into the hall for loving the thing too much, and now he gets to say the thing is beautiful to a girl who'll listen. "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" — she said the true sentence and he answered like she'd found a place he hadn't offered. That's the couch conversation I stay up for.

What I'm watching, cold-eyed: Randi is *gone* from this chapter, completely, and that's not an accident. Last chapter she went still and tightened when Pace described what Vee does to a room — she's curdling, or falling, or both — and now the book builds the deepest Pace-and-Vee beat it's given me yet with Randi nowhere in it. The plan is turning real on the two who made it, just like the jacket promised, and it's turning real by way of Randi being edged out of the frame she built. And the Daphne shape rhymes with Randi's — the older one steering the younger. Somebody in this book is fifteen again and doesn't know it. I'd text a friend: *he called being groomed the luckiest two years of his life and she heard it and let him keep it — I don't know if I want to hug him or run.*
