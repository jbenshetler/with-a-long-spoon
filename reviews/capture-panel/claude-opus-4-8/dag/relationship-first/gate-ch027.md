REACTION

Oh, this is the one. This is the chapter I've been staying for without knowing it was coming. Forty pages of two people under a quilt talking, tea gone cold on purpose because they'd rather keep going — that's my whole shelf, that's the kitchen conversation I came here for, and the book finally just *let them talk* with nobody to report it to afterward. No smoothie lunch waiting to strip-mine it, no Randi in a doorway. For once the room only had the two of them in it and I believe every minute of it moved *them* and not the plot.

And it gave me Pace. Undiluted, and for the first time not in a way that made me colder. The desk in the hall — they took the thing he loved and made it the punishment, and Vee *says* exactly that back to him and he answers like she put her hand on a place he hadn't offered. That got me clean. The saffron old enough to vote, the whole Ohio casserole aria, "cumin on accident" — she's *funny* in this chapter, they're funny together, and I laughed with them, which is different from being handed a scene where a character is described as laughing. The math stuff could've been a lecture and mostly wasn't: the shape you can fill with a cup of paint but never paint the outside of, the coastline that runs to infinity if you've got the patience, the leaf and the stars doing the one thing. "Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it." I'd text a friend that line at 1 a.m., I did in my head.

But here's what's sitting in my stomach. Daphne. Nineteen-year-old assigned *tutor* to a fifteen-year-old, two years, and he hands it to me in that flat careful full-glass voice and calls it the best two years he'd had and names it *luck*. And the book knows. Vee does the arithmetic she doesn't want to do, the clean ugly grown-up word forms under her tongue and she holds it down because it isn't hers to say — and neither of us says it, and that's the most honest the book has been. Because look what it just did to everything: the ask-wait-leave-the-door-open, the courtly patience, the whole seduction machine he runs on Vee and Randi runs on Vee — *he learned it there*. He was the one it was run on. The withholding I've been circling for twenty-seven chapters just got a source, and it's a wound, and he can't see it as one. "That is approximately what happened." God.

And the scar is *Daphne*. Has to be. "Not tonight" — a door with a time on it, not a wall, and she turns her palm up under his over the old seam and that's the whole asking and the whole answer. I've wanted that scar explained since chapter sixteen and the book still won't, but this time the withholding felt like a shape and not a stall, because it finally told me *where* it lives.

The one thing that made me twitch: "That's how you are with me… It's the lift. It's the same face." Vee narrating his technique back to him out loud — the book explaining its own man to me again, the thing I flagged last time. But it's a small tax and honestly her saying it *to* him lands warmer than the narrator saying it *about* him, so I let it go. "Peter," once, into the dark, to keep the word. That's the chapter.
