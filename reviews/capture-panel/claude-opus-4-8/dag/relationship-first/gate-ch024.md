REACTION

Oh, this one got me in the ribs in a way I wasn't braced for. After chapter 23 — Randi with her back turned, the untouched bowl, the eyes shut and no *tell me*, the crack I'd been circling for twenty chapters finally showing — I came into 24 leaning toward Randi's spine in the dark, wanting the reckoning. And the book just... walked me the other way down the hall, into the sun porch, into the gold light and the dress in the window. And I let it, because it was funny. It was actually *funny*, and this book has almost never let itself be funny.

The whole sex-while-saving-the-dress relay — "there's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," him twitching every time she says it, her laughing so hard her leg slides off his hip, the two long stripes down his flanks because he's got his arms full of silk and can do nothing about it, and then him taking her fingers into his mouth to check they're clean with his eyes on hers — that's the first time these two have felt like *people having a good time* instead of a seduction being executed to spec. It's the first scene where his exactness turns into play instead of control. I laughed and I was turned on at the same time, which is the thing I actually came to this genre for and had almost stopped expecting from this book. The heat worked precisely because it wasn't machined. Nobody ran the pencil. Nobody did the inch-and-stop. It was clumsy and joined and warm and I believed every second of it.

And then. And then the ending. She lies there getting away with something and puts the dress on *in her head* and walks into the induction — the high room, the girls in department-store navy, and her coming in late on purpose in a thing that looks flown in from Milan, and letting the room turn, and the looking not costing her a thing: "It just felt like what she was for." And there it is. The whole warm chapter is the sugar around exactly the pill the jacket promised. The dress isn't a gift, it's an instrument, and it points *at the sorority* — Brooke, the tall one, "we missed you Thursday." Randi's world. The thing I keep waiting to come home. He made her a costume for a room she doesn't know is a trap, and she's already daydreaming herself into it, wanting to be looked at, and calling the wanting *what she's for*. Cold as anything I've read here, served under the warmest chapter in the book. I noticed. I'm meant to, I think — but I noticed on my own before the daydream spelled it out, and then the daydream spelled it out, which is the tic I keep flagging: the book making sure I got the thing I already got.

The *Floozy* flare is here again too, of course, one more strike on the wallpaper. But it did something a little new this time — she stayed, made herself look, and the looking stopped being shame *while I watched*, and then she took her own underwear off to give the room the clean line. Her hand on her own waistband. That's the agency the whole book lives or dies on, and it's still working on me: she's the one taking the edge away, she's the one making herself the thing to be looked at. Horror and beauty in one gesture, exactly as advertised, and I still can't fully decide which one the book loves more, which is the only reason I'm still here.

What I miss: Randi's turned back. I wanted 24 to be the morning after 23, the two of them unable to look at what cracked. Instead I got a lovely afternoon that pretends 23 didn't happen. Charming — but it's a retreat, and I feel the plot idling one beat so the trap can dress its girl before spring.
