Okay so this is the morning-after chapter, the come-down and the re-tooling, and I read it two ways at once the whole way through and I think the book wants me to. The Edna Mode bit genuinely got me. The shoe-whore banter, "dinner in Paris," the closet going bone to camel to oatmeal and ending on that one oxblood note. I would have walked into traffic for the woman who said dinner in Paris about me, too. That line's alive.

And under it: this is a grooming scene, start to finish, and the book knows it, and that's the whole horror sitting in the pretty. Vee comes to Randi at the lowest point of her semester and Randi *tunes her.* Reframes the disaster into proof of love — "that's not the disaster, honey, that's the only thing that matters" — hands her marching orders (not today, tomorrow night; don't go in sorry, go in sure; he won't text, don't read it as a door closing), dresses her in borrowed shoes and a stranger's silk, and sends her back to the man they picked her for weeks before she was anything. It's not hiding the mechanism. She's being handled and she's calling it the best friend she's ever had, and both are true, and that's the sick, good thing.

The under-the-skirt shoe fitting — Randi kneeling where Vee can't see, the long skirt over all of it, cool hands somewhere past the hem, the thumb pressed once into the hollow behind her knee, the breath Vee hoped wasn't out loud. That's Randi turning the heat up while wearing the friend-costume, and Vee not knowing which way is up. Charged, withheld, exactly the kind I like. And then the kiss at the door and *she hadn't braced. It was only what they did.* That's the assimilation finishing. The thing that used to make her scan the dark windows is now just background weather.

The freckle detail is the best small thing in the chapter — Randi's cocoa scatter across the nose she never once thinks about, right against Vee spending ten minutes at the sink burying her own cinnamon. Same face, opposite relationship to it. That's shame rendered as a physical fact, and the book earned it without saying the word.
