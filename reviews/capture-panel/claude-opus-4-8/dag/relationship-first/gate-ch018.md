REACTION

Oh, I needed this after seventeen. Seventeen wrung me out — that whole slow drowning on the box, the wet satin, the closed eyes — and coming down into Cassie's room with the highlighter and the "Well?" was like surfacing. This is the register I came to the book for: two girls on a bed, one telling and one working the puzzle, the afternoon going gold on the floor. I'd have stayed in this room forty pages.

And Cassie earns her keep here more than she has in a while. "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?" made me laugh out loud — because it's *true*, it's the thing I've been half-thinking, and it takes the air out of the reverence for a second in exactly the way a real friend does. The "are you sure he's not gay" bit is the same instinct: Cassie is the only one in this book who will say the mundane, deflating, sane thing about Pace, and the book lets her. I love that it doesn't punish her for it. And then Vee's rebuttal — patting her own ass "brisk and clinical, like she was indicating it on a diagram," and then the real reveal, *I was grinding on him* — that's the best beat in the chapter, because it flips the whole power story of seventeen. She's not just the drowning girl on the box. She knows she nearly toppled him and she's *delighted*. That's new and I like her more for it.

But. Here's the honest thing at 1 a.m. This chapter is almost entirely a re-telling of the chapter I just read. I was *in* the sun porch. I felt the wet satin, the withdrawal-and-pencil rhythm, the eyes-shut terror, the grin when she opened them. And now I'm being walked back through all of it a second time, from a step further away, with Cassie reacting the way I already reacted. The book keeps doing this — the debrief-to-Cassie is now its own fixed furniture, same as the deadbolt and the Steely Dan and "her body knew before she did." Thirteen was this. Fifteen was the friends' version of it. Now eighteen. I clocked it in my notes as the thing that would eventually decide whether I stay, and I felt it tonight for the first time as *impatience* rather than pleasure. The individual beats are warm; the structure is a loop, and I'm starting to see the loop.

What saves it — barely — is that the retelling isn't identical. The shirt callback lands harder in the recap ("You wore it to breakfast on Tuesday" / "It's a comfortable shirt") than it did in the moment, because Cassie names the self-deception Vee can't. And the heat detail (turned-up thermostat, "he thought of it then") is the one thing the recap sharpens instead of dulls. That's the tell of what this book is good at and what it's coasting on: it's good at the small planned tenderness, and it's coasting on making me watch Vee discover, out loud, to a friend, the thing I already watched her discover on her own.

I notice too that Cassie's warmth is starting to feel like a function. "Oh, Vee." The hand on the knee, once, and taken back. She's still my favorite, still the floor — but tonight I felt the book *using* her as the register that certifies Pace is good, and I trust her less as a result, or rather I trust the book's use of her less. My proxy is being enlisted as a character witness for the man I don't trust, and nobody in the room is scared. I'm still scared. Where's Randi in all this — the whole apparatus, the report Pace pockets — none of that pressure is anywhere near this cozy bed, and that absence is either the point (dread by omission) or the book relaxing when I need it not to.
