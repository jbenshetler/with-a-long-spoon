REACTION

Okay, so this is the second time I've watched Vee come home and hand the whole afternoon to Cassie across the beds, and I clocked that before I clocked anything else — chapter 13 was the flannel debrief, this is the dress debrief, same room, same highlighter-down, same "Well?", same Cassie-goes-still-in-the-paying-attention-way. I know why the book keeps doing it. Cassie is the only clean water in the story and the debrief is where Vee gets to be *happy out loud* to someone who has no angle. But I felt myself settle in for the retread, and settling in is not the same as being grabbed, and I want to be honest that the previous chapter — the box, the pins, the wet satin she couldn't hide, the opening-her-eyes-to-find-him-grinning-up — did to me everything this one is only *reporting*. This chapter lives downstream of a chapter that already spent all the charge. So a lot of it is Vee narrating scenes I just read, and I don't need the recap; I lived it forty pages ago.

BUT. Two things saved it and one of them saved it hard. The first: this is finally, actually, the women alone talking, which I've been waiting the whole book for — and the tell is what *Cassie* does with it, not what Vee does. Cassie's the one working the puzzle. "He sews." "He's a bodybuilder who makes dresses." And then — bless her — "Vee. Are you sure he's not gay?" That's the realest line in the chapter, because it's the only moment someone looks at this man's behavior from the outside and finds it *strange* instead of swooning at it. Cassie is doing, cross-legged on a dorm bed with a highlighter, the exact thing I keep begging the book to do: notice that a grad student turning up his thermostat hours early and building a woman a silk gown after one kiss is not normal, it is *engineered*, it is a man who has done this before. She reads it as maybe-gay because she doesn't have the darker frame. I have the darker frame. The book let her be suspicious and then let the suspicion resolve into a dick joke, and I laughed, and then I felt the little cold thing in my stomach that Vee doesn't feel.

The second thing: "*I* was grinding on *him*." That's the chapter. That's the one live wire. Because it flips the whole helpless-girl-on-a-box read — Vee's not just reporting being seduced, she's bragging about being the aggressor, she found her power on that platform and she's giddy with it, and the giddiness is *hers*, not something Randi handed her or Pace conducted. When she patted her own ass "grave as a witness" and then admitted the truth with her whole face going wicked — that's Vee moving herself, not being moved, and it's the thing I stay in this book for. I love her most exactly there.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the roommate literally asked if he's gay and I need everyone to understand she is the only person in this book with working eyes.* And also: *why does no one find it ALARMING that he pre-heated the house.* The book keeps serving "he thought of you hours before" as pure melt and I keep tasting the planning under it. He knew she'd be half-naked and cold and he removed the one honest signal — her own goosebumps, her own discomfort — before she arrived. That's not care. That's control dressed as care, and the tell is that he did it *silently*, so she'd discover it as a gift instead of a decision made about her. The jacket told me weeks ago: a secret choice made about her. The thermostat is that, small.

The repetition I'm watching: "he left me a door," "he asked — he didn't use the word but yes," the shirt-with-no-tag, the turned-up heat, Cassie's soft "Oh, Vee." All struck before. First time each of these gutted me. Now I recognize them. The book is running low on ways to say *he waits, he asks, he leaves the last inch to her* — that's four chapters of the same chord, and this debrief chapter added no new information, only new tone. That's the thing that will eventually walk me out.

But not tonight. The grinding confession and Cassie's suspicion bought it back. I want the dress-ready call, and I want to see this new bold Vee out in the world, and I'm bracing — still, always — for what happens to Cassie, who is now on record as the one who noticed.
