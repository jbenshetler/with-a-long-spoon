REACTION

Oh, this one caught me off guard by being small. After twenty chapters of the machine humming I got a girl coming home with no bag and no underwear and a smile that won't come down, and the whole chapter is just her and Cassie and a bowl of cereal gone brown. I love that the book gave me a quiet morning. I've stayed a long time with people doing very little and this is the first time in a while it trusted me to sit in a room where nothing happens except a zipper going halfway down and back up.

But here's the thing that actually landed, the thing I've been waiting for without knowing I was waiting for it: the withholding came home. All this time I've watched Pace hand Randi the curated Vee, pocket the true thing, give back a machined version — and here's Vee doing the exact same move to Cassie. "The old easy words she'd always handed Cassie about a man." She lifts the middle out clean, tells herself it's a kindness, keeps the part that's only her body naming what it wants. She's editing the report now. She's become one of them and doesn't feel it. Nobody in this book gets to be the clean water for long, and watching Vee start to silt her own oldest friendship — that's the cruelest, best thing the chapter does, and it did it without anyone touching anyone.

And Cassie — God, Cassie. "Cassie listened with her chin on her knees and did not pull at the seam. Whether she felt it there, Vee couldn't have said." She feels it. She always feels it. She's the only one who ever has, and now she's being handed a story with a hole ladled out of it and she's choosing, again, not to reach in. My dread for her is worse now, not because the book's going to *do* something to her but because Vee's already doing it — the friendship's going quietly one-directional and Vee thinks she needs to get her brain back from her boyfriend.

The panties, though. I was so sure they were going to Randi, that the centered spot was the report. And the chapter tells me she folded them into *his* palm — so that's still hanging, the actual handoff, the shoulders-going-private moment, whether it becomes Randi's. Deferred, not answered. Good. The toenails I loved: the mother's word coming and Vee not minding it this morning. That's real ground gained. She's genuinely happy and it's the solo, ridiculous, bare-faced-in-his-mirror-laughing kind, and I believed every inch of it.

What still nicks me is the same nick: "She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." I felt the ladle already. I didn't need the sentence after it explaining the ladle. The book keeps doing the move and then narrating the move, and I recognize the narration before I feel it now. But this chapter's short enough and true enough that it bought itself back.
