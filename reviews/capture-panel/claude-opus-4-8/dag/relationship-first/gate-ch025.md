REACTION

Okay, the shoe store. That's the chapter I came to this genre for and then got taught to distrust in the same forty pages, which is a nasty little trick and I mostly loved it.

The banter is real — the yeti canoe-shoe, the pink rhinestone platforms Randi wears down the aisle at her "usual altitude," the clearance flat one brisk walk from becoming a sandal. Vee being the funniest woman in Virginia, folding Randi over the bench with the laugh that costs her something. Two people doing very little in a store with steel racks. I'd have stayed there all night. It's the friendship-novel thing, and the book can *do* it when it wants to.

But I clocked the whole time what the scene actually is. It's a recruitment. Randi walks her off the sensible tan shoe — the one that goes with everything, that she'd actually wear again, that the debit card can cover — and into the brass-and-lacquer hush where the price is written tiny on the sole and starts past where Vee's "most" ends. And Vee puts down the credit card with *nothing under it.* "She did not have the next part." That's the moment the book finally made me flinch — not sex, *money.* The class thing came home. Randi buys loafers for several times what Vee is white-knuckling and never looks at the number, and Vee carries her one box out clutched to her chest like it'll run off. That gap is the con made visible, and it's crueler than any bedroom scene because Vee's going into debt to cosplay belonging in a world she used to watch from outside. That landed.

The toe touch on the fitting bench — Randi down on one knee, thumb across the frosted-plum nail, and Vee's floor tips, her hand shoots to Randi's shoulder. That's the huntress, gentle as a scalpel. Good. Simmer, and I felt it.

What I'm tired of: the book annotating itself again. "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you" — Randi naming the thing and handing it back clean, and Vee's throat doing "the thing again." I've watched this exact move four times now; the first time it gutted me and now I just recognize the choreography. And the Simpson's paradox lecture threaded through the whole scene — same numbers, nobody touched one of them, aggregate reverses — is the jacket's thesis rendered as a chalkboard, and when the red dot landed I sighed a little. Oblique enough that *I* get to make the connection, so it's better than the pencil-narration, but the book is still showing me its own footnotes.

Cassie, though. Cassie doesn't care about the dress, has worn the same fleece two winters, spent the whole lecture watching what the dress *did to her friend* — "the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." She read the made bed before Vee sat down and let Randi have the reveal anyway. She's still the clean water and I'm still braced for what the book does to her.
