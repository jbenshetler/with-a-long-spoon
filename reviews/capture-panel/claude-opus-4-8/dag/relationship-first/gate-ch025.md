REACTION

Oh, this one was a breather, and I needed it — a whole chapter with everyone's clothes on and it still hummed. The shape of it is the thing: Vee walking in "fit to burst," dropping into the seat they left open for her, two-thirds back, one girl on each side. That staged seat. I sat up at that. Both of them got there early and left the middle for her, and the book knows exactly what it's drawing — a girl held between the two people who planned her, happy in the middle, on time now for nothing.

And then the lecture. Simpson's paradox. I actually laughed out loud, the tired 1 a.m. laugh, because the book has decided to just *hand* me its thesis dressed as a stats problem — same numbers, nobody touched one of them, and the answer flips when you put the two seasons together. Each step of it Vee gives freely; aggregate the steps and it's a different thing entirely. "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them." That is the jacket's *every yes was freely given, that was the problem* set to baseball. Part of me loved the nerve of it. And part of me went — there it is again, the book narrating its own move, the pencil-and-withdrawal tic in a new costume. I've flagged this. It's clever and I clocked it as clever, which is not the same as it getting under me. The margin thing from my notes. But I'll give it this: at least the technique-narration is *doing* something here, it's structural, it's the whole horror in miniature, not just a sentence explaining the sentence before it.

What actually got me was Cassie. Vee gives the lecture forty honest seconds, comes up out of the numbers, and realizes Cassie never went *down* into them at all — Cassie's been watching what the dress did to her friend the whole time, because the friend is the only part of any of this Cassie has ever been able to see. God. That's the clean water again. Cassie doesn't care about the dress, has worn the same fleece two winters, never cared what's on anyone's feet. And right there next to her is Randi, who's already three moves into Saturday and shoe-shopping and heel heights Vee didn't know to need. The two ways of loving her, side by side, and the book lets me feel which one is real without a single word of comment. That's the good withholding. I trust the book most in exactly that beat.

The "you lucky slut" landing lower than Randi meant it — Vee's body keeping the night's account, the good soreness, sure the whole room could read it off her — that worked on me, quiet as it was. And the ending: the dress three blocks back, zipped in its bag, shown to not one living soul. That's the book being smart about heat, holding the reveal. I want the induction now, badly, the room turning. That's a real pull and it's the withholding doing it, not the plot.

What I'm sitting with: the shoe date. Saturday, you and me, we don't quit till the shoes are as right as the dress, don't you dare tell me no. That's Randi walking her further in, and I felt the warm little current of it and also the net. And nobody has told this girl a true thing. Still. Cassie's the only working eyes and she picked up her highlighter and let it stand.
