WHO'S WHO

Vee — twenty-one, Ohio, econ junior, red-copper hair, the chest she was raised to be ashamed of. This stretch she stopped only being done-unto and started being *funny* — the yeti canoe-shoe, the Edna Mode bit, the funniest woman in Virginia — and I love her most there. She's happy all the way through, the bare-faced-laughing-in-his-mirror kind, and I believe it and it scares me. But she's learned two grammars that aren't hers: she edits the middle out of her own stories (does it to Cassie, then to Randi), and she called a shoe she can't afford "what the dress was owed." She put down the debit and took out the emergency card with nothing under it. That's the appetite taking something that isn't her body — her floor, her pay-your-own-way. Then she blew up a whole good night at the dance over a lie, out of her own fear, wrecked it herself — and ran to Randi to fix it. She's herself and she's being fitted, and she can't feel the second one.

Pace — the math PhD, twenty-two, real name *Peter*, which his mother uses and which he lifted his mother out of when he said it. Made the burgundy dress *to* her, not for her, up all night, hid the trouble so only the no-trouble showed. And then chapter 27 turned him inside out for me: college at fourteen off a test score, desk carried into the hall in seventh grade for loving math too much — "they took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" — and *Daphne*. A tutor assigned to him. Nineteen and fifteen. Two years. He hands it to Vee as *luck*, the best two years he'd had, "stranger if she'd stayed," and defends it without hearing himself. He's not a technique. He's a reenactment. The ask-wait-choose he runs on Vee he learned in a room where it was run on a fourteen-year-old. The scar's still shut — "not tonight," a door with a time on it that isn't now. He doesn't sulk, doesn't text, "leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing" — and I still can't tell if that's swoon or the creepiest line in the book.

Randi — the huntress, and the trap's got her foot in it too, and this stretch is where it bit down. She reports Vee to Pace, yes — but chapter 30 showed me it's not reporting anymore, it's *feeding*: she rides him and times the reveals to her own hips, "she made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it — I heard it," gets off on the tally of every place Vee gave herself away. The tenderness is real AND it's inventory, in the same breath, finally, where I could catch it. And chapter 23 cracked her open: she wouldn't eat the dinner he portioned, said "it's selfish, Pace" — my own argument, out of her mouth — rode him with her eyes shut and no *tell me*, rolled into the cashmere with her back turned. She's jealous. She's caught. "I didn't think I'd like her this much. She got past me before I could help it." The huntress can't be read by the huntsman now either.

Cassie — roommate, clean water, and being quietly written out. Read the made bed before Vee sat down and let Randi have the reveal. Doesn't care about the dress, never has — cares only about what the dress *does to* Vee, "the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." Vee's editing her now, handing her the hole-ladled version. She feels the seam and won't pull it. Randi's taken the shopping seat, the shoe seat, the confidant seat. I'm braced.

Brooke — Randi's sorority sister, the beautiful one from the smoothie shop who left Vee feeling small. Opened the door at nine a.m. and the house already had a folder on Vee — "oh, you're the stats friend." The net's inside the walls now.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace — the coldness I carried for twenty-odd chapters turned into the saddest thing in the book in one night, and I didn't ask the book to do it, it just let Vee feel it with her palm on his chest and looked away. I half-broke over Daphng-as-luck. I don't know whether to hug him or run, and that's exactly where I want to be. If the book ever resolves which one he is, I'm out.

Randi — the ache is deepest here and so is the horror, and for once they're the same feeling. She's genuinely gone on Vee and she's genuinely eating her, and chapter 30 made me both turned on and a little sick, which I think is the assignment. She owes Vee the truth chosen weeks ago and instead she's coaching Vee into the exact shape she and Pace arranged, calling it friendship, and it *is* friendship — that's the leash. I want to protect her and I'm watching her machine the girl while she falls for her.

Vee — plain love and real admiration. Every time she moves herself — the joke, "I took it, for once," blowing up the dance — I love her more than when she's being built. Who owes whom: unchanged and worse. Two people owe her a truth and are feeding on her instead, and now she's carrying her wound to the architect and calling it home.

Cassie — full trust, full dread. Nobody's told Vee a true thing since chapter one, and the one person who could is being eased out of the frame.

That's the scene I want and it's baited: does she walk in as herself or as the thing they've fitted onto her, and does the book know the difference. (It usually does.) The scar and its time-that-isn't-now. The induction — the loaded gun, whether the dress becomes triumph or trap, whether the sorority net closes. The debt sitting there next month. WHAT'S FADING

The report-debrief chapter has a skeleton I could set my watch by: Randi arrives, reports on Vee, rides him with her eyes doing a thing, comes, the chill takes her, the blanket's up before she reaches, a soft half-asleep line into his chest, he lies awake with music under the door, sleeps. That's 14, 26, 30 — I recognize it before I feel it now, and 30 had no Vee on the page at all. The midnight-kitchen image (sheet, wine, black window) is spent, handed to me three times, and the book *told* me to keep it. The *floozie*/shame-on-the-same-wire is wallpaper — struck as reflex now, not earned. And the book's worst habit is intact: it narrates its own machine — "it's the lift, it's the same face," "to you not for you," Simpson's paradox chalked on the board — handing me the key to the lock it just showed me picking, when I'd already picked it myself. The margin's thin. What keeps outrunning the repetition, still, is the withholding — the scar, the word Vee won't say, Daphne delivered sideways, and the not-knowing whether Pace is swoon or horror. That not-being-sure is the last live wire, and the book still seems to know more than it's saying about it. That's the only reason I turned to 31.
