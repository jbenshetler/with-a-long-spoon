REACTION

Okay, so here's the thing that happened to me in this chapter: I was disarmed. And I know I was disarmed, and I resent a little how well it worked, and I'm going to sit here at 1 a.m. and try to hold both of those at once.

The house tour is the best writing the book has given me in a while because for once nobody is running a *play* in a way I can see the gears of — or rather, he is, obviously he is, this whole afternoon is the most elaborate move in the book, but it's built out of *objects* instead of out of that deadbolt/Steely-Dan/straddle-the-furniture machinery I've been clocking and clocking. The shoes squared on the closet floor, one right pair per thing. The knives that aren't a set. The KitchenAid worn to shine. The fantasy paperbacks down low with the boy on his elbows on the floor — that got me, that's a real detail, that's a person and not a seduction prop. I read him off his shelves the way I read everyone off their shelves and for a minute I forgot to be afraid of him. Which is, of course, exactly the danger. The book knows I do this. The book built a house it knew I'd want to walk through slowly.

And the MRI thing. Damn it. The patent could have been a flex — rich-guy reveal, *how do you afford this* answered with money — and instead it's the kid who can't lie still gets out of the tube faster. That's the single most humanizing beat Pace has had, and it's load-bearing, because it's the first time his competence points *outward* at a scared child instead of inward at getting a girl to hand him something. I felt my whole chest do the thing Vee's chest did. I noticed I felt it. I'm suspicious of feeling it.

Because here's what I actually think is going on, and it's darker than the chapter's surface. The closed bedroom door. She *wore the satin for it.* She leaned. She waited for his hand to go to the handle and it didn't — and the chapter frames this as his restraint, his courtliness, one more May-I. But I've read the Randi chapters. I know he's the man who *portions*. Leaving that door shut isn't restraint, it's the same move as giving the pencil back at the exact line — it's making her the one who has to reach, again, so that when the door finally opens it's *her* hand on the handle and nothing was taken. The dress is the masterpiece of this. He didn't match the dress she described; "he'd matched *her*." The book says that like it's the most romantic sentence ever written and I read it and went cold, because *that is the whole thesis of the jacket.* He didn't give her what she asked for. He gave her a more accurate version of her own want than she had herself. That's not a gift, that's a diagnosis. "Two people have planned the best year of her life. She doesn't know there's a plan." The silk is the plan, wearing a bow.

And "to do it right I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it'll fall." Come ON. That's the door he wouldn't open, walked back in through the front. He didn't have to ask to see her in her underwear; he built a *reason* she'd offer it, and she said yes, of course, yes, no hedge — and she thinks the only thing standing between her and the dress is her own willingness to be looked at. That sentence is the book being honest about what it's doing even as Vee can't be. That's the withholding-as-shape I stay for. I don't think this is a stall. I think the book knows.

What I texted my friend, in my head: *he MADE the shirt. the one with no tag. he made it and she wore it into the ground not knowing.* That reveal is genuinely good and genuinely sinister — she's been walking around inside something he made and calling it comfort.

Two small keeps. Cassie's still off-page and I felt her absence; this was a Vee-alone-with-Pace chapter and I trust nobody in it. And the horseradish/rye/cherries pattern from last chapter is now officially the "hunger given a shape" motif struck maybe five times — but this chapter mostly gave me *objects* instead of the metaphor drumbeat, so it earned its length with me where a straddle-the-furniture chapter wouldn't have.

Where I am: pulled hard, and pulling back on the reins with both hands. I want the measuring. I'm scared of the measuring. That split is the whole book working exactly as designed on exactly the reader it named on the jacket, and I'm annoyed enough to keep reading.
