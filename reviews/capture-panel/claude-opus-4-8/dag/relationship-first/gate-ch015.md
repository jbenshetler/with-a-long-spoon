REACTION

Oh, this is the chapter I've been waiting for and it's the one that finally tipped my hand about where I stand — not on Vee, on the *book*.

Because here's what happened tonight: I got three girls at a dinner table in the gold light and no man in the room, and I leaned all the way in, because this is my thing, this is the exact texture I came for — Kayla who lets nothing finish and Meg who lets every sentence land, and Vee laughing with her whole body between them. And it's good. Meg is *so* good. "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think. He's different." And then — this is the part that got me — "Sometimes they even are." And Vee lets it go by. *It didn't catch on anything.* That's the book knowing more than Vee does. That's the one true warning she'll get from someone with no stake, delivered gently, over cheese, and she walks right past it. I texted that line to nobody because there's nobody up at 1 a.m., but I sat with it.

But here's what's souring, and I have to be honest because I've been tracking it: chapter 15 is chapter 13 again. Vee tells Cassie the trail-and-shirt story in 13. Vee tells Kayla and Meg the trail-and-shirt story in 15. Same shirt, same "he got out in the rain," same "I kept it, he didn't ask for it back," same "we've only kissed once." I *just* read this. Twice now I've watched Vee perform her own seduction back to an audience, and the second time the book even lampshades it — "You keep getting to it and going soft and skipping the middle" — but knowing you're repeating isn't the same as not repeating. I got the actual truck scene in chapter 12, from the inside, and it *gutted* me — the flannel, his back to the cab, her coming up onto her knees and unbuttoning while holding his eyes. That was extraordinary. And now I've heard it recapped to two different tables. The heat of the original is being spent down in the retellings. That's the froyo-overflow pattern, the deadbolt pattern — the book strikes a thing beautifully and then keeps striking it until I recognize instead of feel.

What's actually new here and worth the night: the counting. "Twelve." She didn't have to count. Meg catches her — "You know exactly how many days ago" — and that's a lovely little knife, the girl who insists she's casual keeping a private ledger down to the day. And the invitation itself, the "least information a human being has ever sent me," two o'clock, no meal at two, bring a change of clothes. Kayla's crude and she's right: he's not asking her over to eat. So Saturday is the house, finally, and I know from chapter 7 and chapter 14 what that house *is* — the deadbolt, the Steely Dan (Blondie now), the bench Randi built her toward. Vee's giddy heart doing "something quick and light and stupid" is the most frightened thing in the chapter and she's the only one who can't feel the dread on it.

And where's Randi? Absent this chapter, and I *felt* the absence — after 14, where Randi rode Pace and said "I want us" torn out of her, then couldn't stay in the bed, then left him standing in the cold door — I wanted these two threads to touch. Instead I got new girls, Kayla and Meg, pleasant and disposable, and I clocked that Meg is "Meg's boots," the half-size-big boots that blistered Vee. Small thing. The book's still placing its furniture.

I'm still here. But I'm here for Saturday and for whatever Randi does when she learns the house door finally opened without her hand on it — not for another retelling.
