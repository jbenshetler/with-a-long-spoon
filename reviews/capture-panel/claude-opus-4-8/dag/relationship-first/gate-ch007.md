REACTION

Okay, so this is the chapter I've been waiting for without knowing I was waiting for it. Chapter 3 gave me the game — Pace pointing, Randi picking, the *do you think she'll like the bench* at the end that made my skin go cold in a good way. And then four, five, six were all Vee, all sweetness, the froyo, the pen, the *may I call you* — and I was starting to worry the book was going to leave me stranded in Vee's honey forever, because Vee's chapters are lovely but they're a girl being fed sugar and I already know it's a trap. What I wanted was to get back *behind* the glass. And here we are, behind the glass.

And it's better than I hoped, because the reveal isn't information — I already have the information. The reveal is *Randi's face*. She comes in and locks the deadbolt first thing, same as chapter 1, and she can't even wait for the door ritual, she's on him. *Tell me.* And she's using the wine like she used the apple — mouth to mouth, making him drink her instead of the glass — and the whole time she's running the seduction *at* him to extract a report about the girl she's supposed to be procuring for *him*. That's the thing. She thinks she's the huntress. She thinks Vee is the gift she's assembling. And the book keeps catching her body telling on her: the thighs going tight when he says Vee built a cup like nobody told her when to stop, the *did she eat all of it,* the wanting that's grown a size bigger than it was on the lawn and that she now has to hold her face down over. He even names it to himself — she came here tonight *wanting to hear about a girl.*

What I love, what I'd text a friend about at 1 a.m.: he withholds. She says *tell me* four times and he gives her froyo, gives her *we walked,* and the one true thing Vee handed him on the sidewalk — the nicest anyone's been to me in a long time and it's froyo — he keeps. *It was hers.* He doesn't hand it over. That's the first time in seven chapters I've seen Pace keep something back from Randi, and it landed like a little cold coin dropping. Because the jacket told me this started as a game *between the two of them,* and this is the first crack of light under the door that says the two of them aren't actually one unit pointed at Vee. He's already privately curating what Randi gets to know. She's doing the same to him — *I already do,* bright and fast, over the held want. They're both managing each other. Neither one is telling the whole truth in a room that's supposed to be their conspiracy.

And I notice — the way I noticed Randi in the bathroom in chapter 1 — that this whole book is women (and now a man) arranging faces over things. Randi puts the smile on a beat late. He reads the beat. The intimacy here is *reading the lag.* That's the exact texture I came for, two people who know each other so well the tell is the lag between the feeling and the face.

Heat-wise it simmers hard — the wine kiss, the hands under the wire, her weight on him — but it doesn't consummate, and I didn't want it to. The charge is entirely in what's being extracted and what's being withheld. He hasn't had a drop of the wine and can taste nothing else. Good last line. Genuinely good.

The only thing pricking at me, and it's small: I've now had the *she locks the deadbolt first thing / Steely Dan low in the front rooms / knee to either side of his thighs finding where she wants to be* furniture three times, and the third time I felt the author reaching for the established chord rather than striking a new one. It's deliberate, it's leitmotif, I get it — but I clocked it as a pattern and not as a discovery, and that's the difference between it working and it just recurring. Watch it.

But no. This pulled me. This is the book knowing more than it's saying and showing me it knows by what Pace pockets. I want to see how much longer Randi can keep the face on over a want that's outgrowing it.
