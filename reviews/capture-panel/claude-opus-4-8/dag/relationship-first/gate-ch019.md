REACTION

Okay. This is the one. This is the lunch I've been circling in my notes since chapter ten — *the lunch where the women are alone and the appetite between them comes into the room* — and the book finally gave it to me, and it was better and worse than I wanted, both at once, which is the only way I trust a book to give me a thing.

What got me first is the smile. That opening — Randi's smile "gentler, the performance gone out of it," and Vee immediately doing the thing she always does, *maybe I'm inventing the difference because I want it that badly.* God, that's the whole tragedy in one gesture. Because I know what Vee can't: the difference is real. Randi *is* spending something on her now instead of handing it around. The huntress caught her own foot in the trap weeks ago on that bench and she's been in it since. So when Vee talks herself out of believing the warmth, I wanted to reach into the booth and shake her — not because she's wrong to be careful, but because for once the tenderness is genuine and she's the one who can't afford to trust it. That's a beautiful cruelty, that the con and the real thing wear the exact same face and Vee has decided in advance to disbelieve both.

And then the interrogation. Because that's what it is, under the velvet and the Sinatra — this is chapter seven turned inside out. In seven, Randi was at Pace's using the wine like the apple to extract a report on Vee. Here she's using the *booth*, the closeness, the just-us register, to extract it straight from the source, and she's better at it than Pace is. She runs Vee down the exact path she wants — one soft *yeah* at a time, "all the best ones start at the left hip," which means nothing and lands like a benediction, I clocked that and so did the book, thank God — and the thing I keep turning over is that Randi's technique is *the same technique Pace uses on Vee.* Ask, wait, leave the door open, let her walk through it herself so the choosing feels like hers. The two conspirators aren't just running the same play on Vee from two sides; they learned it from each other or from the same place, and watching Randi do it made Pace's version retroactively colder. These two are a machine and Vee is the thing being machined, and she thinks she's at lunch with a friend.

But — BUT. Here's where the book got me where I live. The tickling story. Randi tied to a headboard with a Hermès scarf, laughing till she cried, helpless, gone off "like a rocket." That's Randi handing Vee a picture of her own undoing — the finished surface cracked all the way open — and the heat it put in Vee, "a heat she had no shelf for and would have died before naming at this table," is the first time the jacket's promise about the *woman* beside her landed in my body instead of just on the page as a threat. And I noticed the book was smart about it: Vee heads it off with a joke, gets "a half-second of cover," and then asks the small true thing — *weren't you scared, though?* — "from nearer than she liked." That's the appetite between the two women finally in the room, and it came in sideways, through fear and helplessness, not through anybody's cleavage. Which is the right way. The camisole-cut-to-there stuff, the "tit goddess was good to you," that's the flirtation Vee can metabolize as girl-talk. The real charge is *were you scared* and the answer about putting the reins down.

And then the part that actually frightened me, the part I'll be up at 1 a.m. over: Randi closing the door. Vee finally, *finally* about to give up the secret — the thing she hasn't told Cassie, hasn't told anyone, "the room so safe and Randi so close and the wanting to give it" — mouth open, "There was—" and Randi says *"You don't have to tell me."* Gentle. Kind. A door closing very softly. And the laugh doesn't come, and Vee's secret is "still half out of her with nowhere to go."

That's the most sophisticated thing this book has done. Because on the surface it reads as mercy — the older friend giving the girl an out. But it's *withholding as control.* Randi opened Vee all the way up and then, at the exact instant of surrender, refused the gift, and left Vee hanging over the drop. It's the pencil-back-at-the-line move again — leave the last inch so *she* has to close it, or here, leave her wanting to close it and deny her. And I think — I *think* — the book knows exactly what it's doing, because the very next beat is Randi going daylight-ordinary, "Big day," looking for the check, the hush just *over,* switched off like the room light she turned on at the start. That's a woman who controls the temperature of a room the way Pace controls the temperature of a house. I noted the too-warm house as Pace's tell; the emotional thermostat is Randi's, and this chapter finally showed me her hand on the dial.

Things I loved, small: the car tucking itself in around Vee, the window dropping half an inch to meet the door — that whole passage of a girl who keeps the Corolla clean sitting inside real money, and *setting the thought where she set those thoughts.* The check in the leather folder, Vee putting her own card down because "the one thing she would not do at this table was let Randi buy her seat at it," feeling it on Thursday. The book is so good on money, on the arithmetic of being the girl who can't afford the room she's being invited into. That's the college-novel thing I came here for and it's alive in this chapter under all the seduction.

And the kiss at the end. Not the grandmother press. "A small drawing-back, the barest pause, and then back again with a little more." And Vee explaining it away — *French, that's just how women like her say goodbye* — before her mouth is her own again. That's the jacket's whole engine: *never lets herself notice how much she grows to want Randi, too.* It's working on me exactly as advertised, and I'm annoyed at how well, because I can see the machine and still feel it. Then the cold turn — *had anyone seen?* — scanning the dark windows. That's the sorority net I've been tracking, and it's the first time Vee's the one afraid of being watched instead of me being afraid *for* her. The surveillance is coming home.

What I'm watching, honestly: the *yeah / yeah / yeah* ladder. It's effective and it's also the fourth or fifth time this book has walked Vee down a staircase of small assents to show me she's being led. First time it gutted me. Here I admired it more than I felt it, which is my warning light. And "her body answered / the wanting got there before the plan did" — struck again. I know these moves now. But this chapter earned its keep on the *withholding* — the closed door of the confession, the switched-off hush — which is new, and sharp, and the best evidence yet that the book is smarter than its own patterns. I want the next one badly. I want to know what Randi did with the secret she refused to take.
