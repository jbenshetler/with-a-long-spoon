REACTION

Oh, this one sat heavy in me the whole way through, and the worst part is how *good* it felt while it did.

Because here's what finally happened: there was no scene to hand over. No dress, no swat, no afternoon that turned. And I watched Vee notice that and panic a little — *there's nothing to give* — like she'd shown up to pay a toll and found her purse empty. And that's the thing that chilled me, because it means the brunch isn't brunch. It never was. It's a collection. She has been bringing Randi her weeks the way you bring a landlord the rent, and the one week she has nothing "worth a picture," Randi says exactly that to her face — *babe, these are two weeks old, you did nothing worth a picture?* — and I wanted to reach into the booth and take the girl out of it. That's not a friend asking. That's an inventory check. Dusty, she called the safe photos. *Dusty.*

And then the photo. God, the photo. Pace said *never share your photograph* and she said *only if you promise you'll never share me*, and three chapters later she hands the whole uncropped length of herself across a tablecloth to the one person in the world who is running a plan on her. And she does it because Randi needled her pride — *you only gave him half?* — *legs like yours?* — and Vee won't be the girl who flinched. That's the whole machine in one move. Randi doesn't take the photo. She makes Vee need to give it. *You don't have to show me,* and she slides the phone back, and that's the exact thing that flips her. I've watched Randi do this at every table and I still felt it work on me a little, which is the horror of it.

But the part I can't put down — the part I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. about — is Randi's *catch*. "Like he wants me. With the body attached. Not the other way around." And Randi goes bright and unfixed and her breath does a thing she can't smooth, and says "it doesn't" low and not quite even. That's the first time the harvester has bled. And I genuinely do not know which it is: does Randi want Vee exactly the way Vee just described being wanted — the whole girl, not the body — and it ambushed her? Or does she know precisely what she's building and the line landed because *she* is the one doing the other thing, wanting the body, the artifact, the Miss November? The jacket says she wants the woman across the table. Maybe both are true and that's what's going to break everyone. Either way it's the most alive Randi's been, and I hate that it moved me.

And the class. The Tuesday-Thursday seven-a.m. class. Vee thinks she's reaching to keep a morning she doesn't want to end, and what actually happens is Randi gets into two more days of her week, early, before anyone's awake, "just us," and that "just us" came out with a roughness under it that Vee filed as nothing. The gap I track — how long since she and Pace were alone in a room — isn't even the gap anymore. The gap now is how little of this girl's life *isn't* one of these two. Gray Monday through Thursday, color Friday to Sunday, and now Randi's prying open Tuesday.

The kiss at the end, Vee leaning up the last half-inch, taking a second for herself, *deciding it was what she wanted.* That's the reaching the jacket keeps promising me is hers. And it probably is hers. That's what guts me. Nothing's being taken. She's handing it over gladly, every door, and the two people on either side of her set the table before she ever walked in.

I'm not happy. I'm hooked. Those aren't the same thing and the book knows it.
