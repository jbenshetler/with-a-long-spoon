Okay so this is the chapter I've been circling my own exit line about, and I knew it the second the counter showed up in the mirror. No Randi. No Sheri. No blonde at the bar. Just the two of them alone in a house for a whole chapter — which is the thing I've been quietly starving for while the book kept dinner-tabling me with Randi — and the book finally, finally leans all the way into the swoon. The drawer. He gives her a *drawer*. He folds his whole squared-off razor-at-the-edge self down into one drawer so she can have the top one, the easy one, the one your hand goes to first, and there's no note because there's never a note, and I sat there with my chest doing the exact thing the book built it to do. Almost. Because here's what I caught, and it's why I'm not gone: the weight loss. "Her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, and she'd never asked it to." The girl who came to them *starving since girlhood* is shrinking, happily, into clothes bought a size too big, and the book lays it down so tenderly that you'd miss that it's the disappearing-Vee thread wearing its nicest dress. And "Peter" — she whispers his real name to the sleeping shape of him, the name he took his mother out of, and he doesn't stir, she didn't mean him to. And she *loves him*, and he has never once said it, and she lies there and talks herself into living "clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying." Three times in this chapter: *he never said.* The not-saying is the whole chapter. The drawer is a gift and it's also one more thing he arranged for her without a word while she held still and waited to see what he'd do — the same wordless managing, just pointed at kindness this time. He watched the mess in the mirror, said nothing, and *acted.* That's him. That's always been him.

So the counter-voice didn't leave the room. The book didn't drop its honesty and ask me to swoon at disregard — it gave me real tenderness with the shadow still bolted on, and the shadow is that she's getting smaller and more his and calling it the happiest she's ever been. That's the line it's walked since chapter one and it's still walking it, just at its most seductive. What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he gave her a drawer and I nearly died and then I remembered she's losing weight and whispering his name to a man who won't say it back, send help.*

I'm suspicious of exactly how good this felt. That's the tell. And I notice the jacket's "three games" hasn't cashed, Randi's nowhere in this one, and a chapter this warm in this book usually means a door's about to close hard. Give me the next one.
