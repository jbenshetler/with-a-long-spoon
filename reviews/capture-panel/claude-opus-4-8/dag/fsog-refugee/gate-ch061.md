Oh. *Oh.* I had the whole week braced in my teeth with her — I saw "There's somebody I'd like you to meet," heard "Sheri," and my stomach did exactly what Vee's did. A woman. Golden, tan, everything she isn't, the midnight blonde made flesh. I was already writing the cruelty in my head: here comes the humiliation, here's where the machine drops the other woman on her and smiles. I bought the dread the same way Vee bought it, buckled my own heels on for the fight.

And then the book did the kindest thing it's done in a long stretch — it gave me Sheri. Five foot nothing, two-tone perm, makeup a little crooked and *daring* you to say so, coming off that stool with her whole weight in a hug. Not a rival. Not a blade. A friend. The blonde "didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand" — God, that's exactly it, that's how jealousy actually dies, not refuted but crowded out by a real person who won't hold still. And that look Sheri gave her — down the keyhole, down the bare January leg, to the heels, and the mouth going round with *oh* — it wasn't the pricing look, the placing look, the ranking math Vee's been fluent in since high school. It was *enjoying* her. Appreciation with no math in it. Vee's laugh breaking out of the middle of her before she voted on it, a month of held breath leaving in one go — I laughed with her. I needed that laugh as badly as she did.

And then Pace *blushed.* "You get to motorboat them things?" and the color came up his neck past anything deniable and he rubbed the back of his neck and looked at the table. I have spent sixty chapters being shown a man with a second floor under every tender thing, a man whose wrist-kiss might run on whoever's in the bed — and here's this tiny loud woman who can turn him pink and make him take it without a defense. That undid something in me. You can't stage-manage a blush. The thing I keep circling with Pace is *is any of this involuntary,* is there a person under the technique — and Sheri is the first witness who gets to the involuntary part. "He does go on about you." "You get the philosophy of his food." He says her name out loud, *glad*, at a volume she's never heard him spend — about Vee, to the one person he tells things to. That warmed me through in a place the cider hadn't reached, to steal her line.

But here's what actually got me, the part I'd text a friend about at 1 a.m.: Sheri's coming-out. "Whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish." Mother, Marine daddy, brother, church, Bible study, the girls she drove to Roanoke a hundred times — gone inside a month. Crying in the middle of a two-step in the one place left with her name on it. And Pace drove her home, asked nothing, showed up next morning with coffee and donuts and fixed the lock the landlord had promised since August, stopped the faucet, bought her a bacon cheeseburger, and never handed her one false word. No *they'll come around.* No *their loss.* He just *was there,* and nobody else was. And — "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen." There it is. The floor under *him,* finally, and it's not a trick and it's not technique. That's where the courtliness comes from, the asking, the leaving-room — he knows exactly what it costs to be the one nobody stays for, so he stays. For two years, for a woman he wants nothing off of. That's the most human I've been allowed to find him, and it came through Sheri, not through Vee's bed.

And the headlights held on her door until the porch light came on. "The exact same easy unannounced way he apparently did everything, everywhere, for everyone he kept, and had never once mentioned." That's the line. That's the thing I've wanted to be true about him and kept being told not to trust.

So why isn't my dread gone? Because I know what I know. Randi's in his bed talking about Vee. This beautiful clean warm evening — a real friend, a real history, a man being genuinely, unperformedly good — is happening *inside the machine.* Sheri doesn't know there's a plan either. She's a true thing the plan gets to borrow. And the cruelest little knife: Sheri, of everyone, is the one who came out, who lost her whole tablecloth for loving honestly, sitting across from Vee who is falling sideways toward Randi and doesn't let herself know it. Nobody at that table said the true thing. "The laugh had something underneath it, and nobody at the table asked what." That's the book's whole architecture in one sentence.
