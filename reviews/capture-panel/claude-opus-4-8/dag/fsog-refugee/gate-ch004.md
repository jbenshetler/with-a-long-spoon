Oh, this is the part that makes my skin crawl, and I mean that as the book doing its job. We just spent three chapters inside the machinery — the bench, Randi's whole begging-and-then-locking-herself-back-up breakdown, the pointing game on the quad where I watched these two literally shop for a girl like she was produce — and now we're dropped inside the girl. And she's *sweet*. That's the gut-punch. Vee counting the minutes of not-looking-at-the-door, writing the date on a clean page just so her hand has somewhere to be, folding her own eagerness down to a manageable size before she lets "yeah, sure, I could do coffee" out of her mouth — I know that girl. I was a version of that girl. And she has no idea the collision on the lawn was choreographed down to the shoulder-weight by a woman who's trained in it. "one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it." *I* know who did it. She doesn't. That gap is the whole horror and I can't look away from it.

And here's the thing that's actually got me by the throat: Cassie. Cassie is the only person in this book so far who's clocked something. The flat "Cassie" that won't play along with Randi's little name-bit. "You keep looking at the door." And then — *Goodbye, Vee.* The whole word. Not see-you, not bye. The narration even flags it, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday," and Vee smooths it over because smoothing over wrongness is her whole survival skill, same as Randi's face in the mirror. That "Goodbye" went through me like cold water. Somebody who loves this girl just watched her get picked up by a shark and said the only thing she could say, which was goodbye. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. just the words GOODBYE VEE and nothing else and she'd know.

What I'm noticing about myself: I'm not turned on, and I don't think I'm supposed to be, and I actually trust the book more for that. This chapter is all dread. The blank transcript thing — that Vee kept the *feeling* of being chosen and none of the actual words, "the leaning-in was the whole content" — that's a devastatingly exact rendering of being love-bombed, and the book knows it's love-bombing even while Vee doesn't. The "I'm not asking, I've decided," the "love you, girl" after one hour, the "see you later" as a decree. I've been on the receiving end of a charm offensive like that and the memory being blank afterward is *real.*

So here's my unease, and it's not about craft, it's about where this book intends to stand. The jacket already told me the yes is the problem, that I'm meant to be "ahead of her from the start" and "fall for it exactly as she does." Right now I am ahead of her and I am not falling for it — I'm scared for her. The question that'll decide whether I stay for the whole series: does the book stay this clear-eyed, does it keep letting me see the con as a con and grieve for Vee — or does it eventually want me to find the con *romantic*, want me to be glad she got taken? Because chapter 1 already showed me it can do interiority this well; it showed me Randi's cost from inside. If Vee gets a real self rendered this carefully and then the book winks at me to enjoy her being eaten, that's my exit. But right now? It's not winking. It gave me Cassie. The book put a witness in the room who grieves, and that tells me the author knows exactly what's being done to this girl. That's the thing keeping me in the chair.

I will say — it's been four chapters and Pace and Vee haven't been alone in a room, haven't even really met. Randi and Pace had the bench, the game. The actual central couple of the jacket is still just a copper head at the far edge of a lawn. I feel that gap.
