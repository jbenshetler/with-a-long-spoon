REACTION

Oh, this is the one that made my stomach turn over, and not because anything happened — because I know things Vee doesn't, and the book made me sit inside her while she didn't know them. That's a cruel trick and it worked on me completely.

Because here's the thing: Vee is *lovely*. She's the girl reading a room the second she walks in, finding her place in it, the one with the right pen who gets handed a tiny office — "the one who knows things" — and glows because someone let her be useful. She's the girl who stood outside the joke on the sign for two years and never sounded it out, and now she's being walked under it. I recognized her so hard it ached. And every warm thing Randi does — the arm squeeze, the *love you, girl*, the hand flat between her shoulder blades — I've been on the receiving end of exactly that from exactly that kind of girl, and in real life it's intoxicating and I'd have fallen for it too. That's what's making me sick. The book isn't making the seduction look stupid. It's making it look *irresistible*, which is the honest and much scarier choice.

And then Randi — I already know from the last chapter that this warmth is a scope with crosshairs in it. *Do you think she'll like the bench.* I have not forgotten that line and now I'm watching the woman who said it turn the full wattage on a twenty-one-year-old who's spinning on the sidewalk with an empty cup. The blank-transcript bit undid me a little: Vee can't remember a single word Randi said, only the feeling of being chosen. That's textbook. That's the whole mechanism of the con laid bare — the words don't matter, the *leaning-in is the content* — and Vee experiences it as the best hour in months. I could see the gears and she couldn't and I wanted to reach into the page.

Cassie. *Goodbye, Vee.* Not bye, not see-you, the whole finished word, the word for a longer parting. That's the only person in the room who can smell it, isn't it. She won't fix the wrongness, she just lets it stand and pulls her cap down and goes. I trust Cassie completely and I'm scared for what she knows.

Here's my honest tension, though. There was no Pace in this chapter, no heat, no two-people-alone that I actually read this genre *for* — it's all machinery and dread. And I'm two things at once: gripped, and braced. The book keeps promising it's psychologically merciless *and knows it*, that it's not winking that this is romantic — Cassie's goodbye is the book refusing to wink. That's the only thing keeping me from feeling gross about being asked to enjoy a grooming. I need the next chapter to prove the dread is the point and not the garnish.
