Okay. 1 a.m. text: *she's not comforting her, she's working her, and she's doing it with her whole heart at the same time, and I don't know which hand to be more afraid of.*

I have been dreading and wanting a chapter like this for fifty-three chapters and it finally landed and it's worse than I guessed, because it's tender. That's the trick of it. Vee comes off a red-eye wrecked, she has nowhere to go, and she goes to the one warm room she knows, and the room is a trap with its arms open. And the thing is — the comfort is *real*. Randi does hold her past the end of the hug. Randi does sweep the Gstaad dresses off the bed without a glance because the wrecked girl comes first. Randi loves her. I came out of chapter 51 with pity for her and now the pity has teeth in it, because she's choosing. She stood in that hotel room on New Year's and knew exactly what Vee was, named it to herself — "I am not brave enough to lose her" — and decided to keep the word under cover so she could have Vee without Vee getting a vote. And here's the collection. She talks Vee into the coat.

And watch *how* she does it, because the book let me watch and I hated every elegant beat: she reframes Pace's silence into "a boy holding himself still," which — fine, maybe that's even true, I half-believe it from chapter 53 — but she's not reaching for the truth, she's reaching for the thing that reloads the gun. She dismantles the Sheri fear (correctly! Sheri's no rival!) only so she can steer Vee back through the door riper than ever. And then the "slut" moment. That's the one that got me. Vee's counter-voice — her mother's voice worn into her own — comes up *hard and hot* and says no, I can't, that's what a certain kind of girl does. The counter-voice I've been clocking as my exit condition fired on its own, and Randi smothered it. "Say it." Made her say the word out loud and then reframed shame into bravery. That's exactly the move the whole genre makes that I left over — except usually it's the man doing it. Here it's her best friend, and it's sold as sisterhood.

But — and this is why I'm not gone — the book is NOT winking. That's the whole difference and it's load-bearing for me tonight. I'm inside the manipulation seeing it as manipulation. Vee's reluctance is rendered as real and reasonable; her shame has a true source; the book shows me Randi deciding to keep the word under cover for her own ends. And then the Brooke scene lands like a bucket of cold water and names the whole architecture: Randi has a *position*, a thing she's protecting, and Vee is "her." "You need to choose." The book just told me, flat out, that this is a woman with something to lose managing a girl she won't risk losing. That's not the genre winking. That's the genre indicting.

The shoes. God, the shoes. The two of them holding the same straps for a second — the induction heels Randi knelt and buckled, and now these, handed over "like a pair of flats." Vee's body answering before her head does, nipples drawing up tight, and her reading it as *tonight, Pace* when the reader can see it's the woman two inches away. "Randi always knew the shoes." That line is a knife.

The kiss at the door. Vee turning back around and taking Randi's face in both hands — Vee *starting it* for the first time in four months, and not knowing what it means, and Randi's breath stopping. That's the one place my heart actually hurt for both of them at once, because something true is happening to Vee that she has no name for and is handing, blind, to the exact person weaponizing it.

What turns me OFF: nothing erotically landed because I spent the chapter watching a hunt. What scares me: that the plan is now in motion and the next door is Pace's, and both of the people steering Vee toward it want her for reasons she can't see. Where's Pace in all this — chapter 53 gutted me, the man sobbing over a truck that wasn't her car, and now Randi's building him a delivery. The two games are converging on one doorstep.

What I'm suspicious of: that Vee shows up tonight bare under a coat in Randi's shoes and the book wants me to find it romantic. It better keep the counter-voice alive in that room. Tonight's the test.
