REACTION

God, this one ached. A whole chapter and she's alone in a twin bed her legs outgrew in middle school, and I felt every inch of that ceiling coming down close. This is exactly the gap I've been tracking without naming it — how long since the two of them were in a room — and the book just made the gap *the whole chapter.* No Pace. No Randi except a screen. And instead of that frustrating me, it gutted me, because the book knows it's the point. The room that loves a girl who doesn't live in her anymore. The synchronized-swimming trophies, the clarinet in the closet, the ribbons dusted around a corkboard of girls who haven't texted in a year. That's the loneliest furniture I've read in ages.

What I love — what keeps me here — is that the counter-voice is still *hers.* She's not pining prettily and waiting to be rescued. She's doing math in the dark again, the way she did in the laundry room, and she catches herself doing it. The thumb over the empty field. *are you—* taken back letter by letter. "There was no message she could send that came back in the shape of what she needed." She already learned that, in another cold room, and she *knows* she learned it. That's not a woman being acted upon. That's a woman who walked out on him and is holding the line even while she bleeds.

And then the thing that actually scared me, the thing I'd text a friend about at 1 a.m.: she couldn't keep her own night. She reached for the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, her body quiet and well-used, the best night she had — went into it to be warm — and *it turned on her.* The hair went gold, the leg went tan, the blonde she built from spite sat down in the wine light where she'd sat. She can't even keep the one good memory as hers. That's what not-saying the word does. He left a vacuum where a name should be and her fear rushed in and filled it with a fashion model at his stove. "He could be anyone's, anywhere, and she had no standing to so much as ask." That's the cost of his withholding laid out plain, and the book isn't romanticizing it — it's grieving it with me.

The Sheri detail twisted hard. She'd folded Thanksgiving away as pity *when she was the one he cooked for* — and now Christmas is a blank week she can't see into, and the stove, the garlic in the oil, the plate set down only when she's aching — "the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark" — is maybe happening for someone else. She made his cooking a language and now the language might be spoken to another woman. I believe her dread even though I don't believe the blonde exists.

Randi, meanwhile. The fairytale text again, and this time *Vee* is the one reaching with an open hand that demands nothing, carrying her own secret — the glass-room name she saw and never said. She envies a poisoned Gstaad "because even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had." That line's the whole class divide of this book in one breath. And the ending split that I've been watching for weeks finally got named out loud by Vee herself: a day she can circle for Randi — term starts, a face that lights up and means *gorgeous* — and *nothing at all* for Pace. No hour, no room he'll surely be in. "And what about Pace?" and nothing comes after it. That's the book telling me where the ground is giving way. Randi is the sure thing. Pace is the void.

What I'm suspicious of — and this is the jacket humming under everything — is how *convenient* this desolation is for the people who arranged her spring. She walked out on him on her own nerve in 49, and now she's spent a whole break marinating alone, with the one warm hand reaching toward her belonging to Randi. If there are three games, this is the stretch where the walkout gets turned into the reeling-back. I know this beat. It's my own Fifty Shades walk-out. The difference — the only reason I'm not bracing to leave — is that nobody's winking. The loneliness is real and hers, not weaponized to make me forgive anyone. Yet.

I'm not turned on by this chapter and I'm not supposed to be. But I'm tender toward her in a way the heat chapters don't get from me, and I want the next page more than I have in a while — because something has to break this silence, and I need to know which way she falls.
