REACTION

Okay, first: they were alone in a room for the entire chapter, and I didn't realize how starved I'd been for exactly that until I got it. No fight, no cold county road between them, no Randi steering from off-screen — just the two of them in his warm house that smells of bread he timed to her waking. That's the thing I read for. A man out in the January dark choosing fruit for a woman who's still asleep in his acreage of a bed. I felt the whole chapter in my chest before I felt it anywhere lower.

And then — God — the pleasure-princess flip. I braced for "kept naked, his to take, pleasure slave," the drawer sliding shut on her own clothes, and I thought here we go, here's the ownership thing dressed pretty. And instead SHE turns it inside out on her fingers in the hallway: he brings her off first, every time, before he takes a thing; he rose in the dark to feed her; she's not kept FOR his pleasure, she's kept IN it. And then the whole bed scene is HER pointing. Her finger. Wrist, breast, other breast, lower — him crawling, obeying to the letter, a gladiator groomed by his own princess. That is the exact trick I've been hunting for years: his control makes room for hers instead of erasing it, and the dominance and the tenderness are the same man, no seam. I was so turned on and so relieved at once I could've cried. The toast burning because she exists. "I got mushrooms" from a man who never runs out of words standing in his door with none.

And THEN the thing I actually care about most: he respects the no. Instantly. She turns her face, "ugh, gross, that's wrong," the child's hand across the mouth — that flat clean installed voice from before she could argue with it, her mother in her throat — and he goes still, starts to withdraw, and she clamps her legs and he *reads which no is which.* One economical wipe, no sulk, no lecture, "okay" against her jaw, and he leaves her mouth for her to come and take back on her own. That's the man who watches for the answer. I keep waiting for this book to betray that and it keeps not doing it.

But here's why I'm still holding the door and not lulled: it did NOT scrub the counter-voice. The ending has teeth. *Why does he like it so much.* The salt she goes hunting for on his lip — "an edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning" — and "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been—" and it slides away and settles minor-key and *waits.* I know things Vee doesn't. That salt landed on me like a cold hand. And the last line — she'll tell Randi everything. Well. *Nearly* everything. Randi always did know what to make of it. There it is, the machine, humming right under the pancakes. She's floating in a kingdom and I can see the strings on the throne.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "he made her a breakfast you can travel across and went down on her like she was the orange he peeled and I am DECEASED but why does the book keep whispering about the salt on his mouth, what does he taste like of, I don't feel good about the salt."
