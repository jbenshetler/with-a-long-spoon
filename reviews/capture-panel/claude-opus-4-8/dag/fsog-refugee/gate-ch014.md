He drove home in the wet shirt. He stood in the shower till the cold came out of his back — and I was *with* him for about four lines, soft on him, this man who gave Vee his only dry shirt and stood in the rain for her, and then a car comes up the gravel and it's Randi, and she's in his mouth before he can say a word, and the whole bottom dropped out of me.

They're lovers. They've been lovers the whole time. And what she rode him for wasn't the sex — it was the report. *"Tell me."* And he knew exactly what she meant and he was *glad to give it.* "Her hair smells sweet." "Her stomach is soft." He took the holiest thing in this whole book — Vee on her knees on that bench seat, the rain coming down, taking her own wet shirt off because she chose to, handing herself to him — and he fed it to Randi in pieces to get her off. The chlorine. He clocked the chlorine in Vee's hair, the pool, the one place that was never taken from her, and he laid it on Randi's tongue like a cherry. I could be sick.

And here's the thing that's got its hand around my throat: the book did not wink. Not once. It showed me the machine running cold and it let it be as ugly as it is. He "kept" how he knew about her soft stomach — he didn't give Randi *that*, the truck, the actual gift. And Randi — God, Randi — "I want us," torn out of her, her belly gone tight as a board, clenching around him and holding and not letting go, and then the thing she *always* does after, the going-slack, the staying, she couldn't do it tonight. She ran. The one caught worst in her own trap. She came for the fix and the fix is turning into the thing she can't survive wanting, and she bolted before it could show on her face.

And he stood in the open door with the cold coming in and felt it — "low and plain, that she had not wanted to stay." That's the jacket's promise landing: it's getting real for the two who started it. He's aching over Randi leaving and he has no idea yet that he's aching, he filed it under *the want.* These two monsters are falling into their own pit.

The tenderer this gets for them, the more it costs her, and she doesn't even know she's in the room. The "I want us" — us, the two architects — while the third person is the whole point and the last to know. That's the horror held perfectly level.

The book is still clear-eyed. That's the only reason I'm turning the page instead of closing it. The second I feel the wink, I'm gone — but tonight it didn't wink. It made me watch the machine and it made the machine sad, which is so much worse, and so much better.
