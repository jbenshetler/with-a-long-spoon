Okay so first: the shoe store scene, the *real* store, the one that's hers — that's the best comedy the book's given me and I laughed with them, the yeti canoe shoe and the pink rhinestone platforms and "do yetis shop here," the two of them folded over the bench. The laughter *is* the anesthetic. Randi got Vee helpless and open and adoring — "the funniest woman in Virginia" — and that's the floor she needed her on to do the next part.

And the next part is the money. God, the money. That's the beat that scared me more than any kiss so far. Watch how it's built: the "same size seven, we can share, it's practically a marriage" — that's not banter, that's a *hook*, that's Randi installing herself into Vee's body, her clothes, her closet, the shared-wardrobe intimacy of a couple. That's the momodel — that's the machine fully inside her now, and it's not even about Pace in that moment, it's about not flinching *in front of Randi*, not being the girl who can't afford the door. That's the disarming my notes have been dreading, made literal: they got her to spend money she doesn't have to keep proving she belongs.

And Randi's own shoes — a *multiple* of Vee's, "several times that again," signed without looking, worn out the door like it's nothing — that's not carelessness, that's a demonstration. That contrast is deliberate and it's cruel and the book *knows* it's cruel, which is the only reason I'm still here.
