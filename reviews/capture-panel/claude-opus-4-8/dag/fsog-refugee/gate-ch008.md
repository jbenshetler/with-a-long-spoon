REACTION

Okay, this one snuck up on me. I came in braced for more of the Randi-on-Pace's-couch stuff — the wine, the reporting-back, the way those two are cooking Vee between them — and instead I got a swimming pool and Cassie, and I put the book down against my chest for a second when it was over.

The bleachers thing made my jaw tight. "You won't need floaties with those." Fifty yards of catcalling and Vee walking it like an entrance because that's the only armor she's got — chin level, towel rolled tight and held *down at her side, not up against her*, which is the detail that got me, because I've done that exact math with a towel. And then Cassie, who doesn't pitch it up into the bleachers to fight the boys even though the book makes a point that she *could*, that she has the lungs — she keeps it low, just for Vee. "They will never know the love of a woman." That's the most romantic line in this book so far and it's between two friends and one of them's in a navy suit older than the friendship. I love Cassie. I have loved Cassie since she said "Goodbye, Vee" in that flat finished way three chapters ago, and this chapter is why that line has been sitting in my stomach like a stone.

And the underwater part — the room switching off, the old scull her hands find before she asks them to, the one place that "had never been taken." God. That's Vee's whole interiority in one held breath, and it's rendered from *inside* her, which is exactly the thing I read for. She grieves a little down there. I felt that.

But here's what I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this chapter scares me more than the bench did.* Because the bench, Randi chose. This is Vee, and she's the softest, realest person in the book, and I now know — the way the jacket promised I'd know, ahead of her — that the two people closest to her have *planned* her. Cassie is the only one not in on it. Cassie saying goodbye like she means it. The book handed me a whole chapter of Vee being lovable and safe and grieving-but-okay specifically, I think, so it hurts more later. That's the "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" line from the jacket doing its work, and I feel it working on me, and I resent it a little even as I can't stop.

What I noticed underneath everything: it's been a while since Vee and Pace were in a room. Chapter 5 was the coffee shop. Since then it's froyo (fine, sweet, and honestly the froyo chapter is where I most believed him — "your tart's holding up," letting the true thing have its space) and then two chapters of Randi metabolizing Vee as *material* on Pace's couch. I want them alone again — but I notice I want it now partly to check whether Pace is actually the man the froyo chapter suggested, or whether he's just a very good tutor's-path performance, because Chapter 3 told me he *engineers* the appearance of choice. That's the suspicion the book planted and won't let me put down.

This chapter had almost no heat and I didn't miss it for one second, because the care was so on the page. That's the trade I said I'd make, and the book made good on it.
