REACTION

Oh, I needed this one. After the truck — after that whole knees-on-the-bench, buttons-in-the-cold thing that I genuinely did not want to stop reading even while I was gripping the book like it was going to bite me — I needed to come back down into this room. Cassie's room. The cracked window, the two inches of cold, her brown shag untangled and dry while Vee lowers herself into the chair like an old woman filing a complaint with her own legs. God, I love these two. This is the first air I've had in the whole book that isn't loaded.

And that's the thing that's sitting wrong in my chest, because it's *supposed* to be air and it isn't, quite. Cassie is doing the bit — the witness statement, the blood type, the windowless van — and it's funny, it's real best-friend funny, the kind where the joke is love. "No van. He took you up a mountain nobody can see from the road, on a Sunday, and brought you back. With your shoes on." She *checked*. She ran the threat model. And every single item on her list came back clean, because Pace is too good to trip a single one of Cassie's wires. That's what got me. My canary sang and the coal-gas is odorless. She's built to catch the guy in the van and this isn't the guy in the van. There's no test in Cassie's whole arsenal that catches a man who packs moleskin and a second sandwich and a dry shirt he means to give away.

And then the turn — "she slowed then, as she'd slowed at the tree the other night, and Cassie, who had been doing the dry thing, went still and stayed that way." Cassie *rapt*. That's the moment I wanted to reach into the book. Because Cassie stops being the guard dog and becomes the girl who's happy for her friend. She hears the flannel story and the getting-out-in-the-rain story and she goes quiet, and what comes out of her isn't a warning, it's "Vee." Just her name. "Like setting something down where it wouldn't break." And Vee says "I know." What do they both know? They think they know it's *big* — first-real-thing big. That "I know" is the two of them agreeing that something serious is happening, and they're right, and they have no idea what.

"Borrow real boots next time." That's what my canary's got. That's the whole of Cassie's alarm, reduced to the one true thing she can actually see: the boots didn't fit and her friend got hurt a little and next time bring the right gear. It's so small against what I know is coming that it made my stomach drop. She's not going to say goodbye this chapter. She said "keep the shirt." She *blessed* it.

What I keep turning over: this is the first time we've heard the hike told *out loud*, by Vee, as story — "she said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed.* He packed lunch." The eating is the romance to her. And she's right that it is, she just doesn't know it was *composed*. "You carry out what you carried in" — she's repeating his line like it's a proverb she's adopted, leave-no-trace, and I flinched, because leaving no trace is exactly what this operation is designed to do to her. Nothing taken that she didn't hand over. No crumb, no pit, no evidence.

But — and this is why I can't just be sick about it — I believe her joy. "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain." That's hers. That's not planted. The machine didn't carry her up; her legs did. And the flannel smelling of soap and a man and her own wet hair. She's spinning again, quieter this time, and I'm protective and I'm also — happy for her, for one second, before I remember.

Where's Randi, though. Two chapters now with no bench, no blue eyes, no cap under the thumbnail. I feel that gap. And there's no Pace in this room either — this is the longest I've gone without the two of them alone together, and I notice it's exactly when the book puts Cassie in front of me that I stop looking for him. That's smart of it. That's almost too smart.
