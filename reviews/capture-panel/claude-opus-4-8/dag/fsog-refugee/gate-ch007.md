REACTION

Okay so this one's short and it's doing a thing to me I don't fully like and can't stop watching. On the surface it's the hottest register the book has — Randi coming through the door with no hello in her, the wine passed mouth to mouth, the tannin, her straddling him and taking his hands where she wants them. If that were all it was I'd have eaten it whole. The wine-kiss especially: him not pouring himself a glass because everything he's going to have of it he wants like that, off her mouth. That's the intensity I read for, a man tuned entirely to one woman.

But that's not what this chapter is. This chapter is two people debriefing a hunt, and the prey is Vee. And I *love* Vee. I've spent three chapters inside her — the right pen, the high table, the froyo cup that got away from her because for once nobody said *that's enough* — and now here are these two, warm and clever and post-coital, turning her hunger over between them like a jeweler with a stone. "She built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop." He hands Randi that — Vee's most unguarded, unpoliced moment, the exact thing that made me tender toward her — and serves it up as an aphrodisiac. That's the line where my stomach turned. Not because it isn't well done. Because it *is*, and I know what it means.

What's keeping me here and not slamming the book: the book isn't winking. It's not asking me to find the predation cute. It's showing me the machinery stone-cold AND showing me that Randi is drowning inside it — the want too big for her face, held level, held harder than on the lawn, and Pace reading it and being *moved* by her keeping the face over it. There's something almost unbearable about her, honestly. She's running this game and she's the one most caught in it. And Pace withholding the true thing Vee said on the walk — "It's hers" — that's the one gesture that keeps him from being purely a hunter. He protects Vee's real moment even while he's feeding Randi the rest.

But I'm scared. This is the thing the jacket promised and the thing I'm most on guard against, in the same breath. Every yes freely given — except Vee doesn't know there's a question. If this book lets Vee stay a specimen, if her waking up never gets to *matter* to her, I'm out. Right now I trust it just enough because it keeps making me feel how alive Vee is. I'd text a friend: *they're grooming the sweet one and I can't stop reading and I hate that I can't.*
