REACTION

Okay, this one I actually loved, and I want to be careful about why, because it's the first chapter in a while that let me just *rest* with someone.

Cassie. God, Cassie. She's the only person in this whole book so far who wants nothing. That's the thing that hit me — everyone else in Vee's life right now is running an operation on her. Randi is steering, Pace is "reading her body's true answer" and pointing at strangers like he's assembling a menu, and even the blurb is standing behind me whispering *she doesn't know there's a plan.* And then here's this woman in a navy suit that's older than the friendship, fighting into it swearing, going exactly rib-deep and no further, planting herself like a fence post and saying "I'll be right here. Standing. Like a person." She keeps the joke *down*, just for Vee, when she could've pitched it into the bleachers. She doesn't ask about the sad thing because asking makes the sad version bigger. She shoots the little sheet of water across the gap instead. That is *love*, rendered from the outside but unmistakable, and it's the first love in this book that isn't also an appointment someone else scheduled.

And the bleacher stuff — "you won't need floaties with those," "legs for days" — I felt that in my own skin. The way Vee rolls the towel tight and carries it at her side, not up against her, and puts her *face on* and takes the walk like an entrance because there's no other route to the water. That's a girl who has decided the only defense is to look like she meant to be looked at. It's sad and it's precise and the book, for once, isn't eroticizing it. It's just letting her be a person who learned to armor up.

The underwater passage is the best writing in the book so far, honestly. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." That line. The water is the one thing that was never taken from her — and the word *taken* is doing so much work, because this whole novel is about things being taken while she hands them over. Down there nobody wants anything from her. She sculls, she hangs weightless, and the thing that stood at attention all afternoon sits down. I got a little teary, I'll be honest.

Here's what I'm chewing on at 1 a.m., though, and it's the thing that keeps me *in* this book instead of trusting it: this chapter is the counterweight. It's showing me what unconditional, no-agenda care actually looks like — Cassie — right next to Pace's version, which *performs* that same attentiveness. "He listened. He didn't look at his phone." Pace does the froyo, the waiting-out-your-sentences, the *may I call you* — and it reads as care, and I was almost falling for it in chapter 5. But now the book hands me Cassie, who does the real thing and gets *nothing* out of it, wants nothing, and I can suddenly see the seam. Pace's attention is aimed. Cassie's isn't. The book knows the difference and I think it's teaching me to feel it, which is either the most generous thing this author could do for me or the cruelest setup — because I know from the jacket that Vee's about to walk deeper into the aimed version and away from the unaimed one.

And Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" from chapter 4 is sitting in my stomach like a stone now. That full word. This chapter is a friendship at its warmest right as the machine closes around Vee. I don't think Cassie survives what's coming. I think she sees it. That's what the flat patient look at Randi has been the whole time.
