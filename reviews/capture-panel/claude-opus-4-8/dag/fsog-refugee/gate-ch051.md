REACTION

Oh, this one got under me sideways. I came out of chapter 50 braced — her out in the cold, the door closed, waiting to see if 3 a.m. drives her back — and instead the book swings the camera all the way around and hands me *Randi's* fairytale, years back, before any of them, and I sat there the whole chapter going: this is the mirror. This is the whole thing turned so I can see its back.

The parents first, and God, that table. "Eating her revulsion." The invented boys safer than the real ones, "they always had been, at this table." The mother all edges and points, the tennis bracelet in the light and Randi keeping her own plain white-gold band *out* of the light — there it is, the band, I've been circling that band for ten chapters and here it just sits on a girl's wrist at breakfast, locked on, already never coming off. I don't have the answer yet but I know its weight now. She comes from a house where love is currency and the best you can do is pass inspection. Of course she harvests. She was raised at the register.

Then John. And here's the thing that scares me, because the book did it *on purpose*: John is the fantasy. He's the thing the genre I left kept selling me. The looming presence at the lift, "play along," conjuring a fake marriage and three ski-camp children in a lift line, the champagne by some string she didn't catch, the white-tie ball, the flown-in chef with a star, the venison and the sour cherry and the shaved truffle, the *sleigh with two grey horses* — I mean, come on, it's gorgeous, it's exactly the page you'd dog-ear. "She walks in beauty" and he can't finish it. Both hands crossed over his heart. And I felt the pull of it even knowing better, which is the trick, that's Randi letting me feel what she felt so the floor can drop.

Because the floor drops. "A good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." The pleasure real and complete and gone before the plate was cleared. She tries to make it be more and fails. The lipstick ring left on him and her own mouth doing it "from a long way off." Warm only where she touched him, the cold hers, carried in and carried back out. I've read that cold before — that's *Vee's* cold, the bunk, "it promised him close and there was no one close." The book is rhyming them and it's brutal about it.

And the dancing — this is where I actually sat up. On the mountain she led and he kept up; on the floor he led and she gave it all up, and she thinks *easier is better, I'm done with the work*. And then: he's a better dancer than Pace. Pace's lead was "the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for and good enough to catch, and it left the rest of the way to her." That's — that's the whole argument of this series in one image, isn't it. The firm unmistakable lead where you never have to guess versus the light pressure that leaves the way to you. The book just told me which one it thinks is love, and it's not the easy one. "Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." She likes not being asked, tonight, with John — because being asked means being *found*, and John never finds her. "Everything done to her and done well," and nothing reaches.

Here's what unsettled me most, and I have to be honest about it. I have spent fifty chapters wanting Pace *indicted*. And this chapter does the opposite — it makes him the deep thing, the real thing, "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best." And the awful part is the book's not wrong about the mechanism — the one who asks, who makes you say it, who leaves the way to you, that IS what I came to this genre for. It's just that it's *Randi* thinking it, Randi the harvester, using Pace's realness as the measure of a man she's discarding at dawn. So the book is simultaneously telling me the true thing I believe about intimacy AND showing me the exact machine that weaponizes it. I don't know whether to trust the warmth or recoil from the hand holding it out. That's a genuinely good knot. That's not the winking cruelty I left — it's smarter than that, and sadder.

And then Vee. "Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of." And the decision — not to tell her, not in a way that lets Vee *decide what to do with it*. "Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways." Keep the word under cover. Let Vee arrive at it on her own. That's the con restated as tenderness and it made my skin crawl precisely because the feeling under it is real — "she was not brave enough to lose her," "Vee was her gorgeous." I believe Randi loves Vee. I also just watched her choose, consciously, to keep Vee in the dark so she can have her without risk. That's the September walk all over again, that's take-and-account-for-it-later, dressed now in something that looks like love and might even BE love and is still, structurally, a trap. "She could have Vee this way and keep the friendship both, as long as the word kept its cover." The word. Always the word. Pace won't spend it; Randi won't risk it. Same house, different exits.

And that last text exchange gutted me a little. Vee from Ohio: *i'm here if you want to talk. or not.* — the kindest, most open door in the whole book, offered by the one person being played. And Randi hands back the fairytale, "every word of it true, none of it the thing," and signs it *miss your face most though, gorgeous* and means that part, and turns the phone facedown. The one true line buried in the performance. That's exactly how she operates and now I've watched it from inside while it happened to the person I love most in this book.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book just spent a whole chapter making me ACHE for Randi, the villain, and I'm furious about it, and also she's the loneliest person I've ever read and the cold is hers and she knows it's hers.* I didn't get my door-reopening with Vee and Pace — they weren't in a room together, they weren't even in the same timeline — but I didn't feel the gap the way I usually do, because the chapter was doing load-bearing work on the thing underneath: it told me what the word costs, why nobody will spend it, and it did it without once asking me to call anyone's cruelty lovely. If anything it called the fairytale hollow to its face. That's the book keeping faith with me.

My worry going forward: that the next move is Vee back through Pace's door "riper than ever," and that this chapter was the book softening me on Pace right before it asks me to find his un-asked-for return romantic. I'm watching for that. But tonight it earned me.
