Okay so I came into this one braced, because the last Randi lunch gutted me — the "cold"/"hmm," the thumbnail working the water glass, "you don't have to tell me" swung shut like a door in Vee's face right as she was handing over the softest part of herself. That chapter was a mugging with a warm hand on the back of your neck. So I sat down to this one waiting for the knife.

And it didn't come the same way, and *that's* what's got me at 1 a.m.

Because this time Randi doesn't corner her. This time she does the opposite — she takes Vee's "he was patient, he was gentle, he was hungry for me" and gently turns it around: *you keep saying "he," like you just stood there and let it happen.* And then she walks Vee, one soft admission at a time — the toenails, the new underwear bought the same afternoon, "you drove over there sure" — until Vee says out loud, "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." And Randi says *good* and covers her hand and lets the word settle in.

And here's the thing that has my stomach in a knot: that's the single most consent-forward, most *empowering* beat in the whole book so far. A woman telling another woman: your desire is yours, you planned for it, you chose it, stop confessing it like a sin. On any other page I'd be cheering. That's the exact medicine I read the genre hoping to find — a heroine's wanting handed back to her clean.

Except I know who's handing it back. I know this is the woman who pointed at Vee across a quad like picking a horse. So the book just used the *real thing I came here for* — "your yes is yours, be proud of it" — as the tightening turn of the screw. That's the jacket's whole cruel promise made flesh: "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." Randi isn't lying to Vee. That's the horror. Every empowering word is *true*, and it's load-bearing for the trap. Vee walks out lighter, prouder, more hers — and more caught.

What I actually noticed, and can't stop turning over: I lost the tell. Last time I could find Randi's seam — the coffee she didn't drink, the crack in the face. This time she's *smooth*. The "sad little story" about the beautiful idiot in a hurry — that's not a confession, that's a warm-up act, a little self-deprecating offering laid down so Vee will feel safe emptying her own pockets. Randi costs herself nothing and calls it vulnerability. And I couldn't catch her doing it in the moment, which means neither could Vee, which scares me more than the visible cruelty did. When I can see the manipulator break, I can hope she'll blow the whole thing up. A Randi with no visible seam is a Randi running the play clean.

And Vee — oh, Vee. The detail that actually hurt: last time she stood on the curb after the kiss and scanned every dark window for who might've seen. This time "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them." That's it. That's the whole acclimation in one sentence. The shame that used to make her check is quieting, and I can't even fully mourn it because the quieting *feels like health*. She's happier than she's ever been. Both things dead true at once.

The mimosa arithmetic that "ended in Thursday," the debit card, being "a half-second behind" in that lit room — she's paying for every one of these lunches, in money she counts and in things she doesn't know she's spending. And she thinks Randi's the friend she wanted at fifteen. She's not wrong that it feels like that. That's the knife.

I'm all the way in. I'm also aware this was a quieter chapter — a debrief, no new ground, coasting a little on the afterglow of the sex and the fitting. But it did the one thing I keep waiting to see the book flinch from, and it didn't flinch: it made me feel the empowerment and the entrapment on the same wire, and refused to tell me which one to feel. As long as it keeps holding both, I can't leave.
