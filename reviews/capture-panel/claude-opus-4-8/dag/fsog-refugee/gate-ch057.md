REACTION

I came into this one still sick from chapter 64 — him under Randi with Vee's name in the room and that *she's not the only one in love* and him not able to tell her she's wrong — so to open 65 inside Vee's head, mining the party for him over his dish-sink back, happy as a clam, was like watching someone sing in a house I've already seen the foundation cracked on. That's the whole chapter, honestly. The dramatic irony has teeth now and the book knows it.

And then Sheri walks in and I *forgive* him for ninety seconds, which is the dangerous thing. Because the blonde — God, the blonde. A month of Vee building that gold fashion-model chassis out of her own stupidity, dressing for her tonight, buckling the induction heels on in January against a woman she invented, and the door opens and it's five foot nothing, two colors of hair, crooked makeup, and the heat in her chest just *stays* because it was never about the blonde. That's so right it hurt a little. I've done that. Built a rival out of nothing and dressed for her.

Sheri running the look down Vee's body and *enjoying* her instead of pricing her, and Vee laughing from the middle of herself, a month leaving in one breath — that's one of the cleanest beats of relief this book has given me. And the motorboat line, and Pace *blushing*, the color going up past his ears, the one thing I've never seen him do. I liked that. I liked it a lot. A man I've only ever seen in control, undone by a small loud woman with a license to him nobody else holds.

Here's what the chapter is actually for, though, and it's load-bearing: Sheri is the one tether with no machine behind it, and she hands Vee the coming-out story. The tablecloth coming off the table, the whole family and church gone in a month, crying at the dance hall because it was the one place left with her name on it. Him driving her home asking nothing, the porch with coffee and donuts, the lock, the faucet, *did I want to come ride Sundays.* And "he never handed me one false word — no *they'll come around.*" That's the knife. Because the thing Pace gives Sheri is the exact thing he withholds from Vee — a true word, no false ones. With Sheri the care has no word buried under it, no band kept out of the light, no plan. With Vee it's all buried word and plan. The same hands, two completely different accountings, and the book put them side by side on purpose. And "he hasn't been home since he was sixteen, went bad with his daddy" — so Daphne isn't the only wound, there was a first one, the father. Of course there was.

The headlights held on her door till the porch light came on, then he backed down the run. Carrying Vee home like something full he had no intention of spilling. I believe the care. I've always believed the care. What I can't stop knowing is that the care and the trap are the same gesture, and chapter 64 is still sitting in my stomach while Vee rides home with her head on his shoulder thinking *bacon cheeseburgers.*

What I noticed underneath: it's been a while since Vee and Pace were alone and it *meant* something uncomplicated. This was a sweet night, but I'm watching her fall deeper into a man I watched in someone else's bed two chapters ago, and the book is letting me ache over both at once. I'd text a friend: *the gay best friend he rescued is the realest love in this entire book and it's not even his romance, and that's the whole problem.*
