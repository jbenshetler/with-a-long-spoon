REACTION

Okay so first — he said it. "Vee's boyfriend." Out loud, in a lit room, to six faces, and then he just kept saying it like it was the only word he owned and he'd finally decided to spend it. And I felt it land in her every single time, because I've been carrying December in my body this whole book: "words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me," the hand lifting in place of the word, her walking out into the cold with nothing. She asked him in that doorway *what am I to you out there* — out there, the public word, the one that costs something — and he wouldn't. And here he is, unasked, in front of her whole world, answering the exact question he folded his arms against a month ago. It's not the big name, it's not *love*, but it's the one she actually demanded and it's the right one and he spent it six times. That did something to me. I caught myself grinning like Kayla.

And the *care* of the whole thing — I'm not made of stone. He kept everyone's names. He had Meg's boots, Kayla talking paint off a wall, the Bricklayers, all the small dumb news she ran at his back all fall, and he took each one down and handed it back to the person it belonged to and then stepped out of the light and let them be their best selves in front of her. That's the thing I read for, honestly — a man whose attention is a room people get taller standing in. The Cassie beat wrecked me a little. My canary, the goalkeeper who watched every man near Vee for two years, sitting in a roadhouse *glowing* at low wattage, telling him the tray-across-the-tray story. Vee watching it from across the room and feeling a whale go under the boat. Me too. Me too.

But. BUT. I know too much and the book knows I know. Randi found the place first. Twenty minutes out on a road with no reason on it and she beat the *hosts*. And the grip — "hard enough to feel the bones" when he says "boyfriend" — Vee reads that as her friend being happy for her and I read it as the knife bleeding out under the table. And then the line that actually put a cold hand on my neck: *"They share an interest." "Who does?" "Randi. Your friends. All of them. You."* He says it like the sweetest thing and it's the literal architecture of the whole book — the two people who planned her, in one room, and the interest they share is the girl who doesn't know there was a plan. And Randi's toast, that glazed quiet, "meeting Vee was the highlight of my year," *more of it arriving than she seemed to have planned* — I know exactly what's arriving. It's the thing she decided not to say. Vee thinks she moved her friend and is *proud* of it, and I wanted to reach into the pine room and turn her chair around.

The truck. Fine, yes, I was right there for it — the stillness, "the man who did six things at once, down to zero," that's her power and it's hers and it's rendered from inside her greed, and I like that this book lets her *want* like that with no apology. The county-road callback, her own hand where no one's hand should be, the mark soaked into the coat she kept — it all rhymes. It's hot and it means something between these two particular people, which is the whole reason I'm still here.

What I clocked hardest, though: her own twinge. Pace laughing with Randi, "him, easy, somewhere she wasn't, being wonderful at a woman" — the exact Christmas shape, the phone that didn't ring — and she talks herself right out of it, *this is Randi, this is my own doing.* And the book flat-out names her instinct and has her dismiss it: "some corner of her didn't know how to watch a thing go right without checking it for the trapdoor." Baby. The trapdoor is real. It's sitting at your table in chambray drinking beer she's never had to drink before. That flash was the truest thing in the chapter and she stepped over it.

I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: HE SAID BOYFRIEND SIX TIMES and I still don't trust a single grain of salt in this book.
