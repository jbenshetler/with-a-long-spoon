REACTION

Okay first thing at 1 a.m.: they were ALONE. A whole chapter, just the two of them in a room, doing something to each other, and I didn't realize how starved I'd gotten for that until I got it. After all the debriefs and the retellings and the group-chat legend of the cornbread, this landed like the heater ticking up in the truck. So on that axis alone I exhaled.

And she topped him. She walked in already knowing where she was going, pushed him down, took his hurry off him before he could run his plan on her — and the thing I've been begging this book for, the heroine's desire rendered from *inside* and her hands on the wheel, it just handed it to me. "She had handed him his hurry. He didn't have any left. She had all of hers." That's the pleasure-princess flip actually earning itself instead of just being cute.

The scar. THE SCAR. She reached for it before and his hand came over hers and moved it off, no words — and this time she put her mouth on it, asked it nothing, and went past. After chapter 62, after "weren't safe," after Daphne, after everything I've been made to stack up under this man without ever being shown it straight — she kissed the worst of him once and didn't make him hold still to be comforted. That undid me more than anything below the waist did.

The painting itself — I braced, honestly. When the brush started doing the keep-still-or-it-goes-away thing for the third and fourth time ("Each time she tried to lift into it, it vanished. Each time she let herself be held by the bed, it returned") I felt my eye start to slide, that's the edging groove I already flagged is wearing thin. But then it kept turning it into something — the walnut, the parsley, the breast-print she smeared on his face on *purpose* and he WORE it the whole time, knew it was there, let her have it. The gesso reveal — that he was priming the canvas with her own wet, that there was *enough of her* to keep dipping the brush back in — took the shame she carries and flipped it into the entire point. "He had taken every part of her the world called too much… and made abundance the whole point." That's the book's whole thesis, done as foreplay, and it worked on me.

But here's what I clocked and won't let go of: he painted her with her own fluid and *did not kiss her mouth.* Every single color came with his mouth and a fruit at her lips — and that one brought nothing. And SHE noticed. "The absence opened there before the thought did." The taste-seed I've been tracking since the blood-orange kiss — it surfaced hard, "would it stay slippery in her mouth," and then she "let the uncertainty disappear into the wave." Deferred AGAIN. And then at the end, riding him, she goes hunting for it — "There was a trace. Salt first… something faintly sour under it, gone when her tongue sought it" — and the thrust takes the question out of her. This book is deliberately keeping that mouth closed. It's the one thing she still won't do, and I now believe that's the whole architecture and not an accident. I'm not frustrated, I'm suspicious in the good way.

No Randi in this one. Not a word. And I felt her absence like a held breath — the trapdoor didn't open, but I spent the whole warm scene half-waiting for it, which is what chapter 60 did to me permanently. This joy is real from inside Vee and I still can't drink it clean.
