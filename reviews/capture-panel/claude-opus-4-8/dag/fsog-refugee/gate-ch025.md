REACTION

Oh, I didn't like where this went, and I think that's the point, and I'm not sure the book earned my trust enough for me to sit still for it.

Here's the thing. The whole chapter is the two of them being funny in a shoe store, and it IS funny — the yeti canoe shoe, the pink rhinestone platforms, "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal," Vee folded over the bench laughing the real laugh. I liked Vee here. I liked that she's funny, that she's good in her own cheap store, that the ground was hers for twenty minutes and she worked the clearance wall like a pro. That's a girl with a self, and I came for the girl with a self.

But I watched the machine run the whole time, and I couldn't stop watching it. The tan shoe. Vee FOUND the tan shoe. It worked, it held, it was the sensible one, it was HERS — bought with the debit, the card that only spends what's behind it, the thing in my notes that's the one clean thing about her, "I've never gotten anywhere on my own legs." And Randi took it out of her hands. Not with a shove — with "a dress a man sat up nights making doesn't want to go with everything." She took the thing Vee had already said inside her own head and said it back one degree truer, which is the MOVE, the exact move from the lunches, and Vee's face went warm because hearing your own secret in someone else's mouth makes it real. I know this move now. I've got it in my notes. And here it is spending Vee's money.

And then the credit card. God. The debit back in the sleeve, the emergency card out, "there was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it." That's the line that scared me. Not the kiss, not the thumb on the toe — the arithmetic. Vee crossing from the TJMaxx math, the Thursday-will-survive-it math, into "the shoes were what the dress was owed," which is not her logic, that's the logic that was installed in her. The book let me watch her get talked out of her own floor and smile so it wouldn't reach her face. And Randi, beside her, buying loafers at a multiple of that number without a glance, boxing her old heels to wear the new ones out — the wealth laid right next to Vee's white-knuckle signature so I couldn't miss what the gap costs one of them and not the other.

The thumb on the toe. "Mm." It went all the way up the length of Vee and her hand shot out to find Randi's shoulder. That's Vee's own want surfacing — the thing the jacket promised, the thing in my notes I've been waiting to watch her let herself know — and it surfaced on her knees in a boutique being fitted, which is exactly the posture of the fitting with Pace, down to the rising onto the toes "the way it had risen onto her bare toes in his mirror." The book rhymed them on purpose. Randi is doing Pace's thing now, the attention-as-engine thing, and Vee's body answered it the same way. I felt the hook set and I felt it set on ME too, because it's genuinely erotic and I hate a little that it is.

And then — the part that keeps me here one more chapter — "it came to her that it might be jealousy. Not of her. Of him. And she found she wanted that for Randi so much it hurt." Vee, inside the machine, loving the woman running it, aching that nobody ever sat up a night over Randi. That's Vee's whole heart on the page, generous and exactly wrong about who needs protecting. And it's probably half true about Randi, which is the sick genius of it — Randi IS the one with the beautiful boys in a hurry and no one who stays. My notes say she's drowning in her own trap. This chapter fed that.

What I noticed hardest: Pace isn't in this chapter and I didn't miss him, and that unsettled me more than anything. It's been — what, since chapter 24 — and the gap I'm tracking has shifted again. It's not "when are Pace and Vee alone." It's that Vee's whole emotional life now runs through Randi, and the two of them alone in a room is starting to be the real center of gravity, and the book knows it and I know it and Vee is the only one who doesn't. The slit, the toes, "to not for," "you lucky slut" landing low on her still-sore body — every beat pointed the same way.

Here's where I sit. The book still hasn't winked. Nobody told me the credit card was sexy. The book showed me Vee go under — "which was its own kind of going under, and stood there holding a quiet the exact shape of what was in her account" — and let it be sad, let it cost. That's the only reason I'm still turning pages. If it had played the overspending as liberation, pure girlboss treat-yourself, I'd be out. It didn't. It played it as a girl being lightly, lovingly separated from her own ground, and knowing, and smiling anyway. That's the horror the jacket promised — every yes freely given — and I believe it's doing it on purpose, not by accident.

But I'll be honest about the ache: I am tired of watching Vee get harvested by people who love her, and a little starved for a room where someone is actually on her side with no plan. Cassie was in this chapter and turned toward Vee the whole lecture and that was the one warm clean thing — "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see" — and then Cassie got left at the dorm AGAIN while the Mercedes took Vee to the real thing. I'm starting to resent it a little. Cassie's my proxy and the book keeps benching her exactly when it matters, which is itself the point, and knowing it's the point doesn't make me less lonely for her.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "she put the sensible shoe DOWN and bought the four-inch strap on a credit card with nothing under it and the other woman's THUMB on her toe and I am not okay, also it was really funny which makes it worse."
