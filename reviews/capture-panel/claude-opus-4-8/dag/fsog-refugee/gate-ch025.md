REACTION

Oh, this one. This is the chapter I've been circling since I marked that scar and the closed "parents" door twenty chapters ago, and it did the thing I hate and love in equal measure — it opened the wound and then made me look at who's holding it.

The Ohio stuff first, because it disarmed me on purpose. The saffron old enough to vote. The dad who hears a wrong note in a jet engine. Vivienne in one mouth meaning love, in the other meaning trouble, and him laying that out for her plainly — "the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other." God, that's exactly the move I've been suspicious of the whole book: he receives her so precisely that the telling becomes a pleasure. She says it herself. That's the seduction, and I can name it, and it still works on me, which is the horror I keep signing up for.

And then Peter. The real name, set down flat with no forward motion. The chemical plant, the mother who "stays home," the answer with the mother taken out of it and Vee letting him. Then the hallway — the desk moved into the corridor, long division while he wanted to write graphics programs, "they took the thing you loved and made it the punishment." That gutted me clean, no counter-voice, and I noticed the book let it be clean, because it was true and it was his and nobody was running a play in it.

But then Daphne. Nineteen and fifteen. Two years. She left when she turned twenty-one. And the book does the exact thing I came to this book braced for and it does it to HIM — the flat careful voice, the good order that is the tell, every reason slotted into place, "it would have been stranger if she'd stayed," and he hands her the thing that hurt him and names it luck. He calls being groomed at fifteen the best two years he'd had. And Vee holds the clean ugly grown-up word under her tongue and doesn't say it, and neither does the book, and that restraint is the most honest thing that's happened in twenty-five chapters. Because now I know where the play came from. He learned it. Someone ran this exact game on him — the older one, the tender one, the soft middle, the distance that was nothing until suddenly it was everything — and he's rebuilt it and turned it on a twenty-one-year-old and named it love because that's the only name he was ever given for it. The scar he won't explain yet, "not tonight," a door with a time on it. I clocked that they linked it — the physical scar and Daphne in the same fireside — and I don't think that's an accident.

Here's what I'd text at 1 a.m.: I came into this scared the book would eventually ask me to root for the trap to close on Vee. Instead it just showed me the trap has a second person already inside it, and he doesn't know. "As true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew there was one." The jacket told me. This is that. And the awful part — the part that keeps me — is I feel tenderness for him now and I don't want to, because tenderness is exactly the tool. Vee fitting herself along his side, palm flat over the place the careful voice came out of. She thinks she's comforting a man. She's being trained by one who was trained. And it's warm. That's the crime. It's genuinely warm.

Still no clean sweetness handed to me. The dread is still doing its own work. I'm not out.
