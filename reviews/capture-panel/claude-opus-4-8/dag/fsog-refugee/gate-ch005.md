REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been dreading and wanting since the pointing game, and it did exactly what it was built to do to me. Because *I* was in that coffee shop before Vee was. I watched Randi say "do you think she'll like the bench" two chapters ago, and now I'm sitting inside Vee's chest while she gets tutored on Euler's formula by the man who already picked her off a lawn like produce, with the girlfriend who chose her sitting eighteen inches away doing that thing with the coffee cup — up to the mouth, no sip, down again. That cup. Rivers didn't have to tell me what was happening in Randi and I knew anyway, and knowing was awful in the good way. He answered her *n*-minus-one question competently and briskly and turned back to Vee, and Randi's hand went to the cup. That's a woman watching her plan work and hating one small human inch of it at the same time.

And here's my problem, the real one, the one this whole book is a machine for producing: the tutoring scene is *exactly* my thing. The giving back the pencil at the precise line where she could solve it herself. Not rewriting her margin, annotating it. Asking what her professor said instead of showing off. "What's drawing you to it" instead of "how do you like it." The *may I call you.* If I'd met this cold, with no chapters one through four, I would have melted into the floor — this is the man who makes room for her instead of erasing her, rendered from inside her own delight. That's the thing I've hunted for years and mostly not found.

Except I *did* read chapters one through four, so I know the room-making is a technique, and the technique is being run on a girl who thinks she got lucky by a coffee counter. And that's the sick genius of it and also the exact edge where I get nervous, because this is stalking-as-devotion, this is a no that never gets to exist because she's never told there was a question. The blurb warned me — every open door a temptation, the reader ahead of her, falling for it as she does. I can feel the book trying to make me complicit, trying to get me to find the hunt romantic *because* the hunt is so tender. I'm not there yet. I'm suspicious as hell and I'm holding onto Vee as a real girl with a real interior — her borrowed poise sitting "better than she'd expected," reaching for her own name back, the whole blank-transcript thing where she keeps the feeling and loses the words. She is not a thing being acted upon. She's fully here. That's the only reason I'm not out.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book is running a con on a 21-year-old and the con is doing everything I've begged men in these books to do and I don't know if I want to hug her or throw the book across the room and I need to know if he actually calls.*
