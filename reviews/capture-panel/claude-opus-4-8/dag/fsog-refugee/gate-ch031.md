REACTION

Okay so first — he wasn't there. Neither was Randi. That's the whole thing about this chapter, that's why it landed the way it did. For thirty chapters I've watched every good moment happen inside their sightline, inside the machine, with somebody in on the game standing just off-frame running her. And then the one time the dress finally gets worn in front of real people, in the good afternoon light she'd let herself picture — Berkeley took him, and Randi's nowhere, and it's just Vee. Alone. With her own leg.

And she did the thing herself. That's what's got me. The first step is the small one, the safe one, the slit lying shut, and the book stops and *lets her choose* — "She took a breath, and made the second step long." Nobody whispered it to her. There was no wrist, no two fingers, no "because Randi had said so." She decided a half-second before her head caught up and her body came up to meet it, same as the mirror, and she gave that dull kind room her whole bare leg on purpose and didn't make herself small. I felt that in my chest. This is the thing I've been reading this entire genre trying to find — desire that's hers, rendered from the inside, a woman waking to what she wants and *taking the walk* — and for once it wasn't handed to her by someone with a plan for her. It was just hers.

And then — God — the photo. She knows to the heartbeat what second Cassie caught, and none of it's in the frame, or all of it is and only she'll ever see it. And she sends it to him *uncropped* and keeps the private thing back. "That part was hers." That's the first time in this whole book I've watched Vee hold something back from Pace on purpose. A little sealed room he doesn't get the key to. After thirty chapters of him seeing her *completely*, of "thank you for telling me what she said," of the folder and the web and the briefed watchers — she kept one thing. I could've cried. I want her to have a hundred of those.

And Cassie. CASSIE. The one clean love, present, holding her phone at the door because past it she couldn't follow — "Go make your mother cry again" — and the bright corners of her eyes she'd deny to the grave, and the terrible sandwich, and "come on, streetlight." She's back in frame and she wants nothing and she just *loves* the girl. After Vee cut the line to her at the dance, having her right there being dry and proud and human — that's the warmth the operators only ever counterfeit. It mattered that she was the one at the door.

But here's what I can't put down, and the book won't let me either: the dress is still a leash even when Vee's the one pulling it. He *built* it so the slit runs to the hip so that to wear it right is to wear nothing, engineered so she'd come bare and careful, engineered so that the only way to walk it proud is to give the room her thigh. He made the machine that made this moment possible and then wasn't there to pull it — and it still worked. That's the psychologically merciless part the jacket promised: her freest, most self-owned act of the whole book is the exact act he designed. She "chose" the long step, and the long step was in the pattern the day he cut the silk. The wire's still there. It's just that this time she's dancing on it alone and calling it hers, and the last line knows it — pride, or the same heat still running, or by now the two coming to the same thing.

And you know what — it did NOT cross my line. I keep checking, because that's the deal I made with this book: the second it asks me to root for the trap closing, cheer the disregard, find a hand-slip past a no romantic, I'm gone. This chapter didn't do that. It gave me the heat AND the dread in the same breath and made me hold both, which is the only honest thing it could do. She had a real yes here, a hers-all-the-way-down yes, and the horror isn't that the yes was fake — it's that it was completely real and completely built. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." I felt the front of that sentence and the back of it at the same time and it's the best that feeling's worked yet.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she wore the dress and made the long step herself and sent him the photo but kept the secret and I don't know if I want to stand up and cheer or throw the book across the room and I think that's the point.*
