REACTION

Okay. The dress came. The thing I've been waiting twenty-some chapters for, and it came to *Vee*, not Randi, midweek, the afternoon he left entirely to her — and God help me, he got me. He didn't just make the dress, he made it and then hid every hour of the making, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like no work at all. "He'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That's the sentence I'll be carrying. That's the whole man in one move, and it's exactly the thing I've hunted for years, devotion that erases its own labor so she never has to feel indebted — and it's *also*, I know, I know, the exact mechanism of the whole trap: leave no seam, no closure she can find, so it looks like it arrived complete from somewhere with a name. The dress has no visible closure. Neither does Randi anymore. He builds the same thing she does and I keep falling for both of them a half-beat before I catch myself.

And then — the sex. This is the first time in ages the two of them were just *alone in a room* being ridiculous together, and I didn't know how much I needed the comedy until it landed. The whole relay of trying not to get her come-slick, come-new dress dirty, the "covered in a lot of me," him twitching in her hands at *nothing said at all*, her laughing so hard he has to stop and wait to undress her — that's the two-particular-people thing. That's the sex meaning something because it's *these two* being silly and undone together. Nobody in a bully book ever laughed like that. And when she wipes her hands down his flanks because it's the one thing in the room she's allowed to ruin, and he takes her fingers in his mouth slow with his eyes on hers — that went through my knees too, thanks. The heat had feeling in it. That's the whole reason I'm still here.

But. The ending. She lies there awake, the only one awake, knowing he was up all night finishing it, and she puts the dress on *in her daydream* and walks into the induction — coming in late, on purpose, the room turning, "she let it turn," "the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." And there it is. The induction is the planted goal. This is the dress arriving exactly where I said I feared it would, and it gutted me precisely as tenderness-that-is-surveillance, because *she* thinks she's dreaming up her own triumph and I can see it's the shape they cut her to. "Getting away with something," the book says, in her voice. She thinks she's the one getting away with it.

This is the closest the book has come to handing me the sweetness clean — no Randi at the table this chapter, no visible harvester, just joy. My exit line said the day it does that with no counter-voice I'm gone. It didn't, quite. The counter-voice this time isn't a character; it's the two words "what she was for," and it's chapter 23 sitting right behind this one, Randi at that same table two nights ago telling him *it's selfish, how long are you going to take* — and now, days later, he suddenly rushes the dress to completion overnight. Did she move him? Is his up-all-night the reflex outrunning the plan, or the plan speeding up on Randi's whip? I can't tell and that not-telling is the only thing keeping the dread mine. It held its nerve. Barely. Again.
