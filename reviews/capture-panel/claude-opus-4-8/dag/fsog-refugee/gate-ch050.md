Oh, she did it. She finally did it. She stood on the mat with her coat buttoned to her throat and she did not turn her mouth up for the kiss, and I actually put my hand over my mouth. All these chapters I've been tracking the gap — how long since they were alone in a room, how long since anyone said the true thing out loud — and here it is, and it's *her* carrying it, loud, in a daytime voice in a room built for murmuring. The coin under the tongue. The shoulders up by her ears since the highway. She drove out there with the fight already loaded and she didn't lose her nerve at the door the way she lost it on the bathroom floor, the way she lost it in the laundry room, the way she's lost it every single time the word came up to the surface and got drowned. This time she let it out at the wrong size for the room and she kept going anyway. Good. *Good.* I have been waiting for this girl to make noise.

And here's what undid me, because I came out of the whole genre hunting exactly this scene and the book finally ran it clean: he's good right up until the one place he isn't, and the chapter *knows it*. "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one" — God, that's such a slick move, and she sees it, she literally thinks *he made the true thing pull against the point*. That's the book being on her side. For the first time it's not Cassie's dry mouth or the doubt in the pool carrying the counter-voice for me — it's Vee, out loud, to his face, and she doesn't get talked down. "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me." I'd text that to a friend at 1 a.m. in all caps. That line is the whole book turned inside out. Everything I half-loved him for — the drawer instead of a sentence, the shirt worn without being asked twice, the heat up hours early so she's never cold — all of it reread in this chapter as the thing he does *instead* of the word, and she names it: "Not the hand in place of the word." She clocks the reaching hand before it even lands and refuses it.

And then he folds his arms. He steps back and holds the line *at cost* and says nothing. That's the tell I've been circling in my notes for ten chapters — the not-saying is the tell — and here it stops being mysterious and courtly and starts being a wall. A man who cooks water-buffalo mozzarella from scratch and sews a dress off a magazine photo and cannot produce two syllables. I don't read that as shyness anymore. I read it as a rule he's keeping, and after the jacket and Randi's planted script and "we chose her together," I think the rule is load-bearing for something.

She cried and turned her face so he couldn't have it, "on top of everything else he had of her and would not name." She got the door open. He said "Vee" — just her name, *and her name was not the word she'd come for* — and she went out into the cold and it was worse and she did not go back in.

That's the door closing. That's the one I come back for. She walked out on him, like the first book I ever read in this genre, except this time I'm not uneasy about the consent in the room — I'm *clean* on it, she chose every inch of the way in and now she's choosing her way out, and the book let her keep her feet. I'm not even a little tempted to set this down. I want to know what he does with an empty doorway, I want to know if Randi hears about this before Vee does, I want to know whether the word he won't say is "I love you" or "wife" or something uglier that explains the band and the plan. Twelve chapters of dread finally cashed into one clean confrontation and it didn't ask me to find his silence romantic. It asked me to feel her leave. I felt it.
