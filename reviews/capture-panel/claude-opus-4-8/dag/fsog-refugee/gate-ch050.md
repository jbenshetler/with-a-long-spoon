REACTION

Okay. So this is the chapter the whole jacket was written to sell me, and I have to be honest about two things at once, because that's what this book always makes me do.

First thing: it's hot. It's the most inside-her-own-desire the book has ever put me. The mirror scene — taking her own weight in her hands, doing to herself what he does, then *stopping on purpose* because she wants him to be the one to finish it — that's not a woman being acted upon, that's a woman authoring her own want and choosing to carry it unspent across forty miles of county road. The denial, the power she feels in leaving herself wound, the ridiculous glorious heels, "last thing, only thing" — that's her. That's the thing I've spent years hunting for, desire rendered from the inside, hers. And when he goes to his knees the second the coat opens, when the wall just *falls off his face* and what's under it is "a starving man" and "a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — God. And "Those stay on," and she blushes and leaves them on, and it's play, not cruelty. And his voice cracking on "I missed you so much." The pancakes at midnight. There's feeling in every inch of the heat. This is my kink and it was executed on me expertly.

Second thing, and I can't put it down: I know exactly how she got to that doorstep. She got there because she asked him a true, fair, human question — *what am I to you, out there, where I have to have a word for this* — and he wouldn't answer it, and then her supposed best friend took that legitimate need and turned it inside out. *You backed him into a corner. Name it or lose me is the one thing he can't do.* Randi rewrote Vee's want-to-be-named as Vee's *cruelty*, and prescribed the cure: stop using words, hand him your body, arrive already being the answer. And this chapter is the cure being cashed. It works perfectly. And the tell is right there at the end — "there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." She's been trained out of wanting the one thing he won't give. She drove out there for the word and came home having decided not to need it. That's the trap closing, and it closed *soft*, in warmth, with pancakes.

The reason I'm not gone is that the book didn't wink. It gave me chapters 46 and 47 first — Randi cold in a perfect man's perfect bed feeling nothing, Vee alone under the little gold swimmers building a rival out of spite — so I walked into this setpiece already holding the wire in both hands. The heat isn't asking me to forget the machinery; it's the same thing as the machinery, and the book knows it. And her yes here really is hers — reckless, hungry, self-made. Nobody's hand slipped past a no. She wanted this all the way down. That's the horror the jacket promised: every yes freely given.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she drove out there needing one word and came back having talked herself out of ever needing it again, and it's the happiest chapter in the book, and I feel sick and I also could not stop reading.* Also: he still never said it. She stopped counting that as missing. That's the scariest sentence in fifty chapters.
