REACTION

Oh, he's good. The book knows exactly what it's doing to me and it did it anyway, with a plate in his hand and his back turned, and I sat there with my whole chest going warm and my stomach dropping at the same time, which is the only sensation this series reliably gives me anymore.

Because on the face of it — this is the thing. This is the thing I came into the whole genre for and left when I couldn't find it. A man who listened. Not the polite version where he keeps his eyes on you while he rests. He kept Meg's boots. He kept the heel coming up out of the right one when she pushed off on the trail in October — a half-sentence she said once to say something — and he took it down off the shelf one-handed without turning around. He kept Kayla talking the paint off a wall. "I want to meet your people." That's the intensity. That's a man wholly focused on one woman, and the proof of it isn't a collar or a drawer, it's that he was *paying attention* to the dumb small news of her floor all fall and filed every name. If I didn't know what I know, I'd have my hand over my mouth like Vee naming children on day one.

But I know what I know. And so every warm beat in this chapter came to me doubled, the way Vee's pleasure comes to her doubled lately. "I want to meet your people" — and the first person up is Randi. Randi, whom he already knows. Randi, whom he chose her *with*, on a lawn, before she was anyone. This whole lovely spreading-out of the dinner — not a table with ends, somebody always ends up at one, let's get everybody in one room — that's not Pace being generous. That's the convergence. That's the two worlds he and Randi have been keeping separate finally being brought into the same room on purpose, and the purpose is the third game, the one more door. He's not volunteering to be among her people. He's staging the set where the next thing happens. And "Let me handle it. Let me pick the place" — I felt the con close its hand right there, polite and warm and him leaning back on the counter with a happiness he wouldn't quite let out. He'll pick it, all right.

And yet. Here's what keeps me honest and keeps me reading: her voice is still *in* this. She clocked the Chili's ghost herself — the game on three walls and the fourth screen, his fork squaring to the edge, "I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you" — the picture just stood there until she'd seen it, and she protected him from it, picked a form of gathering that wouldn't break him. And she protected *Cassie* — wouldn't build a dinner one of her people had to be a good sport at, "I've watched somebody be fine about it before, I'm not going to look at when." That's Cassie-care, that's real, that's hers. The book isn't asking me to find her erased. It's letting her plan with her mouth open, her one method, loving her friends out loud. That's why I'm not gone.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he remembered the BOOTS. he filed her whole life. and I want to throw the book across the room because the first name out of his mouth is the woman he's running a con with and he's going to "pick the place."* The tenderness is real and the trap is real and they are the same gesture, and I cannot tell you how much that costs me and how much I want the next chapter.
