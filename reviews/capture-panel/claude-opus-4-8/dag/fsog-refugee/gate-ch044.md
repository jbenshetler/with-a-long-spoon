REACTION

Okay. So it finally did the thing — not my exit thing, the other thing, the thing the jacket kept promising and I kept bracing for. Three chapters running now it's been walking her up to the word and not letting her have it, and this one is the one with nobody else in the room. No him. No sex. No Randi. Just Vee up in the top bunk at one in the morning with his shirt and the cold, and me lying right there in the dark with her.

The shirt undid me. The whole architecture of it — his shirt over her own shirt for the hall, for Cassie's eyes, the costume of a girl too far gone, and then when it's just her she strips both off and puts his straight on her skin the way she wears it when no one can see. And it works. It always works. And then the sentence that actually put a cold hand on my chest: *it was warm because she was warm. He wasn't in it.* The heat in the cloth is her own, given back. God. That's the truest, meanest image this book has handed me. She's holding a smell in the dark and calling it a person, and she knows it now, she can't un-know it. She can catalog every good thing he does — the things he made with his hands, the plate he sets down once he's built the hunger — and none of it tells her what she is to him. The inventory of his devotion and the silence at the dead center of it, in the same breath. That's the whole book in one bunk bed.

And then she takes it off and the cold comes down and it's *worse* — and she stays in it. She doesn't put it back on. She lies there bare in the cold with his shirt in a heap where a dropped thing lands, not folded, not back on the pillow, and she doesn't sleep. After all those chapters of her folding his flannel and driving it back to be refilled — she lets it lie on the floor. That's the closest thing to a protest she's got, and it's aimed at nobody, and nobody sees it. Cassie breathing slow and easy below her, still not ringing. My canary, asleep through the exact moment the seam finally showed.

What gets me is the withholding is now clearly the machine. "The rule was that he said it first." Where did that rule come from? She bumped into it from the inside and now it's part of her shape. That's not a man making room for her — that's a man who's built a room she can't get out of, and the door is one word he simply won't say. Chapter 42's cracker-and-meal sentence, her one beautiful attempt, and he answers with "I love how good we are together" — *not the same shape,* she says, and can't tell if it went past him or if he chose it, and can't ever test it because testing means saying the words and the words go through him first. That's the trap rendered from the inside so clean it's clinical. He gets the whole of her; what she has of him she couldn't hold up to anyone, it lives only in that bed and stops at its edges.

Am I still in it? Yeah. I'm in it because this is the book being honest about the cost for once instead of feeding me dread inside a gift. This chapter had no gift to hide behind — no aftercare, no drawer, no two-shirts-because-she-asked. Just the bill. I've wanted the counter-voice loud and here it's the only voice. The one place I drifted was the middle catalog — the things he made, the rooms he kept warm — because I've read that exact inventory a dozen times and my eye slid off it like wallpaper. But the bareness at the end earned every re-run of it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she took his shirt off and lay in the cold on purpose and it was worse and she stayed. and the warmth in the shirt was her OWN the whole time. I might not survive this book.*
