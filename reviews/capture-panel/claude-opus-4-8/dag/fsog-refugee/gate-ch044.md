REACTION

Oh, this is the one. This is the chapter the whole book has been quietly building a vocabulary for, and I didn't see it coming dressed as a stats lecture.

The professor at the front of the cold room, clicking his slides, saying *pick a subject that means something to you* and *look at the outliers — you can set those aside if you can account for them* — and me going cold three rows up because that's the book. That's Vee. She is the point off on its own in the white, and the whole machinery around her is the quiet work of accounting for her so she can be set aside. I actually put my hand over my mouth. They told me the thesis out loud and dressed it as homework.

And then Randi. God, *Randi.* The harvester who never slips, who serves the confession and eats none of it, who draws one black line through the pastry and orders coffee — she slipped. MIRA. MIRANDA. Her own father's companies, loan under loan under loan, the money that went up while the people went out the door, sitting there as the single worst point in the county. And the thing that got me wasn't the data, it was *"Cheating bastards"* — ragged, first-time, aimed too near, no clause folded around it to make it safe. For once she wasn't performing. For once the glass wasn't between her and the thing. The woman whose Mercedes and white gold and could-be-anywhere ease I've been chilled by since the seam showed — all of it sits on top of exactly the cruelty the graph just drew. Take, and discard, and account for it later. That's where she's *from.* That's the arithmetic she learned at the table before she ever ran it on Vee. I've been waiting a long time to understand what makes Randi, and this handed it to me sideways and I believe every inch of it.

But here's the part that actually broke my heart, and it's Vee. She watched the person, not the page. She read Randi's face better than Randi knew she was being read — the color going, the breath with work in it, the two words landing wrong — and she chose *mercy.* She slid the laptop over, gave Randi's hands something to hold, didn't say the name, didn't tell her then or after. She protected her harvester. She used the exact tenderness that's being farmed out of her to shield the woman farming it. And she walked out thinking the kindest thing a friend could do was not make Randi carry it in front of anyone. That's Vee entire — her gift aimed, every time, at the people managing her, and the aiming is what keeps her in the trap. I wanted to climb into the page and shake her, and I also understood exactly why she did it, and I'd probably have done it too, and that's the horror.

And *Cassie.* Cassie's back in the room and I didn't know how much I'd missed her. Flat as a board — "my dad got laid off in 2020," set on the table with no handle left out — not knowing she's sitting next to the daughter of the man who did the version of it that mattered. "That's not the exception. That's the one that's easy to see." She can't see the screen. She's the one person in the frame who'd pull the seam and she's looking the wrong way, agreeing with the thing without knowing whose it is. The dramatic irony is almost unbearable. Three girls who love each other over a graph that's secretly a confession, and only the reader and Vee can read it.

No Pace this chapter, no room with a door, and I usually feel that gap — but I didn't, not once, because this was the dread finally cashing instead of just hanging over me. I'm not happy, but I'm *gripped,* and nobody asked me to be happy for anybody vanishing. The counter-voice is everywhere; it's practically the whole chapter. So I'm not going anywhere.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the stats project is the whole book. she's the outlier. and Randi's dad is literally the bad guy in the data and Randi SLIPPED and Vee covered for her and didn't even tell her. I'm not okay.*
