Oh, there it is. There it *is.* I've been waiting forty-two chapters for the door to this room to open and now I've been in it, and it's worse and stranger than I let myself guess.

That alone turned my stomach over. They chose her. Off a set of doors. In the middle of a Thursday. The jacket said it and my notes dreaded it and here it is in plain words, no wink, no shame between them: Randi taught him what to hunt, he hunted it, her face said yes. Vee was a *selection.*

And the goodbye kiss. God. I just read chapter 42 from inside Vee — her leaning up that last half-inch, taking a second for herself, deciding it was what she wanted. And now here's the back of the same kiss: Randi training Vee's mouth "for the threesome," "somebody has to get her used to my mouth," calling it *work,* calling it *not a treat.* The thing Vee thinks is hers, the one hug to carry her the week — it's induction. But — and this is why I can't put it down — the book did the one thing that keeps it from being the cruelty I left the genre over. It cracked Randi open. "That's the one that got me. Twenty-one years old and she found out this autumn that a person can sleep." The hand that went still in the hair since October. The quick ugly "she told me first" when Pace says Vee repeated the asleep-mouth line to him — *jealousy,* real and petty and human. And then, folded over the back of the couch, wrecked: "God, I want her." And the scramble right after to file it back under the plan — "for us, when she's ready, that's all I meant." She heard it leave her. He felt her hear it. Randi is falling, and she's lying to herself with the exact same move she uses on Vee, and Pace — Pace *sees it,* "you don't have to do it for me," said kindly, and it undid her.

He's the only one who flinches at "radiant" not being "all right." But he's also the hand on the thermostat and the man who pointed at the door. The not-saying I kept flagging — here it's him refusing to let Randi off with "radiant," and it's the same man who built this. I can't tell if his care is the realest thing in the book or the most dangerous, and I think the book knows I can't tell, and that's the hook sunk all the way in.

The thing that saves it for my exit line: there is no tender beat here with the counter-voice *gone.* This is *all* counter-voice. The day the book asks me to just be glad she's vanishing with nobody in the frame knowing better, I'm out. This is the opposite of that day. This is the machine with the panel off and the gears showing, and one of the operators is starting to bleed.
