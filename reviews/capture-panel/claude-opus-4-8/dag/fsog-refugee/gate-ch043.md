REACTION

Oh. Oh, they gave it to me. The whole time I've been reading toward the day Vee learns Randi and Pace chose her together — and instead the book did something worse and better: it put me in the room with the two of them, alone, with Vee thirty miles away sleeping the good sleep she just learned she could sleep, and let me watch them run her across the couch like a ledger. This is the chapter the jacket promised on page one — "Randi and Pace chose her together, once, before she was anything to either of them" — and I've been bracing for it for ten chapters and it still went through me like cold water.

Here's what undid me. Everything Vee handed me as hers in chapter 42 — her desire, her nerve, the uncropped photo she worked up the courage to send, the goodbye kiss she *wouldn't let end*, leaning up the last half-inch after Randi — all of it comes back in this chapter as data. Randi reports it. "Saturday she wouldn't let it end... she came after me. She *took* it." In 42 that beat was Vee's agency, the one thing that felt purely her own, and here it's the plan working, reported with pleasure to the man who pointed at her on a lawn. The book made me feel Vee's reaching as hers and then showed me the two people counting it as yield. That's the most brutal thing it's done, and it didn't wink once doing it. That's the part that matters — it's not played as sexy triumph. It's played as what it is.

And the grooming line. "Somebody has to get her used to my mouth." "It's not a treat, it's *work*." The goodbye kiss Vee thinks is "one of their things," a sweet accident that started in a parking lot — it's acclimation. I've read that exact move dressed as romance a dozen times in books I left. The difference here is the book *knows*, and lets me see Randi need it to be work so she doesn't have to say the true thing, which she says anyway, face-down in the wood: "God, I want her." The want is real. That's what keeps this from being the flat cruelty I walked out on elsewhere — Randi isn't a cold manipulator getting off on control, she's a woman who's caught in her own machine and frightened by how much she means it. "It makes me feel real. And some nights it frightens me." I didn't expect to feel the floor tilt under *Randi*.

But here is the thing that stopped me cold and kept me reading in the same breath: Pace. "It isn't you I'm checking on. She consents — every time — or I'm done." That is my own voice, the thing I left the whole genre hunting, spoken out loud by the man in the frame. And I don't know what to do with it, because he is the one who engineered her — he pointed, he ran her face like a dial, he already had the picture in his head of all that red hair on his pillows before he knew her name — and *that same man* is the one auditing her yes and meaning it. The consent is real at the surface and the whole thing is built underneath. That's the vise this book has had me in since chapter 32: the better he is, the worse it is. Tonight he was both at once, in one sentence, and I just sat there.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *they CHOSE her on a WALK. he got hard on a public lawn planning it. and he still stops if she says no. I don't know if this is the most ethical predator I've ever read or the most honest love story and I hate that those might be the same sentence.* Also: he came four times and the fourth one *hurt* and he stayed in it — that detail wrecked me a little, him worked raw too, the machine costing its operators something.

I am not turned on so much as flayed. The heat's on the page — four times, her teeth closed on a sound, the slick of three times' worth — but my body went cold watching them fuel it with her confessions. What I feel is dread with its hand finally shown, and, strangely, the first thing like pity I've felt for either of them.

My exit line did not cross. I said I'm gone the day the book touches Vee tender with *no* counter-voice anywhere and asks me to be happy for her vanishing. This chapter is nothing but counter-voice — it is the machine laid open on a table, Pace himself asking "is she all right," the tiredness, the fright. The book is not asking me to be happy. It's asking me to watch with my eyes open. I can do that. I have to know now.
