REACTION

Okay. So this is the chapter where I finally got to watch her hit the wall I've been circling for weeks, and I have complicated feelings about how good it was.

The sentence in 42 — "I had been eating crackers my whole life, and you fed me a meal" — she built that for weeks, checked it wouldn't read as *I love you*, said it once into the pre-dawn cold, and he handed her back "I love how good we are together." Not the same shape. She named it herself in the laundry room: *not the same shape*, and then wouldn't finish the thought because either end hurt. And now 43 is her whole body refusing to let that go and dressing it up as a fight about Chili's.

Here's what I keep turning over. The mechanics of him are, on the surface, *exactly* the thing I read for. She throws the loud ugly thing — "if we're just sex, I'd like to know" — far too much voice for the dark, and he doesn't flinch, doesn't punish, doesn't get cold. He goes into that woodworker stillness and takes it seriously and hands her back "Where do you want to go tonight?" A plain question, nothing to catch on. And then he actually *takes her out*, does the small talk, does the going-out with good will. And later, "That restaurant — places like it — they're too loud... In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you. Somewhere quieter, I can. And I do. I'm listening now." That's — God, that's the man. That's the one who makes room instead of erasing her. If I'd been handed that speech in a vacuum I'd have swooned.

But I wasn't handed it in a vacuum, and this is the thing the book keeps doing to me and I'm so tired of catching it: the counter-voice is *right there in the gesture again.* Because I watched her lie to get to the forgiveness. "You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail" — and the text says flat out, *it wasn't true, some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold, knew who'd carried a pie up whose porch steps* — she said it anyway *because she needed the line of it to hold.* She rewrote the history of her own fight so she could be the wronged one, so she could "forgive" him, so she could cross the room. "Forgiven, warm and large and easy to wear." She's managing herself now the way she manages Randi. She's doing the play on her own heart.

And the "I'm listening now" — I believe he means it and I also can't stop hearing it as the perfect deflection, the thing that's *near enough to the word* that the fight leaves her, without ever being the word. He gives her the whole of his body, he loses the control she begged him to lose — that part landed on me, I'll be honest, "took her like a man who'd run clean out of the strength to wait," her climbing again with no say in it, that's heat with feeling in it and I felt it — and then she lies in the dark marked all over and does the math I've been doing for her: *she belongs to him, but does he belong to her?* He gave her everything but the one thing. And the book knows. It says it out loud: "He'd given her all of it and not the name."

So what turned me off isn't him being cruel — he isn't, that's the trap. It's that I can now see the withholding is *load-bearing*. The word is the leash. Keeping it back is what keeps her lying awake doing repair work on what she knows about him, keeps the pile of "small flickers" she won't look at. That's not a man making room for her. That's a man — and a book — keeping a door shut so she stays pressed against it.

The nachos, though. The nachos got me. "It was to food what a billboard is to a painting." She *used* to be happy with fine. She didn't know to want past it. He ruined it for her — "not just this." That's the whole horror of the premise in one dead loaded chip: they've enlarged her appetite past anything that can feed her except them. That's the "starving" from the jacket coming home to roost, and it's genuinely well done and it genuinely scares me for her.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he still hasn't said it and now I understand that's the entire architecture, he's never going to say it because her not-knowing is the point, and she just LIED about their fight so she could forgive him and go to bed. I'm not okay. Also the sex was good which is exactly the problem.*

Where's Cassie in all this? 41 was her, quietly — "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" and Vee: "Both." Cassie asked the exact right question and didn't push, and I'm still waiting for her to pull the seam and she still won't. She's the only one in the book who'd say *he won't give you a word for it, Vee, notice that.*

Am I leaving? No. Because the counter-voice is loud and unscrubbed and the dread is doing its work — which is, per my own dumb rule, the thing that keeps me here. The day it goes sweet with the dread gone, I'm out. It did not go sweet. It went the opposite. It handed me the name-withholding as romance and then quietly showed me the machinery under it, and I can't stop reading a book that keeps letting me see the machinery.
