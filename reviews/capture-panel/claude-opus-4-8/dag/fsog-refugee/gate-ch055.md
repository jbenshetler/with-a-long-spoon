REACTION

Okay. 1 a.m. text, all caps: SHE WENT BACK. And I knew she would — I wrote it in my own notes, "riper than she's ever been for the next one" — but knowing it was coming didn't take the sting out of watching it land, because of *who* built the door she walked back through.

Here's my problem, and it's the whole chapter's problem for me: the mirror scene is the best writing of her desire in the entire book. That was hers. Every bit of it — her own hands taking her own weight, the two fingers in and the deciding *no, not yet, I want him to be the one*, turning away from the glass unfinished on purpose and feeling **powerful** for it. That's exactly the thing I came to this genre for and almost never get: a woman's wanting rendered from the inside, hers, chosen, not performed for a watcher. I was all the way in it. The drive with her hand in her lap, the cruiser coming up behind — fine, that bit's a little telenovela, I rolled my eyes a half-inch — but the interiority carried it. She meant it with her whole body, the mark on the coat lining and all. I felt that.

And then the doorstep, and he goes down. The wall "simply gone," the look that's starving-man and forgiven-man at once, on his knees on the cold boards. The pancakes at midnight. Him coming around the counter to drop to one knee beside her chair — *I'm sorry, I never meant to hurt you, I missed you so much*, voice cracking and not coming back. On its face that's the tenderness-and-dominance-in-one-man thing I'd kill for. If I didn't know what I know, I'd be melting.

But I know. I sat in Randi's skull in chapter 51 while she decided to keep Vee by keeping the word's cover. I watched Randi, four months the one who leans in, hand this girl the plan, the shoes, the *bravest-thing-a-woman-can-do* speech, and aim her straight back at Pace — the very man Randi's running the con with — while Randi's own mouth is still warm from Vee's kiss. So the grandest romantic gesture in the book is Vee's puppet strings being pulled by the person who wants her, dressed up as Vee reclaiming her power. That's the sick genius of it. The book isn't winking at me — it armed me, it keeps showing me the machine on purpose — but *Vee* has had her counter-voice lifted clean out of her mouth and replaced with Randi's certainty, and the only doubt left in her tonight is "slutty," which is the wrong doubt, the one that doesn't matter, and it gets talked down to empowerment. The right doubt — *is this a trap, why still no word* — isn't anywhere in her head. That's the loss. Chapter 50 put the fight in her mouth and I loved the book for it; this chapter took it back out and handed her a coat to open.

And notice: he said *sorry, missed you, never meant to hurt you* — three things that are not the word. Same dodge, warmer lighting. After chapter 53 I half-believe it's Daphne-shaped fear and not pure con, and I'm genuinely split, which is the only reason I'm not furious. But he still didn't spend it.

Cassie at the end — "Hmm," back to the book. My proxy stood down again. I wanted the seam pulled. It wasn't.

So: hot as hell, hers, and the trap closed with a bow on it. I'm not enjoying the reunion clean. But I need to know what Randi does with a Vee who's now both her creation and her crush, and whether that word ever gets named. On I go.
