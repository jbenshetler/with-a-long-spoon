REACTION

Okay. This is the chapter I've been bracing for since the jacket said "she has moved the two who arranged her spring," and here it is, and it's worse and smarter than I feared, because I can see every string and the book can see me seeing them.

Randi reels her back in. That's what this is. And the thing that has my stomach in a fist is that she does it by taking Vee's one true, hard-won thing — the thing Vee walked out the door on, *words are cheap and you still won't spend them on me* — and dismantling it brick by brick. "Words are the whole war. You don't do it with words." She said that. She took the grievance that was correct, that I watched Vee *earn* over twenty chapters, and turned it into the flaw Vee has to give up. She reframed Vee's bravery as a cornering, made her see it "from where he was standing," got her to apologize with her body for having asked a fair question. That's the exact door Vee walked out of in ch 49, and Randi just talked her back through it, and called it taking things into her own hands. It is the opposite of Vee's own hands. It's Randi's.

And here's the vertigo: every word Randi says is *true*. I just read ch 53. I watched that man fold the cashmere blanket and sob when a truck didn't turn into his drive. So when Randi says "that's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — she's *right*. The con and the truth are the same sentence. That's the cruelest, cleverest thing this book has done yet, and I have to hand it to Helen Rivers, she did not blink.

But — and this is why I'm still here, white-knuckled, instead of gone — the book is not winking. It is not asking me to cheer. It put ch 53 right before this *on purpose*, so I'd carry Pace's own interior into the room as a counter-voice while Randi handles Vee. Vee walks out "with a plan sitting where the wreckage had been," and I'm not relieved for her, I'm *scared* for her, and the book knows I'm scared. It's handling her and letting me watch the handling. That's not the genre lying to me. That's the genre grieving beside me again.

The slut-shame walk-down — "say it," "it's such a slutty thing to do," and Randi walking her off the ledge a word at a time — that's the needle I was watching for, because it's one inch from someone talking a woman past her own no. But Vee's "no" there isn't no-to-the-act, it's shame-about-wanting, her mother's voice in her mouth, and the want underneath is *hers* — "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's desire from the inside, which is the thing I read for. So it stayed on the right side of my line. Barely. By an inch.

And Randi. God, Randi. Two hugs held past the end, past where a hug stops, "the beat longer and the beat after that, and neither of them let go." Her new Gstaad things swept off the bed without a look because the wrecked girl comes first. The bitter-orange smell of her neck. That's real tenderness, I *felt* it — and it's a con, and both are true at once, and I can't untangle them and neither can Vee. And then the New Year story: Randi who narrates every mouth and where it went goes *quiet* — "and then it got very late, and we went up," and looks at her hands. Vee reads it as discretion. I don't. John was the parent-approved New Haven man, "perfect everywhere but the bedroom," and the quiet is the band still locked on her wrist and the thing she keeps under every fairytale. Everything she tells Vee is true and none of it is the thing. Same as the midnight text. That woman is starving in a tailored coat and feeding Vee to her own appetite with both hands, and I pity her and I do not trust her an inch.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she's going to his door tonight with nothing under the coat and he spent last chapter sobbing over her taillights and I cannot breathe.*
