REACTION

Okay. 1 a.m. text, all caps energy: HE DIDN'T WIPE.

Let me back up. Most of this chapter I was just — warm. Happily, bodily warm. She wakes up in the acreage of that bed with bread in the air timed to her waking, and I let myself have it right alongside her, because the thing I actually came to this genre for is right here on the page: a woman directing her own pleasure from the inside. The whole pointing game — wrist, nipple, lower, the foot, the *wrong* way down the legs — that's her desire, hers, rendered from behind her own eyes, and he's the one obeying. I kept thinking, this is the exact inversion I left all those captive-bride books looking for. He goes down first, always, every time, before he takes a thing for himself. He burned the toast twice because she exists. And when she says no — the real no, the clamp-your-legs *don't you dare stop* no versus the *not that* no — he reads which is which, stops the wrong thing, doesn't push, doesn't sulk, doesn't explain. "Okay." Just that. Do you know how long I've waited for a man in one of these books to hear a two-part no and parse it correctly and not make her pay for it? That's the whole ballgame for me. I could have cried.

And THEN the book turned the knife so gently I almost didn't feel it go in. The wet kiss. The mother's voice coming up out of her — "that's wrong, that's not a thing you do" — installed before she could argue with it, and her hand doing the little-girl thing across her mouth. Fine. But it's what the book does *after* that's got me sitting up in the dark. He liked it. Not tolerated — liked, went down glad, came up wanting to bring it to her like a good thing. And her tongue goes hunting on his mouth and finds — maybe — *a faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning.* And "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been—" and it slides away and she lets it and it *settles somewhere under the affection and stays, minor-key, waiting.*

I know what that salt is. I've been in Randi's head. I know whose mouth he's been at. And Vee just tasted her and filed it under *he forgot to wipe, once, poor luck.* The book handed me the salt over her shoulder and she can't read it and I can and I feel SICK about it in the best way a book can make me feel sick. She tidied it into "a man forgets a thing he's done a hundred times, once." The reader knows it's not that. That's the three-game thing closing its hand.

The "pleasure princess" riff is where I got nervous — it goes long and it flirts with the exact thing that makes me leave, the woman reframing being-kept as being-sovereign, painting a leash gold and calling it a crown. But she's the one authoring it, giggling at her own joke, inverting slave and princess herself, so I let it ride. Barely. I clocked it, though.

What actually has me is the ending. She's going to tell Randi. Nearly everything. The strange salt thing too, because "Randi would know what to make of it." Randi always does. Oh, honey. You are about to carry the one clue you have *straight back to the person it's about.* I want the next chapter so I can watch Randi's face do the thing it did in the glass room.
