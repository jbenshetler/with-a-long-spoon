Oh, this one landed sideways on me, and I didn't expect it to. Two chapters of cold beds — Randi's gold cage and Vee's twin bed — and now the book drops me into a double-wide diner with the one person in this whole book who wants nothing off him, and I could breathe. That's the trick of it. Sheri is the only clean air in the entire series and she doesn't even know what she's sitting across from.

The Paige thing gutted me a little, honestly. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." He says the one true kind thing and she tells him not to waste the good stuff on her, and I believed her and I believed him, and it was the most human either of them has been. Sheri's whole deal — the two-toned hair, the single she slides back to true, the fries she just takes — she's drawn like a real woman and not a device, and I kept waiting for the book to make her a device and it didn't. Yet. I'm suspicious of that "yet," because this book has taught me to be.

But here's what the chapter actually did, and it's why I'm not charmed, I'm cornered: it put Sheri in the chair I've been screaming from for fifty-three chapters. *"So say it." "I can't." "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her."* She says it to his FACE. She calls it exactly what my carry-forward notes have been calling it — the silence is a rule he's keeping, load-bearing, and he'd rather keep it than keep Vee. And watch what he does: he answers the question *next to* the one she asked. Every weekend, nothing else in the room, "the most important thing in my life," brick on brick — the performance review again, the same move he pulled on the porch. Sheri names that too: "that's a hell of a lot of reasons for a man who's already got the girl. She didn't ask you for reasons." I wanted to stand up in the diner and hug her. Finally someone outside the con holding the same arithmetic I am, and the book handing it to her, not winking it away.

And still — STILL — he won't say why he can't. "I don't answer that." He answered the one next to it. That's the tell. The word isn't shyness, it's the hinge of the whole thing, and even Sheri, who has no idea there's a Randi-and-Pace-on-a-couch, can smell that something's off: "Save it for your redhead," then the real knife, that he's gone on Vee like she's never seen, so why can't he spend one word. What I noticed hardest: he decided not to "bring her down today." That phrasing. He carried something in from the truck and chose it could keep — more deciding-a-thing-can-keep, more management. Even his night off is administered. That's the tenderness this book keeps using as the hook, and I feel it, and I don't trust it, and that gap is exactly where I live now.

Say it or don't, John's gone, the cold beds are stacking — put these two in a room.
