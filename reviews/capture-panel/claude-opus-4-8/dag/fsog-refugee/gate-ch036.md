REACTION

Oh, Cassie. Cassie is the whole chapter and Cassie is the one clean thing in this book and I spent the entire time watching her watch Vee and my chest hurt.

Because here's what I noticed: this is a debrief chapter, and I've read a lot of debrief chapters in this book, but this is the *right* one. The Randi lunches are debriefs where the confession feeds the machine — where telling it out loud is the seduction, where Vee climbs a tablecloth one sentence at a time and Randi's "sticky fingers" is a hand on a wire. This is the same shape and it does the opposite. Vee comes home lit up and Cassie opens the door and lets her run, and there's no *using* in it. "Go on, then." "Go to sleep, streetlight." Nobody's harvesting her. Cassie just loves her and is scared for her and won't say the scared part out loud because that's not how you keep a door open with someone this far gone.

And she *is* far gone. The tells landed on me one after another and I hated each one a little. "You don't come home school nights" — not a question, and Vee goes back over a week she "hadn't counted" and finds her own bed empty more than not. She didn't count. That's the thing. She's not tracking her own life anymore. And Cassie, gently, "You've had boyfriends. You never went like this over one." That's Cassie naming the difference between fine and *this*, and I felt the genre-hunger in it — that's literally what I read for, the man wholly focused, the thing that makes fine unbearable afterward. Except Cassie's saying it the way you'd note a fever.

The burn. Okay. The tomato that slid off the fork and caught the side of her breast, and her hand goes to the spot "without her knowing it moved" — that's good, that's her body keeping the record her head won't. And "There's only one way *that* burn happens. Not enough on." Cassie clocking that Vee's eating dinner half-naked in this man's house and calling it plainly. She's not scandalized. She's just *seeing*, and Vee's already skated past it to how he fussed over the burn for days, "like the small hurt was a big thing." Which — is it care, or is it a man who's learned that tending a hurt he can magnify is how you become indispensable? I can't separate them. I've said that about every chapter for ten chapters now and the book still won't let me.

What actually got me, though, the 1 a.m. text: "Do you love him." Flat, the same way she said the rest. And Vee — who has not shut up since the door — goes *small*. "Maybe. I think so." And then "It's all so fast." She knows. Somewhere under all of it she knows it's too fast, she said it herself, and Cassie "let the maybe stand" and didn't say a thing against it and didn't say I know. That restraint is the most loving thing anyone's done in this book and it also broke my heart a little because it's the sound of someone who knows she can't argue a person out of a fever, she can only leave the window cracked its two inches so the person can find the cold air if she ever needs it.

The clothes-getting-looser thing from last chapter is still sitting in me and this chapter doubles it — she "went greedy," she eats "like a field hand" now. He fed a girl who'd spent years pushing the plate away. I keep wanting that to be pure and good, and it half is, and the other half is that he's the one who gets to give her back her appetite, which means he's the one who could take it away.

No heat in this one and it didn't need any. It was a breather, and it was the *right* breather, because it put the one witness back in the frame right when Vee's stopped counting her own nights. Cassie is my thread now more than the held hand is. As long as the book keeps letting me have her clear-eyed and unbought, I'm not being asked to root for the trap. I'm being shown the wire, still. That's the deal, and this chapter kept it.
