REACTION

Okay. So. I need to text somebody at 1 a.m. because I spent the whole chapter in the wrong body and I only figured it out on the last page.

Here's the thing. I came into this book thinking I was reading about Vee — the jacket told me Vee, the starving girl, the one who gets walked toward Pace. And these first chapters are *Randi*, and they've been so good, that bench chapter wrecked me a little, honestly, the care in it, the water bottle left out in advance, the *there you are* — and I was starting to think, okay, this is a man I can actually trust. He asks. He watches for the answer. He left the door she locked alone because a closed door is hers. That's the whole thing I've been hunting for since Fifty Shades, the dominance that makes room instead of erasing her. I was *into* him.

And this chapter took that exact trustworthiness and turned it into something that scared me more than any of the mafia books ever did.

Because look at what he actually does. He asks her the question — "would you like to taste another woman" — at the precise moment her body can't lie, mid-orgasm, undefended, and he *knows* that's why he asked then. He decides it beforehand. He reads her flinch, sees her move the wanting somewhere safe, calls it "beautiful," and then proceeds anyway — but reframed as *her* gift, *her* game, so that every step she takes toward this stranger looks like her idea. "I point. You pick." He engineered it so the choosing feels like hers. He's narrating, the whole time, how he's giving the wanting "the wrapper it needed to come out in." That is not a man making room for her. That is a man who has learned exactly where her doors are so he can go in without ever appearing to touch the handle.

And the thing is — the prose *wants* me to find this tender. It's doing the Chapter One trick, all the solicitous attention, "that mattered more to him than the answer did." But I know things this narration thinks it's hiding. I read Chapter One from inside Randi's head in that bathroom. I know she went *behind the face* and decided, quiet as a deadbolt, *not ever.* So when this chapter shows me Pace congratulating himself on how far she's letting him in, I know he's steering a woman who has already gone into hiding and doesn't know he knows, or — worse — knows and is counting it as progress. The gap between what he thinks he's doing and what's actually happening to her is the most unsettling thing in the book so far.

And Vee. God. Now I see the jacket. "The invitation reached her long before she knew there was one." It's *these two* who are the plan. The glowing, footwork-on-the-brick, "she's got a stats class with me" — that's the trap being sprung on the real heroine, and the two people doing it are both lit up and in love with the doing, and one of them is herself a woman who's being handled. "Do you think she'll like the bench." That line put a stone in my stomach.

Here's my honest problem as a reader, though: I'm gripped. I am completely gripped. This is the inverse of what makes me leave — it's not coercion played as sexy with the book winking along; it's coercion played as tenderness with the book standing *behind* me, letting me see the mechanism the characters can't. "Psychologically merciless in its tenderness" — yeah. The jacket told me and I didn't believe it.

What I'm watching for now: whether the book stays this clear-eyed, or whether it's going to ask me to *root* for the Vee seduction as romance. Because right now I'm reading it as a horror show dressed as a love story and I trust that it knows that. The second it winks and wants me to find the hunt cute, I'm gone. But this chapter didn't wink. It let me feel sick while Randi grinned. That's the hook.
