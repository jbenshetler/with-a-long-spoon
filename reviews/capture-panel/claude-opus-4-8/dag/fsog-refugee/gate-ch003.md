REACTION

Oh. *Oh.* Okay. So that's what this is.

I spent two chapters thinking Randi was the woman — the one on the bench, the one who went somewhere real and then locked the door and decided he could have "the face" and nothing else. I was already wrecked by her. And now Chapter 3 just quietly turns the whole thing over in my hands and shows me the underside: she's not the prey. She's the *co-hunter.* The pointing game. They're walking the quad picking a girl. And the girl they pick — the one Randi can't stop looking at, the one she drops her whole managed surface for — is Vee. The girl from the back cover. The one who doesn't know there's a plan.

I feel a little sick and I could not look away, which I think is exactly what this book wants from me and it's not pretending otherwise. That's the part that's keeping me here. The mafia books and the bully books used to wink at me — *isn't this hot, her no doesn't count, go with it.* This book is doing something colder and, honestly, more honest: it put me ahead of Vee on purpose. The jacket told me. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." I thought that was marketing. It's a thesis. The staged shoulder-collision, rendered as this gorgeous piece of choreography — "it was the redhead who pulled up short, stricken, apologizing" — I *admired* it and then hated that I admired it. That's the trap being sprung on me, not just on Vee.

And Pace. I keep waiting to catch him being cruel so I can leave clean, and he won't give me that — he's all care, all "take your time," all watching-for-the-answer. Which is the genuinely frightening thing, because Chapter 1 already showed me what the care is *for.* The tenderness is the mechanism. He's not forcing Randi; he built her a bench and waited for her to beg in her real voice and then she decided she could never let him see that voice again. The asking is real and it's still a cage. I don't have a word yet for a book that gives me the consent I said I wanted and then makes me watch it be the most effective tool of all. I'm unnerved that it's working.

The opening, with the "would you like to taste another woman" and her coming helplessly before she could make it mean anything safe — that was hot, genuinely, but it curdled the second I understood the walk was shopping. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" was the line that turned my stomach all the way over. That's Randi already imagining Vee face-down on the thing that undid her.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book is doing something I have never read before and I don't know if I trust it or if it's just very very good at making me not stop. the hero asks permission for everything and it's the scariest thing in it.* I need to meet Vee from the inside now. If the author gives her a real self — not just a target — I'll follow this a long way. If Vee turns out to be furniture, I'm gone. But I don't think she will be. The jacket promised me her waking up, from inside. I'm betting on it. For now.
