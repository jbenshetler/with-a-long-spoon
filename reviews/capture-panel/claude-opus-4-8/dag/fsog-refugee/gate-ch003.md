REACTION

Oh. *Oh.* I had it wrong the whole time, didn't I.

Two chapters I spent crawling around inside that woman on the bench, aching for her — the one who couldn't let herself be seen, who begged in a voice that frightened her and then fixed her hair in the mirror and decided *not ever*. I thought she was my heroine. I thought this was her book. And now the lamplight comes up and she's not the girl being taken apart at all — she's the one at Pace's side on the quad, *pointing*. She's the net. And the girl by the dining hall with the red hair thrown back laughing — that's Vee. That's the name on the jacket. That's the one everything in Chapter 1 was training me to understand so I'd flinch at exactly the right second in Chapter 3.

I have to hand it to this book, it's doing something to me I don't love and can't stop. Because here's the thing — Pace *does* all the stuff I came back to this genre for. He asks. He watches for the answer. In bed he can tell a performed release from a real one, he waits for the half-inch where her composure drops. And then he turns every bit of that attention into a *weapon*. He asks Randi "do you actually want to taste another woman" and when she dresses it up as a girlfriend's gift — the little trailing *you dirty boy* voice, the kitten act — he sees straight through it, he knows her body already answered before she could make it mean something safer, and he just... pockets the real answer and lets her keep the costume. That's not consent. That's a man who's decided he knows her better than her own stated no, and the book is *admiring* him for it. "He admired it, a little, even as he saw through it." That's the exact thing I left the genre over, except it's wearing a three-piece suit and calling itself tenderness.

And the hunt. God. The *pointing game*. Watching Randi go from offended-propriety to shopping, from "I'm being a very good sport about your little game" to the bare naked *yes* when he pointed at Vee — the follow breaking frame before the lead calls the step. It's gorgeous writing and I felt myself leaning in and then I hated that I was leaning in, because there's a real girl at the end of that point who has no idea three seconds of trained choreography just got aimed at her shoulder. The shoulder-collision was *flawless* and that's what scared me — how good Randi is at making the predator look like the one who got walked into.

And then. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I actually set the book down in my lap for a second. Because I *know* the bench. I was ON the bench, for a whole chapter, inside the woman it broke open. And now it's a trap being set for a stranger, said in that breathy real voice, the one she only uses when something's true. That line is the whole book in six words and it turned my stomach even as I wanted to turn the page.

Here's where I am, at 1 a.m., texting: I am ahead of Vee and I am sick about being ahead of her. I don't trust Pace one inch now and the book *knows* I shouldn't, which is the only reason I'm still here — this isn't winking at me, it's showing me the machinery with the lights on and daring me to find it beautiful, which, damn it, I partly do. What I need now is Vee. From the inside. If I don't get her own voice, her own desire rendered as hers and not as something being harvested, I'm gone — because I will not sit on the predators' shoulder for a whole volume and call it a love story. But if the next chapter is Vee, waking up, wanting — I have to see it.
