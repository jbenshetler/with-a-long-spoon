REACTION

Okay. *Okay.* I had to put the book down on my chest for a second.

So Randi is the bench girl. Randi — the one from the jacket, the "glamorous new friend" who's supposed to steer Vee toward Pace — she's the one who begged facedown in chapter one, she's the one who locked the bathroom door and pressed the bottle cap under her nail and decided she was never coming out from behind the face again. And now she's out on the quad with the man who found her, and they are *shopping for a girl.* And the girl is Vee. The redhead. The one the whole book is named for.

I actually felt the floor tilt when I put those pieces together. Because chapter one seduced me — it did, I'm not going to pretend it didn't — that was the thing I came to this genre starving for, a man who watches for the answer, who leaves the room for her to choose, whose "no, not yet" is about *her* and not about his power. And I let myself trust it. And now the book turns that exact man around and points him, gently, courteously, attentively, at a stranger laughing with her head thrown back who has no idea she's been selected. The same hands. The same patience. "I point. You pick." My God.

And the horrible thing — the thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. in all caps — is that the *craft* of the pointing game is the same craft that made chapter one so good. He's not choosing Vee. He's watching Randi learn she's allowed to want. He waits. He doesn't rush. He gives her the wrapper so the wanting can come out dressed as play. It's the identical technique of care and attention and consent-shaped patience, and it's being used to run a con on a third person who isn't in the room where any of it is being negotiated. I feel *sick* about how well it's built, and I think the book knows I feel that way. That's what's keeping me from throwing it. It's not winking. It's not going "isn't this hot and harmless." Randi pressing that bottle cap into her nail bed, Randi's bare helpless "yes" that arrived before she could make it mean something safer — the book is showing me the wound and the weapon are the same object.

But here's my problem, and it's the whole reason I read: Vee is the girl who *doesn't know there's a plan.* Everything I love in chapter one was that her desire was hers. And Vee's desire is about to be manufactured, curated, steered — chosen for her by two people over a cheeseburger she won't let herself eat. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" landed like a stone. That's a child being brought a toy. That last line was designed to make my stomach drop and it did its job.

So I'm suspicious of *everything* now. Is Pace tender, or is tenderness just the most efficient tool in the kit? Is Randi a predator or a girl handing off her own damage because being wanted from inside was "unsurvivable"? Both, I think, and that's what's got me. I want to watch Randi. I'm scared of what they do to Vee. And I'm uneasy that I'm being set up to "fall for it exactly as she does," because the jacket told me that's the plan and I can feel it working.

I'm in. But I'm in with my arms crossed, watching for the moment the book asks me to find the disregard romantic instead of just showing it to me.
