REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to text somebody about this book because I don't have a word for what it just did to me.

So last night I read the bench chapter thinking I was watching a dominant-but-careful man take a woman somewhere real, and that final "There you are, Randi" rearranged the whole thing — the woman behind the face, the bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail, the locked bathroom door, the decision to go back behind the face *forever*. That was the most alive writing I've read in ages. I was gutted for her. I thought Randi was the wounded heroine and Pace was the man who almost reached her.

And now this chapter quietly tells me: no. They're a *team*. The two of them are out on the quad on a beautiful Tuesday afternoon shopping for a girl. Pace points, Randi picks. He's running a game and the game is *selecting prey*, and the jacket told me this — "a secret choice made about her" — but reading it happen, watching him steer her through the mirrors and the matched pairs and the room-runners toward exactly the open, warm, unguarded one, is so much colder than the blurb made it sound. He knows her taste. He's *curating her hunger* so it lands where he wants it. That's not a man making room for a woman's desire. That's a man building the maze she thinks she's choosing to walk.

And then Vee. God, Vee. She comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown all the way back laughing, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — and that's the exact thing, the being-seen thing, that destroyed Randi in chapter one. They've picked the one girl on the quad who doesn't armor up, and the choreographed shoulder-bump, the "oh I'm so sorry" landing on *Vee's* mouth, the hand that stays a half-beat too long — it's a hunt executed by a trained operator and it looks like an accident. It looks like nothing. That's what scared me.

Here's my problem, and it's the interesting kind of problem. Everything I left the genre over is in this book — the plan the target doesn't know about, the yes that was engineered, courtship-as-trap. By rights I should be out. But the book is not *winking*. It's not asking me to find this adorable the way the bully books did. It *knows* it's showing me two predators, and it keeps Randi human while it does it — her bare "Yes" that arrives before she can dress it, the kitten voice snapping back over the top, the self-harm, the "I like her" correction that turns her own helpless want into a gift she's offering him so she doesn't have to own it. That's devastatingly precise. She can't want a woman for herself — unsurvivable — so she wants her *for them*. I understand her completely and I'm horrified by what she's become inside her own damage.

"Do you think she'll like the bench?" — that line put the whole tender aftercare scene from last night in a new light and I felt the floor tilt. The bench isn't a place he takes someone he loves. It's a recruitment tool.

So why am I still here? Because I genuinely cannot tell yet whether this book is complicit or clear-eyed, and that's its own kind of suspense. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." If it means that as an indictment, I'll follow it anywhere. If it's going to ask me to swoon as Vee gets eaten, I'm gone the second I feel the wink. Right now there's no wink. There's dread, and craft, and three people I can't stop watching. I want Vee's chapter *now* — not because I'm rooting for them, but because I'm scared for her and I can't look away.
