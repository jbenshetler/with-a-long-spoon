REACTION

Okay, so first: I clocked it. Randi is the girlfriend. The glamorous friend from the jacket who "steers Vee into Pace's path" — she's not some third party, she's *his*, she's been on that bench. That reframes everything from the first two chapters and it's making my skin prickle in a good-bad way. And now I've watched the actual origin scene. The "plan" the jacket keeps threatening — this is it being born, out loud, on a green quad on a pretty afternoon, two people picking a stranger out of a crowd like she's produce at the market. Vee, who I'm apparently going to love, is a *selection.* God.

Here's my problem, and it's the whole reason I read this genre and the whole reason it keeps breaking my heart: Chapter 1 was the best version of the thing I've been hunting for years. The safeword, "tell me anytime, I won't be upset," the checking on her wrists and her back, the *closed bathroom door he refused to open because it was hers* — the Daphne line, "loving someone was a kind of permission and he'd learned it wasn't." I sat up in bed at that. That is a man whose control makes room for her. That is the exact thing.

And now this same beautiful attentiveness gets pointed at a girl who has no idea she's in a room with anyone. "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That line is where my stomach dropped. Because in Chapter 1 he honored the *spoken* word above everything — and here he's decided the mouth is lying and the body is telling the truth, and he gets to be the one who decides which is which. That's the move. That's the exact move the whole genre makes to get around a no, and I *left* books for it. It's dressed up gorgeously here, it's with his established partner, there's a safeword somewhere in the architecture of them — but I felt the floor tilt.

And yet. What keeps me is that the book *knows.* It is not winking at me and pretending this is just cute. It's letting me feel Randi lit up and helpless and real — the bare "yes," the little footwork on the brick she didn't decide to do — while also making the whole thing quietly predatory. It's an honest deal: sit inside the seduction, feel its pull, *and* know exactly what it is. That's so much more grown-up than Fifty Shades ever was with me. The jacket told me "every yes was freely given, that was the problem," and I felt the problem land in this chapter for the first time as dread and not marketing copy.

Vee's entrance genuinely undid me a little. The burning maple, and then her — throwing her whole head back laughing like she forgot she could be seen. Of course that's the one. Of course the two people who armor everything about themselves would go hunting for the girl who *doesn't.* I want her. I'm scared for her. Both those things are true and I think the book wants them both true. What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the girlfriend is in on it and they PICKED the heroine off a lawn and I hate how much I want the next chapter."
