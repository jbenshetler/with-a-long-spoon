REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to sit with this one because the book just showed me its hand and I'm not sure I like what's in it.

So first — the chapter is *gorgeous*. I can't pretend it isn't. The campus walk, the burning maple, the wind in the leaves, the way he feeds her the game one girl at a time and lets a hundred yards go by with nothing in it. The pointing game itself is genuinely one of the hotter things I've read in a while, and it's not even a sex scene — it's two clothed people walking in daylight and I was lit up the whole way. The craft of it. The way Randi stops being "a good sport" a quarter mile back and starts *shopping* and doesn't know it. That coil in her body like a follow waiting for the lead to call the turn. The collision with Vee executed so cleanly it looks like Vee walked into *her*. I was grinning with her at the end, the little footwork on the brick. God help me, I was.

BUT. Here's where I have to stop and check myself, because something just reoriented under me and I think it reoriented the whole series.

This is the SAME Randi. The woman from chapter one on the bench, who begged in a voice that wasn't hers and then went into the bathroom and decided — *not ever* — to go back behind the face and never come out. The woman from chapter two lying under the inadequate duvet in last night's clothes not-crying. *That* Randi. And now she's the one doing the hunting. She's the predator. Pace is pointing and she's picking and the two of them are standing at a retaining wall choosing a girl off the dining hall steps like she's a dish Randi didn't order.

And the girl is Vee. The jacket told me Vee. *Vivienne "Vee" Thorne is twenty-one, a college junior, and quietly starving.* The one the whole book is supposedly for. And I just watched her get selected. Laughing with her head thrown all the way back, throat bare, "forgotten for the length of the laugh that she could be seen" — and that's precisely the instant two people decided to take her apart. The chapter even frames it as tender. Pace imagining Vee's first-woman face "and him there to watch her find out." It's lovingly written. It's *merciless*, exactly like the jacket promised — "psychologically merciless in its tenderness."

Here's my 1 a.m. text: *this book is making the seduction beautiful ON PURPOSE so I'll fall for it the way Vee's about to, and I can feel it doing it, and I don't know yet if that's the smartest thing I've ever read or if I'm about to be complicit in something I left the whole genre to get away from.*

Because — consent. My thing. The thing I came here for. And right now I genuinely cannot tell which book this is. Pace *does* ask. He watched for the answer on the bench, he used the word, he respected Randi's locked door, he carries real wounds about Daphne and not respecting a door once. That's the man I want. And yet the architecture of what he's doing — he and Randi plotting a girl who has no idea there's a plan, "a secret choice made about her," engineering a fake collision so a stranger apologizes for being hunted — that is the kidnapping-as-courtship move dressed in cashmere. The polite invitation. The invitation that reached her before she knew there was one.

What's keeping me honest here is that Randi is not a villain to me. She's the saddest person in the book. She begged, she broke, she decided never again, and now she's weaponizing exactly the thing that nearly undid her — she's going to do *to* Vee what Pace did to her, and some part of her thinks that keeps her safe behind the face. The "Do you think she'll like the bench?" at the end chilled me clean through. That's a woman handing forward the thing that hurt her so she doesn't have to be the one on the bench again.

So am I turned on or am I scared? Both, and that's the trap, and I know it's the trap, and I'm still turning the page. The jacket said "lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does." I am ahead of her. I am falling for it. I hate that it works.

What I'm watching for now — and this is load-bearing — is Vee herself, from the inside. The jacket promises her desire rendered from inside, hers, freely given. If I get Vee as a real person with a real appetite and real yes's, I can ride this a long way, even knowing the game under it, because then the horror and the tenderness are both true and the book is doing something I've never seen the genre do. But if Vee turns out to be a thing acted upon, a mark, a girl who exists to be walked into — then this gorgeous chapter is just the prettiest bait I've ever been offered, and I'll put it down and not feel bad.

Right now? I need the next chapter badly. I need to meet Vee from inside her own head. The whole thing hinges on it.
