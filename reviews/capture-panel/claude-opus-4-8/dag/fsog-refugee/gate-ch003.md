REACTION

Okay, so here's where the floor tilts. For two chapters I've been living inside a man's hands and a woman's cracked-open face, and I let myself half-believe the jacket was going to lie to me in the fun way — that "a secret choice made about her" was going to stay safely offstage while I got my intensity. And now chapter three quietly tells me: no, honey, you've been reading the wrong woman's seduction. Randi isn't the heroine. Randi's the *hunter.* And Vee — the redhead with the thrown-back laugh who forgot she could be seen — Vee is the apple being sliced thin on the cut side up so she'll eat faster.

That reframe is doing a LOT to me and I'm not sure yet whether it's the good kind of a lot.

Let me say what worked, because plenty did. The pointing game itself is genuinely one of the most intelligent erotic sequences I've read in ages — the three-beat landing of "what about her," propriety then confusion then the hunt, him watching it move through her body before her face catches up. "I point. You pick" is hot in exactly the way I come to this genre for: control that makes room, that hands her the choosing. And Pace reading her, always reading her — the kiss with her own taste still on his mouth, timing it so her breath catches — that's the attention I crave. He watches for the answer. The man watches.

And the writing of Randi wanting a woman *helplessly, ahead of herself* — the orgasm that outran anything his body was doing — that's the thing I actually want, desire rendered from the inside, hers before she can make it mean something safer. I felt that.

But here's my 1 a.m. text, and it's a worried one: **who am I rooting for and what is being done to whom?** Because the craft is seducing *me* the way the jacket promised — "lets the reader fall for it exactly as she does." Except now I understand the "she" was never only Vee. Pace and Randi are two closed, armored people doing something real to each other through the body of a stranger they pointed at by a dining hall. Vee apologized for being bumped into. Vee doesn't know she's prey. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — that line put ice down my spine, and I don't think the book winked when it did it. That's the tell I'm watching for. It didn't wink. It let the chill be a chill.

So I'm suspicious, but it's the engaged kind of suspicious, not the leaving kind. What I'm genuinely nervous about: Vee's consent. Everything gorgeous about Pace's "ask before he takes" only counts if the person being taken knows there's a game. Randi knows. Pace knows. Vee walked past laughing. The jacket keeps chanting "every yes freely given" like a man protesting too much, and I hear the protest. If this book is going to earn me, it has to make Vee's eventual yes as real and interior as Randi's begging was — or it has to KNOW that it can't, and be about that. Chapter one's bathroom scene made me think it knows. Randi going back behind the face, choosing it, "not ever" — that wasn't titillation, that was a wound. I'm still here because the book keeps showing me the cost.

It's been three chapters and I still haven't met Vee from the inside at all — she's a copper head at the far edge of the lawn, seen entirely through two predators' want. That's the gap I feel. I want her POV, badly, and I think the book is deliberately withholding it, and that withholding is either the smartest thing it's doing or the thing that's going to lose me.
