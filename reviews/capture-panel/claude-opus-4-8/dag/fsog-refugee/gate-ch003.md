REACTION

Okay. So now I know what I've got in my hands, and it's not what I thought two chapters ago. Chapter one I read Randi and Pace as the couple — the wounded girl and the man careful enough to find her, and that bench scene wrecked me a little, the way he checked and checked and gave her the room. Chapter two she goes home and bricks herself back up behind the face. And now *this*. Now I understand that the tenderness I fell for is the engine of a hunt, and the two of them are standing on the quad *shopping for a girl.*

And God help me it's good. The pointing game is one of the most genuinely erotic things I've read in ages and I hate that a little. Because it's not crude — it's him reading her, watching the "no, too expected," "no, they go everywhere together," feeling her stop being a good sport and start *shopping* a quarter mile before she'd admit it. The follow breaking frame before the lead calls the step. He kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth on the library walk and her composure cracks. That's the exact heat I read this genre for — a man wholly tuned to one woman — except the thing they're tuned toward is a stranger who has no idea she's being selected off a menu outside a dining hall.

And then Vee. The red hair sparking to copper, the head thrown all the way back laughing, "forgotten for the length of the laugh that she could be seen." Of course *that's* the one Randi can't look away from — the woman doing the exact thing Randi built her whole life to never do. That's not an accident, the book knows precisely what it's doing there, and it's the thing that keeps me from throwing it across the room. Because Randi isn't a villain having fun. Randi is picking the girl who is everything she strangled in herself, and she's grinning about it with her feet doing happy footwork on the brick, and it's the saddest predatory thing I've ever watched.

Here's my problem, and it's the old problem. A woman who doesn't know there's a plan. Two people making "a secret choice about her." That is the exact shape of the thing I leave books over — the heroine who exists to be acted upon. If Vee turns out to be a prop they move around a board, I'm gone and I won't feel bad about it. But the writing keeps promising me she won't be. It's rendering these two from so far inside that I have to believe it'll do the same for her, and the jacket swore she's got desire that's *hers.* I'm being asked to be ahead of her, complicit, falling for it as she will — and I knew that when I bought it. So I'm going to hold my nerve one more chapter and see if Vee gets to be a person.

What I'd text a friend at 1am: "the fake shoulder-bump was executed like a DANCE MOVE and the redhead apologized to HER. these people are monsters. I need the next chapter immediately." And then: the last line. *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* — with the bench being the thing that took Randi apart in chapter one. He doesn't answer. He says "let's go feed you." I actually put the book down on my chest for a second.
