REACTION

Oh, this one's quiet and it just about undid me. No scene, no hands, no kitchen — just a girl who can't sleep, naked in a cold room, holding a shirt that stopped working.

And here's the thing — this is the chapter I've been waiting the whole book for, and it finally crossed, and it crossed *inside her.* The shirt was always the tell, right? She washed it so it would smell like him, made him wear it all weekend, slept in a dying man-smell through the nights that weren't his. It was the sweetest object in the book and it was also the saddest, because it was her loving a surrogate. And tonight she lies there and does the arithmetic I've been doing from the outside for twenty chapters: *the warmth in the shirt is her own, given back. He wasn't in it.* She breathes him off her own shoulder and names every single thing he does — the things he makes, the rooms he keeps warm, the plate he sets down once he's built the hunger — and *none of it tells her what she is to him.* She said it herself. She finally said the whole indictment out loud to herself. God.

And then she takes it off — puts it on bare, skin to cloth, the real way — and it still doesn't work, because the problem was never the layer between her and the shirt. The problem is there's no one in the shirt. And then she takes it *all* off and lies naked in the cold and *stays there,* leaves the shirt in a heap where it fell, not folded, not back on the pillow. That detail wrecked me. This whole book she has curated herself into the exact artifact — the cropped photo, the makeup put back on before she'd be photographed, the drawer, the humble pie — and here, for the first time, she lets a thing he gave her just *lie* where it dropped. The curating stopped. She stopped performing even for herself, in the dark, with no one watching but Cassie asleep below.

What's different, and why I'm still here: the counter-voice isn't in the next room tonight. It isn't Cassie's dry mouth, it isn't Randi's black line, it isn't the folder I can see and she can't. It's *hers.* The dread has fully moved into her. She's the one who can't call him anything — not the big word he won't give and not even the small one, *boyfriend,* that goes "small and wrong before she'd finished the thought." That's the exit line I've been carrying moving the *other* way — I said the day the book asks me to be happy for her vanishing with no counter-voice anywhere, I'm gone. This is the opposite. The book is grieving right alongside me now. It is not winking. Nobody is dressing this disregard up as romantic. She is cold and bare and awake and the book is just letting her be cold.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she took the shirt off. she finally took the shirt off and lay there freezing and the book didn't rescue her and I think that's the most honest page in two volumes.*

I'm suspicious, still, always — Randi and Pace chose her together and the MIRA chapter just lit a fuse I'm waiting to hear go off, and the weight she's losing nobody's named. But tonight the book handed me the thing I actually trust: her own eyes, open in the dark, doing the math. I'd follow *that* anywhere. I want the next chapter badly, not because I think she'll get the word — because I think she's finally awake, and I want to see what an awake Vee does.
