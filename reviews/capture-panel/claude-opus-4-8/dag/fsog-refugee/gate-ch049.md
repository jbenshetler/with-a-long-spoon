REACTION

Oh, this one. This is the chapter I've been reading toward without knowing it was shaped like this — not the third game cashing, not Randi's whip, just a girl alone in a cold bunk at 3 a.m. stripping off a shirt because the warmth in it turned out to be her own. "It had promised him close and there was no one close." God. That's the whole book in a line, and it came from *inside her* for once, with nobody in the next room narrating her happiness at her.

Here's what gets me, coming off 47 and 48: the book has finally stopped handing her the gloss. For forty chapters her disappearing kept getting dressed up as radiance — she's glowing, she's starving out loud, she's never slept better. Every marker of her vanishing came wrapped in someone calling it bloom. And now, no wrapper. She's smaller, she's holding a dying smell, and the dread isn't Cassie's dry mouth or my own — it's *hers*, in her own chest, in the dark, with the one honest sentence she can't send because there's no text that makes it come clear. That's the counter-voice I kept waiting to see land where it belongs. It finally lives in her.

And the shirt. The whole arc of that shirt — handed to her in the rain, slept in every night she wasn't there, washed because it went to nothing, handed to him to refill ("smelling like me," which wrecked me as tenderness two chapters ago) — and here it is on the floor, "down in a heap where a dropped thing lands," not folded, not back on the pillow. She took the comfort object and she *refused it*. She chose the cold. That's the bravest and saddest thing she's done, because it means the lie stopped working and she won't make herself believe it anymore. The sawdust and salt, the rooms kept warm, the plate set down once the hunger's built — "all of it was in the cloth... and none of it told her what she was to him." She can name every single thing he *does* and not the one thing he *is* to her. That's the trap sprung clean, and she's lying in it awake.

What scares me now isn't Randi or Pace. It's that this is exactly the vulnerable-and-wide-open moment the plan would want — a girl stripped bare, sleepless, starving for a name nobody will give her. She's never been riper for the next door. The two who arranged her spring couldn't have built a better hunger than the one she's lying in right now, all by herself, thinking it's hers.

I didn't want to put it down for a second. Give me the next one.
