REACTION

Okay, so I'm reading this one with chapter 64 still sitting in my chest like a swallowed stone, and I can't un-know it. Two chapters ago I was in that bed with the two of them — Randi riding him and saying *she loves you, right,* and *she's not the only one in love,* and Pace saying *she kissed me first now, she looks at you too* — the whole machine humming in the open with Vee nowhere in the room. So now Vee's on a waxing table doing a thing Pace asked for and Randi coached her through, and the book lets me sit there knowing both of them have their hands on this and she thinks she's just getting good advice from her best friend. *Barely stings.* The glass of wine. *The most expensive thing in the room.* And not one single word about rolling over. That's the panty-by-the-plate again, smaller and meaner — Randi smooths the road and leaves out the toll, and Vee calls it *restful, being told,* Randi *always one road further along.* I wanted to reach into the booth from last chapter and shake her.

And yet. And *yet.* The thing this chapter does that no mafia-bully-captive book ever once did for me — the wrong hand. Sondra's palm coming down flat, *don't move,* not unkind, not anything, a hand keeping a part of her still so a job can be done to it and wanting nothing from her — and Vee's body knowing that grammar, learning it *in a warm dark room from a man who meant it,* and here's the shape of it with everything true scooped out of the middle, and it opens a hole in her a mile across where the right hand should be. That's it. That's the entire thing I've been trying to say I read for, written from the inside and backwards — she aches for the hand that holds her still *and wants her,* and the book knows the difference between the two hands, and it's the difference between what I left and what I've been hunting. I actually put the book down on my knee for a second.

The *will you,* too. He could just tell her. He makes it a question every time and waits — "that was the thing about him that undid her." I believe that undoes her. I'm undone by it a little myself. But the book won't let me only have that, because it immediately shows me the mechanism: *yes* out ahead of her, fear arriving too late to be any use, *that was nearly the point of it.* It's not winking at me. It's laying the gears bare. He asks, she says yes before her mind catches the sentence, and then she's alone with the cost. That's not disregard dressed as romance — that's the book being honest about exactly how a yes gets built, and letting it hurt.

Where my stomach dropped was the roll-over. *The part Randi hadn't said.* Already bare in front, already past every line, *another line seemed like a joke, a thing for a girl who had any lines left.* That's the erasure actually creeping in — no wanting in that one, nothing lit, nothing for him, just the plain fact of what she's letting be done. And she's alone, no Brooke, no Sheri, no Cassie to name the machine. That's the thing I swore I'd leave on. The only reason I didn't is that *her own voice* named it — the mother's kitchen voice, *the kind of girl who,* and her own flat accounting of saying yes to a whole she never asked the size of. The counter-voice is still in her body. As long as it's there I'm not being asked to applaud.

The last beat — her own hand on the smoothness, and the sound that wasn't pain, and then not thinking about her own hand at all, thinking about his, the right hand learning by touch what she went and did. God. I want that for her and I'm scared of it because I know who helped arrange it. Text to a friend at 1 a.m.: *she thinks Randi is the one road ahead of her. Randi is in his bed. And the book just made me cry about a hand on a wax table.*
