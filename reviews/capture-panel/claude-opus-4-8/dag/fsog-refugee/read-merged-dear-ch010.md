REACTION

Okay, so this was the exhale chapter, and I needed it — I didn't know how much I needed it until I was in it. After eight chapters of being ahead of Vee, of watching the trap get built around her while she glows, this little dorm room with the window cracked two inches and Cassie with her sock feet up was the first place I could breathe. And God help me, I liked them. Cassie is the only person in this whole book so far who isn't working an angle on Vee, and she's the one the book keeps nudging to the margins — declined the coffee, "Goodbye, Vee," gets waved off while Randi installs herself. I'm clinging to Cassie a little. She's my proxy. "That's not a boy, that's a man" — she's charmed too, which scares me, because if Cassie can be charmed by the froyo-and-tea routine then Vee never stood a chance.

What got me, actually got me, was Vee saying it plain: *"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose."* And the thing is — on the page, in the froyo and the tea and the tree, that's *true*. He didn't push. He left the door open and she walked through it. That's the exact thing I came to this genre wanting: a man whose control makes room for her instead of erasing her. If this were a different book I'd be melting. The little spin in the too-small room, taking out the closet door, coming down into the bean bag in a heap — that's joy, real girlish uncontainable joy, and I felt it with her.

Except. Except I've read chapters one through three. I know the froyo cup she built "like nobody had ever told her when to stop" got reported back to Randi on a couch while Randi ground a bottle cap into her own nailbed. I know "Her. Go meet her" happened on a quad before Vee ever felt a thing. So Vee telling Cassie *me, I did it, on purpose* — that's the cruelest beat in the book so far, and it's cruel precisely *because* she's right about her own feeling and wrong about the frame. The book is making me hold both. Her yes is real. The choosing is hers. And it was handed to her by two people who planned it. That's the whole engine of the thing, "every yes was freely given, that was the problem," and chapter 10 is the first time it genuinely hurt instead of just intriguing me.

"Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." Cassie asking what to tell the state troopers — half a joke, and the book knows it's half a joke, and I noticed it didn't let the joke be *only* a joke. Vee reciting her little dossier — *I know where he lives and everything* — as proof she's safe, when the dossier is four words and a direction. That landed wrong in my stomach on purpose. The hike. She's never hiked. Real shoes. He's taking her out west past the gravel to a trail only he knows. I don't love that. I don't love it at all, and I think I'm not supposed to.

What I'm tracking: it's been a while since Vee and Pace were alone with real heat — chapter 9 ended on that kiss under the tree, which was lovely, genuinely, the exception she made herself. This chapter kept them apart and I didn't mind, because the care between Vee and Cassie was on the page and that's the trade I'll always take. But I want the next one. I want to see the house off six-fourteen. I'm afraid of it and I want it, which is exactly where this book keeps putting me, and I'm a little annoyed at how well it works.
