REACTION

Okay. So here's what this chapter did to me, and it's not simple.

On the page itself? It's lovely. It's the loveliest he's been. The five nights away were *hers* — she called it, she needed to study, and he said of course, take the time, I'll be here, and didn't make her pay a cent for it. And I noticed the thing she noticed: some stupid corner of her wanted him to *fight*, to want the nights badly enough to say so, and he never says so, he only opens the door and waits. That's the whole man in one gesture and I've been clocking it for forty-five chapters now. He manages her toward her own best interest and never once says the word. He let her go study and she lasted five nights and came back anyway, and the book wants me to read that as grace. And it half is.

The undressing was fun — genuinely, the hopping out of the jeans one-legged, "you're not helping," "I'm helping," him catching her bra and not pulling, just holding it so she has to unwind out of it herself like something off a spool. That's him all over too: never force, just make the shape of the thing so she walks into it on her own nerve. It's hot and it's funny and I was in it.

And the shirt. God, the shirt. She washed his flannel because it stopped smelling like him and she wore it every night she wasn't here until it went to nothing, and she couldn't stand that, so she brings it back and makes him wear it all weekend so it smells like him again by Sunday. That got me right in the throat, same as it got her. He pulled it on over the shirt he was already wearing, two shirts in his own kitchen because she told him to. "Smelling like me." That's a turn in the bond, no two ways about it. If I'd read this chapter cold, with no memory, I'd have swooned clean.

But I don't get to read it cold. Because two chapters ago I sat in Randi's head and heard "God, I want her," heard the whole lawn-pointing confession, heard Pace say *she consents every time or I'm done* — which is the first time the book's ever let him say the quiet part, and it should reassure me and instead it terrified me, because a man who has to say that out loud is a man standing inside a thing that needs saying. And then chapter 44 put MIRANDA up on the screen, the fraud, the loans that kept nobody, "cheating bastards" said ragged and too near to be about strangers. So I come into chapter 45 with the next room absolutely *roaring*, and the chapter's answer is to close every door and give me nothing but tenderness and a dying shirt.

This is almost exactly my exit line — the book touching her tender with the plot asking me to just be happy for her. The reason I didn't put it down is that the counter-voice isn't gone, it's just moved offstage into me. The dread's doing the work now. "She'd have burned the house down for him" — that's the sentence I'd text a friend at 1 a.m., because that's not love talking, that's a girl who's already gone, and the book served it to me as the sweetest line in the chapter.

And I'm not letting that table go. "The long, unusually proportioned table that stood in the middle of his bedroom." Her thong landed on it and she never looked. Nobody has a weirdly proportioned table in their *bedroom*. That's the one more door the jacket promised, sitting in the room the whole time, and she threw her underwear at it without seeing it. I saw it.
