REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been circling. They were finally alone in a room — really alone, no ladle, no green Mercedes, no lunch table — and it was the warmest they've ever been, and it's the chapter that scared me most, and those two facts are the same fact. That's what this book keeps doing to me and it did it again clean.

The whole first stretch is just tender. The robe off the closet door, the quilt taken down without asking, his palm holding the weight of her breast "as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm" — I felt that in my chest. This is the intensity I came into the genre for, a man wholly there, and the chapter even names why it works: "you're all the way here, it's the lift, it's the same face." She figured out the thing about him herself, from inside her own body, and I love being given her desire as *her reading of him* instead of a camera on her. The cumin, the unopened saffron, Vivienne-means-trouble-in-one-mouth-and-love-in-the-other — he keeps going under the thing to the hand holding it, and I kept softening right alongside her. I'd have texted a friend: he asked her what her mother CALLS her and then heard the whole childhood in the answer. Nobody does that. Damn it.

And then "My real name is Peter." He took his mother out of the answer. The hall — they took the thing he loved and made it the punishment, and she said exactly that back to him and he looked at her like she'd found a place he hadn't offered. God. I was all the way in.

Then Daphne. Nineteen and he was fifteen. Two years. He laid it out in perfect order, every reason slotted, "nobody did anything wrong," "it would have been stranger if she'd stayed," and the good order *is the tell* — the book told me that outright and I could have kissed it for it. Because here is the thing that went cold and heavy in my stomach: he handed her the thing that hurt him and named it luck. That is EXACTLY what Randi does to Vee across every lunch table. "You're not caught, you're lucky." Pace was made by a Daphne. And now he and Randi are being a Daphne to a twenty-one-year-old who thinks she stumbled into all of it. The machine reproduces itself, and the book showed me the seam without once winking, without once asking me to find the grooming sexy — Vee holds "the clean ugly grown-up word" under her tongue *for me*, so I don't have to be the only one in the room who sees it. That's the whole reason I'm still here. The day it drops that counter-voice I'm gone, and it did not drop it. It handed me the dread itself, again.

The scar: "Not tonight" — a door with a time on it, not a wall. Still held, still baiting me, and I'll take the bait. And her last thought, the leaf and the stars, "one piece," "she had never been so fortunate, or so happy" — that landed as bliss and grief in the same breath, because I know what's stitched into her luck now.

This didn't turn me on. It broke my heart a little and tightened every wire I've got.
