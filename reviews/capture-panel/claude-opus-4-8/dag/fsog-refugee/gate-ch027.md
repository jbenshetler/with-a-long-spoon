REACTION

Oh, this is the one. This is the chapter I've been circling in my notes for twenty-six chapters — *does it get real for the two who started it* — and here it is, and the book didn't blink.

Randi stayed down. She ate. She put on his undershirt without hunting for the drawer, second one down, she *knew* which drawer, and pushed her hair out of the collar and stood in the doorway waiting for him like a woman who lives there some nights. Every one of those is a tell and I read every one of them cold, because I've watched her run Vee for twenty chapters and I know exactly what it costs her to be the one who's undefended in a room. And she was undefended. "She got past me before I could help it." "You always get bored — and it doesn't come." That's Randi with the seam split wide, and I've been *begging* for that seam, and now that I've got it I'm sick to my stomach, because the crack isn't remorse. It's competition.

Because here's the knife: Pace starts talking about what Vee does to a room — she comes in and it goes brighter, she has no idea she does it, she's the most alive person standing there and it never once occurs to her — and I was actually moved, that's the tenderness I read this genre *for*, a man seeing a woman that completely — and then the chair goes silent behind him and Randi's shoulders tighten under his shirt and she says "yeah, she is" a beat too late, and I got the chill down my own back. She's jealous. The woman who *built* this, who pointed across the quad, who fed Vee her own desire back over mimosas — she's lying there in his bed hearing him love the girl out loud and she can't stand it. And then she gets up and turns him by the hip and takes the shirt off and says *what she keeps under there, it's criminal* — she's using Vee's body to get back into his hands, she's fighting the girl for him with the girl's own image, and she doesn't even fully know she's doing it. That's the most naked I've ever seen her and it's the ugliest.

And Pace — "he didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." He clocked it. He knows. He knows she's cracking and he let it lie, the same way he lets Vee get there "on her own." He manages Randi exactly like he manages the mark. That's the thing that went cold in me.

What I keep turning over: the book still won't hand me the sweetness clean. There's real feeling in that bed now — that's not nothing, "she got past me before I could help it" is a genuine wound — but it laced the whole thing with dread instead of asking me to swoon. Two people falling into something true while running a con on the third person, and the con's about to eat them too. Nobody's happy in this chapter. Everybody's *fed* and nobody's happy. God, the title. "Gone." Vee's gone over Pace; Randi's gone somewhere she didn't plan to go; the shirt left on the floor.
