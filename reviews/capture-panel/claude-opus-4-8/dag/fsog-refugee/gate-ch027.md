Okay so first — the dance chapter was Vee's one real *no*. The only time in this whole book she's pushed back, been jealous, been ugly, been a person with a grievance instead of a girl being unwrapped. And I loved her for it even though she was wrong, because it was *hers*. And this chapter is the machine coming to clean it up. That's what I'm sitting with. Randi took the single messiest, most alive, most Vee-belongs-to-herself moment in the book and turned it over in her hands and handed it back polished: *you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about, that's not the disaster, that's the only thing that matters, and you handed it to him for free.* And it's true. That's the thing that keeps my skin crawling — it's *true*. Every word load-bearing. The jealousy did mean she was in it. And watching Vee's held breath let go "all at once — because Randi had said so"? That's the line that got in my stomach. Not because Randi lied. Because Vee's own verdict on her own night now needs Randi's signature to become real.

And then the timing. *Not today. Tomorrow night. So it reads like something you chose.* Randi is scheduling Vee's spontaneity. Manufacturing the appearance of a woman who "sat with herself for one day." And the *he won't text you, he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing* — the two of them are running the identical play from opposite ends and I keep watching the seams line up. She's telling Vee exactly what Pace is doing while she does the same thing, and calling it reading his character.

The shoe scene. God. I felt that one the way the book wanted me to and hated that I did. The long skirt over the whole operation so Vee *can't watch* — only feel — cool hands past the hem, the strap at the ankle, the thumb behind the knee, the hand high on the back of her thigh to steady her. And then the sentence: *being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less.* That's the dance lead. That's Pace's hand at her shoulder blade. The book just rhymed Randi's hands with Pace's frame on purpose — surrender rendered as steadiness, control as the thing that holds you up. They are teaching her the same lesson from both sides and it's the same lesson: *you're safer when you don't get a say.* That's the horror, laid clean, and at least the book is still laying it clean and not asking me to just swoon.

The "shoe whore" as an endearment. The scarf she'll be "tying herself tomorrow night" — Randi's hands over Vee's, walking her through it, so that the thing Vee does alone is still Randi's hands. *Dinner in Paris.* Another item she can't afford, except this time gifted, so there's no debit card to flinch at — the cost just disappears into affection, which is worse, because now she owes in the currency you can't count to Thursday.

And the kiss at the end — *she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. It was only what they did.* That's the acclimation beat and the book knows it's the acclimation beat. Two chapters ago Cassie's made bed was a tattletale; now Vee shuts the window Cassie cracks, folds the shirt away, edits herself smaller and smaller into these two people's shape. She keeps not-noticing how much she wants Randi and the book keeps *showing* me the not-noticing instead of hiding it.

I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she finally got mad, she finally had a spine for one night, and they smoothed it right back out and she thanked them for it.* Still no clean sweetness. The dread's still doing its own work. I'm in.
