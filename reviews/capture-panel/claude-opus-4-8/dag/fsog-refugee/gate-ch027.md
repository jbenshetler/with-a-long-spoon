Okay so first — I missed him. This whole chapter is Randi's, and I felt the room go dim where Pace usually is. After chapter 25, after *Peter* and the scar and "not tonight" with the door that had a time on it, I wanted the repair, I wanted his face. And that's exactly what scared me. Because this chapter is the machine, and it's *purring*. Watch what Randi does: she doesn't just comfort Vee, she takes the worst night of the girl's life and flips it over and hands it back shinier — "you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about," "you were so far ahead you couldn't see the race." And then — "the thing Vee had been holding clenched let go of her, all at once — *because Randi had said so.*" There it is. The book put it right on the page. Vee's own nervous system now reports to Randi. She can't self-soothe; she outsources it. That line is the whole trap in eight words and I'm grateful the book still says it out loud instead of just letting me enjoy the makeover.

The other thing Randi does that made the hair on my arms stand up: she narrates *Pace* to Vee. "He won't text you… he doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." She's coaching the mark on the con man's technique — because she's the co-author of it. And it plays as a wise girlfriend reading a good man. Both true, always both true, that's the thing this book won't stop doing to me.

The shoe scene. God. The long skirt over the whole operation so Vee can only *feel* it — cool hands past the hem, a strap closing, a hand up the outside of her thigh to hold her steady, the thumb pressed into the hollow behind her knee "on the way down." Vee's breath doing "something she hoped wasn't out loud." That's not friendship, that's a seduction conducted below the eyeline where the girl can be told it's shopping. And the tell was there — "her eyes went down and rested on Vee's bare feet… and something moved in her face, there and gone." Randi's want leaking. I still get to see it. That's the deal I keep making with this book.

And the freckles. Vee spends ten minutes at the sink burying "the cinnamon excess thrown across her own nose" and then sits across from Randi who has "a few freckles across her nose… she plainly never gave a thought to." The whole shame architecture of Vee in one detail, next to a woman who was simply *allowed* to exist. That got me more than the shoes did.

The kiss at the end — "she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed… It was only what they did." That's the wanting-Randi I've been tracking, and it's still landing as *planted*, not hers. She's being trained not to flinch. I clocked it and my stomach did the thing.

That's *close* to my exit. A heroine's anger treated as the sin. But — it was rendered all the way from inside her spiral, and the bar woman gave her real information, and Randi's the one turning it into fuel. So it read as the machine working, not the book winking that her anger was silly. If chapter 28 makes the reconciliation about Vee apologizing for having boundaries and Pace magnanimously forgiving her, I'm going to feel the floor tilt.

Still. It kept its nerve. The tell's still on the page. I want to see her walk into his door.
