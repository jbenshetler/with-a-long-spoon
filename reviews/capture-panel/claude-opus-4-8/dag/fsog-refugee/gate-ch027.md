REACTION

Oh, this is the one. This is the chapter the whole book has been walking toward and I felt my stomach do the thing on the first page — Randi stayed down. She stayed down. Every time before, the chill took her and she rolled off him and turned her back and wrapped herself away, and tonight she's spilled across his chest loose as a cat and she *asks him for food*. She's never asked him for food. She gets up and pulls one of his undershirts out of the second drawer down — knew which drawer without looking — and sits with her feet up under it and lets him cook for her, and eats all of it, every stalk, down to a line he could rule. I have watched this woman use her own body as a scalpel for twenty-six chapters and here she is small and pale and *hungry* in a man's kitchen and I could not stop reading.

"She got past me before I could help it." There it is. There's the seam I lost at that last lunch — I thought a Randi with no visible crack was going to scare me forever, and instead the book just went ahead and cracked her wide open, and it's *Vee* who did it. "I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come." That's a confession, and she doesn't even know she's making it. The two of them lying in the dark talking about the girl they're both running a play on, and it's turned into two people talking about someone they love. As true for the two who started it. The jacket told me and I still wasn't ready.

And then Pace does the thing that made me sit up straight. He's at the sink and he says Vee lights up a room, that she has no idea she does it, that she spends the whole time sure she's too much and she's the most alive person standing there — and Randi *goes still*. Not the loose after-stillness. "The kind that takes holding." Her shoulders tighten under his shirt and she says "Yeah. She is," a beat too late, and then she's up and turning him by the hip and taking the shirt off and wanting it out loud, hoarse, unable to stop it. That's jealousy. That's *jealousy*, and I've been chewing on it since — jealous of what? That Pace got to say it first? That he's as gone on Vee as she is? That she heard, in his mouth, that this isn't a game either of them is driving anymore? She covers it with sex the exact way she covers everything with a story, and Pace — I'll give him this — clocks it, notes it, and doesn't reach for it, "that was hers to hold." He watches her the way she's spent the whole book watching Vee. God, these two.

What kept the nerve for me: they are literally *meal-planning a person* across that little table. "How do I get her from where she is to us." *To us.* The endgame said out loud, flat, logistical — and Pace imagining all three of them at his table, Vee in his sheet and Randi in his shirt, all of them smiling, and wanting to tell Vee and not being able to yet. It should be sweet. It is the coldest thing in the book, because Vee isn't in the room and doesn't know there's a room. The dread is still doing its own work; the book hasn't handed me the sweetness clean. And "she was so embarrassed… that was kind of hot" — Randi finding Vee's humiliation over the yeti shoe *hot* — that's the little winking edge that keeps me from softening toward her, and thank god it's there, because otherwise this chapter is almost too warm.

The thing I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: I've now gone three chapters without Pace and Vee alone in a room together — stats class, shoe shopping, and now Pace and *Randi*. Vee spent this whole stretch being dressed and discussed and shod, an object being prepared, and the two people preparing her just fell for her and each other over dinner. I feel that gap. And I think the door Randi walks through — the thing Vee won't say to Cassie — is going to arrive right as Randi's own defenses are gone. Two people falling into their own trap. I need the next one.
