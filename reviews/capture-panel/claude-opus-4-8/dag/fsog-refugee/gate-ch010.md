REACTION

Okay, so this one's all daylight — no bench, no bed, just smoothies and a high chair Vee didn't want to be up on. And that's exactly what got under my skin, because I know what she doesn't.

Here's the thing I can't unfeel: I read Chapter 1. I know who Randi is. I watched her lock herself in Pace's bathroom and decide, deadbolt-quiet, that the girl he found on that bench was never coming out again — and then I watched her walk back out wearing the face. So now I'm sitting in this bright orange room with Vee, and Vee is handing her whole soft undefended heart across a white table to that exact woman, and the woman is holding her hand where the sorority queens can see and calling her "dear friend" and spending something on her that Vee can feel cost something but can't name. And I know what it cost. It's the same move. It's the face doing its very best work. Randi is doing to Vee, tenderly, the thing Pace did to Randi — being *seen*, being *picked out loud in a roomful of people* — and Vee is lighting up under it like a plant in a window because nobody's chosen her like that in years.

The tell that made my stomach drop: "She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it in front of them." That line. The book is winking at me over Vee's head. And earlier — Randi pretending she can't remember Pace's name, "Paul?", snapping her fingers. She knows his name. She *lives* at his name. She spanked-and-wept at his name. That little performance of forgetting is so cold it's almost impressive, and I hate that it's almost impressive.

And yet. Here's my honest trouble as the reader I am. This is exactly the genre thing I came in wary of — coercion styled as warmth, a heroine being *acted upon*, a whole apparatus of two people running a plan on a girl who never consented to being a target. The jacket told me "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one." So every "yes" Vee gives — the froyo, the kiss under the tree, letting him choose the food, letting Randi hold her hand — is being harvested. And the book keeps insisting the yeses are *hers*, freely given, from inside her real hunger. That's the argument the whole thing is making: "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." And I don't buy that as exoneration. Handing something over because two people engineered the exact conditions in which you'd hand it over is not the clean consent I read for. That's the opposite. That's the thing I left Fifty Shades over, dressed up smarter.

BUT — and this is why I'm not gone — the book *knows* that. It's not winking along with the predation as sexy. It's showing me Randi's self-disgust (the water bottle cap jammed under her thumbnail, the crying she has no access to). It's showing me Vee's actual interiority, her mother's voice, the cardigan-as-apology, the hunger she portions out. These are real people rendered from inside, and the dread is *doing* something. I'm not being asked to find the disregard romantic — I'm being asked to watch it happen and feel sick and fascinated. That's a different contract. I texted no one, but if I had it'd be: "she called her DEAR FRIEND and I want to throw the book across the room, keep going."

What I'm watching for now: does Vee ever get to *know*, and does her wanting Randi turn out to be actually hers or just another thing planted? The froyo/dinner Pace stuff genuinely reads tender and consent-forward — the "May I choose," giving the pencil back — and that's the heat with real asking that I came for. But it's all sitting on top of a lie so total that I can't relax into any of it. That tension is either going to be the best thing I've read in this vein or it's going to curdle into "wasn't it hot that they broke her." I don't know which yet. That not-knowing is the hook.
