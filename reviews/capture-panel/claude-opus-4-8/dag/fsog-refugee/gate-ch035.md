Okay so this is the chapter I've been circling — the quiet one, the "best of her life" one, laid down with no fight in it — and I went in with my hand on the door, because this is exactly the shape of the thing that would make me leave. Sweetness with the room emptied of everything but sweetness. And it didn't do it. It got right up to the line and it did not do it, and I need to say out loud how it kept me, because it was close and it was clever.

She says she loves him. She lets it be true in her own chest where she can't take it back, and then she lies there and *counts the ways he said it without saying it* — the door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the warm tomatoes. And I felt it, I did, that's a real inventory of a man's attention and it's the attention I read this whole genre for. But then: "he had never once said it to her." He hasn't. She's built a cathedral out of a silence. She's the one supplying the words — she always has been, she supplies the meaning for every gap he leaves, and he *lets the gap do the work*. "He never said. He never said." Three times in this chapter the book puts that in my hand. That's not the counter-voice scrubbed out. That's the counter-voice sewn right into the tenderness, so quiet you could read past it while your throat's pulling tight. My throat pulled tight AND I clocked it. Both. That's the lethal thing the jacket promised and it's doing it to me on purpose.

And then — *Peter.* She breathes his real name to the sleeping shape of him. Barely the breath of it. And I about came off the couch, because I'm the only one in this book carrying both names, and she doesn't know what she's holding. She doesn't know Peter is the groomed boy, the desk in the hallway, the two years he called luck. She thinks she's found her way to the intimate name. She's found her way to the wound and she's kissing it in the dark, and she thinks it's love, and it might even be love, and that's *worse*.

The weight-loss thing put a real chill down me. "Her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him." With a long spoon. That's the title, that's the whole title, and here's the body doing the eating-or-being-eaten in a detail so domestic it passes for a compliment — she eats like a field hand now and her clothes go *looser*. Something is consuming something. I don't think the book put that word "burning down" there by accident, not in a book this exact.

And the drawer. God, the drawer. That's the moment it got closest to owning me AND the moment it fed me the most dread in the same gesture, which is the trick, that's the entire trick of this book in one piece of furniture. He folds his whole ordered life down into the bottom drawer and gives her the top one — the easy one, the one your hand goes to first — laid out "like something prepared and waiting." *Prepared and waiting.* That's the jacket. That's "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one." A place made for her before she asked, room left for more of her than she's brought yet. It reads as the most generous thing a careful man could do and it reads as a cage being fitted, and I could not make it be only one of those, and I don't think I'm supposed to. "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead." That's beautiful. That's also how you make someone yours without ever saying a word they could hold you to. He never says. He never has to. She'll fill it in.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she said his real name to him while he slept and he'll never know and I want to scream, and he gave her a drawer and I don't know if I'm crying because it's the sweetest thing or because it's the door of the trap swinging shut on a hinge that's been oiled since chapter one.*

No Randi this chapter, and I notice the book let me breathe without her — which almost made me more nervous, like the play doesn't need the pointer in the room anymore because Vee's walking herself in now. That was the problem.

It has still not scrubbed the dread. That's the only reason I'm turning the page. The day it hands me this drawer with nothing underneath it — no Peter, no burning-down, no "he never said" — that's the day it's stopped being honest with me and started seducing me clean, and I'm gone. This chapter fed me. So I stay.
