REACTION

Okay, first: I came into this chapter braced. Last night Sheri walked in and she was supposed to be the blonde — the December midnight invention, tall and gold and everything Vee isn't — and instead she's five foot nothing with crooked makeup and a dead-serious appetite for Vee's legs, and the whole armory Vee buckled on just... had nowhere to stand. I loved that. I loved that the book built the rival up for a month and then dissolved her into a woman who came out last March and lost her whole tablecloth in a month and cried in the middle of a two-step. That's not a rival. That's the first person in this entire book who isn't inside the plan.

And that's the thing I keep circling this morning. Sheri *can't want him.* Vee clocks it herself at the end — "the only kind, she was sure now, the world had ever quite let him keep." A friend who's safe because the wanting is structurally off the table. And for one clean minute I thought, good, finally, one relationship in Pace's life with no trapdoor under it. Sheri's the one person I'd trust to actually see him.

But then the bread. God, the bread. He baked a cinnamon-raisin loaf from scratch while she slept off three ciders, left water where she'd reach for it with a paper towel under the glass so it wouldn't mark the wood, put his own undershirt over her head, kissed her forehead in the dark *after he thought she was already gone.* And I sat there feeling all of it — because that's the thing I read for, the man wholly focused, the care that's real in the room — and at the exact same time I could not stop hearing the second floor. Because I know how he learned to do all this. I watched him do the wrist-kiss to Randi. The tenderness is real AND it's a technique that runs on whoever's in the bed, and this chapter handed me the origin of both at once and refused to let me separate them.

The "one friend" reveal genuinely got me. Not one. Not ever. College at fifteen, off the end of every scale, the window that opens once and closed before he got near it. That's a real and awful thing and Rivers earned it — the arithmetic Vee does about her own people, Meg's closet and Kayla's fry tax and Cassie's *oh, Vee,* and Randi doing the triage on every heartbreak — one person can't be all four. And then the floor drops: Daphne was all four. The one, doing the work of everyone, in a building with no one his own age. And Vee *will not say the word,* still, she holds "the ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue and that she kept pressing back down," and she tries to hold it as he holds it, the best two years of his life, and she can't reconcile it and neither can I and THAT is the chapter not winking. That's the book refusing to let the tender version stand clean. "She'd had that much of him to begin with only because of the thing about it that was wrong." Thank you. That sentence is why my hand's still on the door instead of off it.

"Weren't safe or weren't welcoming." He doesn't say a name. Sixteen, not home since. *Less than staying.* I've been carrying that rock since the black-window night and here it is again, and it's doing real work — it's why he's like this, the attention, the showing up on porches, the headlights held on a door till the light comes on. He learned early that being *there* is the whole of what a body can do, because nobody was there for him.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the man has ONE friend and she's a tiny lesbian who came out in March and I am not okay." And then, quieter, the thing underneath — he's the loneliest person in this book and I finally understand how a Daphne happens to a boy like that, and understanding it is so much worse than not, because now the grooming and the ache live in the same sentence and I can't pull them apart.

No Randi this chapter, no sex, and I didn't miss either — they were alone in that kitchen the whole time and the care was thick on the page. That's the trade I said I'd always make.
