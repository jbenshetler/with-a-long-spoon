REACTION

Okay. Okay. There it is. Seventy chapters and the kiss finally lands, and I have to sit here honest about two things at once, because the book made me feel both and would not let me pick.

First thing: it was hot. Genuinely, down-low, hands-not-steady hot — and the reason it was hot is the only reason I'd ever let it be: it came from inside her. The smell arriving "from everywhere," the perfume she'd been "quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping the file," the sound that came up out of her before the question reached her, her own hand going into Randi's hair without asking permission. That's not a woman being kissed. That's a woman who wants it, rendered from the inside of the wanting, and the jacket promised me that — "that reaching is hers, too" — and the book kept the promise. If this had been Randi doing it to a blank Vee I'd have been gone by the second margarita. It wasn't. The want was hers. I believe the want.

And the second thing, which is why I want to throw the book across the room while I'm still clutching it: the want was hers and the *frame* was Randi's, every inch of it, and Randi stole the one thing that was supposed to be Vee's to keep — the knowing. Look at the machine. Randi hands her the skirt that's too short on purpose — "it'll look better on you than it does on me." Randi runs her hips wider on the floor, lifts her arms, opens her to the room, and then — *then* — tells her her panties are showing, manufactures the exposure and the shame in one breath, catches her chin, "they already looked, nobody minded," and hands her the Harrison excuse so there's a door to walk the kiss through. And after the realest thing that's happened to Vee all book, after she stopped performing entirely, Randi folds it back up small and gives it to her as a party trick: "I almost believed it. You put on a show." That's the panties-by-the-plate again. That's the exact thing that was done to Randi at her mother's table, done now to Vee, and called closeness. She took the biggest true thing in Vee's chest and told her it was a bit, so that Vee can't hold it, can't name it, can't bring it anywhere or ask for it again. "There was no version of the night in which she could ask." That's the crime. Not the kiss. The theft of the kiss's *name*.

And the bracelet. God. The band that's been kept out of the light for the whole book, and tonight it gets a name and the name is *Love*, and "taking it off requires tools." Locked on. A commitment she didn't choose standing in for the ones she can't say. The book just set that down in the street under a streetlight like it was nothing and I felt it in my teeth.

What's keeping me in the chair — and I checked, because this is the exact thing I said I'd leave over — is that there was still a voice. It was Vee's own, this time, no Sheri in the room, no Brooke naming it (Brooke's inside the machine now, all warmth, "any friend of Randi's," which is its own quiet horror). But Vee's body wouldn't take the folded version. She "found out twice over: once to the room, and once, worse, to herself." She knows "almost nothing" — not what she did, not what it was — and the not-knowing is on the page as a wound, not a triumph. The book is not asking me to call this her bravery and clap. It's letting me watch her get managed and letting her almost feel the hand doing it. As long as that ringing under the easy voice is on the page, I'm not being asked to find anyone's disregard romantic. I'm being shown it, with Vee half-seeing it too. That's the whole difference, and the book keeps finding it by a hair.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: SHE KISSED HER. And then told her it was a performance. I want to climb into the book and shake Vee and also I want the next chapter so badly I can't see straight, because tomorrow is *Pace's*, and she's carrying this, and she can't tell Randi and she won't tell Pace, and the two of them planned this between them before she was anything, and she is the only one at this table who still thinks there were only two games.
