Okay. So here's what I actually felt, sitting inside this one.

It's the first chapter in a long while where the two of them aren't alone in a room — it's Vee and Randi across a brunch table — and I clocked that gap before I could name it. And the chapter *knows* that's the engine now. Last volume this book kept handing me Pace, the door, the dress-that-hides-its-labor, and I kept my resistance up because every tender beat came with its shadow bolted on. This chapter does the thing I've been dreading since chapter 1, and it does it so cleanly I want to throw the book and also turn the page, which is exactly the trap.

Because watch what it is: Vee comes to brunch *happy*. She wants to talk about how good he is to her. And Randi — "I had him filed under spicy, and this is all very. Vanilla." — reaches in and turns the entire conversation into a draw-the-next-thing-and-the-next machine. "Walk me." "What was the bet." "He checked?" Every question is a hand on a zipper. And Vee's body climbs the whole morning, one confession at a time, across a *tablecloth*, in a glassed-in porch full of strangers' parents, until she has to get up and go to the bathroom and put her own fingers on herself — and *can't*, because Randi's face arrives and she can't be looked at like that and finish. That's the detail that gutted me. The looking has gotten so far inside her that it followed her into the stall and took her orgasm away from her. That's not seduction. That's occupation.

And then — "Sticky fingers?" Said out loud. And the chapter is honest enough not to tell me whether Randi knew. Vee can't tell whether she was handed a door or shown there never was one, and *neither can I*, and that ambiguity is the most skillful cruel thing in the chapter. The pastry she never touched, on her lip, at the end — Randi feeding her her own tongue, basically, the whole harvest in one image. "Best friend in the whole world" dropped at the exact second Vee's guard was down in the one direction she had no guard up. I know that move. I've read a hundred books that do that move and wink at me to find it sexy.

Here's the thing, though, and this is why I'm still here and not slamming the cover: the book is NOT winking. It's not asking me to swoon at Randi. It's rendering Vee's disappearance from *inside* her own delight and letting me feel the horror while she feels the warmth. That's the contract I made with this book — the day it touches Vee tender with no counter-voice in the room, I'm gone. This chapter is *all* counter-voice. The too-blue bathroom light "make her look like exactly what she was, someone caught." The book is on my side even while Randi isn't.

But — and I feel this in my chest — I am tired. This is the pattern now, and I can feel it as a pattern: Vee gets wound up, Vee flees to a bathroom, Vee can't name it. That's three bathrooms in four chapters. The heat is real but it's starting to feel like a loop I'm watching rather than a thing happening to me fresh. And I miss *him*. I miss the room with a door. The last two times Vee and Pace were alone it was genuinely the thing I came for — the "slow, for me," the *asking*, the juice, "wonderful" said like a fact for the record. Randi's table gives me dread with no tenderness underneath, by design, and after a whole chapter of it I want the other well.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she couldn't come in the bathroom because her friend's FACE showed up and she can't be looked at like that and finish — do you understand what that means about what's been done to her. and then the woman said "sticky fingers" and the book WON'T TELL ME if she knew. I hate her. I can't stop.*
