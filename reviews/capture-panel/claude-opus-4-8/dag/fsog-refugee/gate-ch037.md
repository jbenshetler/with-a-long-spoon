REACTION

Okay, the photo. That's the thing I sat up at. I've been dreading a lot of specific objects landing in this book — the credit-card bill, Cassie seeing, the scar's door opening — and I did not have "he asks her for a nude" on the list, and here it is, and I have to be honest about the two things fighting in my chest.

First thing: he *asked*. And when she started to get up to clean herself and make herself pretty he said "you don't have to, I like you exactly like this" — and then when she insisted, he *let* her have the version of herself she wanted, and then he took the picture the way he wanted anyway, posing her, kissing each place he set her. And she got to keep control of the ask — "only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." That's the surface of it and on the surface it's exactly the transaction I say I want: consent, warmth, a man delighted by her. The book even hands me her own "cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this," lets her clock the risk, and has her decide to trust him and calls *that* the brave part.

And that's the problem, isn't it. Because I know what this photo is. I've read the jacket. There's a house with a folder on her, there's a game, there are people who share things about Vee like a project. A nude that "he'd never share" is a *deliverable*. The book let her do the risk-assessment and then walk right past it because she believed his face — and I believe his face too, that's the horror, he probably means every word, he's a groomed boy who genuinely wouldn't share her — but the picture doesn't need *him* to betray her. It's on a phone in a house inside a web. I'm sitting here at 1 a.m. wanting to text a friend GIRL DO NOT SEND THE PHOTO and she already sent it and watched it go to Delivered. "Gave him herself to keep." That line is a knife.

So the wire is fully visible again and the book is not winking. It's letting me be scared while she's happy. That's the contract that's kept me here since chapter 25 and it held this chapter too — barely.

Then the second half, the kitchen, and this is where I actually leaned forward for a different reason. Thanksgiving. "Are you going home?" "This is my home." And she pushes, gently, and — "I don't go back." The level, exact, each-word-set-apart voice. That's the *door with a time on it* from the scar, that's Peter, that's the whole sealed wound showing its edge for one second and then closing. She takes her hand off it. And I ache, because that's the truest thing about him and she's the only person who's ever been let near it, and she doesn't push, and the not-pushing is the most loving thing anyone's done in this book in a while. That's real. I don't think that's the trap. That's just the wound.

But then Sheri's coming for Thanksgiving. And there's the little flicker in Vee — another woman at his table — and the book snuffs it out fast with "she's alone," everyone with nowhere to be. And I don't trust that snuffing. Sheri's a briefed watcher. Sheri reported on Vee at the bar. The two solitary people at the table, feeding each other — the book wants me to find it sweet and I find it *staged*. Not by Pace necessarily. By whoever's running this.

What I noticed most: she's losing weight and her clothes are looser and she reads it as a happy secret. "Her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him." That image sat wrong. That's not thriving, that's a body running hot on something. And "a girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it" — god, that's the exact thing I read this genre *for*, the shame turning to being-seen, and it's beautiful, and it's being spent on her by a man who is (whether he knows it or not) an instrument. The tenderness is real AND it's the mechanism. Still can't separate them. Still won't let me.

No Randi this chapter, and after chapter 34's pastry — the sweetness on her lip from a thing she never touched, oh that was sinister and gorgeous — I actually feel the absence. The Randi thread is where my real dread lives now.

Where I am: I'm still gripping the same thread. Her no still means something (chapters 30, the held hand; even here the ask-and-answer holds). The care still reads real. But a nude photo is now loose in the machine and I know it and she doesn't, and I'm turning the page mostly to find out who opens that phone.
