WHO'S WHO

Pace — the mathematician, finishing a PhD, lives out west of town off six-fourteen past where it goes to gravel, little house he keeps too warm, Steely Dan always low. Builds things with his hands (built the bench, built the good cabinet). Courtly in a way no man his age has any business being — "May I." "May I choose." "May I call you." Reads a person's body one degree at a time and waits for the real answer. The consent king. Also one of the two architects running the game. There's a Daphne in his past — he once went into someone's feelings uninvited, thought love was a permission slip, it cost him, and now a closed door is hers not his. That's his whole ethic and I can't tell if it's real or the most sophisticated lure I've ever seen.

Randi — black hair, startling blue eyes, built her whole life around being looked at for them. Sorority royalty, sits on Standards, has the single room. The OTHER architect — she picks, he points. But she's also the one who got cracked open on the bench in chapter one, begged in a voice that wasn't hers, cried, locked the bathroom, and decided to go back behind the face forever. She wants Vee helplessly and can't let herself own it, so she hands the wanting to Pace as a gift — "a threesome, with you" — because wanting a woman for herself is "unsurvivable." Presses bottle caps and the sharp edge of things into her own nail bed. Monster and most-caught-in-the-trap, both.

Vee (Vivienne Thorne, V.T. like the university) — the prey, and the one I love. Copper-red hair, freckles, lush and built like an athlete, covered to the collarbone on purpose. Econ, good at the applied math, the clean structure under things. Starving — literally portions herself, hears her mother's voice saying "that's plenty." The girl who threw her head back laughing on the quad like she'd forgotten she could be seen. Doesn't know there was ever a plan.

Cassie — Vee's best friend and roommate. Ball cap, navy suit older than the friendship, four words a day, the flat dry floor under every gladness. The ONLY person not working an angle on Vee. Said "Goodbye, Vee" like she meant the long version. My proxy. My lifeline. The book keeps nudging her to the margins and it scares me.

Brooke (chapter president, runs rooms without standing up), Sloane, Kinsey — sorority. The knife-fight-in-smiles crowd.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Chapter one is Pace and Randi on the bench — and the reveal at the end that the woman is Randi, not Vee. Chapter two, Randi seals herself back up, slept in her clothes and shoes. Then the two of them shop the quad for a girl and pick Vee ("Her." / "Do you think she'll like the bench?"). Then we drop into Vee's side: Randi engineers the collision, befriends her, Chi Latte, love-bombs her. Pace "happens" to cross the coffee shop and help with her calculus — gives her back the pencil at the exact line she can finish it herself, takes her number. Froyo date. Randi reports the hunt back to Pace on his couch (the froyo cup "like nobody had ever told her when to stop"). Pool chapter with Cassie — Vee's interiority, the water as the one thing never taken. Then the real dinner date: Lebanese place, "may I choose," the walk home, Vee makes an exception to her own no-kissing rule and kisses HIM, on purpose. Now she's told Cassie all of it, insisted the choice was hers, and agreed to go hiking — out to the house off six-fourteen she's never seen. That's the next thing. The house. The trail only he knows.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace — this is the knot. Everything he does is the exact thing I came back to this genre starving for, and I know it's the method. He ran the identical seeing-her-completely routine on Randi and it left her deciding to disappear. The consent I love is being used as the mechanism of a con. I can't root for him and I can't look away and I need to know if there's a real heart under the method or if that question even means anything for him.

Randi — I'm more tender toward her than I want to be. She's the villain and the most wounded thing in the book. The bottle cap under the nail. The want too big for her face, held level anyway.

Vee — I ache for her, fully. She's the most deserving and the most set up. Her yes is genuinely hers; that's the horror.

Cassie — I trust her completely and I'm bracing for the book to take her out or sideline her.

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

The house off six-fourteen. The hike. The trail only he knows. I'm afraid of it and I want it.
The one question that decides this whole book for me: does it stay clear-eyed — own what's being done to Vee, let her waking-up matter to HER — or does it eventually wink and ask me to find the con romantic, to be glad she got taken? The jacket swears it'll get "as true for the two who started it." If the care becomes real I'll forgive the slow build; if I feel the wink, I'm gone.
Does Pace actually know/run the game or is he the one truest thing in it? How much does he know about Randi's cost?
What Randi DOES with what happened to her — whether she's handing Vee the thing that ruined her, on purpose.
Does anyone catch him. Does Cassie stay. Please let Cassie be okay.
