REACTION

Okay, first: I exhaled. This chapter was almost entirely tender, and it was the good kind — the kind I keep saying I came here for — and I got to just *have* it for a stretch, and I'm suspicious of how good that felt.

The setup got me before the dress did. She brought the dress in her car. No overnight bag, "nothing for a night she wasn't letting herself plan on — and she'd brought the dress." That's the whole girl in one line. She won't admit the plan even to her own trunk, but the plan drives to his house sixty miles an hour. And what she does with it — this is the thing that actually turned me on, and I want to be honest that it did: *she* runs this. She sets him in the recliner. She puts her hand flat out and he sits back down. The wine stays where he set it. She "watched him make himself do it." That's the fantasy I've been starving for — his restraint offered up to her as a gift, the leash in *her* hand for once, him vibrating with the want to stand and choosing not to because she said. After thirty-two chapters of him folding her where he wants her, she folded him into a chair with one flat palm. I felt that in my chest.

And the two-costume structure is so smart and so *hers*. The dress is "for the world." The naked walk is "just for me." He gets it right — "sunrise and sunset," and then he chooses the one that's only his. That's the intimacy engine of this whole book working exactly the way the jacket promised: he sees her, the shame turns to heat under the gaze, and here she's the one staging the gaze. Ten steps down a hall in four-inch heels and nothing else, the blush going down and down, "and she did not cover one inch of it." God. That's a woman *owning* the exhibition instead of being caught in it. Nobody talked her into anything this chapter. She built it.

So why am I not all the way happy.

Because the dress crossing "a stage for everyone but its maker" is doing double work and I can feel the second layer. She gave the induction room the long stride *on purpose*, with a private heat nobody could name, and now she's re-performing it for the one man who wasn't there — and the whole gesture is her arranging her own life into a present for him. The reaching-first thing from last chapter, the "I miss you," the bringing-the-dress — she is pouring herself toward him faster and faster and the book keeps letting it read as her sovereignty. And it *is* her sovereignty. That's what unsettles me. The jacket told me flat out: "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over. Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." This chapter is that thesis in its prettiest dress. She is never more free than when she's walking naked into the exact spot they built for her, and I'm sitting here going *this is lovely* right on cue, "ahead of her from the start, falling for it exactly as she does." I know what I'm doing. I resent that I know.

But — and this is why I'm still here — there was no wire *in the room* tonight. No Randi voice, no briefed watcher, no consent-blur fed to her as an appetizer, no hand slipping past a no. He didn't even touch her. The most dominant thing he did was sit still. If the trap is closing, it closed with her doing all the walking and him doing none of the reaching, and that's the one version of this I can stomach — because the care read as real all the way through and nobody's disregard was dressed up as romance. Where's Randi, though. Where's Cassie. The quiet is the loudest thing in the frame, and I don't trust a quiet this warm in a book that told me it's merciless.

Also, low-key: he never saw the heels Randi buckled. She's wearing Randi's fingerprints into his lamplight and neither of them knows it but me. That little thread pulled at me the whole time.
