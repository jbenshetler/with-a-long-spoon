REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been bracing for since the jacket, and it came in exactly the shape of my dread, and I could not look away from it.

The whole chapter is a harvest and I watched every stalk of it come in. Randi doesn't *ask* Vee anything — she places things on the desk between them "to pick up later," she lets a beat go by, she goes cat-still. "Did you deserve it?" God. That's the whole machine in four words. She takes Vee's own account of a kitchen love-tap and, one tiny turn at a time, reframes it until Vee is agreeing — out loud, "a moment late" realizing what she agreed to — that yes, probably she deserved to be hit. And Randi's smile *brightening* on the agreement. "It went down through her like a swallow of something warm." That's the horror I named in my notes, delivered clean: she never lies, she never forces, she just builds the staircase and lets Vee climb it on her own legs and call the climb hers.

And then the proxy-seduction. The "mouse that roars," the soft-spoken man, over his knee, skirt up, count out loud, "and God, it made me buck" — she's not reminiscing, she's *dosing* her. Every piece watched landing on Vee's face. "On the word welcome something turned over low in Vee's belly." Randi talked a twenty-one-year-old wet in a piano closet and then picked up her bag and said "all better?" like she'd done nothing on earth — "warm as anything, as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And she couldn't tell whether she had." That last clause is the book handing me the knife handle-first. *I* can tell. That's the sick genius of it.

But here's why I'm still here, and I checked, because this is my exit line exactly — tenderness touching Vee with no counter-voice in the room. It didn't happen. The counter-voice is Vee herself, in the bathroom, with the bolt loud behind her, her own hand knowing the way and *her catching it and holding it flat to her knee.* "Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." That's a girl still fighting, still owning one wall, and the book stood with her at that wall instead of winking past it. The dread is still bolted on every beautiful beat. So the deal holds.

The thing that actually broke my heart, though — the reversal. For weeks it was Randi's hand at her elbow cutting her out of the herd. Now it's "Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist," Vee towing her, Vee saying *walk me out* only to Randi, Cassie talking to the side of her head. The harvest worked so well the crop reaches for the scythe now. And that held note at the end, the hug Vee didn't mean to hold — she put it down to being tired and full of him. I didn't. The door's open another thumb's width, and it's *her* hand on it, which is the worst and most exactly-what-was-promised thing.

I'd text a friend: *she literally talked the girl to the edge in a practice room and then said "all better? :)" I need to lie down.*
