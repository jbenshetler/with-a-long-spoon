REACTION

Okay, I braced for this one wrong. I came in with my hand already on the door — because on paper this is the chapter I've been telling myself I'd leave over. Warm. Sweet. Her and him alone, no Randi in the room, no "that's not finished," nobody banking anybody's words. A quiet house on Halloween, curry still on his breath, guitars low. The clean chapter. The one where the book stops supplying its own dread and just asks me to swoon. I read the whole thing with one eyebrow up going *is this it, is this the scrubbed-clean sweetness, am I gone.*

And then I noticed what she's wearing. The book didn't scrub the knife out of the room. It put her in it. The entire chapter is built around the *dress* — the thing he made, the thing that turned a hall full of strangers, the induction-surveillance object, the single most loaded thing in this whole book. It's been in her car since Friday, "fifty feet from the man who'd made it." She brings it in "against her body like something sleeping." That's not a clean scene. That's the counter-voice wearing burgundy. So no — I'm not out. The dread's still here, it just got dressed.

But here's the thing that actually got me, that I didn't expect: *she ran it.* For the first time in this whole book, Vee is the one holding the play. She scheduled it — "get your wine and go to the den, wait for me in the recliner." She put her hand out flat and *he sat back down.* She made him stay in the chair. She decided he doesn't get to guess, doesn't get to help, has to *choose.* All this time it's been Randi pointing and Pace watching-and-waiting and Vee walking toward the thing they set for her — and tonight she's the director and he's the audience she made hold still. The consent ran the other way for once. She handed *him* the room to want in, and made him take his time about the taking. That's the reversal I've been starving for in this genre, straight up: her desire, hers, run from inside, aimed.

And I loved it and it chilled me in the exact same breath, because — where did she *learn* that? The hand-out-flat, the make-him-wait, the two-costumes-choose-one, the give-and-take-back stride she stole off her own induction walk. That's a *play.* That's a Randi move. She's got a play now. Is that her waking up into her own power, or is that the house teaching her its manners? I genuinely can't tell, and that's the book being honest with me again instead of letting me fall clean.

The two costumes killed me though. The dress is "for the world." The nothing-but-the-heels is "just for me." And he picks the one that's only his — of course he does, that's the whole man, the thing no one else gets to see, the chest that's hers because she's the only one who sees it, now mirrored. Sunrise and sunset. I felt the swoon land and I *let* it, which is exactly the sensation I said I'd flee, and I checked it against the door and stayed, because it came off her nerve, not his taking. She walked ten steps down a hall in four-inch heels and nothing else, pink "down to where the mirror gave out," and *went anyway* — "knowing that, and going anyway, was the entire costume." That's her bravery, hers, and I'm not leaving the chapter where she's finally the brave one.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she directed HIM tonight, she made him sit, and I don't know if I'm proud of her or scared she's turning into the woman who built her.*
