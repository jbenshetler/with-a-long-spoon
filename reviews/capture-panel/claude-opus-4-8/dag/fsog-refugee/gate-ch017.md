REACTION

Okay. Okay. This is the chapter I've been bracing for and it did the thing and I'm sitting here with my jaw tight.

Because I *watched* it happen. That's what's killing me. The whole lunch is Randi running the exact play Pace runs, except where Pace uses geometry and food, Randi uses *me* — uses Vee's own hunger to be known. And it's the ugliest, most surgical version of it I've seen yet, because she's not extracting information for its own sake. She's harvesting. "Nobody measures over a bra." "It's just how it's done." She hands Vee permission and Vee hands her the secret, one small *yeah* at a time — "which hip," "the left one," "all the best ones start at the left hip," which means *nothing*, it's pure nonsense, it's a hypnotist's patter, and I could see the rhythm of it, the same closeness-touch-withdrawal cadence Pace used on the platform, except Randi's version withdraws on purpose right at the edge. "You don't have to tell me." The door closing softly *just* as Vee's deepest thing is halfway out of her mouth. That's not kindness. That's the most calculated cruelty in the book so far, because it teaches Vee that the wanting to give it is *hers*, that she reached for it, that Randi never pulled — Randi *never pulls*, she just leaves doors open and lets Vee walk through and blame her own feet. That's the jacket's whole thesis in one lunch. "Every open door is a temptation and every step is hers." I hate how well it's built.

And here's the thing that keeps me from throwing the book: the book is NOT winking. It gave me Randi's tell. The tight thing behind the smile. The thumb working the rim of the water glass — that's the bottle cap under the thumbnail from way back, that's her tell, and the book put it right there where I could see it and Vee couldn't. When Vee says "he looked at me and I was fine," Randi goes *tight*, and Vee — God, Vee — reaches past her own correct first instinct ("she thinks I'm awful") for the kinder read ("she's moved, she's happy for me") because she wants it to be true. And I know, I KNOW, it's the third thing, the thing neither of them names: Randi is jealous. Randi is *gutted*, watching this girl narrate the exact tenderness Randi got benched begging for in chapter one. The undrunk coffee. The tightness. She's the manipulator and she's bleeding out under the table at the same time and both are true and I ache for her and I want to shake her.

The kiss. The KISS. "just as Vee thought it was ending — not ending." That's the jacket promise arriving — the wanting-the-woman-beside-her — and it did NOT feel like Vee's. It felt planted. It felt like the next move. And then Vee doing her little translation — "French thing, that's just how women like her say goodbye" — explaining it away before her mouth is even her own again, that's the same reflex she uses on Pace, and then the cold turn: *did anyone see?* Scanning the dark windows. Counting them. That's Cassie's absence screaming at me. Where is my canary. Nobody's on the benches. Nobody's watching except us, the readers, who are ahead of her exactly as promised and I *hate* being ahead of her, it's the whole horror of the thing.

What I'm holding onto: the book still refuses to call this love. It's letting me see the machine. As long as it keeps showing me Randi's thumb on the glass, I'm in, because that means it knows what it's doing to Vee is a wound and isn't asking me to cheer. The second it stops showing me the tell — the second Randi's cost disappears and it's just seduction played straight — I'm gone. But this chapter kept its nerve. It's the darkest, most controlled thing I've read and it did not once ask me to find the disregard romantic. It asked me to watch a girl be loved right into a trap by two people, and to grieve it. I'll follow that. For now.
