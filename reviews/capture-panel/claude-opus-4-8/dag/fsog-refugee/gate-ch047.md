REACTION

Oh, this one hurt, and it hurt in the good way — the way that means the book has me, not the way that means I'm about to close it.

Because I just read the other side of this. I was *in the hotel room* with Randi three hours ago, watching her lie there next to a faultless man in a faultless room feeling absolutely nothing, "a good sandwich eaten fast," carrying the cold in with her and back out again. I watched her compose that exact text — "it's honestly a fairytale out here" — as a mask laid over a hollow. And now here's Vee in her childhood twin bed under the little gold swimmers, reading that same text as the truth, building a whole envied life out of it, "even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had." I know something she doesn't and it just about killed me. She's envying a night that meant nothing to the woman who had it. She's lying in the cold wanting Randi's snow, and Randi was lying in the cold wanting *what comes off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday.* Two women, two cold rooms, each one certain the other has the warm thing. That's the cruelest and best thing this book has done with structure, and I felt the whole ache of it land because I got both halves back to back.

And the room. God, the room. "It had known Vivienne — knew her still, in high resolution, and loved her." The ribbons, the synchronized-swimming trophies, the clarinet in the closet, the corkboard faces who haven't texted in a year. "The room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." That's the truest thing about going home at twenty-one I've read in ages — the room that fits a self you've outgrown, the ceiling coming down close. I've been that person in that bed. She's breathing bigger air now and came home to a place that no longer holds her.

But here's what I need to be honest with myself about, because I've got a line and I keep checking it against every chapter. This is a dread chapter, top to bottom. There's no sweetness in the present tense — Pace isn't even *in* it, he's a silence and an empty text field and a stove she can't see. What the chapter does is spend the whole fall's accumulated warmth as fuel for her spiral: she reaches for the fra diavolo night, the sheet slipping, the best night she has, "to be warm" — and it *turns* while she's in it, the hair goes gold and the leg goes tan and the blonde sits where she sat. She can't even keep her own memory. The reassurances people gave her — the bartender, "he doesn't take advantage," Randi's "he leaves you the room" — "went straight through her hands. The floor was gone." That's the book doing the thing it always does: it will not touch her sweetly without lacing dread through the same gesture. My exit line is *the day it touches her sweetly with the counter-voice scrubbed out.* It has still not done it. Not once.

So why am I not leaving? Because this isn't the book winking at coercion. This is the book showing me, dead clear-eyed, what the no-name thing *costs her* — and it's on her side while it does it. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." The book knows. It's not asking me to find her ache romantic; it's asking me to sit inside a real one. The thing I hunted years for — a heroine's desire rendered from inside, hers — this is the underside of it, the same interiority turned toward grief. And she still has her floor: she's the one who divided the induction photo, she sees she did it, she's not lying to herself about who went cold at the dance ("some back room of her knew"). She's spiraling but she's not deluded.

What I'm sitting up nights over now: she left his house crying in chapter 45, no word since, and she genuinely doesn't know if it's over — and neither do I. And the blonde she invented "out of spite" — I don't think there's a blonde. But there's Sheri, and there's a whole Christmas week she can't see into, and the book planted that on purpose. And the term starts and *Randi* is the sure thing, "a face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it" — and I know exactly how much Randi means it and in how many directions, and Vee doesn't. The circle's closing and both of them are too cold and too scared to say the true thing to each other. I have to know what breaks first.
