REACTION

Oh, Cassie's back. My canary's back and I felt my shoulders drop just seeing her fold her feet up under her and turn the whole chair around — that's the gesture, isn't it, the one clean thing in this book, a person giving you the entire front of her attention instead of a face pointed at you while she rests. I've been waiting fifty chapters for Cassie to wake up to what's being done to Vee. And here's the chapter where she's finally in the room, awake, all lamps on — and Vee lies to her.

That's what this chapter did to me. Not the warmth, though the warmth is real and I let it in. It's that the coat plan — the naked-under-the-wool drive through the January dark, Randi's heels, the whole engineered thing — gets handed to Cassie as *he shrank my cranberry sweater in the wash.* And Cassie, who "could hear the size of things," laughs the rare full laugh, and "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." God. That line. The one person built to correct her, and Vee has already made sure there's nothing to correct. She's doing to Cassie exactly what she did to her own vocabulary for Pace — shrinking the true thing down to a doll size so it fits in the room without tripping anything. The book knows it, too. "Corrected by nobody" is the counter-voice, quiet but on.

And still — I loved them together. The snowshoes in the house, the brother-in-law standing there "like she'd towed his car," the cookies that'd have been better made of Play-Doh, "the baby is a baby." Cassie's dry little dispatches from a normal Christmas are the funniest thing this book has given me in ages, and I needed the ordinary after two chapters of Vee being unmade on a cashmere blanket. The draft she missed. The polite trickle of the shared shower, three conditioners on the ledge, and her happy in it anyway. That's a girl in love, and it's rendered from inside her, which is the thing I stay for.

But the part that actually got me — got me low and specific, "a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck" — was Cassie's mother. *Thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again.* That's a whole family holding a door open for Vee that has nothing to do with the machine, nothing to do with Randi or Pace or any plan, and Vee can't even answer it at full size, says "tell her I said hi" and hears how small it comes out. There's a real life on offer there and she's already half-living somewhere else. I don't know if the book means that as the warm thing or the sad thing. Probably both, which is its whole trick.

What I clocked and am carrying: the swallowing thing from Saturday — the taste, the flat installed voice, "how many times, and how carefully" — did NOT come up here. She planned to bring it to Randi. So it's still live, still waiting under the affection, minor-key. And chapter 53's dinner, Pace wanting to meet *all* her people including Randi — that's the two architects finally in one room, and I want it badly. That's the collision I've been reading toward.

The hand on the cold cinderblock wall at the end — "the wall was only a wall" — after two nights of his heat at her back. She's fine, she's happy, Friday will come. And I believe her. That's why it aches.
