REACTION

Oh, he broke. Finally. A whole chapter inside him and it's not the smooth exact man with his arms folded holding a line — it's a hungover wreck mopping his own sick off the bathroom floor, bleach reaching the bedroom, folding Randi's cashmere blanket back where it lives (noted, by the way — *Randi's* blanket, in his bed, for the chill that takes her after; the book slid that in and I caught it), eating prison-food chicken out of a bag because his body used what it had and needs more. And then a truck comes up the highway and his whole body lunges toward the gravel cut before his brain catches up, and it's not her, and he folds over the table and sobs. I felt that. I'm not made of stone. That's the man a woman walks out on and he falls apart alone and doesn't text her because he knows there's no message that comes back in the shape of what she needs — which, God, is the *exact sentence Vee had in chapter 52*, both of them lying in cold rooms thinking the identical thing on the same dead night. The book put them on either end of that and it got me.

And here's the word, finally. The thing I've been waiting fifty chapters to have named. It's "I love you." And the rule turns out to be *Daphne* — the first everything, the one he said it to, who left and whose leaving ate his life, and so he taught himself the words were the difference, that keeping them unspoken is what keeps loss survivable. "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." That's a real sentence about a real wound and I believe the grief in it.

But — and I have to say the but, because it's the whole reason I'm still a sane reader of this book — this chapter is a *bid*. It's the book reaching across the table to take my hand and ask me to forgive him, and it's using Daphne to do it. And Daphne explains the silence. Daphne does not explain the *lawn*. Daphne does not explain the pointing, or "she consents every time or I'm done" said like a man auditing inventory, or him and Randi running Vee as a ledger on that couch, or the plan that predates Vee being anyone to either of them. The wound accounts for why he won't say the word. It does not account for the machine he's a part of, and this chapter very carefully doesn't go near the machine. So I'm sitting here genuinely moved and genuinely uncooled — both, probably both, as a certain mother would say. He loves her AND he's running something, and this chapter wants me to feel only the first one.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he CRIED over a truck. the word is "i love you" and it's about a dead first love named daphne and he thinks saying it is what kills you. and i believe every bit of it and i still don't trust him and I don't know what to DO with that.*

Underneath it all I'm counting: five chapters now since those two were in a room together. I feel the gap like a held breath. Three cold people on three ends of one New Year's — Randi faultless and empty beside a faultless man, Vee small in her outgrown bed, Pace drunk and sobbing over nothing. I want them back in a room so badly it's its own ache, which is exactly the thing I keep coming back to these books for.
