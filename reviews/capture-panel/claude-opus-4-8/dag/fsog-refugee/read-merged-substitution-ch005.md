REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where the whole machine clicks into place and I felt it in my stomach, not in a good way — in the way the book *wants* me to feel it, which is the worst part.

Because here's the thing. On its own, this is the exact scene I came into this genre for. A man crosses a room with his eyes on one woman the whole way. He reads her problem like it's new to him instead of trivial — and it IS trivial to him, he's finishing a PhD, he could've solved it in two lines and made her feel small, and he *didn't*. He asked what her professor said. He wrote a margin note instead of a rewrite. He handed her back the pencil at the exact line where she could finish it herself. "He had given her the question and given her back the pencil." God. That's it. That's the thing. That's control that makes room for her instead of erasing her. And "may I call you" — I heard it the way she heard it, a word out of another vocabulary. Four weeks ago I'd have swooned clean off my stool.

Except I know who this man is. I was *in his bedroom.* I watched him do this exact thing to Randi on a bench three chapters ago — the patience, the withholding, the "tell me what you want," the reading of a woman's body one degree at a time until she begged in a voice that wasn't her performing voice. The man is extraordinary at finding the real person under the surface and drawing her out. And he and Randi have *chosen Vee* like picking a girl off a path. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" He pointed, she picked, and now here he is running the opening move on the mark, and it's the same tenderness, the same craft, and I can't tell anymore whether the tenderness is real or whether that's even a coherable question for this man. That's what's making my skin crawl. The thing I want and the thing I fled are being served to me as the same dish and the book KNOWS it and keeps winking.

And Vee. Oh, Vee. She's doing the thing I did at twenty-one — trying on Randi's poise, the "V.T., like the university" bit she tucked away to use later, wanting to be a little more than herself because a man looked at her like she was the reason the day happened. And Randi sitting right there with her hand working the coffee cup, not drinking, twice up and down — jealous, or something colder, I can't read it yet and I don't like that I can't. The book shows me Randi's busy hand and then shows me Vee's brain supplying the wrong explanation ("she's bored"). That gap, where the reader knows and the girl doesn't, is the exact thing the jacket promised and it is genuinely sickening to sit inside.

Cassie. Cassie is my lifeline and I think the book knows that too. "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, last chapter — and now "he looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She sees it. She's the one person at the table not enchanted, and I am clinging to her, and I'm scared the book is going to get rid of her.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book is so good and I feel like I'm being groomed along with the girl. the man is everything I want and he's a predator and I can't look away and I hate it a little.*

I'm not stopping. I'm deep in. But what's keeping me isn't swoon anymore — it's dread, and dread for a character I like, which is a sharper hook than swoon ever was. I need to know if anyone catches him. I need Cassie to be okay.
