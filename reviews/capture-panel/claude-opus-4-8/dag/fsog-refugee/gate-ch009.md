REACTION

Okay, so here's the thing that's wrecking me about this chapter: on its own, it's the exact date I've been wanting a book to give me for years. The "May I choose?" over the menu. Him showering and shaving and wearing the *real* boots, not new ones. Leaving her the quiet in the text so she could carry it around instead of being made to answer on the phone like the other boys demanded. The hand on the walk home that feels less like a beginning than a *resolving* — her hand finally where it belongs. And that kiss. God. He stops where she stopped. He gives back the inch. "Whatever came next was hers too." A man who asks, who watches for the answer, whose control makes room for her. If I'd been handed this chapter cold, with no chapters 1 through 8, I'd be texting a friend at 1 a.m. saying *this is it, this is the one, read it.*

But I'm not reading it cold. I read Chapter 3. I watched them point at women on the quad like shopping. I watched Randi say *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* I know the froyo, the listening, the "what's drawing you to it," the giving-back-the-pencil — I know all of it is the *method*. I watched Pace do the exact same seeing-her-completely routine to Randi on that bench and then watched her lock herself in a bathroom and decide to go back behind her face forever. So when Vee thinks "she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong... the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there" — I wanted to scream at her, because *I* know the bill. I'm the reader the jacket promised would be "ahead of her from the start." And I am. And it's horrible. The tenderness is real AND it's a trap, and the book is making me feel both at full strength at once.

That's the sick genius of it and also the thing that's starting to curdle for me. Because here's my problem: the consent I came back to this genre *for* — the asking, the room to choose — is being used here as the mechanism of a con. "May I choose" isn't him making room for her; it's him running the play he and Randi designed. The choosing-is-erotic thing I love is being weaponized. And I don't yet know if the book knows that's horrifying or if it's going to ask me to find it romantic. The jacket says "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" and "every yes was freely given, that was the problem" — so I think it *knows*. I think Helen Rivers is doing this to me on purpose. Which is either going to be the best thing I've read in this genre or the thing that makes me feel complicit and sick enough to put it down.

Vee herself — I ache for her. The hunger stuff, eating without watching herself eat, the hollow that's been empty longer than since noon, losing the thread of what he's saying and nothing bad happening. That's a girl being fed for the first time in her life, literally and otherwise, and she's being fed *by a plan*. The "legs for days" bleachers chapter already told me what she's been starved of. So she's the most deserving person in the room and the most set-up, and that gap is the whole engine.

What keeps me reading is that Vee's desire *is* hers, rendered from inside, exactly how I like it — that kiss was her crossing her own line, her feet staying planted, her taking it. Nobody made her. That's real. What scares me is that "nobody made her" is precisely the point of the trap.
