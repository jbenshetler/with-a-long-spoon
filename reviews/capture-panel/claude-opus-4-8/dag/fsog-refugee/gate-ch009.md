REACTION

Okay, this one was a breather and I didn't mind it, because the thing I actually read for was sitting right in the middle of it: *Me. I stopped. I made him stop. And I kissed him.* That's the whole ballgame for me. He walked her away from the kiss when she gave him the starchy little rule, no lean, no charm-turned-up, no patient wait — and she came back across that gap on her own steam. And the first thing she does home is make sure Cassie *knows* it was hers. "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." I've been hunting for exactly that sentence in this genre for years — the heroine narrating her own desire as authored by her. When Vee says it, I feel it in my chest a little.

And then. And *then.* Because I'm not a fool and neither is this book — I read the first three chapters, I know Pace and Randi cooked this whole girl up between them over lunch, I know "Vee" was pointed at across a quad like a dish being ordered. So Vee spinning in her too-small room going *look at me* is not just sweet, it's got a cold draft under it, same as Cassie's window. She thinks the not-knowing-yet is "the best pile there was — the pile of things she'd get to find out." Oh, honey.

What's holding me here is Cassie. Cassie is *me.* "How much did he give you to drink." "What've you got on this man." "State troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop." She's the only person in this book with her nose up, and the book keeps letting her say the true scary thing and then letting Vee laugh it off and throw a pillow. That "Goodbye, Vee" from the lecture hall is still sitting in me and now here's Cassie repeating *off six-fourteen, off six-fourteen* like she's memorizing it for a report. I'd text a friend at 1am: *the loyal best friend keeps accidentally reciting the coordinates of the crime and everyone thinks she's being dry.* The isolation detail landed hard — past where the road goes to gravel, no one's seen the house, "he'll have water, he's not going to walk me off a cliff." Vee saying it to reassure. Me, hearing it and not reassured at all.

So I'm split, pleasantly. The romance is doing the exact tender-consent thing I want, and the machine underneath is tightening, and the one clear-eyed woman is being smiled at and ignored. That's a good place to be sitting.

What I notice underneath, though: it's been a beat since Pace and Vee were alone in a room. The kiss was last chapter and it was gorgeous, and now I've spent a whole chapter in the dorm. I want the hike. I want to see that house.
