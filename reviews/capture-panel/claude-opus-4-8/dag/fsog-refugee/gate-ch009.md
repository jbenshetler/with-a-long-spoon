REACTION

Okay so on the surface this is exactly the scene I keep saying I want. Vee spinning around her tiny dorm room, nearly taking out the closet door, going "look at *me*" — a girl who initiated her own kiss and is proud of it. "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That should be the whole ballgame for me. Her desire, hers, from inside, rendered warm. And Cassie's flat dry "how much did he give you to drink" is the exact right friend-question, the one that clears the air so Vee can plant the flag: no wine, just tea, I chose this. I love Cassie. Sock feet, chin on knees, "look at you," "borrow good ones." Cassie is the one person in this book who feels like she wants nothing from Vee, and every chapter she's the pocket of air.

But here's the thing, and it's why I'm not spinning around my own room — I *can't* read this the way Vee reads it, because I've been in Pace's bedroom and Randi's bathroom and the pointing game on the quad. I know she was chosen off a dining hall doorstep like a fruit at a market. I know "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" is the house with the *bench* in it. So Vee counting out her little dossier — "I know where he lives and everything," proud she has this much — landed on me like a stone. She thinks knowing the road is knowing the man. And the book *knows* I know. That's the whole architecture of this thing: her freely-given yes is real, and it's also the product she was manufactured to give. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." I keep feeling the jacket breathing on my neck.

And that's exactly the maneuver I'm most suspicious of in this whole genre — except here it's not coercion dressed as passion, it's something colder and smarter. Her consent is *genuine*. The care is genuine — the man who asks "may I choose," who leaves the room for her. That's the real thing I hunt for. And this book is handing it to me while whispering that the real thing was weaponized before she walked in. I don't know yet whether I trust Helen Rivers to make that mean something or whether I'm being set up to watch a girl get gutted and be told it was tender the whole way down.

What keeps me here is Vee herself. She's alive on the page. The starving-then-fed thing, the "I stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong," the way real shoes are a small proof she gets to *find things out* — she's a person, not a thing being acted upon, even though she is, structurally, a thing being acted upon. That tension is either going to be the most interesting book I've read in this genre or it's going to break my heart in a way I resent. Tonight it's still the good kind of dread. Chapter 9 itself is thin — it's a debrief, a breather, low heat — but it did its job: it made me ache for her precisely because she's happy.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "she keeps saying I CHOSE it and she DID and that's what's killing me, they picked her out at a coffee shop like produce and she's twirling in her dorm proud she knows his ROAD. I might not survive this book."
