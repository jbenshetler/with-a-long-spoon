REACTION

Okay so this chapter is the one where I finally felt the trap close a little, and the awful thing is it closed on *me* too, because I'm sitting here grinning at the froyo and the "may I choose" right along with Vee and the whole time there's a cold hand on the back of my neck going *you know what this is.*

That's the trick of it, isn't it. The jacket told me. Randi and Pace have "planned the best year of Vivienne Thorne's life. She doesn't know there's a plan." So every single warm thing in this chapter is double-exposed. When Vee spins around the tiny dorm room and knocks the closet door and lands in the bean bag going *look at me* — I loved her. I did. She's twenty-one and starving and a man was kind to her over bread and she kissed him on purpose and she's so proud she can't sit down. And then six days later she's up on the smoothie-bar shelf handing all of it to Randi, every soft private piece, "I stopped keeping track of myself," and Randi is *collecting* it. Taking inventory. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." That's not a girlfriend delighting in gossip, that's someone building a file. Mm. Mm. Every "mm" Randi gives her is a note being taken.

What I actually can't stop turning over is Cassie. Cassie is the only clean love in this whole book and she's being edged out of the frame in real time. "Goodbye, Vee" a couple chapters back, the whole-word goodbye that landed wrong. And here — the lecture ends and Vee catches a *look* go past her to Randi and by the time Cassie's eyes come back whatever was in them is "folded away." And then Vee does the thing that made me put the book down for a second: she explains, to herself, that the smoothie place "isn't really her place," makes the little hand-wave at the leggings, and decides it's *healthy* to have your own people. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." God. That's the sound of a girl talking herself out of her own best friend. Cassie's the one who asked how much he gave her to drink. Cassie's the one keeping the address for the state troopers. Cassie is the alarm system and Vee is quietly unplugging it because the alarm is inconvenient to the glow.

And the Pace stuff — this is what's keeping me honest, because taken alone it's *exactly* the thing I came back to this genre hoping to find. He left the choosing with her. He didn't take the kiss, he let her take it, twice, and left the last inch where it was. "May I." That's the man who asks and watches for the answer. If this were a clean book I'd be swooning. But I've read the earlier chapter — I know what he did to Randi on that bench, I know "do you think she'll like the bench," I know the courtliness is a technique he and his girlfriend are running on a lonely kid together. So the asking doesn't read as consent anymore. It reads as *bait*. Consent used as a lure is somehow worse than no consent at all, and this book knows that, and that line on the jacket — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — is starting to feel less like a tease and more like a thesis I'm going to have to watch get proven on this girl.

The Brooke beat was sharp. Vee shrinking to nothing in front of the finished women, and Randi spending real social capital — "Vee's my dear friend," the cool damp hand held on the table in plain view — and Vee feeling *picked.* I believe it cost Randi something. I also think Randi knows exactly what a public claiming does to a girl who's never been chosen out loud. It's grooming with a receipt. And the detail of Vee staring at Randi's expensive eyebrows until she catches herself — the drugstore tweezer, the shared bathroom mirror — that's the whole class ache of this book in one little hungry look.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this girl is being hunted by two people and the hunt looks exactly like finally being loved and I cannot tell if I want to save her or watch.* I'm scared of this book in the right way. Nobody's cruel. That's what's making my skin crawl. It's all tenderness, and the tenderness is the weapon.

The only thing holding me at arm's length: I've now had a full stretch where Vee and Pace aren't alone in a room — this was all Vee reporting and Randi harvesting. I want back in the room with the two of them, because that's where I can't tell the real care from the technique, and that confusion is the thing I actually can't stop reading.
