REACTION

Okay, this is the chapter that's going to keep me up, and not in the clean way. Because taken purely as itself — just this dinner, this walk, this girl and this man — it is *exactly* the thing I've been hunting for years. The "May I choose?" Three words and I felt the relief go through her and through me. A man who asks before he takes the small thing, the *food*, of all things, and then takes it seriously, weighs it, asks her the one real question — anything you won't eat — and builds the meal for her. And then at the tree, the rule: *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days.* And he doesn't lean, doesn't turn the charm up, doesn't do the patient little wait that's really just pressure wearing a nicer coat. He *smiles like she told him something true and fine about herself* and lets it close. He gives it up. And *she's* the one whose feet don't move, *she's* the one who pulls the hand taut and closes the distance. "I'm making an exception." God. That's the whole grammar I read for — his control making room for her instead of erasing her, her desire being hers, rendered from inside. I felt her hunger, both kinds. The froyo cup that got away from her, the hummus, the hollow that opened wider — a girl who's been portioning herself her whole life finally eating without watching herself do it. That undid me a little.

And here's the problem, and it's the book's problem on purpose, and I know it's on purpose, which is why I'm suspicious as hell: I *cannot* enjoy it clean. Because I read Chapter 7. I know Randi's on that couch drinking red wine out of a shared glass, *tell me, tell me, tell*, hungry for the report on this girl. I know "Her" was pointed at across a quad like a purchase. I know this dinner isn't a beginning, it's a *move*. So every tender, perfect, consent-drenched beat here — the quiet built into the text, the hand that felt like something *resolving* — lands double for me. Vee thinks she's being seen. And she *is* being seen, that's the merciless part, the care is genuinely on the page. But she's also being *played*, and the very things that make him safe-feeling are the instruments. The "may" she heard like a word from another vocabulary — I heard it too, and I loved it, and then I remembered what he did to Randi on the bench in chapter one, the *there you are, Randi*, and I got cold.

What I'm actually tracking: is this book going to let the realness win, or is it going to make me complicit in the trap and call it romance? The jacket keeps whispering "nothing was taken that she didn't hand over" like that settles it, and it doesn't, and the book knows it doesn't — "every yes was freely given, that was the problem." So they're not winking at me that coercion is sexy. They're doing something scarier: showing me tenderness that's *real* and *weaponized at the same time* and asking whether I can tell the difference. I can't fully, yet. That's what's got its hook in me.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he asked before he chose her dinner and let her keep her own no and I'm in love with him and he is absolutely going to destroy this girl and I need to know if the book is on her side or theirs.*
