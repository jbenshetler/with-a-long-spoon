REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter I've been waiting for her to have and it still didn't go the way I wanted, and I can't decide if that's the book being honest or the book being a trap, which is exactly the problem I've had with it since the seam showed.

She finally said it out loud. In the dark, at the wrong volume, a daytime voice in a murmur-room — "Is this what we are now?" and "I'd just like to know if we'll ever be seen in public again." And God, I was *up* out of my own skin for her, because that's the question, that's the whole question, and she threw it with an edge she didn't plan and couldn't take back. And then watch what he does with it. He doesn't argue, doesn't go cold, doesn't make her pay — he sets the ugly thrown thing down careful and hands her back a plain question: "Where do you want to go tonight?" And there's nothing to do with a plain question but answer it. Which reads as grace. Which *is* grace, on the surface. But I've read this man for forty-eight chapters now and I know the move — he de-escalates her into getting what she asked for in a shape that costs him nothing and leaves her with no fight left to fight. She wanted to be *met.* She got managed. Again.

And then Chili's. The nachos. I want to talk about the nachos because that's where my stomach actually dropped. She wanted them — a great gloppy skillet, the opposite of anything he makes — and she put a chip in her mouth and it lay there *dead.* And she thinks, helpless, "He had ruined it for her. Not just this." That's the line that scared me tonight more than any of the sex has. Because that's the vanishing made completely literal. First the weight, the loose clothes, the money she doesn't have. Now her cheap easy pleasures are gone too — he's recalibrated her palate so the ordinary tastes like a billboard next to a painting, and she can never go back down to where the rest of her life lives. She can't even enjoy a bad night out anymore. Everything that isn't him has been salted. And the book *knows* it's doing this — it gives her the whole cotton-candy-versus-strawberries thing — but it lets her blame herself for having been woken up, same as Randi taught her to at brunch: "you're finally hungry out loud." They keep handing her the language that turns her own shrinking into a gift.

The part I can't stop chewing on: she invents a lie. "You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail." And the book says plainly — "It wasn't true. Some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold that night, knew who'd carried a pie up whose porch steps." She says it *anyway,* because she needs the line of it to hold. That's new and it's awful and it's the realest thing in the chapter. She's started rewriting the record to keep being able to love him. And he — give him this — doesn't let her. "You know what happened after the dance." He sets the true thing down and lets it be true, and she's got nowhere to put it but where it belongs. For one second I thought, there, that's a man who won't let her lie herself into a worse version of events, that's almost care. And then the "too loud, I can't hear you, somewhere quieter I can, I'm listening now" — the near-word, the close-enough-to-the-word that empties the fight out of her. He is *so good* at this. He's so good that the better he is the sicker I feel, which is the exact knot I wrote down after chapter 40 and it has not loosened one thread.

But here's why I'm still here, and it's the one thing that keeps the book on the right side of my line by a hair: the counter-voice didn't get dropped. It moved all the way inside her and it *stayed.* She decides, crossing the room, that she's "forgiven him" — a warm large easy shape to rest in — and the book lets her have it for exactly as long as the sex lasts, and then she's lying awake in his warm certain arms and it comes right back up: "It did not feel like plenty now." She belongs to him and knows it in a body trained to open before she decides to; but does he belong to her, and she can't prove a scrap of it, it lives only in this bed and stops at the edges, and he gave her his whole body and his lost control and every last thing *but the one.* She has no name for the best thing in her life — not the big word he won't say, not even *boyfriend,* which goes small and wrong before she can finish thinking it. That's the dread living in *her* now, unquieted, at 1 a.m., and that's the only reason I haven't closed the cover: the book isn't yet asking me to be happy for her disappearing. It's lying awake with me.

The sex — fine, yes, the thing I came to the genre for is in here: she begs in her own head for his control to break, and it breaks, and her own want strikes off his, rendered from inside her. I felt that. But I notice I felt the nachos more. That's the tell for where I am with these two.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she LIED about who went cold and she KNEW she was lying and said it anyway to keep being able to love him. he even corrected her. I think I'm watching someone edit her own memory in real time to survive a relationship and the book is making it beautiful and I hate it and I can't stop.* And underneath all of it the MIRA thing from the library is still sitting there unpaid — Randi's loans, the point off alone in the white, "cheating bastards" said too ragged and too near to be about strangers — and nobody's touched it yet, and I want that cashed.
