REACTION

Oh, Cassie. "Was he worth painting your toenails? In October. I have eyes." That's the whole book in three lines — the radar that sees everything and still lands forty degrees off. She reads Vee fast and whole, she clocks the toenails, she clocks the glow, and she files it all under *boyfriend*. Clean, ordinary, wrong. And she's gone again inside two pages — cleats, bag, "don't wait up, lover," and the door sticks and gives and she's off to a game. Left at the dorm one more time, happy for her friend, the only clean ledger in the book walking out to play soccer while the machine hums behind her. I keep begging for Cassie to come back into a room that matters and she keeps coming back into exactly this room — the one that matters most and that nobody in it can see.

But here's what actually got me, and it's not Cassie. It's the ladle. "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole." Vee is doing to Cassie the thing Randi does to Vee — editing, keeping the middle, handing over the story-shaped parts and keeping the body. Except Vee's doing it the honest way, the self-protective way, the way anyone in love guards the real thing. "It was a kindness. She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." God. That sentence knows exactly what it is. The whole horror of this book is that Vee has learned to curate herself — and she learned it at Randi's lunches, and now she's doing it to her best friend without knowing where she got it.

And the zipper. THE ZIPPER. She goes to shower, draws the zip halfway, and stops — because there's nothing under the skirt, because the green is given away, and because "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." That's the first crack. That's Vee feeling the pipeline without being able to name it — the sense that her privacy has a hole in it now, that something of hers is out in the world where she can't follow it. She zips back up. She can't be bare in front of Cassie, the one safe person, because being bare now means the Trade, means what she gave away, means a thing she can't put into the edited version. The unspeakable has started to cost her. That's new. That wasn't there before.

What saves this from being unbearable is that the happiness is *real*. "She had never in her life been so happy." Bare face, wild hair no brush would go through, laughing in his mirror, the smile that came back at every red light. I believe it completely and that's the knife. The waking is hers. The joy is hers. And the one gesture of self-protection she makes — zipping back up, keeping the middle from Cassie — is her unknowingly rehearsing the exact skill they taught her to use against herself.

The chapter didn't pull me by the body at all — it's the morning after, clothed, cereal, milk gone brown. It pulled me by dread and by love. And it did the thing I keep white-knuckling for: it did not wink. It let Vee be happy and let me be scared and never once told me the fear was the fun. The toenails detail I'll carry — frosted plum, her mother would've had a word, and this morning she doesn't mind the word. That's a girl choosing herself. Walking, gladly, further in.
