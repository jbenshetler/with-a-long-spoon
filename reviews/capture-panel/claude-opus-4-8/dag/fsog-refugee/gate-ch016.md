Oh, this is the chapter. This is the one where the book stopped letting me hold it at arm's length.

I came in braced. I always come in braced with these two now — Randi's smoothie chapter had me grinding my teeth, the whole shelf-in-the-window thing, *dear friend* with the cool damp hand, the engineering of it so smooth you could watch the gears and still feel the warmth. And then the truck chapter wrecked me a little, Vee knocking on the glass and taking the wet shirt off on purpose, her desire so completely *hers* and so completely walked into a room someone built for her. And chapter 14 — God, chapter 14, Randi riding Pace and saying *tell me*, making him narrate Vee's chlorine hair and soft stomach like a drug she's dosing herself with, *I want us* torn out of her, and then she won't stay, she flees every time because staying is the thing that undid her on the bench. That one sat in my stomach cold. The architects are using each other now too and neither of them can say it.

So I came into the house chapter waiting for the wink. Waiting for the moment the book nudges me and says *isn't this romantic, aren't you glad.* And here's the thing that's got me by the throat at 1 a.m.: **I didn't feel the wink. I felt the dress.**

The closed bedroom door. He didn't open it. She leaned toward it — she wore the satin *for* that door, she dressed that morning for the door to open — and he said *my bedroom* flat as the kitchen and turned her back toward the light. And something in her dropped and I felt it drop with her, and I hated that I was relieved. Because that's the whole con, isn't it, that's the Daphne ethic weaponized — the closed door is hers not his — and I *know* it's the method and I still felt it land as care. He's not going to hand her a reason not to show him the satin. He's going to make her bring it to him. That's monstrous and it's also the exact thing I left the genre starving for and the book will not let me have one without the other.

And then the dress. Oh, the dress undid me. The russet she named on the rock, half-embarrassed, already let go of inside the same breath — *that sounds like a beautiful dress,* another cherry, talk moves on — and he went and found silk and it wasn't even the magazine shade, it was deeper, it was *mixed for her*, he matched *her* not the dress. The no-tag shirt finally making sense. He made the shirt. He licenses a patent that gets scared kids out of the MRI tube faster and the part he likes is the part that isn't about money. I sat there with her not knowing what to do with my whole chest. That's the line. That's exactly it. I didn't know what to do with mine either.

Here's what scares me and what's keeping me honest: *to do it right I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light.* There it is. The gift is real AND the gift requires her to stand and be looked at in her underthings in the bright porch light, and her one obstacle to the most generous thing anyone's ever done for her is *her own willingness to be looked at.* The shame turned into heat, exactly like the jacket promised. He hands her back the thing she threw away and the price is the surrender she's been circling since chapter one. Every open door is a temptation and every step is hers. I can see the machine. I can see every tooth of it. And I said yes with her. *Yes — of course, yes,* no hedge. I'm the reader ahead of her and I'm falling for it exactly as she does and the book TOLD me it would do this to me on the jacket and I walked in anyway.

What I notice is missing and I feel it like a cold spot: Cassie's one line, Randi nowhere, and the gap between Vee and Pace *alone, truly alone* is closing — this is the most time they've had in a room, and the book knows it, and it's spending it on a man building her a gown instead of taking her to bed. That restraint is the hottest thing it's done. The satin under her clothes, unseen, is a 3 all by itself.

I'm not okay and I'm not stopping. Text to a friend: *he MADE the dress. he matched her hair not the magazine. I know it's a trap I KNOW it's a trap why am I crying.*
