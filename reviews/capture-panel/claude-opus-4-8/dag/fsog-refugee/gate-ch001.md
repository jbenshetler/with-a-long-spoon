REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I have to sit with this for a second because I went into chapter one braced — the jacket told me there's a *plan*, that two people made "a secret choice about her," and I had my guard all the way up, waiting to be asked to find a kidnapping-adjacent thing romantic. And instead the first thing this book does is hand me the man I have been looking for for about six years of reading, and then pull the rug clean out from under me on the last page.

Because here's the thing — for forty minutes I was *in* it. Pace is exactly, precisely the thing. He sliced the apple thin because she eats thin slices. He turned his own chair because she circled her finger in the air. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." He took her hands off his body not to control her but so her hands would be *empty, not a distraction from what she had chosen.* That's the sentence that got me. That is the whole thing I left the mafia books to find — control that makes *room* for her instead of erasing her. He edges her, he denies her, he's completely in command, and not one ounce of it is cruelty. It's attention. The spanking is athletic and hot and I felt it, but what actually had me breathing wrong was him crouching down to her upside-down face going "Hi." "You with me?" The man checks in mid-scene and it's not a speed bump, it's the *hottest part.* I didn't know I could get that from a page anymore. Heat with the feeling welded into it.

AND THEN. It's Randi. The woman on the bench, the one I was watching come apart and cry and beg in a voice from when she was a child — that's *Randi.* The glamorous friend from the blurb, the one who's supposed to be running the game on Vee. The seducer is the one who got undone. And her response to being truly *seen* — not the blue eyes, not the face, *her* — is to lock the bathroom door and decide, quiet as a deadbolt going over, that she is never coming out from behind the face again. "He could have the face. The face is very good." God. That wrecked me a little. She presses the bottle cap into her nail bed where it'll hurt. She flinches when he says *you're beautiful.*

So what I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: THE PREDATOR IS THE PREY AND SHE KNOWS IT AND SHE'S GOING TO BURY IT AND I AM TERRIFIED FOR POOR VEE WHO HASN'T EVEN SHOWN UP YET. Because now I understand the jacket differently — "as true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew." Randi felt the real thing tonight and chose the lie. That's the merciless tenderness. I'm suspicious of Pace now too — who's Daphne, what door did he break down, and does *he* know this is a game, or is he the second mark? I can't tell if he's the dream man or the dream man being wielded as a weapon, and that not-knowing is exactly what's going to make me turn the page.

One flicker of unease: the book is clearly about to use this gorgeous, careful, consenting intimacy as the *mechanism* of a manipulation aimed at a girl who hasn't consented to the frame. I'm watching that knife. But the consent *inside the room* was so real, and Randi's interiority was rendered so completely from inside, that I trust this book knows exactly what it's doing with the gap. It's not winking. It's aching.
