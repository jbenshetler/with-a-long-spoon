REACTION

Okay. Okay. I need to text somebody about that last page.

So I went into this expecting Vee. The whole chapter I'm reading "her," "she," and I've got the blurb in my head — twenty-one, quietly starving, the sorority, the sliced apple, the portioning of every want — and I am *sure* this is Vee, and I'm settling in for the seduction the jacket promised. And it's good. God, it's good. This is the man I've been hunting for across a hundred disappointing books. He asks. He leaves the door unlocked and lets *her* turn the deadbolt. He turns the chair the way she circles her finger and admits — to himself, privately — that being directed by her pleases him. He checks her back, her shoulders, her wrists. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." A closed bathroom door he *will not open* because he learned, once, with a Daphne, what it costs to go in where you weren't invited. That's the whole thing I read for. Dominance that makes room instead of erasing. I was melting. The apple slices cut-side-up because she eats them faster that way — that's a man paying attention at a level nobody in my to-read pile bothers with.

And then: *"There you are, Randi."*

I actually sat back. It's not Vee. It's Randi. The glamorous friend. One of the two people who — per the jacket — planned the best year of a girl's life as a *game*, a secret choice made about her. So this whole tender, exact, consent-drenched scene I just fell into — this is one of the architects. And here's what turned the floor to water under me: the person running the game is, on that bench, being taken apart exactly the way she presumably plans to take Vee apart. She begs in a voice that isn't the sorority voice. She cries and doesn't know she's crying. And then — the part that actually chilled me — she presses the water-bottle cap under her thumbnail where it hurts, and she goes into the bathroom, locks a door she's never locked, and *decides*. He found the real one. And the real one looks at the found thing and says: no. Not tonight, not ever. He can have the face. She's going back behind it.

That's devastating and I did not expect literary erotica to hand it to me in chapter one. The tragedy is stacked so precisely: he's a genuinely careful man, quietly happy, deciding whether to offer her dinner — and he has *no idea* she just walked out of the room he opened and bolted it from the inside. He thinks he answered a hunger. She thinks she got caught, and she's fleeing.

Here's where my antennae are up, though. The jacket told me these two are the predators and Vee is the mark, and this chapter is doing something slippery — it's making me ache for Randi, making her the one who's really being seen and can't survive it. Am I being set up to find the architects sympathetic so the game against Vee goes down easier? Because that's the exact move that makes me leave a book — coercion with the book winking. But so far it isn't winking. Nobody here is styling cruelty as love. If anything it's the reverse: real care landing on someone who experiences being loved as a threat. That's not the same crime, and it's a lot more interesting.

What I noticed underneath: he's the constant. Whatever's coming for Vee, this man's methodology is *this* — patience, consent, the word, the door. If he does to Vee what he did tonight to Randi, and Vee (unlike Randi) doesn't flee it... I think I see the shape of the tragedy the series is building, and it's got Randi standing outside the window she can't climb through.

One a.m. text: "started that book. the tender consent-god scene? it's not even the heroine. it's the VILLAIN and she's the one who breaks. I'm in trouble."
