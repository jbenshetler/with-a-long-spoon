REACTION

Okay, first of all: the jacket told me this was Vee's book, and then the very first chapter puts me in a room with two people who are NOT Vee, doing the thing, and it takes me the whole chapter to understand that the woman on the bench is *Randi* — the friend, the planner, the one steering. That reveal at "There you are, Randi" landed like a hand on the back of my neck. I actually sat up.

And here's my problem, the good kind: the *scene itself* is exactly what I've spent years looking for and mostly not finding. He asks. He checks. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset." The apple sliced thin because he *watched how she eats*. The door she locks behind her and the door she locks in the bathroom that he will not open because some woman named Daphne taught him what it costs to go in where you weren't invited. That's the man I read for — the control that makes room instead of erasing. The undressing on one knee, the not-being-in-a-hurry, the "you're doing so well" that wasn't a line. I was warm the whole way through. The bench itself, the way she *read* it before he explained it, everything-open — that was genuinely erotic and it was hers, she said *show me*.

But then the last third turned the lights on and I went cold in a way I think the book absolutely intended. Because that tenderness, that seeing-past-the-face — she can't survive it. The bathroom sequence, the face that has been lying for years, "he could have the face, the one he found is going back behind it and she is not coming out again" — God. And the *water bottle cap under the thumbnail*, that she doesn't know she's doing. That's not sexy dissociation dressed up. That's a wound. This woman is genuinely hurt and genuinely dangerous, and the thing that undid her is going to be the tool she aims at Vee. The care I was melting over is the *weapon*. That's the merciless-in-its-tenderness the jacket promised, and I hate that it works on me, and it works on me.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the consent is PERFECT and that's the scariest thing about it. he's the real thing and she's going to use him on a 21-year-old and I already can't stop." Also: Pace right now reads as genuinely kind and a little bit oblivious — he thinks he had "a remarkably good evening," he has no idea he just broke something. I don't yet know if he's in on the plan or if he's a tool too. That's a hook.

What I'm suspicious of: whether the book is going to keep the consent this honest when it gets to Vee, who *doesn't know there's a plan*. That's the whole test for me. If the care stays real and the horror stays *named as horror* instead of winked at, I'm all in. If it starts asking me to find the disregard romantic, I'm gone. But this chapter didn't wink. It knew exactly how bad it was. That's why I trust it enough to turn the page.
