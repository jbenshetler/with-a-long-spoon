REACTION

Okay. Okay. I went in braced — the jacket already told me two people are running a game on some girl named Vee, and "psychologically merciless" plus "a secret choice made about her" had me side-eyeing every page before I started. So I spent the whole chapter waiting to catch the trick being played on the innocent one.

And then the last section just reached up and reorganized the furniture in my head. It's *Randi*. The woman on the bench is one of the two architects. He didn't say her name until she'd already cried on the leather and begged in a voice that wasn't hers, and when he said "There you are, Randi" I actually sat up. Because the whole book is supposedly her hunting Vee — and here she is getting caught in the exact machinery she's building. The predator cracked open like an egg. I did not expect to meet her first, and I did not expect to feel for her.

On the craft of the thing that matters to me most — the consent — this is almost unnervingly the thing I left the genre looking for. He checks her back, her hands, her shoulders. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." He takes her heels off mid-scene because he's paying attention to her body as a body, not a prop. And the Daphne beat — that he once went into someone's feelings uninvited because he thought love was a permission slip, and it cost him, and now a closed door is *hers* — that's the whole ethic I've been starving for, written right on the page. When she locks the bathroom door and he doesn't knock, I exhaled. A man whose control makes room for her instead of erasing her. That's the sentence from my own wishlist and somebody wrote it into a man.

The heat worked on me. The apple-slicing — noticing she eats thin slices faster cut-side-up, the whole portioned-appetite thing — that undid me more than the spanking did, honestly. It's attention as an erotic act. The edging, the "I'm not stopping, I'm taking my time," her running out of the apparatus of performance until the *please* came "from below" — I was inside it. That's desire rendered from the inside, even if the inside we're given is his read of her.

BUT. Here's the splinter I can't pull out, and it's the reason my stomach is uneasy even while my pulse is up. This is all from *his* POV, and he is so sure he's done a beautiful thing. "All he had done was find it, and answer it." And meanwhile the book shows me what he can't see: she's retreating forever, "not ever," the face going back on like a deadbolt, pressing the water-bottle edge into her nail bed where it'll hurt. He thinks he liberated her. She experienced it as a catastrophe she has to hide. And the book *knows* the gap is there — it's showing me both. So the very tenderness I'm swooning at is also being set up as the thing that's going to wreck somebody, and I can't tell yet whether the book is going to honor that consent ethic or reveal it as the most sophisticated trap in the whole genre. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." I'm scared of this book in a way I respect.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the villain is the one who got topped and she's TERRIFIED because he actually saw her and now I don't know who to root for and the man is a green-flag consent king which somehow makes it WORSE, what is this book.*
