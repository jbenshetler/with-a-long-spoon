REACTION

Okay. I did not expect to open this book and land face-down on a bench with the wrong woman. I came in braced for Vee — the blurb sold me Vee — and instead the first thing this book does is put me inside the one it called the glamorous friend, and take her *apart*. That's a hell of an opening move and I felt it.

On the pure body level: this is the thing I've been hunting for years and mostly not finding. A man who slices the apple thin because she'll only eat it thin, cut-sides up because she eats faster that way. Who turns his own chair when she circles a finger, and lets himself be pleased by being directed. Who says "tell me your word" and "tell me again if you forget, I won't be upset." Who checks her back, her shoulders, her hands — and when she snaps "they're *fine*, Pace," he grins instead of punishing her for it. Who leaves the water bottle out *in advance* because he's learned she drinks more than she thinks. That's not choreography, that's attention, and attention is the whole thing for me. His control made *room* for her instead of erasing her — that's literally the plot of the scene: he waited her out until the real one came out from behind the pretty face. The "there you are." I felt that in my sternum.

And yet. This is the part that's got me sitting up at 1 a.m. The book knows exactly what it's doing and it is not comfortable. I'm ahead of these people and it *wants* me ahead. Pace ends the night quietly happy, thinking he fed a starving girl and he'll do it again as often as she brings it to him — and she's in the bathroom deciding she will *never* let herself be seen again, pressing a bottle cap under her thumbnail without knowing she's doing it, arranging the lying smile back onto her face. The flinch at "you're beautiful." That gap between what he thinks happened and what happened to her — that's the "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" the jacket promised, and it's real. It's the tenderness itself that broke her open, and the tenderness is what she's fleeing.

So I'm turned on and I'm scared, and crucially the scared isn't my usual leave-the-book scared. It's not coercion dressed as romance. He *stopped* at the locked door — the Daphne backstory, "a closed door was hers, not his" — he's the rare man who won't go in where he wasn't asked. The unease is smarter than that: these two are running a game on Vee, and here's one of the two, and she's not a smug manipulator having fun, she's *this* wounded. That reframes everything. What did it do to her, to be the one who watched from outside? What happens when they turn this on a twenty-one-year-old who really doesn't know there's a plan?

I'd text a friend: "the guy is almost too perfect and that's the point, the book is going to make me fall for it exactly while showing me the damage, and I can't tell yet if I trust it — but I can't stop."

What I'm watching for: I need the heat to keep meaning something between particular people, and I need the book to stay on the side of the women's interiority the way it just was with Randi. If Pace's care ever curdles into a tool that erases her rather than reaches her, I'm gone. Tonight it reached her. So I'm in.
