REACTION

Okay, first thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *it's not Vee. The whole chapter I thought I was reading the girl from the back cover and it's the other one — it's Randi.* That landing, "There you are, Randi," went through me like cold water. The book let me sit inside this woman as *she* the way the jacket promised I'd sit inside Vee, and then named her as the huntress. So now I don't know who's holding the knife and who's on the table, and I love being wrong-footed like that this early.

And God — the consent. This is the thing I've been digging through this whole genre for and mostly coming up empty. He asks. He *waits* for the answer. "Tell me your word." "Red." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." The turned chair, letting her direct him, letting the kiss run as long as she wanted to run it, backing off and letting her come get what she came for. A man whose control makes *room* for her instead of erasing her — it's literally on the page. The apple sliced thin because she'll eat thin and not thick, cut sides up because she eats faster that way. That's attention as devotion, and it's rendered from the inside of his noticing. I felt the heat AND the care, both, which almost never happens for me.

But here's why I'm not just swooning — I'm scared, and I think the book wants me scared. Because all this exquisite care is being administered by one of the two people who, per the jacket, are running a *game* on some other girl. So the tenderness isn't the point of the tenderness. It's a technology. He's very, very good at it, and he's about to point it at a twenty-one-year-old who doesn't know there's a plan. That reframes every beautiful thing he does here as a demonstration of the machine's precision. Merciless in its tenderness — yeah.

And then the aftermath just gutted me and unsettled me at once. Randi behind the locked door — a lock she's never turned before — pressing the bottle cap into her nail bed and not knowing she's doing it. The decision, quiet as a deadbolt: *he can have the face. She's not coming out again.* That's not aftercare glow, that's a wound. He found her by seeing past the mask and it was the most alive she's ever been and it terrified her so badly she's sealing the door forever. That's genuinely tragic and it makes *her* a person, not a plot device — which, given she's supposed to be the manipulator, is a hell of a move. I did not expect to ache for the seductress.

The one thing keeping my guard half up: I need to know the book isn't going to ask me to find the *predation* romantic later, the way it's (rightfully) making the consent romantic now. Right now the craft of consent is real and I trust it on the page. But the frame is a con, and I'm watching to see whether Vee's yeses get the same real "tell me your word" respect, or whether that's the bait. If the care curdles into something the book winks at, I'm gone. Tonight, though? I'm all the way in.
