REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to text somebody, except it's 1 a.m. and this is the first chapter and I have no one to text yet, so it's going in here.

First: I came for this book braced. The jacket told me two people planned a girl's seduction like a *game*, "a secret choice made about her," and everything in me went cold and ready to hate it, because that's the exact machinery I've walked away from a hundred times — the woman as a thing done to, the book winking while it happens. And then the chapter opens and hands me the most *careful* man I've read in ages. The apple sliced thin because she'll eat thin and not thick, cut-sides up because she eats faster that way. The lights-up thing. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." He checks her back, her shoulders, her wrists — and when she snaps *they're FINE, Pace*, he just smiles. That's it. That's the thing I've been hunting for years. A man whose control makes *room*. He backs off the hello-kiss and lets her decide whether to make it more. He takes her hands out of his hair not to restrain her but so her hands are empty for what *she* chose. I was actually turned on and, weirdly, soothed, which almost never happens at the same time.

And THEN the floor drops out. It's not Vee on that bench. It's *Randi*. It's the co-conspirator. The one who's supposed to be running the game on the poor girl in the blurb — and here she is, red and wet and facedown, begging in a voice that isn't hers, and crying afterward and not knowing she's crying. That reframe is nasty in the best way. Because the whole time he's being so tender, she's doing this quiet, terrible thing where she decides she's going back behind "the face" and *never coming out again* — pressing the water-bottle cap into her nail bed where it hurts, locking the bathroom door for the first time ever, arranging the smile on top of the face that begged. He calls her beautiful and she *flinches*.

Here's what's got its hooks in me and also what I'm suspicious of. The consent on the page is textbook-perfect — genuinely the thing I want. But the book is being *psychologically merciless*, exactly like the jacket promised, and the person it's being merciless toward is the woman who's supposed to be a predator. So now I don't know who to be afraid for. Is Randi the villain, or is she the first casualty? Both? And that Daphne line — he once didn't respect a closed door, it cost him more than he knew anything could cost, he learned. So he's a man with a *history* of going in where he wasn't invited, who has trained himself out of it. I don't fully trust that. I want to, and the not-fully-trusting is doing something delicious to me.

The one thing sitting slightly wrong: he ends the night *quietly happy about her*, congratulating himself on how far he "took her," while she's silently deciding she has to figure out what to do about this. He read the whole evening as a triumph and missed the lock turning over. The care is real AND he's slightly wrong about what just happened, and the gap between his tenderness and her retreat is the scariest, most alive thing here.

I'm in. God help me, I'm in.
