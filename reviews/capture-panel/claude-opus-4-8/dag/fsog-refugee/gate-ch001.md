REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to talk about this because I went into this chapter thinking I knew who I was reading and I did not.

First — the *craft* of the consent here is exactly, precisely the thing I have been hunting for through a hundred books that got it wrong. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." The water bottle he left *in advance* because he'd noticed she always drinks more than she expects. The apple sliced thin, cut sides up, because he'd watched how she eats. The check-ins — back, hands, shoulders — that she gets exasperated by, *"They're fine, Pace,"* which is how you write a check-in that's real and not a box being ticked. He takes her heels off mid-scene and holds her bare foot a second. He is *watching the answer*. This is a man whose control makes room for her instead of erasing her, and I have wanted this on a page for years, and it is here, and it is hot in the specific way I need it to be hot — the slowness, the not-being-in-a-hurry, "I'm not stopping. I'm taking my time." God. The edging, the *there better ahead of her and he meant her to have it*. Yes. That's the register. That turned me on and I'm not going to pretend it didn't.

BUT.

The last third flipped the whole thing over in my hands and I don't think I've stopped turning it over yet. Because it's *Randi*. It's not Vee. The woman on the bench is the woman the jacket told me is the co-architect of Vee's whole seduction, and here she is coming apart on a bench, begging in a voice that isn't hers-the-performance but hers-the-actual, crying without knowing she's crying, and then — and *this is the part* — deciding in the bathroom mirror that she is never coming out again. "He could have the face. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." And pressing the water-bottle cap under her thumbnail hard enough to hurt while she does it. That detail wrecked me a little.

So now I don't know what this book is. And I mean that as the compliment it is and also as the thing that's making me nervous. Because Pace, this careful, watchful, tender man — he *found* her, he got past the armor, he gave her something enormous, and her response is terror and retreat and a decision to lie to him forever. That is not a romance beat. That is a much stranger and sadder thing. And "Daphne had built a door once, and he had not respected it" tells me he has hurt someone before by going where he wasn't invited, and *this* time he doesn't open the door — he sits on the bed and respects it — and the horrible irony is that his restraint is exactly what lets her seal herself away from him. He does the right, careful, loving thing and it costs him her.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the consent in this is IMMACULATE, it's everything, AND the man is either the safest person in the world or the most patient predator I've ever read and the book KNOWS I can't tell yet and it's the same warmth either way.* The jacket already told me these two planned a girl. So I'm sitting here having just watched the "villain" break open and be tender with, and I don't know if I'm being set up to love him so that it hurts more later. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — that line's now got teeth.

Am I turned on? Yes. Am I moved? Yes, genuinely, by the apple and the blanket she brought after week two. Am I uneasy? Deeply. Is that a problem? Not yet — the care on the page is *real*, it's not cruelty in a dominance costume, and the heroine's interiority (Randi's, here) is rendered from all the way inside. That's the deal I make. What I'm watching now is whether the book stays honest about the cost, or whether it's going to ask me to find the *planning-a-girl* part romantic. That's the line. I'm not near it yet. But I can feel where it is.

I want the next chapter immediately, and I suspect it's finally going to be Vee.
