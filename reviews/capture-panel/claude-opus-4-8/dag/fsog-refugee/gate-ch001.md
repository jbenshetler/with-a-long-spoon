REACTION

Okay, so first — I did not see the name coming, and I want to sit with that for a second because it's the whole chapter. The entire time I'm reading this, I'm assuming this is Vee. Twenty-one, sorority, the glamour, the blue eyes — and Pace, "a young mathematician," exactly the man from the jacket. I'm reading it as *the* scene, the courtly-exact-asks-before-he-takes man doing his thing to our girl. And then: "There you are, Randi." And the floor drops out. It's *Randi*. The friend. The one who's supposedly steering Vee toward Pace "as if by accident." Randi and Pace are the two who made the plan. And here they are, together, and it's not cold and it's not a game — it's the realest thing in the book so far. That reframe is delicious and a little sickening in the way I think it's supposed to be, and I texted no one but I wanted to.

On the heat itself: this is the thing I've been hunting for and almost never find. He *watches*. He built the bench, yes, but the whole scene is him reading her body and answering it — the apple sliced thin because she'll eat thin slices, cut sides up because she eats faster that way. That detail undid me before any clothes came off. The safeword check-ins are not throat-clearing box-ticking; they're woven into the tenderness. "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset." The taking-her-hands-out-of-his-hair so she isn't distracted from what she chose. The Daphne backstory — the closed door he once didn't respect and learned from — placed *exactly* at the moment he decides not to knock. That's a man whose control makes room for her. This is the dominance-with-tenderness-in-one-body thing, no contradiction. I felt it. Genuinely, physically, I felt it.

And YET. Here's where I'm unsettled in a way that's bigger than turned-on. Because the reveal recontextualizes all that care as *technique aimed at a target*. The jacket already told me there's a plan Vee doesn't know about — and now I've watched Pace run this exact devastating, seeing, unspooling attention on Randi, the co-conspirator, and it *worked too well on her*. Randi got seen and it terrified her and she has decided, "not tonight — and not ever," to go back behind the face forever. That last movement — the mirror, the smile that "settled," the face that "had been lying for years" — is the coldest, saddest, best-written thing here. She had the exact experience the book is promising us Vee will have, and her response is to seal it off. She pressed a bottle cap into her nail bed under her thumbnail. That detail. She's hurting herself quietly to stay in control while a man holds her and calls her beautiful.

So what am I actually feeling? Two things at once. One: this author can *write consent as heat*, better than almost anything I've read, and I trust the craft. Two: I am now deeply, deeply suspicious of what this book is doing to me, because it just proved it can make me fall completely for Pace's attentiveness — and it's about to point that same attentiveness at Vee *as part of a scheme he and Randi cooked up*. The jacket says "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" and "lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does." I now understand that sentence in my body. I'm the mark too. That's thrilling and it's a warning bell.

The one thing keeping me from full swoon: I don't yet know what the plan *is* or how much of Pace's warmth is real versus deployed. If this curdles into cruelty-dressed-as-devotion aimed at Vee, I'm out. But right now, on the strength of this chapter — the apple, the water bottle he knew to leave, the door he didn't open — I want to know these people. Especially Randi. God, Randi. She's not a villain-friend, she's a woman who just met herself on a bench and slammed the door. I need to know what she does about it.
