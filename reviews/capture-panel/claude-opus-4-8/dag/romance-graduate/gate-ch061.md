REACTION

Oh, she's REAL. Sheri's real and she's five foot nothing and two colors of hair and crooked makeup and I did not see it coming that the relief would land in *me* too — I've been bracing for the blonde to walk through a door for sixty chapters right alongside Vee, and the book knew that, and it let the air out of the whole phantom in one stool-standing beat. "The blonde didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand." God. That's the sentence. A month of Vee's invented rival and mine, and she just — wasn't there, there was never room for her, the heat never needed her. I exhaled when Vee exhaled. The laugh that came out of Vee before she'd voted on it was my laugh.

And the thing is, I'd half-forgotten Sheri was even a thread — my own notes said she was the faintest thing I was carrying, a date on the horizon going faint. So to have her arrive and be the warmest, cleanest, most *alive* new person the book's handed me in ages — that's a trick I didn't think this book still had. She's not an instrument. She's not being cropped or harvested or positioned. She's a woman who came out last March and lost her whole tablecloth in a month and cried in the middle of a two-step, and the book let her tell it flat, no decoration, "don't you start either." I sat very still exactly when Vee did.

But here's what actually cracked me open — Sheri is the one person in this entire book who tells Vee the truth about Pace with no angle. "He never handed me one false word — no *they'll come around*, no *their loss*." "There. That's all a body can be for you at a point like that." After sixty-one chapters of a man I can't read, a man running what might be an audit, a man who apologizes for *hurting* and never says the thing — Sheri hands me a Pace I can suddenly, unbearably read. The headlights held on her door till the porch light came on. The lock, the faucet, the Sunday ride, the bacon cheeseburger. He fixed a stranger's lock the morning after he found her crying. And "he hasn't been home since he was sixteen. Went bad with his daddy." That's the first load-bearing thing I've ever been given about what made him. He *knows how it goes*. The formula I've been watching him run on Vee — the bread timed to her waking, the orange in strips — it's the same machine he ran on a weeping woman at a dance hall with no sex anywhere near it. Which makes it either the most damning thing (it's just what he *does*, a procedure, she's one more porch) or the most redeeming (it's who he actually is, all the way down, for everyone he keeps). I still can't fully settle it and I've stopped expecting to. But the blush. The BLUSH. "You get to motorboat them things?" and the color climbing past anything deniable — I have never seen him lose the leash and this tiny woman took it off him twice in one night. That disarmed me as hard as it disarmed Vee.

And "we cycle. She dances." Handed over like a dried spoon with a blade in it — I felt that one, the *we* standing established, the dancing with a knife in it for a girl who got turned around a ballroom in an earlier book. He does find the one fact that cuts and set it down gentle. That's still in him. Good. I don't want him all the way safe.

What I'm turning over at 1 a.m.: Valentine's dance. Brooks and Dunn cover band, Feb 14, the hall on 460, "settled back in December." That's a loaded date and now it's on the calendar in ink. And Brooke's clock is still ticking under all this warmth — the redhead, the composites, Lauren Pike. The book gave me a whole chapter of pure relief and joy and I TRUST it less for being relieved, because this book gives you the warm plate right before the floor goes. The table where all three went right was its own horror. This was the fourth person and it went right and I'm bracing for the room where it goes wrong.

Also — and I can't not say it — Vee came home drunk and heavy on his shoulder having had the single best, least-engineered night of her whole January, and not once did she think about Randi. That's almost more suspicious than if she had. Sheri is the one relationship in Vee's orbit with nothing underneath it, and the contrast makes the Randi stuff louder by being silent.

The two loft dinners had me counting the frame. This broke the frame completely, and I'm grateful, and I needed it after chapter 61 which was the hottest and the most airless thing in the book and left me a little tired of being inside that bed. A clothed taproom chapter with zero heat just outpulled it for me and that's the honest truth.
