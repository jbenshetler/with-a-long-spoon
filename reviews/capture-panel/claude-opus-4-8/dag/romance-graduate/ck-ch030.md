WHO'S WHO

Vee (Vivienne) — still the one I love and the one I'm scared for. Redhead, freckled, the body that came in too much and became her shame. Econ junior, reads the right side of the menu, Thursday-arithmetic girl. Real name-thing: her mother says *Vivienne* (love), her dad says *Vee* (love) and *Vivienne* only when she's in trouble. Ohio, casseroles, church, unopened saffron, nurse mother, jet-engine father who hears wrong notes in engines, one Army brother she loves and doesn't know. This stretch she went into credit-card debt she can't cover for shoes the dress "was owed," blew up a date with Pace at a country bar, and crawled to Randi to get scripted back to his door.

Pace / Peter — the mathematician, 22 (younger than I thought — took the SAT at 14, at college at 15, doctorate now). Real name Peter; he took his mother out of the answer when I asked. The wound came all the way up this stretch: in seventh grade they moved his desk into the hall for a year because he asked math questions his teacher couldn't answer — "took the thing you loved and made it the punishment." And Daphne: his assigned tutor, 19 to his 15, two years, ended when she turned 21 and he was 17 and couldn't follow her into the bars. He calls it luck. He calls it the best two years he had. He doesn't hear himself defending it. The scar on his inner thigh — I touched it, he laid his hand over mine and said "not tonight," a door with a time on it, not a wall. Still don't know what it is. Powerlifter (started at 15, "dormitory full of men"; lifting is where nothing exists but the one heartbeat — same face he wears with Vee, she clocked it). Taught the country-dance class two semesters.

Randi — the co-architect, and this stretch is when she started bleeding out where I could see it. Black hair, blue eyes, the finished face built at 13, white-gold band always on, cold citrus/green perfume, sky-blue toenails now. She has a small designated bowl at Pace's house. She's been in his bed the whole book saying *tell me* at his ear every night — I just wasn't in the room. She shopped Vee, scripted her, runs the lunches, and now she wants her — out loud, getting off on telling Pace about Vee's body. She's cracking.

Cassie — the honest one, ball cap, four-word girl, the one clean love. Reads Vee off a made bed. Watches Vee instead of the dress because Vee is the only part she's ever been able to see. Sees the whole architecture and won't pull the seam — I read it as her being afraid pulling it loses Vee faster. Vee keeps editing her toward the margins and calling it kindness.

Sheri — new, and cold: a woman Pace bikes with who watches women like weather, tracked Vee across the bar for him, "hell of a backside." Another appraising woman in his orbit. The web of women watching Vee keeps widening; Vee's the only one who doesn't know she's watched.

Meg — dining-hall friend, lent the dance clothes and got the ropers from Dana on Third. Kayla mostly gone now.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH

Randi — I can't hate her and it's now unbearable. The bowl she wouldn't eat and then the plate she cleaned to a ruler-line a few chapters later — she's coming off her own footing, regressing into something undefended in his kitchen. She decided at 13 she'd never be the lamp, only arrange the light, and now she's watching the man she started the game with love the girl she shopped, in the exact glad way she rationed herself out of, and she can't get bored of her and can't stop wanting her. The want is out loud now and it's grief wearing want. She's turned herself into the empty third chair at a table she can already picture. She owes Vee everything and is taking everything and I think being right is going to kill her.

Pace — the con still works on me fully seen, which is the point and I resent it exactly right. But this stretch made him a person: the hall, Daphne, the scar he won't open yet, the name Peter with no forward motion in it. He handed Vee the thing that hurt him and called it luck and didn't hear himself. He's caught too now — "listen to you," going and not stopping about her thumb on a chair joint. My one red line — "the dom was just right" — keeps NOT tripping, because the silence and the narration hold more than he performs, and because his own tenderness is quietly indicting him. The kitchen spanking discovery worked precisely because he never named it.

Vee — protective, sick, proud, and newly afraid for her money. She thinks she fixed the fight herself; the fixing was written by the woman sleeping with him. She thinks she's getting away with something. She strips herself now, un-asked, and calls the shape they cut "what she was for." Every yes still hers and still a receipt.

Cassie — the one uncontaminated love and I'm grieving her in advance. She sees and won't say.
