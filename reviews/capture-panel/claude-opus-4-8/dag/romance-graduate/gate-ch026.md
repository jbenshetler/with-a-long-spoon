REACTION

Oh, this one hurt, and it hurt in the good way — the way that means the book finally spent something instead of just winking at me.

First: the dance. He teaches her to dance and it's the whole book in one scene, isn't it. "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead. Being jelly doesn't work." The frame she fights as too close, too fixed, too much rule — and then it's the thing that holds her in the one place the next step can come from. That's the con, that's the surrender, that's the whole thesis, and Rivers had the nerve to route it through leather-soled boots on a cold concrete pad instead of a bed and it landed harder than half the sex has. "There it is," quiet. I felt that in my teeth. And crucially — the narration KNOWS. She goes looking for the bill of it: "how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join." The book put its finger on the exact soft spot and then let the floor take the question, because that's what she does with every alarm. God, it still knows more than she does. That's the only reason I trust it.

But the thing I actually want to text a friend about at 1 a.m. is that Vee got to be WRONG. Ugly-wrong. She weaponized his goodness — "Does your service include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" — and I heard the glass go past the point of catching it same as she did, and I didn't want to catch it either, I wanted her to throw it. Because I've read four hundred heroines manufacture a fight out of one unsent text and it's always the man who's secretly at fault. Here she's just jealous and small and self-poisoning and the blonde played her like a fiddle and she LET herself be played because "floozy" runs on the same wire as everything else. This is the manufactured-conflict trope and it's not manufactured — it's characterological, it comes straight out of her mother's mouth and thirteen-year-old shame, it's earned. That's the difference and it's the whole difference.

And then the bartender. The older woman, economy of movement, "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." I could have rolled my eyes at the wise-barkeep-sets-the-heroine-straight beat and instead it gutted me, because it recontextualizes the entire room — he DOESN'T bring dates, he brought one, on a dance night, and the girls weren't jealous of the girl who has him, they were jealous of the girl he chose. Which is the cruelest possible thing to learn right after you've thrown his decency in his face.

What I'm sitting in: he left. Untouched beer on the bar, wedge of cold, gone. No martyred speech, no chasing her, no "let me explain." He just answered straight — "Yes. Sometimes. When it's required." — and when it didn't matter what he said, he left. That's a real man doing a real hurt thing and I respect the book for not letting him grovel.

BUT. Here's my suspicion, and it's sharpened, not dulled, by how much I liked this chapter. Chapter 24. I know what Vee doesn't. I watched Randi ride him and say "she's not ready, though" and "you'll want to take your time" and I know this whole thing is a plan. So when Vee decides at dawn, glowing with remorse, that she's going to Randi's to fix it — I want to scream. She's going to carry her freshly-broken-open shame and her lesson-learned humility straight to the extraction machine. The person she's going to for help repairing the relationship is the co-author of the trap. That's the knife the jacket promised and it's finally being held right. The dance chapter taught her to stop bracing and give her weight to the frame — and the very next thing she's going to do is give her weight to Randi.

Randi wasn't in this chapter at all and I felt her absence like a draft.

Still waiting — twenty-six chapters now — for Pace and Randi ON the record where Vee could catch it. Ch. 24 gave me them in a room but still not the seam Vee can see. And the Randi-heat, the frosted-plum-toe swing of the room in ch. 23, is the live wire this book keeps charging and never closing. But this chapter fed me. It fed me depth AND it kept the dread. I'm not going anywhere.
