REACTION

Oh, *finally* — a chapter where she does something ugly, and it's HERS. I've been waiting for the machine to catch someone, and instead the book handed me the smallest, realest thing: Vee, three dates in and skinless with wanting him, walking into a room full of women who love him and turning it into venom in her own mouth. "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" I actually flinched. That's the manufactured-conflict move I hate on paper — jealousy, a misread, an unsent-text's worth of nothing — and yet it didn't read manufactured for one second, because the book showed me the whole engine underneath it: the count she starts without meaning to, her mother arriving "flat as ever" with *a man like that has girls,* the shame she's carried her whole life reaching up and grabbing the nearest weapon. She didn't get jealous of the blonde. She got jealous of her own unworthiness. That's the difference between this and every hockey-alpha spat I've DNF'd.

And the dance itself — God, before it all went wrong, the dancing was the heat. There was no sex in the first half and I was warmer than I've been in some sex scenes. "Give me something to push back against… Being jelly doesn't work." "The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." That's the whole book in a frame hold. He's teaching her surrender as a physical fact, teaching her that the thing she braces against is the thing that would carry her, and I clocked every inch of the con AND felt it in my own spine anyway. The moment it "took her" and she stopped guessing — "There it is," quiet — I was gone with her. That's the erotics I came for and can't get anywhere else: it's not his hand on her ass, it's his hand on her shoulder blade and the information going through it.

But here's what got me on the floor, mid-dance, the thing I'll text a friend about: *"how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join."* The book let HER ask the real question, the one I've been asking for twenty-six chapters, and then let the floor take it away from her because the music was loud and he was good. That's the machine working in daylight and her choosing not to see. It's devastating and it's honest.

The bartender. Oh, the bartender. "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." I got played exactly like Vee got played — I spent the whole bar scene braced for the reveal that he IS running a thing on all these women, the harem, the pattern, and instead the reveal is that he's genuinely decent and she torched it. And I'm suspicious of my own relief! Because this is the fourth or fifth time the book has set up "here's the sinister truth about Pace" and then handed me "no, he's just good," and I know — I KNOW from the jacket, from Randi kneeling to buy those shoes, from chapter 24 — that the sinister truth isn't about the dance-hall girls. It's the thing he and Randi built. So the book is using the small false alarm to keep me trusting him on the big real one. That's the con explaining itself again and I resent it exactly right.

What I noticed and it chilled me: she reached for "slut" and "floozy" the second she felt exposed — "she'd spread her legs like a slut." All that unstooping Pace did, one bad night and the shame snaps right back to factory settings. He hasn't cured anything. He's built a beautiful room on top of a fault line and the fault's still there. And the washed shirt — "It came back smelling of fabric softener and no one" — that broke me a little. She rationed him until he went stale, then killed the last of him in the machine, and put the empty shirt on anyway. That's the truest grief-object in the book so far.

Where I'm cooler: this was long, and after the last chapter (Peter, the hall, Daphne, the whole quiet fireside deepening) plus THIS, I've now had a big stretch with no Randi-and-Pace gears on the page. Chapter 24 gave me them in bed, which I loved, but it's still all output — I still haven't watched them *plan*, on the record, and this chapter's whole crisis is one Randi's machine didn't even need to touch. Vee wrecked it herself. Which is thematically gorgeous — she doesn't need a puppeteer, her shame does the work — but I want to see the puppeteers sweat. Randi shopped for those shoes in chapter 23 as a seduction; where's the payoff of THAT thread. And "I'm going to Randi's" as the button — of course she is, she's going to bring the wound straight to the woman engineering her. I want the next chapter for that alone.
