REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where it finally costs her *money*, and I sat up. Every other yes has been her body, her time, her secrets — things I could half-tell myself she was choosing free and clear even knowing better. But that credit card. The half-second where she holds the debit — the card that only spends what's already behind it, the card that IS her, the Thursday-arithmetic girl, the read-the-right-side-of-the-menu girl — and then puts it back and lays down the emergency card with nothing under it. I felt that in my stomach. "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it." That's the jacket line made literal — *nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over* — except now it's an actual receipt that shows up next month with a number on it, and she signed it smiling so she wouldn't flinch in the room. The book made me watch her spend money she doesn't have to buy the shoes the dress "was owed." "The shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed." God, that reframing. That's the whole con in one sentence — you can't want a thing you can't afford, but you can *owe* it.

And Randi runs it so cleanly. She doesn't push the boutique. She lets the tan sensible shoe hang there, says nothing about it, and then springs the shoe-size thing — "That's me exactly. Same feet, baby" — and *that's* the lockpick, that's the yes she was actually farming the whole time. Not the shoes. The binding. "Practically a marriage." Same size, same everything, we can share — which in this book means Vee's foot and Randi's foot are the same foot, interchangeable, and I don't think that's an accident given everything the jacket promised about the woman beside her. Then "you're not buying that" lands like affection when it's a leash.

The toe moment. Randi down on one knee — *kneeling*, the exact position Pace took at the pedestal — holding Vee's bare foot, thumb once across the plum nail, and Vee's whole floor tips and her hand shoots out to Randi's shoulder to stay upright. That's the Randi-wanting thread out in daylight and running straight through her body, and Randi looks up and *says nothing at all and smiles* — she felt it, she caused it, she's not going to name it yet. That's the "you don't have to tell me" move again, in a different register. She never closes the door she opens; she just leaves it swinging so Vee walks through it thinking it was her own idea.

Here's what got me and I hate that it got me: the shoe-store slapstick actually worked. The yeti canoe, the rhinestone platforms, "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal," the two of them folded over the bench laughing. It's genuinely funny and it made the friendship feel *real*, which is exactly why it's lethal — I can't hold Randi at arm's length as pure villain when she's this good a hang, and neither can Vee, and that's the point, and I resent it in the correct way. "She had never once in her life had a friend like this." That line is the trap closing and it's also just true.

And the economics underneath — Randi buying loafers at several multiples of the shoes Vee is white-knuckling, never once glancing at the number, talking the whole time about some woman and a coat, wearing them out of the store like shoes are something you trade mid-afternoon. The book didn't underline it. It just put the two cards side by side on the counter and let me see the gulf. That restraint is why I trust the narration even now.

What I still want, and this is my standing impatience: I got a real taste of the gears in ch23 — Randi at Pace's table, the untouched bowl, "How long, Pace," the sex with her eyes shut and no *tell me* — and I want MORE of that, the two of them on the record, the plan cracking. This chapter is a tightening chapter, a bridge with a knife in it, and it's a good one, but it didn't move the reveal. The induction is loaded now, though — the dress, the impossible shoes, the daydream of walking in late and stopping the room. I want to see her walk in. And I want to see who's watching when she does.
