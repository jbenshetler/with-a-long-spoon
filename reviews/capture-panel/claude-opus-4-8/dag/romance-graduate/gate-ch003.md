REACTION

Okay. So now I understand what I'm holding, and it's not what the first chapter let me think I was holding.

Chapter one, I thought: here's a man and his girlfriend, a bench, a beautiful dom-with-aftercare scene, and the twist that *she's* the one performing, hiding, going behind her own face. The gut-punch was "there you are, Randi" — the name landing on *her*, not Vee. I spent chapter two with Randi under a synthetic duvet in last night's clothes not-crying, and I was genuinely moved by her, this girl who's so good at not-going-to-the-crying that it doesn't even feel like a door anymore. I was *rooting* for her. I thought this was a book about a woman being cracked open against her will to want and I was in.

And now chapter three quietly reorganizes the whole thing and I had to sit up in bed about it. Because the jacket told me — Randi is the glamorous friend who "steers her, lightly, laughing, as if by accident, into the path of a young mathematician named Pace." Randi is a recruiter. And this chapter is the recruitment *being manufactured*, in real time, dressed as foreplay. The "pointing game." Pace points, she picks, and the whole thing is engineered so that by the time they reach the dining hall, Vee — poor warm unguarded Vee laughing with her head thrown all the way back, the one girl on the quad who forgot she could be seen — gets *selected*. And the shoulder-check. God. "It was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth. It was the redhead who pulled up short, stricken, apologizing." That is predation choreographed to look like the prey did it. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." The jacket *told* me and I still felt sick watching the mechanism work.

Here's what's doing my head in, and why I can't put it down: Pace is *the same man* who respected the locked bathroom door, who learned from Daphne not to force his way into someone's feelings. The tenderness in chapter one was real. And now that exact attentiveness — the reading of her body, the not-rushing, the "I point, you pick" that hands her agency — is being used to tool a girl into existence as a target. The thing that made him safe is the thing that makes him dangerous. That's not a trope. I have not read that before. The sensitivity IS the weapon.

And Randi — Randi who was so sympathetic under that duvet — she's *delighted*. Her feet did the happy footwork on the brick. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" That line went through me cold. She's taking the thing that cracked *her* open and she's already planning to do it to someone else, because if someone else is on the bench then she's the one standing beside Pace doing the choosing, safe, behind the face. That's her escape route from chapter one. She can't be the one taken if she's the one taking. The psychology is *merciless*, exactly like the jacket promised, and I usually hate when a jacket brags like that — but it's earning it.

What turned me on, because I have to be honest: the bed scene opening this chapter is genuinely hot and the "would you like to taste another woman" landing in her body before she can make it safe — her coming helplessly off a *question* — that's the real stuff. Desire ahead of consent-to-the-desire. And the kiss with her own taste still on his mouth on the quad, cracking her composure mid-game. That's heat wired straight into the manipulation, which is uncomfortable and effective and exactly the register I came here for.

What I'm suspicious of / scared of: I now know Vee is the heroine of the jacket, the "quietly starving" twenty-one-year-old, and I just watched her get chosen like produce. So this whole book is going to be her falling, gladly, into something two people decided about her before she knew she existed to them. I'm going to spend the series knowing more than she does and, per the jacket, "falling for it exactly as she does." That's dread and want in the same place and I don't fully trust what it's doing to me, which is the most alive a romance has made me feel in a long time.

The one thing I'll flag: chapter three leans a little hard on telling me the subtext — "she was not choosing a woman, she was watching her learn that she could," the follow/lead dance metaphor extended maybe one beat past where I'd have felt it myself. I'm a reader who catches this stuff; I don't need the pointing game *explained* while it's happening. But it's inside Pace's close-third, and his noticing-everything is characterization, so I'll allow it. Barely.

I need chapter four. I need to see Vee from the inside now.
