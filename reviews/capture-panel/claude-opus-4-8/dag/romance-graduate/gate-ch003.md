REACTION

Okay so now I get it. Now I see the machine.

Chapter one gutted me — I thought I was reading a man who *found* a woman, this Pace with his sliced apples and his lights-on patience, and then that gut-punch reveal that "there you are, Randi" and the woman he'd cracked open on the bench was already fleeing back behind her own face before he'd even zipped up. Chapter two was her lying under a synthetic duvet in last night's shoes, not-crying, so good at not-crying it isn't even a door anymore. Devastating. I was in.

And now chapter three flips the whole thing over and shows me the underside. Because the girl on the back cover — Vee, the redhead who forgot she could be seen — that's the mark. And I just watched her get *chosen*, on a brick walk, on a beautiful Tuesday, pointed at by a man leaning on a light pole. "The invitation reached her long before she knew there was one." This is the game. Randi and Pace, the two closest people to Vee, the secret choice made about her. I'm now the reader the jacket promised: ahead of her, sick with it, and falling for it anyway.

Here's what's got me. The pointing game itself is a genuinely brilliant piece of writing. Pace asking "would you like to taste another woman?" *while she's coming*, taking her body's answer as the true one — and then the walk, where he narrates his own read of her taste being "not the closed ones, not the mirrors," refining toward the alive one. It's seduction as engineering, and the prose knows exactly what it's doing. The bit where Randi hands the wanting to *him* — "only in a threesome, with you" — and Pace clocks it: she moved the appetite, made herself the generous instrument instead of the woman who hungers, because a woman who hungers for another woman is "unsurvivable." That is the same wound from chapter one. The face. The thing that lets her give everything away because none of it reaches *her*. This book is consistent about her damage in a way that feels earned, not decorative.

But here's my 1 a.m. text, and it's a mixed one: I spent this whole chapter inside Pace's head, and Pace's head is starting to unnerve me in a way I don't think the book fully intends me to find *sexy*. He is so pleased with himself. "He liked when he guessed right about her." He's reading her like an instrument the entire time, and the tenderness is real but it's also *technique*, and the collision with Vee — the clean predatory execution of it, Randi's decade of dance folded into three seconds of engineered "accident" — I found genuinely chilling. Which I think is the point. "Psychologically merciless in its tenderness." The horror is that it's working and everyone's having a wonderful time and the maple is burning.

What I'm hungry for now: I want *Vee*. I've had two and a half chapters of these two circling, and the person the whole title is about just laughed with her throat bared to the sky and walked off into a stats class not knowing she's already been hunted. I want inside her before I watch this happen to her. The jacket promises me her interiority and I haven't had a drop of it yet — she's still an object being appraised on the quad, gorgeous and unpinned. If chapter four gives me Vee from inside, this book has me for the whole volume.

Heat-wise: the opening scene is good — the coming-up-over-her-still-pulsing, the taste of herself, the question detonating a third orgasm — it's explicit and it's *doing character work*, not just friction. That's the both-at-once I came starving for. My only wobble is that I've now had three sex-adjacent openings that are all filtered through Pace's expert observation, and I could use the POV to breathe. But that's a craving, not a complaint.

I'm not close to stopping. This is the book delivering on its own terrifying premise.
