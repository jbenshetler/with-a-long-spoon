REACTION

Okay. So now I know what the jacket meant, and I did NOT see the shape of it coming this cleanly. Chapters one and two I thought Randi WAS the girl — the one being done to, the one who begged on the bench and then bolted behind her face in the mirror. And she is. But now she's also the one holding the spoon. That's the thing that just detonated for me. The girl who cannot survive being seen is going to spend this book seeing another girl the exact way Pace saw her, and hand her over. She's going to do to Vee what was done to her, because it's the only place she can put all that unbearable aliveness that has nowhere else to go. Oh my GOD. That's the book. That's the whole book and I'm only three chapters in.

And the craft of how he got the yes out of her — mouth still wet from her, sliding in while she's still coming, THEN asking "would you like to taste another woman." So her body answers before her manager can. That's genuinely diabolical and I felt the manipulation and I also felt how good it is, which is the exact discomfort this book keeps handing me and I keep taking. He reads her body as the "true" answer and overrides her mouth. He tells himself it's tenderness — "he wanted it for her" — and I半 believe him and half want to scream at her to run. That gap is where I live in this book.

The pointing game itself — I was braced for it to be gross, two people appraising strangers like livestock, and it IS that, and the book knows it's that (her propriety recoiling on the first beat, the "bald social wrongness"). But then it turns the whole thing into a portrait of Randi's taste, and the taste is a confession. Not the mirror-girl. Not the matched pairs. Not the girl who runs the room. She keeps rejecting sealed, performed, closed women — every version of what SHE is — until Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bare, having "forgotten for the length of the laugh that she could be seen." That's the one thing Randi cannot do and has never once done. Of course that's who she wants. She's not shopping for a threesome. She's shopping for the thing she killed in herself in that bathroom mirror.

The bare "yes." The stride dropping out from under her. She doesn't look away — the one girl all afternoon she can't stop looking at. And then the recovery, the kitten voice, "yeah, I like her," re-owning it as a gift for her man. Same move as the bench. Same move as the mirror. She wants, it shows, she dresses it, she hands it to Pace so she doesn't have to hold it herself. Rivers is running the identical mechanism in daylight that she ran in the bedroom and I clocked it and it still worked on me.

The collision was gorgeous, honestly — the dance training folded into three seconds, making Vee apologize for being walked into. That's Randi's whole competence weaponized toward want for the first time instead of toward safety, and the grin she can't manage afterward, the little footwork on the brick, the "yes" squeaking out high — that's the most alive I've seen her outside the bench. She's happy. That's what wrecks me. She's genuinely, footwork-happy, and it's the opening move of something that's going to hurt someone, probably everyone, and she doesn't know she's the mark as much as Vee is.

What I'm suspicious of: Pace. "The best idea he'd had in a month," "he wanted it for her," the Daphne door he didn't respect once and learned from — I don't fully trust that he's the wise gentle craftsman he narrates himself as. A man this good at getting past a woman's defenses "for her own good" is the scariest possible man and the book keeps letting him sound reasonable. And the jacket already told me it started as a game between two people about a girl who never knew — so Vee is the mark, and Randi thinks she's a player but she started as the mark too. Everyone here is somebody's project.

Heat plus a brain, both firing, no fade-out, no manufactured text-message nonsense. Three for three. I'd text a friend: "the seduction book where the seductress is actually the most seduced person in it and doesn't know it — I can't put it down and I feel slightly complicit."
