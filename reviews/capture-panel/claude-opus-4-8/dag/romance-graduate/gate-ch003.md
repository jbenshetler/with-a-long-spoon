REACTION

Oh, they got me. They *got* me.

I was reading chapters one and two thinking I had the book pegged — gorgeous, slow, a little airless, the bench scene genuinely doing something I haven't felt a scene do in a while, the whole thing about being *seen* past the face. I thought Randi was my heroine. I thought this was her waking up. And then the last line of chapter three lands — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — and the floor drops out, because *Vee* is the girl on the cover, Vee is the one the jacket's been promising me, and I've just spent three chapters falling for the two people who are about to do to someone else exactly what was done to Randi. The "polite invitation." The game. It's *this.* The pointing game is the invitation being built in real time, and I'm watching it get assembled out of a stranger's laugh by the dining hall.

And here's the thing that actually unsettled me, in the good way: I was *enjoying* the walk. The pointing game is genuinely hot and genuinely charming — the "I point, you pick," Randi shopping, her feet doing the little happy footwork on the brick, the kiss where he hasn't wiped his mouth and she catches her own taste and her composure cracks. I was grinning right along with her. The book handed me the delight and the predation in the *same gesture*, which is exactly what the jacket warned me it would do ("lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does"). Except I'm not ahead of Vee — I'm ahead of nobody, I'm right down in it with Randi, and Randi doesn't know she's prey either. She thinks she's the huntress. That's the sick-brilliant part. The girl who went behind her own face at the end of chapter one, who decided *not ever* — she's out here radiant, in her body, cracked open by a stranger's red hair, and she STILL can't own the wanting. "I like her." She moved the appetite onto Pace again, same move as the threesome reframe. The woman cannot say *I want* without handing it to a man first.

Pace. God. I don't know what to do with Pace and I think that's deliberate. In chapter one his attentiveness read as devotion — the thin apple slices, the water bottle, the Daphne door he won't open. Here the exact same attentiveness reads as a man running a long, patient operation on a human being, "letting the camouflage stand," choosing the retaining wall "fast and without seeming to" for its angle on the doors. The tenderness is the technology. He's not a brute, he's *worse* — he's good at it, and he enjoys being good at it, and the enjoyment is the engine. "the best idea he'd had in a month." I believe every word of his interiority and it frightens me more than a villain would.

What I'm suspicious of: the threesome framing. Randi "finds a woman for *theirs*" so she never has to own a want for a woman — but the book keeps telling me, twice now, that her body answered before her mind could lie. I think the series is going to make her pay for that gap, and I think Vee is going to be the one who actually *can* want out loud, and that contrast is the whole engine of book one.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the slow bench book — the boyfriend and the girlfriend are PICKING A GIRL off the quad like they're at a buffet and it's the hottest and most horrifying thing I've read all year and I cannot stop." Also: "I think I was supposed to love the couple in chapter one so that chapter three would make me complicit and IT WORKED, I'm sick."

Three quiet-ish chapters structurally but this one is not quiet — it's a hunt dressed as a stroll, and the reveal is a genuine turn, not a bridge. The prose is still doing real work (the single burning maple, the follow breaking frame before the step is called — the dance metaphor finally paying off on a person). I'm not bored. I'm leaning in.
