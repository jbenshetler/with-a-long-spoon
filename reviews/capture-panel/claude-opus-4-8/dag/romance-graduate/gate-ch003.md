REACTION

Okay. So this is the book. This is what it's doing.

Chapter one gutted me — a spanking-bench scene that was actually about a woman discovering she has an interior she's spent twenty-one years hiding, and the twist landing that the submissive wrecked-open one is *Randi*, the friend from the blurb. That reveal reframed everything. And now chapter three snaps the whole architecture into view and my stomach dropped, because I get it now. Randi decided, in that bathroom mirror, that she would never come out from behind the face again. And here she is one chapter later doing to a stranger *exactly* what was done to her — except she doesn't know that's what she's doing. She thinks she's playing a game. She's recruiting.

Vee. The blurb girl. The redhead who threw her head back laughing like she forgot she could be seen — of course that's the one. Of course Pace pointed at the openness, the woman who takes up room and is glad to, after rejecting every polished mirror-image of Randi on the quad. He's not choosing a body. He's choosing a *capacity*. And the horrible elegant thing is that Randi's helpless *yes* — the bare one, before she could dress it — was her own appetite recognizing its next victim, or its next self, I honestly can't tell which and that's the hook.

What's making me crazy in the good way: the craft of the misdirection *inside* the sex. "Would you like to taste another woman?" landing in her body before her mind, making her come helplessly, and Pace reading the body as the true answer. Then her genius move — "only in a threesome, with you" — moving the wanting so she doesn't have to own it, handing the appetite to him so she's the generous instrument instead of a woman who hungers. I have read a thousand threesome setups and NONE of them have understood that the negotiation is a lie a woman tells herself to survive her own desire. That's the grown-up thing I've been starving for. The sex isn't decoration; it's the whole psychological engine.

And Pace. God. He's tender and attentive and he "asks before he takes" and he is *the scariest man I've read in ages* precisely because of it. The Daphne door-thing in chapter one told me he's learned restraint the hard way — but here he's using every ounce of that patience to walk Randi into becoming his co-conspirator, and he thinks it's a gift he's giving her. "The best idea he'd had in a month." He genuinely believes he's freeing her. The blurb told me it started as a game between two people about a girl who never knew — and I'm watching the game get *built*, live, on a beautiful Tuesday with a burning maple. The maple he didn't plan but was glad of. That detail killed me.

The pointing game itself is such a good scene — the shoulder-collision con executed so clean it looks like Vee walked into *her*, the redhead's hand flying to her own mouth to apologize. Randi's a predator with a decade of dance training and I clocked exactly how dangerous she is and how little Vee saw coming.

And then the last line. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I actually said *oh no* out loud. Because Randi cried on that bench. Randi was undone on that bench and swore never again — and her first instinct about the girl she just charmed is to imagine her face-down on it. That's not a woman offering her boyfriend a gift. That's a woman who's found a way to watch her own undoing happen to someone else, safely, from behind the face. I'm a little sick and completely hooked.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book is about grooming and it KNOWS it's about grooming and everyone in it thinks they're in a love story and I cannot stop.*
