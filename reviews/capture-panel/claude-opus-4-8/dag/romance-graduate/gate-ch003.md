REACTION

Okay, so I have to hand it to this book — I came into chapter three braced for a comedown. Chapter one was that whole bench scene, which was genuinely one of the better slow-build dom scenes I've read in ages, and then chapter two was the morning-after sorority interrogation, two pages of nothing but subtext and I was *into* it, the four-minute smiling standoff with Brooke. So I'm thinking, fine, where does this go. And then the twist I should've seen and didn't: Randi isn't the heroine. Randi is the one *being recruited into the trap.* The jacket told me — Vee, Pace, Randi, a game between two people about a girl — and I'd somehow filed Randi as the glamorous friend who's already in on it. No. Randi's the woman from the bench. Randi's the one who begged and then went behind her face and swore never to come out again. And now she's the one being walked across a quad and taught to *point.*

That reframe is doing a lot for me. Because the pointing game — god. On its face it's a man playing a cute seduction game with his girlfriend, "I point, you pick," and she gets to laugh and call him a dirty boy and feel like a good sport. But I *read chapter one*, so I know exactly what Pace does. He doesn't take. He arranges the room so you hand him the thing yourself and never notice the handing. And he's doing it again, in daylight, with the maple burning, and this time the thing she's handing over is a person. "Go meet her." The whole bit where the only question in her body is never *do I want her* but *am I allowed* — that's the same machinery as the bench, is this mine to take, and it chilled me in a good way.

And he KNOWS. That's what keeps this from being a dumb threesome setup. He clocks that she's moved the wanting off herself and onto him — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift" — and he lets her keep the wrapper because the wrapper is how the want gets to come out. He's that precise. He sees her reach for the sex-kitten voice and respects the craft of it even as he sees straight through it. That's the "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" the jacket promised and it's actually delivering.

Here's my unease, and it's the good kind of unease, the kind that keeps me reading: I don't know who to be scared for. Vee's the named victim on the back cover, the girl who'll never know there was a plan. But right now I'm scared for *Randi,* who thinks she's the predator and is actually being handled, who still has that cap-under-the-thumbnail self-harm thing from chapter one humming under everything. And the last line — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — made me put the book down for a second. Because Randi's already imagining Vee *on the bench.* On her bench. She's recruiting a girl into the exact machine that took her apart and she's *glad about it,* grinning, doing happy footwork on the brick. The predator-training-its-replacement horror of it.

What I love is that nobody's a cartoon. Pace isn't leering; he's genuinely delighted, "the best idea he'd had in a month," and that's worse than if he were sinister. The taste-of-herself thing, him coming up still wet and making her say it, asking the real question while she's undefended and watching her body answer before her mouth can lie — that's hot AND it's a demonstration of method. The heat and the dread are the same substance here, which is the thing I never get. Usually it's hot OR it's smart.

Only wobble: it's three chapters of Pace being omniscient about everyone's interior, and I want to see him *wrong* about something, I want a crack in the operator. He admitted Daphne in chapter one, the door he didn't respect. Give me the version of this where his read fails. And I need Vee's actual head soon — she's a copper-haired laugh on a lawn right now, a target, and if she stays a target the book tips from merciless to just cruel. But that's a want, not a complaint. I'm turning the page.
