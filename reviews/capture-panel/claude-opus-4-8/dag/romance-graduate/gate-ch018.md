REACTION

Oh, this one I have feelings about, and most of them are about Cassie.

So chapter 17 was the event — the pinning scene, the whole slow undoing of Vee on that box, one of the best things this book has done since the first three chapters, both-at-once the way I've been starving for. And chapter 18 is Vee coming home to tell it. I braced, going in, because I've read that beat a hundred times: the debrief chapter, the girl narrates the thing I just watched, the book gets a second helping out of a scene it already served me. And this is that. It IS that. But I didn't resent it the way I expected to, and I've been sitting here trying to work out why.

It's Cassie. It's that the retelling isn't a retelling — it's a *filter*. Watching Vee choose what to hand over and what to keep is its own thing. She skips the wet-satin humiliation entirely — the whole molten center of the last chapter, the thing that undid her, and she doesn't give it to Cassie, and I clocked that absence like a held breath. She keeps that for herself. And she leads with the *heat turned up hours before she came* detail, the thermostat, which is the single most devastating small thing in the whole visit and she knows it, and Cassie goes "Oh, Vee" and so did I, honestly, both times now. The shirt-with-no-tag finally clicking — a week of sleeping in it, the answer in her hands — that landed even on the second pass because now Vee's feeling the click too.

And then the tone management. The way the two of them keep swerving — Vee going big and slapstick with her hands cupping and flying outward, "just OUT, in broad daylight, on a box, here you go I guess," and Cassie cracking up — and then one of them lets the air out and it goes quiet and true. "Like I was supposed to be there." That's the line. That's the whole book's thesis smuggled into a dorm-room joke session. And Cassie: "No. I think I have it." God, I love her. She's still the clean thing. She's still doing the puzzle-frown, still not able to make the pieces fit, and this chapter she says the quiet part — "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?" — which is the reader's own suspicion handed to the one person the warmth doesn't work on. She's SO close to the seam. She's turning the pieces over: bodybuilder who makes dresses, had you in your underwear and picked up a tape measure, "are you sure he's not gay" — she's groping toward *there's something off about the shape of this* and she's landing on the wrong wrong-answer, the funny one, instead of the real one, which is that this is a man executing a method. And that's the tragedy of Cassie in miniature — she can feel the wrongness and she can't name it, so it comes out as a joke.

What I loved, actually, genuinely: "I was grinding on HIM." The reversal. Vee keeping the most wicked thing for last and delivering it sideways. That's real friendship texture, that's the 1 a.m. text, that's a girl who's found a new power and is drunk on getting to say it out loud to the one person who's safe. It's the most *alive* Vee's been.

But — and here's where the reader in me sits back — this is the second quiet chapter, sort of. It's not a bridge, it's a mirror, and a mirror of a scene I only just read. Nothing new *happens*. The dread doesn't advance. Randi isn't here. The game isn't tightening — nobody in this room knows there's a game. And I'm aware, warmly but clearly, that I paid full attention price for chapter 17 already and this chapter is spending my goodwill re-spending it. The heat is all recollected, filtered through the safe register of two friends laughing. It's charming. It's well made. It did not pull me the way the box did.

What keeps my hand on the book is the crack in the last third — Cassie thinking, Cassie almost-getting-it, the "are you sure he's not gay" that's really "something about this doesn't add up." I want her to find the seam. And I want Randi back, badly, because I've now had a long stretch of pure Vee-and-her-happiness and the whole point of this book is the vinegar underneath, the fact that Randi built this and is being eaten by it, and she's been offstage. The jacket promised me the threads touching. Vee "walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking" — that line in ch16 was a little flare of the real structure, and I want the book to go back there.

What I'd text a friend: "the debrief chapter shouldn't work but Cassie is doing so much heavy lifting — she's THIS close to figuring out her best friend is being run and she thinks the mystery is whether he's gay. I could cry. also Vee kept the wet part to herself and I noticed."
