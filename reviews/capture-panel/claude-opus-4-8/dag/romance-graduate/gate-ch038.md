REACTION

Okay, the photo. I've read this beat a hundred times — the heroine sends the nude, the hero swears he'll never share it — and it's usually a flat little trust-badge the book pins on and moves past. Here it made my stomach drop, and not for the reason the scene thinks. Because I'm a step ahead of her, the way the jacket promised, and the whole time she was fixing her face in his mirror to find "the version of herself she wanted him to keep," I was watching a woman hand a man — who is one half of a couple that *chose* her, who tells his lover everything — a photograph. "I'd never share your photograph." The word *photograph* instead of *you* — she asked him not to share *me*, he answered about the picture. I noticed that. I don't know if she's meant to and she didn't. And "everything she confides travels farther than she knows" is on the back cover and now there is a *file* of her. That's the coldest the book has felt and nobody raised their voice.

And yet — God help me — the staging of the photo was genuinely hot. Him moving her limb by limb, a kiss set down at every place his hands left her, the shoulder, the hip, the knee. Laid out like a calendar girl, warm under his hands, no wish to argue. That's the seam they opened in her chapters ago — handed no say, steadied, arranged — and it's being played like a instrument now. "How do you want me?" and he answers with his hands instead of words, which is the whole man in one gesture: he'll give her everything but the word. The thumb-on-the-shutter, "that's the one," grinning at *her* over the top of the phone and not the picture — fine, that landed, it's supposed to, it's the real tenderness threaded right through the thing I'm scared of. I hate that I can't pull them apart. I'm not supposed to be able to. The book keeps proving it knows that.

Then the cooking, which — this is the third food setpiece in a handful of chapters and I'll be honest, I started skimming the lemon rain and the garlic and the glug of wine. It's beautifully done, I can taste it, and I've now tasted it enough. The piccata is lovely and I don't need the full recipe again. What that passage is *for* — "she used to just eat, now she tastes, and she knew exactly whose doing that was" — I already have. He's rebuilt her appetite, we've established the thesis, I want it to *cost* something now instead of simmer.

But the last page. The last page is why I'm turning in. Thanksgiving. "This is my home." Then the door slams shut — "I don't go back," each word set down clean and apart, the level exact voice — and she takes her hand off it, good girl, kind quiet, the closed room from the Daphne chapter sealed right back up. And *then*: "Sheri's coming over." And I sat up. Because Sheri's his Cassie, the outside voice, the one who named the fuse — and if Sheri's at that table on Thanksgiving, is *Randi*? Where's Randi on Thanksgiving? The chapter doesn't say and that silence is deafening to me, because Randi is the one with the claim nobody's named, and Vee flew home, and the house is going to have people in it that Vee isn't counting. The little flicker of jealousy over Sheri — "another woman at his table on a day meant for family" — and she swallows it because "she's alone," and I'm sitting here going: you have no idea who else is alone, Vee. You have no idea whose table this actually is.

I'd text a friend right now: SHE SENT HIM THE PICTURE. and also — why isn't anybody saying the name Randi at the Thanksgiving table. That's the gap I'm reading toward.
