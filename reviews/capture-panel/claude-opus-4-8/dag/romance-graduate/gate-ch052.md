REACTION

Oh, this is the twin to 51 and the book knows exactly what it's doing. Last chapter Randi's in Gstaad in a dress she bought for a stranger, warm only along the one seam where she touches him, going cold everywhere else and carrying the cold in and out herself. This chapter Vee's in a twin bed her legs outgrew in middle school, under the little gold swimmers, cold everywhere, with the furnace in savings mode. Two women, same midnight, same empty text field, and the book lays them side by side and lets me do the math. I love that it trusts me to do the math. Nobody stepped in to explain that these are the same night in two keys — it just let the 11:52 sit there and the *thinking about you 💛* go out from one cold room and land, verbatim, as the thing Randi answered in the last chapter. I actually went back and checked the wording and it's identical and that did something to me. Vee read it twice "for anything that gave her away." Randi wrote it knowing it was the fairytale cover. Two women editing themselves down to a safe line for each other, each sure the other is the one living out loud.

And the ache of the gap between what Vee imagines and what I just watched. She's lying there building Randi's night out of spite-envy — "a devastatingly handsome billionaire, a weekend so wild Vee wouldn't recognize half of what they did" — and I *know*. I was in that hotel room. I watched Randi lie against a perfect sleeping nothing and feel exactly the cold Vee feels, and reach in her own head for Pace and for Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday. Vee envies the mountains, "even a poisoned Gstaad," and she doesn't know the woman in the mountains spent the whole night reaching back toward *her*. That's the cruelest, best thing the book's done with the parallel: they're both lying in the dark wanting the other and each one thinks she's the small one.

The thing that actually got me by the throat: Vee can't keep her own best night. She reaches for the fra diavolo memory — the sheet slipping, the garlic blooming, the evening having "used her well," her body quiet — to get warm, and it turns on her. The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, the blonde sits in the wine light "that had never in its life needed building for," and Pace smiles at the stove. She built the rival out of the exact opposite of her own body and then couldn't evict her from her own memory. God. That's what jealousy actually does and I've never seen it written as a hijacked memory before. She loses the one warm thing she had to the fear.

And the cooking-is-the-sex equation finally pays off the thing I've been sick of. All volume I've been rolling my eyes at "he cooks with his whole self, she tastes each thing apart" — and here it lands as the engine of her terror: "the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark. And now he was doing it for someone, and she didn't get to know who." The Sheri Thanksgiving "she's alone" comes back as a splinter. The food thing was loading this too, apparently. Fine. I'll stop complaining.

The bartender and Randi's reassurances going "straight through her hands" because they only held her up while she knew she was the one — that's exactly right and exactly devastating. The scaffolding only works for the girl who's sure.

What I'm sitting with: she cropped her own leg off the induction photo for her mother's phone, same as she cropped herself for everyone last volume — "she was the one who had divided it." She's still doing it. And "what am I to you" in her mother's mouth now, the question she hates turned into the only question she has. And the last line — the cold room keeping watch "over the girl who was gone." Vivienne is a stranger in Vivienne's room.

Two quiet chapters in a row now, both of them a woman alone in a cold bed with a phone, and I should be restless. I'm not, quite — because the second one recontextualizes the first, and that's a real move, not a stall. But I'll be honest: I don't need a *third* lonely-bed chapter. I want the term to start. I want the collision. I want Randi's face when the class starts and I want to know if Pace texts. Give me a scene with two people in the same room again.
