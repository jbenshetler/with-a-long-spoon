REACTION

Okay so this is the breather, and I know a breather on sight, and I didn't resent it — but I did feel the book exhale after four chapters of me holding mine. No Pace on the page, no Randi at all, and honestly the *absence* of Randi is the loudest thing in it for me. Because I'm sitting here a step ahead of Vee the way the jacket promised, and the one person she should be swimming laps about is the one person who never once crosses her mind. She's turning over "does he love me" and "did he mind the counter" and she has no idea the actual knife is a brunch table. That gap is doing all my work for me.

What got me was Cassie. Cassie's *back*, thank God, and she's still the only clean instrument in this girl's life. "You're doing the streetlight thing again. You're a public utility." And then she just lays the counting down flat — *you don't come home school nights* — not a question, and lets Vee do the arithmetic on her own empty bed. That's the move. Cassie doesn't harvest, she doesn't pre-chew anything and hand it back the way Randi does; she asks "do you love him" and then *lets the maybe stand* and goes back to her book. The contrast between how Cassie holds Vee's confession and how Randi holds it is the whole book in one chapter, and nobody had to explain it to me, which I'm grateful for because this author usually trusts me.

And the swim. I'll be honest, the long middle of the set with the count — sixty-one, sixty-two, hand in front of the shoulder — is exactly where my eyes started to slide, because I don't need the lane lines explained to me. But then it earned itself: *whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting, out of two months of being looked at like that. Four strokes was all it got.* There it is. The first crack of real doubt in Vee's own head, and it's not about Randi and it's not even about the truth she's being denied — it's the quiet terror that she built the man out of hunger. Which is *so close* to the truth and aimed completely wrong. God.

The tomato burn and Cassie going "there's only one way *that* burn happens. Not enough on" — fine, cute, the body keeps leaking into the dorm. The drawer-worry riding alongside the love-question all through the set, *did he mind*, when we the reader watched him give her the top drawer — she's fretting about the one thing he's already answered with his whole chest, while the thing she should fear sits totally dark. That's the ache of this book. I'm not bored. I'm just impatient, because I can feel the three of them still haven't been in a room and I want the wreck.
