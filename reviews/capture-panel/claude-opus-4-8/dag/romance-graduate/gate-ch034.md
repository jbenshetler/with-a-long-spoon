REACTION

So the induction, the loaded gun on the mantel for how many chapters now — and Helen Rivers does the thing I didn't expect, which is she lets the fantasy fail first. The picture Vee built, walking in late, the room turning on her entrance — that's the romance-novel version, the one I'd have written, and the book just quietly says no. The room is warm and dull and kind. Nobody turns for a walk. That's so much truer than the fantasy and it made me trust her before she'd even gotten to the stage.

And then the second step. God. I actually put the book down for a second after "She took a breath, and made the second step long." Because the whole book has been about who's doing the reaching — the jacket keeps insisting the daring is *hers*, the notes I keep writing to myself are all about whether Vee ever moves on her own nerve — and here it finally, cleanly, is hers. No Randi in the wings, no Pace behind a door. She's alone in a room full of somebody's parents and she decides, a half-second before her head catches up, to give them her leg. The body agreeing before the head is sure — that's the throughline, that's the "each on her own nerve" from the blurb finally paying off in daylight with her name hanging over the chairs. I was so proud of her I could've cried, and I'm a little disturbed at myself for it, which is exactly the register this book keeps me in.

The nothing-underneath detail is doing real work and not as a cheap tease — it's the dress Pace built *requiring* it, the logic of the slit, so the exhibitionism is almost an engineering constraint she chose to honor. "A private joke she was in on" turning, under the lights, into something with actual stakes. That's good. That's the gap between getting-ready-giddy and the real moment, which anyone who's ever worn the too-bold thing out the door knows.

The photo to Pace gutted me a little. "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." She's keeping something back from him now — not out of shame, out of ownership. The girl who edited the ladle out of the story for Cassie is now editing *on purpose*, holding a private self even from the man behind the door. That's growth and it's also a tiny alarm bell, because the thing she's learning to keep is the same thing Randi keeps — the sealed room.

What I missed: Randi and the slap doctrine from chapter 33 are nowhere in this one, and I felt the absence. Last chapter planted the spanking seed so hard — "did you deserve it," the whole mouse-that-roars monologue, Vee soaked over a woman talking in a chair — and this chapter is a clean solo. Which I think is deliberate: the induction is the one beat that's fully *hers*, so the two architects are correctly offstage. But it means the heat here is low and inward, not the two-door pull the book runs on.

And Pace missing it — the Berkeley conference — I'm turning that over. Convenient that the one triumph she walks into alone is the one he can't attend. The book keeps engineering rooms where Vee is the only one acting and the architects are absent-but-present (the dress he built, the shoes Randi buckled). She's never less supervised and never more their product. The scar is STILL owed. "Not tonight" still has a time on it.
