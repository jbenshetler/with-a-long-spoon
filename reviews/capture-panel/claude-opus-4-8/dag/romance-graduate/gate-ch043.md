REACTION

Oh, there it is. There it *is.* Forty-three chapters I've been waiting for the plan to come out from behind the curtain and here's the whole machine, humming in a dark lamplit room with Vee thirty miles away asleep in her own bed, not in it. This is the chapter I've been owed since the jacket said "Randi and Pace chose her together, once, before she was anything to either of them," and it did not cheat me. The *quad.* September, second week. He walked Randi across campus letting her think she was the connoisseur picking girls off a lawn — "I thought I was being such a good sport" — and the whole time he was reading *her*, her slack hand, her changed breath, the *maybe* she gave the girl on the library steps, the fast *God, no.* He wasn't choosing from the women. He'd already chosen. The pointing was only to check whether they'd chosen the same. "I pointed at her and watched your face." I have chills. That is the coldest, most precise thing in this whole book and he says it like fondness.

And the picture he let me build — Randi narrow and bound against all that sprawl of Vee, black hair and red hair on his pillows, him kissing the redhead with his hand on Randi's face — he went *hard on a public lawn* with Randi's hand in his, over a girl who at that point had a name he didn't know. That's the founding image of the con and it's genuinely the hottest and the most sinister thing at once, which is the register I've been starving for this whole last stretch. Depth AND charge in the same minute. Finally. The two quiet chapters before this were an empty plate and this one is the full table.

But here's what wrecked me, what I did not expect: *Randi.* "God, I want her." Said down into the wood, with nothing around it, and then she *hears it leave her* — "He felt her hear it" — and scrambles it back into "for us, for the threesome, somebody has to get her used to my mouth, it's not a treat it's *work.*" And Pace, the bastard, with his flat kind knife: "You don't have to do it for me." "I didn't ask who it was for." He took her cover away on purpose and watched it go through her before she had a say. She *came* on it. The architect is the one being read now, the con-woman conned, and she knows and can't stop it. The goodbye kiss — I've been clocking that held-too-long kiss for three chapters as Vee's tell, and now I learn it's *Randi's.* Randi comes off her "and waits," goes back "with more in it than the first one had," and "then I have to hold myself back from going for more." She's been kissing that girl goodbye on a public sidewalk every Saturday like it's the thing her whole week narrows to, and telling herself and Pace it's groundwork. And "since October" — "I got my hand in her hair once and I have not been the same about it since." She's gone. She's further gone than Vee and she started it.

The thing that actually frightened me, in the good way: Pace asking "Is she all right? Is she happy?" and Randi snapping "She is *radiant*" and him refusing it — "That isn't the same answer." He checks the gauge. He asked. For one second he was the one worried about the girl and Randi was the one saying *stop checking the gauge, I know exactly what I'm doing, I have always known what I'm doing* — and that "always known" is the scariest line she's said in two books, because the whole chapter just proved she *doesn't.* She loses the better part of a minute to his hands. She has to stop for air mid-sentence for the first time all evening. She wouldn't let the kiss end either — *"Saturday she wouldn't let it end, Pace. She came after me. She took it."* Both of them reaching now, both filing it under the plan. They chose a starving girl to feed and the feeding is eating the two of them alive and they can't say it to each other except sideways, during, when it slips out.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: THEY POINTED AT HER ON A LAWN. And the one who built the trap is the one caught worst in it and she said "it makes me feel real and some nights it frightens me" and asked him to say something nice and he weighed it because "she would know the difference" and gave her "you are extraordinary" and she held his arm and wouldn't let go. I don't trust him an inch — "I'd never share your photograph" / "I didn't add that he had taken his own time over every one of them, or that he had never once pointed at a woman he did not want" — he's running both of them and he narrates the omissions right to me. But Randi I'm just *sad* for now, clean sad, the way Cassie's sad for Vee. Three people reaching and only Vee doesn't know the table was set.

The scar's still not paid. "I don't go back" got reconfirmed last chapter and the family wound under Randi ("she's alone," Sheri at Thanksgiving, Randi's own "some nights it frightens me") is all orbiting the same empty center and nobody's named it. But after a chapter like this I'll forgive the withholding one more round, because this is the first time in a while the book put the whole thing — the plot engine AND the heat AND the hurt — on one plate and let me eat.
