And 66 is — a lot. It's the longest held-breath sex scene in the book and it's *built*, genuinely built, the paint and the fruit and the "keep still" turned into its own grammar: still means the brush, moving means the brush goes away, until she stops holding the pose and just gives the choosing up and *that's* when she comes. But.

Here's where the book and I parted a little tonight. The gesso. He's painting her with her own wetness and *doesn't kiss her mouth* this one time, no taste follows, and the chapter stands over it and points: "Walnut. Parsley. Grape… This had brought nothing." And then later, the kiss after he's inside her — "a trace. Salt first… something faintly sour under it, gone when her tongue sought it." The taste thread. The salt "that had not been there." I've been braced on that dread since the breakfast chapter and the book keeps tapping the glass, *notice this, notice this.* And tonight for the first time it felt like tapping instead of planting. I don't need the withheld-taste to be underlined with a whole paragraph of fruit-inventory so I don't miss that this one was different. I got it. I got it two chapters ago. The thing I loved about this book was that the key sat on the table and Vee couldn't read it and nobody explained it to *me*. Tonight it leaned over and explained it to me.

And the coda. The coda is the real tell. She reaches for the phone, thinks about Randi's voice on the *other* photo — "Showing him your toes, eh?" — and tries to hear what that voice would do with *this* one, and can't, holds her own breath waiting on it, and "Not that she'd ever show her. God, no. This one's too much." That's the underground irony surfacing — the whole bed she just got wrecked in and the first place her mind goes is *what would Randi say* — and I've been reading toward exactly this. So why am I not more thrilled? Because 61. Because the book already *told* me from the outside. Chapter 61 put me in Pace and Randi's bed and had Randi say "I want her to know exactly what she does to me" out loud, Pace say "she makes me happy," the whole third game spread flat on the sheet with the lights on. I don't want to watch Vee not-hear-herself anymore if I've already been shown the answer in full. The dramatic irony only has teeth when I know and she doesn't and I'm leaning in. Now I know because a chapter *showed* me, cleanly, no work required, and Vee's obliviousness has gone from unbearable to a little bit like watching someone I can't warn do the thing I already watched the other two plan. The gap's still there. The *suspense* of the gap leaked out in 61.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the paint chapter is genuinely hot and the little blonde-that-wasn't is the best character they've introduced in 200 pages BUT the book has started doing the thing, it keeps nudging me about the taste like I'm slow, and I think it accidentally told me the whole ending two chapters ago and now I'm just waiting for the girl to catch up." I'm still in. The prose is still real, the heat is real, Sheri bought them a lot of goodwill. But the thing that made this book singular — subtext you trusted me to carry — flickered tonight. One more underlined revelation and I'll feel managed.
