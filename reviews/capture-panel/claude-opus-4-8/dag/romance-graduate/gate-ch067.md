REACTION

Oh, Cassie. *Cassie.* Of course it's Cassie. Of course the one clean room in the whole book is the one who finally says the sentence out loud, and of course she doesn't say the dramatic one — not *what is she to you*, not *are you cheating on Pace*, not even *what did I just watch on the walk up*. She says "Is this what you want?" Flat. The way she once asked *do you love him*. And that's the knife, because it's the only question in seventy chapters that has no exit built into it. Pace asks things with the reason left blank so Vee can fill it with yes. Randi asks things already answered so Vee just agrees. Cassie asks the one thing that requires Vee to go *look* — and she looks, and the book finally says the thing I've been screaming about since the dark-bed chapter, in Vee's own interior, out loud: "There was a shape there, close and large, and she came up against the edge of it and stepped back off before she could make out its size." That's it. That's the whole book. The thing is now big enough that she can feel its *edge* and she still steps off. Not can't see it — won't measure it.

And then she does the ugliest small thing, the thing that made me actually wince: she reaches for the oldest plank in the room — *you've never liked her* — and lays it down to walk out on, and Cassie "stepped over it without once looking down." God. That's two years of friendship Vee just tried to weaponize to not answer a question, and Cassie clocks it as the move it is and won't be moved. I've watched Vee crop everybody — the shrunk-sweater lie, the PG flash-version — but this is the first time I've seen her try to crop Cassie by attacking her, and it bounced right off, and the shame of that bouncing is all over the page.

But here's what undid me, the turn I didn't expect from a two-page chapter: Cassie doesn't press. She watches, she keeps whatever's in her face — the eyebrow from the skirt night — and then she just says *you didn't have dinner.* And holds out the Cocoa Pebbles. And THAT'S the mercy. She asked the real question, got the plank thrown at her, and instead of pushing Vee off the ledge she hands her a want the size of a bowl. "Here was a want she could find." After a whole chapter — a whole weekend — of Vee unable to answer *do you want to sleep with a woman*, unable to answer *is this what you want*, the book lets her finally answer a want cleanly, and it's cereal. I could cry. It's the tenderest thing in the stretch and it's a box of Cocoa Pebbles held out from a lower bunk by a girl who already knows and is choosing, again, to love her instead of make her bleed.

What I noticed: the book KNOWS I've been counting. The two-page length is doing work — it's the comedown, the held breath after the dance and the lie-in-the-dark and the neck-kiss at the door. It's not a bridge that stalls; it's a bridge that lands. I didn't resent the shortness. I'd have resented two more pages. This was exactly the size of the scene.

And the lie is still sitting there cold in her from last chapter — "the first one she had ever told him" — and now she can't even lie to Cassie, she just *runs out of words*, "she never ran out," and that's almost worse than the lie. The lie she could build. Here she's got nothing. The architecture of her own denial is finally out of material.

I'm scared. Not thrilled-scared, actually-scared. Because the thing with the edge she won't measure — I know its size even if she won't, and Brooke's clock is still ticking somewhere offscreen, and Randi said "I've wanted to do that for the longest time" out loud now, and Vee asked for *brunch* out of the ringing in her neck. Everyone's moving toward the thing and only Vee is pretending the floor's still there. Cassie's the smoke alarm and Vee just threw a coat over it and ate cereal.
