REACTION

Oh, this is the one I didn't know I was waiting for. He gets a POV. After forty-seven chapters of watching Vee try to read the back of his head, the book finally turns him around and lets me stand behind his eyes — and the first thing it does, the very first line, is show me the decision to withhold. "He would not bring her down today." He carried something in from the truck and chose it could keep. "Long practice at deciding a thing could keep." That's the loaded gun from another angle. He's a man who files things and doesn't look at the pile — same move as Vee in the laundry room, and now I know where she learned it, or from whom, or that they're just the same animal.

But here's what got me, and it's not what I braced for. I came in ready for the con to show its seams — for Pace-at-the-diner to be Pace running an op, cold, congratulating himself. And instead Sheri takes him apart. Sheri, five feet of her, two-tone perm, too much makeup worn like a dare — Sheri is the second clean reader in this book and she does what Cassie is too gentle to do. She won't let him stack his bricks. "That's a hell of a lot of reasons for a man who's already got the girl. She didn't ask you for reasons." God. And when he lays up his defense — I'm there every weekend, I gave her the weeknights, I said she's the most important thing — "she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something." He KNOWS. He watched it land wrong and he catalogued it and he still won't move. "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." "I can't." Not won't — can't. And Sheri, no mercy: "from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."

That "I can't" is the whole thing cracking open a different way than I expected. Because I've been reading the not-saying as method — the dom's withholding, the operator keeping the one coin he'll never spend so she stays hungry. And it might still be that. But this chapter plants the other reading right next to it: that "I don't go back," that "this is my home," that Daphne-shaped wound — the word costs him something structural, and he genuinely cannot get it out. Which is worse AND more human. Sheri even flags it: "Most men'd just lie." He won't lie to make it easy. He'll amputate the word before he'll counterfeit it. That's not kindness and it's not pure control either — it's damage with a spine, and it's the most he's ever let me see.

And then — this is the part I'll text a friend about — the book does NOT let him off. It gives him the interiority and then immediately shows me the vanity in it. "The philosophy of your food." Sheri: "Lord almighty. Get over yourself." He's a man who thinks Vee is special because she "gets what it's for," the idea under it — and Sheri, who likes him and gives him hell and wants nothing off him, translates it flat every time. "You mean the sex is good." "Bet those long legs feel like Christmas." She keeps puncturing him and he keeps laughing because he needs it, because everybody else wants something off him and she's the only one who doesn't. That's real. That friendship is the realest non-Vee relationship in the book.

But — and my dread-radar is going the whole chapter — look what he's not telling her. "She find out about the brunette?" "No. Not like that." The brunette is Randi. Sheri thinks Randi is just some sorority girl he brought dancing, "a lot, though," harem applicant, never learned her name. Sheri doesn't know there's a plan. So even in the confessional, even getting worked over by the one person who won't let him lie — there's the whole architecture sitting underneath the table that he simply doesn't put on it. He's honest about everything except the thing. Same as Randi in Gstaad, honest to Vee about the fairytale and never the thing. Same as Vee, honest with Cassie about the dress and not the thing. Everyone in this book tells the truth around the exact center. That's the machine and it's beautiful and it's making me a little crazy.

The single-vs-double burger bit — the new kid setting the double down in front of the man and the single in front of five-foot Sheri "with the grave confidence of arithmetic," her sliding them back to true — that's a whole character in one gesture. I love her. Which is why I'm scared. This book introduces the clean ones so it can spend them.

The thing that keeps my trust: the narration still isn't telling me he's right. It's giving him his reasons in full and then standing next to Sheri while she calls them shitty. The moment I've been dreading — where the book tips over and says the withholding is wisdom, the man is just good — did NOT come. Sheri IS the book refusing to let it come. As long as he has a Sheri across the amber tabletop saying "she didn't ask you for reasons," I'm not being sold the con. I'm being shown it, from the inside, with its own justifications intact and its own witness calling it what it is. That's the deal I stay for.
