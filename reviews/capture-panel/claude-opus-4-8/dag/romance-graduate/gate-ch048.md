REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been waiting for without knowing I wanted it. After three chapters of Vee alone in the cold and Randi alone in the cold and both of them holding phones over empty fields — I finally get to be inside Pace's head, and the first thing he does is the exact thing the whole book has taught me to watch for: *he made the decision before the door had swung shut — he would not bring her down today.* There it is. The man who "doesn't go back." He walked in carrying something and filed it under "can keep," and I know by now that the things Pace decides "can keep" are the scar, the wound, the whole locked room. So the chapter opens on him doing his signature move to himself, and I leaned all the way in.

And Sheri. God, I love Sheri. She's the first person in forty-eight chapters who is completely outside the gravity well. She doesn't want him, isn't impressed, will say so to his face — which means she's the only mirror in the book that isn't bent. And the book knew exactly what to do with that: she's the one who finally says it plain. "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." "From this seat, that feels pretty shitty." I actually exhaled. Somebody said it. After Vee circling it in three different cold beds, after all that tender machinery, a five-foot woman eating his fries just lays the ledger flat on the amber tabletop and he *looks down.* He looked down. That's the closest thing to him losing I've gotten.

But here's what chilled me, and it's the good chill: watch what he does when she pushes. "I told her she was the most important thing in my life. I said it, out loud, in words." He's keeping a record of what he's spent. He lays it up "brick on brick" — the weekends, the weeknights he "gave her," the declaration — and he cannot understand why it doesn't "come to something." That's the horror in daylight again. He thinks love is an accounting of provable deeds — *believe what I do, words are cheap* — and he genuinely cannot see that the one withheld word is the whole wall. And the book lets Sheri name it without letting HIM understand it. "She didn't ask you for reasons." He's a man who solves, and she handed him the one thing that isn't a problem to solve, and he answered a question next to it because that's the only question he has answers to. Exactly what he did to Vee at the door. He does it to everyone. He'd do it to a priest.

Also — Sheri grieves. "Couldn't get right with me not talking to my people." The Paige thing, the estrangement, the sing-song *call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas* killed flat. And Pace's line — "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice" — landed on me sideways, because HE is the one choosing not to say the word, choosing the locked room, and he'd call that grief management too. The cell he's assembling isn't a harem, it's a collection of the unhoused: Sheri alone, Vee starving, the people he feeds because they're alone on holidays. He gathers the wounded and warms them and never once unlocks his own door. That's what I couldn't stop turning over.

The "she wants a declaration" / "is that so hard" / "I can't" exchange is the cleanest the wound has ever been shown, and I notice he STILL doesn't say why he can't. Not even to Sheri, who's safe, who'd never use it as currency. The scar stays shut even here. Daphne's still in the chamber. The detonation I've been waiting for didn't come — but this is the first time I watched someone get their hand on the doorknob.

And then the lightness at the end — the philosophy of his food, Sheri going "Lord almighty, get over yourself," the real laugh — and I'll admit the book earned that laugh off me too. It's the warmth doing its job. But I clocked that the chapter's last gift is HIM getting to set the heavy thing "further off," comforted, fed, let off the hook by a friend, while Vee is still in Ohio not sleeping. He gets the diner. She gets the twin bed. The asymmetry is the whole book.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: HE KEEPS A RECEIPT OF EVERYTHING HE'S GIVEN HER AND CALLS IT LOVE and a woman eating his french fries just told him to his face it's not enough and he LOOKED DOWN. Also the brunette "never did learn my name" — Sheri clocked Randi as a predator in four seconds at a bar and nobody in this book listens to Sheri.
