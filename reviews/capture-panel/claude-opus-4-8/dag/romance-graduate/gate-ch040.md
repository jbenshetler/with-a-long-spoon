REACTION

Okay, so this is the breather, and I don't resent a breather — I've said a hundred times the best scene in a book can be two people talking, or in this case one woman not talking, just swimming. And Rivers earns the form: the way the words come off the chapter the way they come off *her* down there, "it took the words off things one at a time until what was left was the shape underneath." That's a real sentence about what repetitive physical work does to a churning head, and I believed every length of it. The lane as a yard-wide strip of blue, the count arriving whether she wants it or not, *do you love him* getting in off the third wall in Cassie's flat voice — that's good. I was with her.

And the thing I actually sat up for, the only thing that spiked in me the whole chapter: *whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting, out of two months of being looked at like that.* THERE. That's the first time Vee has turned the doubt on the right thing. Not "did he mind about the hairbrush" (who cares, drawer boy gave you a drawer, we did that chapter), but — is this man real, or is he a surface I've been skimming my own hunger across? And the book is cruel in exactly the right way: she gets four strokes of it and the wall takes it away. She can't hold the dangerous thought long enough to finish it. That's the whole machine right there, and *I* know what she doesn't — that he and Randi built her this spring — so watching her almost-almost get a fingernail under the seam and then roll off the wall and lose it gutted me a little. The one awake person in the warm house, still getting away with something, still not seeing.

But. I have to be honest with my thumb. This is the second quiet chapter in a row — 39 was Cassie on the bean bag, now 40 is seventy-five minutes of lap-counting, and in both the only other person is a voice remembered, not a body in the room. No Randi across a table drawing the wet out of her. No Pace. No heat at all, not a simmer — and I came for both, depth AND the charge, and this stretch is handing me depth on an empty plate. The brunch chapter (the sticky-fingers one) is still the high-water mark in my head and I want the book to get back up to that register, where the interiority and the charge are the same thing happening in the same minute. Two chapters of processing after it is the book catching its breath, and I'll give it this one, but if 41 is a third quiet room I'm going to start skimming for the green Mercedes.

What keeps me is that doubt-crack and the fact that the loaded gun — the plan, Randi's drowning daylight want, the scar still owed — is all still on the mantel. I'm not leaving before it goes off.
