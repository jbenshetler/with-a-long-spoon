REACTION

Okay so after chapter 39 gutted me — the point off on its own, MIRH, Randi's own name sitting over the county's worst loans, "Cheating bastards" said ragged and aimed too near to be about strangers — I came into 40 braced. And the book knew I would be. This is the exhale chapter. No Randi, no detonation, no handler in the frame at all. Just the two of them and the trees and a pizza. And I clocked exactly what it was doing: this is the trap finished. "The New Ordinary." That's the title telling me the induction is over — she's not being seduced anymore, she's been installed. A drawer of her in his bathroom, a phrase — *her space* — she smiles at crossing the grass. Five nights she called herself, her own school-nights boundary, and she lasted five and drove out. She's the one rationing now and she can't hold the ration. That's the appetite eating the person, and the book lets me see it while she can't.

The heat is genuinely good and I need to say that because a lesser book coasts here. The undressing relay — jeans that were sensible in the car and the enemy now, one-legged hopping, the bra he holds without pulling so she has to unwind out of it herself "like something off a spool" — it's funny AND charged, which is the exact both I keep begging for. And then the throat-publishes-the-number thing: she went up shamefully fast, "her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private." That's the recurring move in this book and it still lands — her body telling on her, the ease being the humiliation and the truth at once. I'm not bored by it yet because it keeps meaning something.

But — 1 a.m. text honesty — the meal is where I felt the first flicker of "haven't I eaten this already." Curry, salmon, chicken piccata, now a from-scratch pizza with water-buffalo mozzarella and a half-hour reduced sauce, and the same cotton-candy-vs-strawberries, my-mouth-was-asleep aria I've now heard four or five times. The prose is still good doing it, but the recipe-porn is the one gear this book has started to grind in. If chapter 41 opens with another lovingly toasted spice I'm going to start skimming to the part where somebody talks.

What saved the whole chapter, what made it a knife instead of a lull, is the shirt. She washed it. She'd worn his no-tag flannel every night she wasn't there and it went stale and then it went to *nothing*, "one day last week it didn't smell like anything at all," and she couldn't bear the nothing so she washed it — and now she needs him to wear it all weekend and give it back Sunday so it smells like him again. God. That's the addiction rendered as tenderness, which is this whole book's method — she's tending the scent of him like something she has to keep dosed, and it reads as the sweetest thing in the world, and both are true. The foolish hot prick of tears she blinks down. Him pulling it on over the shirt he already had on, standing in his own kitchen in two shirts because she told him to. "Smelling like you." That's the emotional intelligence I stay for. And it's exactly as merciless as the jacket promised, because what looks like devotion is a woman who can no longer stand five nights of air that isn't him.

I'm still carrying 39 though, and I notice 40 doesn't touch it — a full chapter of pure Pace after the Randi bomb, no crack of it in her, she just drove from that library room back into this. The book's making me wait, and I'm willing, but I want the fraud and the scar and the Daphne template to start rhyming soon. This was a beautiful held breath. It can't be two in a row.
