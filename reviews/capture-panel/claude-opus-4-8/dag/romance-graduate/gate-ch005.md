Oh, this one got me. And it got me in exactly the sick, delicious way the jacket promised — because I'm *ahead* of her. That's the whole trick of this book and Chapter 5 is where I felt it land in my stomach for the first time. I've watched Randi go home destroyed in Chapter 2, I watched the pointing game on the quad, I know Pace and Randi picked Vee off the dining hall steps like a dress off a rack, and now here's Vee, forty minutes into an integral she can't solve, having *no idea* that the entire afternoon — the study spot, the stuck problem, the cute stranger crossing the room — is furniture that's been moved into place around her. And I *fell for him with her anyway.* That's the part that unsettled me. I know exactly what he's doing and I still felt the room settle a half-degree when he walked over. God.

The math seduction. I have read a thousand men be competent at Vee to make her wet, and it's always the same — the alpha who Just Knows Things, the mansplain dressed as flirtation. This was the opposite and it was so much hotter for it. He asked what her *professor* said the trick was. He wrote the ugly formula and then told her not to carry it around. He gave her back the pencil "at the exact line where she could solve it herself." That's the tell, and Vee's econ-brain half-catches it — *the path of a person who wasn't performing* — and I loved that her body got there a full second before her mind. That's the interiority I've been starving for. She's not a dumb girl being seduced; she's a *sharp* girl watching a genuinely elegant piece of restraint and mistaking it for chemistry, when it's chemistry *and* a technique, and I know it's a technique, and I still wanted him to call her.

Euler's formula — the thing at right angles that turns two impossible things into one plain knowable thing. Come on. That's the book winking at me. That IS the book. Two people at right angles to a girl who never knew there was a plan. "Substitution." The chapter's called Substitution. I clocked it and it gave me a chill instead of an eyeroll, which is the difference between this and everything else on my Kindle.

And Cassie. *Cassie.* "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter and now "he looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." Cassie sees it. Cassie's the only one in the room not enchanted, and she keeps saying the true flat thing and Vee keeps smoothing it over. I'm scared for Vee through Cassie's eyes. That's such a good use of a side character — she's the reader's proxy, the one going *this is off,* and Vee laughs it away.

The thing I keep turning over: Randi's hand on the coffee cup. Up and down, no sip happening, twice. Vee reads it as *she's bored.* I read it as Randi sitting six inches away from the man she was destroyed by in Chapter 1, watching him aim all that attention at the girl they chose together, and *not being able to drink her coffee.* That detail is doing so much quiet violence. Randi begged on that bench. Randi decided never to come out from behind the face again. And now she's the one who says "you should go out with him" and does the closer's work — *he forgot his coffee, a man doesn't do that.* She's finishing the sale on her own boyfriend. What is it costing her? The book won't tell me from inside her head this chapter and that withholding is working on me.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "ok this book is doing the thing where I KNOW it's a trap, I watched them set it, and I'm still going 'call her, PLEASE call her' — I think I'm the girl. I think the book made me the girl on purpose and I'm not even mad."

The one thing keeping me from a perfect score is that structurally this was a soft chapter, heat-wise — no bench, no bodies, just a coffee shop and a phone number. But it didn't matter, because the dread and the interiority carried it, and the last line — she lets herself want the phone to light up — put my thumb on the next page before I finished thinking about it.
