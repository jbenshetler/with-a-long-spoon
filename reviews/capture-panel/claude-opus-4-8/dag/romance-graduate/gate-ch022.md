Okay, so I braced for this to be the fifth debrief and I was ready to be impatient — but this one did something the others didn't, and I have to give it to Helen Rivers: she put both of them in the same room at the same time, and she used the room itself. The Simpson's paradox lecture running under the whole scene is doing so much work it made the hair on my arms stand up. Same numbers, nobody touched one of them, and the answer flips depending on how you aggregate. That's the whole book. That's *Vee*. Every single yes she gave was real, freely given, out-hitting every boy she's known — and put the two years together, step back and pool the data, and the picture is somebody else's. The professor with his little red dot on the one number at the foot of the column, saying nobody touched it — that's the con articulating itself again, the way the error-correcting codes did, the way the MRI signal-in-noise did. This book keeps handing me the key to its own lock and trusting me to be too warm to use it. I'm not too warm. I clocked it. And it still got me.

But here's what actually reached me, and it wasn't Randi's lockpicking for once — it was Cassie. That forty seconds where Vee dutifully goes down into the numbers being a good student, and comes back up to realize Cassie *never went down at all.* Cassie turned a few degrees in her seat and watched what the dress did to her friend, "because the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." I felt that in my chest. Cassie doesn't care about the dress. Cassie cracked the made bed hours ago and let Randi have the reveal anyway. And that little "Was it a good night?" — "Yes." "Oh, yes." — and something easing in Cassie's face and her picking the highlighter back up. That's the one clean love in this whole book and it's the one Vee's editing toward the margins. The eyebrow that stays in reserve. The eyebrow that goes up when Randi commandeers Saturday and comes back down. Cassie sees the whole architecture and she's choosing not to pull the seam because pulling it might lose her the friend faster. God, I'm scared for both of them.

And Randi. "You lucky slut," low from the aisle, and the note it hit — Vee's body keeping the account since eight, the good soreness, sure the whole room could read it. And then Randi reaches in and finds the one snag in the gold day: the shoes. Solves it in her hands before Vee's finished naming it. "That's not a problem, that's a *date*. You and me, Saturday." Don't you dare tell me no. And it goes through Vee *warm.* That's the thread I've been waiting to move in daylight and here it is — the shoe date is the next lunch dressed as girl-fun, and Vee's body-answer to Randi is right there in that warmth. Randi always finds the one thing still unsolved and makes herself the one who solves it. That's how she stays indispensable. I see the machine. I see it and I'm still charmed, which is the crime.

Last line gutted me quietly: on time now for nothing, the dress three blocks back, "still shown to not one living soul." She's holding the most her she's ever been and it's sealed in a bag, and the only people who get the account are the two who authored her. That's the loneliness under all this warmth and I felt it land.

Where I'm still impatient: I've now had FIVE debriefs and Pace-and-Randi in a room on the record STILL hasn't happened. But this chapter earned its keep by being about Cassie's seeing more than Randi's harvesting, and by the paradox doing the thematic heavy lifting instead of another warmup story. It bought back my patience. Barely, but it did.
