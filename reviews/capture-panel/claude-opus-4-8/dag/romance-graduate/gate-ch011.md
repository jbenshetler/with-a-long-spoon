Okay so — no Pace, no bed, no heat, and I still sat all the way forward for this one, which surprises me a little. This is the third soft chapter in a row by my count and I came in with my arms crossed, owed the both-at-once, ready to be annoyed. And the book knew that, I think, because it stopped feeding me Pace and fed me the thing I actually said I was reading for: Randi's want with the lid rattling on it.

The hand. *That's* the chapter. "Vee's my dear friend" and the cool damp hand closing over hers on the white tabletop, in front of Brooke, in no hurry to let go. That's the first time the Randi-line has been laid on the table literally instead of held behind her eyes, and Vee felt the *weight* on the warmth and couldn't name it and didn't want to — "a warmth that came with weight on it." God. That's the jacket's promise finally showing skin: *without quite letting herself know it, wanting the woman beside her.* And it's smart that it came dressed as rescue, because now Vee's read it as loyalty, as being chosen out loud, and filed it under gratitude instead of under the thing it actually is. Randi spent something "without appearing to count it" — except Randi counts everything, that's the whole architecture, so either she's running the play or it genuinely cost her and she's the one slipping. I can't tell which and that's exactly where I like being.

But let me say the two things that actually chilled me, because they're both small.

One: Cassie at the top. My alarm proxy just got walked off the page — "I'll see you at the dorm," easy, already going — and Vee didn't watch her go because Randi had her. She's doing the consent-brief again, this time about dropping her best friend, and handing it to me as evidence, and I want to cover her mouth again. That "let it lie" is the whole book in three words.

Two: Randi's date story. Tall, dreamy, dressed like money, talked about himself all night — "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." That's not a catastrophe, that's a *setup.* She built the exact negative of Pace so Vee would hear her own night as the answer to it. Because of him." — and Vee had the dignified version *right there ready,* "I made an exception," and it left her as the smaller truer thing instead. Randi does to Vee what she did to Pace in chapter seven: milks the report, holds the want, calls it friendship. The technique's identical and I can see the seams and Vee can't, and that gap is the only suspense the book needs.

The high chair I loved as a picture — "she had been set up on a shelf," feet not reaching the floor, a thing the room could turn and look at. And the cardigan "covered her like an apology" in a room of bare arms. That's real writing, that's Vee's whole shame-body in one garment, and it's why I don't resent the soft run the way I would in a worse book.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the girl finally put her hand on her IN PUBLIC and called her "dear" and our girl thinks it's LOYALTY. also she just quietly ditched her best friend and told herself it was healthy. i'm not okay.*

This chapter earned its place. A fourth like it and I'm skimming for the door. I want the house. I want a room with two of them and the lid off.
