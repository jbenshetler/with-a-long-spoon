Oh, this one got its hooks in me differently than I expected. I came in braced for the third quiet chapter in a row and half-ready to be annoyed, and instead Randi did the thing I've been circling all book and it made my stomach drop.

Because watch what she does. Vee tells the date like a girl in love, and Randi doesn't just listen — she takes each true thing Vee's dressed up and gently strips the clothes back off it. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Vee has the *dignified version sitting right there ready* — I made an exception — and Randi keeps reaching in and pulling out the smaller, truer thing instead, and Vee hands it over every time and feels *grateful* for it. That's the whole con in miniature and it's the most erotic thing in the chapter and nobody touches anybody. This is the both-at-once I was owed. Not a sex scene — a seduction scene, running at full charge, disguised as girl-talk over smoothies. I felt it in my teeth.

And the "ask and then ravish" beat — Randi dropping to Vee's own volume, "the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume," half up the trail beside her — God. That's Randi's want getting out through the mask for one second. The book told me flat out it was the one moment she stopped performing and matched Vee's register, and it slid the two of them into the same fantasy together. Vee doesn't clock it. I clocked it. That's the gap the whole book lives in.

But the part that actually scared me — genuinely, coldly — is the front of the chapter. Cassie. That look going *past* Vee to Randi, and by the time it came back "whatever had been in them was folded away." Cassie saw something and put it away because Vee wasn't reachable. "It isn't really her place." She's building the excuse to leave her clean friend behind and she thinks it's a healthy thought about people having their own people. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." THAT. That's the isolation happening in real time and she's narrating it to herself as maturity. My alarm-proxy just got walked out of the room, waving, saying she'd see her at the dorm. I hate it. I mean I love it, it's exactly right, and I hate it.

The Brooke moment worked me too. It cost Randi nothing, or it cost her exactly what she meant to spend. The chapter is titled "Dear." She named her. That's ownership dressed as tenderness and it landed on Vee as being finally, publicly picked. "She had a place now, and the place was here, next to Randi." The door swinging shut on Cassie at the top of the chapter and the door opening to Randi's world at the bottom — same chapter, matched hinges.

The bra joke landed as real, actual human warmth, which is the trap — the con is threaded with genuine affection and that's what makes it unbearable. I laughed with them on the shelf and then hated that I did.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "she left Cassie in the lecture hall to go get emotionally undressed over a beet smoothie and she thinks she's THRIVING." Also: chapter's called "Dear" and I need to lie down.

No new heat on the body, still — this is the fourth soft chapter and I'm aware of it. But it wasn't a stall. I want the hike now. Sunday. Him. I want a thread to actually touch.
