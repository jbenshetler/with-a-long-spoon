REACTION

Oh, there it is. There it *is*. Ten chapters of the middle going soft on me and I was one bridge-chapter away from shelving this, and then chapter eleven walks me up a mountain and unbuttons a wet shirt one button at a time and I forgot I was mad.

What got me — and I want to be honest because I've been burned by this exact setup a hundred times — is that the heat came back *through her*. It wasn't him circling her, doing the courtly-dom thing at her while she melts. She's the one who came up on her knees on the bench. She's the one who rapped the glass three times and crooked her finger and made him come back in. He sat there. He didn't lean in, didn't lift a hand, didn't do the alpha-grab I could've recited in my sleep — he just was *glad*, and the gladness was the whole answer, and God help me that landed harder than any hand on a throat would have. The jacket promised shame-into-heat and this is the first time the book actually put it in my body: her mother's voice saying *floozy* in her mother's exact word and her thighs pressing once, hard, on their own. The voice IS the heat. That's the trick the whole book's been building and this chapter finally spent it.

And the horseradish. I know that sounds insane but the sandwich did as much work as the striptease. The hunger-made-first, the food-worth-the-hunger, the hike existing *to make her hungry* — "that was the trick of it, that was what it was for" — that's the con stated out loud in a sandwich, and it's also just genuinely erotic, the plain animal rightness of needing a thing and being handed it. This writer knows appetite is appetite is appetite. The cherry pit. "We take nothing and leave nothing." She gets taught a manners-thing she was never taught and he doesn't make her small doing it — and I clocked exactly what that IS. That's the grooming. Leave No Trace is the chapter title and it's the *method*, it's what he does to her, no evidence, every yes hers. I saw it and it still worked on me and I resent it in the good way, the way I told myself back at chapter ten I needed the book to keep doing.

The dress. The russet silk she pointed at in the lobby magazine and then set back on the shelf — "you put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking" — and he says *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and doesn't pick it up again. And I know. I KNOW that dress is going to arrive. He filed it. He's a man who folds cherry pits into wax paper; he does not forget a want you showed him. That's a loaded gun on the induction-dinner mantel and I want to be in the room when it goes off.

Two things kept it from being pure swoon, and they're the two things keeping me trusting the book. One: him standing in the rain with his back to her, giving her his only dry shirt AND the truck, taking the cold for nothing she could see — that's the tenderness-as-technique thing, and part of me went *oh that's lovely* and part of me went *that's a move, that's the most expensive move in the book, he engineered a moment where she has to invite him back into his own truck.* But then his hand was actually shaking. He'd actually been cold. He'd been "a long way from the question of whether he was cold." And that's the jacket's promise creeping in — how real it gets for the ones who started it. He lost the thread of his own plan for a second up there. I want more of that. That crack is the most interesting thing in him.

Two — and this is the one that made me actually gasp — the cop. The siren eating the end of her sentence, the thing bigger than any words she had and she never gets to say it, he's pulled away with the lights behind him and gone. I love a chapter that won't let the girl land the big feeling. It's the froth pulled back out from under. And it's *funny*, in a book that hasn't let itself be funny — the fire-lane, the red curb, the campus cop breaking up the holiest moment of her life. Perfect thumb-mover.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: SHE unbuttoned the shirt. HE just sat there being glad. I have never been this undone by a man not moving. Also I think a man is about to buy her a dress and I'm going to lose my mind.
