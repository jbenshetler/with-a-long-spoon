REACTION

Okay. Okay. This is the chapter I've been circling the whole book waiting to see if it could actually land, and it landed, and I'm a little furious about how well.

Because here's the thing — I came in braced. I know this man. I know the apple, the froyo, the wine. I know he *finds where a woman portions herself and takes the portioning away and she feels it as freedom,* I wrote that down myself. So the boot scene, the moleskin, the sandwich made that morning with the lettuce laid against the bread so the wet doesn't go through — every single one of those is him doing the thing, and I was ready to roll my eyes at the hundredth demonstration of Pace Cares With His Hands. And instead the book did something that actually got me, which is: it let HER drive. The whole second half is Vee. He gets out of the truck and stands in the rain with his back to her and that's the master move, right, that's the designed gesture, the shirt-and-the-truck-both, I saw the architecture of it instantly — but then the book doesn't let him collect. *She* raps on the glass. *She* crooks the finger. *She* undoes the buttons with her eyes on his the whole way. He sits there and doesn't lean in and doesn't lift a hand and the gladness is the whole of the answer. I have read four hundred truck scenes and the man always closes the distance. This one didn't, and it was filthier for it.

And the button thing — looking up, keeping his eyes, *she didn't have to look down for the fourth, she could've taken it by feel and kept his eyes the whole way, she looked down anyway and his eyes went with hers.* That's the hottest sentence in the book so far and nobody touched anybody. That's what I came here for. That's the both-at-once I keep saying I'm starving for — the heat is the interiority, the mother-voice saying *floozie* IS the pull of arousal, she's turned on by being exactly the thing she was warned against and she gives it to him to see. God.

But — and this is why I'm still not comfortable, which is the right way to be — the reach. She reaches for his hand to bring it to her mouth, to start the next thing, *because her body was already going at the next thing and the next thing was more,* and his hand is shaking. He's freezing. He's been freezing the whole time, he gave her his only dry shirt and went and stood in it. And the book lets her be the one who stops. *You're freezing. We should go. Please.* She mothers him. She, for once, is the one reading the body in the room instead of being read. I don't fully know what to do with that. Part of me went soft — oh, it's mutual, she took care of him, the care runs both ways now. And part of me, the part holding my carry-forward notes, is saying: or that's the next escalation of the exact same hook. He engineered a situation where his need became visible and real and she got to meet it, and now she's not just the girl being cared for, she's bonded to him as someone who cares FOR. That's deeper water than the froyo cup. That's the thing that doesn't wash off.

And the cop. The cop, oh my god. The siren bark that takes the end of her sentence — she was about to say the big thing and put a small word in its place, and the book doesn't even let her have the small word, it just rips her out of the cab and she's standing under the gutter-sheet in his flannel watching his taillights go. That's a chapter ending that knows exactly what it's doing. Left the whole confession unspoken, sitting in her, which means it has to go somewhere, which means — Randi. She's going to take all of this to the white table on Monday, the shaking hand and the buttons and the thing she didn't get to say, and pour it into the one person who is spending her like currency. I felt the whole machinery click one tooth forward even inside how moved I was. That's the sick genius of this book. I'm being worked the exact way Vee is and I can feel it and I can't stop.

The russet dress. The russet dress almost undid me more than the buttons. She points at it in the magazine, *that one, I want that one,* and then puts it back on the shelf herself — "there's a black dress in the back of my closet, it'll be good enough" — and he doesn't pick it up. He just says *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and lets it lie there on the rock between them. And I know, I KNOW, that dress is coming. That dress is going to show up russet silk in her size and I already know whose hands send it and I already know she'll feel it as being seen. I'm watching the gun get loaded and the book trusts me enough not to explain it to me, which is the one thing it keeps doing right that keeps me from quitting.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "he gave her his ONLY dry shirt and stood in the rain and she undid every button with her eyes on his the whole time and nobody touched and then his hand was shaking and SHE stopped it and I have never been so turned on and so scared at the same time. also the dress. remember the dress."

This did not feel like a bridge chapter and it is not the second quiet one in a row — it's the opposite, it's the furnace. No skimming. I'm in.
