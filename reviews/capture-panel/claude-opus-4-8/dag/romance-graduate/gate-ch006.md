REACTION

Okay so this is the first genuinely *light* chapter of the whole book and I could feel the book knew it needed to give me a breather — and the horrible thing is, it worked, I liked the froyo date, I was smiling, and I hated that I was smiling because I know exactly what this is.

That's the whole engine of this thing, isn't it. The jacket told me. Chapter 1 showed me Randi cracked open and deciding *never again* on that bench. Chapter 3 showed me the pointing game, Randi hunting Vee down like a retriever bringing back a bird. And now I'm inside Vee, and Vee thinks she got *chosen* — the right pen, the high table, "love you girl," and now a man who says *may I call you* and waits out her sentences. And every single thing that's landing on Vee as the-nicest-anyone's-been-to-me-in-years is a thing I watched them build in a bedroom. "You put chocolate on tart and it's an argument the whole way down" — sir, you have thought about *most things,* I have read the chapter where you thought about the angle of a light pole. The froyo has a *system* the way the bench had a system.

What genuinely got me was the small stuff, because Rivers is playing it so quiet. "Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." The apple slices in Chapter 1 — Randi trimming herself to nothing, the thing that *moved* Pace. And here's Vee's cup getting away from her, the cone leaning, and she doesn't care, and I got a genuine chill because I know that unportioning-yourself is the exact hunger he feeds on. He's not going to fix Vee's relationship to appetite out of kindness. He's going to *use* it. The froyo is the apple. It's the same move, one house over.

And "I've thought about most things" — no apology, and she laughs, and he "looked pleased in a contained way, as if he'd set something down exactly where he meant it to go." That line. He is placing her. She thinks it's chemistry and it's *placement.*

The froyo-stack-holding-up as a closing beat was maybe a touch cute — I saw the metaphor coming from the flavor placard and it wrapped a hair too neat. But honestly I forgive it because it's doing work: her stack is holding up *tonight,* which means it's going to come down later, and I'm already braced.

Am I turned on? No — and I don't need to be every chapter, this one isn't for that. But it is doing the thing I'm starving for, which is making me *complicit* and a little sick and unable to look away. I want to text a friend: "the villain took her for frozen yogurt and I'm scared." The dread is the heat right now. I trust this book. Five chapters in it hasn't wasted a page and it hasn't flinched.

The one thing I'm holding: this is now the second consecutive chapter that's essentially a charming-Pace-in-daylight scene (Chapter 5 coffee shop, Chapter 6 froyo). Both good. But if Chapter 7 is a *third* soft courtship beat with no turn — no Randi, no crack in Vee, no heat — I'll start feeling the sweetness without the knife under it, and the knife is why I'm here. Cassie's "as long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" and the flat way she reads Pace are the little grounding wire keeping this from going full rom-com, and I'm watching her.
