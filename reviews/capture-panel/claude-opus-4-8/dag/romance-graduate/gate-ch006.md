REACTION

Okay so this one's a palate cleanser and I know exactly what it is. After that bench chapter — which is still sitting in my chest days later, honestly, that's the one that made me trust this book — chapter 6 is froyo. It's cute. It's *deliberately* cute, and that's the thing that's got its hooks in me: I'm not reading a sweet meet-cute, I'm reading the bait. The jacket told me the invitation reached her before she knew there was one, chapter 3 showed me Randi *shopping* for Vee across a quad like she was picking produce, and now here's Vee melting under fluorescent lights going "this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time and it's froyo" — and I want to reach into the book. Baby. *Baby.* The froyo is the least nice thing that has ever happened to you.

That's the whole engine of this chapter and it's working on me. Every sweet beat has a second floor. "He asked me things the way her grandfather used to ask things, as though the answer mattered and there was no wrong one" — that's genuinely tender, it's the exact thing I'm starved for in these books, a man with the patience to *wait out a sentence*. And I also watched him deploy that exact attention on Randi on the bench, and I watched Randi hand him a girl on a lawn. The tenderness is real AND it's technique, and the book refuses to let me have one without the other. That's the psychologically merciless thing the jacket promised and it's actually delivering it instead of just claiming it.

The froyo cup as metaphor is a little on the nose — she overfills it, doesn't hear her mother's voice for once, "tart on the bottom holds up," "your tart's holding up" as the closer. I clocked it. I don't fully mind it because appetite-and-portioning is the through-line for both women (the apple slices with Randi in chapter 1!) and it's earned, but it's the one moment the book winked at me a little too hard.

What I *notice*: no heat in this one. None. And I'm okay with it for one chapter because the dread is doing the work — I'm not bored, I'm braced. But if the next one is also all courtly restraint and froyo and I don't get either the body or another turn of the screw, I'll feel the sag. The thing keeping me here isn't Pace being nice to Vee, it's that I've read chapter 1 and I know what "nice" costs in this house. I'm reading Vee's happiness like a horror movie. That's a good place to have me.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "he PAID FOR THE FROYO and she thinks that's love and I know what's on the bench in his bedroom and I might scream."
