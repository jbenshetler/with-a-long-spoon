REACTION

Okay so this is the debrief chapter, the girlfriend-couch scene, and I want to be careful because on the surface it's the thinnest thing this book has handed me — no heat, no bench, no Pace on the page at all, just Vee coming home glowing and Cassie being dry about it. If a normal KU romance gave me this I'd skim it in forty seconds. But I'm not skimming, and I need to sit with why.

The reason is Chapter 1. I know what Randi knows. I read the bench. I read "there you are, Randi," I read the woman in the mirror deciding *not ever, he could have the face.* And now I'm watching the mark walk in the door — because that's what Vee is, the jacket told me, the game was played *about* her — and she's spinning in a beanbag chair going "look at ME," and the thing that broke my heart a little is that everything Vee is proud of is the thing that was engineered. "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." She says it three times, she NEEDS Cassie to have it, and she's completely right and completely wrong at once, and that gap is the whole book. Every yes was freely given. That was the problem. I felt that line land in my stomach reading her defend her own agency.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. Cassie is the only person in this book who keeps flinching at the wrong words. "Goodbye, Vee" two chapters ago, the whole finished word. And now — "How much did he give you to drink?" "Is he rich?" "What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers." She's the reader's dread wearing a ball cap. She's asking the safety questions and Vee is answering them like they're charming, like knowing "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" is a *dossier,* is *plenty* — "See. I know where he lives and everything." She knows nothing. She's never even seen the house. And I, the reader, know that house. I've been in that bedroom. I know what leans against the wall in it. So Vee proudly reciting the location of the room with the bench in it as proof she's safe — that's the most quietly horrifying beat in the chapter and it's dressed as two girls laughing.

What I'm most impressed by, and what's keeping me: the writing didn't get lazy in the low-stakes chapter. "The room too small to hold what she had to do with it." The window's cold thin line she doesn't feel because she's warm. The way the debrief voice *changes* — brass-cage voice for the froyo stuff, then dropping smaller and one-at-a-time for the kiss. Rivers knows exactly what this chapter is for and doesn't pad it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she's telling her best friend she made the choice herself and she's RIGHT and that's the whole trap and I want to scream into a pillow.* Also: watch Cassie. Cassie's going to be the one who gets hurt trying to pull her out, or the one nobody listens to.

Do I want the froyo chapters, though. Honestly the froyo/pool/dinner stretch (5-8) is the softest patch of the book — sweet, well-made, but I came for the merciless thing in Chapter 1 and I've now had a run of pure courtship. I trust it's a slow-tightening noose because the frame is so good. But I'm ready for the dread to start paying off on the page, not just in my head.
