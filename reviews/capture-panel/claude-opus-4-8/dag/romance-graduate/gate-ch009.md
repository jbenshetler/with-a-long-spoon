REACTION

Okay so this is the debrief chapter, and I know exactly what it's doing — she comes home lit up, she tells her best friend, we watch her be happy from the outside for once. And I don't resent it, because after that restaurant chapter I *wanted* to see her carry it back into her own life and hold it up in the light. The spin in the too-small room that nearly takes out the closet door — "*Look at me*" — got me. That's the whole engine of this book so far, isn't it: this girl who has portioned herself down to apple slices and cardigans-over-the-good-shirt, and the thing she keeps saying is *me. I did it. Me.* She's not marveling at him, she's marveling at herself having a want and acting on it. That's the real seduction and it's landing.

And Cassie. God, I love Cassie. "How much did he give you to drink?" and then "That's not a boy. That's a man." She's the reader's dry floor — she's *me* at 1 a.m. asking the suspicious question so I don't have to. And here's the thing that's keeping the hairs up on the back of my neck: I know what Cassie doesn't. I read Chapter 1. I know Randi is not a friend who "steered her lightly into the path of a young mathematician" by accident — I know Randi picked Vee off a lawn on Pace's *point*, that the pool and the coffee and the froyo tutor are a constructed cage, and that this exact address — "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" — is the house with the bench in the bedroom. So when Vee recites her little dossier, *see, I know where he lives, I'm not going to go missing, he's not going to walk me off a cliff* — that is the coldest thing in the chapter and she has no idea. She thinks knowing the road is safety. Cassie keeps poking at it — "off six-fourteen, that's the whole address" — and the book is letting Cassie be almost-right in a way that made my stomach drop. The state troopers joke is a joke that isn't.

What I noticed, though, honestly: this chapter is *soft*. Zero heat, and that's fine for one bridge — I said out loud once the best scene in a book was two people talking, and this is two people talking well. But it's the second quiet-ish chapter in a row after the froyo/hiking material, and it's a short one, and it doesn't end my thumb somewhere new. "Borrow good ones" is a nice button but it's a button on a scene where nothing turned. I'm not stalled — the dramatic irony is doing all the work a hot scene would do — but I'm aware I'm being fed relationship connective tissue and I want the machine to *move* again. I want the hike. I want Randi back in the frame, because the second Randi walks in I get the vertigo of watching the trap breathe.

What I'd text a friend: "the dry roommate just told her to borrow good hiking shoes and I wanted to scream DON'T GO OFF SIX-FOURTEEN. she thinks knowing the road means she's safe. book KNOWS."
