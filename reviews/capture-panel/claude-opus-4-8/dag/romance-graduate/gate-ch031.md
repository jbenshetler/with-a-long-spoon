REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been quietly hoping for without knowing it. A whole chapter outside Vee. No Vee, no Randi, no bed — just Pace on a bike in the cold with Sheri, and I get to watch him from the one angle the book's never given me: a person who knew him before, who owes him nothing, who clocks him.

And it's short. Two pages, really. I've never read a chapter this short in a book like this and I keep waiting to resent it, and I don't, because it's doing a thing. It's a breath on a climb. The whole chapter is built like the ride it describes — flats where they talk, climbs where they go quiet. That's not accidental and it doesn't feel like a shortcut; it feels like the one chapter the book could afford to make this small because its whole job is one tell.

And the tell is him talking. Sheri asks about Vee and he's *gone* before he decides to be — the walnut chair, her thumb down the grain of the joint he spent a weekend on, the math book she pulled and put back like it burned her. "He heard himself going and didn't stop." That's it. That's the con catching him. "Listen to you" lands for the SECOND time now — Randi said a version, Sheri says it flat — and "he didn't know what there was to listen to" is the whole horror in one line. Everyone can hear it but him. The man who reads the world in three sentences of Newton can't read his own voice. God, that's good, and it's the payoff I flagged in my notes — "listen to you" needed to pay off and here it is, ringing a second time, louder.

Sheri's a gift. "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that" — she talks about women like weather, two years across a handlebar, and she's the one person in this book with no stake, no folder, no plan. So when SHE does the arithmetic anybody could run — "Two of them at once. How do you sleep?" — and then "She know about the redhead?" "She knows. She likes her." "And the redhead?" "Not yet." — that *not yet* is the loaded gun said out loud by someone outside the house. The architects never say it this plainly. It took the stranger on the bike to make it a countdown. "If that's not already complicated, it will be. But I bet it's worth it." She's the chorus and the weather report and she's right.

And "You want me to not like her?" / "No. She couldn't take your heat." — I liked that more than I expected to. He protected Vee, reflexively, lightly, and it's the first time I've seen him move to shield her from a woman instead of toward a bed.

What I don't get, and I think that's the point: he STILL doesn't see the seam. Sheri hands him the shape of the whole thing — two women, one who knows and likes it, one who doesn't know yet — and he just rides on, cold going out of the morning. He can't hear himself about the chair. He can't hear the countdown. The scar's not here, Daphne's not here, but this is the same man: the one who narrates his own grooming as luck can't narrate his own tenderness as danger.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the little bike chapter is two pages and it's the professor FALLING and he doesn't know it, the bike girl just says 'listen to you' and he goes 'what's to listen to' I'm UNWELL." And then: "also 'not yet' — someone finally said the clock out loud."

It's a bridge. I know a bridge on sight and I don't resent one — and this one earned its length by being exactly as long as a held breath on a climb. But I'll be honest about the shape of my want: a bridge this good makes me want the *crossing*, and the crossing is still Vee walking back in to Pace, which I've been at the door of since chapter 30. This fed me sideways. I want the main course now.
