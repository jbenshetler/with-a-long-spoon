REACTION

Okay. So this is the one the whole book has been winding toward, and I'll give it this: it delivered the heat without once going coy, and it kept Vee's head on the whole way down, which is the only reason I'm not annoyed.

The mirror scene did the most for me, honestly, and that surprised me. Not because it's the hottest thing I've read — it's a solo build-and-deny, I've read a thousand — but because of the *hands correcting the plan*. The drawer. Reaching for the everyday black bra on autopilot, then the good lace she saves for him, then the little gut-punch of realizing Pace gets *neither*, because tonight she is the thing no bra beats. The sound of the drawer closing being "funnier and worse than she expected." That's the stuff I stay for — a body and a nervous system doing something while a scared girl narrates it honestly. And "last thing, only thing" about the shoes. She's funny in here! She giggles alone in the room and I believed the giggle. That's a Vee I've wanted — not shrinking, not being arranged, deciding, in her right mind, and getting to be a little gleeful about her own nerve.

And the denial-as-power beat actually landed: leaving herself wound and unfinished on *purpose* so he's the one, carrying it out into the cold. That's her choosing to make herself the gift instead of being posed as one. After fifty chapters of his hands putting her limb by limb into a photograph, her doing the composing herself is the payoff the leaving earned.

The cop. The cop made me laugh and then made me a little uneasy and I'm still turning over which one wins. On the one hand, "caught, just like this" is a genuinely good jolt and the reframe — the wet mark on the lining meaning *she meant it, nothing hung back* — is a real emotional move, not just a stunt. On the other hand it's the one beat that felt staged *for me*, the author reaching for a spike. But fine. It's a small sin and it earned its keep.

Here's my suspicion, and it's the thing sitting under all the warmth: this whole gorgeous surrender came out of *Randi's mouth*. The coat, the nothing-underneath, the shoes pressed into her hands, "that's the bravest thing a woman can do." Vee thinks she took this into her own hands. I know — because I've read 51 through 54 and she hasn't — that Randi scripted it top to bottom, the same way Randi scripted the love-sentence in the practice room back in Volume One. And I know *why* Randi did it: ch54 ended with Randi's hand against her own mouth in the foyer, Brooke telling her to choose. Randi is drowning and she just sent the girl she wants back into Pace's bed with a set of her own shoes on her feet, shoes that'll stay on because *he* said so. The heels are Randi's. Vee is wearing Randi into that house. That's doing something to me that the sex scene by itself couldn't.

So the reunion itself — hot, yes, graceless-on-the-couch yes, the "three hobbling steps" toward a bed they don't reach is the realest detail in it. And "you sure know how to make an entrance. Vee." The *name*, finally said easy and warm, the same name that wasn't enough in December. That cracked her open and it cracked me a little too. BUT. He still didn't say the word. He said sorry, he said I missed you so much, his voice broke — and Vee, full of pancakes, decided tonight there was "no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." Which is the book telling me, gently, that she folded. She drove out there to get him back and she got him back by taking the one thing off the table that she left over. He paid every currency except the word, *again*, and this time she accepted the payment. The folded arms from December didn't get answered. They got outflanked by a coat.

And he's about to run the threesome. The ch53 drunk chapter and the kitchen picture of the three of them — he's building toward it, and Randi just hand-delivered Vee primed and grateful and convinced it was all her own idea. I'm not comforted by this reunion. I'm scared by how good it felt.

Cassie's eyebrow and "Hmm" at the end is perfect. The one clean witness, filing it, saying nothing. I trust that eyebrow more than I trust the whole warm kitchen.

The pancakes. I said I was sick of the cooking-with-his-whole-self note and I stand by being sick of it, but pancakes at midnight after is at least the right-sized version of it, and "made and not poured" is a decent line. Fine.
