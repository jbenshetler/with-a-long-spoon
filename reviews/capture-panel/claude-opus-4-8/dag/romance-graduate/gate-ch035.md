REACTION

Oh, the drawer. That's the whole chapter and it's the thing I'll be carrying — he gave her the top drawer. The easy one. The one your hand goes to first. And he folded his own careful ordered self down into a single drawer, pressed too tight, to do it. No note, there's never a note. That's the exact grammar of everything he does: he doesn't *say* it mattered, he *makes her a place*. And I know — I KNOW, the book has trained me — that this is the machine running at its tenderest and most total. This is how you move a girl into your life without her deciding to move in. You don't ask. You clear a drawer and let her find it and let her feel *chosen* down to the floorboards. And it works. It worked on ME for a second, my chest did the thing before my head caught up, which is precisely Vee's whole condition and now it's mine, and that's the trick this book keeps pulling and I keep letting it.

But here's what I noticed and what keeps me trusting the narration: the chapter architected the fake-out on purpose. The scrunchies are *gone* from the counter and the bottom goes out of her morning — "he'd minded after all, and quietly, the way he did everything, he'd gathered up her sprawl and put it somewhere out of his sight." She reads it as rejection. The dread was real for a beat. Then the drawer. So the book is showing me the two readings sitting inside the same gesture — the man who tidies you away and the man who makes you a home are doing the *identical action*, and only the drawer's contents tell you which, and even then. That's the merciless-tenderness the jacket promised, and it's landing clean.

And "Peter." She breathed his real name to the sleeping shape of him. She doesn't know what she's holding — that Peter is the boy Daphne had, the name he took out of the answer. She thinks she's found the private tender core of him and she's actually put her mouth to the wound. I felt that one in my teeth.

The weight-loss detail got me too, quietly. Her body "quietly burning down whole weekends of him," bras riding higher, and she never asked it to. It's tender and it's also faintly sinister in this book's key — she's being consumed and thriving on it, literally shrinking into the shape he's making. The salmon, the warm tomatoes off the sill, the coming-back-an-hour-later — she's inventorying all the ways he says *I love you* in nothing but deeds, and deciding she can live inside the everything-he-does and not go hungry for the words. Which is the loneliest brave thing in the chapter. She's teaching herself not to need the one thing he withholds. That's a girl being trained to accept less naming than she gives, and calling it plenty.

What this chapter did NOT do: it's the quietest one in a long while. No Randi, no con visibly turning, no new information about the plan. After the brunch chapter — which was the coldest, hottest thing, the pastry glaze on her lip she never touched, God — this is a breather, a soft domestic exhale. And I let it breathe because the drawer is real writing and the "Peter" gut-punch pays a debt I've been carrying since chapter 25. But I'm aware I got no ledger, no Randi reckoning, no forward motion on the thing I'm actually waiting for. This is consolidation, not escalation. Fine. Once. The prose earned it and the dread-under-the-tenderness never once slipped into telling me the drawer means what Vee thinks it means. It stays ahead of her. That's my whole condition for staying.
