REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been dreading and I couldn't look away from a single line of it. Because here's what the book did: it took the bravest thing Vee has ever done — the coat, the cold road, the nerve held by her own fingernails — and it let me know, the whole time, that *Randi built it.* Randi picked the move, Randi talked her down off the shame ledge word by word, Randi put the shoes in her hands. Vee drove out through the black fields thinking she was finally doing something that was *hers*, carrying her own body to him because the words had failed — and it was scripted three states deep by the one person steering her back into the trap. "You take this into your own hands. You don't do it with words. Words are the whole war." Of course Randi says words are the war. Words are the one thing the whole house is built on not-saying.

And then the thing that actually made me put my hand over my mouth: **"Vee, I l—"** He opened the door and the *first thing that came up in him was the word.* He went for it. After a month of silence, the door opens on her and his mouth starts to make it — and she doesn't let him finish, because she's got a better card, because Randi taught her the card beats the word. The one time in fifty-two chapters Pace reaches for it unbidden, Vee shuts it in his throat with her coat. I want to scream. The gap between them didn't just stay open, she *stepped over the thing she's been dying for* to win the night a different way. And she'll never know it was there. He'll file it under the wall he put right back up afterward and she'll go on inventing blondes.

The heat itself — fine, yes, it's real, it's hot, she earned it and so did I. The mirror, the edging, taking herself to the brink and refusing to finish because she wants *him* to be the one — that's the whole Vee thesis in a body: everything wound tight and carried out into the cold to be put in his hands unspent. The cop lights were a genuinely nasty little jolt, the shame-arithmetic of it, and then the cruiser just *going past*, after something that was never her. The doorstep oral in the open door banging in the wind, him on his knees the second the coat opened — I felt every degree of it, the book promised me I would and it delivered.

But I can't get warm in it, and the book doesn't want me to. **She's wearing Randi's shoes to fuck Pace.** "Those stay on." He doesn't know whose they are. She doesn't know what they are. I do. And the sweet midnight pancakes, his face cracking open — "I'm sorry. I never meant to hurt you. I missed you so much," the voice that wouldn't come back — that's the realest tender beat he's given her, and it's sitting on top of a reconciliation that the architect ordered, with the word still unsaid underneath the apology. He apologizes for *hurting* her. He does not say the thing. He never says the thing. The apology is the mechanism handed back one more time, warmer than ever.

And upstairs, before any of this — Brooke. Brooke saw it. "I've seen how that girl looks at you… you remember Lauren Pike." The architect just got told, by the one woman in that house who prices every room, that she has to choose. Randi with the back of her hand to her own mouth like she's fifteen — *that girl kissed her.* It went real for Randi in the foyer, the thing the jacket promised, and now there's a cost with a name attached to it. The fuse is wearing a third detonator now and it's institutional.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: SHE WORE THE SHOES. HE TRIED TO SAY IT AND SHE CUT HIM OFF. I am not okay. The one honest thing Vee's done all book was handed to her pre-written and she doesn't know and she's happier than she's been in the whole book and I want to throw it across the room. Give me the brunch. Give me Randi having to look at the girl she dressed for this.
