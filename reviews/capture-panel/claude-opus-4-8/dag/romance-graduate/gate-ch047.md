REACTION

Okay so this is the mirror chapter and it's brutal and I saw exactly what it was doing and it still got me.

46 gave me Randi in the fairytale, awake in the gold with the perfect nothing asleep behind her, cold everywhere she wasn't touching him, running the scenario she can't run to the end — Vee's face. And now 47 puts Vee in the twin bed in Ohio with the lavender plugins and Wheel of Fortune still in her ears, cold in a room that loves a girl who doesn't live in her anymore, running her own scenario she can't run to the end — whose arms he's got around instead. Two women, six time zones apart, both cold except where a body touches them, both holding a phone with an empty field, both sending the exact texts I already watched from the other side. I KNOW what those texts cost now. I read Randi compose "you ANGEL 😇" as a fairytale handed over a wound. And here's Vee receiving it clean, envying the poisoned Gstaad she personally watched get poisoned in the glass room — "even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had." That line gutted me. She knows whose money it is. She wants it anyway. That's the most honest thing in the chapter.

And the structural cruelty of it — Vee imagines Randi's night as "a white-tie crush where the whole room knew Randi's name, or Randi slipped off somewhere with a man out of the stories." She got it RIGHT. She invented the actual night. The white-tie ball, the man out of the stories. And she's using her correct guess to feel small, to feel unseen, while the real Randi lay in that exact scene feeling nothing, wishing she were across a Tuesday table from Vee. They are each other's cure and each is starving in the dark inventing the other's feast. I could scream.

The thing that actually scared me, though, the cold-water thing: the fra diavolo turning. She reaches for HER best night — the top sheet, the wine, him building the sauce, the sheet slipping and her not fixing it — reaches for it to get warm, and it turns on her. The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, the blonde she built out of spite sits down in the wine light where Vee sat, "a body that had never in its life needed building for." She can't keep her own memory. That's what jealousy actually does and I've never seen it written that exactly — not the fear that he'll replace you but that you'll replace yourself, hand him the better woman with your own hands at midnight because you can't stop. And then the bartender's testimony, Randi's "he leaves you the room" — "they had only ever held her up while she was the one." The floor's gone. All the evidence was conditional on being chosen and she doesn't know she's chosen anymore.

And here's where I clock what the book is doing to ME. Sheri at Thanksgiving — "She's alone" — filed as pity in November, comes back tonight as a splinter. Christmas, "a whole week she couldn't see into." I've been waiting for Sheri, the two-at-once, since the handlebars. Vee's spite-blonde is a decoy; the real other woman has a name and a bike and a seat at his table, and Vee half-touches it and slides off because she doesn't have standing to ask. That's the dread doing its patient work.

What keeps my trust: the narration STILL knows more than Vee's gladness — and more than her misery. It's not telling me Pace is cruel or Pace is good. It's holding "the wanting and the fear in the one image, and no way on earth to know which was the true one." That's the whole book's honesty and it hasn't flipped. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." The exact same act, revalued by fear, and the book won't tell me which valuation is correct. That's what I stayed for. The day it tells me straight, I'm gone. It didn't today.

The one thing I'm chewing: two consecutive interior chapters, no scene, two women alone in beds. 43 and 44 already did Vee-alone-in-the-dark-with-the-shirt; 47 is a third pass at the same note. Gorgeous, but I can feel the loop. I don't want a fourth. I want the term to start. I want Randi's face lighting up across the desk while she carries what I know she carries, and I want to be in the room when Vee finds out there was a plan. Get me out of these beds.
