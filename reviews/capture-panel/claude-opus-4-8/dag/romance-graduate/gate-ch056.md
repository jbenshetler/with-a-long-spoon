REACTION

Oh. OH. I've been waiting sixty chapters to be inside this room and I didn't know it until the first line put me here. "Randi came against his mouth." No preamble, no establishing — we're just *in* it, and I spent the first paragraph scrambling to catch up with what it means. They're lovers. They've been lovers. The jacket said "chose her together" and I filed that as backstory, logistics, two people conspiring — I did not understand it was *this*, that the brunches and the midnight pancakes and the "bring Theo" are all happening while these two are in a bed together with her name in their mouths.

And here's what undid me: it's not a scene about two people cheating on Vee. It's a scene about two people in love with Vee, *using each other's bodies to survive wanting her*. That's so much worse and so much better than anything I was braced for. Randi offering her own breast to Pace's mouth and narrating "her nipple in my mouth" — making him stand in for Vee, routing the girl she can't have through the man who has her. "As if the wanting could cross through the body she offered him." I had to put the book down for a second. That's the whole book's grammar — the gift that carries the giver's spirit, Marsh's *hau* — and it just walked into the bedroom and lay down.

The thing I've been screaming about from the outside — Randi breaking from the inside — I just got it from *her own mouth*, and it's naked in a way she never is at the loft. "I want her to know exactly what she does to me." That's the sentence. That's the architect with the blueprints on fire. And Pace's control *breaking* at it — the man I called an audit, both hands on the leash — he loses it right there, because he's not running anything, he's as gone as she is. "She's not the only one in love." "He couldn't tell her she was wrong." The two coldest, most-controlled people in this book confessing it sideways to each other while they can't say it to the one it's about. The whole power game they run on Vee, they can't run on each other — they just handed each other the truth.

And the information passing between them — "She kisses me first now." "She looks at you, too." "She kissed my neck again." They're *pooling her*. The flinch I've been obsessing over — "I nearly turned around. I wanted to turn around. Put my hand on her" — Randi finally says what the flinch was, and it was her holding the line, and Vee breaks it with a joke every time without knowing there's a line. "She knows exactly when I'm about to ask for more." She doesn't, Randi. That's the horror. She has no idea.

The heat itself is real and I came for exactly this — the control play, him lifting her off him, "my turn," the taste going back and forth, the "subtle"/"elegant" laugh that's genuinely tender and funny in the middle of it. These two *like* each other, which I somehow never considered. But what's got its hand around my throat isn't the fucking, it's that every stroke is a conversation about a third person who thinks Randi's just her friend and Pace is just her boyfriend. The taste thread I planted dread around — "why does he like it so much" — here's part of the answer, and it's not sinister, it's that he's spent God knows how long tasting two people off his own skin. The salt. God.

What I'd text my friend at 1 a.m.: THEY'RE TOGETHER. They've been together the whole time. Every single thing Vee thinks she stumbled into. And they're not villains, they're wrecked, they're in love with her and with the shape of wanting her, and nobody in that triangle knows the triangle is a triangle except these two and me. I am not setting this book down. I need to know what she does to them when she finds out — and she has to find out.
