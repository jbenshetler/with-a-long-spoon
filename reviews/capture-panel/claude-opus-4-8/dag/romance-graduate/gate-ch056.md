Okay so the first thing that happened is a professor walked in and handed me the whole book on a chalk tray, and I sat there deciding whether to be furious about it. Mauss, the *hau*, the spirit of the giver riding inside the gift, the gift that can never be free because it always wants to go home. That is the entire machine. The book put the key on the table in front of the one person who can't read it, and let *me* pick it up. That's the move that saved it. If Vee had thought "like me and Pace" I'd have thrown it across the room. She didn't. So it's a frame, not an explanation, and I'll allow it — barely, and with my eyes narrowed.

Randi topping up the wine without being asked, the fingernail working the rim of the glass back and forth, the palm laid flat high on Vee's chest "this did it" and then *adjusting the wrap a quarter-inch truer on the way back* — Jesus. She's eating. The widest irony-gap in the book and it's two women at a loft table and only one of them knows what the dinner is.

Except she didn't. For the first time there's a thing Vee is keeping, and she doesn't know she's keeping it, and it's the one thing in the whole weekend that scared her. The compact's got its first crack and neither of them clocked it.
