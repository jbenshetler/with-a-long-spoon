Okay so this is a breather chapter and I know it the second I'm on a bike with a woman I've never met. Sheri. Two years across a handlebar, remarks on women the way people remark on weather, faster than him on the climbs and never lets him forget it — she's another appetite in the shape of a person, another Randi-rhyme, and part of me went *how many of these women is this book going to hang around him.* The harem keeps widening. First it was the pointing-game girls, then Randi, then the whole Rusty Nail chorus of women he taught to dance and got home safe, now the cycling partner who clocks Vee's backside from across the bar. He's a sun with a lot of planets and the book keeps introducing me to new ones.

BUT. Two things landed and they're the reason I'm not annoyed.

One: I got to watch him be *gone.* The narration let him run — the redhead's thumb down the grain of the walnut joint, the math book she didn't put back like it burned her, "he heard himself going and didn't stop." And Sheri: "Listen to you." That's the first time I've seen Pace unguarded from the *inside*, caught feeling something he didn't schedule. The whole book he's been the machine, the one who rations, who asks-before-he-takes with his hand not shaking for HER benefit — and here he's just a man who can't shut up about a girl to his friend. I liked that. It's the thing I've been half-afraid the book would never give me: evidence the con is catching him too.

Two — and this is the one I'd text at 1 a.m.: *"What happened to the pale sorority chick?" "Randi. That's not finished."* THAT'S NOT FINISHED. He said it out loud. He's running Vee and Randi *at the same time,* concurrent, live, and now it's on the record in his own mouth to a third party. And Sheri, God bless her handlebar wisdom: "Two of them at once. If that's not already complicated, it will be." That's the jacket clause — *as true for the two who started it* — being loaded into the chamber. Chapter 24 already put Randi astride him so I've got the physical seam; this is the emotional one. The two threads aren't parallel lines anymore, they're a collision the book is now openly counting down to.

What I'm still starving for: heat. This one's dry — no sex, no charge, a cold-morning bike ride and some banter. As a chapter of feeling it's thin; Sheri's a device, not a person yet. But it's doing structural work I actually wanted done, so I'll take the interlude. Just don't give me three more of these in a row.
