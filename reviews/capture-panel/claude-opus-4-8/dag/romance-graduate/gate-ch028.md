REACTION

Okay. So. This is the chapter I've been circling the whole book without knowing it, and I have about four feelings happening at once.

First — the dance lesson on the concrete pad. I want to be annoyed that we got a whole mechanics-of-the-three-step tutorial, and in anybody else's book I'd have skimmed it. But it's the measuring scene again, isn't it. It's the same engine. "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead." "The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." That is the whole book in a dance frame and he's saying it to her feet. The man teaches her to surrender by calling it technique and he's not even lying about the technique. "There it is," quiet, when she stops guessing. God. He does this. He builds the thing onto her and lets her think her body found it.

And then the bar, and I actually sat up, because this is the first time Vee's own poison turned outward instead of in. The shame's always run inside her — the mother's voice, the cardigan. Here it finally had somewhere to go and it went straight at him. The blonde with the daylight-wrong makeup, "knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to," "gets a girl home safe" — and I KNEW, I knew the second I read it, that it was a knife dressed as a compliment, and I knew Vee would swallow it whole because jealousy is the one appetite she doesn't question. "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" Oof. The glass past the point you can catch it. She's been so careful her whole life and she threw it.

Here's what got me, what I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: the bartender. "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." I made a noise. Because the whole chapter I was braced for the book to let Vee be right — for there to be a seam of actual sleaze in Pace, finally, the thing I've been waiting twenty-seven chapters to catch him at. And instead the one clean witness in the room, the woman with no stake, tells her the plain truth: he leaves them at the door, plenty tried, he doesn't bring dates, he brought you. And it's TRUE. That's the horror of this book in miniature — the one time Vee lashes out, she's wrong, and being wrong about him binds her tighter. She walks out at dawn "going to fix it," going to Randi's, humbled, more his than she was when she walked in. The fight didn't crack the thing. It welded it.

And I'm sick about it because the manufactured-jealousy plot is the EXACT tired move I came to this book to get away from — conflict from one misread moment at a bar. But Helen Rivers knows that. She made the misunderstanding real and then made it land as more evidence of how gone Vee is, how her shame reflexes run her, how even her anger serves the thing being done to her. That's not the tired version. That's the tired version autopsied.

What I'm turning over: she's going to Randi's. Randi. The one who "chose her together, once." Vee runs straight to the person who built the table to be told she overreacted and should go make it right with Pace. I don't think Randi set the blonde up — this felt like Vee's own machinery — but Randi will absolutely harvest this. A humbled Vee who's just learned to distrust her own jealousy is clay. I'm scared of what the next chapter does with that.

One soft spot I'll give it: "she'd slept in it and not washed it, rationing the smell of him, until what was left of him went stale and wrong" — that detail is real and it hurt. The persona who DNFs literary books for arm's-length clinical distance, that line is the opposite of that. That's a body on a bed.

No heat tonight and I didn't want any. This was the fight, and the fight was better than most of the sex in my Kindle.
