REACTION

Oh, she went *alone*. I spent the whole carry-forward bracing for the three of them in that room — the dress loaded, the shoes bought, Randi on his chest scheduling who watches whom — and the book did the thing I didn't see: it emptied the room of all of them. No mother. No Pace, in Berkeley, sorry in the quiet way. No Randi. Just Cassie at the doors, holding her phone, and Vee three rows from a microphone with nothing under the silk and nobody in the room who ever taught her what to be ashamed of. That line landed like a hand on my sternum. That's the whole book in a sentence and for once it didn't explain itself to me — it just set it down.

And here's what I wasn't ready for: I loved her in this. Not sick-for-her, not scared-of-her — just with her. Because this is the one discovery she's made that *doesn't* travel. It won't get brunched to Randi and ferried to Pace in bed. She took the long step on a stage in front of nobody's parents and gave the room her leg on purpose and it was *hers*, start to finish, no one handed it to her, no one chewed it first. The "half-second before her head was sure of it" — the body agreeing low and certain the way it did in the glass — that's the seam Pace and Randi have been prying open as a weapon, and here she picks it up herself and uses it on a dull warm kind room and walks out owning it. I didn't expect the induction to be the first thing in forty chapters that's purely, cleanly hers.

The part I can't stop turning over: she pictured walking in late, the room turning to look, and the real room *wouldn't turn*. Warm and dull and kind, nobody watching the navy girls any longer than the walking took. The fantasy was going to be denied her — and then she made it happen with one long step instead of a small one. She manufactured her own turning. That's growth and it's also exactly the appetite Randi named — "finally hungry out loud" — and the book knows it, and ends on her not being able to tell pride from the heat still running, "whether, by now, the two came to the same thing." That's the tell I trust. It didn't tidy her. It left her standing in the doorway not sure which engine she's running on.

And she sent Pace the uncropped frame. "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." For once *she's* the one with the locked room. She's keeping a door the way he keeps the truck surrender and the bar words. God, she's becoming him a little and becoming free a little in the exact same motion and I can't pull them apart either.

What ached: Cassie. "Streetlight." The bright corners of her eyes she'd deny to the grave. She got the side of Vee's head again — "how did I look" was the only thing in Vee's head the whole walk, not her own name, not Cassie. The cold spot's still spreading even in the chapter where I was happiest. And the relief about her mother not coming, the true thing she couldn't hand Cassie — that honesty is still only half-said out loud.

What I noticed and liked: Pace being *absent* is the most generous thing he's done. He made the dress to watch it and then didn't get the watching, and there's no manipulation available in Berkeley. For one chapter the reenactment machine is just a man who'll miss it. I needed that beat and didn't know I did.

No wreck yet. But this was the exhale before it and it earned the whole room going quiet.
