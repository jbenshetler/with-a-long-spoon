REACTION

Oh, this one got me, and it got me from an angle I didn't expect. After two chapters of watching Randi and Vee both lying awake cold in beds that won't warm them, I thought I was braced for a third quiet one. And technically it is quiet — it's two people in a double-wide diner eating cheeseburgers. But it's the first time in fifty-three chapters I've been inside *Pace's* head with a witness across the table who won't let him perform, and I didn't realize how badly I wanted that until Sheri started taking the skin off him.

Because here's what I've been waiting the whole book to find out: is the folded-arms man actually in pain, or is the withholding the installation? And this chapter is the book finally putting its cards partly down. Sheri — God, I love Sheri, she's the only person in this entire book who wants *nothing* off him — asks him flat out, "Is that so hard?" and he dodges. He answers the question *next* to the one she asked. "I'm there every weekend. I told her she's the most important thing in my life." And Sheri, bless her, names it: "that's a hell of a lot of reasons for a man who's already got the girl. She didn't ask you for reasons." And then the kill shot: "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." And he *looks down.* He has no argument. That's the whole thing — he's not wrecked, he's not unable, he's choosing, and the first honest person to sit across from him makes him look at the floor.

So my carry-forward read holds. He's holding a line at cost, and the line has a reason, and the reason is the thing I still don't have — the scar, the plan, whatever MIRANDA is under all this. But what I didn't expect was to feel for him anyway. "Every brick was true and the true things were supposed to come to something." That's a real ache. The man who built the gauge genuinely does not understand why saying four true things doesn't add up to the one word. Or he understands exactly and that's why he can't say it, because it wouldn't be true the way she needs — she was never only his, he knew it before she existed. Both. Still both.

And the Sheri texture is just *fun* in a way this book hasn't let itself be in a while. "The philosophy of your food." "Lord almighty. Get over yourself." She punctures the one note I'm sickest of — the whole cooks-with-his-whole-self thing — right to his face, and he *laughs,* the first full laugh of the night, and I laughed with him. I needed that. A character inside the book being tired of the same thing I'm tired of is the book telling me it knows. That bought it a lot of goodwill.

The things I'm filing: Sheri's own grief — Paige, the girl from the house of seven at the table, who couldn't get right with Sheri not talking to her people. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." The book keeps building this cell of the estranged, the chosen-family people, and Sheri's the hinge. The Brooks and Dunn cover band, February, "I don't care what the redhead's got to say about it" — that's a flag planted, she wants him out among people, and Vee's one disaster was asking to be taken out among people. And "She find out about the brunette?" / "No. Not like that." — so Sheri knows about Randi, knows there's a brunette, and "not like that" is doing a LOT of work. He decided before the door shut that he would "not bring her down today" — bring *who* down? The thing in the truck. The thing he carried in. That's the chapter telling me he's sitting on his own secret even here, even with Sheri.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the man finally has a scene where someone makes him flinch and it's a five-foot biker lesbian eating his fries and it's the best Pace has ever been.* And then, lower: he misses her. "I want Vee. I miss her." Flat, true, and it doesn't fix anything, and he knows it doesn't, and that's exactly the trap the whole book is. He can miss her all the way down and still not spend the word. That's not a man who can't. That's a man who won't, and now even his friend has told him what that makes him.

I want the next one. Not frantically — this was a breath, a good one — but I want to see which of these three cold beds thaws first, and I want it to be the term starting, the collision loading.
