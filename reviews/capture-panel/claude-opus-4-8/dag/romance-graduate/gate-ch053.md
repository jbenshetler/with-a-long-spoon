REACTION

Oh, this one got me sideways. I came in still buzzing off the taste-thing from 52 — I was braced for Tuesday, for Randi across the little table, for Vee trying to find a way to say the strange small thing that doesn't sound like what it sounds like — and instead the book skipped all that and dropped me into his kitchen on some later night, her naked on a chair he built, running her mouth at his back. And I let it, because within four lines I could feel this was a bridge chapter doing real work, not treading water.

Here's what it did to me: it took the cruelest thing about Pace and made it *worse* by making it tender. All fall I've been carrying "words are cheap, believe what I do" and "I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you" — and now it turns out he was listening the whole time. Not politely. He *kept* it. Meg's boots a half-size big, her heel coming up when she pushed off. Kayla talking the paint off a wall. A half-sentence on a trail in October, filed on a shelf with her friends' names and taken down one-handed without turning around. That undid me a little, honestly, because it's the most romantic thing a person can do — hold every small dumb piece of you — and it's being done by the exact man who will not spend the one word. He hears everything except the thing she needs him to *say*. He's fluent in her and mute in himself. God.

And the move underneath the sweetness: he wants to meet her people. He wants *in*. The man who folded his arms in December and would not name her "out there" is now, unprompted, asking to be let into her whole world at once — Cassie, Kayla, Meg and Danny, even Theo, the boy who asked her out, and he doesn't so much as blink at Theo. That's him paying in his own currency. He can't say the word so he's buying the whole village instead. Part of me swooned. Part of me went cold, because I know this book, and a man giving you the exact thing you left him over — without ever saying why, without the word — is not the same as the word.

The bit that made me sit up hardest was Cassie. "Bring Cassie, too" landing in the middle of her little dinner and just *sitting* there — and Vee running the dinner forward and not being able to find a restaurant to put it in, every table in town with somebody being a good sport at it. "She'd watched somebody be fine about it before. She wasn't going to look at when." That's her, for one second, standing at the edge of the whole machine — *fine*, the word she hates, the word all the other girls use — and choosing not to look at when she last saw someone be fine. She skims right past her own knowing again. She always does. But the book put it in her mouth for a beat and I saw it.

And then the last line. "Let me handle it." I actually made a noise. Because that's the *architect* voice. That's Randi's "let me handle it," that's a plan being made about her while she thinks she's making it. Every dinner in this book has been engineered by someone who isn't her. And here's the collision I've been starving for buried in a cozy dish-washing scene: Randi and Pace, the two people who built her between them, at one table. Vee thinks she's finally introducing her two favorite people. I think she's about to put the two co-authors in a room and watch them recognize each other. If that dinner happens, something detonates.

What I'm suspicious of: the taste-thing got parked. Chapter 52 ended pointed straight at Tuesday and Randi, and 53 quietly stepped around it. I don't think it's dropped — it's "settled somewhere under the affection, minor-key, waiting," her words — but I clocked the book choosing not to pay it off yet, and I want it back.

This was quiet and I didn't resent one second of it. It moved them an inch and the inch was toward the cliff.
