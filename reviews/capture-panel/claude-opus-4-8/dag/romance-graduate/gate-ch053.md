REACTION

Oh. Oh, I did not see the shape of this one coming and now I can't look at anything else.

So here's what Rivers just did, and I'm still a little stunned by it. For fifty-some chapters Randi has been the architect. She planted the sentences, she posed the brunches, she staged the whole spring off a lawn before Vee existed. She is the one who leans in. And in this chapter she does it *again* — she takes a wrecked girl off a red-eye, gets her up the stairs away from the eyes, and talks her, warm beat by warm beat, into walking into Pace's house bare under a coat and a pair of stiletto straps that aren't there to hold the shoe on. It's a seduction dressed as rescue, and the horrible thing is *the advice is good.* Every word of it is true. He does keep his phone in the other room. He is a boy holding himself still so it won't show. Randi is lying to Vee by being completely right. That's the con at its most refined: she doesn't even have to invent anything, she just has to point Vee back at Pace so the brunches keep their cover and nothing gets decided out loud.

And then the floor dropped. *Vee turned around and went back and kissed her.* After four months of Randi being the one who leans in, the girl who holds still and is sweet about it and thanks her — Vee got both hands up on Randi's face and took her time and wasn't embarrassed until after. And Randi, who in chapter 51 lay in a faultless man's faultless bed and felt *nothing*, who carried her own cold into Gstaad and out again — Randi stands in an empty foyer with the back of her hand against her own mouth like she's fifteen. The architect got moved. She did not plan that and it undid her more than a billionaire and a sleigh and a white-tie ball could. I actually said *oh my god* out loud. The whole volume has been Randi engineering Vee's wanting; here Vee reaches past the plan and touches the real thing under it, by accident, out of gratitude, and it's the first time Randi's cold room has had heat in it that she didn't manufacture. God. That's the best beat in the book and it's a thank-you kiss under a wall of composites.

And THEN Brooke. I've been clocking Brooke as nothing but ambient warmth — "oh honey," always passing through — and Rivers has been loading her the whole time. "You always say goodbye under the composites." The warmth never moves off her face and she's threading a needle: *play all you want, for the boys, privately — but the day he thinks it's true.* Lauren Pike. Choose. The house president naming the exact fault line, kind as anything, and it's a threat with a smile sewn right into it. Randi has a position and she has Vee and she's being told she can't keep both, and that's the first external wall that's ever been put up against this thing. The vise just got a second jaw.

What's wrecking me is the dramatic irony stacked three deep. Vee thinks she got rescued. She thinks Randi gave her a plan to get Pace back — and she did, and it serves Randi's threesome, and Vee has NO idea she just kissed the woman who arranged her. Randi thinks she finally got reached and is standing there glowing — and doesn't yet know Brooke saw all of it and is about to make her choose. And Pace is home alone crying over a truck that wasn't her car (that last chapter gutted me, the Daphne reveal, the freezer chicken, "he ate anyway"). Everybody's holding a different half of the truth and they're all about to collide, and the collision I've waited the whole book for — *Vee finding out there was a plan* — just got a new fuse: what happens to Randi when the girl she built actually wants her back?

The shoes. Randi handed Vee stilettos for a night Vee thinks is about Pace, and I don't for one second think Randi isn't thinking about herself when she swings them off two fingers. "Randi always knew the shoes." The altar's getting set and Vee's being dressed for it and she thinks she's dressing for a doorstep.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: SHE KISSED HER. Vee started it. And the sorority president just cornered Randi in the kitchen like a mob boss who brings you soup. I cannot put this down.

One thing I'll say plain so I'm honest with myself: the chapter leans hard on Randi being *right* about Pace to make the manipulation land, and there's a flicker where the "fear needs a face" talk is a touch too neat, a touch therapist-on-demand. But it earns it, because we know what Randi's actually doing and Vee doesn't, so the neatness reads as craft, not a crutch. And the foyer turn burns all of that off anyway.
