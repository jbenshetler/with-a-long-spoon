REACTION

Okay so this is the one where the book finally hands me the scar, and I have complicated feelings about getting it.

First: Randi's cashmere blanket. "The one for the chill that took her after." After. It slid off HIS bed in the night and he folds it and puts it back where it lives. Where it *lives* — it has a place in his bedroom. So Randi and Pace sleep together, present tense, it has furniture-rights in his house, and I'm sitting here recalibrating the whole triangle while he's hungover. That's the detail I'd text a friend about at 1 a.m. before anything else. The two who chose her together are still a *they* in the most ordinary bodily way and Vee has no idea the woman who calls her gorgeous has a blanket for the chill that takes her after, in the bed where Vee sleeps in the middle with her red hair on the white cotton. God.

Then the man got blackout drunk and mopped up his own vomit. Bleach foam crusted above the waterline in the bucket in the hall. Pace — courtly, exact, the one who never fights, who leaves you the room — fell apart alone and cleaned it up himself before anyone could see, which is the single most *him* thing he's ever done. He even deciding, walking in last chapter, "he would not bring her down today" — long practice at deciding a thing could keep. This is the cost of that practice. It keeps until it's a bucket of bleach foam and a man sobbing over a truck.

And the truck. That's the one that got me. His head lifting before he knew he'd moved, the fork stopping halfway, his whole body gathering toward the cut in the hill, gravel, a truck turning in — and for one second the house gives him Vee, her face becoming the smile she gives only him — and it *goes past.* And he bends over the table and sobs until he can't hear it anymore and a few breaths after. That's the mirror of her in Ohio reaching for the best night and watching it turn blonde on her. Both of them alone in separate cold rooms conjuring the other and getting a counterfeit. The book earned that rhyme and I felt it in my chest.

Here's my one real snag, and it's the thing I always flinch at: the Daphne section explains itself to me. I didn't need "He had taught himself that the words were the difference" spelled out, or "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." I'd already assembled that from the folded arms and "I don't go back" two chapters back — that's the pleasure, assembling it. Having it stated flat, as a thesis, took a little of the air out. BUT. It's inside a hungover grief-fog, it's spare, and the *content* of it floored me anyway: Daphne was his tutor because he could do the math but didn't know which dining hall, where to stand when everyone else was born knowing. That's a whole class wound I didn't expect. And "There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told." That's the kind of sentence that redeems a little overexplaining around it.

So the chapter that was supposed to be finished curing me of him did the opposite — it cracked him open right when I'd written him off, and now I'm back in the ache for BOTH of them, which I think is exactly the trap. "He ate anyway." Same as her. The machine runs them both.

My only impatience: this is my fourth chapter away from the doorway. Randi's origin, Vee in Ohio, Pace and Sheri, now Pace alone. I wanted to be walked back to the live wire and instead I'm orbiting it. I don't resent it — every one of these paid — but I can feel myself starting to count. One more processing chapter and I start skimming for a collision.
