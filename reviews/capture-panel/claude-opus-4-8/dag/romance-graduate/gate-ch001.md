REACTION

Okay. Okay. I need to text somebody.

So I went into this bracing for the usual. Man keeps the house too warm, builds furniture, control-freak with a praise kink — I've read this guy a hundred times, I know his henley, I know his "can we leave them up?" I was three paragraphs in going *fine, show me something.* And the thing is, it DID. Not because the dom-and-the-bench setup is new — it isn't — but because somebody actually bothered to live inside the woman. The apple. The thin slices, cut-side up because she eats them faster that way, apples on the safe side of the line she and her mother both drew with a knife in it. That's not set dressing. That told me more about her hunger than the entire spanking did, and it told me *before* I knew who she was.

And the heat is real heat. The not-addressing of him being hard, the edging, the taking-the-hands-away so she has nothing to hold but him — that "offering" she makes without deciding to — that's the thing I'm always begging these books for and never getting: eroticism that's psychological, where the charge is in what's happening in her head, not a checklist of body parts. "Greed now would spend the night early." He's patient on the page in a way that made ME impatient in the good way. I was leaning in.

But here's where I actually sat up. The whole chapter I'm reading her as the naive heroine — twenty-one, sorority, the girl the jacket told me about. And then: *"There you are, Randi."* And the floor dropped out. This is the *friend.* This is the architect. The one who, per the blurb, started the game. And she's the one on the bench, begging in a voice that came from under the face, crying into the leather without knowing she's doing it — and then LOCKING THE BATHROOM DOOR and deciding, cool as a deadbolt going over, that he can have the face but the one he found is never coming out again.

That reframe is vicious and I loved it. Because now I know this predator-ish setup is a mutual trap, and the woman running the con just got caught by her own appetite harder than her mark ever will. The bottle cap under the thumbnail — pressing into the nail bed where it hurts — nobody narrated that for me, nobody explained it, it just sat there and I felt sick in the right way. And Pace sitting with the Daphne memory, respecting the locked door he once violated — so he's not a cartoon. He's got a wound and a code. Everybody in this room is doing something to someone.

What I'm suspicious of / what I love: the book is telling me, out loud, "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — and this chapter is the demonstration. Every yes freely given. I can feel the thesis being built and for once I don't resent it being pointed at, because the prose earned it instead of explaining it. The one thing that made me twitch is that occasionally it DOES over-narrate its own subtext — the "the face was lying, the face was good at lying" riff goes a beat past where I'd already gotten there myself. But it's a small tax.

I would text my friend: *put down book one chapter and I already need to know who Vee actually is, because the girl I thought was the heroine is the spider.*
