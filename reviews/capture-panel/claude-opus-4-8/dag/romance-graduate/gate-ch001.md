REACTION

Okay. So first: the twist. I did not clock it until "There you are, Randi." I spent the whole chapter thinking this was Vee — the jacket primed me for a young woman being undressed under a patient man's gaze, and I let the book walk me right into assuming the woman on the bench was the girl I was promised. And it's *Randi*. The friend. The co-conspirator. The one who's supposed to be running the game. That reframe hit me in the sternum, because it means Chapter 1 isn't the seduction of the target at all — it's the seduction of one of the *hunters*, the one who thought she was safe because she's on the other side of the plan. "as true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew there was one." The jacket told me that and I still didn't see it coming in the moment. That's the good kind of being played.

And the heat — thank god, actual heat. This is what I've been starving for. It's explicit without being a parts-inventory. The apple. The *thin slices, cut-side up because she eats them faster that way* — that's a man who watches, and the watching is the erotic engine, not the mechanics. The bench reveal, her reading it piece by piece, "That's not a table" / "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you" — I actually made a noise. The edging, the three *pleases* and the distinction between the waiter-please and the please that comes "from below" — that's the most precisely observed thing I've read in a spicy book in a year. It understands that the turn-on is the *collapse of the performance*, not the spanking. The spanking is just the crowbar.

But here's what has me genuinely rattled, in the way that makes me want to keep reading rather than the way that makes me quit: Randi's interiority in that bathroom is *devastating* and a little sinister. "He could have the face. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." She had the best experience of her life and her response is to lock it in a box forever because being *seen* is the one thing she can't survive. And she's the predator here! She and Pace planned Vee. So now I'm holding this awful, delicious question: if Randi — experienced, armored, in on it — cracked this completely on the bench, what is going to happen to Vee, who has no armor and doesn't even know there's a game? That's dread, and I want it.

The one thing I'm side-eyeing: the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail, "where it would hurt," and she doesn't know she's doing it. That's a flare — self-harm signal, and the book dropped it deliberately, twice-echoed with "she did not seem to know she was doing it." And Daphne, the door he didn't respect. This book is telling me Pace is not just a tender dom, he's got a body count of feelings behind him. Good. I don't want a clean man.

Prose is doing the both-things I never get: it's genuinely written *and* it feeds me. The Steely Dan, the quartersawn oak, "like a sheet being drawn." It's not showing off; every detail is load-bearing on character. What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *it's not the girl from the blurb. it's the FRIEND. and she just quietly decided to never be a real person again and I have to know what happens to the actual girl now. this one's real, read it.*
