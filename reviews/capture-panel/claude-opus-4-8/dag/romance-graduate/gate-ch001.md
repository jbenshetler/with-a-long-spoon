REACTION

Okay. Okay. I need to text somebody about this book but it's the kind of thing you can't actually text anyone.

First — the switch at the end. I spent the entire chapter reading this as *him and Vee*, the jacket's Vee, because the jacket front-loaded her so hard. The whole time I'm thinking, this is the mathematician, this is the girl waking up, this is the thing I was promised. And then: "There you are, Randi." And my stomach dropped. This is Randi. This is the glamorous friend who's supposedly steering Vee toward him "lightly, laughing, as if by accident." And she's already his. She's been coming to his house, locking the deadbolt first thing, straddling him in the kitchen, for weeks. So the whole seduction of Vee is a thing these two cooked up — and I just watched one of the architects get taken apart on a bench. That recontextualizes everything the jacket told me and I'm genuinely thrilled by it. The book withheld her name for the entire chapter on purpose and I didn't feel manipulated, I felt played *well*.

And here's the thing that actually got me, the thing that's better than the sex: the bathroom. The face. The whole interior monologue about the face being the thing that receives being-looked-at so nothing reaches *her* — "what was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing." That's a real psychology. That's a woman I understand in my spine. The decision she makes in there — not tonight, *not ever*, he can have the face, the one he found is going back behind it and not coming out again — that's the actual plot of this book declaring itself. She's the predator in the jacket's game and she just got cracked open by her own prey-to-be's lover, and she chose the lie. I did NOT expect to get a fully-realized tragic interior from the *villain* in chapter one. That's the grown-up thing I've been starving for.

The heat itself: the bench, the edging, the apple slices sliced thin and cut-side-up because he noticed she eats them faster that way — that detail is filthier than the spanking because it's *attention*. The man's entire eroticism is noticing. "Tell me your word." "Red." The aftercare is not decoration, it's the whole engine. And it's explicit without being a parts-list. I've read ten thousand edging scenes and this one actually tracked a nervous system reorganizing. I was in it.

What I'm suspicious of / what's got a hook in me: Daphne. "He had built a door, once, and he had not respected it... it had cost him more than he had known." So he's got his own wound and his own hard-won restraint, and the irony is that his beautiful respect-the-closed-door ethic is exactly what lets Randi hide from him. He thinks tonight was a triumph. He thinks he found the hunger and answered it. He has no idea she just bricked herself up in the bathroom. The dramatic irony between the two of them on that bed — him "quietly happy about her," her with the hum in her ribs "the size of a key turning in a lock" — is *delicious*.

The water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail where it would hurt. The flinch at "you're beautiful." These people are both doing small self-harming things and I clocked both. This writer trusts me.

My only faint worry is whether a whole book can sustain this density, and whether the Vee seduction will land as hard now that I know the frame. But that's a worry about the future, not this chapter. This chapter is exactly both things I wanted. I'm annoyed I have to write a gate instead of just hitting next.
