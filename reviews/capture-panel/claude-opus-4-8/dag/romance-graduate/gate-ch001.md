REACTION

Okay. Okay. I need to text somebody about this one.

So first — the jacket told me the game. Two people planned her best year, she doesn't know there's a plan, Randi's the glamorous friend steering Vee toward Pace. I walked in braced for yet another "watch the innocent girl get managed" setup and ready to roll my eyes. And then chapter one just… isn't that. It's Pace and *Randi* — the architect — on the bench. The predator, facedown and begging and crying and then locking herself in the bathroom to put her face back on. That's the move that made me sit up. The book handed me the schemer first, and showed me she's the one in actual danger. That reframes the whole premise before I've even met Vee. I'm suspicious in the best way now: if this is what Randi is underneath, then whatever she's "planning" for Vee is going to recoil on her, and the book already knows it. The jacket said "as true for the two who started it" — I usually hate when a blurb tells me the turn, but watching it *seeded* in chapter one instead of promised is different.

And the heat. Thank god. This is what I've been starving for. It's explicit — fingers, slick, the whole thing is right there on the page, no closed door, no "he took her to heights" nonsense — but every physical beat is doing psychological work at the same time. The edging isn't choreography, it's him systematically dismantling her script. The bit where she tries to beg in "the voice she said please in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water" and he *clocks that it's not real yet* — that undid me. He's not after her orgasm, he's after the voice underneath the voice. And the book earned the word "merciless in its tenderness," because the tenderness is the weapon. "There you are, Randi" dropping as the reveal of her name — I actually made a noise.

The apple. The thin slices, cut-sides up because she eats them faster that way, the mother on egg whites "with a knife in it," the water cap pressed under her thumbnail where it'll hurt while she's being held — this is a heroine with a whole interior architecture of control and self-punishment and I got all of it through objects, not through someone explaining it to me. The prose trusts me. When she's at the mirror and the face "had been lying for years" and the decision to go back behind it arrives "quiet as a deadbolt going over" — that's the same deadbolt she turned on page one, the one she always locks first thing. The book planted that and paid it off in nine pages without nudging me. I noticed and it didn't make me feel dumb for noticing.

What's she so afraid of that being *seen* is the unbearable thing and being spanked raw is the safe thing? That's the hook. Not "will they get together" — they're already together — but *she just decided, mid-afterglow, to wall off the realest thing that's ever happened to her and let him love a mask.* That's a tragedy I can feel coming and I want to watch it.

Pace I'm slightly wary of — the Daphne backstory, "loving someone was a kind of permission," the whole courtly-restraint thing could curdle into insufferable if the book worships him. Right now he reads as genuinely careful rather than smug, and the fact that he's completely wrong about tonight — he thinks it was "a remarkably good evening," he has no idea she just left the building — gives him a blind spot that keeps him human. But I'm watching him.

Only thing: it's one long sustained scene, basically one note held for a whole chapter. Gorgeous, but I want to see if the book can *talk* — two people in a room, clothed, with this much going on underneath. If chapter two is another scene like this I'll still be here; if it's three of them I'll start wondering if the heat is the only gear. But that's a worry for later. Right now I'm already turning the page.
