REACTION

Oh, this is not what I thought I was buying, and I mean that in the best way. I came in from the blurb thinking Vee was my girl, that I'd be riding shotgun with the twenty-one-year-old waking up — and instead Chapter 1 drops me straight into the bedroom of the two people running the game, and the "her" on the bench is *Randi*. That reveal — "There you are, Randi" — I actually sat up. Because the whole chapter I'd assumed, lazily, the way the book wanted me to, that this was Vee. And it's the handler. The one who's supposed to be doing this *to* someone, and here she is undone on a bench, leaking tears into leather, and deciding in the bathroom mirror that she will never let it happen again. That's a hell of an opening move. The book is a step ahead of me and it knows it, and it didn't explain the trick to me afterward either, which I was braced for and grateful not to get.

And the sex — finally. FINALLY. This is the thing I've been starving for. It's explicit, it's wet, it's a spanking-and-edging scene with a bench he *built* for her, and not one beat of it is the recited choreography I could do in my sleep. The heat is entirely psychological. He's not hurrying because every man before him hurried; he edges her not to be cruel but to strip the apparatus of performance off her until the *please* comes from somewhere real. The apple slices cut thin because she eats thin ones faster, cut-side up — that detail did more to me than any ten "he gripped her hips" sentences. This man pays attention. The whole scene is about being *seen*, and the book makes seeing the erotic act, which is exactly the grown-up thing I keep begging for.

But what's got its hook all the way in me is Randi's interiority in that bathroom. The face she's had since thirteen. The face that takes the wanting and passes it inward in amounts *she* decides. "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." And tonight something got behind the face and she can't get it back, so she decides — quiet as a deadbolt — to go back out and give him the face forever and never come out again. That's devastating and it's *character*, real architecture, a woman I understand completely in one chapter. The cap edge pressed under her thumbnail. The lock turning over for the first time. Christ.

Pace I'm suspicious of in the most delicious way. He's tender, he's exact, he respects the closed door because of some Daphne he violated once — and he's also, let's not forget, one of the two people running a *plan* on a 21-year-old. So all this courtly attentiveness I'm falling for is the exact machinery the blurb told me to be afraid of. He thinks tonight was a triumph. He has no idea she's already gone behind the glass. The dramatic irony is stacked three deep and I love being inside it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the spanking bench chapter turned out to be a CHARACTER STUDY and the top is the one who broke, start this book."
