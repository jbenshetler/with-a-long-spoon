REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to sit here for a second.

I bought this off the jacket thinking I knew the con — "two people planned the best year of her life, she doesn't know there's a plan" — and I walked in expecting to be the reader who's ahead of a sweet naive Vee. And then the very first chapter pulls the rug out and hands me *Randi* on the bench. This isn't Vee's night. This is the woman who's supposedly the co-architect of the seduction, and she's the one who just got taken somewhere she can't uncross. That is a *filthy* structural move and I am not over it.

Because here's the thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: the sex is actually written. Not "throbbing member" written, not fade-to-black written — written. The apple sliced thin because she won't eat thick slices, cut-sides up because she eats faster that way. The furniture he built that she can't read the price tag on. The lights she asked to lower on night one and he said *can we leave them up* and then spent an hour looking at her. I have read a thousand dominant heroes and not one of them noticed that his sub eats apples faster when the cut side shows. That detail did more to establish his particular attentiveness than any amount of "he was a man who noticed everything" telling. The heat is coming FROM the characterization, not despite it. That's the thing I've been starving for and thought didn't exist.

And the whole scene is edging — his AND mine. He keeps taking his hand away and I kept feeling the withdrawal in my own chest. The "please" architecture — the please she says to a waiter versus the please that comes from below — is the most genuinely erotic thing I've read in ages precisely because it's psychological. He's not chasing an orgasm, he's chasing the moment her performance apparatus runs out. *"There you are."* Then — *"There you are, Randi."* I gasped. Withholding her name the whole chapter and dropping it at the exact moment she's most stripped is a gorgeous, cruel little knife.

But the part that flipped it from hot to genuinely unsettling — in the good way, the way I want more of — is the bathroom. She locks the door (deadbolt callback, don't think I missed that). She looks at the face she built at thirteen, and she decides: he can have the face, the one he found is going back behind it and never coming out again. And the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail where it hurts, that she doesn't know she's doing. He, meanwhile, is out there tender and pleased, thinking he *fed* a starving girl and will do it as often as she wants. Two people in the same bed, one of them has just quietly bolted a door forever and the other thinks it was the best night. That's not a spanking scene. That's a horror scene wearing a spanking scene's clothes and I am so here for it.

And it reframes the whole jacket. If *Randi* — the seducer, the one who "planned it" — is this closed, this armored, this frightened of being actually seen, then what is she going to do to Vee? The predator has a wound. The Daphne backstory (Pace opening a door he wasn't invited through, and it costing him) plants that he's capable of real harm even while being "courtly." Nobody here is safe and everybody thinks they're the one in control. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." Now I feel that line in my teeth.

Things I'm suspicious of / watching: it's very close third on Pace for most of the chapter, and his self-satisfaction ("all he had done was find it and answer it") is either the book being naive or the book setting him up, and I need it to be the second one. I trust it's the second because the narration clearly knows more than he does — it lets us see her bolt the door while he doesn't. If that ironic distance holds, this book is going to gut me. If it collapses into "actually the dom was right and she just needs to submit emotionally too," I'll be furious. But one chapter in, the intelligence is real.

This is depth AND heat. Both hands. First time in a long time.
