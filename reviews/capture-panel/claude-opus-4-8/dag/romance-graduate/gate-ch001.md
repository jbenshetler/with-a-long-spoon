REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to talk about this.

First — the trick. The whole jacket sold me a girl named Vee being lightly steered toward a mathematician by her glamorous friend Randi. And I walked into chapter one thinking I was about to meet Vee, and instead I'm on a bench with *Randi*. The book didn't tell me it was Randi until the last beat — "There you are, Randi" — and I actually made a noise. Because I'd spent the whole chapter reading her as the innocent, the one getting undone, and she's the *architect*. The predator. The one who, per the blurb, started this as a game. And she just got taken apart by the man she's supposedly running the game with. That reframe landed like a trapdoor. I'm not reading a seduction; I'm reading the seducer discovering she's got a soft spot she can't afford, and *locking it in a room forever* in the last three pages. That's not a trope I can recite. That's the good stuff.

The heat itself — this is what I've been starving for and couldn't find. It's explicit, it is genuinely explicit, the fingers and the slick and the smell of her, none of it fades to black, none of it goes clinical. But the charge isn't the mechanics. It's the *waiting*. It's a man who is deliberately not in a hurry and a woman whose entire operating system is built on hurrying men to a conclusion so nothing reaches her, and he just... refuses. He edges her past her own apparatus until the performance runs out and the real "please" falls out of her. I've read a thousand edging scenes and they were all choreography. This one is a psychological siege. The "please of a woman who asks a waiter for water" versus the please "from below" — I felt that distinction in my body. That's a writer who knows exactly what the difference is and made me feel it before he named it.

And the apple. Thin slices, cut sides up, because she eats them faster that way. The girl who portions herself, whose mother lives on egg whites "with a knife in it." He *fed* her. Before any of the rest. God.

But here's what's turning me inside out and why I'm actually a little scared of this book: the bathroom scene. She wins the orgasm and *loses herself*, and she knows it, and she stands at the mirror and makes the coldest decision in the book — the face gets him, the real one goes back behind it and is "not coming out again." "Quiet as a deadbolt going over." She locks the door for the first time ever in his house and I clocked it and then the book made the deadbolt a metaphor and I still wasn't mad. And the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail where it hurts, and she doesn't know she's doing it — that little self-harm tell, dropped in without comment. This woman is *damaged* in a specific, legible, awful way and I understand her completely.

Meanwhile Pace. The Daphne door he didn't respect once and learned from. He's not a brooding alpha, he's — careful. Attentive to the point of it being almost unbearable. And he thinks he's found her hunger and answered it and it "moved him," and he's sitting there quietly happy, having *no idea* that the exact thing he's proudest of is the thing that just made her decide to wall herself off forever. The dramatic irony is vicious. He won and he doesn't know he lost her. Or — and this is what I'm suspicious of — is *he* running a longer game too? The blurb says it started as a game between the two of them about Vee. So they're co-conspirators who each may be getting genuinely caught. I want to know which of them is really in control and I don't think it's either of them.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the innocent from the back cover IS the villain and she just got emotionally destroyed on a sex bench and decided to fake being fine forever and I have never wanted to know what happens next this badly in my LIFE. also it's filthy AND it made me sad. read it."

The one thing giving me a flicker of pause: the prose leans hard on repetition-as-incantation ("the face had begged, the face had said, the face was hers") and it *works* here but if every chapter does it, it'll curdle into a tic. And I know almost nothing yet about where the plot goes — this is one closed room, one night. But as an opening? It didn't clear its throat for a second. It opened on a hidden bench and a woman locking a deadbolt.
