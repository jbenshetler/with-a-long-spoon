REACTION

Okay. Okay. I did not think this book was going to do what it just did.

Here's the thing — I came in braced. The jacket told me the whole shape: two people plan a girl's seduction, Vee thinks it's chance, Randi is in on it. So I opened Chapter One expecting to meet Vee, our sweet unknowing lamb, and instead the book hands me *Randi* — facedown, begging, crying on a bench she didn't know was a bench — and lets me watch the woman who's supposedly running the con get taken apart. That's a hell of a move. The predator is the one getting hollowed out in the opening scene, before I've even met the mark. I'm suspicious in the best way. What kind of game is this if the huntress can be reduced to a raw sound she's never made before?

And the *craft* of it. I have read a thousand spanking scenes and I could recite most of them. This one didn't work like any of them. The whole engine of the scene wasn't the impact, it was the *withholding* — the hands rising up her sides and turning back before they arrive, the edging, the "not yet," the three-pleases-that-weren't-the-right-please. He's not topping her body, he's topping the gap between her performed self and her actual self, and the book made me feel the exact seam where the sorority-waiter *please* cracked into the real one. When he stopped and just said "No. Not yet" and let her breathing break — I felt that in my stomach. That's the thing I've been starving for. Restraint written by someone who knows restraint is hotter than a hundred thrusts.

The apple. THE APPLE. Thin slices, cut-sides up because she eats faster that way, a girl who portions her wants down to almost nothing — and then the whole scene is a man feeding her the thing she won't let herself have. That's not decoration, that's the entire book announcing its thesis in a fruit bowl. And the water bottle cap under her thumbnail at the end, the little self-hurt she doesn't know she's doing — God. This woman is a wound wearing a beautiful face.

The turn at "There you are, Randi" genuinely got me. I'd been reading her as "she" the whole time, an anonymous polished girl, and the name landing right when she cracks open — that's the reveal doubling as the emotional gut-punch. Chef's kiss.

But the part that actually scared me, that made this more than very good erotica: the bathroom. The *not ever*. She goes behind the locked door — the first door she's ever locked in his house — and decides, quiet as a deadbolt, that the one he found is going back behind the face and never coming out again. And he, meanwhile, is out on the bed feeling "the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he thought she'd go," misreading the whole thing as tenderness landing. He thinks he fed her. She thinks she got caught. Two people in the same warm room, and one of them just quietly closed forever, and he doesn't know. The Daphne thing — the door he didn't respect once, so now he *won't* open this one — is the cruelest irony in the chapter: his hard-won decency is exactly what lets her escape being reached. That's psychologically merciless like the jacket promised, and it earned the word.

What I'd text my friend at 1 a.m.: *this book opened with the villain sobbing on a bench and I have never been so sure I'm in trouble. the man asks permission like it's a religion and it's the filthiest thing I've ever read. also she's already gone and he thinks he won.*

I have no idea how a two-page-chapter book works because I've never read one, but this wasn't a two-pager — this was a full, long, complete scene and it delivered a whole novel's worth of interiority inside one night. If Vee shows up next with even half of Randi's inner life, I'm not putting this down for weeks.
