Okay. Okay okay okay. I picked this up braced for another "courtly dom teaches ingénue to feel" setup — the blurb basically promised me Pace-the-perfect-mathematician and Vee-the-starving-good-girl — and then the chapter spent its entire length making me fall for exactly that, the warm house, the pre-sliced honeycrisp with the cut sides up because he noticed she eats them faster that way (that detail *got* me, I'll be honest, that's the kind of attention I keep saying I want), the ask-first, the safeword check-ins that actually read like check-ins and not seasoning — and then it signed the whole thing *Randi.* "There you are, Randi." I made a noise out loud.

Because now I understand what I was reading. This isn't Vee on the bench. This is the *other* woman from the blurb, the friend, the one who "steers Vee lightly, laughing, as if by accident" — and she's not the puppeteer I assumed, or she's not *only* that. She's a mark too. Or she's a co-conspirator who just got conscripted by her own body. The blurb told me "it began as a game between the two people now closest to her" and "how real it would get: as true for the two who started it." So this is one of the two who started it, and I just watched her crack open on a bench and decide — quiet as a deadbolt going over, God, that line — to seal herself back up and never come out again. I am *so* interested. That's a genuinely clever thing to do with a first chapter and it retroactively made the pre-sliced apple sinister in a way I liked.

And the heat — the heat delivered. This is what I've been starving for and couldn't find. It's fully explicit, it's slow, the edging is agonizing in the right way, but every physical beat is doing psychological work. The spanking isn't choreography, it's him dismantling her performance. The whole thing is *about* the difference between the please she gives a waiter and the please that comes from below. That's the exact register I want and almost never get — the sex IS the interiority, not a break from it. And her interiority is genuinely there: the food thing, the eyes she built a life around, the flinch at "you're beautiful," the water-bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail where nobody can see. That self-harm beat she doesn't know she's doing — that's not a stupid book.

What I'm side-eyeing: Pace. He's rendered as the ideal — the Daphne backstory, the respected door, the "that's not what tonight was for." He's almost too clean, and the blurb keeps whispering "psychologically merciless" and "a secret choice made about her," so I don't trust the tenderness. I think the book knows I don't, which is why it's letting me love him while Randi decides to weaponize her own face against him. The predator/prey lines are blurred in a way that's making me lean IN, not out.

The one thing nagging me: this was long and it was one sustained scene. Gorgeous, but I want to know the *machinery* now — the game, the plan, who's doing what to whom. If chapter two is another single-POV set-piece I'll get restless. Right now, though? I'd text my friend: *put down whatever you're reading. the ingénue on the cover is not the one on the bench and I need to talk about it.*
