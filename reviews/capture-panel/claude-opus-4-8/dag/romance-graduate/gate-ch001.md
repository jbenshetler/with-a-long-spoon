REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to text somebody.

So first — the whole chapter I'm reading it as Vee. Obviously. The jacket set me up: glamorous Randi steers naive Vee toward Pace, courtly exact man who asks before he takes. So this facedown-on-the-bench girl who locks the deadbolt first thing, who portions herself down to thin apple slices, who's built a whole life around blue eyes and a face that receives being-wanted and passes none of it inward — that's my Vee, right? The starving one. And I'm all the way in it. And then he says *There you are, Randi* and I actually made a noise. Because the book didn't just withhold her name for a cheap reveal — it withheld it so that when it lands, the entire architecture flips under me. This isn't the mark. This is one of the two people who planned the game. The seducer got seduced, and she's the one who can't let herself be seen. That's the *psychologically merciless* the blurb promised and I did not think it would cash the check in chapter one.

And the heat. God. This is what I've been starving for and couldn't name. It's not the choreography — I could recite spanking-bench choreography in my sleep. It's that the whole scene is *about* something. The edging isn't edging for edging's sake, it's him hunting the difference between her waiter-voice *please* and the *please* that comes from below, the one from the crying child she used to be. He's not trying to make her come, he's trying to make her *arrive*, and I felt the exact seam where performance ran out of apparatus. "She could not pout her way back." I've read a thousand alphas who "see her completely" and it's always a flattering lie the book tells the heroine. This actually *shows the seeing* as a mechanism, and then shows it as a violation she has to flee into the bathroom to survive.

The details are what got me. The cut sides of the apple facing up because she eats faster that way. The blue toenails as the one place she steps out of line. Him not correcting her that it was a table — *"No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you."* — that's the whole seduction ethic in one line, plausible deniability offered as a gift. And the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail while she's wrapped in the cashmere, the little self-hurt she doesn't know she's doing. That's the tell that this "asks before he takes" man is holding a woman who's coming apart in a way tenderness can't reach, and the tenderness is precisely the thing she flinches from. *You're beautiful* makes her flinch. That reversal — where the soft word is the unbearable one — is so much smarter than manufactured conflict.

What I'm suspicious of, deliciously: Pace's read of the night is *complete* and *completely wrong*. He thinks he found her hunger and answered it and she'll bring it back to him as often as she wants. He has NO idea she just decided, quiet as a deadbolt going over, *not ever* — that she's going back behind the face and locking it. He's sitting there deciding whether to offer dinner. The dramatic irony is exquisite and it makes him a little frightening, actually — not because he's cruel, because he's so good and so sure and so unable to see the door she just built. And the Daphne backstory: he learned to respect a closed door *because* he once didn't and it cost him. So the one man disciplined enough to leave her the lock is the man she's using the lock against. He's going to respect his way right into losing her, or into a war neither of them names.

The two-page-chapter thing I fretted about in the abstract — irrelevant. This was a full chapter, fifteen-plus pages of it, and it knew exactly where to put my thumb. It ends on the bench in lamplight, damp in two places, waiting. I'm not setting this down.

One tiny flicker of worry: the "the face did X, the face did Y" anaphora in the mirror ran a beat long, three sentences past where I'd have cut it. But it's earning something, not showing off, so I forgave it before I finished being annoyed.

I came for both and I got both. First chapter. I'm nervous about how good this is.
