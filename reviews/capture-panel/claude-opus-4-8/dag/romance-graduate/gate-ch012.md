REACTION

Okay so first — I got my heat back. Finally. I was owed it after that long soft run and this chapter paid. But the thing that actually got me isn't the shirt coming off. It's that *she* did it. She's on her knees on the bench, she raps the window, she crooks the finger, she takes his eyes and holds them and undoes the buttons one by one while watching his face. And he — the master of "may I choose," the man who asks before he takes — sat there and *didn't move.* For once he wasn't running the play. She ran it. And the book knows exactly what it did, because that's the jacket's whole thesis made flesh: *every step is hers.* She took it. Every yes freely given, and she's the one saying yes so loud she's undressing to it in a cold truck on three dates. God, it's good and it's awful and I couldn't look away.

The horseradish. The moleskin. The cherry pit. I want to be annoyed at how much this book loves its little training rituals — every scene he teaches her one more thing, corrects one more small wrongness her mother's voice screamed about, and does it without making her small. "We take nothing and leave nothing." The chapter's literally called Leave No Trace and I clocked it — nothing taken that she didn't hand over, no fingerprints on the crime. He picks up her pit and sets it next to his so her shame has company. It's the tenderest gaslighting I've ever read and I keep falling for the tenderness anyway, which is the horror of this whole book working on me exactly like it works on her.

But here's what actually cracked me open, and I did not expect it: him getting out of the truck. Giving her the only dry shirt AND the whole cab, standing in the rain with his back turned so she can be alone with the choice. And then — *he was shivering.* She reaches for his hand to kiss it and it's shaking and it stops her cold. That's the first moment in twelve chapters where I believed something in him wasn't calibrated. He'd "been a long way from the question of whether he was cold." Either that's real — he's actually losing the plot, actually caught in it, the jacket promised it'd get real for the two who started it too — or it's the deepest move yet, the sacrifice that makes her feel like the one in power while he stays the one who set the whole thing up. And the sick genius is I can't tell, and *she* takes care of *him,* "you're freezing," pulls his hand under the flannel against her belly. She thinks she just topped from the bottom. Maybe she did. Maybe that was the gift he built the whole hike to hand her.

Randi's fingerprints are all over this chapter and she's not even in it. "Ravish." The picture she hung at the white table — hand her up over the rock, out of breath at the top, and then he turns and asks — Vee walked up that literal mountain *inside Randi's fantasy* and re-ran it the whole climb watching his back in the blue shirt. Randi scripted the scene and Pace staged it. The two of them, at right angles, and the girl who never knew. I felt them both in that cab with her.

The cop at the end made me laugh out loud and then made my stomach drop — the siren literally taking the words out of her mouth, the big thing she was going to say swallowed by a squawk, and him pulled away into the rain with the lights behind him. She's left on the curb in his flannel that smells like soap and a man and a gym bag, carrying it home the way she carried "picked" home last chapter. That's a thumb-moving ending. That's the shape done right.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: SHE unbuttoned it. On her knees. Holding his eyes. And HE was the one shaking. I don't know if he's caught or if that's the con and I'm scared of how much I need chapter 13 to tell me.
