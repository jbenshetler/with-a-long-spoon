Okay, so this is the debrief, and I have complicated feelings about being handed the same hike twice in one sitting — I *just* watched her take her shirt off in that cab, I don't need the wax-paper cherries recapped to me forty pages later. Structurally I clocked it immediately: chapter 11 was the event, chapter 12 is Vee narrating the event. That's the pattern I flagged in my notes — she keeps converting her life into a story she tells someone at a desk. And normally the second telling would make me skim.

But it didn't, because of who she's telling it to, and because of what Cassie *does*.

Cassie is my tripwire. Cassie is the one who said "How much did he give you to drink?" and "Goodbye, Vee." Cassie is the one honest instrument in this whole rigged orchestra. And I sat down for this chapter braced for her to squint, to poke a hole, to say *he had a kit, he had a spare dry shirt, he had it all pre-loaded* — because that's exactly what it was, it was staged, the man packs a hot-spot kit and a folded clean flannel because he *knows how the day ends*. And Cassie starts there! She starts with the file, the blood type, the witness statement, the windowless van. She's doing her job.

And then she melts. "Cassie was rapt." That line went through me like cold water. The one person reading the con clearly went *still and stayed that way* and got charmed by the standing-in-the-rain-with-his-back-turned bit — which, granted, is a genuinely good bit, it worked on me too in chapter 11 — but Cassie falling for it is the scariest thing that's happened in six chapters. The exit is closing. The tripwire got tripped and didn't go off. "Borrow real boots next time" is not "Goodbye, Vee." She's in the story now too. That's the horror underneath a scene that reads as two girls being sweet.

What I'm noticing, and grudgingly respecting: the chapter knows Pace's whole seduction is prep and lets *me* hold that while Vee and now Cassie can't. Nobody says "isn't it strange he had exactly the right thing every single time." The moleskin, the second shirt — it's all recited as tenderness. That gap is still the engine and it's still working.

But I'm going to be honest about the heat, because heat is the whole reason I'm here. Chapter 11 gave me the truck, finally, the shame-into-want in her own mother's voice, the buttons — that was the payoff I'd been waiting six soft chapters for. Chapter 12 gives me none of that firsthand; it gives me Vee *describing* it in past tense to a friend, sanded down to "I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose." That's the after-photo, not the thing. And what I really want — Pace and Randi in a room, on the record, the machine from the inside — this book keeps not giving me. It's been two full chapters now that live in the aftermath.

Still. It's a two-page bridge and it earned its size by doing one real thing: it moved Cassie. I'll allow it. But I want the next chapter to be *present tense*, and I want Randi, whom I haven't seen do her extraction in a while, and who is going to want her turn at this story too — and *that* debrief, the one where Vee hands the truck to Randi, is the one I'm actually leaning toward.
