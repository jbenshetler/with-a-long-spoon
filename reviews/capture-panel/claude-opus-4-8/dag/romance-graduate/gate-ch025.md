REACTION

Okay, so I finally got my room last chapter — Pace and Randi across the table, the bowl she wouldn't eat, "It's selfish, Pace," the eyes-shut drive-it-home sex with no *tell me* — and I've been coasting on that ever since. So I came into this one already fed, and this is the book letting me down easy after. It's a bridge and it knows it's a bridge, and mostly I didn't mind.

What I loved: the credit card. That's the first time the machine has cost Vee something I can count. All the debriefs, all the surrender — that's been emotional freight, gorgeous and awful, but *abstract*. This is a number. She puts the debit back and takes out the emergency card and there's nothing under it and she knows it and she signs anyway and won't let it reach her face — and right beside her Randi drops a multiple of that on loafers she'll wear out the door without a glance. That's the whole class gap made physical, the whole "you can afford to be this free and she can't" of it, and the book didn't say a word about it, just let the two cards sit on the counter next to each other. That's the writing I stayed for. The shoes are what the dress is *owed* — she talks herself into debt the exact way she talked herself into everything else, and calls it math. I felt the floor go out.

The shoe store being funny actually got me too — the yeti canoe, the rhinestone platforms, "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." I've said the best scene in a book can be two people talking, and two women being genuinely silly is rarer and harder than a sex scene and this one landed. And then Randi on one knee on the pale floor, thumb across the frosted-plum toenail, and Vee's hand shooting out to grab her shoulder because the room swung — *there's* my daylight thread, moving exactly where the jacket promised, in the middle of a shoe fitting, over toes Vee painted for a man. "Same feet, baby. Same size." The marriage joke. It's all so warm and it's all a net.

What poked me wrong: the Simpson's paradox lecture. Same numbers, nobody touched one of them, put the two years together and it flips. I *see* what you're doing, book — that's the con, that's the parts-versus-the-whole, that's the thing I clocked twenty chapters ago with the error-correcting codes and the MRI signal. I don't need the econ professor to stand at a whiteboard and diagram the trick for me while I'm watching it work. It's the closest this book has come to explaining its own subtext, and from a book this confident it read like a nudge in the ribs I didn't ask for. I'm a big girl. I found the signal in the noise a long time ago.

But it's one page in a chapter that otherwise trusted me. I want the induction now — I want that dress in that room, the door, the whole thing turning, and I want to see whether the debt comes due before or after the glory. Text a friend: *she just went into credit card debt she can't cover for shoes and the other woman was on her knees touching her toes while she did it and NOBODY in this book has said the quiet part out loud yet except the baseball stats.*
