REACTION

Oh, Cassie. Cassie is the whole chapter and Cassie is why I'm still holding my breath.

After 32 through 35 — the bed, the brunch that nearly killed me, the drawer he emptied for her — this one is quiet on purpose, and the quiet is the point. It's the debrief chapter, the one where Vee comes home the "streetlight" and spills, except this time the person on the other side of the couch isn't Randi. And the difference is everything. Randi draws her open to widen the wound; Cassie draws her open and then just... sits with what falls out, with no face on. "Cassie for whatever you say." I love her so much it aches, and I'm scared of exactly that, because the book has taught me that the honest instrument is the one that gets edited out or hurt.

The genius small thing: her hand going to the side of her breast without her knowing it moved. She's telling the tomato-burn story and her body narrates the truth underneath it before she can. And then Cassie, dry as a bone — "There's only one way that burn happens. Not enough on." She *sees* it. She clocked that Vee's naked at his table, clocked it in one line, and didn't make a meal of it. That's the tell that Cassie is the only person in this book reading Vee instead of running her. Randi would've built a whole confession-machine out of that burn. Cassie just names it and lets it lie there.

And "You don't come home school nights." Not a question — the same weapon Pace and Randi use, the flat statement that isn't asking, except in Cassie's mouth it's not a trap, it's a friend counting empty beds and worrying. "You've had boyfriends. You never went like this over one." That's the closest thing to an alarm bell anyone's rung for Vee, and it's rung *kindly*, which is what makes it land. Cassie's not trying to pull her out. She's just marking that this is not normal, that Vee has never once dissolved like this, and letting Vee hear herself run out of words. Vee — who "hadn't stopped talking since the door" — goes small. "Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast." And Cassie lets the maybe stand. Doesn't say I know. Doesn't say be careful. Just "go to sleep, streetlight."

What got me is the losing-weight-she-never-asked-for thread carried forward from 35 and how it sits next to Cassie's "not enough on." Her body is being burned down and warmed up and remade in that house and she narrates it all as being *cared for*. And it is care — that's the horror I keep chewing. Nothing here is cruel. The man really does fuss over a burn for days. The drawer really is a tender thing. And Cassie is the one clean mirror who can see the shape of the surrender without a single accusation, and she's being quietly, lovingly overwritten too — Vee comes home lit up and doesn't feel the cold, and Cassie keeps the window cracked its two inches and keeps letting her in.

The dread constant I said was my exit — the moment the narration stops knowing more than Vee's gladness — still hasn't come. This chapter, if anything, the narration knows MORE than usual, and it planted Cassie as the one who knows too. That's what keeps me. I'd text a friend: *the honest roommate just said "you don't come home school nights" and I felt it in my whole chest, she's the only one not running a con and I'm terrified the book is going to spend her.*

Thinner chapter than the brunch, no heat to speak of — a cooldown. But it's the right cooldown, and it moved the one piece I care about most. Cassie is either the exit Vee won't take or the casualty. I need to know which.
