The lemon-caper chicken, the wine glugged into the pan, "the pan needed it worse than she did" — I want to live in that kitchen. It knows how to feed me, literally and every other way.

BUT. The picture. Oh, the picture. This is the chapter where I stopped trusting the gladness and I think the book *wants* me not to.

Watch the choreography, because I did: she offers pinup, glamour, arranges herself — and he says *"No, not like that. You'll have to wait,"* and then he *poses her himself.* Turns her shoulder, sets her elbow, lays her hand along her hip, moves her legs, one knee ahead of the other, a kiss at each place he sets her. Now there's a photograph of her, composed to his exact specification, that lives on his device. "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." "Never. I'd never share your photograph." I have read enough of these to know a promise sitting that clean and that early in a Book One is a loaded gun on the mantel. And it's WITH A LONG SPOON, book one — the spoon you sup with the devil. That photo is going to matter and it is not going to be sweet.

But honestly the thing that actually scared me wasn't even the photo mechanics. It was the drawer. Chapter 36 and the top drawer — he empties his own ordered drawer, folds his whole careful self into one below, and gives her the easy top drawer, "the one your hand goes to first," no note, never a note. On my first read that's the most romantic thing in the book.
