REACTION

Oh, this one got me, and it got me the quiet way, which is the way I didn't see coming.

I came in braced for the same thing Vee was braced for — the summit, the two of them in one room, the card finally turned over. And the book did the smartest possible thing: it made the dread *recede*. Randi beat them to the roadhouse, of course she did, twenty minutes out on a nothing road, car square to the door, and for one line I thought *here it comes, the architecture showing itself.* And then it just... didn't. It became a genuinely warm party. And I spent the whole chapter unable to decide whether that warmth was the book letting me breathe or the book pickpocketing me while I admired the honey-colored pine.

"Vee's boyfriend." Six times. I felt it land low every time, same as Vee did, because I've been waiting for a *word* from this man for a volume and a half — the withholder, the one who taught himself words are the whole war — and here he is spending one, out loud, at volume for three, in front of the one witness it matters most in front of. And Randi's hand closing hard enough to feel the bones. God. Vee reads it as *she's happy for me*, and I read it as the exact opposite — that grip is Randi taking the hit. He said the word he'd never say to Daphne, never say to anyone, and he said it in the one room engineered to make Randi hear it. That's not him failing to notice the running joke. He never fails to notice anything. The joke was *for her.*

And then the thing he actually does all night, which is the most chilling tender thing in the book so far dressed as the nicest. He "sets them up." Hands each person their first line and stands back out of the lamplight and lets them be the best version of themselves — Danny never learns his bench number, Theo's shoulders come down, Cassie *talks in paragraphs*, Meg finishes her sentences. He made every single one of Vee's people glow. And what did he collect? Himself, invisible, at the edge of the light, having learned the whole geography of each of them off Vee without them knowing they'd given it. He's doing to the whole roster exactly what he does to Vee: feeding them their own taste back and keeping everything. "They share an interest. Randi. Your friends. You." That line stopped my breath. He mapped the room and the center of the map is her and he *told* her so and she let it warm her instead of hearing the survey being taken.

The twinge — him at Randi's elbow, Randi laughing, the ponytail — and Vee's arm coming up across herself before she knew it moved, the Christmas shape, the kitchen she never saw. And she talked herself out of it in half a second: *this is my own doing, I put them in the room.* That's the trapdoor line, and she stepped right over it. Some corner of her can't watch a thing go right without checking it for the floor, and she's decided that corner is paranoia. It isn't. It's the only part of her still awake.

The toast. "Meeting Vee was the highlight of my year." And then the glazed quiet, the hand going down off the table into her lap, the eyes in the middle distance — and Vee *proud* of being able to move Randi, reading it as a story landing. I know that quiet is Randi going under at the table, right there, drowning in front of both of them while Vee pats her wrist and loves her so much it aches. The saddest staging the book's done yet, because the cover's right there in the text: *Vee is a willing player in two games. She doesn't know there are three.* That toast was the third game bleeding through the floor and Vee filed it under friendship.

Then the truck. And here's where I'll be honest with myself: it was hot, genuinely, the stillness of his hand in her hair, the man who does six things dropping to zero — I've been told that's his tell and the book cashed it and I felt it. The blowjob in the dark cab, the jeans worked down inch by graceless honest inch, the lace she pulls aside herself as an offering she has to keep choosing — the book keeps insisting the reaching is hers, keeps putting the choosing in her hands literally. And it half worked on me and half made my skin crawl *because* it worked, because I know by now that "her own nerve" is the exact thing the whole machine is built to manufacture. The callback to the September mountain road, him shivering soaked and never saying a word, versus him dry and easy and nothing-trembling now — that's a real ache, that's earned, I felt the want come up through her "so plainly it was almost calm." And the last line — the small sentence standing at a window watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time, that couldn't make itself heard tonight and *didn't try* — that's Vee's own witness going quiet voluntarily. That scares me more than the sex. She's training herself out of her own alarm.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: HE SAID BOYFRIEND SIX TIMES AND IT WAS A WEAPON AIMED AT THE OTHER WOMAN AT THE TABLE AND VEE THINKS IT'S THE SWEETEST NIGHT OF HER LIFE. Also the toast broke me. Also I need to know what Cassie meant by "you can stay."

What I'm turning over: Cassie. "You can stay" — her blessing, finally, the goalkeeper lowering her hands. Which means the one clean witness has signed off, and I don't know yet if that's the book telling me I'm wrong to dread, or telling me even Cassie got set up. And the MIRANDA fraud, the scar still owed, Brooke's "choose" — none of it surfaced tonight, which means it's all still loaded.

This was a bridge chapter that didn't read like one because the charge was continuous under it the whole time. No stall. The opposite of a stall — it's the quietest the dread has ever been and the loudest I've ever heard it.
