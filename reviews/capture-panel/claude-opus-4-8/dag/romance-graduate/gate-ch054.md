REACTION

Okay so this is the fourth person in a row I've been alone in a cold room with over this break, and I clocked that — 51 was Randi and her billionaire, 52 was Vee in her childhood twin bed, 53 was Sheri at the diner, and now Pace hungover on his back with his tongue stuck to the roof of his mouth. The book scattered everybody to their corners after she walked out, and I feel the apart-interlude in my bones; I've read this shape a hundred times and I know the term starting is going to snap them back together. By a fourth solo chapter I'd normally be skimming for the next time two of them are in a room. I was not skimming this one. But I noticed the want starting.

Because here's what this chapter is: it's the book handing me the engine. The reason for the folded arms. And it's Daphne — the tutor, the first everything, who left and "consumed his life," so he taught himself that the words were the difference, kept them in his mouth with every woman after and loss stayed survivable. "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." There it is, the whole withholding explained from the inside.

And I have two feelings about it at once and I'm not going to pretend I don't. One: that litany — *tutor, then best friend, then first girlfriend, first lover, first love, then she left* — is the single most off-the-rack wounded-alpha backstory in the genre, the exact thing I came to this book to get away from, and for about four sentences I felt the familiar boredom rise. Two: the book is smarter than that, and I think it's smart on purpose. Because this is Pace narrating Pace to himself, and his story of himself leaves out the entire thing I already know — that he chose Vee off a lawn with Randi, composed the painting before she existed, read Randi's pulse the whole walk. None of that is in here. He gets to be a man with one clean old wound who's simply scared, and the engineering is just… gone from his own account. So I don't read this as the truth. I read it as what he tells himself so he gets to keep being the tender one. That's the only way this backstory earns its place with me, and I think that IS the way it's built.

What got me, actually, under the suspicion: the engine on the highway. His whole body gathering toward the cut in the hill, gravel, a truck turning in — and it goes past. And then he bends over the bowl of prison-food chicken and sobs until he can't hear it anymore. That's the real thing. The man got blackout drunk, there's a mop and a crust of bleach foam outside the bathroom, he threw up his control over the break. I believe the grief even while I distrust the story he's draping over it. "He ate anyway." Of course he did. He's a man who tells his own body what to do and it obeys.

No heat here, none wanted. This is an elegy. The barefoot-in-his-shirt memory is the only warm thing and it's warm the way a photo of a dead summer is warm.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *okay the book FINALLY cracked him open and it's a sob over a bowl of microwave chicken and I'm moved AND I don't trust a word of it because he conveniently forgot the part where he PLANNED her. four chapters of everyone crying alone in the snow though, I need the dorms to reopen.*
