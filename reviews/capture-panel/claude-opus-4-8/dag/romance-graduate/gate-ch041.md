REACTION

So here it is. The photo. I've been circling this thing since chapter 37 and now I watched it actually leave her phone and cross the bar to Delivered, and my stomach did the thing.

And God help me, the chapter *earned* the dread by being good. The open is him rolling all the way across her, cheek on the lower slope of her breast, the little kisses she narrates for him — "they're not about sex for him" — and then the pale pink drawing tight and bright under his mouth "with no say from her" and ruining her own theory. That's the register I've been starving for, the depth-and-charge in the same minute, and the pool and Cassie didn't give me that. This gave it back. I believed every second of her lying there thinking *he's all mine.* That's the trap talking, I know it's the trap talking, but it's warm and it's exact and that's exactly why it works.

But. *But.* The whole architecture of this scene is him taking the photo out of her hands. She offers — "would a picture help," nail tapped three times on her tooth, her idea, her nerve — and the second she tries to arrange herself he says *no, you'll have to wait,* and does it himself. Sets her elbow. Draws her hair. Lays her hand on her hip. Carries one knee ahead of the other. Kisses each place after he's placed it. He *poses* her, limb by limb, like he did in 37, like a woman in an old calendar, and then he even shoots it on *her* phone "because hers took the better picture." He crops the lamp glare, turns it long, "that's the one." He made the image. He directed the image. She thinks it's the thing she pulled out of herself and it's him, down to the tilt of the shade. The jacket told me on page one: she believes she pulled out the chair herself.

And "I'd never share your photograph" — I flagged that line in 37 as *share with whom, Pace,* and here it comes again, verbatim, as the condition she extracts: "only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." "Never. I'd never share your photograph." The repetition isn't lazy, it's the point — it's a line he already had ready. Something now exists in the world that can be shown. The lever is built. She even has "the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this" and then calls believing him "the brave part" and sends it before the nerve can leave her. That's the knife. The book let her see it and sent it anyway, and dressed the sending as courage. I wanted to reach into the page.

Then the kitchen, and I'll be honest: by the time he was grating the lemons into "a fine bright rain" I was a little restless. He cooks with intent, she tastes each thing apart on her tongue, "she used to just eat, now she tastes, and she knew exactly whose doing that was" — yes, I know, the book keeps telling me he's remade her palate the way he's remade everything, and it's the same note I've had three times now. Lovely sentences. I've eaten this meal.

What woke me back up was Thanksgiving coming around *again* — "I don't go back," word for word off the jacket and off my notes, the ease going out of his face, "level and exact," the door with no handle. And then Sheri, the pie she'll pretend she made, "she's alone." The cell assembling, exactly where Sheri said she'd be. And Vee's little flicker — another woman at his table on a day meant for family — flaring and going out as fast as it came. She put her hand on the parents thing and took it off. I keep waiting for her to leave a hand on something.

The scar. Still owed. He did the careful-voice withholding again — "I don't go back," full stop, no door — and that's the *same* deferral in a new coat. I'm past six now. If the scar ever rings it had better detonate, because the tease has officially stopped buying him anything with me.
