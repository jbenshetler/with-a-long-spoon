REACTION

Okay so this one's a swerve and I clocked it immediately — no Pace, no Randi's cold citrus, no bench. Just chlorine and two girls and a navy suit that "had a job to do." And I did not resent it, which surprises me, because normally a heat-free chapter this far in is where my thumb starts drifting to the home screen.

What got me: the water. That paragraph where the room switches off and it's just green and cold and her own heartbeat and "something in her chest that had stood at attention all afternoon quietly sat down" — God. That's the whole book's thesis smuggled into a swimming pool. This girl has spent every waking minute being *looked at* or bracing to be looked at, and the one place nobody can see her is the one place she's ever felt like a person. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." I actually put my phone down for a second. That line does more for why Vee is catchable than any of the froyo courtship did.

And Cassie. Cassie is the only person in this entire book who wants nothing from Vee, and the book KNOWS it's telling me that, and it terrifies me. The splash trick — "outrage out of all proportion to the actual amount of water" — she reads Vee coming up with "her face on wrong" and instead of asking (asking is for people who want the sad version to get bigger, which, ouch, true) she just *shoots water at her*. That's love with no agenda. Which is exactly why I'm bracing. Remember Chapter 4? Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word. This book planted that and I have not forgotten it. Cassie sees Pace and Randi clearly and Cassie is going to lose, and this chapter is making me care about the one person positioned to get shut out of Vee's brighter life. That's not filler. That's the book loading the gun it's going to fire.

The bleacher boys — "You won't need floaties with those" — and Vee putting her face on to walk fifty yards of it, the game face breaking only when Cassie mutters "they will never know the love of a woman." I felt that laugh in my own chest, the held-breath-going-out-at-once one.

What I'm missing, honestly: heat. This was tender and true but it's a closed-door chapter in a book that sold me on open doors. Two of these in a row and I'd twitch. But it earned itself as a breather, and it's doing structural work — it's showing me what Vee is when she's not being harvested, so I'll feel it when she gets harvested. I trust this writer's cruelty. I want to see the froyo turn into the bench.
