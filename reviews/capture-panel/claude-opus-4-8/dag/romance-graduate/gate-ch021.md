REACTION

Okay. So this is the payoff chapter for the dress, and I came in braced — because a dress-reveal in a lesser book is exactly where the machine collapses into pure swoon and I lose the thread I've been holding for twenty chapters. It didn't collapse. That's the first thing. The narration still knows what he did. "He'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That's the whole con of him in one sentence, said out loud, in the middle of her crying happy over it — the seam is still visible to ME even while it's invisible to her. That's the trick this book keeps pulling and I keep falling for and being glad I fell.

And the thing that actually got me — got past my guard the way it got past hers — is the lining. "A soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away." He built a dress that opens her completely, gives her whole leg to a room, sets her chest out frank, and then quietly armored the two places he decided the room doesn't get. That is unbearably intimate and it is also control dressed as tenderness and the book KNOWS it's both, that's why it lands. He decides what she gives away. She experiences it as being protected. Same wire. No seam. God.

The underwear moment is the best beat in the chapter, maybe the best solo beat she's had. "Her first thought was to keep her steps small, so it wouldn't show. The second came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed." She takes the edge away herself — strips bare on purpose to be looked at, and the shame crosses into wanting before it's even finished being shame. That's her whole arc in one gesture, and crucially she's ALONE. He's in the other room. Nobody asked. She did it to the mirror. The book has been so careful to keep every yes hers, and here's the yes she gives when literally no one's in the room to receive it. That's the scariest, truest thing in the chapter, because it means the surrender has gone internal. She doesn't need him present anymore to perform it. It's hers now.

And then — okay, I did not expect this chapter to be FUNNY. The whole slapstick relay of trying not to come on the dress, "there's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," him twitching, her laughing so hard her leg slides off his hip. I've read ten thousand standing-sex scenes and they are always this breathless self-serious cathedral thing, and this one is two people cracking up and fumbling and it's HOTTER for it, because it's real bodies with a real logistical problem and real affection. He twitches at nothing said at all, just the memory of the joke. That's a man actually present, not a brooding delivery system. The finger-cleaning — "he studied them with the care he'd give a hem" and then took them in his mouth — that's the exact same courtly-precision-turned-filthy move and it still works on me, still went through my knees like it went through hers. The heat is real. Both. Finally a book that gives me both and doesn't make me choose.

But. The daydream at the end is where the dread came back cold. "She put the dress on, in her head, and walked into the induction." There it is — the russet/burgundy dress arriving at the induction, the thing I've been waiting for, and it's arriving as her fantasy, her wanting it, her choosing to make an entrance in the thing he built so the room turns and "the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." What she was for. She's building her own induction now. He didn't push her toward that room; she's walking herself in, in his dress, imagining being looked at as her purpose. That's the plan completing itself through her own desire, exactly as the jacket promised — every step hers. And Randi's not even in this chapter and I felt her in that last paragraph like a cold spot. This is what the lunches were FOR. She's ready. She wants it. Nobody's making her.

What I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: I'm happy for her and it's making me sick, which is the only response this book will accept from me and it earns it every time. He was up all night finishing it, went down hard because he'd been awake building the trap, and she watches him sleep tender and grateful, "the only one awake in the warm house," "getting away with something." She thinks SHE'S the one getting away with something. She has no idea whose house is warm on purpose.

Still no Pace-and-Randi in a room. Twenty-one chapters. The dress arrived, the induction's on the horizon, her body's answering the mirror with nobody in it — every thread I'm carrying tightened this chapter except the one I want most. I'm not leaving before that room.
