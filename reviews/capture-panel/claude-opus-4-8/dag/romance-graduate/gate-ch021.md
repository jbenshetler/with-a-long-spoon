Okay. So I've been standing at the door of this book for twenty chapters holding a grudge — *show me the gears, show me Pace and Randi in a room* — and this chapter just... didn't. And I don't even care right now, which tells me something about what it did instead.

This is the dress. The thing I've been carrying since the hike ("that sounds like a beautiful dress" and he let it lie) and the fitting. And Helen Rivers understood the assignment: the payoff isn't the induction, it's *this*, a private unveiling in the sun porch with the screw heads still bright on a mirror he hung so she could watch herself become someone. The detail that got me exactly where it got Vee — that he "took the further trouble to hide the trouble." The seamlessness. That's the whole con in one image and I know it's the con and it still opened the place the tears come from, same as her. The dress that arrives looking store-finished from somewhere with a name is *Pace*: all the labor buried so the only thing you see is the effortless gift. I clocked it and it still worked and I'm furious in the good way.

And then — thank god — the sex was FUNNY. Do you know how rare that is? How many hundreds of reverent grinding scenes I've read where nobody laughs? The whole "covered in a lot of me" bit, him twitching in her hands at the phrase, her laughing so hard her leg slides off his hip and he has to catch her — the *relay* of trying not to ruin the dress while both of them are past stopping. I was grinning like an idiot. The two dry stripes down his flanks because it's the only thing in the room she's allowed to touch, and then "are they clean enough" and him taking her fingers into his mouth with the care he'd give a hem — that's the hottest thing in the chapter and it's also a *joke landing*, heat and play in the same gesture. That's the both-at-once I came here starving for and can't find anywhere else.

What actually moved me, though, wasn't even the heat. It was her taking the underwear off. First thought: small steps so it won't show. Second thought, hot behind it: doesn't care what shows — and she strips herself bare *to be looked at, on purpose*, and names it as shame and lets it cross into wanting before it's even finished being shame. That's Vee running the whole length of the thing herself now. Nobody asked. Pace isn't even in the room. That's the terrifying, gorgeous progress of this book: the surrender is becoming *hers*, self-generated, and the daydream at the end — walking into the induction late on purpose, letting the room turn, the looking not costing her a thing — "it just felt like what she was for." God. *What she was for.* That line is a knife. Because I know who decided what she was for, and it wasn't her, and she's never felt more like herself than while stepping into the shape they cut.

The quiet gut-punch: she watches him sleep, knows without being told he was up all night finishing it, "the only one awake in the warm house," "getting away with something." She thinks *she's* the one getting away with something. That inversion is the book's whole cruelty and it's doing it with a feather.

What I'm suspicious of / still owed: no Randi this chapter, no gears, and I'm officially past impatient into a kind of dread — I think the book is *saving* the room-with-the-plan for when it'll do maximum damage, and I've decided to trust that instead of resent it, for one more stretch. The lined bodice "over the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" — a soft wall built into exactly where she's most exposed. He protects her and displays her in the same seam. That's him entire.
