REACTION

Oh, this is the morning-after I didn't know I needed, and it's a quiet one, and it landed harder than half the hot chapters have. After twenty chapters of being handed over, measured, extracted, this is just Vee in a cold dorm room with cereal-breath Cassie and it's the most frightened I've been for her in a while — precisely because nothing happens except a zipper going back up.

The toenails. God. That's the detail that does it — Cassie clocking the frosted plum in October and firing the spoon like a prosecutor, and Vee letting the heat climb her neck, and the word her mother would have had and *finding she doesn't mind it this morning*. The shame isn't gone, she's just happy enough to outrun it for one day. That's new. That's the thing the book's been building the whole time — the shame and the heat on the same wire — and here it is reading, for once, like joy.

But the real gut-punch is the ladle. "She lifted it out clean as you go, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole." That's Vee learning to do to Cassie exactly what Randi does to Vee. She's editing. She's keeping the middle. She hands Cassie "he's sweet, he's a good kisser, the old easy words" — the same hollowed-out coins, and she *tells herself it's a kindness* and then the narrator twists the knife: "She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." She's becoming a keeper of secrets. She's growing the exact muscle the game needs her to have. Nobody taught her in this chapter. She did it herself, over cereal, grinning. That's the horror and it's so soft you could miss it.

And the zipper. She goes to shower, reaches for the skirt, and stops — because there's nothing underneath. The green is *gone*, traded, folded in thirds in his palm, and the next thing off her body wouldn't be a story she could edit, it'd just be the bare fact on the floor with nothing left to say. So she zips back up in front of her best friend. She can't be naked in front of Cassie anymore. The trade reached all the way back into this room. I felt that in my stomach.

Cassie — "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend," kind, certain, *wrong*, and then "Don't wait up, lover" and the cleats and she's gone to her game, out the sticking door, and Vee's alone in the emptied cold room and lets the skirt fall at last and "She had never in her life been so happy." That last line is a scalpel. It's true. That's what's unbearable about it.

This is a two-page chapter and I was braced to find out whether that's a technique or a shortcut, and — it's a technique. It's the right length. Any longer and it'd be explaining itself. It knows exactly how much to say and it ladles the rest out clean.

What I'm missing: I still haven't gotten the camera on Randi receiving the trade, and I want it so badly it aches. But this earned its quiet by doing something — it turned Vee into an editor of her own life. I'm not setting it down.
