REACTION

Oh, this one's dangerous, and it's dangerous in the quietest possible way, which is exactly why it worked on me.

Twenty-one was the dress and the sex and the relay-race comedy of trying not to get come on handmade silk — a whole chapter of heat and I loved it, I'd text a friend the "covered in a lot of me" bit at 1 a.m. But twenty-two is the chapter I'll actually lie awake over, and there's barely a hand laid on anybody in it. That's the thing I keep clocking about this book: the hot chapter leaves me sated and the quiet one leaves me scared.

The stats lecture. God. Simpson's paradox — each year on its own the first man's the better hitter, put the two years together and it flips, *same numbers, nobody touched one of them.* And the author just lets it sit there behind the professor's cereal-box voice while Vee's being adored from both sides, and does NOT turn around and explain it to me. That's the whole book diagrammed on a chalkboard and she trusts me to carry it out of the room myself. Every single yes in isolation is a yes. Pool them and the picture's something nobody consented to. I could kiss her for not footnoting it. (I was braced for the explain-the-subtext move — my hair-trigger — and it never came. The red dot settled on the number and he said "nobody touched one of them" and let them go. Clean.)

But here's what actually got in under my ribs, and it's the opposite of what I expected from my own notes. I came into this chapter wanting the scalpel. I wanted Randi's designed tenderness turned on Vee, the harvest, the spider. And Randi DID do the thumb-across-the-toenail thing in the boutique — the frosted plum chosen for his hands and Randi found it anyway and the floor tipped and Vee's hand shot to her shoulder — and that's the move, that's tradecraft, I saw it. And yet. The thing that scared me more than the toe was the kneeling. Randi down on one knee on the pale floor in her own beautiful clothes, "I've got her," waving the shopgirl off, doing the little ankle buckle with two fingers. Because I can't tell anymore — and I don't think *Randi* can tell — whether that's the method or whether she just can't stop herself. The chapter before, Pace stopped being readable to me as pure operator when he saved the bowl. This chapter Randi stops being readable as pure operator when she goes down on the floor. The machine's supposed to be running Vee. It keeps catching on real feeling and I think it's going to tear.

The money. Oh, the money did me in, and it did it so quietly. Vee putting the debit card back — the card that only spends what's already behind it — and taking out the emergency credit card and signing for the most she's ever handed across a counter that wasn't tuition or rent, *knowing there's nothing under it, knowing it'll be sitting there next month,* and not letting one flicker of it reach her face because the one thing she won't do in that room is flinch. And right beside her Randi buys loafers for a multiple of that without looking at the number, because every day of her life is the occasion, and wears them out of the store like you simply trade shoes mid-afternoon. The book put those two signatures side by side on the same counter and said nothing. Vee is paying — again, literally, with money she doesn't have this time — for the privilege of being spent. The wine that cost more than her meal has become a credit card with nothing under it. And she's never been happier.

"He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." That landed on me the way it landed on Vee. Because it's true and it's the most seeing thing anyone's said about the dress AND it's Randi, of all people, naming out loud the exact thing the tears were about — Randi, who knows precisely how he builds a thing onto a woman so she reads it as freedom, because she's the one who pointed him at this woman. The accuracy is the horror. She reads the charmeuse and the weight and the cut in two seconds, "a thing you're going to have to account for," and then she reads *Vee* just as fast and tells her what she is, and Vee cries, and I'm sitting here going: that's the apple slices. That's the lowering-the-cost-of-telling. Except Randi's hand stayed on the shoulder seam and she went somewhere behind her own face and the breath ran long, and Vee read it as jealousy and wanted that FOR her — "nobody who had ever sat up a night over anything of hers" — and the terrible thing is Vee's probably right. It IS jealousy. Vee's generosity keeps being accurate and it keeps being aimed at the person harvesting her.

And Cassie. The whole middle of the lecture is Cassie not going down into the numbers at all, turned those few degrees in her seat, watching what the dress did to her friend because the friend is the only part of any of it she's ever been able to see. "Was it a good night?" and Vee's "Oh, yes" with more in it than the word carries, and something eases in Cassie's face and she picks the highlighter back up. That's the one clean exchange in the chapter and it's the one that'll break my heart later, because Cassie's reading the glow and trusting it, and she can't see the two-year table, nobody's shown Cassie the pooled numbers. The eyebrow that stays in reserve. Cassie never once caring what's on anyone's feet while the entire rest of the chapter is about what the shoes cost. She went the other way to a lab and Randi walked Vee home, and I felt the custody shift in that like a cold spot.

The dress still shown to not one living soul at the end of the lecture — "zipped in its bag, still shown to not one living soul" — and then she puts it on for Randi in the dorm instead of just holding it up on the hanger, because the wanting-to-be-seen got there before the plan. The first person she wears it for isn't the induction, isn't Cassie, isn't even really Pace again. It's Randi. And Randi makes a ceremony of it. The custody shift again.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: "she bought shoes she can't pay for to stand next to a dress she can't pay for in a room full of girls in the one navy dress they own and she's going to walk in LATE on purpose and I am going to be sick." I'm not reading toward the couple. I haven't been since my notes. I'm reading toward the collapse and this chapter loaded three more rounds into it — the credit card with nothing under it, the dress worn first for the wrong person, and the induction daydream now fully armed with the shoes to pull it off. The dread's doing exactly what I want dread to do. I want the next chapter. God help me, I want it the way Vee wants the next thing.
