REACTION

Okay so the thing I was waiting for — "the first time the two tables want the same piece of her and she can't crop it for both" — it came, and it came quieter than I braced for, and that's exactly why it gutted me. There's no second table in this chapter. There's no Pace in the room at all. It's just Vee, alone with Randi's knee against hers for one hour, and the whole machine the book's been building collapses down into a single Lifesaver passed between two fingers and a graze on the inside of a wrist. "She held very still, and it went on not being static." That's the line. That's the whole chapter. The book finally stopped letting her charge the heat to anybody — no truck, no county road, no boyfriend's body under her to pour it into and call it his — and the second there's no him for it, there's just *her wanting Randi*, naked, unaccounted for, nowhere to put it. And she can't do a thing about it.

What I noticed, and what gives me chills honestly, is the structural cruelty of it after 71. Chapter 71 was the pressure valve — she drove the county road at midnight specifically to drive this out of her, used Pace like "a wall at my back," and the book told us flat it didn't work, "it didn't go." So 72 is the morning-after of that failed exorcism, and it's worse, because now there's no exorcism available. Monday with nothing in it. The hand on her stomach, "a lid held down on a pot," refusing to go lower not because anyone forbade it but because she knows exactly whose name would arrive. That's the most honest beat about desire I've read in ages — the policing isn't external anymore, she's doing it to herself, and the reason she won't touch herself is that touching herself would make it *true*.

And then the lie. She told Pace "nothing" in 71 — her first lie to him — and I thought that was the big cold thing she'd carry. But 72 shows the lie wasn't really to him. The real tell is the last beat: Cassie asks the plainest question there is, "What was that with you and Randi," and Vee has *nothing*. Not a dodge, not a crop — she literally doesn't have an answer because she doesn't know. The girl whose whole identity is that she tells everything, that she never keeps things, is now standing in a doorway unable to narrate herself to her clearest mirror. That's the scariest thing the book's done to her. She's lost the story.

The neck kiss. Oh, I was so ready to roll my eyes at "how do you like it" — it's cute, it's a callback to the thing she got told not to weaponize — but what it did to me was Randi, again, getting to do the real thing under cover of a joke. She's still running it. "I've got to go discipline a bad girl" — Randi can say the hot true thing out loud as long as it's wrapped in a committee groan. She's the same coward in the same costume my notes flagged, and it's working, and Vee turns her face away from the mouth and *that's* when the mouth finds her neck and a sound gets out of her she can't catch. The body keeps telling on her. Every single time.

And "brunch" — she blurts it out of the ringing, "not out of any part of her that was thinking." She turned down two hours across a table because she couldn't hold her face, and then invited herself to Saturday brunch anyway. She can't stay and she can't leave. That's the trap closed.

What I'm turned on by and uneasy about in the same breath: the Lifesaver/wrist thing is genuinely a 2-heat moment done with almost nothing, and I respect that enormously — a book that can make a cough drop erotic doesn't need the truck. But I clocked that this is now the *third* chapter in a row running the exact same engine: Vee wants Randi, Vee can't name it, Vee's body betrays the not-naming. 71 did it through sex, 72 did it clothed. It's gorgeous and it's also starting to feel like it's circling the drain of a single realization. I need the drain to actually open. Marsh's lecture — reciprocity, "a gift is a debt you lay on someone whether they wanted it or not" — the book handing me its own thesis about Randi and Pace choosing her, and I half-want to tell it I already *have* the frame, stop narrating the subtext at me. That's the one place it nudged my DNF trigger.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "she can't even lie to CASSIE anymore. she's got NOTHING. the whole book she's been the girl who tells everything and now she opens her mouth and there's no story in it." And then: "also the neck kiss. also I need Pace back in frame because him being absent this chapter is the loudest he's been."
