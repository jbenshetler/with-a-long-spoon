REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter I've been circling the whole book — the house off six-fourteen, finally, the one I wrote down that I wanted to *see* — and it delivered it the way you deliver something to someone who's been waiting: slowly, room by room, no rush. And I have to be honest about what it did to me, because it worked, and I'm a little furious that it worked.

The thing is, I came in armored. I know Randi picked her. I know the bench. I know that every courtly beat he runs on Vee he ran on Randi first, course by course. So I walked into this house with my arms crossed, waiting to catch the choreography. And the house kept *refusing* to be choreography. The shoes paired on the closet floor — one right pair for each thing. The kitchen that actually cooks. The patent that's about getting a scared kid out of the MRI tube faster. The joints with no screws in them. That's the part that got under my skin: none of it is performed *at* her. He stands in the workshop doorway and lets her look and doesn't walk her in. He doesn't explain the Sanskrit down to her size. The interiority here is real — his, and hers watching his — and that's the thing I'm starving for and almost never get, and the book knows it, and it's feeding me on purpose, and I know it's on purpose, and I ate anyway.

The kiss reversal at the door. *He* stopped it. "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs." That's the line that made me sit up, because that's the hook, isn't it — that's the exact mechanism the jacket promised, the ask-before-he-takes, the leaving-her-the-room — and watching Vee's body register it as *paying attention* while her want just hangs there lit with nowhere to go, that's the most honest thing about desire in a long time. The closed bedroom door. She wore the satin on purpose, she leaned, she waited for his hand to go to the handle, and it didn't. Something in her dropped. God, that drop. He withholds the one room and she's the one left wanting through the door — that's the right-hand-rule, the last inch left for her to close, and it's being used on her now and I can feel the trap of it even as I feel the pull of it.

And then the dress. The *dress.* I'll be straight: I felt my own eyes go hot right along with hers, and I resent it a little. Because I was there on the rock when she said the russet thing and let it go inside the same breath, the way you set a thing back on the shelf and keep walking — my notes even have the honor-society induction in them — and he kept it. He didn't match the dress from the magazine, he matched *her.* The burgundy with purple under it, mixed for her coloring. That's devastating. That's the single most romantic beat in the whole book so far and I'd be lying to say otherwise.

But here's what keeps me from just melting into it, and it's exactly why this book is good: "the way you walk past a wrapped box on the counter without asking who it's for." The too-warm house — she files it and lets it go, and I *don't* let it go, because I know who keeps that house warm, I know whose car crunches up that gravel with the deadbolt going over first. And "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it'll fall." He needs to see her. The gift requires her to be looked at. The whole thing resolves, every single time, into *her willingness to be seen* being the only thing between her and the beautiful thing — which is the engine of the entire seduction, the shame turned to heat, and he's just built a two-week, bespoke, silk-covered reason to run it again. The dress is a loom and she just said yes to being measured on it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he MADE the shirt. he made the furniture. he's making her a GOWN because she said one sentence on a mountain and he kept it and I am not okay AND I want to scream at her to look at the thermostat.* That's the sick genius of this — I'm falling for it at the exact rate she is, ahead of her and falling anyway, which is the thing printed on the jacket, and knowing the trick doesn't disarm the trick, which is the only kind of this book worth reading.

One tiny flag: this is the chapter where I started to feel the pattern of him-as-perfect-withholding-man flex toward *too* clean — the man has no bad room except the office-mess that's charming-mess, every reveal lands as another virtue. If chapter 17 is another inventory of his excellences I'll feel the gears. But it put the measuring tape in his hand and her "yes — of course, yes" in the air, and I know what two weeks and *good light* and *what you'd wear under it* are walking toward. I want the next one.
