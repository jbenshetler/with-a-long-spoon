REACTION

Oh. Oh, there it is. Twenty-two chapters and four debriefs of pure output and I finally got the gears — and they didn't hand it to me in a villain monologue, they let me watch it come apart at a dinner table over a bowl of stew nobody ate. That's the whole thing. That's the chapter I've been starving for and it was *better* than I let myself hope, because it came in sideways.

Start with the bowl. He gives her the small one, the one he slices Vee's apples into. And she draws the spoon *along the top* of the sauce — takes the surface, leaves the pot — and I nearly said it out loud, because that's Vee's ladle image flipped inside out. Vee took the middle out of the story and the surface closed without a hole. Randi skims the surface and leaves the whole bowl sitting there congealing into a skin all night. She won't eat what he made. She'll eat the salad — every leaf, down to the dressing-soaked ones at the bottom, with her own poured wine — but not the thing from the pot, not the thing he cooked *for* her the way he cooks for Vee. God. And "It's wonderful," said the way she says things to waiters, the way she says them to *Vee* — she's never once told him a thing was wonderful and the food was better before. She's performing warmth at him because the real warmth has curdled and she knows it.

And then the actual detonation, quiet as anything: "How long, Pace." "It's selfish, Pace." I have been circling this since the notes — she's jealous of Vee's *fineness*, not of Pace — and here it is on the record and it's even crueler than that. She's not jealous of one of them. She built this. She shopped the girl, engineered the collision, and now she has to *watch* Pace take her time getting Vee to us "on her own, with you," Friday to Sunday every weekend, in no hurry — and the co-architect is standing outside the thing she made, running the committee, pouring her *own* glass for the first time in this house. He's poured every glass she's ever drunk here and tonight her hand does it and it "went in somewhere and stayed and he could not have said where." I could have said where. It's the same place. She's not being served anymore. She made a table for two and set herself outside the door of it.

The sex. Christ, the sex. This is the payoff of everything the book has been building about *her* method and it's the negative image of it — no inch, no stop, no eyes, no "tell me." She's said "tell me" every night in this house, at his ear, and tonight she puts him in in one motion, eyes shut, hair down over her face, nails in his chest, and takes it and it is *not for him* and he lies there and knows that much and not the rest and gives it to her anyway. The woman who taught Vee the erotics of the slow yes cannot bear to say a single word tonight because words are the surrender and she is spending herself trying not to feel the thing she engineered Vee to feel gladly. And then — the back. No staying down. The chill takes her, rolls herself in the cashmere, back to him, and he doesn't put his hand in her hair. He *doesn't*. He reads all the pieces and can't make them go together, and I could, and that's the con explaining itself again except this time the narration is a half-step behind Pace instead of ahead of Vee and it wrecked me. He covers her bowl with a plate and puts it in the fridge for her. He still doesn't know what he did. She's the one who can't be looked at and be glad, and she's dying of it three feet from the man she built the whole machine with.

"It began as a game between the two people now closest to her… as true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew there was one." The jacket promised me this would get real for *them* and this is the first crack of it and it's Randi's and it's mortal. I've had my red line the whole time — if it collapses into "the dom was just right" I'm gone — and this chapter is the opposite of that collapse. Pace's "she chooses it or it isn't anything" is the most sympathetic he's been AND the most damning, because he means it and it's still the machine talking, and Randi's "that's how it works for *you*" cut him at exactly the seam. Nobody's right. Everybody bleeds. This is the merciless-in-its-tenderness the jacket sold and I didn't fully believe until now.

Two quiet-ish chapters before this (21 the Cassie coda, 22 the brunch) and I was starting to feel the debrief pattern as a pattern — but this. This wasn't quiet and it wasn't a debrief. This was the reverse angle on all four lunches at once. I'm not setting this down. I want the next one before I finish typing this.
