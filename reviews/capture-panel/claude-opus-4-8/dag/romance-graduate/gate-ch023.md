REACTION

Okay so here's the thing — I came into this chapter braced. The dress got made in 21, it got worn in 21, the daydream of walking into the induction already happened in 21, and 22 was the debrief. So 23 is *shoe shopping.* On paper that's a nothing chapter, the kind of connective-tissue errand a lesser book pads with. And instead Helen Rivers used the errand to run the entire con in miniature, in daylight, with a receipt at the end. I'm a little furious at how good it is.

The move that got me: the dorm. Randi walks in and the room *reorganizes itself around her* — the Stevie Nicks poster Vee stopped seeing years ago suddenly visible again, smaller. That's exactly what Randi does to Vee's whole life. She makes the ordinary look shabby by standing next to it so the girl will reach for the other thing. And then — *"He didn't make this for you. He made it to you."* I actually put the book down. Because that's the same word my own notes have been circling — the dress built *onto* her while she held still — and Randi reached in and handed her the language for the most intimate thing that's happened to her. That's the machine at its purest: not taking, *naming*. Giving Vee the word so the word becomes Randi's. Vee's throat does "the thing again" and it's Randi who made it do that, not Pace. She's teaching Vee to feel her own life through Randi's vocabulary.

The shoe store slapstick — I clocked exactly what it was doing and it still worked on me, which is the horror. The yeti canoe shoe, the pink rhinestone platforms, "do yetis shop here," the flat that's one brisk walk from becoming a sandal. This is the tickle-scarf story in a new dress: lower the guard with play, get her laughing till her legs quit, then extract. And the extraction is *"That's me. That's me exactly. Same size, same everything."* Size seven. "It's practically a marriage." She literally frames sharing shoes as marriage and Vee has "nowhere on earth to stand." The whole silly bit was built to arrive at *we are the same body.* That's not clumsy foreshadowing, that's the seduction wearing a clown nose so Vee never sees the knife.

Then the toe. THEN THE TOE. Randi on one knee on the pale floor — kneeling, which is Pace's whole posture, the fitting, the going-down — holding Vee's bare foot in both hands, the frosted plum polish chosen to catch the light like the silk, and the single thumb across the big-toe nail, and Vee's floor *tips,* her hand shoots to Randi's shoulder to stop the room swinging. "Mm." That's it. That's the woman-wanting the book keeps opening a door on — except this time it didn't close soft. It landed in her body, undeniable, and Randi *looked up and smiled and said nothing at all* and went back to the box, which is Pace's exact "you don't have to tell me" — the choosing-not-to-take that's the most effective take there is. My notes have been begging for the Randi-heat to break for ten chapters and here it is, disguised as a shoe fitting, and it's hotter than the actual sex in 21. I mean that. The thumb on the toenail did more to me than the slit-up-to-the-hip.

And the receipt. God, the receipt. Vee puts the debit card back — "the card she paid her own way with, the one that only ever spent what was already behind it" — and takes out the credit card with nothing under it, knowing it'll be sitting there next month, and signs and *smiles and lets none of it reach her face because the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch.* That's the chapter's whole thesis in one gesture. Randi engineered a room where Vee's actual life — her account, her limits, her honest tan sensible shoe that "goes with everything" — becomes the thing she has to hide to belong. "A dress a man made *to* you doesn't want to go with everything." They killed the sensible shoe. They killed the debit card. Every yes freely given, and she's now literally in debt for one. The jacket's "nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — she handed over her own solvency and felt *chosen* doing it.

The book still knows more than Vee does — that's the only reason I'm not out. The narration never once lets the toe-thumb read as swoon; it's "a small swing of the room," it's her hand shooting out to *stop* it. It's watching her get run. And the last line — "she had never once in her life had a friend like this" — landing right after Randi casually spent a multiple of Vee's white-knuckle emergency purchase without glancing down, is the cruelest beat in the chapter. The asymmetry is the whole thing. One of them is playing with money; the other one just went into debt to keep up, and calls it friendship.

What I'm still not getting, and it's the thing I've asked for in every note: Pace and Randi in a room, on the record. Twenty-three chapters. The two of them still never touch onstage. But — and this is why I'm continuing hungry instead of continuing annoyed — 23 finally gave me Randi's hand on Vee's body doing what Pace's does, in daylight, deliberate, and the parallel is now so exact (kneeling, the light-catching detail chosen in advance, the not-taking) that the book is basically confessing they're running the *same play from the same playbook.* That's the seam, almost. I can nearly see it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "she put the debit card BACK. and the toe thing. I need you to read to chapter 23 so I can scream about the toe thing."
