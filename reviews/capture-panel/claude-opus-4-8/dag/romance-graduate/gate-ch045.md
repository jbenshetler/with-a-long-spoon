REACTION

Oh, this is the one. Forty-seven chapters locked behind Vee's eyes and then Helen Rivers just *opens the side of the house* and lets me walk straight into Randi's head on the last night of the year, and I didn't even resent losing Vee for a chapter — I'd been starving for exactly this and didn't fully know it until it was in front of me.

What floored me is the *shape* of it. This whole chapter is the fairytale Vee has been crying for — three chapters straight of Vee asking *what are we, say the word, give me the name, let me be seen in public* — and here's Randi handed every single thing Vee can't have, gift-wrapped. A man who *names* it (wife, three kids, before they're off the lift). A man who *leads* — and the book is precise and merciless about it: John leads like a cotillion, firm, unmistakable, "never a half-beat where she had to guess," and Pace's lead is "the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for." And Randi, tired, thinks *easier is better. I'm done with the work.* A man who *assumes* — walks her up the hotel steps without asking, and she *likes not being asked*, because "Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." Every single thing Vee begged Pace for on that cold mat, Randi gets, effortlessly, from the perfect man — and it means *nothing*. "A good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." I actually put the book down for a second at that line.

And the cold. God, the cold. "She was warm only where she touched him... except the cold did not belong to the room. It was hers. She had brought it in with her and she would carry it back out." That's the whole book turned inside out — every one of Vee's chapters is Pace bringing the heat up *hours* before she arrives so she's never cold under his roof, and here's Randi in a five-star fairytale radiating cold from her own chest outward. The lipstick ring left on a sleeping stranger. "Nothing had happened to her eyes." I'm wrecked.

But here's what the chapter actually *did* to me — it resolved the thing my notes have been circling for forty chapters without me being able to name it, and it resolved it toward *more* heartbreak, not less. Randi loves Vee. Flatly, really. "She had never once before said that to anyone without meaning it as currency... With Vee there was neither." The architect, the woman who runs every scenario to the end, who built this entire trap — she hits the ONE scenario she cannot run: "she could not picture Vee's face while she said it." And her decision — *don't tell her, let the word keep its cover, let Vee arrive at it on her own, keep the brunches exactly as they are* — that's the machine still running, yes, but I can see now it's not strategy anymore. It's cowardice. "She had never had to be brave... She had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be." That's the saddest sentence in two books. She'll spend Vee's ignorance for the same reason Pace does — because telling the truth costs her the thing she can't afford to lose.

And the parents. That cold breakfast — the mother all "edges and points," the invented boys at the table because "the invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been," the father saying keep an eye out for an eligible bachelor and her laughing brittle. Now I *understand* her. The whole performed-perfection machine she runs on Vee is the exact machine she was built by. She's not a predator who learned a technique; she's a girl who was taught the only safe thing is the beautiful lie, and she's doing to Vee what was done to her, and she *knows* it, and she can't stop.

The thing I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: John is the *approved* life, the one at the good end of her mother's table, and she lay down in the middle of it and felt nothing, and "the flaw was hers, carried into the room the same as the cold." She knows Pace and Vee are the real thing. She knows it in her body. And she's going to keep lying to the one of them she loves because the truth means being brave for the first time in her life. I don't know who breaks first anymore but I know this chapter just lit the fuse under *Randi*, exactly where my notes said it would come from.

I'd text my friend: *the villain took herself on a perfect date with a perfect man to prove to herself she's broken and oh my god I love her now.*
