REACTION

Okay. So this is the two-pager I've been wondering about — and it's not a shortcut. God, it's not a shortcut. It's the whole book pressed down into one gesture, and the gesture is: she takes the shirt off.

I've been carrying that flannel since the hike. The no-tag one, the one she slept in every night she wasn't there, the one she *washed and gave back to him to re-scent* three weeks ago — "smelling like you / smelling like me" — and that was one of the tenderest things in the whole book, I had it starred. And now it's back and it's been turned inside out on her. Watch what Rivers does with it: shirt over her own shirt (armor, cover, the thing a girl wears so Cassie sees and doesn't have to say anything). Then she can't feel him through the layer, so she strips down and puts it on bare — and for a while it *works*, it always works, and she can walk down every part of him inside that smell, the sawdust and the salt, the plate he sets down once he's built the hunger. And then the sentence that gutted me: "none of it told her what she was to him." She can inventory everything he *does* and the cloth is still silent at the center. He builds, he keeps rooms warm, he feeds her — and not one thread of it is the word.

And then the turn I actually gasped at, alone on my couch: the warmth in the shirt is *her own, given back.* "He wasn't in it." He's across town, down easy, nothing grinding in him, and she's holding a smell in the dark that promised him close and delivered no one. That's the thesis of the entire book and she found it herself, in a dorm bed, with no Randi to pry it out of her and no Pace to narrate it into something she could live with. For once she's the one who sees.

So she takes it off — and the cold comes down and it's *worse*, and she does not put it back on. She lies there bare in a cold room and stays in it. That's the first no I've seen from her. Not a confrontation, not a text — she couldn't even fill the text field, there's no message that comes back as what she needs, she knows it, she puts the phone facedown. Just: she will not take the comfort anymore because she's finally felt that the comfort is a trick she's running on herself. The shirt in a heap where a dropped thing lands, not folded, not back on the pillow. After weeks of that cloth being a sacrament. That heap is the loudest thing in the chapter.

And Cassie breathing slow and easy below her the whole time — the one person who'd tell her the truth, asleep, two feet away, and Vee up top in the cold choosing not to be comforted. The window goes grey. She doesn't sleep.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she took the shirt off and stayed in the cold on purpose, this is the first time she's refused to let herself be managed, I think she's about to crack open and I'm scared because the one thing that would crack her open is finding out about Randi and that's the thing loading.* Two pages and it did more than most of the climb chapters did. I was braced for a two-pager to feel like a shortcut and instead it's the book holding my face still and making the looking the touch — the exact thing Pace does to her. Rivers used the form on *me*. I'm not setting this down.
