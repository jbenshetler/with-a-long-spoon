Okay, this is the one where he finally becomes a person and not just a beautifully-oiled instrument, and I have complicated feelings about how good it is at that, because I know what he is.

She keeps clocking the flat voice, the good-order voice, the "held the careful way a person carries a full glass so it won't go over the rim" — she's reading his undersell the exact way I read hers. That's two people who both survive by lifting things out of the pot so the surface closes without a hole, sitting under one quilt, recognizing the technique in each other and calling it intimacy. God.

And then Daphne. I did the arithmetic the same second Vee did and it went cold and low in me. Nineteen and fifteen. Two years. A tutor assigned to a fourteen-year-old boy dropped into a dormitory of grown men, and the one soft thing in it was a woman who was, by any clean grown-up word, doing something to a child. And he has filed it under *luck.* "It was the best two years I'd had." He sits there setting each reason in its slot — she was sensible to go, it would've been stranger to stay — and the tidiness IS the wound, and he cannot hear himself defending the person who hurt him. That's the thing I'll be up at 1 a.m. about: he's running on Vee the courtly, patient, all-the-way-here seduction, and the source code is a nineteen-year-old and a fifteen-year-old, and he thinks it's the best thing that ever happened to him.

Where I got wary: the math sermon ran long. The scar deferred *again* — "Not tonight," palm to palm over it. Third time it's been laid out and not opened. It's earning suspense, but I'm putting the book on notice: open it or it becomes a tease I stop trusting.

That gap is the whole engine and it still has torque. No heat here to speak of, a hand on a breast, a hand on a hip, all tenderness — and I didn't want heat in this one. This was the bond chapter and it turned the bond.
