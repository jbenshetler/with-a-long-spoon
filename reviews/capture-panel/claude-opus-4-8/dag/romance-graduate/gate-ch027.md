REACTION

Okay. FINALLY. This is the room I've been begging the book for since chapter — god, since the first debrief. Pace and Randi, on the record, in bed after, talking about Vee like she's the shared project she is. Four chapters of output and no gears, and now here are the gears, and the book did something smarter than I braced for: it didn't give me the planning scene. There's no scheming here. There's the opposite. There's the two architects discovering, out loud, in the dark, that the thing they built has gotten loose from them. That's the "as real for the two who started it" line off the jacket detonating, and it detonated exactly where the blurb promised and I still felt it go off.

The tell that gutted me: Randi eating. She's the woman who reads the left side of the menu, who orders a mimosa without looking, who drew ONE line through the top of Pace's stew last chapter and left the bowl to skin over — that was her whole vocabulary of refusal, control worn as appetite-lessness. And tonight she asks him, for the first time ever, "is there anything to eat in this house." She puts on his undershirt. She pulls it down over her knees. She eats the chicken and every stalk of asparagus and the couscous "to a line he could have drawn with a ruler" and he doesn't say so. She's regressing into something undefended in his kitchen and the narration knows it's an event and refuses to underline it, and THAT restraint is what let me feel it. Last week she wouldn't eat. This week she's hungry. Something in her has come off its footing.

And I know why, and so does the chapter, and it does the thing perfectly: it lets Pace kill her without either of them knowing he's doing it. "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp." He's not looking at anything, he's seeing Vee. And behind him the chair makes no sound. Randi's gone still — "not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding." That's her decision in the mirror at thirteen coming back to collect. She decided she never gets to be the lamp, only the one who arranges the light for someone else, and here's the man she started this game with describing the exact glad looking she can't have, aimed at the girl she shopped for him. "Yeah," she says, a beat too long. "She is." I want to put my head down. She is teaching Vee the surrender she can't survive, and now she's watching Vee be loved in the way she rationed herself out of, and she can't get bored of her, and she can't stop wanting her, and the wanting is out loud now — "God. What she keeps under there. It's criminal" — dragging her own body down his chest to show him Vee's. She's cracking straight through the middle. She's turned herself into the third chair at that table she can already picture and she knows it and she reached for him to stop the room swinging.

What I love is that Pace is genuinely tender here and genuinely oblivious to what he's costing her, which keeps him human and keeps the con humming under it. "Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body." "And you're showing her she doesn't have to be." He believes his own project. And he wants to tell Vee — he has the tableau in his head, all three of them at his table smiling, and "he could not, not yet." So the plan IS still running, the reveal is still coming, and now I have the emotional charge under it: it's not going to land as gotcha, it's going to land on Randi hardest.

Heat here was a low simmer and rightly so — this wasn't the chapter's job. The Sade, the undershirt on the kitchen floor, her voice dropping half a register — it's warm but it's grief wearing want, and I read it as grief. What this chapter did was structural and it's the best thing the book's given me in ten chapters because it finally moved the machine I've been circling. My one red line — "the dom was just right" — is nowhere near tripped; if anything Pace is being quietly indicted by his own tenderness. I texted my friend at 1 a.m. already: SHE ATE THE ASPARAGUS. She's gone. The one who was never supposed to fall is gone and the book didn't tell me, it fed me a plate cleaned to a ruler-line and let me find it.

I'm reading on tonight. This is the turn I stayed for.
