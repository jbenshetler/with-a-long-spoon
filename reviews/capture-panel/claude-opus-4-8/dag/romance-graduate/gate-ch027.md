REACTION

Okay so first: the fight didn't get to breathe. That's my main gripe and it's a real one. Chapter 26 was the best thing this book has done in a while — Vee finally acting like a person with a wound instead of a smooth surface, saying the ugly venomous thing, self-sabotaging in a way I recognized in my own gut. And chapter 27 is the machine sanding it flat. She wakes up, decides she was Wrong, goes to Randi, and Randi hands her the verdict — "that's not the disaster, that's the only thing that matters, and you handed it to him for free" — and the thing Vee had been holding clenched "let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so." Because Randi said so. The book KNOWS. It put that line right there. That's the tell, and I clocked it, and I'm grateful it didn't try to hide it. But God, I wanted her to sit in the mess for more than one gray dawn.

And here's what actually kept me: the bartender did the work last chapter, not Randi. The rehabilitation of Pace — "wasn't him they were jealous of, honey" — came from an outside woman with no stake, closing up her bar, done being asked for things. That's the one voice in this book that isn't running an angle, and she's the one who turned Vee around. So by the time Randi gets her hands on it this morning, Randi's not convincing Vee of anything — she's taking a done thing and installing herself as the author of it. "So don't you worry about a single piece of how. We're going to get you ready, and the rest takes care of itself." She's converting Vee's own hard-won turn into a thing that happened *because of Randi*. That's the lockpick and it's the cruelest one yet because it's disguised as the morning-after her best friend held her hand.

The "not today, tomorrow night" thing — I actually sat up. Randi is *scheduling* the reconciliation. Managing the tempo of Vee's apology so it "reads like something you chose." She knows Pace won't text — "he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." Of course she knows. She's the co-architect. She's coaching the mark on how to please the other operator and calling it girl-talk, and she's doing it in a robe with no makeup on, freckles out, having gotten out of bed five minutes ago for this. That undefended morning face is doing a LOT and I don't fully trust it and I also half-believe it and that's exactly the poison I signed up for.

The dress-up scene. Fine. FINE. This is where the book stopped pretending the Randi thread was subtext. Vee stripping to her bra as "nothing — dress-up, the oldest game," and then "Randi looked, and that was the part Vee felt." The shoes going on under the long skirt so Vee "never watch it happen," being *tended to* blind — hand up under the hem to the outside of her thigh, thumb into the hollow behind her knee, thumb high on the back of the thigh. That's a seduction staged as a shoe fitting and it's a straight rhyme with Pace measuring her in good light. They're running the same play on her from both sides. The parallel is the whole point and I felt it land in my stomach. And the closet — bone to camel to oatmeal ending on one oxblood "like the last note of something" — that's a gorgeous line, that's real writing, I'll give it that.

"Shoe whore." "Dinner in Paris." "Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet." Randi is telling her exactly what she is, out loud, as flirtation, and Vee laughs because between them the worst words are endearments. The book is letting Randi confess and Vee mishear it as banter, over and over, and I can't decide if I find that thrilling or if I'm getting impatient for someone to actually *hear* it.

The kiss at the door again, and this time: "she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." That's the Randi-wanting thread I've been waiting to see move in daylight, and it moved. The surrender to Randi is now automatic, un-thought, the way the dance took her once she stopped counting. Same mechanism. Everyone in this book is teaching Vee the same lesson — stop bracing, stop guessing, let the lead arrive — and every single one of them is a person with a plan about her body.

What I'm annoyed by: still no Pace-and-Randi-on-the-record in THIS chapter (26 gave me chapter 24, the bed scene, which scratched it hard — that was the gears, finally, and it was worth waiting for). Chapter 27 is a connective-tissue chapter, a getting-ready chapter, low on heat and mostly moving pieces into place for tomorrow night. It's charming and it's smart and the shoe scene has a real charge, but it's a bridge. If I hadn't gotten chapter 24 recently I might be more restless. But I did, so I know the machine is real and I know it costs Randi something, and this chapter is her spending herself on Vee in broad daylight, freckles and all, and half-meaning it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "she scheduled the apology. she's booking the girl's reconciliation with the other handler like a travel agent and calling it a girls' morning and Vee just RELAXED because mommy said it's okay. also they made out at the door and Vee didn't even flinch this time. I am unwell. keep going."

I'm in. The heat was low this chapter but the writing's real and the con is tightening and I need to see tomorrow night.
