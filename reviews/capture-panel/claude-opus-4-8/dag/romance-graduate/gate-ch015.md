REACTION

Oh, she did it. The whole lunch I was sitting across the table from Vee with my heart in my throat because *I* could see the hook Randi was setting and Vee couldn't, and the book finally — finally — let me watch the extraction happen in real time instead of telling me about it secondhand. This is the chapter my notes have been begging for since chapter 4. "Over long lunches Vee tells Randi everything." I've been taking that on faith for a dozen chapters. Here it is on the page, and it's *worse* than I'd braced for, because it's so gentle.

The craft of it undid me. Watch the moves: Vee has a planned version, the clean one — dress, burgundy, romance — and she means to walk past the measuring. And Randi doesn't pry. She *lowers the cost of telling* one increment at a time. "Nobody measures over a bra. It's just how it's done." That's not curiosity, that's a locksmith. She tells Vee the shameful thing is ordinary so the shame stops guarding the door, and the second it's open she's through it: *were there any challenges?* And Vee, who'd been safe a sentence ago, goes "it was cold in the house" — flat, defensive, already busted — and then the giggle of the cornered and "actually it was really hot." Randi didn't take it. Vee *handed* it over, laughing, relieved, feeling closer than ever. That's the apple all over again. That's exactly the hook from my notes: find where a woman portions herself, remove the portioning, let her feel it as freedom. Randi runs Pace's own play on Pace's intel.

And then — God — the one place she *stops*. "You don't have to tell me." Right at the threshold of the real secret, the thing Vee's never told Cassie, never told anyone, the arousal on the box. Randi closes the door *herself*, softly, and the laugh that always came doesn't come, and the secret's left hanging half-out of Vee with nowhere to land. I had to put the book down for a second. Why does Randi retreat right there? I have two readings and I hate both. Either she can't bear to hear it — the Vee-on-the-pedestal-wet-for-Pace picture is the one thing that actually cuts her, because it's *her* man and it's getting real — or she's a master and she knows you don't take the last thing, you leave it aching so the girl comes back to give it next time. The thumb grinding the nail under the water-glass rim while Vee says "he looked at me and I was fine" — that's the tell. Something went tight behind the smile and Vee, bless her, reaches past "she thinks I'm awful" to "she's moved, she's happy for me," because she needs it to be that. She reads every room but this one.

But here's what flipped the whole book sideways for me and I need to text somebody about it: the car. The kiss. The second press "that had something in it the first one hadn't." The jacket told me Vee never lets herself notice how much she wants Randi too — and I'd filed Randi as pure predator, all tradecraft. Now I don't know. The gentler smile "spent rather than handed around." The tickling story that was *also* a gift — Randi handing Vee the picture of herself tied up and helpless, and the heat it put in Vee "that she had no shelf for." That heat is real and it's mutual and it's the thing nobody at that table can say. The operation and the actual wanting are the same gesture now and I can't pull them apart, which I think is the point and which is the first time this book has genuinely scared me in a way I didn't see the shape of in advance.

What I noticed, cold: Vee paid. The debit card, the number she'll feel Thursday, one glass of Randi's wine costing more than Vee's whole meal — "she was spending her like currency" and now the currency is literal. And the last beat, Vee scanning the dark dorm windows counting who might've seen, heart going — that's chapter 1. That's Randi in the bathroom sealing the real self away. Vee just took her first step onto the exact road, and the book didn't tell me so. It just let me feel the cold thing spread.

Four straight chapters now circling one Saturday, and I should be restless — but 15 and 16 were the fitting, which was molten, and 17 is the debrief that turns the fitting into a weapon, and together they're the engine of the whole book clicking into gear. I'm not skimming for dialogue. I'm reading every line twice.
