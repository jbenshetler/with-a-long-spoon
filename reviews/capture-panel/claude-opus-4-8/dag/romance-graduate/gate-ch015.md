REACTION

Okay. THIS is what I bought the ticket for. Fifteen chapters in and I'd started to wonder if Helen Rivers was going to make me wait until Volume Two for the shame-into-heat the jacket promised, and instead she spends an entire chapter on a man measuring a woman for a dress and it is the filthiest, most patient thing I've read in ages, and not one item of his clothing comes off. I need to sit with that. She got me to hold my breath over a *tape measure*.

What kills me — what actually kills me — is that the mechanism is exactly the jacket's thesis made flesh. "The shame she has carried since girlhood turns, impossibly, into heat." I've read that sentence on a hundred back covers and it's always a lie, it's always just a hot guy and a wet heroine. Here it's *literal choreography*. The wet spot. The satin she chose "with hope," the hope sized to be seen across the room, her shame dressed up in the exact fabric she picked for the occasion and the occasion turning out to be this. That's not a euphemism and it's not clinical either — it's the specific humiliation of a body that won't obey, rendered from the inside, and then it gets *rewarded* instead of punished. He never looks down. The whole time she's dying he's got his face craned up waiting at her eye level for her to open her eyes. God. GOD. When she finally opens them and it's a smile "of a man who had just received an unexpected gift" and her spine unstoops on its own, comes up onto her toes toward "something she had no name for" — I felt that in my sternum. That's the shame turning. On the page. Doing the thing.

And the heat is *real* heat, not fade-to-black and not held at arm's length. The rhythm she names herself — closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil — is edging. He's edging her with a measuring tape and the withdrawal is the point, the ringing where his hands were. "She had thought the measuring would be the exposure. The measuring was only half of it." The inside seam landing like walking in on a pop quiz. His hands on her ass for the first time, flat, learning the whole shape of her and holding once. Her bare breast against his jaw — accident, accident, and then *on purpose*, "Oops. Sorry," not remotely sorry — and him going red, fighting the grin, losing, the single laugh through his nose. I would text a friend at 1 a.m. IN ALL CAPS about him going red. Because that's the other thing this chapter finally gives me: **Pace cracks.** The contained careful man nearly topples. "Keep still," a beat too late, after a silence she watches him spend deciding, the choosing that costs him. He kisses the pinprick before he decides to. For fourteen chapters he's been the instrument that watches everyone else; this is the first time I've seen the needle move on *him*, and she's the one who moved it. "She'd nearly done that. Her." That's the power transfer the jacket keeps promising is hers, and it actually feels earned instead of asserted.

Now. The part of me that took notes. I have not forgotten what I know and Vee doesn't. The heat working on me does not un-know it. This whole tender hour — the pre-heated house so a woman can stand half-bare and not be cold, thought of *hours* before she arrived; the dress conjured from one offhand summit confession she'd already let go of; the riser built into the corner of a woodworker's house that puts her wettest part at his kneeling eye level "as if she'd stepped up for the express purpose" — none of that is spontaneous. That's *set*. That's the machine. He and Randi chose this girl on the quad and he has built a room, an afternoon, a garment, and a thermostat setting around delivering her the single experience she's most defenseless against: being seen at her most ashamed and not found wanting. It's the most loving thing anyone's ever done for her and it's a designed harvest and both are completely true at once, which is the exact vise the book keeps me in. "She had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." I believe her feeling entirely. I also clocked that it's the deliverable.

And still. I want the door to stay closed on him the way the narration keeps the door closed on his bedroom. So far it holds — the prose knows more than Pace does, the riser detail is *narrated as engineering*, not as swoon. My one live fear, the same as it's been: if the book ever forgets that this is a con and lets it become "the dom was simply right, she just needed to be seen," I'm out. This chapter didn't collapse into that. It let both readings sit on the box together. Trust holding, dread intact, and — finally, after that soft middle — genuinely, physically turned on. The sag is over. She paid it off.

Also, quietly: Randi wasn't in this chapter at all and I felt her absence like a cold spot. "He'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking" — Vee using her as proof of being chosen, not knowing Randi is the one who wrapped her. That line's going to gut me later.
