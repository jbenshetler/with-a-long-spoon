REACTION

Oh, this is the one. This is the chapter that explains the whole woman and it did it sideways, by never once leaving Gstaad.

I came into this braced for Vee on her knees or Vee having moved them — and instead the book pulls me years back and hands me Randi before she was Randi-who-schemes, and I didn't resent a single line of it. I was afraid, honestly, of a flashback here; I've been burned by backstory chapters that stall a cliffhanger. This didn't stall anything. It detonated quietly. Because every beat of it is the counter-image to the thing she's drowning in now, and the book trusted me to hold both without underlining it.

The fairytale is flawless and that's the horror. The sleigh, the two grey horses, the champagne, the venison and the sour cherry and the truffle shaved until heads turned — the book builds the most perfect night a girl pointed-at-marriage-since-birth could want, and then makes me watch her feel *nothing* inside it. "A good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." God. After fifty chapters of Pace feeding her meals and me being sick to death of the food-as-love note — here it finally pays off. The sandwich line isn't the note again, it's the note turned into a blade. The best the acceptable world can do and it's fast food. She knew it in her own mouth, "felt her own mouth do it from a long way off." That dissociation is the whole tragedy of her in one clause.

And John — "play along," the invented three children packed off to ski camp, "she walks in beauty" and he can't finish it, the hands crossing over his heart on the stair. He's charming. He's GOOD. He's a better dancer than Pace and the book lets her admit it and lets her admit she *preferred* the easy lead, "I think I'm done with the work," and that's the self-indictment, isn't it — she chose easy, she chose safe, she chose the man who'd never find her underneath because being found is the one thing she can't survive. "She had put nothing on the table and had won exactly that." That's the sentence I'll carry. That's the entire architecture of her life and she says it to herself naked in a perfect room.

The lead contrast gutted me. Pace's lead "the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for" — leaving the rest of the way to her. John's lead tells her body exactly where to go, no half-beat of guessing. And tonight she wanted the one that required nothing. The book has been showing me Pace do that to Vee the whole time — the held-open door, make her say it, make her reach — and here it shows me it's not a trick he runs on girls, it's the thing Randi fled. "Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." She likes not being asked because being asked means being present. That's such precise, grown-up, awful interiority. This is what I've been starving for.

And then the texts. Vee from Ohio, 💛, "i'm here if you want to talk. or not." And Randi gives her the fairytale — every word true, none of it the thing. "miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋" — the one line she meant, sent facedown. The whole decision about whether to tell Vee, run to the end of every scenario except the one she can't — "she could not picture Vee's face while she said it." That she's never said "I love you" without it being currency, and with Vee there's no qualifier, and that terrifies her more than the perfect empty man ever could. "She was not brave enough to lose her. She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be." That's the thesis of Randi and it's devastating and it tells me exactly how the scheme was born — she built a THREESOME, an engineered structure, so she'd never have to walk down a stair and say a plain true thing to Vee's face. The whole plot is this woman's cowardice wearing the costume of a plan.

What I love most: the book did NOT explain it to me. It didn't say "and this is why she schemes." It let me land it myself. For a book I've accused of over-cooking the food metaphor, this chapter had the discipline to shut up and let the sleigh bells do it.

Heat's low and I don't care — the sex is deliberately cold, the lipstick ring on him, the pleasure "real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared," she's watching herself from a long way off. It's anti-heat and it's supposed to be. The charge in this chapter is all dread and ache.

Only thing nagging: I wanted Vee, and I'm one chapter deeper into Randi instead of into the fallout of the leaving. But this didn't feel like a detour. It felt like the book finally paying me the thing I've been owed about Randi before it pays me the collision. And it bought enormous credit doing it. I'd text my friend at 1 a.m.: *the ski chapter. the SANDWICH line. she built the whole thing so she'd never have to be brave. I'm not okay.*
