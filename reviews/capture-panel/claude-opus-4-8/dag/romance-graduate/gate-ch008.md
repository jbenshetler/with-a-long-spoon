REACTION

Okay so here's the thing — I came into this chapter braced. After the froyo and the pool stuff I was getting nervous the book was going to coast on sweetness, and a whole date chapter, dinner, walking the long way home, could've been the exact kind of gauzy nothing I DNF over. It wasn't. It got me anyway, and it got me on the strength of one word she keeps hearing: *may*.

"May I choose?" Three words over a menu and I actually felt the same drop she felt. That's the trick this book keeps pulling and I keep falling for — it makes a surrender to a *food order* feel more charged than most books' actual sex scenes. The Mediterranean spread, the bright thing and the dark thing, her not watching herself eat for once — that landed square in the thesis the jacket promised, the starving girl who portions herself finally being fed. It's the apple from chapter one again but warm and public and she's the one it's happening to now instead of Randi. And I love/hate that I can see the machinery. I've read chapter one. I KNOW what "may I choose" turns into on a bench in that man's bedroom. So when Vee's chest goes up a degree it has no cause to, I'm not swooning — I'm dread-swooning. Every open door is a temptation, the jacket said. Watching her walk through them gladly with my stomach in a knot is a genuinely new feeling for me in this genre and I'm here for it.

The hand-holding. "less like something beginning than something resolving." God. That's the good writing doing its job — that's exactly how the best version of this feels and it's exactly the hook that makes it dangerous. She thinks her hand has been wrong its whole life for want of his. That's not romance, that's recruitment, and the book KNOWS it's recruitment, which is why I trust it.

And then the kiss reversal — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" and he just *smiles and lets it close and turns them back down the path.* Doesn't push, doesn't sulk, doesn't do a single thing every alpha in my Kindle would do. And THAT'S what makes her stay planted, that's what makes her drop the rule herself and close the distance. "I'm making an exception." He gets the yes by refusing to reach for it. That's the whole con of him and it is deeply, deeply effective and I felt it work on me a little even knowing what he is.

What keeps this from being too much sugar for me: the low hum of chapter one is still under everything. Christine knowing him, "recently, for this," the right hand every time (a rule he keeps that she doesn't understand — same energy as the bench he didn't correct her about). This man is a system and Vee is inside it and she has NO idea, and I have Randi's whole broken evening as my proof of where this goes. That gap is the engine. I'd text a friend: "he asked to choose her dinner and I gasped, and I hate that I gasped, because I've SEEN the bench."

Heat's still mostly deferred here — this was a tender chapter, not an explicit one — but I got chapter one's furnace already, so I'm willing to let a date breathe. Still fully hooked.
