REACTION

Okay so this is the first time the book has left me completely alone with Vee — no Pace, no Randi, no bench, no charge — and I didn't resent it for a second, which surprises me. That underwater passage got me. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." God. That's the whole chapter in one line. The bleachers lobbing *water wings* and *legs for days* at her and her putting her face on to walk fifty yards of it like an entrance — and then going under and the room switching off and something in her chest that had been standing at attention all afternoon quietly sitting down. I know that feeling and I did not expect this book to know it too.

And it rhymes, is the thing. Randi portioning herself to apple slices in chapter one, and now Vee in a suit that "gave her nowhere to hide," hearing her mother's *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Two women starved in the exact same way, and the book keeps laying them next to each other on purpose. I see what it's doing and I still fell for it.

Cassie. *Cassie.* She's the one I trust now and she's the one I'm scared for the book on Vee's behalf. She never asks — "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger" — she just shoots a low sheet of water across ten feet without stepping closer. That's the most loving thing anyone's done in this whole book and she does it in a navy suit older than their friendship. And I cannot forget that *"Goodbye, Vee"* from the coffee shop. Flat, finished, the word for a longer parting. This chapter just made me love the one person who's already standing at the edge of the deep saying *I'll be right here* — and I, the reader who read the jacket, know exactly what's coming for Vee and that Cassie can't wade out to it. That dread is doing real work on me.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book keeps giving me the sad girl underwater and then I remember two people are literally shopping for her and I want to throw it across the room in the good way.*

No heat here at all, and I clocked that — I came for both and this was pure depth. But it's one quiet chapter, it moved the emotional stakes an inch that mattered, and it bought Cassie a whole soul in eight pages. I'm not tired of a pattern; if anything it made me hungrier for the next turn of the screw.
