WHO'S WHO

Randi — the one I met first, on the bench. Black hair, startling blue eyes, the face she built at thirteen and hides behind. Sorority somebody, Standards board, has her own room, near the top of that house. Glamorous, warm on the outside, hollow-armored underneath. She's the co-architect — she and Pace are running a game on Vee, and she's the one who "shops" for the girl. But she got broken open on that bench first: she begged, she cried, she felt more alive than ever, and it terrified her so completely she decided in the mirror that Pace gets the face and never the real her. Never again. Cold citrus perfume. Portions herself down to nothing — apple slices, egg-white mother's voice in her head.

Pace — the mathematician. Grad student finishing a PhD, older-seeming, lives out west of town off six-fourteen past the gravel, built his own furniture, built the bench. Ordinary-looking, forearms that aren't a tutor's, moves like he's never had to think where his body is. Dominant, courtly, "may I," asks before he takes, gives the pencil back at the exact line she can solve it herself. Real tenderness AND pure technique, inseparable. Watches everyone like an instrument. Backstory: Daphne, a door he opened once that he wasn't invited through, and it cost him — so now he respects closed doors. Self-satisfied in a way I keep watching. He thinks he "feeds" starving girls.

Vee (Vivienne Thorne) — the mark. The redhead, lush and freckled, laughs with her throat bared, "forgot she could be seen." Econ, stats class, twenty-one, junior. Doesn't know the invitation reached her before she knew there was one. Chosen on the quad, harvested by an engineered shoulder-collision. Same wound as Randi — covers to the collarbone on purpose, portions her appetite, her mother's voice ("floozies") living in her mouth. Falling hard and fast, proud that every choice was hers.

Cassie — Vee's real friend. Ball cap, four words a day, the team, the lab, wants nothing from Vee. The only honest person in the book, the one reading the con clearly. "Goodbye, Vee." "How much did he give you to drink?" "Is he rich?" The tripwire. Being gently edged out.

Brooke — chapter president. Warm-voiced, stays seated to stop you, appraises Vee top to bottom and stays smiling. Runs the house. Sloane, Kinsey — the other sisters in the common room. Christine — the older woman at the restaurant who knows Pace and is glad to see him.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Ch1: Randi on the bench, taken apart, begs for real, cries, then bolts the door in the bathroom and decides Pace only ever gets the face. Ch2: Randi's morning-after at the house, the four-minute knife-fight interrogation from Brooke, then lying in last night's shoes not-crying. Ch3: in bed, Pace asks would she like to taste another woman — her body says yes three times before she can dress it as "only in a threesome, with you." Then the walk on campus, the pointing game, and he points at Vee. Randi's bare "yes." Randi executes the collision, comes back glowing: "Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together." Ch4–10: the trap closes. Randi befriends Vee in stats, Chi Latte, "love you, girl," "dear friend." Pace "happens" into the coffee shop, solves Vee's calculus by giving her back the pencil, gets her number. Froyo date, then the real dinner ("may I choose"), the kiss Vee initiates herself, then hiking planned. Vee debriefs to Cassie and to Randi, handing everything over. Randi tunes her with a fake catastrophe-date story and lifts the true small confessions out of her.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH

Randi — I can't hate her because I saw the mirror. She's the villain and the wound at once. Watching her cool damp hand close over Vee's and say "dear friend" made me want to throw my phone — I know what it cost and it's the opposite of what Vee credits her with. She's extracting, grooming, and it's dressed as the most loving lunch. I'm afraid for her too, honestly. She's spending herself on this.

Pace — suspicious, dread-swooning. The tenderness works on me even knowing what he is, which is the point and which I resent a little. I NEED the book to keep its ironic distance on him — if it collapses into "the dom was right, she just needs to submit emotionally," I'm out and furious. So far the narration knows more than he does. Trust holding.

Vee — protective, sick with it. She thinks freedom is the proof, "nobody did anything to me, I did it on purpose," and she's right and completely wrong and that gap is the whole book. Baby.

Cassie — I love her and I'm scared for her. She's going to lose, or get shut out, and she's the only one who sees.

WHAT I'M CARRYING

The apple sliced thin, cut-sides up because she eats faster that way. "There you are." Then "There you are, Randi" — the withheld name dropped at her most stripped. The water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail where it hurts, that she doesn't know she's doing. The deadbolt. "It felt like there being no door" — scariest sentence in the book. The please she says to a waiter vs. the please that comes from below. Vee underwater, "she had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." Cassie's whole-word "Goodbye, Vee." "May I choose?" The hand that felt "less like something beginning than something resolving." Randi's coffee cup lifted to her mouth twice, no sip happening. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem."

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

Pace and Randi in a room together, on the record — I want to watch the machine from the inside, hear them plan. The heat to come BACK, and to come back through Vee's body, the shame-into-heat the jacket promised. What Cassie knows or senses, and whether she's the exit Vee won't take, or the one who gets hurt trying. How "real it gets" for Randi and Pace — the jacket says it becomes as true for the two who started it as for the girl. When and how Vee learns there was a plan. Whether Vee ends up on that bench.

WHAT'S FADING

The exact geography of the pointing-game women on the quad — the expected one, the matched pair, the camel-coat project, the beanie girl who got a "maybe," the room-runner who got a hard no — they blur; only the shape (not the closed ones, not the mirrors, the alive one) stays. The specific plot of Randi's fake lacrosse-boy catastrophe date. Which sister was Sloane vs. Kinsey. The actual content of any conversation Vee had with Randi — fittingly, since Vee can't remember it either; I only keep the feeling and "love you, girl." The froyo chapter is nearly gone except the tart-holding-up line. Some sag in the middle: chapters 5–10 have run soft and heatless and are starting to smear together into one long courtship, which is exactly why I need the next one to pay off.
