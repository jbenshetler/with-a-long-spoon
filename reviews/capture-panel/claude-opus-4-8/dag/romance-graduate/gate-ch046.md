This girl is handing out a different edit of herself to every single person at the table and the book *knows* it and isn't explaining it to me, just letting the title do the work. Good. The dad stuff is where it got me. "My father would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." I've never had a romance heroine lay out the shape of her own father's blind spot that cleanly. I believe this family. I believe the burned rolls and Mom touching her own collarbone instead of saying anything about the shoulders.

But here's what's sitting cold in me. The whole chapter is Vee cataloguing what she *can't say* — can't tell Dad about the cooking, the house, the sewing; put a question mark on the word "boyfriend" out loud like she was *asking her parents* what he is. And then Cassie, my clean mirror, lands the only question that matters: "So what is he. To you." And Vee's got nothing that fits in Cassie's frame. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" "Both. Probably both."

That *both* is the whole chapter. She's been cropped so many times — by Randi, by the parents, by what Pace will and won't give her — that she can't produce an uncropped version even for Cassie, even for herself. And the last line undid me a little: Cassie doesn't push, Vee's grateful, "She was also, a little, not." She *wants* the seam pulled. She wants somebody to make her say it. Cassie won't, because Cassie knows pulling the seam loses the friend. Same grief as always, three feet away, holding her tongue.

Vee can't say what Pace is because she doesn't know what any of it is. And I'm watching her decide, gently, not to find out.

No heat, and I didn't want any — this chapter's job is the ache, not the burn, and it did the ache. What I'm impatient for is the PPP bomb going off, not another debrief. One more quiet one and my thumb starts moving.
