REACTION

Okay, so this is a bridge chapter and I know one when I see one — Vee home past eleven, backward on the desk chair, telling Cassie the shape of her weeks. And I don't resent it, because Cassie is the one person in this book I trust completely, and putting Vee in front of her is the only mirror that doesn't lie. After two straight chapters of me watching Vee get opened up — the bet, the finger, the drawer he cleared out for her — I needed somebody sober in the room. Cassie's that somebody.

"You're a public utility." God, I love her. That dry thing she does, and then the look held a beat and given over, *glad* — because for all the needling, Cassie wants Vee happy, she just also sees. And what she sees is the thing I've been screaming about from the cheap seats: *you don't come home school nights.* Not a question. She's counting Vee's empty bed the way I've been counting it. And "I don't get it. You've had boyfriends. You never went like this over one." That's Cassie doing the math I can't get anyone inside the book to do except her.

And the burn. Vee's hand going to the side of her breast without knowing it moved — that's good, that's the body telling the truth the mouth is dressing up. But Cassie's line gutted the romance clean in half: "There's only one way *that* burn happens. Not enough on." She said the quiet part. Vee's running around a warm house with nothing on getting burned by his cooking and calling it tenderness, and Cassie just — clocks it, flat, and lets it lie there. That's the whole book in one exchange: Vee narrating devotion, Cassie reading the actual weather.

What I'm sitting with: Vee can't find the word that isn't "fine." The ones before were fine. He's not fine. And she laughs at herself reaching and comes up empty, and that emptiness is real — that's a person genuinely further out past her own vocabulary than she's ever been. "Do you love him." "Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast." And Cassie *lets the maybe stand.* Doesn't push, doesn't warn, doesn't say I know. Goes back to her book. That restraint is the most loving and the most ominous thing in the chapter, because Cassie's decided she can't pull the seam without losing the friend, so she's just... bearing witness and holding her tongue. The grief I logged in my notes is all over this. She's three feet away, she sees all of it, and she says "go to sleep, streetlight."

Here's what I notice, though, and it's the first flicker of impatience: Randi is nowhere in this chapter. Not named, not thought of. And after 37 — the brunch, the pastry Vee never touched on her lip — the whole engine of this book is that Vee is being drawn two ways and only half-knows it. A debrief chapter with Cassie that's *entirely* about Pace, where Vee doesn't even brush against the Randi thing, feels like it's holding water. It's honest to Vee's self-deception, sure — she only confesses the Pace half because the Randi half has no name yet. But I felt the book keeping its cards instead of advancing them.

It's warm, it's true, it's well-made. But it's quiet, and it's the quiet-after-two-hot-ones kind, and I can feel myself wanting the triangle to *move* rather than be reflected on. One more of these and I'd start skimming for Randi.
