I've read the jacket. That's the horror of these middle chapters: I'm watching a con and the mark is this sweet econ girl who just wants a place to sit.

The thing that actually got me, though, and I mean *got* me, is the information architecture. Watching Vee tell Randi the whole night — handing it over, wanting to hand it over — and the reader knowing that every soft true thing Vee surrenders ("because of him," not "I made an exception") is going straight into the file. Randi weighing each confession like a jeweler. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." That's not a friend listening, that's intake. And then the chapter is *warm*, it feels like friendship, it feels like the best day Vee's had — and it's dread, pure dread, dressed as a smoothie. I love being made to hold both at once. That's the grown-up thing. The book isn't explaining the trap to me, it's letting me be the only one in the room who can see it.

Brooke did real work too — the "we missed you Thursday," the whole private week Vee isn't part of, Vee going "slightly the wrong size for the room." I've *been* the wrong size in that room. And then Randi spends something real to say "dear friend" and take her hand, and I believe it cost her, and I don't know yet whether it's tenderness or tradecraft, and *that's* the hook — because the jacket told me Randi is also getting caught in her own net. The cool damp hand on the white table did something to me I wasn't ready for.

Heat was basically nil here and I didn't want any — this was the right place for the book to go quiet. But I notice I'm three quietish chapters deep now (the pool, the dinner-told-twice) and the froyo/dinner is getting narrated to me a second time from Vee's mouth after I already lived it in chapter nine, which is the one spot my thumb slowed. I don't need the recap; I was there. Tell me something new.
