REACTION

Okay so this is the comedown chapter, and I know a bridge when I see one — Vee coming home lit up, Cassie on the bunk, the whole date getting retold at half the size and twice the glow. On its own it's sweet. Vee spinning in a room too small to spin in and nearly taking out the closet door got me, genuinely — that's a real body doing a real thing, and this book keeps giving her a body that moves before she's decided to move it. "Look at me." I felt that. The girl who portions herself apples is standing up out of a bean bag chair claiming a kiss she initiated, and she needs a witness for it, and Cassie is the witness.

But here's the thing — I am not reading this the way Vee is living it, and that gap is the entire engine of the book, and it's starting to hurt in the good way. Every single thing Vee counts off as *proof she's safe* lands in my stomach as the opposite. "I know where he lives and everything." Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel. She's reciting a dossier to feel like she has control and all I could hear was the jacket: *the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one.* Cassie, bless her, is the only person in this book with working instincts — "what to tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop," "that's the whole address," *is he rich* — and she's playing it as a bit, and nobody including her knows how right she is. And Randi isn't even in this chapter and she's *all over it*, because Pace pointed and Randi picked and now Vee is doing a clumsy joyful pirouette about a hiking date that two other people planned for her weeks ago.

What I keep turning over: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That's the thesis of the whole thing said out loud by the mark herself, and it's true, and that's what's merciless about it. Every yes is hers. That was the problem. The book told me that on the cover and now it's just quietly collecting them.

Heat-wise this was nothing, and I don't resent it — it earned a breather after the restaurant chapter, which was one of the most erotic non-sex scenes I've read in ages, all bread and a hand and the word *may*. But I'll be honest: this is the first chapter in a little while that didn't advance the thing I actually came back for. It's charming and it's doing real structural work — Cassie's dread, the hiking setup, the house we haven't seen — but it's the retelling of a scene I already read, and I could feel myself wanting the camera to cut to Randi's phone, or to the trail, or to that house off the gravel road. I want the next step, not the recap of the last one.

What keeps me is that this book has never once wasted my time yet and has never explained its own knife to me. So I trust the quiet.
