REACTION

Oh. *Oh.* Twenty-three chapters I've been begging for this in every note and it just — gave it to me. Pace and Randi, in bed, after, on the record. The seam I said I'd leave the book if it never showed me. Here it is, and it's better and worse than I wanted, which is the only way it could've been good.

The thing that guts me: they're not scheming. I braced for a villain-monologue, the two puppeteers cackling over the strings, "phase two of the plan," some reveal that makes them cartoons. Instead it's *pillow talk*. It's two people who are actually in love with each other talking about a third person they're both falling for, and the horror is that the falling is real. "She got past me before I could help it." "I keep waiting to get bored of her and it doesn't come." That's not a con report. That's a woman confessing she lost control of her own machine. The jacket promised it gets as true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew, and this is the chapter where that bill starts coming due and I felt it land in my stomach.

And the yeti-shoe story retold from HIS bed — that's the knife. I just read that whole afternoon from Vee's side, the canoe shoe, the bigfoot walk, the older couple, her going red. Sweet, funny, the best friend she's ever had. And now it's *content*. Randi's bringing it home to Pace like a hostess bringing back a dish from a party, and the tell is "She went so red I thought she'd go up… That was kind of hot." There it is. The Randi-heat I kept saying the book keeps opening a door on and closing soft — it's not closed here. She wants Vee. She said it in his bed, undefended, and he *heard* it and deliberately didn't pick it up: "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." God. The restraint of that. He rations HER too.

But the real punch, the one that made me actually put the thing down for a second: Pace saying Vee lights up a room, she has no idea she does it, "she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her." And Randi going still. Not loose-still. *Held* still. "The kind that takes holding." And the beat too long before "Yeah. She is." That's the whole book in four lines. Randi decided at thirteen she'd never be the girl who gets to be fine, and here's the man she loves describing the fineness of the girl she brought him — the fineness she can't have — and she has to lie there and hold her own face together and then grind down onto him to change the subject. She fed him this girl and now she has to watch him fall, and she's the one bleeding. She built the trap and she's standing in it. I'm not sure I've ever read jealousy this exact.

What I love — what keeps my trust — is the narration STILL knows more than either of them. "I want her to get there on her own" from Pace could've been the moment it collapsed into the-dom-was-right, he just wants her to choose freely, isn't he good. But it's framed inside the fact that they've already chosen FOR her, weeks ago, and "get there" means somewhere they picked. The tenderness is real and it's the instrument of the taking, both at once, no seam — same wire, like the shame and the heat in Vee. This book keeps its nerve.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *THEY'RE IN LOVE. it's not a scam it's a MARRIAGE and they're both falling for the girl and she has no idea and Randi WANTS her too and I think Randi's going to be the one who breaks. I take back every impatient thing I said. keep reading.*

The scar's still not explained. Daphne's still a ghost. Vee still doesn't know there was a plan. But I got my room, my two people on the record, and it paid off harder than ten chapters of waiting had any right to. I'm all the way in.
