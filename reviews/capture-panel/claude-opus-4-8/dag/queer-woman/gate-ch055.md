REACTION

Okay, this one's a glass of orange juice. Sweet, warm, nothing in it that's mine. After two chapters of Vee coming apart three times over and that small minor-key thing lodging under her ribs at the end of 52 — the taste, the *why does he like it so much*, the question she's going to have to find a way to say to Randi — this is the book letting everybody catch their breath in a sunny room. Which is fine. Books do that. But I read for the charge and there wasn't one in here, so mostly I sat in the light with them and watched.

What I actually clocked, though, and can't put down: the cover story. Vee tells Kayla and Meg the shrunk-sweater version — the cranberry sweater, the too-hot wash, dye in the good pants, *that's why I was in his t-shirt* — and the book says the record "stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." That's the same girl who cropped her own induction photo at the waist for her parents, who built the Rusty Nail lie so she could forgive Pace for a thing that went the other way. She's fluent now. She lied to Cassie's face last chapter, lies to her whole floor this one, and it costs her *nothing* — it comes out of her mouth pre-shaped, funny, load-bearing. She flashed a man on his porch naked in January and the truth of her weekend is unsayable to the people who love her, so she's built a doll-sized sweater to stand in front of it. And she's *happy*. That's the part that sits cold under all the butter. She's got the middle of it the whole time, the book keeps saying — but the middle of it is a thing she can't say out loud to a single soul at that table.

And then the dinner. This is the thing I'm actually watching. Ch53 was Pace at the sink taking down every name off her shelf — Meg, the boots, Kayla and the paint, Cassie, *Theo, who asked her out once* — and not turning around, and "let me handle it." This chapter is the invitations going out. The field's assembling. He wants to meet her people, all of them, in one room, and he's picking the place, and Randi's going to be in it. The collision I've been owed for fifty-some chapters is being *scheduled*, and it's the architect scheduling it. A man who doesn't flinch at "he asked me out once" — who says *bring Theo* and reaches for the next pan — is not a man without a plan for that room. I don't trust the warmth of it one inch. The reader in me that loved Vee at the door wants her to have her happy loud table; the reader with the guard up is watching Pace build the set.

What's missing is Randi, and that absence is loud to me because I hold the mirror. Vee ends 52 saying *Randi will know what to make of it* — the tongue, the taste, the small light that won't go all the way out. And I know what Randi is, and I know what it's going to cost Randi to sit across a little table Wednesday and be handed that, in that room, with Pace at the head of it. The book's keeping her offstage and I can feel the shape of her being kept there.

So: thin, sweet, connective. Nothing turned me on, nothing scared me, one thing stayed cold under the syrup. But the next chapter has a dinner in it with all three of them at one table for the first time, and I am not leaving before that door opens.
