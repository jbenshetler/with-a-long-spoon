So the book finally opened him up and let me all the way in, and I want to be careful about what I actually saw in there, because this chapter is doing the exact thing I've been braced against for forty-nine chapters and it's doing it beautifully, which is its own kind of tell.

Here's what's real: he got drunk. *Pace.* The man who squares his fork to the table edge, who folds his whole ordered self into one drawer, who never once loses the thread — he mopped up his own sick and I can smell the bleach through the whole chapter. The engine on the highway and his head coming up before he knew he'd moved, his body "gathering toward the narrow cut in the hill" — that's the involuntary thing, the withdrawal-of-motion equivalent I trust, the tell that fires before the mind can dress it. He's not performing grief here because there's nobody watching him grieve. The truck goes past and he bends over the table and sobs. Okay. That landed. I felt it in my chest and I'm annoyed about it in exactly the way the jacket promised I'd be.

But — and this is the whole but — look at what the chapter *does* with Daphne. It hands me the machine's origin story. The tutor who was older and knew how things worked, who showed him where to stand when everyone else was born knowing, first girlfriend first lover first love, he said it and meant it and then she left and it "consumed his life." And so he built a rule: the words are the difference, the words are what makes loss unsurvivable, so never say the words. That's the withholding-as-grace I've been humming under every soft thing since chapter 20, and now the book has given it a wound to stand on. "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." The book wants me to read the withholding as damage instead of method. As a scared man, not a man running an engine.

And here's where my wariness sits up. Because I know there's a plan. The jacket told me on page one — two people planned the best year of her life, it began as a game, a secret choice made about her. So this whole aching hungover chapter, this Daphne backstory that recasts the coldest technique in the book as trauma — I have to hold it next to the fact that *this man is one of the two architects.* Is the Daphne wound real, or is it the deepest layer of the same withholding, the sob I get to witness because the book needs me to forgive him before the reveal, same as I flagged Randi's MIRH blindside was doing? The jacket's own thesis: "as true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew there was one." So the book is explicitly promising me it got real for him. This chapter is that promissory note coming due. It got real. He's wrecked.

The thing is — I believe the wreck and I still don't trust the frame. Both. A man can be genuinely destroyed and still be a man who did a cold cruel deliberate thing on that porch, watched his smaller truth "reach her and fail," and *let it stand.* He says so himself. He watched it fail and chose it anyway. The chapter frames that as weakness-in-the-moment, but it reads to me as the method holding even when it costs him everything, and the fact that he's crying about the cost doesn't unmake the choice. "At the moment she asked him to be brave he had been weak." Sheri already said the true version last chapter, flat and unanswerable: *then not saying it matters more to you than having her.* This chapter is his rebuttal and it's a good one and it's still not enough, which the title knows.

The man who builds hunger and then feeds it, alone, feeding himself prison food on purpose because he's not entitled to the meal. "He was not hungry. He ate anyway." That's a good last line and it earned itself. And the detail that the whiskey took his balance and his dinner and hours he couldn't use but "left every memory of Vee intact" — he tried to drown it and it wouldn't drown. That's the mirror of Vee in chapter 44 taking off the shirt because the warmth was only her own given back. They're both lying in the cold now, each one holding a thing that won't warm them. The book is good at that rhyme.

What I notice I'm NOT getting, still: one word of the plan. He's alone in his own head, wrecked, honest to himself about Daphne and fear and weakness — and there is not one flicker of *Randi,* not one flicker of the game, not one thought of the thing they set in motion. A man cataloguing his every failure with Vee and the architecture of his own withholding, and the biggest secret he's carrying doesn't surface even in private.
