REACTION

A breather chapter, and I know a breather when I'm handed one — the machine's off-stage, Randi's a scent and a scrunchy and nothing else, and the whole thing is just Vee and the man in the small clean house. So I read it the way you read the quiet stretch: with my guard up higher, not lower, because this is exactly where a book like this does its real installing, in the part that doesn't announce itself.

And God, it's well-made, which is the problem. "Peter," she says, to the sleeping shape of him — the real name, the boy's name, the Daphne name, the wound's name. That's the book telling me she's all the way inside now, past Pace-the-built-thing to the person she thinks is under it, and she picked the one syllable that's actually his soft spot without knowing it's a soft spot. It went in under my guard the way I keep warning myself these plain-voiced beats do. I felt it and then I resented feeling it.

The drawer. Of course the drawer. He folds his whole ordered self down into one crammed drawer and gives her the top one, the easy one your hand goes to first, laid out and waiting with *room for more of her* — and no note, there's never a note, because the not-saying is the technique, the not-saying is how he says everything without ever putting a claim on paper she could hold up later. I've watched him run "ask before you take, leave her the room" for thirty-four chapters and here it is again in furniture: he doesn't tell her the sprawl didn't bother him, he *builds her a place* so she'll never ask. It's the most romantic thing in the book and it's also him managing the exact moment she worried she'd been too much, closing it off before it can cost him anything. Both. It's always both, and the book knows it's both, and it will not let me have the join.

The detail that actually snagged me — sharper than the drawer — is the weight. Her clothes gone looser, her body "quietly burning down whole weekends of him," losing since she met him and never asked it to. That's the one place the tenderness curdled a little for me. A woman being consumed into a smaller shape without deciding to, her body spending itself on him in the dark — I don't think the book means it sinister, it means it as bloom, fed-at-last. But *quietly burning down* is a phrase with teeth and I don't think it's an accident. Something is being spent.

What I clocked most, coldly: this is the "him" arm getting its cement, and it's completely sealed off from Randi. No collision, no bill, no Cassie. The book parked the dangerous machinery and just let the man be wonderful for a whole chapter, and I'm suspicious of the parking. You don't get a chapter this soft in a book this merciless for free. I'm being softened right alongside her, on purpose, so that whatever's coming lands on a reader who just spent a chapter falling for him clean.
