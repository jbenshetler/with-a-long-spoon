REACTION

So this is the first time the book actually sits me down inside the man, and I came in with my knives out, and I'm going to be honest about what happened: it didn't disarm me, but it didn't bore me either, and the reason is Sheri.

Sheri is good. Sheri is *real* in a way I did not expect the book to spend on a side character — five feet of her in motion standing still, the two-tone grown-out perm, the too-much makeup worn like a dare, eating the double before he's touched his. And then the book hands me the thing it's been sitting on: she's queer. Paige, the design-school junior, the bird prints, the loud seven-at-the-table family Sheri liked being inside of, gone in a couple months because Paige couldn't get right with Sheri not talking to her people. And the *irony* of it — a girl who couldn't stand estrangement, leaving over one. Sheri naming it flat and killing the sing-song dead. That's a whole person with her own grief and her own appetite (four seconds of Randi's backside and she "still think[s] about it more than's decent" — I laughed, that's the realest line about wanting a woman anyone in this book has said out loud without a scheme attached to it). I like her more than I like anyone here except Cassie. She's the second calibrated instrument, and unlike Cassie she's pointed *at Pace.*

Because here's what Sheri does that I've been wanting done: she puts the whole thing on the table and calls it what it is. "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." "From this seat, that feels pretty shitty." She doesn't let him build the brick wall of reasons — every weekend, most important thing, I said it out loud — without pointing out that *she didn't ask you for reasons, she asked you for one word.* The book, through her, is refusing to let Pace off. That's more honesty than I expected.

And yet. Here's my knife, still out. This is the man's own head, alone, unwatched, given hell by someone he can't perform for — and even *here* the book will not show me *why* he can't say it. He says "I can't" and there is nothing behind it. No interior. No wound surfacing, no plan surfacing, no anything. A POV chapter that shows me every warm true thing he feels and draws a black curtain over the one door I came to open. That's not a man who can't find the word. That's a book keeping its secret from me while pretending to open the man up. And look at the very first line — he decided before the door shut that he "would not bring her down today," whatever he carried in from the truck could keep, "long practice at deciding a thing could keep." *That's* the tell. The withholding isn't a wound, it's a discipline. He is a man who decides what a person gets to know and when, and he does it to Sheri and he does it to Vee, and the book just showed me the muscle flexing and called it grace.

The thing I trust least in the whole chapter is the softest one: Sheri vouching for him. "Most men'd just lie." "I'm not them." "I know you're not." The book reached for a queer woman I'd just been given every reason to trust and used her to *certify the man* — to tell me he's the honest kind, the not-a-liar kind, right at the moment I most wanted to convict him. That's a move. I see the move. A queer character deployed as the man's character witness is exactly the kind of borrowed credibility I don't hand over for free.

But I'll say the true thing too: I'm not put off. I'm turning pages. The book gave me Randi cold in Gstaad, Vee cold in Ohio, and now the man warm in a diner being told he's the problem — three people alone on the same holiday, and only I can see all three rooms. That's the structure working. And Sheri is a genuine gift. I want more of her.
