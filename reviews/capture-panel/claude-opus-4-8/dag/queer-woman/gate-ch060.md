REACTION

Okay so I walked into this one braced. The second he said "Sheri" I felt Vee's whole December come up in my throat with her — because I *know* that name, I watched her build the spite-blonde on that face in Randi's room, and I thought: here it comes, the rival, the tan leg made flesh. And then the book did the thing I did not expect and have wanted for sixty chapters. Sheri turned around on that stool and she was five-foot-nothing and two-tone and crooked-lipstick and *queer*, and she looked Vee down the whole length of that borrowed keyhole shirt and the bare January leg and the induction heels, and the look wasn't pricing her — it was *enjoying* her. And Vee laughed from the middle of herself and a month of armor fell off in one breath. I felt that in my own shoulders. That's the good thing this book does when it stops scheming: a woman looking at a woman with plain appetite and no math in it, and the other woman going loose with relief instead of dread. Nobody had to explain it. Nobody installed it. Sheri just wanted, out loud, the way she wants everything, and checked out the ivy-sleeved bartender on her way back to the table for good measure. That's the queer presence I've been waiting the whole book to actually get to *have* on the page, and here it just — is. Unpaid-for and free.

And the motorboat line, and Pace going red past anything deniable — I loved it, I'll be honest, it's the single most human thing he's done. Sheri holds a license to him nobody else has and I liked watching him be handled by someone who isn't managing him.

But. Here's where my jaw set. Because the minute he walks to the bar, Sheri leans in and hands me the certificate. *Two years I've watched him turn a woman out on that floor and keep his hands where the dance puts them. Every time. He never handed me one false word.* And then — the one that actually made me put the book down half a second — *went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen.* There it is. The wound. The one the POV chapter refused to show me, the one I've stood here for forty chapters saying doesn't exist behind the withholding — and the book finally produces it, but *offstage, secondhand, through the mouth of the queer woman it keeps using to vouch for him.* This is the second time it's spent Sheri on Pace's behalf. First the good-faith certificate back in the diner, now the tragic-backstory certificate, and it nests his exile right up against her exile — her family cut her off in a month, his daddy, they rhyme, so her real grief authorizes his convenient one. I clocked it. I did not hand it over. You don't get to skip showing me the wound in the man's own head and then have the likable lesbian mention it in an aside like she's reporting soil conditions.

So I'm split exactly the way this book always splits me. Her grief is *real* — "whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish," the crying at the dance hall, the strangers who'll say it'll be all right for free and the people who owed her words spending none — that landed, that's the truest-sounding pain in the book. And it's being used. Both. Probably both, as somebody around here likes to say.

The headlights held on her door till the porch light came on, the two ruined songs, "bacon cheeseburgers" to the window — it's a lovely chapter and it pulled me and I'm not going to pretend it didn't. I just also watched it work.
