REACTION

Okay, so the trick of this chapter is that the whole time I thought I was watching the girl from the jacket — the starving twenty-one-year-old, the honeycrisp sliced thin, the sorority, the blue eyes — and it's *Randi*. It's the architect. The last line of the bench section is a genuine drop-your-stomach move: "There you are, Randi." I actually sat back. Because the jacket sold me Vee being taken apart, and instead the very first thing the book does is take apart one of the two people who supposedly built the trap. That's not what I expected and it's the smartest thing it could've done. The schemer is not safe from her own scheme. Good. That's the version of this premise I can trust further than the other one.

Now — the part I have to be honest about. This entire chapter is *his* eyes. Every inch of her is inventoried by a man who is quietly pleased with his own patience: the muscle "deliberately made," "years of cheer written into the muscle of it," the appraisal of her expensive underwear, his own restraint held up to the light over and over so I can admire it with him. There's a stretch in the middle of the spanking where it's less about her and more about how unhurried and masterful he is, and that's the closest I came to rolling my eyes. I've read a hundred men like Pace and the book clearly wants me to find him devastating. I don't, quite. I find him *effective*, which is different, and I think the book might actually know that.

Because here's what saves it: the bathroom. The last third flips into her head and it's the best writing in the chapter by a mile. The face as armor — the thing that takes the being-wanted and lets none of it through — and the decision, arriving "quiet as a deadbolt going over," to go back behind it and never come out again. That's real. That's a person, not a type. And the tiny brutal detail of her pressing the bottle-cap's edge under her thumbnail without knowing she's doing it — that told me more about Randi than the entire catalogue of her body did. She got seen, it was ecstatic, it terrified her, and her response is to close the door forever and hand him the mask. He, meanwhile, is on the bed congratulating himself on how far he "took her." The gap between those two rooms is the whole book, I think.

What I'm suspicious of: that this stays his show. It's M/F so far, all male gaze, and the queerness I came for is nowhere yet — it's a promise on the jacket. If the pattern is going to be "man renders woman from the outside while women get rendered *at*," I'll get tired. But this chapter earned a lot of goodwill by making the seducer bleed, and by giving me Randi's interior in a voice that doesn't flinch. I want to know who she's fleeing from and what it means that she's the one running the game on Vee while privately falling apart. What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *it's not the ingénue on the bench, it's the villain, and she cried, and then she decided to lie about it forever — I'm in.*
