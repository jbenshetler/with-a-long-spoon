A cool-down chapter, and I know what those are for — you don't sprint out of a scene like the doorstep and chapter 52 without the book needing to let me breathe. So I read this one slower, watching what it did with the quiet.

And what it did with the quiet is a lie. That's the whole spine of chapter 54 for me. Vee comes home lit up like the athletic-dining-hall windows, ready to tell Cassie everything, and the thing she tells her is a story about a cranberry sweater felted down to doll size in a too-hot wash. It's a *good* lie — she's got his flat delivery down, she's got the beats, she paces the "he burned the toast" callback so Cassie laughs the rare full laugh — and I sat there watching her be charming and thought, that's the thing she's charming *with*, now. She's using the real texture of the weekend, the true details, to upholster a false frame. The mushrooms are real. The oranges are real. The reason she was in his t-shirt is not. And the line that got me, cold and clean the way this book gets when it wants to leave a mark: *the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody.* Cassie is my sightline and Cassie just got handed a doctored photo and filed it. Same move as the induction photo cropped at the waist. She divides the frame, she hands out the decent half. She's fluent now. Nobody made her do this one — Randi wasn't in the room, there was no play to run. This was just Vee, on her own, protecting the shape of it.

And I notice I'm not even mad at her for it. That's what's unsettling me. It reads as tender — two girls laughing in an overcooked room with a cold line of air coming in — and the tenderness is exactly the anesthetic. She *missed a draft.* She missed the dorm. There's a real person here with a real friend and a real other life, and the book keeps letting me love that so I don't flinch at the editing.

The thing that actually reached in and touched me, though: *my mother says thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again.* And Vee's hand stops halfway to the towel and goes to her collar instead, and something goes through her middle "warm and specific, like a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck." That's Cassie's *family.* Vee has a belonging over there — a mother who wants her back, a whole ordinary claim on her — and she says "spring break, sometime, tell her I said hi," which is not the size of it at all, and the book knows it's not the size of it. There's a life Vee has that isn't Pace, that isn't Randi, that's just *hers*, and she's letting it go faint the way the shirt goes faint. I clocked that. I'm keeping it.

The bunk at the end — her arm going out and finding cold cinderblock a hand's width off, after two nights of all that acreage and the heat of him at her back. She leaves the hand on the wall and the wall is only a wall. It's a lovely small ache and it's also, let's be honest, doing recruitment: teaching me, with her, to feel the dorm as the lesser place, the narrow bed, the polite trickle of a shared shower, the *less* of it. She's happy in it anyway, the book says twice — but the whole paragraph is built to make his house the real house. I see the machine idling under the sweetness. I always do here.

What I'm still carrying from 52 and this chapter didn't touch: the taste. The "ugh, gross, wrong" and the tongue that went hunting anyway, the small light left on under the affection, minor-key, waiting. She said she'd tell Randi "nearly everything." This chapter she told Cassie *less* than nearly everything — she told her a fiction. Two friends, two different edits. And I'm watching to see which one she brings the real thing to. My bet's Randi, and my dread's Randi, for the same reason.

Thin chapter. But it moved the Cassie thread exactly one click in the direction I've feared since chapter 41, and it did it while I was smiling.
