Okay so this is the chapter where the machine reaches all the way down and remodels her body to spec, and the awful thing is it's also the best-written wanting I've gotten in a while, and the book knows exactly that those are the same sentence.

The wrong-hand passage is the real thing. "It held her the exact way she wanted to be held and it did not want her at all." A bored stranger's gloved palm comes down flat on her thigh, *don't move*, and Vee's whole body lights up because it learned that grammar in a dark room from a man who meant it — and now here's the shape with the meaning scooped out, and it opens "a hole in her a mile across where the right hand should have been." That's desire rendered from the inside, physical and specific and hers, arriving sideways under the last hands on earth that should trigger it, and appalling her while it happens. That I'll keep. That's the book telling the truth about how a body works — the want fires off the *form* of a thing, not the intention behind it, and she can't vote on it. I felt it in my own stomach. Good. Genuinely good.

And then right up against it: the whole chapter is her being made bare *because he asked her bare*, "taken apart and made smooth for him," a part of herself she'd "never once in her life thought of as a thing that got done" getting done because she said yes three nights ago in a bright kitchen before her mind caught up to the sentence. This is the jacket's thesis at its most literal — the appetite installed, the body reshaped to another's specification, every yes freely given. And I want to be clear with myself: the book didn't flinch from that. The "kind of girl who" voice out of her mother's kitchen, the shame, the folding-her-underwear-inside-her-jeans, the courtesy of the privacy "as though privacy were coming at all" — it makes the compliance *cost* something, it doesn't pretend she's just liberated and fine. The false floor — "that's the hard part behind you," *okay, roll over* — is the cruelest structural move and it's about consent given blind. She never asked what the whole of it was. She never asks. That's the horror the book is actually about and it staged it clean.

What I didn't get: any of the charge on my side of the glass advancing. The two girls on the couch — the one who came to be looked at, the one who came to be touched "for one set of hands," and Vee knowing "which one she was" — that's Vee placing herself in a taxonomy of desire, and it's nice, but it points straight at Pace. What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the wrong hand pressing her down and her body going off anyway, the exact shape of being held with nothing inside it — that's the truest thing this book has done in a while, and it happens inside the single most "he remodeled her to spec" scene in the whole thing, and I cannot tell if I'm being moved or handled and I've stopped expecting to be able to.*
