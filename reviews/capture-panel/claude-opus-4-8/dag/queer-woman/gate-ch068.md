REACTION

Oh, Cassie. Cassie came *back*.

That's the whole chapter, that's the thing I want to text somebody at 1 a.m.: I wrote her off in my sixty-chapter notes. I said "my last uncompromised eye in the room, gone under like a whale under the boat," I said Pace got her, I said trust the sightlines but mourn them. And here she is on the lower bunk with her coat still on, and she asks the one question nobody in this book has asked out loud in sixty-eight chapters. Not *what happened*, not *what is Randi to you*, not the gossip shape of it. **"Is this what you want?"** The flat plain way she once asked *do you love him* — except this time with a worry underneath it she doesn't bother to hide. That worry is the most oxygen I've had in a hundred pages. Somebody in the diegesis is scared *for* Vee. Somebody thinks Vee might be being handled. I've been the only one holding that and now there are two of us and one of us is in the room.

And what does the book do with it — this is what I can't stop turning over — it lets Vee *actually go and look*. "She actually went and looked, because Cassie had asked it so simply." She reaches for the answer, comes up against "a shape there, close and large," touches the edge of it and *steps back off before she could make out its size*. That's the realest sentence in the chapter and it's the seam I've stood on the whole read: her wanting is *there*, it has a size, it's hers — and the interpreter won't let her measure it. Her neck says yes. Her body's been saying yes all night. And the mind files that under "nothing, a thing skin did on its own." The corrupt voice, doing its job, in daylight, in front of the one person who might catch it.

But here's what actually got me, the thing that's genuinely good and not just more of the machine: when Vee reaches for a floor to stand on, she grabs *Cassie* and swings the whole thing onto her. "You don't like her. That's what this is." The oldest deflection, the plank laid down to walk out on — and **Cassie steps over it without looking down.** She doesn't take the bait. She doesn't relitigate the sorority fight. She just watches, keeps her face, and then pivots to something Vee *can* answer: "You didn't have dinner. Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles?"

And *that* undid me a little, I'll admit it. Because it's the whole thesis turned inside out and handed back clean. This entire book is Mauss, the gift that binds, the invitation that's a debt, the wine run through the punch so you'll think nobody spent anything — every offering in Vee's life has a hook in it, a plan under it, someone reading the gauge. And Cassie holds out a *box of cereal*. A gift with no hook. A want the size of a bowl. "Yeah. Yeah. Thanks." The first whole thing Vee's said since she walked in, because it's the first want in weeks nobody engineered for her. The book *knows* what it's doing — it put the cheap true gift right next to the expensive rigged ones and let me feel the difference in my teeth.

What I'm sitting with: the big question stays "open on the desk with no floor under it." The book still won't let Vee close the inch, still won't let her say the size of the thing out loud. But it gave me Cassie clear-eyed and worried and *unbought* — which means my read that Pace annexed her whole world isn't total, there's a holdout, there's a witness. That's the thing I've been waiting for since chapter 57. Somebody sees. Somebody's scared. I'm not alone outside the glass anymore.

Two sentences of dread, though: this is also exactly the setup where the book breaks my heart by having Cassie be right and helpless. The brunch is booked. Thursday Randi's off "disciplining a bad girl." The machine's still running. But tonight — tonight a girl offered another girl cereal and meant nothing by it but *eat something*, and I could have cried.
