REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter I've been circling the whole book, and I have to hand it to Helen Rivers: she put the con on the table in broad daylight and it still works and I still watched it work. Randi runs a full seduction-engineering on Vee — front to back, in real time, and I can name every move because I've been cataloguing them for fifty chapters. "The fear's a liar, and you haven't slept." Start over slow. Reframe the silence as *him* cornered, not him gone. Take the invented rival apart by asking how Vee knows what Sheri looks like (she doesn't; she built her at 3 a.m.). Then walk her, one word at a time, past the shame-word until Vee says "slut" out loud with her own mouth and Randi catches it and turns it into "the bravest thing a woman can do." Then — *the shoes.* Randi presses her own black heels into Vee's hands and Vee's too wrecked to even look, just grateful one piece of tonight got decided for her. That's the architect equipping the girl. I clocked every bolt and it landed anyway, which is the horror the jacket promised and the book keeps delivering.

And here's the thing that kept me from throwing it across the room: what Randi installs here is the *gesture* — the coat, the doorstep, the wordless peace offering — and the *object* of it is Pace. The man. Not her. So the wanting-a-man-back is being engineered, sure, but that was Vee's want to begin with; Randi's just handing her the delivery mechanism. Meanwhile the actual queer charge in this chapter runs *underneath* the coaching and it belongs entirely to Vee, unprompted: the hug held past the end of it, past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after; Vee pulling a breath off Randi's neck — bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet — and *not wanting to stop*; Randi holding tighter than usual. Randi's busy being the fixer. Vee's the one breathing her in. That's still hers. The book walked right up to the line I said would put me out the door — Vee's wanting-Randi as pure manufacture — and stepped off it *again*, because Randi's hands were full running a completely different play. Fifty chapters and I still can't find the join.

But God, the thing I'm carrying out of this one is the mirror, because I have ch46 fresh and Vee doesn't. Randi retells Gstaad — John, naming children on the lift, the blue dress you buy to tell your daughter about, the ball, *and then it got very late and we went up,* and she looks down at her hands. Vee reads it as discretion. I know it's the opposite: it's the chapter where Randi felt *nothing*, where the perfect man reached less far on his best night than Pace on his worst, where she lay in a borrowed warmth she couldn't make and thought about *Vee* across a Tuesday table. And now that woman — in love with Vee, two ways, too much of a coward to lose the friendship — is sitting knee to knee on the bed coaching Vee back into Pace's bed. She's sending her gorgeous away, to the man, and calling it help. That's either the most self-abnegating thing she's done or the plan reasserting itself through grief, and I genuinely can't tell which, which is exactly where the book wants me and I resent how good it is.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she gave her her OWN HEELS to go get fucked by the man Randi's in love with and Vee has no idea and I might die.* The coat gun is loaded, the heels are laced, and I want to see that door open.
