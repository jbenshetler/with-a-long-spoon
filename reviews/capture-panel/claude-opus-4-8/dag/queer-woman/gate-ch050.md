REACTION

Okay. I have to talk about this one in two halves because I was two different readers inside it.

First half: I watched Randi run a con on a wrecked girl and I got that specific cold feeling in my stomach, the one I've been bracing for the whole book. Vee comes in off a red-eye with her face destroyed, no sleep, no floor under her, and Randi sits her on a bed and *walks her down a ladder* — word by word, her own description — from "I can't, that's a slutty thing to do" to "and nothing under it," until Vee hears herself finish the sentence Randi deliberately left hanging. That is the installation. That is the exact shape I came in allergic to: a woman's move getting engineered by someone with a plan, dressed as encouragement, dressed as *bravery* even — "that's the bravest thing a woman can do." And the shoes. God, the shoes. The stilettos with the straps that don't hold anything on, pressed into her hands, "just the thing for tonight," and Vee's nipples drawing up tight and her telling herself it's *the thought of Pace* while the reader can see perfectly well it's the whole scene, it's Randi, it's the shoes Randi chose. Desire arriving sideways in the body before the mind will name it — which is the thing I love most — except it's arriving inside a room another woman is steering. That's the knife's edge this book keeps walking and I was ready to be furious.

And then the book did the thing that bought it back. Vee gets her hand on the latch, and she *turns around and goes back*, and she takes Randi's face in both hands and kisses her, and takes her time, and isn't embarrassed until after. That is not in Randi's script. Randi didn't coax that. And the proof is the back half of the chapter: Randi standing in the empty foyer with the back of her hand against her own mouth, a thing she hasn't done since she was fifteen, short-circuiting — "four months of being the one who leaned in. Four months of a girl who held still for it and was sweet about it and thanked her and never once started it." The schemer got moved. The corrupter is the one undone in the hall. That is the subversion I did not fully trust this book to reach for: the predatory-queer script picked up and *turned over*, so the appetite turns out to be Vee's, finally with room, and the one with the plan is the one standing there with her heart going like she climbed three flights. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. in all caps about that flip.

And then Brooke. Oof. That scene is doing real work — the warm, bright, merciless house president who knows every room's worth to the inch, Lauren Pike, "you need to choose." That's the institution with its hand on Randi's throat, and it makes her closet real instead of decorative, and it raises the price on everything that just happened upstairs. The book isn't collecting the charge for free here; it's showing me what it costs her to be seen.

What I'm sitting with: I don't fully know yet whether the kiss was Vee's own appetite or a thing Randi's months of leaning-in finally conditioned into her — and the beautiful, maddening thing is the book seems to *know* that's the question, and is holding both. Randi handed her the coat and the shoes and the whole "go to Pace" errand, and what she got back was a mouth she didn't plan on. Which of those is the true one. I'm reading toward Vee's.

Also: Randi not narrating the John night to Vee, going quiet at "and then it got very late, and we went up," after fifty chapters of narrating exactly what a man's mouth did — that quiet told me everything I needed about chapter 51 landing on her. She can't perform that one.
