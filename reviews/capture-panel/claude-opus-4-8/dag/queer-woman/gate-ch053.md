Okay so this is the quiet chapter after two loud ones, and I clocked what it's doing about four lines in and still sat there getting worked on by it, which is the thing about this book that keeps making me mad.

He wants to meet her people. And the way he does it — the *inventory*. Meg and the boots a half size big. Kayla talking the paint off a wall. The heel coming up out of the right boot when she pushed off. He's got a shelf with her friends' names on it and he takes each one down one-handed without turning around from the sink. And I felt exactly what Vee felt, the warm-side-down thing, the *he was listening, he kept all of it* — and then I remembered who I'm reading. Because this is a man who has demonstrably, across fifty-two chapters, curated what reaches her and what doesn't. A man who withholds one word on principle and calls it grace. And now the book wants me to melt because he remembers the size of Meg's boots. He remembers everything. That's not the same as *no plan*. A man with a plan would also remember everything. The attentiveness that reads as love reads *exactly the same* as the attentiveness of someone running an operation, and the book knows that, and it's serving me the tender version with the sink and the lavender sweater so I'll forget I know it.

But here's what actually made me put the book down for a second. "Bring Cassie, too." *He* said it. Into the sink, no weight on it. And it lands in the middle of Vee's little three-top and she can't find a restaurant to put Cassie in — and she thinks *she'd watched somebody be fine about it before. She wasn't going to look at when.* God. That's the sharpest thing in the chapter and it's Vee doing it to herself. She won't look at Randi being fine about John, won't look at herself being fine, at "fine" the word all the other girls use. The chapter is stuffed with the word fine and she keeps sliding off it. She's protecting something by not looking.

And the roster keeps opening — Kayla, Meg, Meg's Danny, and then *Theo*. She volunteers Theo. A man who asked her out. And she over-explains it, "it's fine, it's nothing, we never even—" to a man who didn't ask, and Pace just says "Bring Theo" and never looks around. That's the tell I'm circling. He is completely, serenely un-jealous. He wants the whole world in the room. A normal besotted boyfriend has at least one twitch about the guy who asked her out. Pace has none, because — and this is what I'm suspicious of — a man assembling a *set* isn't threatened by any single piece of it. "Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one." No table. No ends. Everybody in one room, all her worlds, at the same time, and *he* picks the place. "Let me handle it." That last line went cold on me. She hands him the whole guest list of her actual life and he says let me handle it and there's a happiness in his face he won't let all the way out. He's not planning a party. He's planning a *field*.

And where is Randi in all this? Named for ten minutes, the one Vee tells everything to, and Pace's ask arrives *wearing Randi's name* — "It arrived as Randi" — and then he immediately widens it past her. The two architects are about to be in the same room, and Vee is the one setting the table for it, thrilled, because her two favorite people finally get to find out about each other. Except they already know about each other. They know everything about each other. That's the whole engine and she's the only one at the table who can't see it.

What I keep waiting for: I want Vee to look at *when*. I want the sentence she won't finish. And I noticed — no queer charge in this one at all, it's pure Pace-and-the-set, Randi held at arm's length as a name. Fine. The book's building a room. I want to see who's in it.
