REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where I have to admit two things at once and hold them both.

One: Helen Rivers can *write* a meal. The bright green thing and the smoke-dark thing, one to wake you and one to fold you under, the bread torn with care for the heat, Vee not watching herself eat for once — that got into my body. The hunger-under-the-hunger, the hollow that's been open since long before noon, the way the food goes down into something that was empty a lot longer than an afternoon. That's the good stuff, that's desire rendered from the inside, and Vee's is specific and physical and *hers*. The froyo cup that gets away from her because for once her mother isn't in her ear saying *that's plenty*. I felt that one.

Two: I know exactly what I'm watching, and so does the book, and that's the whole sick engine of it. Every single thing Pace does in this chapter I have already seen him *decide to do*. The "may I choose," the leaving-the-quiet-in-the-text so nothing needs her right this second, the not-telling-her-where, the not-arguing at the kiss line — I watched this man build a bench in his bedroom and edge a woman into begging in a voice she didn't know she had, and now I'm watching him deploy the exact same instrument on a twenty-one-year-old over baba ghanoush. So when Vee thinks *that's not a boy, that's a man,* through Cassie's mouth, I don't get to feel it as swoon. I feel it as the trap closing. The tenderness reads as technique because I *know* it's technique. That's the book being honest about what it's doing, and I have to give it that — it is not pretending Pace is a nice surprise. It's letting me fall for it and be nauseated at the same time, which is a genuinely mean trick and it works.

But here's my problem as *this* reader: this chapter is all him. This is the het courtship, front to back, and the wanting I actually came for — Vee's helpless flat *want* at the Chi Latte window, the thing that has nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her* — is nowhere in this chapter. This is the vehicle, not the cargo. And the vehicle is a man with a plan installing an appetite in a girl. That's the exact shape I said lands closest to home, the desire-done-to-her, engineered. The only thing keeping me in the chair is that the book seems to *know* that's the shape and isn't selling it to me as romance — it's building dread. Cassie is my lifeline here. Cassie's *Goodbye, Vee* two chapters ago, Cassie watching Randi's hands do their little coffee-cup theater, Cassie the one person who sees the look slide off her every day and can name exactly who it's landing on. Cassie is the reader-surrogate and she's already grieving. I trust Cassie.

The 1 a.m. text: *he picked her off a lawn like a produce item and she thinks the froyo means someone's finally kind to her and I want to reach into the book and get her out but I also need to know if the girl she actually wants ever gets to be real.*
