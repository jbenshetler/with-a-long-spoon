REACTION

Okay. No Randi in this one, and I felt the absence like a held breath — twenty-five chapters and the charge I've been waiting for is still parked at the curb where Randi left it. So going in, part of me was already tired: another whole evening built to make the man deep. And that's what this is, top to bottom. The fire, the quilt, the robe that smells like him, the cataloguing of Ohio and casseroles and the unopened saffron — it's lovely, it's warm, it's exactly cut to her, which is the whole problem with everything he does.

But then Daphne.

I'll be honest, that landed. Nineteen and fifteen. Two years. The tutor they *assigned* him, the older girl who was the soft middle of a boy shipped off from home too early into a building full of grown men. That's the ugly grown-up word Vee holds under her tongue and won't say, and I was saying it for her. And here's the thing that got its hook in me: the book knows. It knows exactly what it just did. "He handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck." That's the machine's own grammar turned around on its maker — the same move Pace runs on Vee, the same move Randi runs, the reframe where the thing done to you gets called the best thing that ever happened. And the book puts it right there where Vee can almost see it and I can see it completely. So now I've got an origin story for the method. He didn't invent "ask before you take, leave her the room to choose, collect the proof of her wanting." Daphne built that into him at fifteen the way he builds a dress onto Vee. He's running the thing that was run on him.

And I hate how well it works on me. Because that's the trap, isn't it — give me the wound, the desk in the hallway, the boy who loved the one shape of math no one else could see and got it made into a punishment, and suddenly the operator has a soft place and I'm leaning toward it. The scar under her palm, the "not tonight" that's a door with a time on it and not a wall — that's *good*, I felt the room go quiet, I felt Vee turn her hand over so their palms met over the thing he won't tell her yet. It's the most human he's ever been because for once he wasn't performing, he was *held*, going one degree flatter to keep the glass from going over the rim. I believe that flatness more than I've believed any of his tenderness.

What I'd text a friend: *the book just gave the seducer a grooming backstory and had the girl recognize it as grooming and not say the word — and I can't tell yet if that's the book being brave or the book buying him an alibi.* Because a wounded predator is still the shape I leave books over, and "he was made, he can't help it, be gentle with him" is one of the oldest passes ever written. But Vee didn't excuse him. She clocked it. "That he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck" — she keeps the word behind her teeth because it isn't hers to say, not because she bought it. That distinction is the only reason I'm not annoyed.

Still. Zero Randi. Zero of the charge in Vee's body as hers. Another beautiful chapter that went entirely to the man. The scale still has his thumb on it, and I'm counting.
