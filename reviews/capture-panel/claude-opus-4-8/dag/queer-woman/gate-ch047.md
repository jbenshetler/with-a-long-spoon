REACTION

Oh, that's the cruelest thing this book has done yet, and it did it by just laying the two chapters side by side and letting me hold them. I read Randi's fairytale last night — the sleigh, the venison, the perfect nothing asleep behind her, the cold she carried in and carried out — and now here's Vee in the twin bed her legs outgrew, under the little gold swimmers, doing the *exact same not-sleeping.* Both of them reaching for a phone. Both of them typing something and taking it back letter by letter. Both of them sending the warm demanding-nothing text and turning the phone facedown and then turning it back over to check it sent. The book put those two gestures in two different chapters and trusted me to feel the rhyme, and I did, in my chest. That's the thing I keep giving this book credit for — it knows exactly what it's doing and it doesn't underline it.

But here's what got me, what I'd actually text a friend about at 1 a.m.: the texts they send each other are *both lies of omission and both true.* Randi sends "it's honestly a fairytale out here" from a bed where she just felt nothing next to a faultless man. Vee reads it and envies the poisoned Gstaad — "even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had" — and I wanted to reach into the book and shake her, because I *saw* Randi's fairytale, I know it was ash in her mouth, I know she wrote "miss your face most though, gorgeous" as the one true line in a paragraph of set dressing. And Vee's line back, "i'm here if you want to talk. or not" — that's the open hand, the thing she folded away in the glass room, the MIRH thing she never once let on she saw. Two women lying tenderly to each other across an ocean and both of the lies are love. I only get to see that because I've got both beds. Neither of them does. That mirror I said I'd be carrying — the book just made it the whole architecture of the night.

And the wanting is still *hers.* That's what keeps me in the chair. The blonde she builds out of spite — "a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's" — and then the horror of it, the thing that actually made me put the book down for a second: she reaches for her own best night, the fra diavolo and the falling sheet, to get *warm* in it, and it turns while she's inside it. The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, the blonde takes her seat in the wine light, and *she can't keep her own night.* That's a real thing jealousy does and I've never seen it written that exactly — losing possession of your own memory in real time, watching someone else get installed into the frame where you were happy. That belongs to Vee. Nobody engineered that. That's just what it is to love someone with no name on it and lie awake with the door of them shut.

Which is the knife the whole chapter's built on: she has *no word.* Not the big one he won't give, not the small one she can't find. "How do I say what this is to Cassie? To my father?" She can't even test whether they're still together — "Maybe it was over. Maybe he thought nothing had happened at all." And Sheri comes back to knife her sideways: "She's alone," in November, folded away as pity, and now Christmas is a week she can't see into, and Vee builds a stranger to put at his stove. The stove is the tell — she knows his method is the same in the kitchen as in the dark, the building and the giving, the garlic drawing the hunger out before she'd call it hunger, and now she's imagining him running it on someone else. She's learned to read the machine too. She just reads it as loss instead of as the con it is.

What I'm sitting in: this is the low point of the ledger for both of them, deliberately, at the exact turn of the year, and the book is holding the two of them apart so it can slam them together when term starts. "She'd have Randi back the day the term started — the one sure thing the new year held." That's the door I've been watching. And Pace "came with none of that: no hour she could name." The book is lining up its own answer to the question I've asked for forty-seven chapters — and it's letting *Vee* line it up, in the dark, half-asleep: the one she can circle on a calendar is the woman. She thinks nothing after "and what about Pace." I noticed that nothing. So did the book.

Still no join I can find between hers-underneath and installed. This chapter fed me *hers* by the hardest route again — a jealousy that eats her own memory, a longing for Randi she frames as friendship and I read as the truer heat. I'm not out the door. I want the term to start as badly as she does, and I'm a little ashamed of how much.
