WHO'S WHO

Vee — twenty-one, in love, no name for the thing she's in, and this whole stretch she was more author than anyone gives her credit for. The sentence she built for weeks (crackers her whole life, and he fed her a meal) and offered across the quilt in the cold — her whole chest handed over in the only shape the rules let her use — and he didn't say it back. She staged her own walkout at his door (ch45), didn't cross the mat, made him arrive at the fact the hello wasn't coming, said the truest sentence in the book to his face: all of it's *in here, with the door shut*, and out there I have no name for you. She refused the hand that gets used in place of the word, and left. Then she cracked over the break, built a spite-blonde to put at his stove, lost her own best memory to it — and drove back out in the coat with nothing under it and made herself an offering. She got him back. Tonight she didn't need the word. That's the part that scares me.

Pace / Peter — the man, and ch48 finally sat me down inside his head and still would not show me the one door. Alone in a diner, given hell by Sheri, unwatched, unable to perform — and "I can't" has *nothing* behind it. No wound surfacing, no plan. The tell is line one of that chapter: he decided before the door shut that a thing "could keep," "long practice at deciding a thing could keep." The withholding isn't a wound, it's a discipline. He decides what people are allowed to know and when. He answered her *I love you* with "I love how good we are together" — a sentence about the machine. At the door he folded his arms and held the line at cost and said nothing. And then she showed up bare under a coat and the wall was "simply gone," and he went to his knees, and still — still — never the word. "Believe what I do." "Words are cheap." She got him back and he paid in pancakes and a cracked voice and her name, never the word.

Randi — the book finally spent her (ch46) and I'm furious about how it worked. Gstaad: the perfect man John, the sleigh, the truffle, and it meant nothing — "a good sandwich eaten fast," cold everywhere she wasn't touching him, "nothing had happened to her eyes." She's not a con artist, she's a coward in love who arranged her whole life so she'd never have to be brave, and the two people who reach her are Pace (who terrifies her) and Vee (who doesn't know). "Vee was her gorgeous." And she has *decided* — not to tell, to keep the brunches exactly as they are, let Vee arrive at it on her own, "Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways." That's the shape I'm allergic to, in her own voice — except it's paralysis, not scheme. Then ch49: she ran the whole machine on a sobbing red-eye Vee, reframed everything true, built the coat plan, made her say *slutty* and then called it the bravest thing a woman can do, picked the heels — and steered her right back into Pace's bed. She keeps a plain white-gold bracelet locked on her wrist and out of the light. Also load-bearing: *"Pace made her say it, out loud, every time."* She's been taken apart by his exact method. These two aren't schemers running a mark — they're the same wound facing opposite directions.

Cassie — reduced to one syllable. "Hmm," and back to her book, ch50, because Vee was too happy to explain. Earlier (ch41): "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" and Vee's "Both. Probably both" — the most honest she's been — and Cassie didn't push, and Vee was grateful and a little *not.* My last calibrated instrument, going quiet one un-said thing at a time.

Sheri — the gift I didn't expect. Five feet, two-tone grown-out perm, too much makeup worn like a dare, eats the double before he touches his. Queer — Paige, the bird prints, the loud seven-at-the-table family, gone because Paige couldn't get right with Sheri not speaking to her people. Grief named flat. She's aimed at Pace, tells him "not saying it matters more to you than having her," "from this seat that feels pretty shitty." The one thing I don't trust: the book used her — a queer woman I'd just learned to like — to hand Pace his good-faith certificate. "Most men'd just lie." "I'm not them." "I know you're not." That's borrowed credibility and I didn't hand it over.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

The POV chapter didn't redeem him, it convicted him: the withholding is muscle, not wound. He got her back by outlasting her and the book let him call it grace. No.

Randi — the hate is gone and I resent that it's gone. She's cold, in love, cowardly, grieving, and she just steered the girl she wants back into a man's bed at her own cost — which is either the plan needing Vee bonded to Pace, or love handing someone back her happiness, and it's both, and the bill is going to be enormous. The bracelet kept dark on purpose is the truest thing I know about her. She owes Vee the truth twice over (MIRH plus this) and won't pay it because she can't picture Vee's face.

Cassie — dread only now. She's being worn down to a syllable and Vee keeps choosing the happy silence.

Sheri — real affection, one flag up. Best new person in twenty chapters, and the book already tried to spend her on Pace's behalf.

WHAT'S FADING

The words go, the feeling stays. The Gstaad fairytale is already just *John, perfect, meant nothing, cold everywhere she wasn't touching him* — the sleigh and the truffle are dissolving into one glittering nothing, which is exactly what they were. The break's two cold beds are collapsing into a single shape: two women, two phones, tender lies, only I saw both. The exact sequence of the doorstep — mirror, then car, then cop, then coat — is softening to *she wound herself up and refused to finish and carried it to him unspent.* The Chili's/Rusty Nail mechanics are going; what stays is *he ruined ordinary food for her and called it love, and she laundered her own memory to forgive him.* What stays hard and bright: the sewn dress, the failed shirt, the refused hand at the door, "nothing had happened to her eyes," the bracelet kept dark, and the doubled mirror — two women keeping the identical secret from each other, and I'm still the only one in the room for both. That's the thing I'll have next time.
