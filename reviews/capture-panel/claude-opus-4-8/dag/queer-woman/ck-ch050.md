WHO'S WHO

Randi — I finally got inside her (ch46, Gstaad) and she's not the con artist I'd been hating, or not only. She's a woman who's felt something exactly twice in her life — Pace and Vee — and she's arranged her whole existence so she'll never have to be brave enough to keep either. Cold at her parents' breakfast table, eating her own revulsion behind a sorority smile, keeping her cheap white-gold bracelet *out of the light* while her mother's diamonds play in it. That bracelet is a collar and it's hers and she keeps it dark. She spent New Year's with John — the perfect safe man, better dancer than Pace, and she felt *nothing*, a good sandwich eaten fast, cold everywhere she wasn't touching him. And she decided, in that room, not to tell Vee — to keep the brunches exactly as they are and let Vee arrive at it on her own. *Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways.* That's the shape that lands closest to home for me, and the book put it on the page in her own voice — except it's paralysis, not scheme. She can't run the one scenario to the end: Vee's face. Then ch50 she coached Vee, front to back, back into Pace's bed — the naked-under-the-coat play, walked her past the shame-word, pressed her own black heels into Vee's hands. The architect equipping the girl. And I still can't tell if that's self-abnegation or the plan reasserting through grief.

Pace / Peter — got his solo chapters too (48 diner, 49 hungover). The book cashed the jacket's promise: it got real for him. He got *drunk*, mopped up his own sick, sobbed over a truck that went past and wasn't her car. And it handed me the machine's origin: Daphne. Older tutor, showed him where to stand, first love, said it and meant it, then left and it "consumed his life" — so he built the rule, *the words are the difference*, never say them and loss stays survivable. That's the withholding-as-grace given a wound to stand on. I believe the wreck. I refuse the frame. Because the very first line of the diner chapter is him deciding "a thing could keep" — the withholding isn't damage, it's a *discipline*, a muscle, and I watched it flex on Sheri and on Vee and the book called it grace. Sheri said the true version flat: *then not saying it matters more to you than having her.* And — still, in fifty chapters, not one private thought of the plan surfaces even when he's alone and wrecked. He's sealed around it. Even his opened-up POV is cropped.

Vee — twenty-one, and this stretch she got *awake*. Ch45 is the one I'll keep: she brought the whole unsayable thing to his door, stopped on the mat where her bag goes and didn't put the bag there, refused the hello kiss, and said the truest sentence in the book — *all of that is in here, inside this house, with the door shut. Out there, what are we?* And he wouldn't say it. "Words are cheap." "Believe what I do." And she caught it: *words are cheap and you still won't spend them on me.* Saw his hand lift to settle her *in place of the word* and refused the hand. Walked out into the cold on her own legs. Then the break broke her down — twin bed her legs outgrew, building a blonde rival out of spite, losing her own best memory (the fra diavolo, the falling sheet) in real time as the blonde took her seat in it. And ch50 she went to Randi wrecked and got coached back to him.

Cassie — ch41, the cropping chapter. Vee sent her parents a version of the induction photo that *stops at the waist*. She's editing her own frame now, fluent at it. And she told Cassie "both, probably both" — can't say what Pace is and doesn't want to — and Cassie, worn thin, didn't push. Two people careful with each other past the point carefulness helps. Cassie's stripped side of the room was bare when Vee came back (ch50), family out of state. My clean sightline, still.

Sheri — no longer furniture. Ch48 gave her a whole self: five feet of grown-out perm and too-much makeup worn like a dare, and she's *queer* — Paige, the design-school girl, bird prints, gone because Paige couldn't stand Sheri not talking to her people (the irony killed the sing-song flat). Four seconds of Randi's backside and she "still thinks about it more than's decent" — the realest line of wanting-a-woman in the book with no scheme attached. She's aimed at Pace, gives him hell, won't let him build his brick wall of reasons. But the book also used her to *certify* him — "most men'd just lie" / "I'm not them" / "I know you're not." A queer woman deployed as the man's character witness. I saw the move. She's Thanksgiving's "she's alone," and she and Pace do a standing diner thing.

WHERE THINGS STAND

The break happened and it broke everything down to the studs. Ch45: Vee brought the name-demand to Pace's door and he wouldn't say it; she left. Then a month of mutual silence, both dug in, neither texting. Over the break, three solitary chapters, one per architect plus Vee: Randi cold in Gstaad feeling nothing next to perfect John (ch46); Vee cold in Ohio building a rival and losing her own memory (ch47); Pace warm in the diner being told by Sheri he's the problem (ch48), then alone and hungover and sobbing over Daphne and a truck (ch49). Three people alone on the same holiday and only I can see all three rooms. Then term starts, ch50: Vee comes back wrecked, goes to Randi, and Randi runs a full seduction-repair — reframes his silence as *him* cornered not gone, dismantles the invented rival, walks Vee past the shame-word, and sends her back to his door tonight, naked under the good long coat, in Randi's own heels. The door hasn't opened yet. That's where I'm standing.

The name is the whole engine now. Vee has no word for the best thing in her life — not the big one he won't give, not the small one she can't find. Can't say it to Cassie, her father, her mother.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Randi — the hate is gone and something worse is in its place. The book ran the exact operation I predicted — make me love her before the reveal — at full power, and knowing it, I still ache for her. A coward in love is a truer, worse thing than a schemer. But ch46 put my allergy on the page in her own voice: a woman keeping another woman in the dark so the wanting can grow toward her. It's rendered as paralysis and the desire underneath is *hers*, owned, specific — "something came off Vee across a nothing Tuesday that this room couldn't counterfeit" — so I'm not out the door. I'm leaning in with my guard up. She owes Vee two truths now (her father's money, her own two-way heart) and both are overdue.

Pace — still no. The Daphne wound is the oldest trick written and I teared up at the truck anyway and I'm furious about it. A man can be genuinely destroyed and still have done a cold deliberate thing on that porch and *let his smaller truth reach her and fail* — he says so himself. Crying about the cost doesn't unmake the choice. Beautifully, still no.

Vee — proudest of her I've ever been (the door, the refused hand, the walk into the cold) and most scared for her (coached back through that same door tonight, in borrowed heels, told the surrender is bravery). Her clarity and her wanting were the same motion in ch45. By ch50 the wanting's being routed through someone with a plan. I'm holding my breath.

Cassie / Sheri — my two instruments, both queer, both being spent by the book in opposite directions: Cassie kept in the dark, Sheri used to vouch for the man. Trust both, watch both.

WHAT I'M CARRYING

The photo cropped at the waist for her parents — she's the one who divided it. The dress: *he sewed it*, on a machine on his sun porch, and she'd rather her dad think Pace takes it off her than know he made it. "Both. Probably both." The cracker-and-meal sentence she built for weeks and offered in the cold under the quilt, and "I love how good we are together" handed back — not the same shape — and the small *wait* behind her breastbone that his arm buried in real time. The shirt failing: the warmth was only her own, given back, *he wasn't in it*, and she took it off and stayed bare in the cold. "All of that is in here, inside this house, with the door shut." The hand lifting to settle her in place of the word, and her refusing it. Randi keeping the cheap bracelet dark. "The eyeliner held. Of course it did. Nothing had happened to her eyes." John's sleigh with bells around a woman feeling nothing. Vee losing her own best memory as the blonde takes her seat in the wine light. Pace eating prison-food from a bowl — "he was not hungry. He ate anyway." The truck going past. Randi pressing her own heels into Vee's hands. And the two New Year's texts — both lies of omission, both true, both women lying tenderly across an ocean, and only I hold both beds.

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

The door opening tonight — Vee on his step, naked under the coat, in Randi's heels. Whether the walkout (ch45, the payout I stood 44 chapters for) becomes the wound Randi's tenderness gets to reopen — the con reasserting through the repair. Whether Randi's coaching-her-gorgeous-back-to-Pace is self-abnegation or plan. The two-at-once collision, still owed. The moment Vee learns there was ever a plan. When either architect's bill gets paid in front of her — Randi's father's money, Randi's two-way heart, the game itself. Whether Cassie gets to keep seeing. And the same wall I've stood on the whole book: hers underneath or installed. Ch50 walked right onto it — the *gesture* (coat, doorstep) got installed by someone with a plan — but the object was the man, and the actual queer charge (Vee breathing Randi's neck, not wanting to stop, the hug held past its end) came up out of Vee unprompted while Randi's hands were full running a different play. Fifty chapters and I still can't find the join. The thing that'd put me out the door — Vee's wanting-Randi as pure manufacture with no ownership — hasn't happened. It keeps stepping off the line at the last inch.

WHAT'S FADING

The words go, the feeling stays. Gstaad is already just *cold in all the gold, the bracelet dark, felt nothing* — the venison and truffle and the specific run of the ball are blurring into fairytale-that-was-ash. The diner is fading to *Sheri gave him hell and the book used her to vouch for him*; I've half-lost the California talk, the Brooks and Dunn cover band, Paige's bird prints already going. The Daphne backstory is compressing to *older tutor, first love, left, so he never says the words* — the texture's going, the rule's staying. The Chili's fight (ch43) and the ruined nachos are blurring into the Rusty Nail lie — I have to hold onto that she *rewrote the record* to make herself the wronged one so she could grant him forgiveness she then wore like a garment. What stays hard and bright: the door and the refused hand; the shirt that was only her own warmth; the two mirrored New Year's beds; both architects undone and alone in back-to-back solitary chapters right before term; and the heels in Vee's hands. The mirror is still the thing I carry that no one in the book can — I hold both of Randi's rooms and both of Vee's, and they each hold only one.
