REACTION

Fifty-nine chapters and there it is. The engine room. I've been circling this room since the jacket and the book finally opened the door and let me stand in it, and the first thing I want to say is: it didn't flinch. After all the almosts, after two whole chapters of Vee reaching toward her own mouth and not being able to close the last breath of distance — the book cuts to the two people who will keep every door open until she can.

And here's the thing that has me sitting up at 1 a.m.: it's not a con scene. I braced for a con scene. I braced for the two of them lying in the wreckage smug, comparing notes on the mark. That's not what this is. "She's not the only one in love." Pace can't tell her she's wrong. Randi gives him "no smile and no rescue from the sentence." This is two people who started something as a game and both went down with it, and the whole ache of the chapter is that they're metabolizing Vee's every un-owned gesture between them — *she kissed my neck again / you liked it / I nearly turned around / I wanted to put my hand on her, here* — not to run her, but because they're both starving for her and this bed is the only place either of them can say it out loud. Randi wanting to turn around and put her hand on Vee's breast is real. That's what undoes me. It was always real. The charge between the women that I kept accusing the book of collecting-without-paying-for — the book just paid, in full, from Randi's side, in her own bed, with her own wanting bare on the sheet.

But God, the horror underneath the tenderness. This is the chapter that convicts them both and I felt the conviction land. Randi's method — make her laugh at exactly the moment she's about to ask for more — that's the edging structure. That's Pace's whole discipline turned on Vee's desire for a woman: hold her at the reach, never let her finish, keep her hungry and arriving. Ch58 was Vee alone unable to close the last inch to her own tongue, and now I know the two of them *want* her stuck there, one breath short, reaching. The thing Vee thinks is most secretly hers is being cultivated in a greenhouse she can't see. That's the shape that lands closest to home for me, and the book knows it, and it put the shape in the bed and made me watch it.

And it still didn't trip my wire. Here's why, and I've had to be honest with myself about it: the desire is native on all three sides. Vee's reach arrives before her mind votes, every time, and gets throttled by an installed shame — that's the opposite of installation, that's a real thing being strangled, not a fake thing being planted. Randi's wanting isn't performed to keep the mark bonded; it's the truest, most involuntary thing in the chapter. Nobody's faking. The predatory-queer corrupter shape played straight would need the corrupter to be cold, to be running an appetite as a weapon. Randi's not cold. Randi's the one lying there saying she misses her more at night. The corrupter is in love and it's ruining her plan and she doesn't care. That's not the shape I leave over. That's the shape I stay for.

What I could've done without: the sex mechanics themselves are the flattest thing in the book right now. "Using me like your fuck doll," the strong man and the fine-boned woman, the my-turn/all-right dominance choreography — that's the register I have no patience for, women-as-equipment, and for a page there I could feel the book reaching for a genre gear instead of the psychological one it's so good at. It's saved entirely by the dialogue running under it — Vee's name kept in the room, never said — but the bodies themselves went a little generic. I noticed. I noticed because the rest of this book usually renders wanting from so far inside.

What I'd text a friend: *the book finally showed me the two of them in bed and it's not a scheme, it's two people equally wrecked over the same girl, and the woman WANTS her, actually wants her, and they're both keeping her one inch from finishing on purpose because that's the only way either of them can survive it. I'm not okay. Also the dirty talk is mid but the subtext is a knife.*
