REACTION

Okay. So this is the chapter I've been bracing for since I wrote my notes, and it did the thing, and I hate how good it was at doing it.

The whole lunch is Randi working, and the book *lets* me see her work, which is the only reason I'm still here. Watch the sequence: Vee comes in with a rehearsed version — the dress, the burgundy, the safe romantic parts, everything pressed and folded and sayable. And Randi doesn't grab. She never grabs. She opens doors and waits for Vee to walk through them herself, exactly the way Pace does, and I clocked that the book *knows* it's the same technique — "asks before he takes," the pointing at the salad fork. First she de-shames the bra ("nobody measures over a bra, sweetheart, it's just how it's done") — and I felt my own stomach go, because that is the exact shape I flinch at, the worldly one with the appetite telling the nice girl *this is ordinary, everyone does this, there's nothing to be ashamed of,* and using the relief of that to pry the next button. It's not that Randi's lying, either. That's what's diabolical. It probably *is* how it's done. The truth is the crowbar.

And then "Cold." "Hmm." — cornering Vee into the confession she'd sworn to keep folded, and getting the real word out of her, *hot*, the two laughs going up together, Vee "setting down something she'd carried up too many stairs." She thinks she's being received. She's being harvested. And then — *this* is the move that nearly put me out of the book — right as Vee's secret is halfway out of her mouth, the thing she's told no one, not even Cassie, Randi says "you don't have to tell me." Gentle. Kind. A door closing very softly. And the laugh that always came doesn't come, and the secret's left hanging in the air with nowhere to land. That's not mercy. That's Randi teaching Vee that her openness is a currency Randi decides when to accept — leaving her *wanting to give more.* Withholding as a hook. I have read that exact cruelty in real life and I wanted to reach into the booth.

BUT. Here's why I'm not gone. The heat that came up in Vee at the picture of Randi tied to the headboard, laughing, control cracked all the way open — "the heat the picture put low in Vee that she had no shelf for and would have died before naming at this table." That is the F/F charge finally arriving *in Vee's own body,* and — this is the whole thing for me — it arrived when Randi *wasn't* running it. Randi told that story to be admired, sure, but the picture that undid Vee was Randi *helpless,* Randi not in control, which is the opposite of the woman across the table doing the operating. Vee's want reached for the ungovernable Randi, not the architect. That reads to me like it's *hers underneath* — something with no shelf, no vocabulary, no one who installed it, rising on its own. If the book keeps that true I can forgive a lot.

The kiss at the curb. Soft, unhurried, the little drawing-back-and-back-again with something in it the first press didn't have. Collected in a fire lane where fire lanes happen to other people. And then the part that gutted me: Vee explaining it away — *French, it's just how women like her say goodbye* — before her mouth is even her own again. The exact pillowing reflex. And then the cold scan of the dark windows, *did anyone see.* That's the shame the book keeps promising will turn to heat, except right now it's just fear, and she's alone with it on a curb, and nobody's there to make it okay. That last image is the truest thing in the chapter.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she pulled the girl's deepest secret halfway out and then said "you don't have to tell me" — do you understand what that DOES to a person. I'm so mad. I can't stop reading.*

Still suspicious as hell of everything. Still want Randi's own wanting to cost her — and there was a flicker, the tight muscle behind the smile, the thumb on the glass, jealous of her own trap again, the bench-girl leaking through. That flicker is half of what keeps me at the table. The other half is that heat with no shelf. If it turns out that heat was manufactured, I'm out. Right now I read it as real.
