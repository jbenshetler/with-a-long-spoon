REACTION

Oh, this one's a relief and also it made my chest hurt. After six chapters of two people running a con on a twenty-one-year-old — the apple slices, the pointing game, the *may I call you* — this is just Vee and Cassie at a pool, and nobody wants anything from anyone, and I could feel my whole body unclench the same way Vee's does when she goes under.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. The book has been so careful about her — "the look that slides off Cassie every day of her life," the flat *Goodbye, Vee*, and now here it is out loud, low, just for the two of them: "They will never know the love of a woman." That's the tell, and I love that it's dropped as a throwaway, a joke Cassie makes into the middle distance, not a Reveal with a capital R. This is the queer woman in this book. And notice what she is — she's the one person on the page who has never once tried to install a want in Vee, never steered her, never lightly-laughing-as-if-by-accident. She wades to her ribs and stops. She keeps the joke *quiet* when she could pitch it into the bleachers. She never asks, "because asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger." That's love rendered as restraint, and it's the exact inverse of everything Randi does. The book knows that. The book is putting them side by side and trusting me to see it.

Which is the thing I'm turning over at 1 a.m. Because I've read the version of this where the good desireless queer best friend exists purely to make the predatory queer legible by contrast — Cassie as moral furniture, the one who Feels Correctly so we know Randi is the wolf. I don't *think* that's what this is yet, because Cassie has too much specific body in her — the navy suit older than the friendship, the locker key on the stretchy wristband, taking the wave full in the chest without changing expression. She reads like a person, not a foil. But I'm watching. If Cassie's whole function turns out to be to pine and protect and never get her own wanting honored, I'll be mad about it.

The bleacher scene got me too. "You won't need floaties with *those*" and "legs for days" and Vee taking fifty yards of it with her chin level and her game face on, and then breaking on Cassie's one dumb loyal word — *tossers* — because it was hers, kept for her. The way this book does shame is genuinely good; it's not lurid, it's just true. Vee has spent her whole life with her mother's voice metering out how much of her the world gets. And the water is the one place the metering stops — "she had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." That line's going to stay with me.

I notice I'm reading this chapter almost *against* the plot — like, please, let Vee keep this one thing that isn't part of the design. Cassie is the only relationship in the book that isn't an operation, and I'm now quietly terrified the book is going to make me watch Randi and Pace's machine cost Vee this exact friendship. That *Goodbye, Vee* is still ringing.

It's a quiet chapter and it pulled me more than the froyo did, honestly, because it's the first time the book let a woman be tender toward Vee with nothing under it.
