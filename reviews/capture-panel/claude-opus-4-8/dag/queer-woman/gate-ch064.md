So this is what the wax was for. Three chapters of setup — the "yes" that jumped out ahead of her mind in his kitchen, the roll-over horror at the salon, the smooth new skin she couldn't stop touching — and it all lands here, and I want to be honest about what it did and didn't do to me.

Craft-wise it's the most sustained set piece the book's given me in a while, and I can see every joint of it working. The feeding thread is genuinely lovely — walnut, parsley, grape, cashew, cantaloupe, cherry, each color arriving at her lips before she's allowed to see the picture, so she's building the painting blind out of taste. That's a real idea, executed. The black cherry over her sex and her hoarse "a bit on the nose?" made me actually laugh. And the orange breast-print she smears on his face on purpose and he *wears it the whole scene* — that's the one place in this chapter he lets her author something back, and it's the best beat in it.

But here's my flat truth: this is the house style at full length and it is entirely, wall-to-wall Pace. The whole grammar I've been clocking for sixty chapters is here again in its purest form — *still means the brush, moving means the brush leaves.* That's a training scene wearing a painting smock. He conditions her to hold open by taking the pleasure away every time her body does the honest thing, until she "gives up" making the choice and the relief of not-choosing "broke something loose inside her." The book keeps handing me surrender-as-release and calling it the good part. I know it knows what it's doing — "the choice was his, and the relief of no longer making the choice" is the jacket thesis said plain — but knowing doesn't make it *land* on me, because there's no woman on the other side of the glass here. It's the man's composition, start to finish. "Her body was the art. His paint only said where to look." She's literally the canvas. The book celebrates her abundance, fine, but she's still the object being celebrated *at*, by him, for him.

The two things I'll actually carry: the gesso. He paints her primer coat in her own wetness, gathering it off her with the brush and laying it back down, and it's *so much* it runs past his picture, and the shame-flood and the answering pulse — that's the ch59 thread (she can't bring her own taste to her own mouth) turned inside out. He's the one handling her taste; she's kept from it again. And then the very end, when she finally gets his mouth and reaches up to kiss him and *searches* — "salt first... something faintly sour under it, gone when her tongue sought it" — that's her hunting for herself on his lips and not being able to pin it, and letting the question "disappear into the wave." Sixty-four chapters and she still can't get a clean taste of her own body; it always has to come routed through him. That's the thread I'm here for and it's the only place this chapter touched me under the ribs.

What's missing is Randi. Completely. One glancing salon-referral memory in 63 and then nothing — this whole stretch (61, 62, 63, 64) has been the Pace show, and 62 in particular worked *hard* to make me pity him: the one-friend confession, the Daphne-as-lonely-boy reframe, the water by the bed, the bread. I don't fully buy it and I resent being courted by it, but I clocked that the book is spending real chapters warming me back toward the man right before it presumably detonates the plan. Sheri's good, by the way — genuinely good, the one queer woman so far the book let *live* instead of deploy as an oracle, the taproom look that was just *enjoying* Vee. If the book had more of her and less brushwork I'd be happier.

I'm not turned off enough to leave. But I'm restless. I've been promised the plan blowing up and a woman wanting a woman with room, and I just read four chapters of the man being tender and in control while the thing I care about sat offstage.
