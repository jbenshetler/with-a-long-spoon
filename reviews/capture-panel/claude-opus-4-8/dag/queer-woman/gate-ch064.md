REACTION

Okay — first thing, she topped. She walked in the door and put him on the bed and went down on him before he could run his evening, and she did it *on purpose*, to get there first, to hand him his hurry and keep her own. That's the most agency Vee's driven with in a long time and I felt it. "It was the gladdest she had ever been on a floor." Good. That's hers, the pride is hers, nobody installed the wanting to wreck him with her mouth. And she goes past the scar — the one he covered her hand off of, months back — kisses it once, asks it nothing, moves on. She's the one extending the tenderness there, not receiving it. I liked her a lot in the first three pages.

And then he takes it back. Because of course the second she's naked and desperate it becomes the *game* — hands on the rail, keep still, move and the brush goes away, hold still and it comes back. Which. I have now read this exact grammar so many times I could paint it myself. Move = punishment, surrender = reward, the body trained in real time to give up its own agency as the price of pleasure. It's dressed up gorgeously — the grapevine, the fruit fed mouth-to-mouth between strokes, the cantaloupes, it's a genuinely pretty conceit and the walnut-then-champagne detail is lovely — but underneath the still-life it's the same conditioning machine running that's been running since the pancakes and the heels. "The relief of no longer making the choice broke something loose inside her." That sentence is the whole book's thesis and the book keeps making me swallow it as ecstasy. I'm not un-turned-on. I'm just tired of the one gear.

But then — the gesso. He paints her with her own wetness and he *does not kiss her mouth*. Every other color came with his mouth and a taste; this one comes with nothing, deliberately, and she notices the absence before she can name it, and she wonders what she'd taste like, and "the question had nowhere to go. She did not give it words." That's ch59. That's the two fingers a breath from her own mouth she couldn't close. And now it's not just her shame withholding it — it's *him*, engineering the one flavor she's never been allowed to have and pointedly keeping it off her tongue. Then at the very end, when he's inside her and she finally reaches for his mouth, there's "a trace" — salt, something sour, gone when she chased it, blurred into champagne and him until she can't isolate it. He let her almost have it and then dissolved it. The book knows exactly what it's doing with that. It's the most precise cruelty in the chapter and it's disguised as the tenderest kiss.

And the coda. God, the coda. He makes the photograph and *gives it back to her and keeps nothing* — doesn't ask for a copy, doesn't reach for his own phone — which after sixty-some chapters of a man who keeps everyone's file lands as either the one thing he doesn't take or the smartest thing he's ever not-taken, and I genuinely can't tell which and that's the good kind of not-being-able-to-tell. But the part that had me sitting up: she lies there in the dark and tries to hear *Randi's* voice on the picture. Not his. Randi's. She replays "Showing him your toes, eh?" — that low hoarse thing Randi does — and she reaches for what Randi would say about *this* one and it won't come, and she realizes she's been holding her breath waiting on it. Her pleasure isn't complete until she's imagined narrating it to Randi. That's the tell I've been tracking the whole read, and it's completely unprompted, it's just *there* in her body in the dark. And then she slams the drawer: "Not that she'd ever show her. God, no. This one was too much." "She would not be telling her about this." The stone. The rows of everything not invited. She's building the untold thing brick by brick and the untold thing is always, always about Randi.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she painted him with her own mouth to win, then lay in his bed afterward trying to hear another woman's voice on the photo. the book will not let her taste herself and it will not let her call Randi and it knows exactly which one is the real hunger.*
