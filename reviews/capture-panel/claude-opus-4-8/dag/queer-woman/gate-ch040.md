REACTION

Okay so this is the exhale chapter, right after the MIRH bomb — the book sat me down in the trees and made me a pizza. And I know what it's doing. It just watched Vee catch her friend's whole rotten inheritance sitting in gray government print, and instead of following *that* thread it cut straight back to the house in the woods and the long slow undress in the hallway. Deliberate. Give the reader the sugar so she doesn't chew on the thing she saw.

And here's what's honest about my own response: the undressing-down-the-hall bit worked on me anyway. The jeans that "had been sensible in the car and were the enemy now," the one-legged hopping, no dignity anywhere, "she'd have burned the house down for him" — that's her wanting, it's specific and physical and clumsy and *hers*, and clumsy is what makes it real instead of choreographed. Same with her body publishing the number she'd tried to keep private, going up too fast after five nights and lying there with her face hot because her own throat told on her. That's the thing I stay for — desire from the inside, arriving before permission. Fine. The book can still do it.

But I've read this man's tenderness eleven times now and I can feel the machine humming under every soft thing he does. The five-nights business is the tell of the whole chapter. She pulls back for school, and he *doesn't fight for it* — "some unreasonable corner of her had wanted him to fight" — and the not-fighting "cost her more than a fight would have." That is the method in one clean move: never make the demand, make her supply it, let the grace be the hook. He opens the door and waits on the far side for her to walk back through, and she lasted five nights. I don't read that as love. I read it as a man who knows exactly how long the line needs to be.

And now *Vee's* the one installing the rituals. She's got her drawer; now she washes his shirt so it'll go stale, so she can hand it back and make him refill it — she's built her own version of the scrunchie-drawer, running the same grammar on him. The shirt is genuinely the one thing in the chapter that got under my guard clean: "it went to nothing, and one day last week it didn't smell like anything at all." A woman breathing a dying shirt every night she's not there. That's real and a little unbearable, and it's hers, unasked. I'll give it that.

What I *clocked*, though, cold: that table. Twice now — "the long, unusually proportioned table that stood in the middle of his bedroom," and again "the long, unusually proportioned table." A bedroom is not where a table lives, and the book has flagged it like a rifle on the wall two chapters running. She threw her thong onto it and didn't look. I'm looking. That's a plan-shaped object and I don't trust it.

But mostly: this is a chapter with no Randi in it and no F/F charge in it at all, one week after the book finally let something *land* on Randi — the MIRH reveal, her face going still, "cheating bastards" ragged and aimed too near. I wanted to stay in that. Instead I got the het idyll at full tenderness and full technique. It's competent, it's warm, it's Vee's, and it gave me almost nothing of the thing I'm actually here waiting to watch fall to one side. The book knows it can spend a whole page on Pace-worship and I'll still turn it because 39 left a hook in me. It's right. Barely.
