WHO'S WHO

Randi — the one I started inside, though I didn't know it at first. Face-down on a bench in chapter one, begging in a voice that wasn't her performing voice, and then the mirror: the girl who decided, quiet as a deadbolt, that the man who saw her was going to get the face and nothing else, *not ever*. That's the architect. Glamorous, near the top of her sorority house, blue eyes she built her whole self around being looked at for, an appetite trimmed down to apple slices. She and Pace are running a game on a third girl. She is also, clearly, paying for it — she can't drink her coffee while she watches him work. Jealous of her own trap.

Pace — the man. Mathematician, finishing a PhD, lives off six-fourteen past where it goes to gravel, builds his own furniture and his own benches. Courtly, exact, never a wrong move — slices the apple thin because she eats it thin, leaves the water bottle because she always drinks more than she expects, gives back the pencil at the exact line she can solve it herself, says *may I*. Narrates his own coercion as generosity and thinks he's *freeing* women. The most skin-crawling thing in the book precisely because he's tender. There's a Daphne in his past whose door he didn't respect; he learned restraint from it.

Vee — Vivienne Thorne, twenty-one, econ, the mark. The redhead who threw her head back laughing like she'd forgotten she could be seen. Hungry in a way nobody's fed — mother's voice metering out how much of her the world gets, cardigan over the better shirt on purpose. The one I'm scared for. Her wanting is real and specific and *hers*, which is the whole horror.

Cassie — Vee's real friend. Quiet, four words a day, navy suit older than the friendship, takes the wave full in the chest without blinking. Queer ("they will never know the love of a woman," dropped sideways). The one person not running a game on Vee. My proxy. My lifeline.

Brooke — chapter president, warmth-as-surveillance, doesn't stand up because she doesn't have to. Read Vee top to bottom and stayed warm the whole way. A threat Randi "rescued" Vee from while also being the delivery system.

Vee — protective, aching. I like her. I want to reach in and get her out, and I also need her wanting to stay hers.

Cassie — trust, completely. The only clean warmth in the book. Terrified of what the machine costs her.
