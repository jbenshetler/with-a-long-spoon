REACTION

Okay. So this was the hiking date, and I read the whole thing waiting for the floor to open, and the floor did not open. It was a man putting moleskin on a blister and making horseradish sandwiches and folding cherry pits into wax paper. That's the chapter. That's the whole chapter.

And here's what's making me crazy: I can SEE the machine. I can see it perfectly. The blister he "didn't think to ask about" so he gets to kneel and take her sock off and hold her bare foot in the cold — that's a move, I know it's a move, it's the boot version of ordering her dinner. The dress. God, the dress. She points at the russet silk in the magazine like the deer she is, puts it back on the shelf, does the whole self-denial thing in her mother's voice — and he just says "that sounds like a beautiful dress" and lets it sit there. And I KNOW that dress is going to show up. That dress is a receipt. He filed it. That's the coldest thing in the chapter and it's the only thing that gave me a flicker, because it's the program still running underneath the picnic.

But a flicker in eleven chapters is not enough. The flannel-shirt-in-the-truck thing is where the book wants me to melt — he gives her his only dry shirt AND stands out in the rain with his back turned so she can be alone, both, the double sacrifice, and she calls him back and unbuttons her shirt keeping his eyes the whole time. And I'll give it this: her doing it, her running her OWN mother's voice as the turn-on, "wanted to be the very thing the voice said" — that's the actual thesis of the book, the yes farmed out of her, and it's the closest the present tense has come to the ch.1 heat. Her thighs pressing on their own. Fine. That worked a little in my body.

But then he's SHIVERING. He's cold. She stops the whole seduction to take care of HIM. And I get it, I get that it's another turn of the screw — his restraint is the bait, the not-touching is the weapon, he "didn't lean in, didn't lift a hand," the gladness is the whole answer. I've been told this. My own notes say the "may I" IS the weapon. But knowing the theory and feeling the danger are two different organs and only one of them is being fed. Where is the knife? A man asks permission, gives her his shirt, stands in the rain, and shivers. That is a Hallmark movie with better prose. The cop showing up and squawking the siren and taking her sentence away — cute, a little ominous, no last name no plate no anything, he peels off with the law behind him — but it's ominous the way everything here is ominous, in the FUTURE tense, in the reader's head, never on her skin.

I miss Randi. Randi's not even in this chapter and she's the only pulse I've got. There is no Randi, no Cassie, no bench, no teeth. It's a beautiful, competent, seductive chapter about nothing happening slowly, which is precisely and exactly the thing that turns me off, and it's the tenth one in a row.

I'm at the end of my patience. The book keeps promising it's a spider and keeps showing me a picnic. One more sugar chapter and I'm out. The dress is the only reason I'm turning the page — I want to watch that trap spring.
