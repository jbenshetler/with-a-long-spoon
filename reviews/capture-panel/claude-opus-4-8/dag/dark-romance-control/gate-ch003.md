REACTION

Okay so here's my problem, and it's the same problem I've had since chapter one: this book is genuinely predatory and I keep waiting for it to touch me the way predatory is supposed to touch me, and it just… won't put its teeth in. It KNOWS where the teeth go. It's holding them right up against my throat. And then it licks instead.

The pointing game — I'll give it this — the pointing game is the coldest, best thing that's happened yet, and it happened in broad daylight on a quad which is somehow worse. Two people shopping for a third human being like she's produce. Him pointing, her picking, both of them thinking they're playing and only one of them right about who's playing whom. That's a capture fantasy at a scale I don't usually get — not one man taking one woman, but a conspiracy, a secret choice made ABOUT a girl who's off laughing by the dining hall with her head thrown back, no idea she just got selected. When Vee finally came out those doors and the whole afternoon narrowed to her copper head and Randi's bare involuntary "Yes" — that landed. That's the trap-jaw. That's the thing.

But here's what's eating me. Pace. Everyone keeps telling me Pace is a threat and structurally, sure, he's the spider. But on the page he's the most patient, considerate, safeword-checking, forehead-kissing predator I've ever met, and my whole nervous system is built for the opposite. "He asks before he takes." The jacket said it and meant it as the hook and for me it's the off-ramp. I want to be scared. I am not scared of a man who arranges a girl's afternoon endorphins so she can "weigh it and choose." I'm not turned on by a man being gentle with someone he's actively grooming — the gentleness makes it more disturbing, yes, but disturbing isn't the same as hot for me, and this book keeps mistaking one for the other.

And the meta-thing I actually can't stop circling: the real prey in this chapter isn't even Vee, it's Randi. She's being hunted and she thinks she's the huntress. She handed him her wanting dressed up as a gift — "only in a threesome, with you" — and he SAW her do it and let her, and that's the merciless part, the letting. He watches her build the costume and lets her wear it. That's genuinely cold and I respect it. But it's cold in a literary, watching-from-a-pole way, not in a hands-around-my-throat way.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "this book is SO smart about how a woman lies to herself and it will not just grab me by the hair. it's foreplay written by a therapist." I'm on the fence. The architecture of the trap is delicious enough to keep me one more stretch — I want to see Vee walk into it — but if Pace doesn't grow a knife soon I'm out, because clever-cold only carries me so far without the danger in my actual body.
