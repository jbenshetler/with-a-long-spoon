REACTION

Okay so here's the thing — on its own, this chapter is exactly what I'd normally bail on. Two girls being adorable at a coffee shop, the right pen, the high table, oat-milk lavender lattes, "love you, girl." If I didn't know what I know, I'd have put this down at the granola bar. There's no man in it. There's no threat in it. It's a college-freshman crush rendered very tenderly, and tenderness before teeth is my off-switch.

BUT. I do know what I know. I sat through Chapter 3. I watched Pace point at this exact redhead across a quad and say "her," I watched Randi hunt her down and stage that "collision" with a decade of dance training, and now I'm reading the collision from *inside Vee's head* — Vee, who thinks she got lucky, who thinks the queen picked her for mysterious wonderful reasons, who "walked back to her dorm not feeling her feet." And that's the whole sick pleasure of it. Every warm beat in this chapter is a hook going in. "See you later" isn't sweet to me, it's a *scheduled event on a calendar Vee can't see* — the book even says that, and it made the back of my neck go cold in the good way. The transcript-is-blank thing, where Vee can't remember a single actual word Randi said, only the feeling of being chosen — that's grooming. That's a mark being loaded up on the drug and not clocking the dealer. I know exactly what I'm watching.

Cassie. CASSIE. "Goodbye, Vee." Not bye, not see you, the whole flat finished word, and the little thing that passes over her face that Vee won't name. That's the one person in the room who can smell it, and she's already saying goodbye to the girl she knows she's losing. That landed. That's the knife, and it came from the one direction I wasn't watching.

So — is the knife under THIS silk? Barely. It's under the silk of the *previous* chapters and it's bleeding through. On its own merits this chapter is thin and warm and I was tapping my foot for Pace to come back, for the bench, for the actual danger. I don't want to watch a friendship bloom for fifty pages. I want the trap to *spring*. My worry is that Book One spends its whole middle in this sunlit-girl-crush register and makes me wait too long for the cruelty the jacket promised. But the dread architecture is genuinely good and I trust, for now, that they're building the drop.
