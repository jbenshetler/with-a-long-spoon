REACTION

Okay. So this whole chapter is… two girls at the pool.

No Pace. No Randi. No bench, no game, no knife under the silk. Just Vee and her plain-suited best friend Cassie fighting a swimsuit, taking some catcalls from bleacher tossers, and having a splash fight to cheer Vee up. That's it. That's the chapter.

And here's the thing — it's *well done*. I'm not going to pretend it isn't. The underwater bit got me a little, honestly: "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." That's a real sentence about a real ache, and Cassie shooting the sheet of water across the gap instead of asking if she's okay — that's a good friend rendered by someone who knows what good friends do. If I were reading a literary coming-of-age novel about girlhood and bodies, I'd be underlining.

But that's the problem. I am not reading that book. Or I *bought* the other book. I came here for the bench. Chapter 1 was genuinely unsettling in the way I want — Pace running that girl to pieces and then *there you are, Randi*, the reveal that the girlfriend is the co-conspirator, the deadbolt-turning dread of it. That chapter had teeth. Chapters 3 through 6 were the machine tightening around Vee, the pointing game, the whole "every yes freely given" horror I was promised, and I could feel the trap. That's my shelf.

And now, right when the hook is set, the book pulls me into wholesome. A tender friendship interlude. Body warmth. Cassie is lovely and I have zero use for her, because nothing dangerous happens to anyone in these pages. This is the "slow domestic warmth" that puts me to sleep. The predators are offstage and I *miss* them, which is a weird thing to say but it's the truth of what I want.

What keeps me from bailing: I know this is a breather, not the book turning soft. The architecture is still merciless underneath — Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee" two chapters back is still sitting in my gut like she can see the cliff coming that Vee can't. I trust Helen Rivers is winding up, not going wholesome for real. One thin chapter after six strong ones earns patience. But if the next one is *also* offstage from the hunt, I start doing math on my to-read pile.

Text to a friend at 1 a.m.: "book gave me a whole tender pool chapter with the nice friend, no villain, I almost skimmed it, but I think it's the calm before she gets eaten, holding on"
