REACTION

Okay so here's the thing — on the surface this chapter is everything that puts me to sleep. Froyo. Mint tea in little brass cages. A boy who says "May I choose" and holds doors and walks on the street side and doesn't make her answer the text that night. Two people who like each other and eat dinner. If this were the WHOLE book I'd have already thrown it across the room, because tenderness before teeth is exactly my off-switch and this chapter is nothing but tenderness.

Except I read Chapter 1. And that's the only reason my skin was crawling in the good way the whole time.

Because I KNOW what "May I choose" is. I watched him do the courtly-permission thing to Randi facedown on a custom bench he built and lied about, edging her until she stopped being able to perform and begged in the voice she used as a crying child. "Ask before he takes, leave her the room to choose, until choosing becomes the most erotic thing" — that's not romance, that's the METHOD. And now I'm watching him run the exact same protocol on a 21-year-old he was pointed at like a girl in a shop window. Every "he waited through all of it" and "he built the quiet into it without being asked" isn't sweet to me — it's a man who has done this before, who knows precisely which untended hunger to feed. The froyo cup that "got away from her" because for once her mother's voice wasn't there to stop her? He's not indulging her. He's disabling her brakes one at a time and she's thanking him for it.

That's the wrongness I come here for. It's just wearing a cardigan.

What I'm actually nervous about is that the book might mean it straight. That the danger stays this quiet, this off-page, this psychological-only, for three hundred more pages while these two eat their way around a Mediterranean menu. I don't want subtext-danger. I want the knife to come OUT. The bench came out in chapter 1 and gutted me. Chapter 8 gave me a hand-hold and a kiss-rule exception, and if I hadn't had the bench in my back pocket this would've been a two-sentence "cute, bye."

The "I'm making an exception" ending — she thinks it's her choice. I know it isn't. That gap is the whole drug. Keep him a threat and I'm yours. Let him actually just be a nice patient boyfriend and I'm gone by chapter 11.
