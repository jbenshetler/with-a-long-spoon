REACTION

Okay so this was the sugar-crash chapter and I felt every empty calorie of it. Two girls on a bunk bed, sock feet, throwing a pillow, gushing about *tea in little brass cages* and hiking shoes she has to borrow. On its own? This is exactly the stuff that makes me put a book down — the debrief, the "look at me" twirl into a bean bag, the giggling. If I'd opened the book HERE I'd have been gone by paragraph three.

But I didn't open it here. I opened it on the bench. And that's the only reason this chapter has a pulse. Because I know what Vee doesn't. I know Randi isn't a friend she bumped into — Randi is the one who got *pointed at her* on a quad like a girl being selected off a menu, giddy, "her name is Vee and we've got a stats class together." I know Pace didn't wander over to help with calculus by accident. So when Vee is lying in a bean bag glowing about how *nobody talked her into it, she kissed him on purpose, it was HERS* — that's the knife. The whole horror of this book is that every yes really is hers and it's still a trap. She's proud of the exact thing that's being farmed out of her. That's genuinely nasty in the good way and it's the only thing keeping me in the chair.

And Cassie. God bless Cassie, she's doing all my dread for me. "What do I tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop." "Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" — a man with no last name, no address, just a *road* and a trail he "goes to," and Vee counting it off like a dossier, like it's PROOF she's safe. It's the opposite of safe and Cassie's flat little "off six-fourteen" repeated back is the sound of someone smelling smoke nobody else smells. That landed cold and I liked it.

Here's my problem though, and I'm being honest with myself. This man asks permission for *everything*. "May I choose." "May I call you." He *let go of the kiss* when she said no. That's usually my exit — a man who waits, a man who's tender before I've had teeth. Except this book is doing the one thing that makes the courtesy work on me: the permission IS the weapon. He's not asking because he's soft. He's asking because the yes is the whole point, because making her hand it over freely is more merciless than taking it. That's cold and I respect it. But I need that coldness to keep showing its face. Two chapters of froyo-and-hummus tenderness with only Cassie carrying the menace and I'm going to start feeling like I'm reading the wrong genre.

What I'd text a friend: "the villain says 'may I' and it's scarier than if he grabbed her, but if this book makes me sit through one more oat-milk-latte debrief before something with teeth happens I'm going to scream."
