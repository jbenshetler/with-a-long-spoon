REACTION

Okay, this one got me, and I want to be careful about *how* it got me, because getting-got is exactly the state I'm supposed to be watching in myself.

Sheri is wonderful. That's the plain fact. The book shifted registers on me — loud, crooked-makeup, five-foot-nothing, "you get to motorboat them things," singing filthy over the radio until Vee's begging — and I laughed the way Vee laughed, from the middle, before I'd voted on it. After sixty chapters of butcher paper and blood oranges and the frictionless lead, a person walked in who *isn't* smooth, and the relief of that was enormous. I felt the same shoulders-dropping thing Vee felt. So first: yes, this chapter is warm, and the warmth landed on me and not just on her.

And that's precisely when I go looking for where the book put the knife, because the book's whole method is to bury it in the warmth. It didn't disappoint me. Two things.

One: "that got settled back in December, he knows it's settled... I told him then: he's dancing, and if the redhead had opinions about it she could bring them to the hall." Read that again. In December — the December of the cracked-voice reunion, the withheld word, the cold — Pace and Sheri were already arranging "the redhead" into a dance hall in January. Vee was being scheduled into futures with named strangers before she knew there was a Sheri. The jacket's entire sentence — *the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one* — just got a second data point, dropped like a dried spoon, and Vee sailed right past it. I didn't.

Two, and this is the colder one: Sheri, trying to *praise* him, describes his method exactly and files it under virtue. "He'll turn a woman out on that floor like she was the only thing happening in the state, walk her back, thank her, go get a water. Hands stay where the dance puts them." She means: he's the one man in the hall not making a down payment. But I've watched the roadhouse. *Dancing with a woman like she's the only thing in the state IS the down payment* — it's the frictionless lead, the thing that costs him nothing and binds her completely. Sheri thinks she's found the exception. She's described the engine. And the book knows she has, because it plants the tell right there: "there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when." Even the warmest, realest, most abandoned person in this book is *calibrated*. The book told me so, in the middle of making me love her.

So is Sheri a witness or is she kept? The chapter hands me the word: "everyone he kept, and had never once mentioned." *Kept.* Vee thinks it tenderly, a good man's quiet good deeds. I read it as a stable. He collects people at the exact hour their whole tablecloth came off the table — Sheri out to her Marine father, her church, her Bible study, all of it inside a month — arrives as the only one there, fixes the lock, buys the burger, and becomes the one person left with your name on it. Two years later she thinks she's his friend. It is not lost on me that this is *precisely* Vee's shape: isolated, quietly starving, met at the moment of maximum need by the man who's the only one who *sees* her. Sheri is Vee, five years on and platonic. Or Sheri is genuinely just a friend and Pace genuinely just showed up, and I'm the paranoid one. The chapter keeps both alive and won't let me settle it, and that's the book still holding its instruments.

The blush is the part that worked on me against my will, so I'm flagging it as my danger ground: Pace going red to the ears at "bet those legs feel like Christmas," Vee deciding she could watch it all night. It's disarming and it's *meant* to be disarming, and the book says so through her, which is the book covering itself — but I clocked that I softened, and softening toward Pace is the thing I've been burned by.

And the leg-look. Sheri coming down Vee's body with no math in it, the mouth going round, *enjoying* her — and Vee laughing loose because a woman looked at her with pleasure and something let go. That's the jacket's promised thread surfacing sideways, not through Randi this time, and I noticed Vee didn't relabel it. She just felt lighter. File it.

What I did NOT get was the thing I'm braced to leave over — a chapter that unbraids the tenderness from the machine and plays it *straight*, warmth with the awareness switched off. This chapter is warm as anything in the book and it *still* buried the December arrangement and the described-engine in the sugar. Awareness on. Barely, on purpose, the way it always is.

I'd text a friend: "new character, tiny loud lesbian, I'm obsessed with her AND I think she might be the ghost of Christmas future for the heroine and neither of them knows it."
