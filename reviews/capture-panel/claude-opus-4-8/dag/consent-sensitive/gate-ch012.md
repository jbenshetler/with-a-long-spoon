REACTION

Okay so this is the debrief chapter, and I went in braced — because Cassie is the one clean thing and the book keeps polishing her, and I keep waiting for the run-over. And what actually happens is... she's fine. She's warm, she's dry, she's got her blood-type joke ready. And I notice how relieved I am, and I notice how the book USED that relief on me, and I have to sit with both.

Because here's what Cassie does in this chapter, and here's why I trust this book a little more after it: Cassie is the reader-surrogate for the *real* danger — she says the windowless van, the nobody-can-see-it-from-the-road, the "I had your blood type." She names the crude, obvious predator-shape. And then the crude shape doesn't materialize — he brought her back with her shoes on — and Cassie visibly *revises the file* and stands down. And that's the trick, isn't it. That's the exact trick. Pace is not the windowless-van danger. He was never going to be. The danger was never going to look like the danger Cassie was screening for, and watching Cassie's alarm system get satisfied by "he fixed her blister and didn't make it weird" is the scariest thing in the chapter to me. The conscience of the book just got shown the thing that reassures her, and it reassured her. That's the machine working on its best sensor.

And the line — "Vee," soft, "Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." I felt that. Cassie still hears something. She doesn't have words for it, she doesn't escalate, she lands on "borrow real boots next time." She's downgraded from *goodbye Vee* (the funeral, last time) to logistics. Which means the recon worked on her too, or means she's chosen to stop being able to say the thing. Either way the one person who could pull the cord just set it down gently.

What I clocked hardest: Vee narrating the food the way other girls narrate the kiss. "And then we ate — she said it the way other girls said and then we kissed." The book KNOWS. That's the tell that keeps me here. It knows the whole seduction of this man is that he feeds a starving girl and she's been trained to experience appetite as the transgression, so being fed IS the sex. The rye, the horseradish, no crumb, no pit, carry out what you carried in. The chapter title is "Rye." Not "Pace," not "The Mountain." The bread. It knows.

And "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain" — God, that landed clean, no irony, and then immediately the irony arrives on its own because she got up the mountain he chose, on the day he chose, in the boots that didn't fit that he'll fix, and the achievement is real AND administered. Same as everything. The book isn't hiding that from me. It's letting the two truths sit in the same sentence.

The one thing I'm turning over: this is the calmest chapter in a while, and calm is when I get suspicious. It's a breather, it's Vee happy and sore and glowing, and it's *lovely* — and I don't fully trust lovely from this book because the jacket told me I'm ahead of her and I'm supposed to fall for it "exactly as she does." I'm aware I just spent a whole chapter enjoying her joy. That's them doing to me what Randi does to Vee. I see it. I'm still here.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the roommate who was going to save her just stood down because he tied a good knot. we're not okay."
