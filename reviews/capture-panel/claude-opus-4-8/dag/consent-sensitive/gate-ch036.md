The burn. Her hand goes to the side of her breast "without her knowing it moved" — the body keeping its own record again. And Cassie, dry: "There's only one way that burn happens. Not enough on." One line, and Cassie has clocked the whole arrangement — the too-warm house, the nakedness Vee no longer thinks about — and named it sideways without cruelty. She sees it. She's the only one who sees it and can't say the size of what she's seeing, which is precisely where the book has kept her all along.

What I love and what scares me are the same thing here, and that's the book still being in good hands. Two sentences of plot, an ocean of dread and tenderness. Thin on incident, but it did the thing I needed after all that heat: it put me back in the room with the one person who loves Vee without an angle, and let me feel exactly how far Vee has drifted from shore by how hard she is to reach.
