Okay, so this is the exhale chapter, and I read it holding my breath a little differently than the book wants me to.

Here's what's actually good, and I'll give it its due: Vee spinning in a room too small to hold her, taking out the closet door, landing in the bean bag — that's real. That's a girl who did a thing she chose and is drunk on having chosen it. And the book keeps letting her say the thing I need her to keep saying: *nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to.* Which — yes. On the froyo/dinner level, that's true. She kissed him. She pulled him back. The reflex-rule and then the exception was hers. I'm not fighting the book on that.

But I am not Vee. I'm the reader the jacket promised would be "ahead of her from the start," and so I'm sitting here watching this scene do something quietly sickening while it plays as a slumber-party giggle. Because what is this chapter, structurally? It's Vee assembling, out loud, cheerfully, a **dossier on her own isolation** and calling it a pile of good things to find out. *I know where he lives. Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel. He'll have water.* She's counting it off as proof she's safe — *see, I know where he lives and everything* — and every single item is actually the opposite. She's never been to the house. He lives out past where the road turns to gravel, west of everything, and she's going hiking alone with a man she's known a matter of *days*, and the one instruction he's given her about her own body — *real shoes* — she doesn't even own the equipment to obey.

And the book KNOWS. That's the thing keeping me here. Cassie is the whole conscience of this chapter and she's playing it in a flat dry key that lands every time. *How much did he give you to drink.* *What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop.* *Off six-fourteen. That's the whole address.* *Is he rich? Rich people hike, poor people walk.* Cassie is naming, one after another, exactly the things I'd be naming, and Vee is throwing pillows at her. So the author is not naive here — the author has built the alarm system into the room and is letting me hear it go off under the giddiness. That distinction matters enormously to me. I'm not being asked to simply cheer. I'm being handed the giddiness AND the flat floor under it and told to hold both.

What I can't shake — and this is the whole engine of my dread — is that I've *read chapter one*. I know Randi. I know the bench. I know that "Randi," this warm citrus goddess who steered Vee into Pace's path on the quad like an accident, is the woman facedown and begging and then locking herself in the bathroom to go back behind the face. I know Pace built a piece of furniture for the purpose. So when Vee says *he lives off six-fourteen, past the gravel, he'll have water, he's not going to walk me off a cliff* — I know exactly whose house that is and what's in the bedroom, and Vee is packing borrowed hiking shoes to go there, glowing. The froyo and the tea and the *may I choose* were all real and all lovely and all, I am now certain, *engineered*. Cassie's *goodbye, Vee* two chapters back — the whole word, the word for a longer parting — is ringing in my ears.

So no, the kiss being freely given doesn't reassure me. *Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.* The book told me that on the jacket and it's making good on it here in the most patient, deliberate way: it's showing me a girl freely, joyfully, walking her own reconnaissance into the trap and mistaking the reconnaissance for safety. That's not a book that's confused about what it's depicting. That's a book that's merciless and knows it.

The one thing I'm watching warily: Vee's interiority in this chapter is thinner than it was in the froyo/dinner chapters. Here she's mostly *up*, mostly giddy, and Cassie carries the weight. If the book keeps outsourcing Vee's awareness to Cassie — if Vee becomes purely the lit-up ingénue and never gets to have her own suspicion — that'll start to feel like the book enjoying her blindness. It hasn't crossed that line. Cassie's still in the room. But I'm noting where the line is.
