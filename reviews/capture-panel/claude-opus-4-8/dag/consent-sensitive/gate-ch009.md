REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter I was actually nervous about, because it's the pure courtship beat — no bench, no heat, just a man being lovely to a hungry girl over dinner — and quiet is exactly where a book like this gets caught. If Helen Rivers were going to accidentally start rooting for Pace, forgetting he's running a play, it'd be here, in a chapter with nothing to hide behind. And she doesn't. That's the thing that kept me leaning in.

Because I've read Chapters 1 through 3. I know the froyo and the "may I choose" and the built-in quiet of "he didn't ask her to answer that night" are not a nice man being nice. They're the exact tooling he used on Randi with the apple slices — cut-side up because she eats them faster that way. Every single gesture in this chapter is a technique, and the book *knows* it's a technique, and it lets me sit inside Vee's melting while I hold the blueprint in my other hand. That double exposure is the whole engine and it's working on me. I'm charmed and slightly nauseated by being charmed, which I'm fairly sure is the intended dose.

What sold me that the awareness is switched on: the hunger. Vee hasn't eaten since breakfast, the hollow's been open "a lot longer than since noon," her mother's voice metering every cup of froyo — and Pace aims straight into the deprivation and *feeds* it. That's not romance, that's the wound and the exact instrument built to fit it, and the prose frames it as her defenses dissolving, not as a flower opening. "That low background readiness she carried everywhere... and it just wasn't there, and she let it not be there." She's being disarmed and the book calls it disarming.

The right hand. Always the right, never the left, "some consistent rule he kept." Vee can't read it. I can — the left is the working hand, the one that reddened Randi on the bench. The book planted that on purpose and didn't underline it. That's a book in control of what it knows.

And the kiss at the end — this is the one that separates the aware book from the naive one, and it's the tightest beat in the chapter. She names a rule ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"), he respects it *instantly*, no lean, no charm-up, no wait — "he just smiled, like she'd told him something true and fine about herself, and let it close." A naive book plays that as: what a gentleman. This book has already told me that giving up the kiss *is* the move that gets the kiss, that leaving her the room to choose is precisely the mechanism because choosing is the thing that undoes her. So when *she* doesn't go, when *she* closes the distance he vacated — I read it as engineered, and I think the author does too. He surrendered nothing. He never surrenders anything.

What I'm watching, going forward: this can't be a lot of chapters of everything-is-lovely. One aware seduction chapter is riveting. Four in a row where the book forgets to keep Vee's wound visible and just wants me to swoon would lose me fast. Right now the instruments read true. Text a friend: *this book is making me fall for a predator on purpose and it KNOWS, and I can't decide if that's the smartest thing I've read this month or if I'm being handled exactly like the girl.*
