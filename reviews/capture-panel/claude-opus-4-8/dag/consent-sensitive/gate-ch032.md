Okay so first thing: Randi's not in this. The whole chapter is just the two of them and I kept waiting for the third presence, the debrief-in-the-wings, and it never came. Part of me relaxed and part of me went cold, because a book that's been showing me the machine this whole time chose *this* chapter — the tenderest, sexiest one yet — to take the handler out of the room. That's not an accident. This is the chapter designed to make me forget there's a game. And I want to be honest that for stretches of it, it worked. That's the scary part. I felt it work on me while I watched it work on her.

The play-fight is genuinely wonderful and I hate how much I loved it, the shorts-as-flag, her finding out *she's faster than him* — "all that mass of him had to gather and come around like a ship" — that's the first time in this whole book she's had power in a room with him, physical, gleeful, hers. And it's real! For a minute she's not being handled, she's just a fast girl on an open floor being brave. The flashing-him to make him come at her — "some small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it" — that's the interiority I keep staying for. She's present at her own recklessness. She's not being written as a puppet; she's being written as a woman surprising herself, and I trust that voice.

But then the bet. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." And there it is, the thing I can't unsee: he's built a proposition where her own body is the evidence against her, where the wet on the finger is the verdict, and the "choice" is rigged before it's offered because the outcome was decided "hours ago" and "every night this week in the dark." That IS the jacket's whole thesis — "asks before he takes and leaves her the room to choose, until choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done." He's running the exact instrument. What keeps me here — barely, but it does — is that the book says it out loud: "She was being offered up to her own wanting, and both of them knew it." The prose is not naive about the rigging. It names the ease as a wound: "The ease went through her worse than any sting had. Her body had decided without her." That's the book knowing. If it had played the wet finger as triumph, as *see, she wanted it all along, isn't that hot*, I'd have closed it. Instead it played it as the old shame doubled, and gave her the wondering-worse-than-knowing. Awareness. I stay.

The "Slow. For me." undid me a little and I resent it. Her loving that he asked — "It was hers now, give or keep, and she loved it" — this is the seduction working at full tenderness and the book flagged it in the very next beat by making it *consensual and lovely and also a slow strip performed on command*. Both true at once. That doubled vision is the only thing separating this book from the ones I throw across the room.

The aftercare is where I got the most suspicious, actually, not the least. The juice, the "wonderful, wonderful" until she goes under, the head in his lap — and then the *scrunchies*. A whole form of them, no note, "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." That's not a gift, that's a claim staked. It's possession dressed as thoughtfulness, the same grammar as the dress, the same "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." Plenty as a message. I clocked it. She read it as being treasured. I read it as inventory.

But — the mirror monologue saved the chapter for me. "Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together." "My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky." She is *funny* about herself, self-aware, standing in his bathroom naming her own pattern out loud. A groomed girl doesn't get that voice; a person does. As long as Vee can stand in the glass and diagnose herself, I don't think the book is asking me to cheer. It's asking me to watch her walk in with her eyes open, which is worse and more honest.

The scar again, still withheld, and now she's *decided not to ask* — "a thing about a person you've decided to keep." That's her doing to him the thing he does to everyone: possessing by not-knowing. Noted. Filed.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book keeps making the seduction beautiful and then handing me the receipt for it in the same breath and I don't know whether to trust it or be furious that it's this good at the thing it warned me about.*
