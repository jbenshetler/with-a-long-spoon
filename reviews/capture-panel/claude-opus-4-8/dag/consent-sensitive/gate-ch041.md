Okay so first — the sex-and-aftercare opening is doing exactly what this book always does to me, which is make it good, genuinely good, so that the good and the dread come up in the same breath and I can't get one without the other. Him rolling all the way across her and laying his head on the low slope of her breast, the little not-kisses, her running her theory in her head — *they're not about sex for him* — and then the book gently spoiling her theory and pleasing her. That's tender. It's real. And it's Vee doing her thing, the interiority roaring, building a little narrative of what he is out of the evidence in front of her. I love her there and I'm scared for her there and it's the same feeling.

But then the photo. Oh, the photo. My stomach dropped. This is the thing I've been half-waiting for, and the book handed it to me clean — Vee is the one who offers it ("Would a picture help?"), she's the one who tries to keep her own agency in it ("Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone"), she sends it "before the nerve could leave her," and the book flags the cold sane thought — "the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this" — and then has her override it because "believing him was the brave part." And here's the tell that keeps me in: the book KNOWS. It named the cold sane thought. It didn't pretend the danger wasn't there. It just showed her walking past it on trust, and called the walking-past brave — which is exactly how it feels from inside and exactly the setup for it to be a catastrophe. There's a folder on her. There's a house apparatus. Randi's in bed with him debriefing. And now there is a photograph of Vee, composed and certain, that lives in his phone, on the record, an asset filed. He posed her himself — "No, not like that. You'll have to wait" — moved her limbs like the dress on the pedestal, like the shoe strap, like everything he arranges. She thinks she's giving; he's collecting. The line "I'd never share your photograph" — the careful phrasing, *your photograph*, not *you*, not *this* — I clocked that. That's a man leaving himself room.

The cooking, the chicken piccata basically, the wine cooked into the pan she's drinking — fine, lovely, the ongoing project of remaking her palate, "there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was." Even the food is a reformation of her appetite in his image. The book keeps SAYING this out loud, which is why I trust it.

And then the two little Thanksgiving beats, which are the real payload. "I don't go back" — the level, exact, careful voice, each word set apart. That's Peter. That's the hallway kid, the Daphne kid. Sheri's coming for Thanksgiving. "She's alone." Sheri's the one who named "two of them at once," and now Sheri's at the table.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: SHE SENT HIM THE PICTURE. And the book let her know it was dangerous and let her do it anyway and called it brave and I am not okay. Also he gave her the top drawer last chapter and now there's a photo of her in his phone and a woman named Sheri coming for Thanksgiving and I do not like the math.

Thirty-seven chapters and the author still sees it. The photo is the biggest new hook the con has planted and the prose named the danger in the same sentence it walked past it. That's the whole reason I'm still here.
