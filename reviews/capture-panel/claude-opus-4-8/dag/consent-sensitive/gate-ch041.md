"Cropped." Of course that's the title. She crops the photo at the waist for her mother's purse, she crops the story to "the parts that fit" for her dad, she crops herself down to something that stands up cleanly in the telling — and the book lines all three croppings up in a row so I can't pretend it's just about the picture. That's the thing about this book that keeps me here past chapter forty: it names its own gesture. She is a person now edited for every audience, and the one place she has left where she doesn't crop is Pace's house, which is exactly where the con lives. The uncropped version only exists in his phone. Let that sit.

But the detail that actually put ice down my back: he *made the dress.* Sewed it himself, on a machine on his sun porch, because she mentioned once she couldn't afford one she saw in a magazine. I've been carrying the induction dress this whole time as the instrument — the thing on the pedestal, the thing that did its job the one night the architect wasn't there. And now I find out he *built the instrument with his hands.* He listened to one offhand want and manufactured the object that would walk her into that room. That's not a boyfriend buying a gift. That's a man fabricating the exact tool, to spec, from a wish she didn't know he'd filed. And Vee's read on it — "my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it" — is so close to the truth and so wrong about which part is the alarm. She thinks the sewing is just *weird,* endearing-weird, the tender secret too intimate for her father. I think the sewing is the whole machine visible for one second. He makes the costumes. He is a costumer.

And the dad, pricing the dress, going quiet — "too good is its own kind of suspicious" — that's the reader's own instinct handed to a minor character and then walked past. Her father has the correct alarm for two seconds ("an expensive gift means his daughter is in deeper than he'd pictured") and Vee manages it, feeds him "the parts that fit," and the alarm dies at the table. Everyone who could catch it gets handed a cropped version. That's the design.

Cassie. God, Cassie. "So what is he. To you." Careful, exact, not prying — she's not fighting anymore, my notes had that right, but she hasn't stopped *asking,* and this is the cleanest ask yet. That's the going-small from the dorm, deepened — the roaring interiority that has language for everything has no language for this one thing, and the absence is the tell. Some part of her wanted Cassie to push, to make her say it, to be the wall Pace never is — and Cassie won't, because Cassie's decided it's too late. Even Vee's own vigil-keeper has stopped being the friction, and Vee can *feel* the missing friction and mourn it in half a sentence. That "a little, not" is her reaching for a hand that isn't reaching back.

This was a quiet chapter and it did more to me than some of the sex scenes.
