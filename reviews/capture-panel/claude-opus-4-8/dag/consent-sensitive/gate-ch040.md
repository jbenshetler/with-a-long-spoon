REACTION

So after the MIRH grenade goes off in 39 — Randi's own father, the worst point in the county, "cheating bastards" said raw and unrehearsed, aimed too near to be meant for strangers — this is what I get? A pizza chapter. And I know exactly what that is. This is the book handing me the pure uncut drug right after it let me see the machine that manufactures it. It's a breather, it's a lull, and it's positioned on purpose: *this is what being inside it feels like, Vee's whole gray week going technicolor Friday to Sunday.* The contrast is doing work even when the chapter itself is soft.

But here's the thing that kept me leaning in instead of drifting — the five nights. She called that boundary herself. She's the one who said she was drowning, needed the time for school, and he said "of course," didn't argue, didn't go cold, didn't make her pay. And the book does not let that be simply lovely. It says it straight: *the plain grace of that had cost her more than a fight would have,* that some corner of her wanted him to fight, to want the nights badly enough to say so. That's the author still seeing. He never gives her anything to push against. That's the whole method, it's *"give me something to push back against"* from the dance, inverted — he removes every wall so there's nothing for her to brace on, and the frictionlessness reads as saintliness while it's actually the tightest grip in the room. She lasted five nights against a man who did nothing at all. That's not a man being generous. That's a man who's built the vacuum she can't stay out of. The book knows. Good.

The shirt, though. God, the shirt. This is the one that got past my guard and I want to be honest that it did. My notes had *"the washed shirt that came back smelling of no one, worn anyway"* — and now she's washed it on purpose, lost her nerve on the drive, and handed it over asking him to wear it two days so it smells like him again. She wore it every night she wasn't here until it went to nothing. That's not cute. That's a woman self-soothing with a scent because the actual person is a locked box she's decided to keep — the scar she still won't ask about, the parents he shut the door on, "Peter" she says into the dark to hold something he won't give her. She's building a ritual of dependency and the book lets it be tender, and it *is* tender, and it also frightened me. He wore two shirts in his own kitchen because she told him to, and the book calls that "the whole of his answer, as it always was." That last clause is the tell again — his answer is always the gesture that costs him nothing and binds her completely. He'd never say the word. She's counting his nights, keeping a tally her mouth would never read out. He knows the number and lets her never say it.

What I'm a little tired of: the mouth-waking-up passage. Cotton candy versus cut strawberries, tasting every layer separately — this is the fourth or fifth time we've done "nobody told me my mouth was asleep," and this time it started to feel like the book petting me instead of the book knowing something. That's the one place the awareness thinned.

And I notice Randi is completely absent, one chapter after she was cracked wide open. I want back into that. The wire's still live and this chapter refused to touch it, which — smart, actually. It makes me hungry.
