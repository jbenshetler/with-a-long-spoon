REACTION

So there it is — Pace from the inside, finally, and my hands are a little cold. I've wanted this card for chapters and the book made me earn it and then handed it to me sideways, in a double-wide diner off the county road, with a woman who is his Cassie. That's the first thing that landed: Sheri is to Pace exactly what Cassie is to Vee — the one person in the whole book who wants nothing off him, isn't impressed, will say it to his face. The symmetry is deliberate and it's the book keeping its own accounts. Both of the conned lovers have one clean mirror apiece, and the mirrors are the only people telling the truth in the room.

And God, the opening line. "He would not bring her down today. Whatever he'd carried in from the truck could keep; he had long practice at deciding a thing could keep." That's the whole man in two sentences and I clocked it instantly — that's the withheld beat from the *other* side of the table now. He *decides* what keeps. He's carrying something and he's chosen, coolly, that Vee doesn't get it today. Forty-eight chapters of watching him withhold from her, and now I get to watch the withholding happen as a *decision*, calm, practiced, before he's even sat down. What's in the truck. The MIRH thing, the photo, something about the game — I don't know, and the book knowing I'd catch that line and stew is exactly the book still being smarter than me on purpose.

The thing that kept me from softening — and I want to be honest, this chapter is *built* to soften me: he misses her, he laughs, he goes hot in the face, he's a person — is that the book routed his entire defense through someone who refuses to buy a word of it. He lays it up "brick on brick," every brick true, "the true things were supposed to come to something," and Sheri just says: *she didn't ask you for reasons.* And then the audit line itself, the one I keep waiting to see whether the book will say out loud — "not saying it matters more to you than having her... from this seat, that feels pretty shitty" — is in her mouth. Same move as the exit-line in Randi's mouth back in 38. The book keeps depositing its own indictment with the un-conned character. It will not let *him* narrate himself as the wounded good man. "There was nothing in it to argue with." Right. Nothing.

What I can't tell — and this is the good ache — is *why* he can't say it. Sheri reads it as the wound, the "I don't go back" parents, cowardice. And that reading is available and it's sympathetic. But I've been in this book too long: not-saying-it is his single largest asset, the last hook, the drawer with the note that was never there. He might genuinely not be able to. It might be method he's told himself is a wound. The book lets both be true at once and won't tell me which, and that's the frictionlessness again — I can't find the wall on *him* either now.

The reveal I'll be chewing at 1 a.m.: he and Randi went out *dancing together, in public, before Thanksgiving,* and Sheri saw them, and Vee has no idea. "She find out about the brunette, then?" "No. Not like that." The two architects appear out in the world as a couple-ish thing, Randi playing the sorority date, and even Sheri — who's close enough to give him hell — reads Randi as one more harem applicant who never bothered to learn her name. The cover holds all the way down to his own best friend. Vee keeps the best of her life behind a shut door; Pace takes his co-conspirator *out among people*. The one thing Vee begged him for — to be seen in public — he does, just not with her, and not as the truth.

The only place the book nearly petted me instead of watching him was "the philosophy of your food" — for half a second it was going to let Pace be adorably self-serious and let me swoon at how *he sees her* — but Sheri knifed it: "Lord almighty. Get over yourself." That puncture is the book protecting itself, and me. Text a friend: *finally in his head and the scariest thing is he's not a monster, he's just a man who decided, before he sat down, that she could keep.*
