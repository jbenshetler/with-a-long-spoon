And the OUTLIER. God. Miranda Holdings, up and to the right, alone in the white, "the biggest one, off on its own, easy to see if you knew to look for it, invisible if you didn't." The architect is herself a point off the line. She's spent the whole book being the one who sees the field, who reads the data on everyone, and the one data point she can't spin is her own name in gray government print. "The country isn't my county" — she said it to win an argument and the county detonated in her hand.

That's the gentle handling, the not-naming, the giving-her-the-thing-to-hold — Vee has learned the technique from being on the receiving end of it, and she runs it on Randi by instinct now, out of love. The mouse is handling the cat. And I don't think Vee knows she's doing it any more than she knows what half her own body is doing.
