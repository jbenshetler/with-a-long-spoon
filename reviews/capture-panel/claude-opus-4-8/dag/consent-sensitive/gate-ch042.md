REACTION

Oh, this one is quiet and it's a knife.

She built a sentence. For *weeks*. She assembled it piece by piece the way you memorize a poem, checked it "carefully," made sure it was warm and true and — crucially — *not I love you in any shape he could take it for*, because the rule is he says it first and she found the rule by bumping it from the inside. That whole paragraph is the con rendered as architecture: she's now doing engineering *on herself*, drafting language that will smuggle her own heart past the border he installed. The girl who used to run the clean-machine bookkeeping is now running compliance review on her own tenderness before she's allowed to feel it out loud. That's the horror in this book distilled — she experiences the withholding as *her* problem to solve, a puzzle of phrasing, never once as a wall he built.

And then the crackers-and-a-meal line. It's beautiful. It's also — God — it's the mouth-waking epiphany the book has run at me a dozen times, except now she's handing it *back to him* as her love, her whole offering, the best thing she's got. She fed me a meal. I didn't know what a meal was. That's her saying I was starving and you're the one who — no. That's the exit-line from ch38, "you've been starving your whole life and calling it fine," fully digested and reissued in her own voice as gratitude. The propagation again. She's speaking their grammar as if it's hers.

And he doesn't say it back. That's the whole chapter. "I love how good we are together." Not *you*. Not *I love you*. He loves the arrangement. He loves what her body does — "what your body does — I've never" — the body, always the body, the asset. The book lets his hand do that unbearable thing: press once, stop, "as if he had begun two different responses with his hand and put both down before either had finished." He *heard* it. He clocked exactly what she was doing and he declined it, smoothly, warmly, with two breaths she'd never heard him take. He gave her something to hold that isn't the thing. Give-them-something-to-hold, ch32, still the move.

And here's why I'm not leaving — here's the audit and the book passing it again: it does NOT let her have the warm ending. It moves the *wait* behind her breastbone into the body "where small waits go when the quilt is warm," and then it drags her to the dorm laundry room two days later and makes her feel it. "The two things had not been the same shape." "She had said her best sentence. He had not said the sentence back." And the pile — "the place was not empty. There were other things in it already — small flickers... where what she knew about him had had to do small repair work to keep functioning." The book NAMES the repair work. It names that she won't look at the pile. It names that the one person who could resolve it is the one she can't ask. That is the book knowing, cold and exact, forty-two chapters deep, still refusing to play the harvest as liberation. The tenderness and the extraction are the same gesture and the prose sees the seam even as she can't.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she wrote him a love letter that took her weeks to word so it wouldn't count as a love letter and he still didn't say it back and now it's in the pile and she folded her laundry.* And: *the cracker line is going to live in my chest.*

The retiring of it at the end wrecked me. "The cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech." She tried once. It failed. It goes back into her body where she'd been carrying it. She's smaller by one sentence now. That's the whole book — she keeps losing language and calling it peace.
