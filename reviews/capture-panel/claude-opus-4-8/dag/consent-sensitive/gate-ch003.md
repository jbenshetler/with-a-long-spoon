Oh, this is the chapter that shows me the machine. And I have to hand it to Helen Rivers — she built it in daylight, in a burning-maple sunlit quad, holding my hand the whole way, and I still felt the cold coming off it.

The jacket's "a game between the two people now closest to her" — here it is, being born on a lawn. And the thing that's making my skin prickle in the good-scary way is that the book keeps naming its own apparatus out loud. "She had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." "He points, you pick." "The choosing was hers. He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could." That's grooming described as liberation, and the prose *knows* it's describing grooming. It hands me the euphemism and the truth in the same breath. That's the tell I read by, and it's there.

The bed scene got me — I'll be honest — and it got me while I was also going cold at "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That line is the whole book in miniature. Because I have Chapter 1 fresh: I *watched* Randi's body say yes while the woman inside bolted the bathroom door and pressed a bottle cap into her nail bed. A rougher man would leave a mark that made her flinch. This one leaves nothing to point at. That's scarier, and the book is not naive about which is scarier.

What I'm turning over at 1 a.m.: this chapter withholds Randi's interiority the way Chapter 1 gave it. We're inside Pace, uninterrupted, warm, sure. Normally that's the exact configuration that loses me — sunlit certainty with the awareness switched off. But it doesn't lose me *here*, because Chapter 1 armed me. The whole point of the sun and the maple and the frisbee is that I'm being seduced right alongside Randi, "ahead of her from the start, falling for it exactly as she does," just like the jacket warned. The book told me it was going to do this to me and then did it. I respect that enormously and I don't fully trust it, which is the correct place to be.

The target manufactured into feeling like the initiator. That's the entire con of the book demonstrated in three seconds on a brick walk. "Let's go feed you." Vee is food.

The place I came closest to setting it down: not a craft failure, an appetite failure. I need the next chapters to give Vee a real interior, the way Chapter 1 gave Randi one. If Vee stays prey, if she's only ever the thing being fed, the awareness switches off and I'm gone. But I don't think that's the bet this book is making. I think it's going to make me love her and hate what I'm complicit in. I want to find out.
