REACTION

Okay, so here's what I noticed first, and it's the thing that kept me in my chair: this is the most sympathetic Pace chapter in the whole book, delivered in the quiet, on a hungover morning with bread cooling on the counter — and the book did not let me off the hook once. It would have been so easy. Man bakes cinnamon raisin from scratch while the girl sleeps it off, leaves the ice water on a folded paper towel so it won't ring the wood, tucks her into his own undershirt, kisses her forehead *after he thinks she's already gone* — that last one is precision-engineered to gut me and it nearly did. And then he hands her the wound: one friend. One. College at fifteen, the window that opens once and shut before he reached it, *weren't safe or weren't welcoming*, not home since sixteen. I know what that's building. I'm meant to stack him up against my own Megs and Kaylas and feel the cold of the empty Thanksgiving campus. And I did feel it. That's my danger ground and I know it is.

But the book put the tripwire right where I needed it: *"It isn't tragic. He believed it, the way he'd believed the two years with Daphne had been luck. He set it in good order and the good order was the tell."* That's the instrument reading clean. The composure is the tell. The book is still watching him the exact way I am. And — this is the one that made me exhale — it let Vee circle Daphne and refuse the word. *The thing about it that was wrong, the thing Vee still would not set a word to.* She's pressing *grooming* back down under her tongue and the narrator names the pressing. So the ugly truth is lit underneath the pity, not replaced by it. The book hasn't switched the awareness off. Thank God, because I had my hand on the door.

Here's my 1 a.m. text though: *he handed her his whole sad childhood like a dried spoon and I cannot tell if he meant to.* Because Marsh taught me the hau — the gift carries the giver and pulls you back toward him. Water, bread, forehead kiss, and now the backstory, which is the biggest gift of all because it's the one that says *nobody has ever had this of me.* A wound offered is a gift that binds harder than jewelry. And the book flagged it for me, just barely: *"misreading her quiet for doubt, or not misreading it."* That little *or not* is the whole book in three words. He might be reading her to the millimeter and pouring out exactly as much vulnerability as the moment can metabolize. I can't prove it and neither can she, and that's the point.

Sheri, though. I came in braced for Sheri-as-character-witness, a planted alibi — *hands stay where the dance puts them, two years, every time* — and I'm still a little braced, because deploying an unimpeachable queer best friend who physically cannot be a conquest is exactly what a careful book does to buy its predator a clean bill. But she reads *real*, damn it. The coming-out, the tablecloth coming off the table and nobody catching a dish, crying in the middle of a two-step — that's written from the inside. And the thing is, even if Sheri's testimony is true, it doesn't exonerate anything about Vee. Pace can be genuinely decent to a grieving woman he'll never touch AND be running the longest harvest I've ever read on a 21-year-old. Both. The book keeps making me hold two true things that won't reconcile, which is what it's been doing to Vee with Daphne all along, and I notice it's now doing it to me on purpose.

What I clocked as the real move: *"I'd love to meet your other friends."* She opens a door and he closes it — *Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else.* And watch what that does. It makes her the second person alive who gets inside. It makes the relationship precious by scarcity. It's the oldest one in the book and it works because it's probably *true.* The most effective lies are.

And Randi's not here. Two chapters now mostly Randi-free, and I felt her absence like a held breath — because last I saw her she was in bed with him, in love with Vee, running the console. This warm isolated-orphan portrait of Pace sits very strangely next to the engine room. The book's letting me keep that dissonance. I want to know what it does with it.

So: moved, pulled, and still suspicious — which is the only honest place to be, and the fact that the book keeps me able to be all three at once is why I'm not leaving.
