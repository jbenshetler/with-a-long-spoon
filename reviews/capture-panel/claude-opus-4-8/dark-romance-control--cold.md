GATE 1 — The Bench
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 4
ALMOST-STOPPED: "He had learned. A closed door was hers, not his; you did not open it just because you could hear through it." — right there, when the whole thing tipped into a man congratulating himself on his gentleness and restraint. That's when my hand drifted toward the back button.
WHY: This is beautifully written, I'll give it that — the apple slices, the bench, the mechanics of it are precise and hot in a technical sense. But it's the *wrong* heat for me. This is a Dom who asks for the safeword, checks her wrists, respects her door, and calls her a good girl for feeling safe. There's no threat in him. He's a therapist with a spanking bench. I kept reading because Randi herself is interesting — the woman going back behind the face, the deadbolt-clicking, the water-bottle-under-the-thumbnail detail is genuinely unsettling in a good way. She has more menace in her than he does, and *that* pulled me forward. But I need the knife under the silk and so far the only edge in this book is hers.

GATE 2 — Standards
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 5
ALMOST-STOPPED: "She lay under the duvet and looked at the ceiling and did not cry." — a whole chapter of a girl lying still under a duvet in her shoes. Nothing happens. No danger, no man, no heat. I nearly left out of sheer sedation.
WHY: Okay — the sorority interrogation scene had teeth, the Brooke power-play of not standing up, the four minutes of smiling war. That's a knife under silk, just a *social* one. And I'm starting to suspect the real predator in this book is Randi, not Pace, and that's the only thing keeping my thumb off the button. But two chapters in and I have zero of my actual thing: no obsessive man who's a threat, no capture, no wrongness aimed at *her*. This is turning into a literary character study of a hollow girl. Gorgeous. Not my shelf. I'm giving it two more because the Randi-as-danger thread might pay off.

GATE 3 — The Pointing Game
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 6
ALMOST-STOPPED: "If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her, good enough that the good feeling and the wanting became one fact." — the Pace interiority is SO pleased with itself, so gently-therapeutic, that every time we're in his head I start to slide out of the book.

It's not MY dark — no obsessed man, no capture-of-the-heroine — but there's finally a real threat with a body count coming, and I want to know who gets hurt. The craft is undeniable. I'm in for the fourth.

VERDICT
WOULD PAY: no — but it's close, and it's not a quality no. This is a smart, controlled, genuinely dangerous book. It's just not the danger I buy for. I want a man who's a threat and an obsession pointed at the heroine; this is a predatory *woman* and a man too busy being wise to be scary, and the dread is slow-cooked literary suspense, not the dubcon-thrill dark romance I go through three of a week. I'd rather be captured than watch a grooming from the groomer's boyfriend's smug POV.
MAILING LIST: no — not my shelf, so I wouldn't want more of it in my inbox, even admiring it.
TELL A FRIEND: Okay so this is NOT our thing, do not go in expecting a villain who'd burn the world down for her — the man is a gentle spanking-bench therapist who checks her safeword and respects her feelings, I nearly died of boredom. BUT. The writing is scary good and there's a real twist in what it's doing: the actual predator is the girlfriend. She's this hollowed-out sorority queen who hunts a sweet lonely redhead across campus like she's selecting livestock, and her boyfriend thinks he's helping her "come out of her shell." It's a grooming horror story wearing a campus-romance sweater and by chapter four I was genuinely afraid for the prey. If you ever want to read a dark book that's dark in the *literary* way instead of the fun way — female villain, slow dread, gorgeous sentences, a spanking scene that's honestly technically perfect even if the guy has no teeth — this one. Just don't expect to be turned on. Expect to be a little disturbed.
