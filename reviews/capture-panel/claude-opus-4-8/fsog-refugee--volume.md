VERDICT
BOOK TWO: yes — I'd buy it right now, tonight, before I slept, the way I bought the second Fifty Shades. The door didn't close so much as the floor tilted: three people now snared in a thing two of them built, and Vee still doesn't know the room she's standing in. MAILING LIST: yes — a writer who can make a tape measure the most erotic scene in a book, and thread Simpson's paradox under a sorority coffee date, and grieve her own villains, gets my email. I want to know what else she's doing.

TELL A FRIEND: Okay — so you know how I left all those books because the "possession" always turned out to mean some man overriding a woman's no and the book grinning about it? This is the opposite and it wrecked me. It's literary, it's filthy in the best-written way, and the man ASKS — there's a whole scene where he fits a dress on her half-naked on a little platform and never once looks down, he waits for her face, and I had to put it down and walk around. Her wanting is hers, rendered from the inside, and the shame she carries turning into heat because someone finally sees her — that's the exact thing we always wanted this genre to do and it never did. BUT. Here's the catch, and it's a big one and you have to know going in: the "glamorous new best friend" is in on it. She and the man planned Vee like a purchase before Vee ever knew there was a plan, and you're ahead of the girl the whole time, falling for it exactly as she does, which is horrible and brilliant and made me complicit and a little sick. The book knows. It's not winking at the manipulation like the old ones winked at the kidnapping — it treats it as a real cost and the two schemers are coming apart because they didn't plan on it being real either. My one gripe: the structure gets a touch mechanical in the middle third — scene, then the best-friend Cassie debrief, then the other-friend Randi lunch where she excavates Vee's secrets, over and over, and I clocked the pattern. And I'm nervous, genuinely nervous, about Book Two — because the whole thing is balanced on a knife: when Vee finds out, does the book honor her, or does it use "every yes was freely given" to tell her the lie was fine? If it does the second thing I'll throw it across the room. But it's Volume One and it earned every bit of my trust so far, so I'm in. Read it. Then call me the second Vee finds out, because I am not going through that alone.
