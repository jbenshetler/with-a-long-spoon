VERDICT
BOOK TWO: yes — I'd buy it right now, this minute, without checking the price the way Vee reads the right-hand column first. This whole volume is a loaded gun that never fires, and I refuse to be left in a house with it cocked. I need the reveal, I need the photo promise to come due, I need to know whether Randi ever says the word or spends Vee down to nothing, and whether Cassie's the one who finally pulls the pin.

MAILING LIST: yes — Helen Rivers can have my email. Anyone who can write consent this scrupulously *inside* a con this cruel, and make me feel the seam of both at once for fifty chapters, has earned a standing invitation. I want to know what else she's willing to look straight at.

TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — this is the one I've been complaining for years that nobody writes, and also it's a trap, and I mean that as praise. It's the heat and the intensity and the man who *asks*, who watches for the answer, whose control makes actual room for her — every single time she takes her clothes off in this book it's her idea, her hand, her choosing, and I nearly cried in chapter fifteen because a woman who thinks her body is the shameful thing gets looked at and *admired* instead of used. That's the good. Here's the catch, and it's the whole book: the girl is being played. Two people planned the best year of her life and she doesn't know there's a plan, and you're told that on the jacket, so you spend all fifty chapters ahead of her, falling for it exactly as she does and hating that you are. But — and this is why I didn't throw it — the book never once *winks.* It knows precisely what it's showing you. The manipulation costs the manipulators; both of them fall in for real; it's less a romance than a slow beautiful crime with three victims. The prose is genuinely literary, the food and the sex and the silences all carry what nobody can say. My one real reservation: there's a spanking-bet scene that walks right up to my line about overriding a woman's "no," and it stays on the right side of it, but only just, and you'll feel the edge. And it ends without firing — no reveal, nothing detonates, it's all Volume One. So don't start it unless you're okay being left desperate for the next one. I was furious and I already want Book Two. Read it, and then call me, because I need to talk about Randi and that pastry.
