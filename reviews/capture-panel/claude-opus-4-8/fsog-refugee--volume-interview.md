# Capture panel — fsog-refugee · volume-interview

T1 — Where it leaves me with each of them, right now.

Vee. Tender, and a little protective the way you get about someone you've watched be brave without an audience. She ends the book *more herself* than she started — chapter 31, the photo she cropped for her parents and sent Pace whole, "that part was hers," and the midnight coat over nothing that she chose down to the last inch. That growth is real and I love her for it. But she got Pace back inside a plan she doesn't know she's standing in, walking on Randi's line in her own head — "you've got the goods, gorgeous girl" — and so what I mostly feel is dread on her behalf. She's the only honest person at the table and she's the one with the least idea what the table is. I ache for her. Clean-hearted, and cornered, and doesn't know it.

Pace. This is the one that's complicated and I resent that it is. Chapter 25 handed me the machinery — the tutor, the hallway, the thing he loved turned into the punishment — and I understood him, and understanding him is not the same as trusting him, and I wrote that down at the time and I still mean it. But by the end my patience curdled into anger, and it's a *specific* anger: not at the con anymore, at the cowardice. Forty-five chapters of "believe what I do," the folded arms, letting her cry in the dark rather than spend one word. Sheri said it to his face — the not-saying matters more to you than she does — and she was right. So: I find him magnetic and I'm furious at him and I no longer think those cancel. The drawer, the two shirts in the kitchen — real. The silence at "I love how good we are together" — unforgivable in the small daily way that actually breaks people.

Randi. Here's what surprised me. I came in ready to hate her and the book took that away from me. By chapter 46, Gstaad, the perfect man and the perfect ball and her feeling *nothing* — I pity her, fully, and it cost me something to admit it. She loves Vee helplessly and is too much of a coward to risk the friendship by naming it, so she farms it instead and calls the grooming lunch. The pastry in 34 is the cruelest thing in the book and I still can't hate the woman who did it, because I watched her be that alone. So where she leaves me is the worst place: pity and alarm in the same breath. I'm frightened of what she'll do and frightened for what's been done to her, and I can't put those down.

(And Cassie, since she's the fourth chair — she leaves me steady. She's the one I trust and the one I'm bracing to see spent.)

T2 — What it refused to give me. What it still owes.

It refused me the reckoning, and it did that *on purpose*, and I both admire it and resent it — which I think is the intended weather. I said going in that my one real risk was an ending that never cocks the gun. It cocked it. Vee finally *asked* — chapter 45, at the door, "what am I to you out there, what do I tell my mother" — she named the exact wound I'd been sick about for forty chapters. So I got the crack. What I did not get, and what I was told on the jacket to expect eventually, is anything landing. Nobody sees. Nothing detonates. Cassie says "Hmm" and the volume ends.

So what it owes me is specific, and I'm keeping the receipts:

- The reveal. Vee finding out there was a plan. If that never comes, or comes as a cheap gotcha, the withholding I've been calling scrupulous becomes withholding I resent, and I'll feel used the way Vee's used — and I won't enjoy that mirror.
- The photo promise. "I'd never share your photograph," chapter 37, in a book *built* on things being shared. That's a loaded object. It had better come due.
- Whether Randi ever says the word, or spends Vee all the way down to nothing. I need her to arrive somewhere. Pity without consequence would be its own kind of cheat.

That's not the book failing me. That's the book writing an IOU in front of my face and signing it. But it is an IOU, and I'm owed the payment.

T3 — Your problem: Pace deceives her all volume and ends loved, trusted, unexposed. Nothing lands on him.

I feel the force of it and I mostly don't share it, and I want to be precise about why, because the precise version is also where I agree with you.

Where I don't: "nothing lands on him" is true about *exposure* and false about *cost*, and the book worked hard to make me feel the difference. The con got real for the con artists — chapter 24, in their own bed, Randi undone, Pace saying Vee's the most alive person in any room and meaning it. He's not a man running a scam and skating; he's a man caught in his own trap, and the trap has teeth for him. And the book never once *winks* at what he's doing. It says the ugly part out loud — "he took the body's answer for the true one, he usually did," chapter 3, flat, as a thing it wants me uneasy about. That's the whole reason I stayed. A book that styled his deception as charming would've hit the wall by chapter 10. This one indicts him in its own prose while making me feel the pull, and holds both. That's not him getting away with it. That's the *setup*.

Where I'm with you completely: he ends the volume with the whole of her and nothing spent back, and the book *let* that be the ending. That's what my T1 anger at him is — not the con, the cowardice, the "believe what I do," Sheri naming it and him still not paying. So yes: on the ledger of this single volume, he's up and she's down and no bill came.

Does it change whether I buy Book Two? No — and here's the honest mechanism. It's *because* nothing landed on him that I'm buying. I said it in my verdict: I refuse to be left in a house with a cocked gun. If Pace had been exposed on the last page I'd feel closed out and I might browse. Him ending unexposed is exactly the debt that makes me need the next book. So your problem is real, and it's the reason I'm in, not a reason I'm out.

The one thing I'll say back to you: my *yes* is conditional and I'd tell any friend the same. I'm buying Book Two to see the bill delivered. If Volume Two also ends with everything landing on Vee and nothing on Pace — if the reveal never comes or comes cheap, if the photo promise turns out to be decoration — then your problem stops being tension and becomes the book's actual position, and at that point I'm the one who got conned into finding a man's disregard romantic, which is the exact thing I've spent years walking out of rooms over. This volume earned the benefit of the doubt. It hasn't been *granted* the verdict yet. That's Book Two's job, and it's precisely why I want it.

Now call me, because I still need to talk about that pastry.
