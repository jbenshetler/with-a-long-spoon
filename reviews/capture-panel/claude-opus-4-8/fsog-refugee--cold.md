GATE 1 — The Bench
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 8
ALMOST-STOPPED: Early on — "the thing she had built her whole adult life around being looked at for," plus the apple-slicing described with that surveillance-level attention ("he had laid the slices in the bowl with the cut sides up because he had noticed that she ate them faster"). For a second I thought: is this going to be a man who's *managing* her, and am I supposed to find the management romantic? That's exactly the thing I leave for.
WHY: But it kept earning it. He backs off and lets her decide. He asks her word, twice, tells her to say it anytime and that he won't be upset. The safeword isn't decoration — the whole scene is built around him watching for her actual self to surface instead of the performed one, and when it does he's tender, not triumphant. And then the book does the thing I almost never get: it goes *inside her*, and complicates the whole thing — the begging is real, the being-seen is unbearable, she retreats behind the face. That last movement turned this from a well-written sex scene into a story. I'm genuinely unsettled and I want to know what she does.

GATE 2 — Standards
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 7
ALMOST-STOPPED: "She lay under the duvet and looked at the ceiling and did not cry." I worried for a beat this would tip into pure bleakness — a heroine who only gets acted upon and then dissociates in a cold room, chapter after chapter. The water-bottle-under-the-thumbnail detail from ch.1 and the not-crying here signal real damage, and I needed to trust the book knows that's damage and isn't just aestheticizing a sad pretty girl.
WHY: The sorority interrogation scene is *sharp* — the whole power grammar of who stays seated, the fraction-of-a-second pauses, the class-anxiety about "math, PhD" and the pickup truck. It's a completely different register from ch.1 and it proves the writer can do social knife-work, not just bedroom intensity. And it deepens Randi instead of just decorating her: the face, the portioning, the not-going-to-the-crying. I still want the heat-with-feeling I came for, and this chapter had none of the heat — but it bought real credit with the feeling.

GATE 3 — The Pointing Game
DECISION: CONTINUE
CAPTURE: 7
ALMOST-STOPPED: "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." And the whole current of him reading her helpless clench as consent while she keeps verbally deflecting. This is the closest the book has come to the exact thing I flee: a man deciding what a woman *really* wants over her own stated framing, and the narration siding with him. If Randi's "yes" ends up being something he manufactured and the book calls it a gift, I'm gone.
WHY: What keeps me is that the book seems to *know* this is the danger. It's not naive about him — it's showing me a man whose care is real and whose reading of her is also a kind of appropriation, and it's letting both be true at once. The pointing game itself is genuinely erotic and genuinely uneasy, which is the tension I actually read for. But the introduction of Vee in ch.4's setup worries me: is Randi now going to do to Vee what's being done to her? I'll follow it one more chapter to see whose side the book is on.

"the queen having decided there would be a later and that Vee would be in it."

WHY: This chapter is the pivot, and it's a smart, cold one — the POV hands off to the prey, and every warm beat (the right pen, the high table, "love you, girl") reads as grooming to me and as heaven to Vee. It's genuinely accomplished. But my *capture* dropped, because this is where the book stops being about the thing I was hunting for — heat with real consent and real warmth between two particular people — and becomes something more like a literary novel about damage reproducing itself. I admire it. I'm not sure it's giving *me* what I sat down wanting.

VERDICT
WOULD PAY: yes — but at $9.99 and with a slightly braced feeling, not a hungry one. The prose is far above genre grade, Randi is a real person rendered from the inside, and the consent architecture in chapter one is the most careful I've read in this genre. I'm paying for the writing and because I have to know whether the book indicts what Pace and Randi do or romanticizes it.
MAILING LIST: yes — I want to know what this author does next regardless.
