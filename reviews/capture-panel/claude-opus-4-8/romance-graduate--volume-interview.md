T1 — Vee, first, because she's the one who's under my skin. Where I'm left with her is not worry, exactly — it's a kind of protective dread that has love folded into it. She's the smartest, most alive consciousness in the book and she just discovered, in a kitchen, over a dish argument, that there's a door in her she thinks she opened herself. And I *cheered* for that moment and hated that I cheered, because I know who built the room the door opens onto. So right now I feel about Vee the way you feel watching someone you love walk happy into a house you know the floorplan of and they don't. I'm not detached from her. I'm complicit, same as I've been since the pointing game, and the book made sure I'd never get to feel clean about her joy. That's where she leaves me: rooting for a happiness that's a trap and being unable to stop.

Pace I'm frightened of and — this is the ugly part — not repelled by, which the book did to me on purpose and I resent a little. After the Daphne chapter I can't hold him as a villain and I can't acquit him, and the discomfort of holding both is exactly where the book parks me. He'd pass a lie detector. That's the horror. Every tender thing is the instrument and he can't tell the difference between tenderness and the trap because tenderness is *how he was trapped*, at fifteen, by a nineteen-year-old, and he's paying it forward and calling it care. So I don't feel warmth toward him and I don't feel clean hatred either — I feel the specific nausea of understanding someone I should condemn. He's the most dangerous person in the book precisely because he means the least harm, and I end the volume unable to want him gone, which unsettles me more than if I could.

Randi is the one I actually ache over, and that surprised me — I came in reading her as the co-author, the calm hand on the table, and I'm leaving with her as the person most likely to get destroyed by what she built. She lost the game to her own heart on two fronts: she wants Pace and she wants Vee, and she's told herself the second is a gift she's arranging for her man. That's the lie she can't survive keeping. When she *left* in "Underneath" — she's never left — that was the crack, and by "Gone" she's holding a want she can't set down because it's hers, not the plan's. So I feel tenderness toward Randi and a fair amount of grief already booked. She owes Vee a debt so large it can only be paid by wrecking herself, and I think some part of her knows it. And I know you only asked about three, but I can't answer honestly without saying: the person the ending leaves me sickest over is Cassie, who isn't in your question, and that's the point.
