REACTION

Oh, this is sinister. This is *so* quietly, beautifully sickening, and I am completely hooked.

Look at how the jacket told us the truth, but reading the actual mechanism is a completely different kind of horror. The blurb pitched this lush, romantic, psychological awakening where Vee is the protagonist entering an erotic dream curated by two fascinating people. But experiencing the architecture of the trap from the inside of Pace's head—and seeing Randi's total, terrified compliance—makes the skin on my arms prickle.

Pace is terrifying precisely because he thinks he's being benevolent. That’s the genius of the framing here: the author isn’t writing a mustache-twirling villain; she is writing a man who genuinely believes he is giving Randi "gifts," who believes he is setting her free, while systematically dismantling her boundaries and engineering her desires. When he asks her in bed if she wants to taste another woman, he catches her in that completely vulnerable, post-orgasmic state where she can't defend herself. And then Randi—who in Chapter 2 was lying frozen under her duvet, terrified of being seen, realizing *he's hard to say no to*—does what cornered people do: she scrambles for a script. She offers a threesome because that makes it safe, makes it about *his* pleasure, puts the mask back on.

And Pace's internal monologue? He congratulates himself on seeing through the mask, decides he knows what she wants better than she does, and immediately weaponizes it. "I point. You pick." The casual arrogance of turning the quad into a hunting ground, framing it as her liberation, when it is entirely his design! He leads her right to the cafeteria. He points at Vee. He scripts the collision.

And then Vee walks out. The contrast between Randi (rigid, starved, hyper-managed, living on thin apple slices and panic) and Vee (lush, athletic, laughing with her throat bare to the sky, entirely uncured by the sorority machine) is devastating. Randi doesn't just desire Vee; she desires the freedom Vee possesses. And Pace sees that hunger and points a gun at it. The calculation of that staged bump—the dancer's weight, the apology extracted from Vee—it's so sharp, so socially exact. Vee is already being managed before she even knows they exist.

What makes this work so brilliantly for me as a reader who is usually hyper-alert to manipulation is that *the book knows*. The text isn't asking me to think Pace is just a hot, dominant dream-boat who understands women. The prose lays bare the terrifying precision of his paternalistic control, set against Randi’s frantic need to appease him and maintain control of her own unraveling psyche, with poor, unsuspecting Vee caught in the crosshairs. It’s predatory, it’s psychological, and the book is looking straight at it without blinking. I need to see the trap snap shut.
