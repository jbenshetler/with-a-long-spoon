REACTION

Oh, this is sickeningly good and thoroughly chilling.

Chapter 1 and 2 set up Randi's brittle, meticulously controlled armor—the absolute terror she felt at being truly seen and undone on Pace's bench, the way she had to lie to her sisters and lie to herself just to survive the exposure. And now, Chapter 3 reveals the engine of the trap.

Pace asks her in bed if she wants to taste another woman, and her body betrays her before her mind can construct a defense. But watch what happens next: she can't own that desire as her own—it's too dangerous, too destabilizing to her carefully managed identity—so she repackages it as a gift to *him*. "Only in a threesome. With you." And Pace, with all his quiet, devastating psychological acuity, sees right through it, *knows* she's deflecting the hunger onto him to make it safe, and instead of leaving it alone or letting her process it, he weaponizes it into an afternoon game.

"I point. You pick."

The dynamic between Pace and Randi here is so layered it makes my hair stand up. He acts out of what he genuinely frames to himself as benevolence and generosity—he wants to "give that wanting air in daylight without making her pin it beneath a solemn name," he wants to indulge her appetite, he wants to take care of her. He truly believes he is freeing her. But the reality is that he is directing an engineered hunt, subtly training her to act on a taboo appetite under the guise of an errand for his pleasure. He is giving her permission, but he is also handing her the leash.

And then enters Vee.

God, the description of Vee—laughing with her whole body, completely uncurated, taking up space, eating the cafeteria food, loose red hair, clothes that just cover rather than construct a persona—she is the antithesis of everything Randi polices in herself. Randi's immediate, involuntary, breath-stealing *yes* is so sharp because Vee embodies the unmonitored aliveness that Randi starves herself of.

And then the execution: "Go meet her." And Randi's predatory, seamless, dancer-honed "bump" into Vee. Watching Randi deploy all that sorority training and grace to manufacture an innocent, laughing collision—making *Vee* feel like the clumsy one, making *Vee* apologize—while Pace leans against a light pole like a satisfied puppeteer... it is masterclass manipulation.

What makes this completely riveting rather than repellent is that the book is hyper-aware of the horror. It’s not asking me to think Pace is just a sweet, kinky boyfriend doing a fun exercise. The mechanics of power, self-deception, and predator-prey dynamics are laid out with surgical precision. Randi is being managed by Pace, and now Randi is managing Vee, and Vee doesn’t even know she’s on the board yet. The blurb's promise—"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem"—is clicking into place with terrifying elegance. I am completely hooked.
