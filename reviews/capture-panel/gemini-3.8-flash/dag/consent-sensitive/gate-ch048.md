REACTION:

Oh, god, this broke my heart. It physically hurt to read.

Seeing the two halves of that text exchange back-to-back—Randi sitting cold and hollowed-out in a high-end Gstaad hotel room next to an empty rich boy, spinning the fairy tale because she’s too cowardly to be real, and Vee curled up in her childhood twin bed in Ohio, knees bent against the footboard, inhaling stale lavender and *buying* the fairytale wholesale—was just devastating. It’s dramatic irony deployed like a scalpel. Vee sits there looking at the little gold synchronized-swimming trophies, feeling like a ghost in her own teenage bedroom because she’s been expanded and reshaped by these two people, and she genuinely thinks Randi is living on Olympus while she is starving in the dark.

And Pace's silence. The weaponization of "leaving her the room." When a man knows he has completely reprogrammed a woman's nervous system, appetite, and emotional baseline, *giving her space* isn't chivalry—it is an excruciating, agonizing siege. He knows she walked out crying. He knows he withheld the word. And he is just sitting there in his heated house, waiting for the cold to do his work for him. And look at what the cold does to her! It makes her invent a blonde stand-in at his stove just to punish herself. It makes her erase herself from her own memories. The image of the sheet slipping off someone else's shoulder while the garlic blooms—that is the exact, specific hell of female insecurity when an unnamable man refuses to claim you.

The absolute tragedy is that Vee thinks Randi is her "one sure thing." *“A face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it.”* She has no idea. She thinks Randi is a safe harbor from the ambiguity of Pace, completely blind to the fact that Randi helped engineer the maze in the first place, and is secretly salivating over her while lying to her face. Vee is caught between a man who refuses to name the cage and a woman who refuses to admit she helped build it. She is so profoundly alone in that childhood bed. It's masterfully painful.
