REACTION

Oh, this book is so sharp it makes my ribs ache.

Seeing the sorority house after the high-heat, high-surrender claustrophobia of Chapter 1 is brilliant. The transition from the warm, wood-and-leather vacuum of Pace’s bedroom to the bright, chilly, razor-thin policing of the sorority common room exposes *everything*. The social economy in that foyer—Brooke not standing, Brooke keeping the laptop open, the subtle paper-cuts about the car, the discipline masquerading as effortless collegiate sisterhood—is just another kind of control, but unlike Pace’s, it’s petty, predatory, and demands total performance with zero safety.

And Randi—God, Randi lying on her bed with her shoes still on, under the synthetic duvet that doesn't warm her, unable to even cry because the "not-going" is a muscle she’s flexed her whole life. That line: *“He’s hard to say no to,” she heard, in her room, in a different key.*

Because here is what I know from the jacket: Randi isn't the victim here; Randi is the *architect* (or co-architect) of what is coming for Vee. Or rather, she is going to be. And seeing her unraveling right now, seeing how terrifyingly dismantled she was by Pace’s exact, unhurried exposure of her, makes the impending trap for Vee so much darker, so much more complex. Randi couldn't stand being seen, couldn't handle being the one on the bench, begging, undone. The sheer psychological terror of being known by a man who refuses to play the sorority game, who peels the veneer away... and now I am watching her retreat behind the deadbolt of her own face, already anticipating how that unprocessed panic and hunger is going to redirect toward someone else.

The prose completely understands the trap. It knows what Brooke is doing; it knows what Randi is doing to survive Brooke; and it knows that Pace’s courtly, gentle dominance felt to Randi like an existential threat precisely because it actually worked. I am riveted.
