REACTION:

*Oh.* Holy shit.

I started this chapter assuming I was reading Vee—the blurb promised Vee being seduced, Vee being the project. So the entire time in the kitchen and on the bench, I was reading this with a sickening, razor-sharp dread, watching this meticulous, patient man take apart a sorority girl who rations apple slices and performs her whole life. The clinical, almost terrifying tenderness of Pace—the fact that he doesn't rush, that he waits for the performance to crack, that he *engineered* the exact angle of vulnerability—had my heart in my throat. I was terrified of him. The power dynamic is dizzying because he gives her every technical choice while completely controlling the architecture of the room.

And then he says: *"There you are, Randi."*

The floor completely dropped out from under me.

This isn't Vee. This is *Randi*. The "glamorous new friend" from the jacket. The architect of the "game." The co-conspirator.

The moment she locked that bathroom door, the book blew wide open for me. Because this isn't just dark erotica about an older/dominant guy dismantling an innocent girl; the book *knows* exactly what it is holding. Pace thinks he's being this benevolent, profoundly perceptive shepherd leading a guarded woman to her authentic self ("That's not what this night was for"—god, the terrifying self-righteousness of a dominant who thinks he's purely selfless!). He thinks he won. He thinks he gave her a gift.

And Randi is in the bathroom having a catastrophic identity crisis because she didn't just get spanked and edged—she got *breached*. The horror of having your carefully managed armor peeled back by someone who thinks he's loving you, the absolute humiliation of the genuine *please*, and the bone-chilling realization that she is already building the deadbolt back up: *"The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again."*

The jacket said two people planned the best year of Vee's life. But Randi isn't doing this to Vee from a position of cool, untouchable superiority. Randi is doing whatever comes next *because of this*. She is running from this terrifying exposure, or trying to redirect Pace's gaze, or trying to triangularly survive him. The psychological machinery here is breathtakingly precise. The prose is quiet, weighted, totally unhurried, and completely aware of the danger in every single room. I am hooked.
