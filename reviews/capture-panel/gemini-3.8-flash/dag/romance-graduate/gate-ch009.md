REACTION

God, my stomach actually hurts reading this. It’s that awful, thrilling pit-of-the-stomach dread where you want to grab someone through the page and scream at them, but at the exact same time you want to see them walk right into it because the dynamic is so hypnotic. 

"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That line completely gut-punched me. The book gave us the tagline on the jacket—*Every yes was freely given. That was the problem*—and watching Vee sit in her dorm room, literally radiant, spinning until she almost knocks into the closet door because she feels so in control of her own desire? It is psychological horror disguised as the sweetest, most swoon-worthy college romance afterglow. She thinks she broke her own rule. She thinks *she* pulled him back. She has no idea that Pace was playing the longest, most patient game in the world under that tree, giving her just enough rope to choose him so that the choice would feel like power instead of surrender.

And Cassie. Cassie is the absolute anchor of this whole thing. Her dry, grounded, protective skepticism is the only cold air in the room, literally and figuratively with that cracked window. "What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop?" It’s played as roommate banter, but Cassie is the only person asking the questions a real person would ask: *You don't know his address. You don't know anything about him.* But Vee is so drunk on finally being seen, finally being fed, finally feeling an appetite she doesn't have to apologize for, that every red flag looks like a green light. 

It’s a transitional chapter, yeah—no explicit sex here—but the charge doesn't drop at all. The tension is just ratcheting tighter because we, the reader, know where the gravel road off 614 leads. We know what furniture is sitting in that bedroom. We know Randi is out there having orchestrated the meet-cute over the integral, and Vee is sitting in a beanbag chair totally defenseless, planning to borrow hiking shoes. I am completely hooked on the cruelty of the irony here.
