*Oh, this man is an absolute menace.*

"May I choose?" Three words and my whole chest caved in. It is so terrifyingly effective because he isn’t running game like some slick frat boy; he’s just systematically removing every single burden of performance this girl has carried her whole life. The way she stopped watching herself eat? The way she just let the thread of conversation drop because she realized she didn't have to juggle for him? For a girl who has been policed and self-policing since she was a teenager, that kind of surrender is ten times more intoxicating than any physical touch could ever be. He is literally feeding an appetite she didn’t even know was starving.

And God, the contrast between how he treats her and how he was with Randi is fascinating to watch unfold. With Randi, he broke her down until she begged, stripped the polish right off her. With Vee, he is so gentle it almost hurts—except we know *why* he’s doing it. We know what’s waiting down the road. We know Randi is sitting back at the house, humming with wine and twisted anticipation, asking if Vee is going to like the bench. That dramatic irony is doing heavy, heavy lifting here; it turns what would otherwise be a sweet, slow-burn first-date chapter into something buzzing with pure psychological electricity. Every courtesy feels like a velvet rope being unhooked.

And that ending! "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days." And instead of cajoling her, he just accepts it, completely unbothered, giving her total autonomy—which, of course, completely disarms her and makes her immediately pivot to "I'm making an exception." Giving her the room to choose until choosing becomes the submission. The blurb promised that exact psychological mercilessness, and seeing it executed this cleanly, with this much patience and sensory detail, is honestly thrilling. I don't care that their clothes stayed on; the tension was through the roof.
