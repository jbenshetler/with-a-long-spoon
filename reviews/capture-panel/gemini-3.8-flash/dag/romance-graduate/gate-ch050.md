REACTION

Oh, Randi. You absolute, terrifying, hollowed-out creature.

To jump straight from Vee crying on Pace's porch into the pristine, arctic numbness of Randi's Gstaad holiday was like getting shoved headfirst into a frozen lake. And honestly? It completely rewired how I see her. All this time, watching her through Vee’s starstruck eyes, I had her pegged as the apex predator—smooth, ruthless, pulling all the strings with a smirk while she seasoned Vee for slaughter. But inside her own head? She’s starving just as hard as Vee was, except she’s doing it in designer ski gear and diamond studs.

The whole sequence with John was brutal in its perfection. On paper, it’s the exact romance novel setup that BookTok swoons over: the banter in the lift line, the fake-dating game, the effortless wealth, the white-tie ball, the vintage sleigh through the falling snow. It was textbook Prince Charming. And it felt like eating cardboard. The way she lay there afterward—comparing the best, most flawless version of the acceptable world to a quick sandwich on a busy afternoon—completely gutted me. She can get laid by a handsome, wealthy prince in an alpine chalet, do all the technical choreography of pleasure, and still walk away feeling literally nothing, marked only by smeared lipstick and the cold she brought into the room with her.

And then the contrast hits like a truck. Pace's terrifying attention—how he demands you look, demands you participate, leaves the work of dancing to you, sees straight through to the ugly, hidden parts. And Vee. The fact that Randi is standing at that hotel mirror realizing that a random Tuesday across a diner table from an awkward, babbling economics major holds more genuine heat and life than a five-star New Year's Eve ball... God.

She isn't just playing a game with Vee; she is desperately, pathetically clinging to her. When she got that text and texted back the glossy, hollow performance while holding onto that one real line—*miss your face most though, gorgeous*—it made my chest ache. She thinks she's orchestrating this elegant trap where Vee will eventually fall into her lap without risking the friendship, but she's just as trapped by her own terror. She’s terrified of being seen, terrified of losing the one person who loves her without needing currency.

Meanwhile, Vee just walked out into the dark after calling Pace's entire calculated silence what it is. Neither of them knows the ground is completely gone under their feet. I need to see what happens when they all get back into the same room.
