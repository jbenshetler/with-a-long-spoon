WHO'S WHO

- **Vee (Vivienne)**: The dam has broken. She isn't just an awestruck girl basking in gourmet food and mind-melting orgasms anymore; the silence where a name should be has finally started starving her out. She went from timidly asking what they were at Chili's, to stripping off his flannel shirt in the freezing dorm dark because his reflected scent wasn't enough, to standing in his hallway in her coat and walking out into the cold when he told her to "believe what I do." Back home in Ohio for Christmas, she feels completely alienated from her narrow childhood bed, scissoring her bare leg out of the induction photo so her parents only see the safe half. She is grieving a relationship she can't even prove existed, terrified Pace has already replaced her with someone easier, and clinging to Randi as the one sure thing waiting for her in the spring.
- **Pace (Peter)**: The fortress has a massive crack in it. We finally got inside his head without the smug architectural sheen, and what’s underneath is classic, cowardly emotional trauma. He had an older tutor, Daphne, who taught him how to survive the world, said "I love you," and then left him—so he decided the *words* were the curse and that keeping them locked away would make loss survivable. He deliberately deflected Vee’s stunning "crackers and a meal" speech into gross physical compliments (*"what your body does"*) purely out of terror. Sheri dragged him for filth over a diner burger, calling out his self-righteous code for what it is: pure cowardice. He spent New Year's Eve getting blackout drunk alone on whiskey, vomiting over his floor, and weeping over a cold bowl of boiled chicken when a passing truck wasn't Vee's car. Yet, terrifyingly, even while sobbing over Vee, he is *still* mentally sketching out the threesome with Randi in his kitchen.
- **Randi (Miranda)**: Living in her own gilded hell. Her high-gloss armor took a brutal hit in the library when the stats project flagged "MIRA"—discovering in cold government data that her father took massive PPP loans and fired everyone anyway, exposing the rot behind her family's wealth. Vee covered for her with total grace, deepening Randi's twisted guilt. In Gstaad for the holidays, she played the perfect sorority princess, bagged a gorgeous, pedigree-perfect lawyer named John, and found the whole thing completely dead inside—no more filling than a quick sandwich. She admitted to herself in the mirror that she is hopelessly, desperately in love with Vee, that Vee is the only person she's ever loved without calculation, but she is too terrified of losing her to tell the truth. So she feeds Vee fairytale texts while hiding behind the word "friendship."
- **Cassie**: The razor. She ran the loan data that exposed Randi’s dad, and she sat across from Vee in the cafeteria asking the only clean, load-bearing question in this whole book: *"So what is he... Not to your dad. To you."* When Vee admitted she couldn't and wouldn't say, Cassie let the silence sit. She sees Vee slipping, sees the physical transformation, and remains the only person grounded in reality.
- **Sheri**: A godsend. The five-foot, foul-mouthed, bike-racing country girl who sees straight through Pace’s high-minded bullshit. She stole his fries, mocked the "philosophy" of his food, and told him flat out that if he can't say the words, not saying them matters more to him than keeping Vee.

WHERE THINGS STAND

The entire foundation of the book just shifted from an intoxicating secret game into a three-way emotional catastrophe over the winter break.

In mid-November, the stats group project hit a landmine: Cassie pulled PPP loan fraud data, and Randi pulled up her own county to prove her dad was an honorable exception—only to click on the biggest outlier ("MIRA") and realize her father took the cash and laid off all his workers. Randi went white, muttered "cheating bastards," and Vee immediately stepped in, sliding the laptop away to give Randi cover without saying a word.

Two days later, Vee broke her five-day study fast and rushed to Pace's house. After frantic, desperate sex on the bed, he made an intricate homemade pizza with water-buffalo cheese. She nervously handed him his washed flannel shirt, confessing that she needed him to wear it all weekend so it would smell like him again for her lonely dorm nights; he put it on immediately over his clothes with a rare, open smile.

Over Thanksgiving, Vee went home and had to field her parents' questions about the burgundy dress and her sudden weight loss, admitting to Cassie afterward that she couldn't tell them Pace made the dress because they'd understand him taking it off her far better than sewing it. On Sunday morning, November 29, out on Pace’s cool porch under his quilt, Vee finally worked up the courage to offer him her heart, offering the breathtaking metaphor: *"I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal."* Pace froze, took two heavy breaths, and deliberately gave her the safe, cowardly deflection: *"I love how good we are together... What your body does... I've never."* Vee felt the door slam shut in her chest, burying the hurt in the dorm laundry room two days later.

By December 4, the poison had spread. Vee snapped in the dark, demanding to know if they were just sex and why they never leave the house. Pace calmly agreed to take her out to Chili’s. The bright, roaring restaurant overwhelmed him; his eyes darted to the TVs, he squared his fork with obsessive irritation, and Vee felt the humiliation of realizing he hated being seen with her. The nachos tasted like salted cardboard compared to his cooking. Back at his house, she accused him of punishing her, and he disarmed her with the lethal, seductive line: *"In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you... I'm listening now."* She "forgave" him and begged him with her body to lose his clinical control. He broke, taking her with raw, shaking urgency—but lying in the aftermath, she realized that even undone, he still hadn't said a single word of commitment.

A week later, December 11, Vee lay awake in her cold bunk, realized the flannel shirt only held her own body heat, stripped it off, and left it in a heap to freeze. The next evening, she drove to his house, left her bag in the car, and kept her coat buttoned to the chin. When he offered his usual curated evening, she demanded: *"What am I to you out there?"* Pace gave her the back-cover deflection: *"Believe what I do."* Vee countered with the ultimate cut: *"Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me,"* and walked out into the cold, leaving him frozen on the mat.

New Year's Eve fractured them completely:
- In Gstaad, Randi spent a glittering, picture-perfect night skiing, attending a white-tie ball, and sleeping with John, a wealthy law student. It felt completely empty. She looked in the mirror, realized Pace reaches further into her than John ever could, and admitted that she is hopelessly in love with Vee—and too cowardly to risk saying it. When Vee texted her a sweet, supportive check-in, Randi texted back the glamorous "fairytale" lie, slipping in a genuine *"miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋."*
- In Ohio, Vee froze in her childhood bed, suffocated by lavender plug-ins and old swimming trophies, obsessing over Randi’s feed and spiraling about Pace. Convinced Pace had already replaced her with an easy, slender blonde at his stove, she choked down the midnight hour alone, counting on the spring semester with Randi as her only lifeline.
- In Virginia, Pace had burgers with Sheri at a greasy-spoon diner. Sheri dismantled his excuses, calling him out for letting his refusal to say "I love you" cost him the girl. Pace went home, got blackout drunk on whiskey alone in the dark, and threw up all over the hall. On New Year's morning, hungover and scrubbing with bleach, he finally admitted his past with Daphne—the tutor who broke his heart after he said the words—and realized he gave Vee the smaller truth out of sheer terror. When a truck rumbled past on the road and didn't turn into his drive, he collapsed over a cold bowl of chicken and sobbed.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

- **Vee**: I have never been prouder of her, and my heart has never hurt for her more. Watching her demand daylight at Chili's, throw off the false warmth of that damn shirt, and walk out of his house when he tried to pay her with physical touch instead of words was magnificent. She finally found her spine. But seeing her lying in her childhood bed, scissoring her own identity in half and convincing herself Pace has happily moved on, is agonizing. She’s walking right into Randi’s arms next semester because Randi is the only one who makes her feel seen, completely unaware of the viper she’s hugging.
- **Pace**: He finally got what was coming to him, and it made him a thousand times more interesting. For fifty chapters he’s been a smug, god-complex architect hiding behind a stove and an ethical theorem. Seeing him get dragged by Sheri, puke on his own floor, and sob like a baby over cold boiled chicken because a truck wasn't Vee was the exact medicine he needed. The Daphne backstory explains his pathology, but it doesn't excuse the fact that he's a coward who hurt Vee to protect himself—and the fact that he's *still* picturing Randi and Vee together at his table proves his arrogance isn't fully cured.
- **Randi**: A stunning, tragic mess. Watching her discover her father's fraud stripped away the clean illusion of her pedigree, and her New Year's Eve in Gstaad was a masterpiece of psychological isolation. She had the exact Prince Charming her parents want for her, and she felt nothing because she's drowning in desire for Vee. She’s a villain, yes, but her cowardice makes her deeply human—she would rather feed Vee pretty lies and play with fire from behind the safety of "friendship" than risk the truth.
- **Cassie**: The rock. Unbothered by Randi’s gloss, completely immune to bullshit. She asked the question that peeled the skin off Vee’s denial, and when the fallout happens, she is going to be the only anchor Vee has left.
- **Sheri**: An absolute breath of fresh air. Put her in every chapter. She gave Pace the exact verbal beatdown the reader has been screaming to give him for weeks.
