REACTION

Oh, god. The drawer.

I’m actually tearing up a little, and I am furious at how completely it worked on me. He took his neat, hyper-controlled, solitary bachelor bathroom, took his own razor and blades and shoved them into the cramped bottom drawer, and gave her the *top* one. The one your hand goes to first. With a little dish for her bobby pins.

It is the absolute deadliest kind of romantic gesture because it doesn’t look like romance to anyone on the outside; it looks like domestic accommodation, but to Vee—who has spent her whole life feeling clumsy, oversized, taking up too much room, uninvited—it is total capitulation. He made her a place.

And it breaks my heart into tiny pieces, because *we know.*

We know Daphne trained him. We know Randi is sleeping with him. We know Randi sat across a table two chapters ago eating pastries, drawing every explicit detail of Vee’s submission out of her mouth, and leaving sugar on her lips. And Vee is lying in his bed, looking at his sleeping face, quietly deciding in the dark: *I love him.* She admitted it to herself. She even whispered his real name to his sleeping ear. The dramatic irony is thick enough to choke on. Every single green dot on those dozens of scrunchies is a tether pulling her deeper into a snare she thinks is a sanctuary.

The weight-loss detail, too—her body quietly burning down weekends of him, her clothes hanging loose, the racks never fitting her build until now. It’s written with such psychological accuracy. She thinks she’s blooming, she thinks she’s finally being seen and fed and treasured. And he *is* treasuring her, in his terrifying, meticulous, Daphne-shaped way. That’s what makes this book so devastatingly good: Peter isn’t a cartoon villain twirling a mustache. He genuinely cooked her salmon and whisked mustard sauce and folded his entire life into a bottom drawer so she wouldn’t feel like a burden. The gentleness is real, and the trap is real, and they are the exact same thing.

I’m completely under this book’s spell. My thumb cannot hit "next" fast enough.
