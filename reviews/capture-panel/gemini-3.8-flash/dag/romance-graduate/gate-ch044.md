REACTION

*Oh.*

Oh, Pace, you absolute, mathematically broken bastard.

I’m sitting here with my hand over my mouth because that broke me. That actually broke me. Seeing the other side of New Year's Eve—after seeing Vee curled up in her childhood bed holding the lavender sheets to her chin, convinced he was at the stove smiling at some generic tan blonde—and finding him eating a diner burger with Sheri, drinking himself sick alone in the dark, throwing up in his bathroom, and then sobbing over a bowl of cold steamed broccoli because a truck on the highway *wasn't her car*.

Sheri is fantastic. I already liked the idea of her from the porch mention, but having her sit across from him in an old double-wide trailer diner, stealing his fries, drinking a chocolate shake, and calling him on his absolute bullshit was pure gold. *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you—from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* THANK YOU, SHERI. Say it louder for the brilliant idiot in the back. And her teasing him about the "philosophy" of his food—God, he needed someone to pop that velvet bubble so badly. He lives so entirely inside his own head, building these airtight, immaculate little systems where he gives a woman custom-sewn dresses, cleared drawers, four orgasms a night, and artisanal pizza, convinced that if he just builds the structure perfectly, he never has to risk his heart.

And then the reveal of Daphne. *The girl with brown hair and glasses who laughed with her hand over her mouth.*

It makes the most terrifying, heartbreaking sense. Of course his emotional paralysis wasn't malice—it was raw, unhealed scar tissue. He was a kid who didn't know how to navigate the world, this older girl taught him how to survive it, he gave her the word, and when she walked, it wrecked his entire existence. So he built a mathematical equation out of his grief: *If I say the word, losing her destroys me. If I don't say the word, loss is survivable.* He literally convinced himself that the syllables were the curse.

And the horrific tragedy of it is that he *knows*. He sat there with his hangover and his cold boiled chicken and admitted it to himself: *He had known what she offered him on the porch and wanted to answer it... He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him.*

He loves her. He actually, fully loves her.

And yet—the thorn in the velvet that keeps me from letting him completely off the hook—he's *still* nursing that fantasy of the three of them. Flipping through the albums, looking at Randi, remembering the sidewalk kiss Randi bragged about while he was inside her, and picturing the three of them laughing in his kitchen. He still has no idea the radioactive bomb he's sitting on. He doesn't know Randi stole Vee's panties. He doesn't know Randi's father is a crook. He doesn't know Randi is unraveling. He thinks it's all going to neatly click into a morning where he cooks and they both sit there in his sheets.

The contrast between the two halves of this chapter—the sharp, funny, grounded diner conversation with Sheri versus the quiet, wretched, bleachy hangover morning—was exquisite. When his fork stopped because he heard tires on the road, and his whole body leaned toward the gravel drive, and it just... kept going? Oof. A physical blow to the sternum.

He knows what he has to do. The question is whether his arrogant, terrified control will let him do it before Randi burns the whole house down.
