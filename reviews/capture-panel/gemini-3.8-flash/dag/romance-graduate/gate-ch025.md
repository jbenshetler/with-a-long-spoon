REACTION:

Oh, my god. Oh, my *god*.

I am sitting here with my hand over my mouth. I went into this chapter bracing myself, honestly, because chapter 24 left me with this sick, electric dread—Randi riding him in that low lamplight, the two of them coolly discussing Vee like a prize filly they’re breaking in together. I was waiting for the other shoe to drop, waiting for the trap to spring, waiting for the manipulation to show its teeth.

And instead, Helen Rivers just completely dismantled me.

This chapter is the quiet after the storm, and it is devastatingly, heartbreakingly tender. The writing here isn't just "good"—it’s terrifyingly observant. The contrast between Vee’s warm, beige, church-basement Ohio childhood (the unopened carousel jar of saffron old enough to vote!) and Peter’s stark, hyper-isolated reality.

*Peter.*

Finding out he’s twenty-two knocked the wind out of me. He carries himself with this immense, terrifying gravity, this master-craftsman courtliness that makes you think of a man in his thirties who has lived five lifetimes. But no—he was fifteen. He was a fifteen-year-old child who loved something so purely that an adult woman put his desk in the hallway to punish him for it. And then they threw him into a dorm full of grown men where he had to literally build an armor of muscle so nobody would hurt him.

And Daphne. *Christ.* The arithmetic Vee did in her head—nineteen and fifteen. "That he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck." That line cut right through my ribs. The predatory older girl assigned to "tutor" him, the clean, ugly word Vee swallows down because Peter still defends it like a shield. It explains *everything* about the way he operates. The hyper-control. The total, terrifying absence of impulse. The way he needs every single thing to be chosen, consented to, measured, and held with the absolute, agonizing focus of a man under a squat bar bent like spaghetti.

Vee seeing him—truly seeing him, recognizing that the way he fucks her is the same presence he brings to the bar, and calling him *Peter* into the dark—made my chest ache. She isn't just falling for a fantasy of a sexy older lumberjack-mathematician. She is falling in love with a damaged, brilliant, fiercely guarded boy-man who is letting her touch the scar on his thigh and simply saying, *Not tonight,* without taking her hand away.

And the horror—the absolute, suffocating romance-tragedy of it—is that *I know what Randi is doing.* I know the architecture of the game. But sitting on that couch under the quilt with the tea gone cold, it doesn't feel like a game. It feels like two starving people finding the only other person on the planet who speaks the language. When a book makes you fall in love with the hero at the exact same moment it reminds you that the ground beneath the heroine's feet is hollow... that's the high-wire act. That's why I read. I need the next chapter immediately.
