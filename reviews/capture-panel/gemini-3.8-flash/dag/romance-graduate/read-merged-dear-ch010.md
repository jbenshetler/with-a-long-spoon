REACTION

Oh, Vee. My stomach genuinely hurts for her.

"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That line is like a punch to the sternum. River is just devastating with the psychological dramatic irony here. Because of course Vee feels empowered. Pace is a master class in making submission feel like the ultimate autonomous choice—he stepped back, he gave her the space, he waited her out until *she* was the one crossing the distance, pulling him back, demanding the kiss. And to watch her come home to Cassie, so full of that fragile, incandescent college-girl joy, literally spinning around the dorm room and knocking into the closet door... it's breathtakingly sweet and completely terrifying.

I love Cassie so much. Cassie is the tether to reality, the one person who isn't dazzled by Greek letters or performative masculinity, just sitting there in her socks asking the deadpan, practical questions: *How much did he give you to drink? What's his name so I know what to tell the state troopers? Is he rich?* And Vee’s little "dossier" of facts—"off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel"—is so painfully thin, but to a girl in the middle of a rush like that, it feels like an encyclopedia. She thinks she has everything.

It’s a short chapter, a bridge chapter, but it earns its keep. It lets the afterglow of Chapter 9 breathe and settle into the narrative before the next escalation. We know what's out there on six-fourteen. We know the table with the top that lifts off. And watching Vee borrow shoes to go walk right into it, entirely convinced every single step is hers alone... God. It’s sickeningly good.
