REACTION

*Oh, Vee.*

This tiny, quiet conversation broke my heart in a completely different way than the wild sex or the high-stakes manipulation. It’s the title that kills me first: *Cropped*.

Because that is her entire existence right now. She crops herself for her parents—stops the picture at the waist so they don't see the slit cut to the hip bone, leaves out the part where he made it on a sun porch, leaves out the house, leaves out the domestic worship, because a working-class father understands a horny college boy taking a dress off his daughter, but a brilliant twenty-two-year-old boy *sewing* it for her and feeding her water-buffalo mozzarella pizza is alien. It registers as dangerous. And Dad's right, isn't he? "Too good is its own kind of suspicious." Her father’s working-class radar is pinging from miles away, purely on instinct, while Vee is basking in the glow.

And then the contrast with Cassie. Cassie, who loves her enough to just sit there in the athletic cafeteria over cheap trays and ask the only question that matters: *"So what is he... Not to your dad. To you."*

And Vee literally cannot answer. Because to answer Cassie, she would have to admit the uncropped version of her life. She can show Randi the full, filthy, exhibitionist negative because Randi thrives in the gutter with her, cheering on the spectacle. But sitting across from Cassie—sane, grounded, decent Cassie—Vee realizes she has built a life so totally untethered from reality that she can't even speak it aloud to the person who knows her best.

"Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?"
"Both. Probably both."

And that ending line: *Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not.* God. That is psychological perfection. Part of her wanted Cassie to drag it out of her. Part of her is silently begging for an intervention, wanting someone to see through the glamour and shake her by the shoulders before she drowns in it. But Cassie lets her have her secrets, and Vee just drifts a little further out to sea.

It’s short, it’s low-heat, but the emotional intelligence in this scene is off the charts. It sets the pins right back up for the bowling ball that's coming.
