REACTION

*“Pace. Shrank your sweater.”*

I let out the loudest, ugliest laugh at Cassie’s deadpan delivery. God, I love Cassie so much. Vee spinning this frantic, ridiculous cover story about a ruined sweater and hot laundry water to explain why she was wandering around in his shirt, and Cassie just letting the silence hang like a dropped weight before bursting out laughing. Cassie knows. She knows the sweater didn't shrink, she knows Pace is an exact freak who probably reads the care label on a dishtowel before washing it, and she just lets Vee have her little lie without grilling her. That is what real friendship looks like when you’re twenty-one. It’s warm, it’s funny, and it’s completely un-performative.

And the contrast between the two rooms is what really got me here. This entire sequence—the luxury of his house, the sensory overload of the mushrooms, the acreage of the bed, the sheer erotic glut of the weekend—and then Vee comes back to the suite shower that trickles, the all-in-one shampoo, the radiator clanking, and the draft Cassie leaves in the window on purpose. It doesn't make her resent her real life; it grounds her. But then that tiny, quiet moment at the end: *“My mother says thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again.”*

My throat actually got tight reading that. Because Vee’s own family is a cold, lavender-scented trap where she suffocates and hides her body, and here is Cassie’s mom, the working-class woman with a closet full of saved gift boxes and a baby in the house, genuinely asking for Vee. That hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck—that's family. Real family. Not the transactional ice of Randi’s parents in Gstaad, not the quiet distance of Vee’s own.

It’s a bridge chapter, completely quiet, but it breathes so well. After the sheer, unhinged heat of the doorstep and the bed, we needed to see Vee touch down on campus and feel the difference in the air before the semester starts swinging. Because the storm is still coming: Randi is waiting for her debrief, Pace wants to meet all her friends (and oh boy, when Randi and Pace sit in the same room pretending they didn't hand-pick Vee together, the floor is going to drop out), and Cassie still has that spreadsheet. But right now? Vee is in the top bunk, her hand against the cinderblock, just quietly, deeply happy. I’m letting her have it. For about five minutes, anyway.
