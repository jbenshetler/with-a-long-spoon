REACTION

My god, the *food*. The entire architecture of this book is written in what people eat, what they refuse to eat, and who is allowed to pour the wine.

Pace making her chicken and potatoes in cream, ladling it into the small bowl—the one he slices her apples into, like she’s an exotic pet he’s domesticating—and Randi taking a single spoon, skimming the top, and just… setting it down. Leaving it to grow a skin under the fluorescent light. That hurt him worse than a slap. When she reached out and grabbed the neck of the wine bottle to pour her *own* glass, I physically held my breath. That was the first true act of rebellion against him in twenty-three chapters. He controls the environment, he controls the pacing, he pours the drink, he heats the room. And Randi just took the bottle.

And then she laid it completely bare: *"How long are you going to take?"*

What kills me is that they are both so utterly, terribly right from their own twisted angles. Randi sees the reality: Pace is luxuriating in Vee. He has this massive, warm, responsive, gorgeous girl utterly under his spell, coming over on Friday, losing her mind on his sheets, drinking wine in his kitchen at midnight, and Pace gets to pretend he’s this high-minded saint who is just "letting her get there on her own." Randi calls him out on the absolute luxury of his patience: *“You’ve got her all weekend, every weekend, and you’re in no hurry, and I can’t think why you would be.”* She sees that his "care" is a form of hoarding. He’s gorging himself on Vee’s innocence and calling it chivalry.

And Randi is starving. She’s on the outside. She has to sit on a sun porch eating mimosas while Vee glows about how gentle and patient he was, knowing damn well Pace doesn't treat *her* like that, knowing she engineered the whole thing and is now being edged out of her own design.

The sex on the bed after—bleak. Utterly bleak, fast, shut-eyed, mechanical. No *“tell me.”* That’s the detail that broke it. She usually needs his commentary, needs the narration of Vee to get off, and tonight she shut her eyes, used his body like a blunt instrument to stop the noise in her head, came against his shoulder without looking at him, and rolled into the cashmere with her back turned.

And Pace standing in the dark kitchen afterward, putting a saucer over the cold stew to save it for her because he literally cannot compute a problem he can't solve with patient, methodical domesticity… chilling. He thinks he’s so smart, but he is completely blind to the rot spreading inside Randi. This thing is going to detonate.
