REACTION

The eating in this chapter did something to me. Not because it was food, but because of what food has meant in every chapter before this one: Randi and her tiny sliced apples, Randi’s mother living on egg whites, Randi refusing the quad cheeseburger because *I can’t eat that*, Vee swallowing her mother’s voice about what’s *plenty* and *enough now*. And then Pace takes her to this little dark, garlic-smelling room and says, *May I choose?*

God, that line. It’s so courtly and quiet, and it could be terrifying—we know what he’s doing, we know Randi is sitting back on her own bed with that low hum in her ribs, waiting to see what he brings home—but inside Vee’s perspective, it feels like the first breath of clean air she’s had in years. To simply hand over the calculation. To sit across from a man who uses his hands to tear hot bread and dips it without ceremony, and not have to police your own plate. When she loses the thread of the conversation because he licked smoky eggplant off his thumb—*yes*. That is exactly how it feels when you’re twenty-one and starving for something you can’t name, and a grown man shows you what an appetite looks like without disgust.

And the choreography of the kiss at the end was flawless. The little sorority defense popping up like a knee-jerk reflex (*"I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"*), him immediately honoring it with zero sulking, zero negotiation—which is the exact thing that makes her pull him back by the wrist. He doesn't take; he just builds a space big enough for her to realize she wants to step into it.

What’s chilling—and brilliant—is that the book isn't letting me forget that this tenderness is also a trap. He backed off with Randi in chapter 1, too. He let Randi decide, too. Every move he makes on Vee is an echo of the precision he used to break Randi open on the bench. But right now, watching Vee bloom under it, watching her actually *eat* and rest her mind... I’m hooked on the sheer, cruel luxury of the pacing.
