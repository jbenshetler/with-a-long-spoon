REACTION

God, this is where the friendship novel really pays off, and it makes what’s coming almost sickening to watch.

Cassie is just the absolute salt of the earth. The detail about her highlighter abandoned, the sock feet up on the lower bunk, and that beautiful, cynical, bone-deep protective instinct: *“How much did he give you to drink?”* followed immediately by her genuine relief and pleasure when Vee says *“I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.”* That line broke my heart a little bit. Because Vee *did* do it on purpose, and she *did* want to, and she is so incandescently proud of herself for breaking her own rules and claiming her own desire—and the reader knows that every single inch of that turf was surveyed, cleared, and manicured for her by Pace and Randi before she even took the first step. The psychological cruelty of leaving a person all the room in the world to "choose" so that the trap feels entirely like their own freedom is so insidious.

And then the comedy of her dossier: *“He lives out west of town, off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel.”* I love the contrast between Vee's romantic, floating fog—where a gravel road and "real shoes" feels like an adventure—and Cassie’s flat, grounded realism: *“Rich people hike. Poor people walk.”*

It’s a quiet chapter—just two girls in a dorm room with the window cracked two inches to fight the radiator heat—but the texture of it is so completely right. The contrast between Vee’s dizzy warmth and Cassie’s steady, dry loyalty makes the dorm feel like the safest place on earth, right before Vee walks out into the woods with a man whose house already contains a custom-built spanking bench.
