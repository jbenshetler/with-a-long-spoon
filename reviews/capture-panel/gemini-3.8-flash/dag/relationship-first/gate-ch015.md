REACTION

God, I love a dining hall scene where women just dismantle a text message like an unexploded bomb.

There is such a specific, joyful texture to having dinner with the girls who don't live in the high-stakes sorority panopticon—Meg with her cheese and her level head, Kayla running at ninety miles an hour, stealing fries and diagnosing men. It felt so grounded and so *normal*, which made the shadow hanging over it feel three times as dark.

Because we just came from Chapter 14. We just saw Randi drive up that gravel road, unbutton her wool skirt, ride Pace until she was screaming into his mouth, clench around him with pure possessive terror when he mentioned Vee's soft belly, and then flee into the dark without letting him feed her. Pace was left shivering in his kitchen, utterly bewildered by his own trap. And here is Vee, sitting in the golden west-facing light of the athletic dining hall, getting a text that says *would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock.*

Kayla is right. Two in the afternoon is not dinner. Two in the afternoon is an engineered threshold. And the reader knows *exactly* what lives inside that house off six-fourteen. We know about the walnut-and-leather spanking bench. We know about the Blondie playing low on the stereo. We know what happened to Randi in that bedroom. Vee thinks she's trying to figure out what outfit says "casual but pretty"; she has no idea she's being invited onto an altar.

What I also noticed—and loved—was Vee's edit of the truck scene. To Cassie, she confessed the raw truth: *I called him back in, I took off my shirt on purpose, I wanted him to see me.* But to Meg and Kayla, she cleans it up into a charming, slightly self-deprecating romantic comedy beat: *I'm not going to kiss a man who's about to go hypothermic on me, then the cop blared his siren.* That is *so* true to how women calibrate intimacy across different friendships. You tell your roommate the terrifying, shameless heat; you tell the dining hall girls the funny, cinematic version.

Meg's line caught in my throat, though: *"Everybody's different at the start. Sometimes they even are."* God. If she only knew how "different" this man actually is.

I am tense down to my marrow. Saturday at two. She’s walking straight into the cut.
