REACTION:

Oh, thank God. Thank God for Cassie, and thank God for this pool.

After the suffocating, hyper-managed tension of the last few chapters—Pace calculating every degree of a woman's surrender, Randi wound tighter than a cheap watch, the sickeningly staged "accident" on the lawn—this felt like walking out of an airless room into an actual breeze. It’s the first time in the entire book that two people are together without an angle.

The detail of the synchronized swimming suit—that she wore it at sixteen before she had "a body to be a problem," and the way she goes under the surface just to switch the world off—hit me right in the sternum. *Nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything.* That quiet ache of having a body that has become a public event, a spectacle that men bark at from bleachers and that she has to manage with a tight towel and an arranged face. It breaks my heart a little, especially knowing what’s being engineered for her on the other side of town. The trap is so soft, and it’s closing so cleanly around a girl who literally just wants to float where nobody is demanding something from her.

And Cassie. "Standing. Like a person." "They will never know the love of a woman." Cassie’s dry, unbothered ballast is the only clean thing in Vee’s life right now, and the book knows it. The splash fight felt completely real—not cute, not performed, just two college girls being idiots in a rec pool until the sadness gets knocked loose. I needed this chapter desperately. If it had been another scene of Randi and Pace passing the narrative scalpel back and forth, I might have choked on it. This reminded me why I care about Vee in the first place.
