REACTION

*Jesus.*

The sheer, staggering, monstrous benevolence of this man. I am sitting here with my hand over my mouth.

Look at what he just did. Look at the architecture of it. She comes to his house having explicitly put on the nice satin underwear because she expects—she *wants*—to be seduced, to have that bedroom door open, to finish what started in the truck. She kisses him on the porch with that desperate, overdue hunger, and *he* stops it. He plays the perfect, courtly, unhurried host. He leaves the bedroom door shut, and she feels that tiny sting of rejection, of dropping.

And then he takes her into the light—that big, glassed-in sunroom with the mountain views—and reveals the bolt of burgundy silk. Not the brick-red she asked for, but the color that flatters *her*. The revelation that he hand-tailored the flannel shirt she’s been sleeping in. And then the trap snaps shut, so gently, so sweetly, with all the velvet in the world: *“To do it right I’d measure you first — down to what you’d wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it’ll fall.”*

He didn’t push her into the dark bedroom. He orchestrated a scenario where she is going to strip down to her underwear for him in broad daylight, surrounded by glass, completely vulnerable, and she is going to do it *voluntarily*, weeping with gratitude because he listened to her talk about cherries and an honors dinner. *Every yes was freely given.* The jacket blurb is just ringing in my ears like a bell.

And the title of the chapter: *Two Towels*. In the bathroom, on the tour she barely registered: two towels on the rod. Randi was just there. Randi rode him into the mattress, wept out *“I want us,”* and fled into the night leaving him starving at the fridge, and now Vee is walking past her ghost in the hallway, looking at the square handwriting on his math proofs, completely oblivious.

The detail of his patent—that he created an algorithm to find faint signals buried in overwhelming noise—is almost too pointed, but it works because of who he is. He filters out the static of Vee’s mother, of her shame, of her clumsiness, and zeroes in on the exact frequency of what she hungers for: to be seen, to be chosen, to be fed, to have someone else make the decisions.

The heat in this chapter was entirely under the floorboards, but God, the tension is unbearable. He is about to put a measuring tape around her bare ribs in a room full of glass.
