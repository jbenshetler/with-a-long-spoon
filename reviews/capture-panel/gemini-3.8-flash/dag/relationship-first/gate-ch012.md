REACTION

God, the truck. My chest is still tight.

I went into this chapter bracing for him to steer her straight toward his house—the gravel road off 614, the walnut bench waiting in the quiet room—and the fact that he *didn't* is ten times more devastating. He didn't have to trick her into anything. He just took her up a mountain, fixed her heel with moleskin without an ounce of grossness, fed her cherries, and gently pocketed the pit she spat out so she learned the rule without ever feeling scolded. He gave her space to put down that russet silk dress she can't afford, and he just looked at her and said, *"That sounds like a beautiful dress."* He leaves everything so clean that she is practically climbing out of her own skin to make a mess of it.

And then the rain. When he stepped out of that truck to give her privacy to change, standing in the freezing downpour with his back turned so he wouldn't compromise her—it’s such an impossibly chivalrous, courtly gesture, and it was the exact move guaranteed to shatter every defense she had left. He didn't push. He withdrew so completely that *she* had to knock on the glass. She had to crook her finger. She had to kneel on that bench seat and peel her own soaked shirt open button by button, dragging her mother's shrieking voice right along with her, because the shame has become the exact engine of her arousal.

The heat in that cab was suffocating. Not because they did anything—they didn't even kiss!—but because she made him watch, and he gave her that terrifying, unmoving stillness where a man isn't pouncing, he's just taking you in like a sacrament. And then finding out he was shivering so hard his hands shook? Bringing his cold knuckles under the flannel to press against her stomach? It’s completely ruthless writing. He didn't take a single liberty, and she went back into her dorm smelling of his gym bag and his soap, carrying his shirt like a flag.

The campus cop barking the siren at the red curb was a brilliant, jarring slice of reality to break the spell. Now she’s walking back into that room with Cassie, dripping wet, wearing a man’s flannel, her heel taped up, with that russet dress sitting like a loaded gun in the conversation. When Pace inevitably buys her that dress—and we all know he's going to buy her that dress—it's going to hit like an avalanche.
