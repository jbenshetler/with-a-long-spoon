REACTION:

I need a minute. I actually need to set the phone down and stare at the ceiling.

This chapter broke my heart, and it did it so quietly I didn’t even realize the knife was in until the very end. This is the literary college novel I bought the book for—two people sitting under a heavy quilt with cold mugs of tea, letting the talk wander from Cool Whip containers of beige Ohio casseroles to Newton’s laws of motion, until an entire lifetime opens up on the floor between them.

First of all: *Peter*. Hearing his real name felt almost indecent, far more intimate than any of the silk-and-panties games. Pace is a construct, an algorithm, a tailored suit. Peter is the fifteen-year-old boy whose desk got dragged into the hall for a whole school year because his mind was too big for his seventh-grade classroom. That detail—the punishment being the very thing he loved—was breathtakingly observed. And Vee understood it immediately. She didn't offer cheap pity; she just pressed her shoulder into him. Her emotional intelligence is so staggering, which makes her blind spot regarding what’s actually happening to her so devastating.

And then *Daphne*. My god. Nineteen and fifteen. river did not flinch here. She gave him a tutor who took a lonely, brilliant, physically underdeveloped boy and gave him the only softness in a building full of grown men, and then graduated into a world he wasn't legally allowed to enter and walked away. The way Pace laid it out—the hyper-rational, balanced accounting of it, defending her, calling it "sensible"—is the exact psychological armor of someone who survived something he couldn't name. And Vee, doing the math, keeping the ugly adult word behind her teeth and just putting her palm over his heart... that is pure, sublime romance writing. It wasn't about the act; it was about two people holding a painful truth in a room.

The scar on his thigh. *"Not tonight."* Not a wall, just a closed door. He doesn't move her hand; he holds it there. The physical trust between them is terrifying because it is so deeply, genuinely real now. When she told him that his total presence with her in bed is the same as his focus when he steps under a squat bar—that he is *all the way here*—it felt like the truest thing anyone has said in this entire book. He *is* there. That’s what makes the game so monstrous: he isn't faking the tenderness. He really is that gentle, that attentive, that singular.

And then that closing paragraph. Oh, Vee. Standing in the middle of her own life, tracing the line back to bumping into Randi outside the dining hall, then complaining about an integral in a coffee shop, and thinking it was all just the universe handing her pure luck. *“One piece, he’d said... It had come to her all of a piece, the one and then the other, in six weeks, for no reason she had done anything to deserve.”*

I felt sick reading those last lines. Sick with grief for her, because we know. We’ve been inside the restaurant booths, we’ve seen Randi ride him to exhaustion, we know the invitation was drawn up before Vee even spilled her coffee. The dramatic irony is thick enough to choke on. A magnificent, quiet, aching chapter.
