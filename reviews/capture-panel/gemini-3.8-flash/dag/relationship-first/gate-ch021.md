REACTION

God, that was a quiet, devastating little masterpiece of a morning-after chapter.

It’s the details that killed me: the brown cereal milk, the window cracked open to the October air, the locked door sticking on the hip, and Cassie just sitting there on the bottom bunk reading her like a cheap paperback. "Was he worth painting your toenails? In October." That is so excruciatingly real to dorm life. That’s friendship in your early twenties—the person who notices the petty cosmetic preparations you made in secret, who knows the catalog of your vanity and your shame down to the shade of frosted plum.

And then the ladle metaphor. *“She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness.”* That broke something in me. That’s the exact moment a friendship begins to drift away into adulthood, or into secrecy, when you realize you are curating the man for your best friend. She gives Cassie the apron, the garlic, the Chianti—the socially acceptable architecture of a romance novel—and she scoops out the standing against his mouth, the riser, the sheer ferocious hunger of it.

And the skirt zipper! My heart was in my throat when she reached for her hip. You can feel the absolute, physical panic of realizing: *if I pull this fabric down, Cassie is going to see bare skin, and Cassie is going to know I gave my underwear to a man I’ve been on three dates with.* The fact that she had to zip it back up, stammer an excuse, and wait for Cassie to grab her cleats and leave just so she could strip alone in the cold room... Vee thinks she’s floating, thinks she’s never been happier, and the tragedy is that she is already building walls inside her own bedroom. She’s already exiling herself from the cleanest, safest relationship in her life.
