REACTION:

God, Cassie is such an anchor in this book. "You're a public utility." That whole exchange in the dorm room was so real it hurt a little. When Cassie asked, "Do you love him," and Vee choked on it—said *maybe*, said *it's all so fast*, even though she was just lying in his bed a few nights ago naming it clearly in her own heart—it felt so achingly true to being twenty-something and falling in deep water. You can feel a thing with every cell in your body when you're under his roof, but the second you're back in a linoleum dorm room smelling of cracked-window cold and your roommate’s dry pragmatism, the reality of it terrifies you. To say it out loud to Cassie would make it real, and making it real means admitting how precarious it is. Admitting she doesn't actually have a name for what they are.

And then the swimming. Oh, I loved this sequence. Helen Rivers writes the body so brilliantly, not just when it's being touched, but when it's just *working*. The rhythm of the laps, the smell of chlorine, the old green-and-white suit, the black line on the bottom of the pool—it gave Vee back to herself. For chapters now, Vee’s body has been this hyper-sensitized instrument played by Pace and analyzed by Randi. To see her just put her head down, breathe every third, and let the sheer physical labor strip the words off her panic until she was just an engine... that was gorgeous.

That thought she had around the seventh hundred gave me a genuine chill, though: *"she could not tell, for about four strokes, whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting."* That is the heart of the whole trap. She is so hungry, and he feeds her so precisely, but the jacket blurb is always whispering over my shoulder. He *is* hiding something. Randi *is* in on it. Vee’s intuition is trying to flag it, even if she can only frame it as her own insecurity.

It was a quiet chapter—no Pace, no heat, just Vee alone with her thoughts and her sore muscles—but it grounded her completely. It makes me love her more, and it makes what's coming feel that much more perilous.
