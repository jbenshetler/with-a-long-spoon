REACTION

Oh, my stomach is in absolute knots.

First of all, I love seeing Vee with Meg and Kayla in the athletic dining hall. It’s such an important grounding beat for her character. When she was with Randi at the smoothie place, she felt small, wrong-sized, and constantly hyper-aware of being looked at. Here, with two girls who just eat their food, tell dumb roommate stories, and steal fries off each other's trays, Vee gets to laugh with her *whole body*. It reminds me of the girl she actually is before she gets swallowed by the glamour and the curated perfection of Randi’s orbit.

And the girl-talk! It is so painfully, perfectly college. Every woman has sat at a table exactly like that, dissecting a twelve-day dry spell, passing a phone around like a hot potato, screaming over a six-word text: *"Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock."* Kayla is so right—that is practically a ransom note. And Vee sitting there, climbing the walls, saying: *"He asks me first... and I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner."* God, I felt that in my bones. That’s the entire magic trick of real consent and slow-burn dominance. When a man refuses to push, when he makes you wait and gives you all the room to want it, the anticipation becomes completely agonizing.

...Which brings me to the absolute sickening dread sitting right behind my ribs.

Because *we* know what that house is. We were just there in Chapter 14. We watched Randi stride through that exact door, peel her clothes off, and ride him while demanding to know how Vee smelled, how Vee's belly felt. That house isn't just Pace's bachelor pad—it has the custom bench in the bedroom. It has the table with the removable top. It’s the slaughterhouse, and Vee is sitting in the athletic cafeteria getting her hair planned by her sweet friends, entirely convinced she’s walking into a fairytale romance.

*"There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat. He wants you for the afternoon."*

I felt sick reading Kayla say that. She thinks it's just spicy college fun. Meg thinks it’s Danny 2.0. And Vee thinks she’s finally stepping into the driver's seat of her own desire. The contrast between this bright, gold-lit, fry-stealing cafeteria conversation and the cold reality of the snare waiting for her off 614 is almost unbearable. I am completely hooked, terrified, and desperate for Saturday to come.
