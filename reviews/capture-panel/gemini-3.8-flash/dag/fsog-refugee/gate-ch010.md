REACTION

Oh, Vee. My stomach hurts for her in that sickening, gorgeous way where you just want to reach through the page and shield her, but you also understand *completely* why she is floating.

"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That line went straight through my sternum. That is *the* line. That is what every woman who has ever felt handled, or coerced, or passive in her own romantic life wants to feel—that fierce, proud ownership of her own desire. The tragedy, of course, is that she *is* right about her own desire—she *did* want it, she did choose it—while being completely, heartbreakingly blind to the scaffolding built around her to deliver her to that exact choice.

And Cassie is such a vital anchor here. Every romance heroine needs a Cassie: the friend who doesn't buy into the fairy tale haze, who asks "how much did he give you to drink?" not to be cruel, but because she actually loves Vee enough to check the foundations. The dry humor between them is so lived-in and real ("Rich people hike. Poor people walk"), which makes the dramatic irony cut even deeper. Pace's house "off six-fourteen." We know what's in that bedroom. We know about the table with the removable top, and the oiled leather, and Randi's tears in the bathroom, and the fact that hiking was literally on Pace's mental itinerary of things he does with girls he's breaking in.

It’s a short chapter, a transition breath after the intense intimacy of the dinner, but it’s doing immense psychological work. Seeing Vee so radiant, so giddy that she's spinning into beanbag chairs and throwing pillows, makes the trap feel impossibly high-stakes. She isn't being dragged into the dark; she's skipping into it because the light in front of her is the warmest thing she's ever felt. God, I need to see this hike.
