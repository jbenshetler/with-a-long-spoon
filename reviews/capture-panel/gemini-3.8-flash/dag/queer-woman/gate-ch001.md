REACTION

Wait. *Randi.*

I had to read that sentence twice when it hit. The jacket told me about Vee Thorne, the quiet college junior starving for something real, steered into Pace’s path by her glamorous friend Randi. So the entire opening of this chapter, I thought I was watching Pace break down Vee. I was watching this polished, hyper-controlled sorority girl with the dancer’s calves and the managed appetite, thinking, *okay, here is our heroine walking into the trap.* And then he bends down to the leather and whispers, *"There you are, Randi."*

God, that was a fantastic misdirection. Because it’s not Vee at all. It’s the architect. It’s the girl who is supposed to be the cool, detached puppeteer of the jacket copy. And she isn't detached at all; she’s in so deep her ribs are humming with terror.

The scene itself took its time, and I appreciated the physical specifics—the way Pace operates not with frantic male greed, but with that terrifying, patient carpentry of a man who builds custom furniture and custom scenes. The psychological anatomy of her control was so sharp: the apple sliced thin because her mother raised her on egg whites and knives; the way she uses being *looked at* as a shield against ever being *seen*. When she goes into the bathroom and looks in the mirror and realizes she can’t quite snap the mask back on—that the face that begged is now underneath the photographic smile—that hurt. That was real. It wasn't just kink mechanics; it was an autopsy of vanity and armor.

What hooks me completely, though, is the sheer architecture of the mess they’re building. Randi is terrified because Pace saw through her, and she decides in that bathroom: *He gets the face. The one he found is going back behind it, and she is not coming out again.* If I know anything about people who run from being known, they deflect. They bring in a third. They give him someone else to look at. The blurb says Randi and Pace started this as a game, an "invitation" to Vee. Now I see why. Randi is going to feed Vee to him to save herself, or to watch him do to another woman what she can't survive him doing to her—or worse, because she wants to watch. And the jacket says Vee grows to want Randi, too.

If this book is setting up a dynamic where the desire between the women is just collateral damage or an engineered trick, I’ll drop it like a hot iron. But right now? The interiority is entirely too sharp for cheap tricks. Randi isn't a villain; she's a girl who just got split open on a walnut bench and is frantically trying to rebuild the wall before morning. I want to see what happens when Vee walks into this.
