REACTION

God, Cassie is the absolute anchor of this book.

"Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman." I felt the entire tension of the previous six chapters just drop out of my shoulders for three minutes. That is what real, unvarnished, female friendship looks like when it isn't being run through a pastel sorority blender or weaponized for bedroom triangulation. Cassie doesn't care about looking pretty, she doesn't care about Chi Latte, she wades into the pool up to her ribs "like a person," and she knows *exactly* when Vee has gone under the surface and come back up with "her face on wrong." Splashing her in the chest to knock her out of her own head? Perfection. It made my chest ache because you can already see how badly Vee is going to need an anchor when this shiny, engineered glass house shatters.

And that quiet, breathtaking moment at the bottom of the pool—Vee sculling in the green, weightless, grieving for the time before her body became "a problem." It adds such a heartbreaking layer of depth to her. She isn't just a lush girl with curves; she carries the exhausting, relentless burden of being constantly looked at, constantly catcalled by frat boys on bleachers ("You won't need floaties with *those*"), constantly policed by her mother. Under the water, nobody can look, and nobody wants anything from her.

Which brings us right to Pace's trap. Because what did Pace do? He didn't gawk at her chest. He sat down, asked for her pencil with his eyes, respected her brain, and then—in a move so psychologically brilliant it made my teeth click—asked her to dinner and immediately said: *"Don't answer tonight."*

*Who does that?* Vee asks herself in the locker room. I'll tell you who does that: a man who knows that taking away the immediate pressure to perform creates an obsession. He took her "yes," put it on a high shelf where she couldn't reach it, and walked away with his hands in his pockets. It’s the exact same deliberate, maddening denial he used on Randi with the honeycrisp apple and the spanking bench. He refuses to take the easy, reactive response; he forces them to sit in the *waiting* until the wanting is all they have left.

Vee sitting on that wet locker room bench, still warm from the shower, winding her hair around her finger and wondering what those thick-knuckled hands do besides math... she is already cooked. She's standing at the crosswalk letting the walk sign change while her thumb hovers over the screen. The hook is so deep in her mouth and she doesn't even feel the line tightening.
