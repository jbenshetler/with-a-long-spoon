REACTION

Oh god. The contrast between this sunny, hilarious dorm-girl dinner and the cold, shivering wreck of a bedroom we were in four days ago is sickeningly good.

Seeing Vee surrounded by normal college noise—sneaking into the athletic dining hall because the food is better, Meg steadily working through a mound of melted cheese, Kayla talking at ninety miles an hour about boys waving across the room—grounds her so deeply in reality. This is what twenty-one is supposed to feel like! Fries on a plastic tray, laughing until the next table turns around, dissecting text messages like ancient runes with your friends. Meg and Kayla are so real, and I love how they treat Vee’s romance like a soap opera they’re all tuning in for: *“Meg, who followed the Pace situation in installments like her shows.”* That is so true to female friendship.

And hearing Vee retell the truck story... god, my heart twisted. She edited it! Of course she did! She turned the terrifying, scorched-earth erotic intimacy of stripping naked on the bench seat while holding his eyes into a safe, cinematic rom-com anecdote: *“I’m soaked, he gives me his shirt, he stands in the rain, I was about to kiss him but he was hypothermic, then the campus cop chased us.”* She kept the true thing—her own bare skin, his shivering hand on her belly—in the exact same private vault where Pace keeps it. She doesn't even know she's doing the same thing he is: protecting the sacredness of what they did in that cab from outside eyes.

And the way she tries to explain his appeal to Kayla: *“He asks me first. The small things... It sounds like nothing. I can hear it sounding like nothing. And I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner.”* That killed me. She knows how crazy it sounds, because in a world of frantic, grabbing college boys, Pace’s spacious, courtly patience feels like magic. She thinks it’s pure, uncomplicated gentleness.

Then the text arrives.
*“Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock.”*

My stomach dropped straight through my shoes.
*Saturday. Two o'clock.*
Kayla’s dead right: *“There’s no meal at two, Vee. He’s not asking you over to eat.”*

He’s bringing her to the house. The house with the walnut bench that has a false top on it. The house where Randi rode him into the mattress on Sunday night before fleeing into the gravel driveway without staying the night. The house with the deadbolt.

Does Randi know? Is Randi going to be there, or is this Pace going rogue after Randi left him bare-chested and starving on Sunday? Or worse... is this the next phase of the plan they concocted together?

The innocence of Kayla and Meg planning her outfit—*“We’re doing hair Saturday... What do you wear that says his house without saying trying too hard?”*—while we, the readers, know that house is an interrogation chamber for female vulnerability is suffocating. Saturday is coming, and Vee is walking right through the front door.
