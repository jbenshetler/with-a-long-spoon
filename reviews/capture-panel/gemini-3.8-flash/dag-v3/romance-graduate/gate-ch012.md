REACTION

*Two in the afternoon.*

Kayla is completely right: "There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat." He wants her for the afternoon. And after chapter eleven—after Randi tore through that house like a wildfire, mounted him with that desperate, weeping *"I want us,"* and fled into the gravel before her skin was even warm—Pace is inviting Vee over to his house.

The *house*. The house where the bench is. The bench that currently has a tabletop over it, hiding in plain sight. My stomach is in my throat.

I loved being dropped back into the dorm dining hall with Meg and Kayla. This was the exact breath of air the book needed after the suffocating, dark heat of chapter eleven. Meg eating her cheesy pasta like she has all night, Kayla treating college romance like a full-contact sport, the golden light coming through the athletic dining hall windows—it feels so young, so real, so grounded. It reminds you that Vee is twenty-one years old. She isn't a chess piece on a board; she’s a girl sitting with her friends, stealing fries, flustered because an older PhD student is asking her what she wants to order for dinner and it's making her climb the walls.

And look at the edits Vee made when she told the truck story! That was brilliant. She didn't tell them she rapped on the glass, crooked her finger, stripped her wet shirt off, and warmed his hands against her bare belly while her thighs clenched over her mother's shame. She kept that locked in her chest. She gave them the PG-13 rom-com version: *he gave me his shirt, stood in the rain, then the cops showed up.* She’s ring-fencing the sacred, filthy, beautiful core of what they have, just like Pace kept the truck cab to himself when Randi was demanding details on the bed.

Meg’s line stopped me cold: *"Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think. He's different... Sometimes they even are."* God. The dramatic irony is sharp enough to cut glass. Pace *is* different—he isn't Danny, he isn't some college boy who grabs and takes. He is courtly, patient, reverent, and deeply focused on her. But the foundation under that reverence is a lie. He didn't stumble on her; Randi pointed a manicured finger at her outside a dining hall.

And now Vee is going out to that house off 614. At two in the afternoon. With Kayla threatening to do her hair and Meg asking what she owns that says "his house without saying trying too hard."

What is Pace planning? Is he taking her to bed? Does he think this is the next step in their courtship, or is he bringing her into his space to replace the ghost of Randi's slammed deadbolt? And sweet Jesus, what happens if Randi drives up that gravel cut? Saturday afternoon. I am gripping the arm of my chair.
