REACTION

*"May I choose?"*

God in heaven. The absolute, dizzying eroticism of handing over the wheel.

This chapter made the hollow behind my own ribs ache. Rivers is writing hunger with an accuracy that feels like a trespass. For Vee, hunger isn’t just an empty belly; it’s an entire lifetime of self-policing, of hearing her mother’s voice at every meal and every outfit change, of bracing for the hidden invoice attached to every interaction with a man. And Pace doesn't just feed her—he systematically removes the labor of being a person. He asks for permission, he weighs the decision with real gravity, he orders food she doesn't have the vocabulary for, and then he just lets her *eat*. Without judgment. Without commentary. He normalizes her appetite. And for a girl who has been quietly starving herself to fit into a world that only rewards women for taking up less space, that kind of surrender is filthier than anything they could have done with their clothes off.

And the craft of Pace's seduction is just devastating. The contrast between this and chapter one is giving me chills: with Randi, he feeds her thin, counted slices of apple because she’s terrified of food, and he strips her down to a weeping plea on a wooden bench to make her real. With Vee, he orders a feast of hot blistered bread and smoky eggplant, and he dismantles her defenses by giving her complete, unhurried permission to choose her own pace.

Look at that walk under the trees. When she throws up her little defensive rule—*"I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"*—he doesn't push. He doesn't pout. He doesn't deploy charm to negotiate the boundary down. He just *accepts* it, honors it, and keeps walking. And by genuinely respecting her "no," he makes it completely impossible for her not to say "yes." He leaves that last inch hanging in the dark air until she crosses it herself. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." The blurb is practically screaming in my ear now.

And the tragedy of it is that Pace is being so *tender*. He isn't acting like a villain running a con; he's genuinely charmed by her, he's wearing boots that have lived, he shaved for her, he ducked his head when Christine teased him. He isn't performing a role—he's falling into her. Which means when the floor drops out, when Vee realizes that this entire courtship was launched from Randi's bedroom as an erotic scavenger hunt... it is going to destroy her.
