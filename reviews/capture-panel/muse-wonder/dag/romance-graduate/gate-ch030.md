REACTION — God. Okay. That one got me in a completely different way than I expected.

I thought the induction walk was the payoff — the whole room, the slit, her deciding to take the long step instead of keeping herself small. And it was, it *was*. But this? Bringing the dress to his house without ever quite admitting that's what she was doing, no overnight bag because that would mean Planning The Sex, but the burgundy silk laid across the back seat anyway? That's so painfully, exactly her. She can lie to herself about a bag. She cannot lie to herself about wanting him to finally see the finished thing on her, properly, the way it was meant to be seen.

And then she makes him sit down. I loved that maybe more than the undressing. She puts out one flat hand and he *sits back down*, wine untouched, and you can feel what it costs him. Pace has always been the one who's so careful about not taking — the waiting, the face tipped up, the last inch left for her — and usually that's been him giving her the power. This time she's actually *using* it. Directed it. "Get your wine and go to the den. Wait for me in the recliner." Ma'am. Who is she now.

The walk back down the den toward him, recreating the induction step for the one person who wasn't there... I had to put the thing the hell down for a second. Because it's not just sexy, it's her saying: everyone else got the performance, you get the intention. You made this to me, and you missed watching it do what it did, so I'm doing it again, slower, in your lamplight, where I can watch *you* watch it.

And costume two. Jesus.

That's the dread that lives under every good scene in this book now. Even when it's just the two of them and it's this tender and this hot, the third person's hands are literally holding her feet up off the floor.
