Okay, this one hurt in a completely different way than I was braced for, and I think it's because Cassie *knows*.

Not knows-knows, maybe not the coat and literally nothing underneath and Randi's heels — but that silence after "Pace shrank your sweater." The highlighter turning end over end. The beat that's exactly the length of her deciding not to correct the record. And then she laughs, that rare real laugh, and lets Vee keep the whole ridiculous story. Doll-sized sweater. Dye in the good pants. Toast burned because of a t-shirt at thigh-height.

I was laughing with them and also kind of wanting to cry at my Kindle at 1 a.m., because look what Vee has gotten *good* at. In September she couldn't crop a photo without feeling it in her stomach. Now she can sit in her robe, still basically glowing from a weekend of being naked in that man's house on purpose, invent a laundry accident in real time, make it funny, make it *charming* — and watch her best-since-before-Randi friend accept it because Cassie is too decent to make her say the real thing in a dorm room. That's the edit happening live. Randi gets "nearly everything" on Tuesday, pre-decided. Cassie gets mushrooms on toast and a cover story with comic timing.

And god, the mushrooms-on-toast part almost made it worse, because the *true* parts she does give Cassie are so good. The blood oranges cut down to the jewels, three grains of salt on the persimmons, doing his flat voice — "They do it that way in Japan." That's real intimacy, that's her lit up and wanting to share him. She *wants* Cassie to get it. She just can't let Cassie get *her* in it. The naked part, the deliberate part, the part where she wasn't a girl whose clothes tragically shrank but a woman who chose to bring nothing and stay bare and tease him until he burned breakfast. That Vee travels only to Randi now. And to Pace. The two people who — I cannot stop circling this — built the room she does it in.

Then that tiny moment with Cassie's mother and the card stopped me cold. "My mother says thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again." And Vee's hand going to her collar instead of the towel, that small "Oh." There's a whole life there where Vee is known and welcomed plainly, no cropping, no performance, somebody's mother just... expects her back at spring break. And Cassie hears the size of Vee's answer and recaps the highlighter and lets it go *the exact moment before it would need anything.* Everyone in this book who loves Vee cleanly keeps making room for her to not tell them things. It's generous and it's breaking my heart a little.

Now it's logistics. *He's hers again, Friday will come.* I believe her happiness, I do, the bread smell and the wall that's only a wall because she isn't exiled anymore. But Pace still hasn't said it. He said sorry, he said he missed her, he got on one knee with pancake in — and she decided tonight there was no room for wanting anything he hadn't given. Tonight. I'm happy for her and I'm quietly terrified of how much of this peace is built on not re-asking the question that blew up December.

This is the second quiet one after the party-planning chapter and normally I'd start doing the thumb-hover thing — but the quiet here isn't empty, it's Cassie-shaped, and I need to see what happens when Pace tries to walk into a room with all these people in it, in public, where Vee can't edit anybody. And Tuesday. Randi hearing the coat story. I already don't trust what Randi will do with "nearly everything."
