REACTION

Okay. I did not expect the hiking chapter to be the one that got me.

Because on paper — and God, I have read this exact chapter in a hundred other books — it's boots and sandwiches and rain, and then he gives her his dry shirt. I should have been skimming for the truck. But I wasn't. I was *in* it, in that awful slipping boot, in how he kneels down on the trail and takes her sock off and doesn't make it into a Moment, he just fixes the heel. Moleskin. Different lacing. He apologizes because he didn't think to ask about the boots. That did more to me than most shirtless reveals I've read this year.

That's the thing this book keeps doing to me. Pace never performs care. He just... notices the practical place where she's hurting and puts his thumb there and holds it warm. Lettuce against the bread so it doesn't go soggy. Carrying the cherry pit back out on the wax paper so she learns the rule without being shamed for not knowing it. *"That sounds like a beautiful dress"* and then he does NOT pick it back up. He lets her put the russet dress down. I sat there feeling that in my ribs, how rare it is for a man in one of these books to hear a woman want something out loud and not immediately solve it or buy it or turn it into a grand gesture. He just witnessed the wanting. Let it stay hers.

And then the rain. And the truck.

I texted nobody because it's 1 a.m. in my head even if it isn't, but if I had: *she called him back in.* She knocked on the glass. Three little raps. She crooked a finger at him.

I loved that it was her. Not him being noble in the rain and her being accidentally sexy and him losing control anyway — the standard script, God I'm tired of that script. He gets out so she can change *privately*, full back turned, hands in pockets, getting soaked, no peeking-as-foreplay. And she sits there in the cold cab listening to her mother in her head — *floozie, girls in trucks, three dates* — and the voice doesn't kill the heat, it *is* the heat, and she decides anyway. Eyes on his the whole time she undoes those buttons. She looks down on purpose so his eyes go with hers. That's grown. That's a woman choosing to be seen instead of being caught being seen, and the difference almost knocked me over.

And he doesn't touch her. Not because he's a saint in that bloodless way some of these heroes are, where you can feel the author patting him on the head. Because he's *overwhelmed*. His face does that small, unreadable-to-anyone-else thing. He lets her call his eyes back up to her face. And then — his hand shaking, not with want, with *cold*, because he's been standing in mountain rain in a wet blue shirt so she wouldn't feel watched. She notices. She stops the seduction she started to warm his hand under the flannel against her belly. If you wanted to show me who these two are to each other, you could burn the rest of the book and keep that beat.

I am also not stupid, and I know what I know from the jacket and from Randi. I know Vee was pointed at on a quad like merchandise in a game that started in somebody else's bed. I know Pace went and got her number in that coffee shop with Randi sitting right there watching her own hand not drink her coffee. So every tender thing he does lands double for me. Is this him falling for real inside a plan? Is this the plan working exactly as designed — make her feel chosen, listened to, never rushed — because that *is* the trap? *"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem"* has been sitting in the back of my throat for ten chapters, and in the truck it got louder, because her yes in that cab was so freely, bravely given. She took her shirt off for a man who has been lying to her by omission since before he knew her name. That should sour it. It doesn't sour it, it *hurts*, which is worse and better. I'm furious at him and at Randi on her behalf while also completely understanding why Vee keeps the flannel.

The cop at the end — siren barking right over whatever she was about to say — normally that kind of interruption would make me roll my eyes. Here I actually felt robbed in the right way. She was about to put a small word where a big thing went, and the world took it, and now she's standing in the dorm doorway in his shirt smelling like soap and gym bag and her own wet hair, telling Cassie *I'm keeping the shirt* like it's a decision she gets to make and keep. Cassie just saying her name, soft. Not *Oh, Vee*. Just *Vee*, like setting something down where it won't break. I love Cassie. She's the only person in this whole triangle-square-whatever who says exactly the plain true thing and doesn't dress it up, and I'm getting more and more afraid for what happens to her place in Vee's life as Randi takes up more room.

Ten chapters in and nobody has actually — I mean, Vee and Pace haven't. And I am not bored. That is the miracle I'm sitting with. The kiss under the tree, the menu she couldn't read, the sandwich with horseradish that made her eyes run on top of a mountain — this author is feeding me the whole time with wanting instead of just teasing me with delay. I'm full and I'm still hungry. That's exactly what I said I'd been starving for.
