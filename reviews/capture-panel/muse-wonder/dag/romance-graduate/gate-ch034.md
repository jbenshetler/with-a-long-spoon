REACTION

That promise is going to live in my head rent-free and not in a good way.

*Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone.*
*Never. I'd never share your photograph.*

And I sat there with my stomach dropping because — he already *is* sharing her, isn't he? Not the photo, not yet, maybe not literally that file, but her. Every story, every confession, Randi in his bed hearing about the sound behind her knee, about her legs, about how wet, how easy. So when he says it with that plain open face and she decides believing him is the brave part... God, Vee. That hurt in a very specific place. Because of course she believes him. Look at how he's just held her. Look at the whole evening before it.

That's what this book keeps doing to me and why I can't put it down even when I want to throw it across the room. The photo scene should have been standard spicy-romance fare — pose me, pinup, send it — I have read that scene four hundred times — and instead it was her putting herself back together in his bathroom mirror first. Not the wrecked sated girl on the sheet, the composed one. The version *she* wanted him to keep. And then him moving her like furniture he reveres, knee a little ahead, hand on hip, kiss at every place his hands set her. Shoulder. Hip. Knee. Unburrried. It wasn't even mostly about the picture for me, it was that she got to watch herself become beautiful because his eyes were doing the looking and she trusted the translation. "Lovelier than anything she'd have caught of herself alone." Yeah. I know that feeling and I've never seen it written that honestly without turning coy.

And before that — his head on her breast. Not grabbing, not starting something, just... laying there. His ear against her skin, those barely-kisses wandering up, her thinking *they're not about sex for him* and then him getting hard against her hip and her being delighted to be wrong. That spoiled-theory moment made me laugh out loud in a way that felt embarrassingly fond. No man has ever wanted her body like this — a girl who *has* a body, not a body that happens to have a girl attached. That line. That is exactly the hunger underneath this whole book for her, and when it's fed like that I melt right along with her. Three times in one evening and she's at his kitchen table wrung out and smug and demanding to be fed and used again soon, and he's blowing on spoonfuls before they reach her mouth because of the tomatoes days ago. He remembers burns. He learns her. It makes the betrayal architecture physically sickening because none of her evidence is fake. He *is* this tender. He is also lying by structure every day.

The cooking got me again, quietly. Lemon zest, him listening to the pan, feeding her tastes and reading her face instead of asking, moving her wine an inch closer after the parents question closed in his face. *I don't go back.* Level, exact, each word set down apart. And she, beautifully, takes her hand off it. Doesn't pry. Lets the quiet be kind. I love her for that and I am also screaming a little, because "he never said / he never says" is becoming the refrain of her whole happiness. The drawer he gave her by cramming his own ordered life into half the space. The counter he smiled at. The word love she said to a sleeping man as *Peter.* Everything true in action, nothing ever named out loud, and she keeps choosing to live inside the actions — which is mature and romantic and also exactly how these two have room to keep deceiving her. If he never has to say anything, he never has to lie in words. Except he just did. *I'd never share your photograph.* My skin crawled.

Sheri for Thanksgiving landed soft and then not. A beat of jealousy, honestly owned, honestly put out again when he says *She's alone* like someone glad to be able to feed a person. That is the best of him. Also: Sheri who knows about Randi, who laughed about two at once, will be at that table. Everyone at every table knows more about Vee's life than Vee does.

I texted nobody but I thought it: please don't let Randi see that photo first through Pace's phone in bed. Please let that one thing stay only his. I do not believe this book will give me that mercy, and the fact that I am begging fictional people for it tells me everything about where I am.
