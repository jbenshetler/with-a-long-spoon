REACTION

Oh, Randi. Oh, this *girl*.

This is the chapter I've been waiting for without knowing I was waiting for it — not the reveal, not the explosion, just Randi alone with the perfect life laid out end to end, actually trying it on, and feeling *nothing*. I was braced for John to be a creep or a dud in bed or secretly awful so she could leave clean. He isn't. That's the cruelty of it. He's gorgeous and rich and funny and good at everything — better skier because he doesn't need to prove it, better dancer because his hand actually *tells* her where to go, champagne, white-tie ball, a sleigh out of nowhere, good sex, pleasure delivered correctly — and she lies there afterward warm only where their bodies touch, like heat can be borrowed but not made. *A good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon.* I felt that in my stomach. Real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared. I've read a hundred "the other guy isn't him" chapters and they usually cheat. This one didn't.

And her parents at that breakfast table. Inventing boyfriends because the invented ones are safer, eating her revulsion behind the sorority smile, her mother all edges and a tennis bracelet in the light while Randi keeps her own bracelet *out* of the light. That tiny gesture told me more about her whole childhood than any flashback could have. This is the table she was built for. John would sit at the good end of it. Her mother would clock him the second Randi walked in on his arm. The life is right there, whole and approved, waiting on her yes — and she laid down in the middle of it and her eyes didn't even smudge. *Nothing had happened to her eyes.* The eyeliner held because nothing reached her. I had to put the phone down for a second at that.

What undid me, though, is how clearly she can suddenly name the other two. Pace on his worst night reached further than John at his best — because Pace goes straight to the person she keeps hidden and doesn't look away, and he will never, ever belong in the life she built to stay hidden. That's her whole trap in two sentences. And Vee — *Vee was her gorgeous.* She loves her, and she's told her so, and for the first time in her life there's no currency in it, no private qualifier running underneath. She has never said that to anyone clean before. That's a real turn, not a tease.

And then she decides, in the very same breath, not to tell her. That's where I wanted to shake her and hug her at the exact same time, which is becoming my permanent state with this book. I *believe* her reasoning — she's not wrong that saying it plainly over eggs would change the talk forever, that Vee would be kind and gentle and find something soft to say and they'd never be *this* again. She's trading honesty for keeping the most important hour of her week. I get it. I'm terrified of losing people too. But listen to what she lands on: *let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were. As long as the word kept its cover.* Randi. That's not restraint, that's the same stage-management she's always done, just with a sadder heart behind it. She won't risk asking, so she'll keep steering and call it letting Vee choose. She knows Vee heard “gorgeous” one way while she meant it two — and she's choosing the two-way meaning, on purpose, because it's the only way she gets to keep both.

Then that text. Vee in Ohio, in a warm kitchen with parents who love her out loud, sending *i'm here if you want to talk. or not* — pure Vee, plain and true — and Randi sending back the fairytale, every word true and none of it the thing, plus the one line she actually meant tucked inside the emojis like contraband. *Miss your face most though, gorgeous.* And turning the phone facedown and getting back into bed with the warm, asleep nothing because borrowed warmth is what she knows how to survive on.

I'm still furious at her for the Saturdays, for carrying Vee's confessions to Pace's bed. This chapter didn't erase any of that. It did something worse — it made me understand exactly what she's starving for and exactly why she'll keep stealing it sideways instead of asking for it out loud. She has never had to be brave; she arranged her whole life so she never would. And now the one person she'd need to be brave for is sitting across a brunch table believing her.

Meanwhile Vee walked out of Pace's house into the cold two chapters of my life ago and I have not forgotten it for one page of this. Randi just confirmed, from inside the fairytale, that nobody else reaches either of them the way those three reach each other. That doesn't make it safe. It makes the eventual collision so much bigger.
