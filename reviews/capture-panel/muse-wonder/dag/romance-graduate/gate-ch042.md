REACTION —

Oh, I felt that in my teeth.

Two nights in that cold bunk smelling a shirt that was only warm because *she* was warm — I already knew she was going to go over there with nothing in her hands. No bag. That detail undid me before she even got out of the car. She has a drawer, she has scrunchies and cream and a whole life arranged for her in that house, and she deliberately left the bag in the car because she knew if she carried it in she'd stay. That's not a girl manufacturing a fight from an unsent text. That's a grown woman walking in knowing exactly what she can and cannot survive another night of.

And then he does it. He does the whole thing perfectly, which is the worst part. Heat up hours before, wine already poured, towel over his shoulder, face opening at the door the way it always opens. Of course she hates that it still goes through her. I hated it too and I wanted it too, and that's the whole trap of Pace — none of her evidence is fake. He is not lying with his hands. He's just never, ever going to lie — or tell the truth — with his mouth if he can feed her instead.

*"You're the most important person in my life."*

Sir. That is not an answer. That is a beautiful evasion and he knows how to make it sound like a vow.

When she finally gets it out — inside this house, with the door shut, all of it is in here. Out there, what are we? How do I say this to Cassie, to my father, what do I tell my mother, that he feeds me and we have a lot of sex? — I literally had to put a hand up. Because that's it. That's been it for forty chapters. With no name on it the best thing in her life sounds sordid in her own mouth, and she has been swallowing that ugliness for him because inside the house it doesn't feel ugly at all.

And his answer. God. *Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?*

I wanted to throw my Kindle. It's true! That's what's enraging. It's true, and it's a magician's trick. Answer her question with a better question so she has to do the arguing for him. He reaches for the thing she's asking for and doesn't find it anywhere in himself — I actually believe that line, I believe he looked — and comes back with the truest thing he has. And the truest thing he has is still not *her*.

*Words are cheap. Believe what I do.*

*Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me.*

There it is. That's the line I'd text someone at 1 a.m. with no context. She has been building sentences for weeks, retiring them, forgiving him for things he didn't do, drowning the word in orgasm, counting his acts like coins — spin, salmon, coming back an hour later, flannel over another shirt all weekend — and she finally stops counting and asks for the coin he won't mint. One cheap word. Boyfriend. Girlfriend. Mine. Love. Anything that would survive outside that front door. And he folds his arms and holds the line at cost. That stillness — he is not indifferent, that's what kills me, he is holding a line *at cost*, like saying it would break some law in him — but the effect on her is exactly the same as indifference. She gets his name for her, *Vee*, once, from behind her, and her name is not the word she came for.

She did not want him to have the crying. That might be the bravest thing she's done yet, and she's done brave things naked in hallways. She turned her face to the door to keep it from him because everything else she has — body, hunger, confidences, the uncropped photograph of herself — he already has, and will not name. And then of course he'd already seen, and of course the cold went straight out of him and the hand came up, reaching to settle her with the hands that settle everything. *Not the hand in place of the word.* I felt so proud of her for knowing that in real time. A hundred younger heroines — a hundred younger versions of me — would have taken the hand because the hand is so, so good.

She went out into the cold and it was worse and she did not go back in. Just like the shirt. She'd rather be bare and freezing than warm in a thing that's only returning her own heat. I am gone for this girl. Completely gone.

What I am not doing is forgiving him because his jaw shut like that. Something went across his face and *shut* — there is something behind that shut door, the mother he won't touch, *I don't go back*, Daphne, all of it, and I know this book is going to eventually make me understand it, but understanding is not the same as Vee not standing on a mat in a too-warm house crying with her coat still buttoned to the throat. He left the door unlocked for her. He pours the wine before she arrives. He will not say one word that would let her walk into any other room in her life holding her head up. That is a choice he is making every single weekend, dressed up as a principle about words being cheap.

And Randi is hanging over all of this like smoke I can smell and Vee can't. Because we know why the naming might be structurally impossible, don't we. We know there's a third person holding a claim in the other room. Vee thinks she's asking a coward for courage. She might be asking a man with a lover for a word he's already spent — or promised — somewhere else. That makes this chapter hurt in a place she doesn't even know she has yet.

Please let her not text him tonight. Please let Cassie be awake when she gets back to that cold room. Please let her stay out in the cold long enough to find out it's survivable, because she just learned the shirt was.
