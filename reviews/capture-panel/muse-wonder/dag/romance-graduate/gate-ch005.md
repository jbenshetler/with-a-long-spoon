Okay, this is the first chapter where I actually felt a little sick — in the good way? In the *oh no, I see exactly what they're doing to her* way.

Because I've been inside Randi on that bench. I've watched her put the face back on in the bathroom and decide, very quietly, *not ever*. And then I have to sit at this sticky little coffee shop table and watch Vee do the exact same thing in reverse — walk straight toward being seen like it's sunlight.

The math thing should have been corny. A hot PhD solves the integral, please. I've read that scene 400 times and I can usually predict the dialogue beat for beat. This wasn't that. It was the pencil. He gives her back the pencil. He writes the ugly formula she *could* memorize and then basically says no, don't carry that around, you already know a true thing you thought was useless. And then she solves it herself. I felt that in my chest before I understood why I felt it — which, Jesus, the chapter even clocks that her body gets there a full second before her mind does.

That's the Pace thing, isn't it? Not the forearms — although, yes, the forearms, the rolled cuffs, the hand steady on the page, I am not immune — it's that he asks *what's drawing you to it?* and then actually stays there. He waits through the end of her sentence. He waits while she talks for five minutes about a project she's never told anyone about. Every man Vee knows asks the party question, Pace asks the real one and then doesn't flinch when she gives a real answer. I understood instantly why she was already pressing her palms flat to her thighs under the table trying not to fall through her chair.

And Randi. God. Randi sitting right there smiling the supportive-friend smile with her hand around a coffee she never drinks. *He was cute. You should go out with him.* She texted him in — I know she texted him in, I watched Cassie watch her do it — and then she has to watch him look at Vee the way... well, the way he does *not* look at a great-aunt, thank you Cassie. That little detail of Randi not doing her little name bit for him. Just "Randi." One word. When she does it for everyone else like a party trick. That told me more than any internal monologue would have.

Poor Cassie saying "Goodbye, Vee" in the last chapter and now this. She sees it. She's the only person at that table who isn't performing something, and she's being edited out of the story in real time and she knows it. That "as long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" — I laughed, and then I felt awful for laughing, because she's trying to keep Vee tethered to who she was yesterday.

I'm suspicious of how much I want Pace for Vee and how much I *don't*, knowing what the jacket told me. Every yes was freely given, that was the problem — I keep hearing that line now. Because this yes *is* free. He's courtly, he says "May I call you?", he leaves without his coffee, he texts about frozen yogurt and pays and holds the door with his back. It's the nicest a person has been to her in a really long time and it's fucking froyo and she says it out loud and he doesn't make it weird or big, he just says "Your tart's holding up." That restraint is hotter to me than half the explicit scenes I've read this year.

But I'm also sitting here knowing Randi chose her *because* she said yes on that quad with her whole body before her brain could dress it up. So is Pace falling for her, or is he playing the game exactly as designed and enjoying that it works so cleanly? I don't trust how pleased he was in chapter 3, and I don't trust how much I liked him in this one. Both things are true and that's exactly why I can't stop.

Vee eating too much frozen yogurt without hearing her mother in her head for once — that tiny thing undid me more than the integral. She's already portioning herself less around him after one afternoon. I know where that road goes in this book. I want her to get fed, literal and otherwise, and I'm terrified of who set the table.
