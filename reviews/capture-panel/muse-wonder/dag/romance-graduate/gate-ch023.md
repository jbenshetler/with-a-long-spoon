REACTION —

Okay the dancing got me in a way the sex sometimes doesn't, which is embarrassing to admit and also exactly why I'm still reading this book.

That whole lesson on the concrete pad. *Give me something to push back against. Being jelly doesn't work.* Close the space or you'll get stepped on. Don't anticipate, don't count, feel the lead arrive through his hand at your shoulder blade before your feet know. I was sitting there thinking, oh, we're not being subtle at all, are we — and then immediately thinking no, actually, we are, because that's *her*. That's Vee in bed, in the fitting, in every conversation. She either goes soft and apologetic or she braces and tries to guess the next step so she won't be caught wanting the wrong thing. And then that one moment where guessing just... stops, and they go around the pad twice and she's busy, she's not performing being good at it. I felt that in my chest. That's the same girl who rose up on her toes on the riser.

And Pace teaching. Of course he taught the class. Of course he's patient and specific and doesn't make her feel stupid for stepping wrong. *There's no sorry unless you take off a foot.* I laughed and then I was just gone for him again, which made the bar part hurt properly.

Because I knew the jealousy was coming — the hugs, the women lighting up, the count she didn't mean to start, I was doing it *with* her — and I still wanted to reach into the book when that blonde crossed the floor. Not because the blonde was even that cruel. She was precise. *Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to. All us girls love him. He's so good about getting a girl home safe.* Every word technically a compliment and every word designed to make Vee feel like a product on a conveyor belt. And it worked because it landed exactly on her mother's voice already in her head. *A man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them.* She didn't invent that insecurity in the bar, she brought it in with her.

What killed me is she *heard herself* do it. *Does your service include getting the girls home safe from here, too?* She hears the venom as it's leaving her mouth, she knows before he answers that it won't matter what he says, some part of her wants to stay angry. That's so much more honest than the usual romance fight where the heroine is just righteously wronged for twenty pages. She's not righteous. She's humiliated and flooded and she picks the ugliest available interpretation of the best thing about him — that women trust him because he has never once spent that trust — and throws it at him.

And he just... leaves. No chase, no big speech, sets the untouched beer down and goes out into the cold. I don't know if I respect that or want to shake him. Both, probably. He answered her straight, which is all he ever does, and then he removed himself. Meanwhile she's at that bar inventing his entire sexual history with every girl in a cut-off shirt, knowing she's doing it, doing it anyway. *She'd thought she was special. She was just next.* God. Starved girls would rather be next than risk believing they're chosen and be wrong. I have been that stupid at 1 a.m. in a man's shirt that no longer smells like him. That detail — she rationed the smell until it went stale, washed it, it came back smelling like fabric softener and no one, and she put it on anyway — that is the saddest, truest thing. I would text that line to a friend with no context.

The bartender scene saved it from being manufactured-conflict for me, barely, but it saved it because she didn't comfort her. *Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey.* Flat. Done being asked for things. And Vee doesn't get to have a cute cry and be forgiven by proxy; she gets to sit there under the lights coming up and do her own arithmetic: he could have had any of them, he hadn't, he'd chosen her, and she punished him for being exactly the man she wanted. That shame didn't feel like the old *floozie* shame either. It felt earned and specific and adult, which is worse.

Now she's going to Randi's in the morning to fix it, and my stomach just dropped, because of course she is. Where else would she go? Randi is the person who hands her hunger back to her as a gift, Randi will know exactly what to say about Pace and the girls and being chosen — and Randi is the *last* person on earth who can hear this confession cleanly. Vee thinks she's walking toward help. I'm watching her walk into the other half of the trap with her eyes open and her embarrassment gone cool and useful, and I hate how much sense it makes.
