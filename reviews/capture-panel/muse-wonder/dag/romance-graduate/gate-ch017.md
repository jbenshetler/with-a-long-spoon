REACTION — God, this one got under my skin in a completely different way than last night in his kitchen did.

Because I *felt* that first scene. Coming home still lit up, hair a disaster, no bag because packing a bag would have meant admitting to herself what she was driving over there to do — that is so painfully, exactly true. And then standing there with her skirt half-unzipped and realizing the evidence isn't a story she can edit anymore. She gave the green set away. She *folded them in thirds and handed them to him*. Of course she zipped herself back up in front of Cassie. Of course she ladled the middle clean out of the telling and called it kindness.

That broke my heart a little, actually. Cassie is the person who splashes her instead of asking if she's okay, the plain-speech person, and Vee is already learning which pieces of herself go where. Cassie gets *he was sweet, he cooked*. She doesn't push at the seam — I couldn't tell if she felt it and let it go out of love, or if Vee is getting better at lying than I want her to be — but either way, something small and important just moved out of that room.

And then Randi.

Randi does not let the seam exist.

I kept having two reactions at once through that whole brunch and they would not separate. On one level: yes. *Yes.* Somebody finally says to this girl, you painted your toes for him, you bought underwear hoping he'd take it off you, you knew how that night was going to end before you got in the car — why are you saying it like a confession? That is the exact grown-up permission Vee has been starving for her whole life. Not her mother's *floozie* running on a loop under the heat. Just: *I wanted it. And I took it. For once.* / *Good.* I felt that land in my own chest. I wanted to text someone at 1 a.m. just: she said it out loud!!!

On the other level I am watching Randi's hands the whole time. The sad-little-story first to clear the air — a beautiful boy, a hurry, dinner at least, poor me — it's so disarming, and it's a trade she's not actually making. She gives Vee a funny, costless version of a bad night and then collects the real one. *Tell me everything. I'm running on fumes here.* And Vee goes soft and quick over the middle exactly the way she did with Cassie, and Randi just... waits her out. Points the flute. *You left out everything in between. Everything.*

What undid me was the toes. Not the sex questions — the *toes*. Randi noticing the frosted plum, asking what it's called, asking why she picked it, and just sitting there warm and still while Vee realizes mid-sentence that the color is the silk, is the dress he's still making, is the thing she turned over in the dark all week. She never has to accuse her of anything. She just asks the next gentle question until Vee's face does the telling. And then — this is the part that makes Randi so dangerous and so hard to simply hate — she covers her hand and says *you're not caught, sweetheart, you're lucky*, and I believe she means it. I also believe she is memorizing every word.

There's something changing in her voice in that scene too, the esses going long, eyes liquid with one mimosa still mostly in the glass, and Vee files it under drunk because she has nowhere else to put it. I have somewhere to put it. I put it right next to *Tell me* in Pace's lap with the wine in her mouth. Randi is not mining this for gossip. She is *feeding* on it, and getting Vee to love her for the feeding, and the worst part is Vee is not wrong to love it. Nobody else listens to her like that. Nobody else makes wanting something she'd planned for feel like power instead of shame.

And then that goodbye kiss again. Longer on the second press, hand at the hinge of her jaw. First time, Vee explained it away as French and scanned the dorm windows in a panic. This time she half-expected it, she met it, and she didn't look. I sat up straight at that. Not because it's hot in the usual way — because of what's quietly being trained here. A mouth on hers in broad daylight at a curb is becoming *theirs*, becoming normal, becoming something she doesn't flinch from being seen doing. Randi isn't in a hurry either, is she.

Vee drove home lighter. Singing, radio up, day is good. And I closed the chapter feeling happy for her and sick about it in the same breath, which I think is exactly where this book wants me, and I resent how well it's working.
