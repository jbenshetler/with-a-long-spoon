REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay.

He didn't say the word. Let's get that out of the way first, because I came into this chapter braced for exactly that disappointment again — Vee naked at his little kitchen table, wine going warm, talking at his back because his back is somehow easier to be brave to than his face, and I thought: here it comes, another beautiful evening that stays *inside this house with the door shut.*

And then, with a plate in his hand, casual as asking her to pass the salt: *I want to meet your people.*

I actually had to stop there. Because this is the thing. This is maybe *more* than the thing? The word would be for her, in private, in the dark where everything true about them has always lived. This is him volunteering to walk out of the house. Into a room. With Cassie watching him with her flat streetlight eyes, and Kayla talking the paint off the wall, and Meg, and Danny, and Theo — *Theo*, who once asked her out, and Pace just says "Bring Theo" and reaches for the next pan like a man who has never once needed to mark his territory because he knows exactly where he lives in her. I am not well.

And he *remembers*. That's the part that undid me, more than the party itself. Meg's boots. A half-sentence on a trail in October, her heel coming up out of the right one when she pushed off — he kept that. He kept *all* of it, all fall, all those nights she thought she was just running on at his back filling the quiet while he washed up. He wasn't resting his face in her direction. He was shelving her whole life, name by name. My chest did something embarrassing at "Kayla's the one who can talk the paint off a wall?" — her own line handed back to her in his voice. God. There's being listened to and then there's finding out months later that a man has been *keeping* you.

But the thing I'll be thinking about at 1 a.m. is Vee. Because she does it — she almost builds the pretty little dinner anyway, Randi and real napkins and Cassie being a good sport under a pendant light, and then she *catches herself*. She runs every table in town and sees somebody having to be fine about it at each one, and she won't do it. She won't build a night one of her people has to endure politely. I don't know if she knows yet where she learned to watch for that — somebody being fine about it, in a room that wasn't built for them — but her body knows, and she listened to it, and just like that the dinner wasn't a dinner anymore. "Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one." *Him.* He got there in one sentence. Of course he did. A man who can't hear you in a loud room knows exactly what it costs to sit at the wrong table.

Which is why the end landed the way it did. That flash of him at Chili's, fork squared and turned and squared again over the nachos, drowning politely while she thought he was leaving her — she doesn't explain it, she doesn't make a speech about accessibility, she just says *Will you pick the place?* and lets him handle it. That's the most grown-up exchange in this whole book and nobody even touched anybody. Her trusting that he'll choose a room he can actually be in. Him getting to choose it instead of enduring it and calling that love. *Let me handle it*, and his face doing the thing it only does when it's happy and trying not to show it.

And underneath all of it, the exquisite, awful irony that I am simply supposed to sit here and hold: she is *so excited* to introduce him to Randi. Properly. Her two favorite people at one table at last, finally getting to find out about each other. Girl. GIRL. They chose you on a quad. She has reported your Saturdays into his bed. He heard about your goodbye kiss while he was inside her. And you're going to sit between them glowing because your worlds are finally in one room — and you're right, they will be, just not the way you think. When this dinner-that-isn't-a-dinner happens, I will be reading it through my fingers. Randi across from Cassie, who misses *nothing*. Pace in a room he picked, where he can hear every word. Everyone Vee loves, with all their separate pieces of the truth, eating snacks. It's a bomb with a guest list and she just addressed the invitations.

He still hasn't said it. I notice that. I'm not letting it go just because he offered me a party instead — *most important person in my life* and now *I want to meet your people* are both real, they're both more than most men in these books ever give, and they are both still him building the house bigger instead of saying the word for what's in it. But this chapter moved the walls. Out there is coming inside. Whatever happens when it does, it won't be containable behind his door anymore, and I think he knows that, and he asked for it anyway.
