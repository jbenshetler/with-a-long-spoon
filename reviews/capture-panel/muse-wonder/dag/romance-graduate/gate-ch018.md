REACTION — God, the small bowl broke me before either of them said a word.

He sliced her apples thin. He knows she portions herself, he's *known* it the whole time, he has a whole quiet system for feeding her without making it a thing — and tonight she draws one line through the top of the sauce and that's it, that's all she can do, and then she watches him be *glad* about Vee's Friday and doesn't even try to hide that she's watching him be glad. I felt that in my stomach. Not jealous-girlfriend theatrics. The real thing, where you're sitting across from the person who knows your body best in the world and he's lit up about someone else and you helped *build* that, you pointed at her, and now you're finding out that authorship doesn't get you a seat inside it.

Because that's the accusation, isn't it. *You've got her all weekend, every weekend, and you're in no hurry, and I can't think why you would be.* She's not wrong and she's not fair and both of those at once is exactly what makes it land. He keeps saying *she chooses it, every step of it, or it isn't anything* — and I believe him, that's the Pace I've been undone by for seventeen chapters, the pencil handed back, the last inch left — but oh, honey, listen to how clean that sounds from where he's standing. Of course he's in no hurry. He's the one getting chosen, Friday to Sunday, at his own pace, in his own house. Randi's getting the report afterward, over stew she can't eat. His ethics are real and they are also, right now, a very comfortable place for him to live. I wanted to shake him and I didn't stop trusting him, and that combination is why I'm still here.

And then the sex. Jesus. She doesn't do the inch-and-stop, she doesn't say *tell me* — every night, apparently, she's said it, and tonight she takes him with her eyes shut and her hair in her face and comes loud and fast and rolls away and gives him her *back*. That's hotter on paper than it felt, which is the point. It felt like watching someone use the one language she trusts to say a thing she cannot say out loud, and even that language failed — he lies there afterward doing inventory, *speed, eyes shut, no tell me, the back*, setting the pieces beside each other like one of his proofs and they "would not go together into anything he could name." A man who can read Vee's breathing from across a room cannot read this woman at all tonight. Or won't. *It was the want, with an edge on it tonight, from somewhere.* From somewhere! Pace. She told you from where. At your table. With her hands flat on the cloth.

What finished me was the refrigerator. Her uneaten bowl, skin forming over the peas, the one line still visible — and he covers it with a plate and puts it away *for her*, and turns the stove light off. He still doesn't know what he did. He keeps the food anyway. I texted nobody, I just sat there. That is the saddest, most married gesture in this whole book and neither of them is married to anything except this game they built, and the game is now eating its architects. Randi said *not ever, not that part again* in his bathroom mirror in chapter one and I have been waiting for the bill on that ever since. I think it's arriving. She's going to have to be seen wanting Vee, and wanting Pace, and being *left out* of the thing she invented — in daylight, with her face on wrong — or she's going to do something elegant and terrible instead. I'm a little afraid of which one she picks.
