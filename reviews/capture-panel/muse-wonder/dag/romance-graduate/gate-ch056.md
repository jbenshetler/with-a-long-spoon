REACTION

Okay, I went into this braced in a way I didn't even realize until Sheri got off that stool.

Because I've carried that blonde around with Vee since December. I *built* her with her. Tan legs, wine light, sitting in Pace's kitchen being the woman who got the version of him I don't get. And then Sheri is five-foot-nothing with two-color hair and crooked makeup and a voice that makes the whole taproom decide whether they're listening, and she hugs him like she means it with her eyes shut — and the jealousy doesn't go anywhere. That's what got me. It just... changes shape. It's not, oh phew she's not pretty enough to be a threat, because the book is too honest for that cheap relief, and Vee knows it in the room. The look Sheri gives her going down — keyhole, bare January legs, those ridiculous induction heels — I felt that in my stomach. It wasn't pricing her. It was *enjoying* her. Same way she watches the bartender go back to the taps. Unhurried. And Vee laughs out loud in the middle of a bar for no reason because a whole month of holding her shoulders up just leaves her body at once. I laughed with her. Embarrassed for her, fond of her. That borrowed skirt and dressing to fight a ghost — god, I've done versions of that. Not the skirt. The arming yourself sole to collar against a woman you've invented because the real fear has nowhere to sit.

And Pace blushing. PACE. *“And you get to motorboat them things?”* and the color just climbs his neck into his ears while he looks at the table and rubs his neck and doesn't fight it. I've seen that man composed through every humiliation this book owns — serving, kneeling, a room full of her friends, sex that would flatten lesser romance heroes into a smirk — and Sheri with one filthy aside in a taproom turns him into a boy. That's the whole friendship in one beat. She has a license nobody else has. Not even Vee yet. Not even, I think, Randi in quite that way. Sheri can tease the exact animal fact of him right in front of his girlfriend and he just *takes it*, pleased and mortified. There's history there that isn't competition. It's older than Vee and it doesn't need her permission, and the book lets Vee feel the blade of that — *he tells me you get the philosophy of his food, you get a philosophy, two years and I get derailleurs* — and also lets her be warmed by it. Because he DOES go on about her. Somewhere she isn't, to the one person he tells things to. She heard that. I heard that.

Then Sheri telling the real story when he's at the bar. I had to put the book down for a second at *the ones who owed me words weren't spending any.* Because I know what Sheri said to him in that diner in December — *she didn't ask for reasons, say it* — I've been carrying that woman like a knife for Pace for weeks. And now I get why she's the one who could say it. She knows exactly what it costs when people who owe you the words won't spend them. Her mother, her daddy, her church, the whole tablecloth. And Pace, mid-two-step, no questions, coffee and donuts on the porch next morning, fixed the lock, fixed the faucet, *he was there.* No false words. No *they'll come around.* Just there, and stayed there, for two years.

And then that one flat sentence slipped in under it — *went bad with his daddy, hasn't been home since he was sixteen* — every word Sheri has of it in two years, and she hands it to Vee and shuts the folder with no seam showing. That rearranged something in me about him. His whole religion of showing up and not saying it — the porch in September, standing his headlights on Sheri's door tonight until the porch light comes on, waiting without making a thing of waiting — it's not just temperament, is it. That's a boy who learned at sixteen what it is when home stops being a place that keeps you, and built an entire manhood out of being the person who stays in the truck until the light comes on. It makes the not-saying-it worse and sadder at the same time. He *knows* what withheld words do. Sheri is living proof he knows. And he still can't spend them on Vee. Sheri told him plain and he still can't. Jesus, Pace.

What stayed with me most, weirdly, is the end. No heat, really — she's drunk on cider, head heavy on his shoulder, both his hands at ten and two *like something full he was carrying and had no intention of spilling.* And her just thinking *bacon cheeseburgers* at the window because Sheri got one on the worst morning of her life and Vee is only now learning that's his native language. He feeds people. He waits with the headlights on. He says *Vee's boyfriend* six times in a roadhouse but he cannot say the other thing, and the book keeps making me love him harder for the first list while I stay furious about the second. I'm so deep in this man's damage I could draw a map of it and I still don't know his father's name or what happened at sixteen, and neither does Vee, and Sheri barely does.

I texted nobody but I thought it: the rival chapter turned into the chapter that cracked him open widest, and Vee doesn't even know she's holding the pieces yet.
