REACTION —

Okay, this is the chapter that should have lost me on paper — no Pace, no Randi, no sex, basically a girl going swimming with her roommate and then sitting in a towel thinking — and instead I sat in it completely.

Because this is finally Vee from the *inside*, not Vee being looked at across a quad or a coffee shop table. And god, the water part got me. That beat where she goes under and everything just... switches off. The noise, the bleachers, having to hold her face in the right arrangement. And she stays down a beat past where her lungs ask her to come up, and *grieved, a little.* I felt that in my ribs. That's not the interchangeable curvy-heroine-confidence speech I have read four hundred times. That's a girl who loved something before her body became a public event, and the water is the only place it hasn't been taken from her yet. I have not been given that from Randi, not really — Randi is all surface we watch cracking — but Vee just handed it to me in three paragraphs underwater.

And Cassie. I LOVE Cassie now, which I did not expect. Not the sassy-best-friend quip machine. She sees Vee come up with her face on wrong and she doesn't do the *are you okay?? talk to me* thing. She just starts a water fight from ten feet away, badly, on purpose. Keeps it low on the walk down past the guys yelling "You won't need floaties with *those*!" too — doesn't make a scene for her, just says it for the two of them: "They will never know the love of a woman." That's real friendship writing. That's someone who knows exactly how much rescue you can stand being seen receiving.

Also — and this is the romance-reader brain in me at 1 a.m. — "Don't answer tonight."

Who *does* that?? He asks her to dinner Saturday, watches the yes literally form in her mouth, and tells her not to give it to him yet. Every other book would have him do that as a power move, smirk included. Here it just sits there odd and courtly and a little unsettling, and she's turning it over in the locker room half-dressed thinking about his knuckles and how he folded into a chair like he'd measured it first. I am right there with her winding that wet curl around her finger. He's not gorgeous and she knows it and it does not matter at all, which is so much hotter to me than another described jawline.

It's a quiet chapter. I know it's a bridge — we're killing time until Saturday dinner. I don't care. This is the first chapter where I stopped thinking of Vee as the girl Randi and Pace picked and started thinking of her as a person they're going to *do this to*, and that makes the whole thing in the jacket copy suddenly feel a lot less sexy-fun-game and a lot more like I'm bracing. In a good way. In a keep-reading way.
