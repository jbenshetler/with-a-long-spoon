REACTION

Oh, this one hurt in the exact quiet way this book keeps hurting me.

Reading Vee's side of that text after living through Randi's night — God. I already knew the fairytale was a lie, I watched her put her face back on over nothing, with that perfect, empty man asleep behind her, and then Vee gets *it's honestly a fairytale out here* and believes it completely. Lights up for it. Is *grateful* for it, lying there in that cold little-girl room while the year ends. I wanted to climb through the phone. Not even to tell her about John — to tell her Randi wasn't warm and chosen and out living some huge visible life while Vee hides hers behind a shut door. Randi was just as cold in that bed. She just had better scenery.

That was the thing that got me most, actually, how both of them are doing this tonight. Both lying awake next to — or wanting — a man, both constructing the other girl's night as the real one. Vee envying Randi being *seen*, Randi in the last chapter knowing damn well being seen by everyone in that ballroom meant absolutely nothing because nobody in it found her underneath. And Vee knows the MIRA thing! She knows whose money bought Gstaad, she sat there and watched Randi blanch and she has never, not once, used it or even let on she saw it — she read her own sweet little text twice to make sure nothing in it gave her away. She protects Randi's secret instinctively. Randi protects hers by lying prettier. I keep coming back to that difference and it makes my chest ache for both of them in completely different ways.

And Pace. Or the absence of Pace, which is somehow worse than the fight. Chapter 42 I was so proud of her — *words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me* is a line I will be thinking about at 2 a.m. for weeks — and now this is the cost of it. No villain, no blowup, just... nothing since. She doesn't know if she's broken up with. She doesn't know if *he* thinks anything even happened. She starts *are you*— and deletes it letter by letter and is *glad* he won't see the dots, and that detail undid me, because that's exactly right, that's the specific humiliation of wanting someone who leaves you all the room in the world. Room is a lovely thing until it's the only thing he's giving you.

The jealousy spiral should have felt like a trope and it didn't, because of how it was built. She doesn't just imagine him with someone else in the abstract — she reaches for her *best* night, the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, her body quiet and used well, specifically to warm herself up, and the blonde walks into her own memory and sits down in her chair. She can't even keep the past. That's such a true, ugly, midnight thing. Your brain doesn't give you a new bad picture, it evicts you from the good ones you were living on. And the girl she invents being her exact opposite — tan, model's body, never needed building for — that's her mother's voice and every dressing-room mirror she's ever stood in, dressed up as a rival. She handed him that girl. Lying there at 11:52 telling herself she did without him before and can do it again and not believing a word of it.

I'll be honest, part of me registered: this is another quiet one. No heat on the page, nobody touches anybody, it's a girl in a twin bed scrolling photos. Two chapters ago I might have started skimming for dialogue. But this didn't feel like the book stalling — it felt like the book trusting me to sit in the part where nothing happening *is* the thing happening. The room that loves Vivienne and has no idea who's in the bed. The trophies dusted. Her legs too long since middle school. That's not throat-clearing, that's the whole girl, before and after, in one cold room.

What I'm carrying into the next chapter is that last ragged little sorting she does: Randi she gets back on a day she can circle. Pace she cannot place anywhere. And she falls asleep still reaching, holding both of them, setting neither down. I need term to start. I need to see what she does when she has to look at him in daylight and find out whether there *is* a him to look at anymore — and I need, God, I need Randi's face when she calls her gorgeous across a table like none of this winter happened.
