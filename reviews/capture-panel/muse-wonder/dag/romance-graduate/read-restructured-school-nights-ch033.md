REACTION

Okay, this one got me in a quieter way, and also — I'm noticing I'm doing the thing where I keep waiting for the other shoe to drop because this book has trained me that the quiet chapters are never actually quiet.

Cassie asking her flat-out, book still open, "Do you love him" — and Vee, who has not shut up since she walked in lit up like a streetlight, suddenly going *maybe, I think so, it's all so fast.* That tiny voice. God. Because I *know* she already said it to him sleeping. I know she laid there in chapter 32 and put the word down plain in her own chest and whispered "Peter" to a man who didn't hear her. And then someone who actually loves her and isn't trying to get anything from her asks her in daylight, and she shrinks it. That doesn't feel like lying, exactly. It feels like she can only afford the truth when nobody's awake to hand it back to her. That broke my heart a little.

And Cassie letting the maybe stand. Not pushing, not doing the girlfriend speech, just "go to sleep, streetlight." I love her more every time she appears and I'm also getting more scared for her, because she's the only person in Vee's life whose attention doesn't take a piece of her, and Vee is already spending more nights out of that room than in it. Cassie counted it before Vee did. Of course she did.

The pool section — listen, this is the kind of thing I would DNF in a lesser book. A whole long swim set, stroke counts, breathing every third, no dialogue. But it worked on me because it's the first time her brain actually shuts up. Everywhere else she's narrating herself, editing herself, watching herself be watched. In the water her body just *works* and doesn't have opinions, and you can feel how rare that is for her. "It never answered anything, the swimming. That was the whole use of it." Yeah. I felt my own shoulders drop reading it.

That one flicker in the middle, though — four strokes where she can't tell whether Pace is really that good or she's built him that good out of wanting to be looked at like that. Four strokes and then the wall comes and she rolls out of it. I wanted to grab her. Honey, stay with that thought. That's the most honest question she's asked herself in thirty-three chapters and she swam right through it, and I understand why she did, and that's exactly the problem.

The *did he mind* thread running under the whole swim lands differently because I already know about the drawer. I already know he cleared his own stuff into the bottom drawer and gave her the top one. So I'm watching her churn through laps worrying she's too much, too spread out, too messy on his clean counter, knowing he's already answered her in the only language he actually speaks — and she hasn't found it yet at this point in her timeline. That's sweet and it's also kind of damning for him, isn't it? He will rearrange his whole bathroom rather than say "you're not too much, stay." She is going to keep drowning in the silence between what he does and what he won't say, and the drawer is going to feel like proof it works, and that worries me even while it melts me.

I will say — this is the second slower, interior chapter in a row for me after the drawer chapter, even if the dates shuffle around. I'm not bored, the writing is too real for that, the pool earned its pages. But I'm starting to feel the itch in my thumb. I need someone to actually *do* something soon — Cassie to say the plainer thing, Randi to slip, Pace to use a word. The pressure is all loaded. Somebody needs to set it off.
