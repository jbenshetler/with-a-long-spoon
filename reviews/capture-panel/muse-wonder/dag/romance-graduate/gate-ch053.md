Okay. *Vee's boyfriend.*

He said it six times. To every single person. Flat, plain, like he was giving them his name because that *was* his name now, and I swear my body reacted before my brain did every time. After December — after *what am I to you out there* and *words are cheap* and her walking out into the cold and a whole month of silence — he doesn't give her a speech. He doesn't finally choke out the other word in private where nobody can hold him to it. He walks into a pine-paneled roadhouse twenty minutes out in nowhere that he picked specifically so he could actually *hear* people, puts VEE on butcher paper, and introduces himself as the boyfriend to Kayla, to Meg, to Theo who is visibly terrified, to Cassie.

Cassie. Oh my god. *You can stay.* From Cassie that is a knighting. And watching him do what he does — I finally get to see it from the outside, the way he does it to Vee but deployed on everyone — Danny never notices he never got a bench number, Theo's shoulders literally coming down out of his ears at *where does it start, for you*, Meg getting funnier because someone set her up and then got out of her way, Kayla's sister's wedding dress extracted in minutes. He remembered everything. Every stupid little detail she ever told him in bed or over tea, he kept it, and he spent it all making her people feel like the most interesting person in the room. That's not charm. Charm is cheap. This is a man who listened for months and brought the receipts. I was sitting there glowing for her like an idiot.

And I need to talk about Randi at that table because my stomach did the most complicated thing. The *highlight of my year* toast — the teasing just falling out of her voice mid-sentence, going true right in front of Pace, glass up, *to my beautiful best friend* — and then that quiet afterward. The glazed quiet, glass turning a quarter at a time, thumbnail going white under the rim. I know that description now. Vee thinks she's proud of being able to move Randi. Baby. That is not a moved woman. That is a woman holding something down with her whole hand. And Pace was right there, tipped back out of the light, watching the way he watches everything, and then he *reported* Randi's brunch line to Vee like a man delivering a compliment — and for one heartbeat Vee panicked about what "knows" might mean, and honestly so did I. The twinge she had too, watching him laugh with Randi by the buffet, her arm coming up across herself before she knew she'd moved it — Christmas jealousy, the phone that didn't ring — she talked herself out of it in ten seconds because *this was her own doing*, and she's right, and also that half-second is going to matter someday and we both know it. She's getting so good at filing things.

Then the parking lot. The part where she can't keep it to the bedroom, where a whole evening of watching him be wonderful at her people lands on him against the truck, hands pulled up under her own sweater, and then in the cab — the stillness. *The man who did six things at once, down to zero.* That line destroyed me. That's the detail you can't fake and can't get from any of the interchangeable alphas on my Kindle, his hand going forgotten in her hair because she has knocked him completely offline, and her staying greedy about it, and *I need you to take me to bed now / Yes, ma'am, not quite evenly.* And then the drive, her doing it herself, unbuttoning her own jeans in a moving truck, holding the lace aside for him — an offering she has to keep making — while he drives steady one-wristed like the road is another thing he's doing to her. She came in the truck with her jeans at her knees and just... stayed undone. Didn't fix anything. Rode home like that.

That's the whole book in one chapter for me. The naming she begged for, given in public where it counts. Her friends folded in instead of kept in a separate sealed room. And heat that isn't decorative — it's literally the overflow of being claimed out loud. Except it's also built on the one small sentence still standing at the window watching Randi's glass turn, and she knows it, and she drove right past it tonight because her blood was too loud. I would have too. God help her, I would have too.
