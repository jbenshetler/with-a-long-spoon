REACTION

Okay so the whole chapter is a con and I fell for it, which — fine, I'll take it. I spent forty minutes reading what I assumed was Vee's night, getting mildly bored with how *thoroughly* the book was appraising this girl, and then: "There you are, Randi." And every catalogued detail flips its meaning. The sorority monogram, the apple sliced thin, the resting face of slight surprise. She's the one with the plan and she's face-down on the furniture. That's a good move. That's a genuinely good structural move and I sat up.

Because here's what it does to the jacket's problem. The blurb sold me something I'm constitutionally suspicious of — the woman who *engineers* another woman's wanting, the appetite that arrives because someone installed it. That shape has burned me enough times that I almost didn't buy this. And chapter one, before we've met Vee at all, takes the schemer and shows me the inside of her ribs and it's *terror of being seen*. Not appetite, not predation — a woman who has spent twenty-one years building a face so that nothing reaches her, discovering there's a person back there and deciding, quiet as a deadbolt, to never let her out again. If that's the woman who's going to be having lunches with Vee and "drawing her further open" — then the game is not a spider thing, it's a woman doing to someone else the only thing she knows how to survive. I'm still not sure the book will pay for it. But it's not the cheap version. Not yet.

The bathroom is the best thing in here by a mile and it's not close. The mirror, the smile that "sat on top of the face that had begged," the eyes she won't look at twice. *The face took the being-looked-at and none of it reached any further than the face.* I know that woman. I've dated her. And the water bottle cap pressed into the nail bed — slow, steady, where it would hurt, and she doesn't know she's doing it, and he takes the bottle away without registering a thing. That detail is worth the whole spanking sequence.

Which — let's talk about it. The long middle is where I nearly wandered off. It's all his POV and his POV is a *connoisseur's*, and after a while the strike-soothe-check-in-strike rhythm started reading like a procedure manual written by a man very pleased with his own patience. He's too good. He built the bench, he sliced the apple at the right thickness, he noticed which way the cut sides face, he left the water bottle out in advance, he has a tastefully tragic ex named Daphne who taught him to Respect The Closed Door. I clocked the prose being a little in love with him and I got my back up about it. And then the last page saved it, because he's *wrong*. He's sitting there warm with the pleasure of having taken her further than he thought she'd go, thinking about a girl who finally let someone feed her, while she's three feet away having made an irrevocable decision to hide from him forever. The book knows he missed it. That's the difference between a fantasy and a novel and I'll extend credit on the strength of it.

Also — yes, fine, the begging worked on me. The two *pleases*, the one that's the waiter voice and the one that comes from below, and him hearing the difference. I was clocking the machinery the entire time and it still got me. That's an honest trade.

What I'm holding it to: there is not one woman-with-woman beat in this entire chapter, and I bought this book for what the jacket promised between Randi and Vee. I don't count that against chapter one — it's smart to break Randi open *before* she touches Vee, so whatever happens there is already loaded. But the clock is running. If Randi's wanting for Vee turns out to be a move in the game rather than something the book will look at as squarely as it just looked at this mirror, I'm out, and no amount of beautiful furniture will hold me.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *it's not the college girl on the bench. it's the one running the con. and she went in the bathroom and locked the door and decided to never be a person again. I'm in.*
