REACTION

Oh, that's *mean*. In the good way. You gave me Randi's night first — the sleigh, the truffle, the faultless man asleep in the faultless room, *warm only where she touched him* — and then you walked me into a twin bed in Ohio under a shelf of little gold swimmers and let me watch Vee *envy* it. I knew. I knew the chalet was hollow, I knew the text she was reading was composed over a sleeping stranger with her lipstick still on him, and I had to sit there while Vee read *it's honestly a fairytale out here* and thought *she's fine, better than fine* and felt poor. I've read maybe four books that have made me want to reach into the page and I'm in one.

And both texts come through twice, word for word, and both of them are *true and not the thing.* Randi's "every word of it true, none of it the thing" and Vee reading her own message twice "for anything that gave her away." Two women passing each other identical sealed envelopes and each one thinking she's the only one with a secret in hers. Vee was in the glass room. Vee saw Randi blanch at MIRA and has never said one word and *envies the mountains anyway, because she knew what bought the dream and wanted it regardless* — that's an ugly honest thing to put in your heroine and I respect the hell out of it.

The thing that actually got me, though, the thing I'd text about at 1 a.m.: she went back for the fra diavolo night. Her best night, the sheet slipping, the wine she hadn't drunk, the body quiet. She went in there *to be warm*. And it turned on her while she was inside it. The hair went gold, the leg went tan, and the blonde sat in her chair in the wine light and Pace kept cooking. She can't keep her own memory. Jealousy doesn't just invent a rival, it *repossesses your archive* — I have never seen that written and I've absolutely lived it. And the bartender and Randi's reassurances going "straight through her hands" because they only ever held her up while she knew she was the one. *The floor was gone.* Yes.

Also: "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That's my note from fifty chapters ago handed back to me. The man's whole virtue is that he never pushes, and now the non-pushing is a silence she has no standing to break. She has no name for herself so she has no claim on him, so she can't even ask who he cooked for at Christmas. The architecture of this is so tight it's cruel.

What I'm sitting with: she ranked them. A day she can circle for Randi — class, Tuesday, seven to eight-twenty, a face that lights up and calls her gorgeous and *means it* — and for Pace, nothing. No hour, no room he'll surely be in. And then *and what about Pace?* and nothing came after it. Her own mind went blank where he should be. The book is tipping, out loud, and the woman it's tipping toward is lying in a hotel in Gstaad with a cold she brought in herself and a decision already made to never say the word.

Two gripes, and one of them's getting louder. The small one: a couple of lines explain themselves to me. "Same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it" — I'd already got there from the scissored leg, I didn't need the caption. Same with the last clause about the room keeping watch over the girl who was gone; the ribbons and the clarinet had already done that work beautifully.

The big one: she spent a week in her mother's kitchen. A nurse mother, casserole and potatoes and the good meat, wrapping presents at the table for days, and this chapter even *walks right up to it* — food that was never what she was hungry for — and still, fifty-two chapters in, not one person has said *honey, you've gotten thin.* And then she describes her own body in the dark as the thing a man would trade for a fashion model. If that's a gun on the wall, fine, I'm braced. But a whole Christmas at home was the natural place for it to go off and it didn't, and I'm starting to wonder if the silence is load-bearing or if it's just a hole.

Also — two chapters now with no Pace on the page and nobody speaking out loud to anyone. I don't resent a bridge, and this diptych earned itself. But I felt the second one. If chapter 53 is a third interior night I'm going to start thumbing for dialogue.
