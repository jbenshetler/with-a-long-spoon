Oh, I see what you did. You gave me the same night twice.

I asked for chapter fifty-one with my whole chest. I wanted Vee in that car on the dark highway with no bag. And what I got is the far side of a text message I already watched get composed in a Gstaad hotel room — I know every word of *you ANGEL 😇* before it lights her ceiling, I know it was true and not the thing, I know Randi turned the phone facedown afterward and went to lie in borrowed warmth. So the beat the chapter builds toward, the phone going off near midnight, landed on me like nothing. I was forty minutes ahead of the book the whole way and the book made me walk it anyway. That's the first time this series has had me waiting on it instead of the other way round.

And yet. *She could not keep her own night.*

That's the thing I'll carry out of here. She reaches back for the best thing she owns — the top sheet, the wine she didn't drink, the fra diavolo coming together while her body sat quiet and used-well — reaches for it the way you'd reach for a blanket, and the blonde *takes the chair.* Same garlic, same pepper blooming, same tomatoes going in, and the leg on the chair is tan now and the sheet is slipping off a body that never once needed building for. Her own memory, overwritten in real time, in her own head, and she can't evict her. God. I know that. I have done exactly that at midnight in a cold bed and it is the most accurate thing this book has done since the empty text field. And it's worse than jealousy — it's dispossession. She can't even be alone with the good part anymore.

The other one that actually opened a hole in the floor: the reassurances going through her hands. *He doesn't take advantage, I'd have heard. Pace don't bring dates. Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey.* She's been living on those for a hundred pages and so, frankly, have I — every time I got nervous about him I reached for the bartender wiping glasses. And now the book points out, flatly, that none of that was ever load-bearing. It only held her up *while she knew she was the one.* Take away the standing and the evidence evaporates, because it was never evidence about him, it was evidence about her position. That's brutally smart and it also pulled the chair out from under *me,* which I didn't love and did respect.

The room, I'll give it. The ribbons and the little gold swimmers with their toes pointed and the clarinet in the closet, a shrine kept dusted to a girl who doesn't live there, and the ceiling coming down close because she's been breathing bigger air. The question coming at her from a fresh angle every day and always the same underneath. *She had no answer that would fit in this house.* Fine. That's good. But then the book turns around and tells me *same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it* — I wrote four lines about that in my own notes a chapter ago, I got there on my own, I was proud of getting there, and having it handed back to me with a bow on it makes me feel managed. Same with *the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark.* I'd already felt the garlic and the hands rhyme. You don't need to draw me the diagram. The one that made me put the book down on my chest for a second, though — *the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone* — that's a sentence doing a little performance at the end of a chapter, and I noticed it performing.

What's genuinely moving underneath all of it, and what makes me not quit: Pace has gone *silent for an entire break.* Not cold — absent. No word since the door, and she has no name for herself so she has no standing to send one, and she knows it, and so she sits there with her thumb over a field she's already learned is useless. And he said nothing at all about Christmas. A whole week she can't see into — after Thanksgiving, after *she's alone,* after the pie. I have not forgotten Sheri and neither has Vee, and the book is being very careful to keep that door shut and unlocked.

And then, so quietly it's almost cruel: *a day she could circle for the one and nothing at all for the other.* The term starts and Randi is a guaranteed face in a guaranteed chair twice a week, seven to eight-twenty, *just us,* and that is now the single thing in the new year Vee can count on. While Pace is a maybe. That's not a chapter about missing a man. That's the trap closing on schedule, in the dark, while the girl inside it calls it *the year's gift she had literally stumbled into.* **She envies a life she knows is poisoned and says so out loud and reaches for it anyway.** She was in the glass room. She saw MIRANDA. And she still wants the chalet. I don't think the book is condemning her for that and I don't either, and the honesty of it is the best argument this chapter makes for itself.

Texting a friend at 1 a.m.: *fifty-one is her side of a night I already read and the payoff text is verbatim. BUT she tries to remember the best sex of her life for comfort and another woman walks into the memory and sits down in her chair and she can't get her out. i'm sick about it.*

I want the first day of term. I want her to walk into that Anthro room, and I want to watch the kindest person in her life be kind to her on purpose.
