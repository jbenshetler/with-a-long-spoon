REACTION

Oh, *finally*. Fifty-three chapters and somebody in this book just said the thing to his face.

I got my Sheri table. Not the Thanksgiving one — a better one. A double-wide on a county road with photos under amber glass and a waitress who says "the usual?" already writing, because there's never been a question and never had been. That line took something out of me, and not the sweet way. Vee is lying in a twin bed in Ohio inventing a tan blonde out of spite because she cannot name what she is to him — and the actual answer is that he has a *standing order* somewhere. A regular night, a regular booth, a waitress who knows them, a woman who steals his fries. The exact public, ordinary, witnessed life Vee asked for on the mat and didn't get. He has it. Just not with her. That's the cruelest thing this book has done and it did it with a diner tee and a half-apron.

And Sheri. God, Sheri is *great*, and it is such a relief that she's not a threat in the way Vee's 11:52 brain needed her to be. She doesn't want him, isn't impressed by him, gives him hell, and — Paige. Junior in the design school, birds mostly, a laugh too big for her, a house with seven at the table all hollering. *I liked it there.* Lasted a couple months. So she's queer, she's lonely, she's part-time now because nobody's behind her writing checks, and she will not talk to her people. And *call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas* — and Elvis coming on behind him about being home for Christmas, and "somebody always plays it," said down into a plate without looking up, and then nobody feeding the machine again. I had to stop reading for a second. That's four lines doing what most books need a flashback for.

Here's what put my hair up: she doesn't go back, and he doesn't go back, and they have been eating cheeseburgers together for two years in the only place for eleven miles and neither one of them has ever asked the other to fix it. *People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice.* He doesn't touch the irony. She says *don't waste the good stuff on me.* These two are the same object and the book put them across a table from each other and did not once lean in and tell me so. Thank you. Thank you for not explaining it.

But then she turns around and does to him what nobody in Vee's whole life has had the nerve to do to *her*. "That's a hell of a lot of reasons for a man who's already got the girl. She didn't ask you for reasons." And he's laying it up brick on brick — I'm there every weekend, the weeknights were hers, nothing else is in the room, I said she was the most important thing in my life, *out loud, in words* — and I could feel him doing the thing where a man believes a sufficient quantity of true statements eventually equals the one he won't make. "And she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something." He *heard* her. He understood what happened on the mat. He just can't do the arithmetic that ends in the word.

"Is that so hard?" and he doesn't answer. "So say it." / **"I can't."** / **"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her."** And he looked down. He *looked down*. I've watched this man hold a line with his arms folded while she cried, and a five-foot biker in a chambray shirt got his eyes off him in one sentence. And the worst part is she's right from where she's sitting and I'm one of the only people who knows she's sitting in the wrong seat — because I know what's on that couch, and I know *can't* isn't a stall, it's a load-bearing wall. He'd have to lie by the size of a whole other woman. "Most men'd just lie." / "I'm not them." / "I know you're not." No praise in it, just a fact. That exchange is the whole architecture of him in eleven words.

**"She find out about the brunette, then?"** I sat up. Sheri *knows there's a brunette*. The girl Pace eats dinner with every week knows, and Vee doesn't, and Randi met Sheri at the dance hall, looked at her like one more harem applicant, and never learned her name. Three women in a triangle and the only one who's been given the full field is the one with no stake in it. And his answer — "No. Not like that." *Not like that.* Which means it comes some other way, and he knows it's coming.

Also: the fork. He squares the fork to the table edge again, and this time it's not a crowded room, it's him reaching for the true shape of a sentence. So the Chili's fork may not have been only panic — it was a man trying to find a word with the noise coming down through the ceiling. That recontextualizes an entire chapter and the book never says a syllable about it. I love this book when it does that.

And then it lets him be ridiculous, which I needed. *The air's different when she's in the room. It crackles.* / "You mean the sex is good." / "That's not what I meant. But, yes. It is." And then he goes and says she understands the *philosophy* of his food and she stares at him and says "Lord almighty. Get over yourself," and he *laughs*, first full one of the night. Somebody finally punctured him and the puncture didn't cost him anything. Meanwhile she's hooting about the backside and the long legs and Christmas, and his face is hot and he keeps going — so the book handed Vee's body back to her, appreciated, in another woman's filthy affectionate mouth, in the same week Vee lay in the dark deciding her body was the loser version. I noticed. It hurt in a good way.

One honest thing: that's three chapters now with nobody in a room together — Randi in Gstaad, Vee in Ohio, Pace out on the county road. I felt the third one coming and I was ready to be annoyed. This one got away with it because it's all talk, and the talk has teeth, and talk is the thing I'll always take. But I want those two in the same house. I'm owed a doorway.

**Brooks and Dunn cover band. The hall. First Saturday in February. "You're coming, and you're dancing, and I don't care what the redhead's got to say about it."** That's a date on a calendar in a book where Vee just thought *a day I could circle for the one and nothing at all for the other*. I have a date and she doesn't, and I'm going to be sick about it until we get there.
