**Pace** — mathematician, finishing a PhD, younger than you'd expect for it. Not big, not striking, pleasant ordinary face, forearms that aren't a math tutor's. **Vee / Vivienne Thorne** — "V.T., yes like the university." HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

**Randi** — I can't hate her and I've stopped trying. She's not cruel, she's *evacuating*. Vee is the workaround that lets her be alive without ever coming out from behind the face again. And she's already losing control of it: the want for Vee was bigger on the couch than on the lawn, and the smile sat on top of it crooked, the same way the photograph smile sat on the face that had begged. She owes Vee everything and will not pay it. She owes Pace honesty about what happened in that bathroom and will not pay that either. I'm rooting for her anyway, which is embarrassing.

**Pace** — I'm attracted to him and I don't trust him, and the book refuses to resolve that for me, which is why I'm still here. He isn't sinister. He's *wrong*. He read her body perfectly and her mind catastrophically. He's doing genuinely good things to Vee — the pencil, the *may I*, the last inch left uncrossed — and every one of them is also technique, and I think it's both, and both is the horror. The crack: he withheld Vee's froyo confession from Randi and called it respecting what was hers. That's the first asset he's kept back from his partner in the game. That's where it comes apart.

**Vee** — protective, helpless about it. She thinks not-knowing is anticipation. "The pile of things she'd get to find out, which was the best pile there was." She knows a road.

**Cassie** — I love her and I'm pre-grieving. Every check she ran came back clean, which is worse than if one hadn't. She said the long word on purpose. She's the thing that gets spent.

**Brooke** — the only person in the book with power Randi can't manage, and she has the name written down.
