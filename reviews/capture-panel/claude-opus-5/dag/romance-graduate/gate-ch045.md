REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where the book exhales, and I can tell it knows it — *The New Ordinary*, which is an honest title, I'll give it that. After the MIRA chapter I came into this one braced. I was ready for Vee to be sitting in a glass room with a name still in her eyes. Instead: five nights off, a cold drive, and a hallway.

And the hallway is genuinely great. The jeans. THE JEANS. Cold from the car, stuck on her hips, no dignity anywhere in it, hopping one-legged and kicking them at the office door — I laughed out loud, and then it got hot *because* of the laughing, which is the thing nine hundred alphas in my Kindle have never once managed. "You're not helping." "I'm helping." And him catching the back of her bra and not pulling, just *holding it* so she has to unwind herself out of it like something off a spool — that's the whole man in one gesture. He never does the work of taking. He arranges it so you walk out of it yourself. I've been saying that about him for twenty chapters and this is the cleanest little illustration yet.

Then the part that actually got me, which is her coming apart in about ninety seconds and being *humiliated by the arithmetic*. "Her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private." That's it. That's the engine of this entire book. She tells herself a careful story about needing nights for school and her own throat rats her out. And earlier — she called the five nights herself, and he said *of course*, and some unreasonable corner of her wanted him to FIGHT for them. Nobody writes that. Everybody writes the man who sulks; almost nobody writes the woman who's disappointed he didn't, and knows it's unreasonable, and resents the grace. That cost her more than a fight would have. Yes. Correct. Painfully correct.

The flannel. Oh, the flannel. I was ready to be annoyed at another cooking set piece and then she knelt over the bag and there was a folded square at the bottom and I knew before she said it. She wore it every night she wasn't there until it *went to nothing* — the smell dying by degrees, stale and then faint and then gone — and she washed it rather than keep a dead thing. And his face doing the puzzlement and then the little flinch of hurt because he thought it was being returned. And *"This is still my shirt. You don't get to keep it."* And then — this is the one — he pulls it on over the shirt he's already wearing and stands there in his own kitchen in two shirts because she told him to. Five hundred books of grand declarations and what wrecks me is a man wearing two shirts. She finally *asked for something out loud*, with instructions and a deadline, and she's the girl who keeps things. That's a turn in the bond and I'm counting it as one.

Now. The complaints, because I have two and one of them is large.

Small one: the pizza. I wrote in my own notes a while back that the cooking inventories had collapsed into one dish and I'd skim the next one, and reader, I skimmed. Water buffalo cheese, cornmeal like a handful of sand, fine, lovely, he'd have made it for an empty house, I *know*, you've told me. But then we got the tasting-it-apart paragraph for the third time — the yeast, the acid, the late heat, the separate pieces laid out to count — and then the book turned around and explained it to me: *it was the difference between cotton candy and a bowl of cut strawberries.* Don't. I had it at "my mouth had been asleep." I had it two chapters ago when she said it to Randi and Randi said *he woke you up* and Randi's whole face did a thing. You don't get to hand me that simile. That's the one move in this book that makes me feel managed.

Large one: chapter 44 happened and this chapter does not know it. Randi read her father's name over the worst point in the county and said *cheating bastards* to a screen, and Vee saw all of it and said nothing, and then we cut to Vee driving out to get railed in a hallway with Randi nowhere in her head. Not once. Not a flicker at the goodbye kiss, not a thought in the car. I don't mind a breather chapter — I've said the best scene in a book was two people talking — but this is the *second* domestic Vee-and-Pace-and-food chapter in this stretch and it came directly after the single most loaded thing that's happened since the brunch, and it let the air out. I'm not bored. I'm impatient, which is different and more dangerous.

And the thing I'm going to be insufferable about at 1 a.m.: *she threw the thong and it landed green on the long, unusually proportioned table that stood in the middle of his bedroom.* I'm sorry — the WHAT. A long, unusually proportioned table. In the middle of his bedroom. Dropped in a subordinate clause like a coat hook. We have been in that bedroom nine thousand times and nobody has ever mentioned a table and the first time we do it's *unusually proportioned*. The jacket says one more door raised and not yet opened. That is the door. It's been standing in the middle of the room the whole time and she's never once looked at it, and I'm now going to look at nothing else.

Also noting for the file: she flies home for Thanksgiving. The mother is finally about to be in a room. And he feeds Sheri, who is alone, and Randi has no Thanksgiving at all in this chapter — Randi who was lying on his couch two nights ago saying *some nights it frightens me.* Somebody is going to spend that holiday badly and I don't think it's Vee.
