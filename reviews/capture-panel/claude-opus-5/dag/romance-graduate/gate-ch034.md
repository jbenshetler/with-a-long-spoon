REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where nobody touches her and I'm the one who got hit.

*Do you love him.* Flat, book open on her chest, Cassie not even making it a question — and Vee, who has not shut up since the door, goes small and says *maybe.* And then lies in the dark with the answer sitting right there, easy to reach, and no idea in the world why her mouth wouldn't hand it over. I love that the book didn't explain it to me. It just let her not know. Because I know: she said *maybe* because the real answer requires a word from him and she can't say hers into that silence, she can't be the only one holding it out in a room. She knows it in her chest in bed with him asleep (she said it to herself last week, she *said* it) and she can't say it to a girl in a bean bag chair. That's the whole book in one syllable.

And then the pool. I was braced for a metaphor and instead I got the best interior forty minutes she's had all book. Her body — this body that has been the war the entire novel, the thing somebody taught her to be ashamed of, the thing that outs her by going wet before she's decided anything, the thing he builds oyster-pale walls into a dress for — down there has *no opinions.* "It only worked." I put the book down on that. Lane three that nobody assigned her and everyone arranges themselves around. The suit that's had nowhere to go on her for years, and she looked at the water instead of at that. The questions getting into the lane with her off the third wall and then getting sanded off by the count, one word at a time, until *did he mind* is nothing at all and *do you love him* is just a pressure under the ribs with dread riding it. That's not a therapy insight, that's what swimming actually does, and I believed every stroke of it.

And then the four strokes. **Four strokes.** Whether the man she's turning over is Pace or somebody she built out of wanting him, out of two months of being looked at like that. That's the first thought in this entire book that is hers alone — not Randi's framing, not her mother's voice, not the blonde at the bar handing her a lie, not Cassie waiting her out. Her own head, in the quiet wide part, asking the actual question. And the wall came, and she rolled, and it was gone. I wanted to grab her by the cap. Of course she doesn't get to keep it; she's never once in six weeks had a thought about him she didn't immediately route to somebody else, and the one time it arrives in a place where there's no one to hand it to, it lasts four strokes and a flip turn. God.

Cassie. Cassie is the only person in this novel keeping count. *You don't come home school nights* — not a question, and Vee had to go back over the week herself to find out her own bed was empty more nights than not. The glass has a third pane now. And *there's only one way that burn happens — not enough on*, dry as toast, no judgment, no follow-up, she just drops it and lets Vee bowl past it. She knows. She's known for weeks. She asks one question a chapter and it's always the one with the hook in it, and she lets the *maybe* stand and says go to sleep, streetlight, and goes back to her book. I'm going to be destroyed when she finds out she's been getting the brochure.

What I notice is who wasn't here. No Randi for a whole chapter, after the brunch — after *sticky fingers* and the glaze on her lip from a pastry she never touched, which I am still not over, which is the single most frightening thing in two books. And the absence has weight: Vee's in the lane alone with the two questions and neither of them is *what would Randi think*, and that's only because Randi isn't in the room. One quiet chapter after that brunch is a mercy, honestly. My pulse needed it.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she asked herself if she invented him and it lasted four strokes and then she went and thought about a sandwich.* Also: nobody in this book has said the word out loud yet and now THREE of them are carrying it and the only one who's said it to another human being said it face-down on his chest about *her.*

Craft note to myself: zero stats lectures this chapter, thank you. The swim did the thinking and the swim is hers since she was twelve, so it's earned, not imported.
