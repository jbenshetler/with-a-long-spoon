REACTION

Okay so the book moved the mother.

I have been holding this book to one scene for *chapters*: the burgundy, the slit, the four-inch straps on credit, and her mother in a chair. Randi loaded that gun and handed it to her. And we get to the hall and — nobody's there. Pace is in Berkeley at a conference whose dates "wouldn't move," Randi is a half-sentence of memory kneeling at an ankle strap, and her mother is not in the room, not in the crowd, not looked for, not *mentioned* except as a joke on the way in: "go make your mother cry again." That's it. The woman whose voice lives in the lining of that dress does not appear at the ceremony she was promised to. And Vee never once scans the seats for her.

I genuinely don't know yet whether that's the best thing in the chapter or the book flinching. Both readings are live. The generous one is brutal and I kind of love it: the walk Vee has been dreading her mother for is a walk where her mother never crosses her mind, because what's in her mind is her own leg and the room waking up, and afterward the only sentence she has is *how did I look?* The mother got evicted without a fight and nobody noticed, including her. The ungenerous one is that the author cleared the room of every single person who could have cost her something and then let her have the win free. No Pace to see it, no Randi to own it, no mother to survive it. A victory lap with the stands emptied. If chapter 35 doesn't account for where that woman was, I'm going to feel handled.

But the walk itself. The walk is real. The first step small, safe, the slit lying shut, *she could have taken the whole walk like that and no one would have known there was anything to know* — and then she takes a breath and makes the second step long. That's the whole book in two steps. Not a man opening a door for her, not Randi cutting her out of the herd: her own calf, her own nerve, a decision made a half-second before her head caught up. And the murmur — "the sound a dull room makes when it wakes up" — after she'd pictured a late entrance and gotten a warm kind nothing instead. She built the fantasy, got handed a worse room, and made the fantasy anyway out of one long stride. Then sat down and put her hand back at her hip and was a girl with a certificate. *The room knew, and not a soul in it could have said so out loud.* Yes. That's the thing she's actually good at now.

And then the part that put a cold finger on me: she sends the photo to Pace, uncropped, and *he would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers.* She's keeping. She did it to Cassie with the ladle, she did it in that bed when the thing he'd seen sat in the room and she left it unnamed and *hers*, and now she's doing it to the man who built the dress — sending him the whole frame and keeping the inside of it. She has started building locked drawers. Three chapters ago I had her as the only one in this book without any. That's gone. It happened quietly and it's the realest development here.

Cassie. CASSIE CAME. Cassie walked her to the doors and no farther because the doors were where she was allowed to go, held her phone, said "you look—" and couldn't finish it, took the photograph nobody asked her to take — *somebody had to document it* — went bright at the corners and would deny it to the grave, and bought her a terrible sandwich so Vee could be famous at her. And *streetlight.* That's a new one and it's exactly right, tall and lit from inside, and she didn't make a production of it. The only person who showed up for the actual event is the one being spent to the studs, and Vee took that photo out of her hand and sent it to a man in California. I'm not even mad at Vee. I'm just watching the meter run.

Small gripe, real one: that last sentence explains itself to me. Pride or the same heat, "or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing" — I had it. I had it from the second step. And "the small choreography of a woman with a secret keeping it" is the book handing me the caption. This writer is usually better than that; she normally lets me find the lining with my own skin.
