REACTION

Oh, this book is *mean*. In the way I've been begging for.

So chapter one ends with "There you are, Randi" and I sat up, because the jacket told me Randi is the glamorous friend who steers Vee into Pace's path — and here I am watching the steering get invented on a Tuesday afternoon on a pretty quad with a maple on fire. I'm ahead of them. The jacket literally promised me I'd be ahead and I thought that was marketing. It isn't. It's the whole engine. I know what Vee is walking toward and Vee is currently laughing with her head thrown back outside a dining hall, freckles, wind in her hair, no idea she's been *pointed at*.

Let me talk about the sex, because the sex is doing work I've almost never seen sex do. He comes up over her while she's still coming, still open and pulsing and undefended, and *then* he slides in, and *then* he kisses her with her own taste in his mouth, and *then* — while she's soft and can't lie — he asks the question. And her body answers before she does. She comes on the question. He notes, calmly, in his own head: *He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did.*

I want to text a friend at 1 a.m. about that sentence. Because it's hot as hell and it's also the book handing me the knife. He is interrogating her. He's found the exact five seconds in which she cannot manage her own face, and he asks the thing he wants to know inside those five seconds, and then he calls the answer she couldn't stop the *true* one and himself a generous man for asking again later "when she was gathered back into herself, free to weigh it and choose." Free to choose *after* he already banked the answer he wanted. That's the most sophisticated dressed-up override I've read in years, and the book knows, and he does not. "That mattered more to him than the answer did." Sir. It very much did not.

And I'm not even mad at him, which is the terrifying part. He *is* good at this. The tenderness is real — the water bottle, the apple slices cut thin with the cut sides up, holding her foot in both hands. He's not a brooding hockey alpha barking mine. He's worse: he's attentive. He pays closer attention to her than anyone in her life ever has and then uses every single thing he learns. The care and the acquisition are the same gesture. I don't know how to be turned on and alarmed simultaneously and yet here we are.

Randi is the one I can't stop thinking about, though. After chapter two — Brooke pinning her to the floor of her own common room with nothing but a friendly voice and the refusal to stand up, then Randi lying down in last night's clothes with her shoes on under a synthetic duvet, not crying — she came into this chapter with the whole face back on, and I watched it slip four separate times. The dropped stride. The bare "Yes" with nothing around it. The little happy footwork on the brick she didn't decide to do. The grin she failed to hide. And each time she reaches for the kitten voice to put the wanting back in its box — "Only in a threesome. With you." Handing the appetite to him so it belongs to a girlfriend being generous and not to a woman who wants. *The same act. A different owner.* God.

And then the cheeseburger. "No. I can't eat that." Two paragraphs later a woman comes out of that same dining hall built on a lavish scale, laughing with her whole body, having genuinely forgotten she can be seen — and Randi says yes before she can stop herself. The book put the hunger and the answer to the hunger thirty feet apart and didn't underline it once. I made a noise.

The shoulder-check is the thing I'll still be chewing on tomorrow. Thirty feet of geometry so gradual it looks like chance, and then it's *Vee's* hand that flies to her mouth, *Vee* apologizing, stricken, for something done to her. Pace standing at a light pole being nobody, glowing about it. That's the novel in three seconds. Nothing was taken from her that she didn't hand over — including the apology.

The one place I skimmed a little: the middle of the walk, the parade. "the matched athleisure that read like a uniform," lavender and sage and the chunky trainers and the scrunchies — by the third specimen I was reading a lookbook, and the blazer girl with the blowout felt like a slot being filled rather than a person. I get that the cataloging *is* Randi's grading eye, that's the point, but it went one girl long. Then Vee walked out the doors and I forgot I'd been restless.

What I'm suspicious of: Daphne. One sentence in chapter one, a door he didn't respect, cost him more than he knew anything could cost — and he's built his whole courtly ask-first architecture on top of it. That's not a backstory, that's a loaded gun. And Randi's private decision in the bathroom — *he could have the face, she's not coming out again* — means this "gift" she's fetching him is also a maneuver. She's putting a third person between herself and being seen. She thinks she's buying cover. The jacket says it got real for the two who started it, so I know the cover fails.

Also, quietly: she liked looking at Vee more than she liked offering Vee. Nobody in this chapter said that out loud and everybody knows it.

Give me Vee. I want to be inside her head before he ever gets near her, and I want to know whether I'll be able to stand it.
