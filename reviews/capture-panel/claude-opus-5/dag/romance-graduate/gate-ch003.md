REACTION

Okay so I knew this was coming — the jacket told me — and it still got me, which is annoying and also the best thing that's happened to me in a book this month. I spent the whole quad walk watching him point at girls and feeling smug, like, cute game, and then the red hair came out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bare and I went *oh no* out loud. Hello, Vee. She gets picked out of a lunch crowd like a melon. And the thing that wrecked me is she's the *nicest* one on the page — she's the one who apologizes, she's the one whose hand flies to her mouth, and Randi's hand stays on her arm a half-beat too long and that's the whole book right there. That's the invitation. That's the "polite" part. I got actual goosebumps.

Also: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Hand to god. I reread that line three times. It's monstrous and it's *flirting* and it's said into his chest while she's holding on to him, and he doesn't answer it, he says *let's go feed you*. Which — the feeding. She said "I can't eat that" about a cheeseburger without even turning her head, and he just filed it and fed her at the end instead of arguing with her about it. He's doing the apple thing again and it's the most tender he's been and it's also exactly how he handles everything, which is to say he gives you the thing you wouldn't ask for. I don't know whether to swoon or back away.

The question asked *while she's coming* — mid-thrust, mid-undefended — and her body answering before she can get a lid on it. That's the most intelligent erotic move I've read in ages. And then the recovery: "Only in a threesome. With you." She doesn't take the want back, she *relocates ownership* of it, hands it to him so she can be a generous girlfriend instead of a woman who hungers. I have known women who do this. I have *been* a woman who does this. Nobody in a KU romance has ever once done it on the page and had it clocked.

Which brings me to my one real gripe, because I was close to snapping at the book: he narrates his own read of her at length, in italics practically, and there's a stretch after "only in a threesome" where he sits there appreciating the architecture of her defense mechanism for a full paragraph. I'd already got it from the flinch and the tongue touching her lip. I don't need the subtext essay. Same with the first/second/third-beat counting at the ponytail girl — I can feel a beat land, I don't need them numbered. His POV is the engine of the book and also the thing most likely to make me put it down, and it's walking that line in daylight here. The sex, too, went a bit summary on me — "he worked the third one out of her deliberately" is a sentence that tells me a thing happened rather than putting me in it, and after chapter one's bench, which was *all* in it, I noticed the drop.

But god, the sorting of the women. The expected one, the sealed pair, the one who runs the room (strong no, real feeling in the no — she can't stand a performed ease, probably because it's hers), and then the "maybe" on the library steps, the soft one in the beanie taking up room comfortably. He's triangulating her taste and *she's* learning she's allowed to have one, and he kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth as a reward for the maybe. That's the hottest thing in the chapter and nobody's clothes came off.

What I'm sitting with: Randi locked the bathroom door two chapters ago and decided *not ever*, and she went into the closet and couldn't remember why, and lay down in her shoes and didn't cry — and now she's bouncing on brick in happy little dance feet because she gets to have a girl. She's found a way to be alive that doesn't require coming out from behind the face. Vee is the workaround. That's so much sadder and nastier than a plot, and he thinks he's giving her a gift. He has no idea what he just pointed at.

Suspicions I'm keeping: Brooke filing the name *Pace* like evidence, the "you're not in one of his sections" fishing, and that water-bottle cap under Randi's thumbnail. Somebody is going to use all three.
