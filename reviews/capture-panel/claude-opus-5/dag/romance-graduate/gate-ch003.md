REACTION

Okay so I was wrong about what book I'm reading, and I'm delighted about it.

Two chapters in I had this pegged as Randi's story — the bench, the mirror, the face, the deadbolt going over in her ribs. And now chapter three walks me out onto a quad in good light and reveals that all of that was *setup*. That the woman I've been watching get cracked open is the hand that's about to reach for someone else. The blurb told me this. I read the blurb. I still didn't feel it until she said "Her" — until *he* said "Her," and Randi's stride dropped out from under her.

The pointing game itself is the most genuinely erotic thing I've read in probably a year and there is almost nothing physical in it. It's two people walking on a path holding hands. That's it. And I was *squirming*. "I point. You pick." Four words and the whole power arrangement reorganizes and she laughs before she can decide whether to, which is the tell, that's her whole character in one clause. And the kiss on the library walk — he hasn't wiped his face since the bed, he kisses her mouth in public and waits for the moment her own taste reaches her. In *daylight*. On campus. Her breath catching and holding. I put the book down for a second there. That's not a sex scene and it landed harder than most sex scenes I've read this year.

Also: the question in bed. Asking it while she's still coming down, so the body answers before the woman can, and she comes *from the question* — and then he asks it again later, on purpose, when she's gathered and defended, so she gets the chance to dress it up. And she does. "Only in a threesome. With you." He sees exactly what she did, names it to himself — she moved the ownership, she made herself the instrument of *his* appetite instead of a woman who wanted a woman — and he *lets her have the wrapper*. He thinks that's kindness. It might be kindness. It's also how you get someone to walk somewhere they'd never go if you made them say where they were going. I don't know yet whether this book thinks he's good. I think it might think he's both, and I have not had that from a romance in a very long time.

What I'm suspicious of: Pace's head. He is so pleased. "It was the best idea he'd had in a month." "He liked, even better, the places she surprised him." Every read he takes of her is generous and mostly correct and it's all still *about how well he reads her*, and after chapter one — where she locked the bathroom door and decided, in a voice he cannot hear, never to come out again — I know something he doesn't. He's sitting there at his light pole being warm in his body while he watches her, and I'm going, my guy, she has already gone behind the face permanently and you think she's blooming. That gap is the actual engine of this book and I love that it exists. Daphne, too. Whoever Daphne is, she's coming back.

And Vee. Oh, they knew what they were doing with Vee. Every other girl on that quad is catalogued by what she's wearing and how she's graded — lavender, sage, box-fresh trainers, the Greek pastel, the grammar Randi lives in — and then a woman comes out of a dining hall with her throat bare to the sky, laughing with her head all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That's the line. That's what they're going to take from her, and the book just told me so on the page where it introduced her. Also she's the one who apologizes. She's the one whose hand flies to her mouth. Randi's shoulder-check is three seconds of surgery that looks like nothing and *Vee says sorry for it*. Bleak and beautiful.

And then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I made a sound. I actually made a sound. She's not asking if Vee will like *her*, or like Pace. She's asking about the thing she was crying facedown on four days ago, the thing she has refused to look at since — and she wants to put a stranger she's met for two minutes on it. That's not generosity and it isn't even lust, quite. It's *let it happen to someone else so I don't have to be the only one it happened to.* Or: I want to be on the other side of it. Either way. Chilling. Perfect chapter ending.

The only place I drifted was the middle third — the maples and the colonnade and the two-centuries-ago composition of the eye's travel, and then four candidates in a row each with a full outfit inventory. I get that the catalogue *is* the point, that we're being made to look the way Randi looks. But around the rec-center pair I noticed I was reading fast, waiting for him to point again. And it's still all his read of her — I got Randi's interior in chapter one and it gutted me, and here I'm outside her the whole time, being told what she feels by the man feeling pleased about it. Effective. Also I want back in her head.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: THE BOOK IS NOT ABOUT THE GIRL ON THE COVER YET. Also: they picked her off a quad like produce and she said sorry for the collision. Read faster.
