REACTION

Okay. "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I had to put the book down and sit with that for a second. Not because it shocked me — the jacket told me there was a plan, I've been reading two chapters knowing a trap was being built — but because of *who said it*. Randi. Forty-eight hours after she locked herself in his bathroom and decided never to come out again. She found the exit. That's the exit. You don't have to be the one seen if there's someone else on the table.

And that's what's got me, because Pace doesn't know. He thinks he's handing her a gift. He thinks he's being generous, and patient, and careful — all the chin-tipping, *I point, you pick*, let her choose, don't make her name it. He is being all of those things! He's genuinely tender with her, the slow kiss for no reason, the *let's go feed you* at the end after the cheeseburger she can't eat. And the whole time he's reading the wrong woman. He thinks the hunger he found on the bench is hunger for being fed. It's hunger for cover. I watched him sit outside that locked door and respect it, and I watched him be completely, catastrophically wrong about what was happening behind it, and that one mistake is the only reason I can stand a man who's this good at reading people. Without it he'd be unbearable. With it he's the most interesting man I've read in a year.

The thing that actually chilled me: the shoulder bump. Thirty feet of geometry arranged so gradually it looked like chance, and then *the redhead's hand flew to her own mouth.* Vee apologized. The girl being hunted apologized for being in the way. If this book has a thesis that's it, and nobody said it out loud, it just happened in three seconds on a brick walk. I actually made a noise.

Also — asking her the question while she's mid-orgasm and undefended, having *decided that beforehand*. That's the bench again, isn't it. Same technique, different room: get her past the place where she can lie, take the true answer, then give her a graceful way to dress it up afterward. And she dresses it up brilliantly — *only in a threesome, with you* — handing him the appetite so she's the generous instrument instead of a woman who wants a woman. I believed that move completely. It's exactly the girl from the mirror.

Vee herself: three paragraphs and I'm already protective of her. Head thrown all the way back, throat bare, laughing like someone who forgot she could be seen. That's the detail that did it, because *being seen* is the whole currency of this book, and she's the only person in it who isn't managing it.

Two gripes, honestly. The parade of candidate women ran one woman too long — I started skimming the athleisure around the sage-and-lavender pair, I'd gotten the game by then. And every so often the book leans over and tells me what it just did: *the same act, a different owner*, *the wrapper the wanting needed to come out in*. I got there on my own, I don't need the gloss. Trust me a little more.

But I am not putting this down. I want Vee's head. I want to know what she thought a nice senior girl from stats class wanted with her.
