REACTION

Oh, that's the trap closing. That's the whole blurb snapping into focus and I didn't expect it to happen this fast — I thought the "game" between them would be a thing I pieced together over a hundred pages, and instead I got to stand at the light pole with him and watch them pick her.

The thing I'd be texting at 1 a.m.: Randi chose Vee because Vee was laughing with her head thrown all the way back, throat bare, *having forgotten she could be seen*. Forty-eight hours after Randi stood in a locked bathroom and decided she was never coming out from behind her face again. She said no to the girl who could have been her sister — "too expected" — and no, hard, with real feeling, to the one whose ease was performed and aimed outward, which is the most self-aware thing Randi has done in three chapters without knowing she was doing it. She is shopping for the thing she just buried. That's not a plot beat, that's a diagnosis, and the book never once stops to tell me so. Thank God. That restraint is the best writing in the chapter.

And it had the nerve to put the *one* uncostumed syllable she has left in her in that moment. "Yes," with nothing around it. She has routed her entire interior life through a performance now — the kitten voice, the "only in a threesome, with you," handing the appetite to him so she can be its generous instrument instead of its owner — and the only thing that got out raw all afternoon was the word she said looking at a redhead by the dining hall doors. Her body keeps telling the truth and her mouth keeps filing an amended version. The clench when he asked the question in bed, before she'd even processed it — that worked on me in the way this book keeps working on me, which is that it's hot *because* of what it costs her.

He's so pleased with himself. That's the part that's starting to get frightening instead of swoony. In chapter one I was more than halfway in love with him — the apple slices, "tell me again if you forget," the closed door he wouldn't put a hand on. And he's still that man. He waited for the afternoon to go ordinary before he asked for real, because he wanted her gathered and free to choose. He noticed her flinch and respected the costume she built over it. He's *kind*. And he is sitting in his own head going "it was the best idea he'd had in a month" while the woman curled against him is a sealed vault he thinks he opened. He flinch-read her and still read her wrong, because the thing she decided in that bathroom is the one fact about her he does not have. Every tender thing he does from here is going to land on a face. And he doesn't know. He's running a seduction on someone who already surrendered and withdrew, and he thinks the gift is moving toward her hands. There's a Daphne in his past and a door he didn't respect, and now he's overcorrected into a politeness so perfect it's blind.

Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I said something out loud. That's a closing line. Vee isn't even a person to her yet, she's a slot on a piece of furniture, and I know from the back cover that Vee is the one I'm going to spend this book inside of — that she walks toward Pace because everything in her has been waiting to, that she tells Randi everything over lunches too intimate to name. So now I've watched the shoulder-bump get engineered. A decade of dance folded into three seconds so clean the *victim* apologized. And I've got "we've got a stats class together" sitting in my stomach like a stone.

What I didn't love: the first half talks too much. Pace's head does a lot of explaining — "the same act. A different owner," "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy." I'd already *felt* her do it; I didn't need the glossary. Chapters one and two trusted me more, and this one occasionally flipped into its own footnotes. The sex also went by in summary where chapter one was in the room with me — "he worked the third one out of her deliberately" is a sentence about a sex scene rather than a sex scene. And the parade of candidate women ran one girl long; by the blazer and the blowout I was ready for the path to deliver.

But the game itself — "I point. You pick," and watching her learn she was allowed to want — is the most genuinely erotic thing I've read in months and nobody took their clothes off for it. Give me chapter four. Give me Vee, and let me be the one who already knows.
