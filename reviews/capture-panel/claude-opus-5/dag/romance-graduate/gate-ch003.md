REACTION

Okay so the jacket told me the plan started in bed and I nodded along like yeah yeah, premise, and then this chapter actually *showed* me it and it was not what I thought. I assumed "Randi and Pace have a plan" meant two predators with a whiteboard. It's so much worse and so much better: the plan is a dodge. He asks her a question mid-fuck and her body answers before she does — she comes *on the question* — and then the second he asks it again with the heat gone and her gathered back up, she instantly repackages it as a gift. "Only in a threesome. With you." I actually said *oh* out loud. That's the same move as the locked bathroom door in chapter one. She can't be a woman who wants a woman, so she becomes a girlfriend being generous. And he sees it, names it to himself as beautiful, and lets her keep the costume. That's the most emotionally intelligent thing I've read in a romance in two years, and it happened in about four sentences without anyone explaining it to me.

And then the walk. God, the walk. I was braced for a cringe — man points at women on a quad, what is this — and instead it's the cleanest piece of character work in the book so far, because the *rules* are the point. "I point. You pick." He hands her the choosing and she doesn't even notice she's been handed anything until she's already shopping. She tells him she's being a good sport about his little game while she's scanning ahead of his hand. The coil he reads in her, the follow leaning into a turn that hasn't been called — I know that feeling from the inside and I didn't expect a book to get it this exactly. She's not humoring him. She's starving and she found a cafeteria.

And the taxonomy of nos is doing so much work: too expected, those two go everywhere together, *God, no* with real feeling at the room-running blonde whose ease is performed. Of course that's her hard no. That one's a mirror and she can't stand it. The maple she didn't plan, the burger she can't eat, the apple from chapter one — this book keeps feeding her and she keeps declining.

Then Vee comes out the doors laughing with her throat bare and her whole body in it, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen," and I got cold. Because that *is* the thing Randi lost on the bench and locked away in a bathroom three days ago. She's not picking a woman she wants to kiss. She's pointing at the thing she can't be anymore. And then she drops her stride mid-step and says "Yes" with nothing around it and *doesn't look away* — the one woman all afternoon her eyes won't release. Her first unmanaged word since the bench.

The collision is a gorgeous bit of ugliness. Thirty feet of geometry, half a head of weight, and the redhead's own hand flies to her own mouth to apologize for being walked into. Randi engineered an apology out of the person she's hunting and then put a warm hand on her arm and held it half a beat too long. I was thrilled and horrified in the same breath, which I think is the deal this book is offering me.

And then the little happy footwork on the brick. The squeaked *yes*. The second *yes* in her bed voice in daylight. She is so happy and she has no idea what she's doing, and that's the bit that scares me, because he doesn't either — he thinks it's the best idea he's had in a month, he thinks he's giving her a joy. Nobody in this scene is being cruel. That's the problem. "Every yes was freely given."

"Do you think she'll like the bench?" — I put the book down for a second. Vee hasn't said eleven words yet and she's already been sized for the furniture.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: this man is dangerous specifically because he's kind, and the woman is dangerous specifically because she's drowning, and the girl with the copper hair just said "oh I'm so sorry" to the person who aimed herself at her. Also I want Vee's POV so badly my teeth hurt — three chapters of Pace's camera is starting to feel like a choice I'm going to have opinions about, and if chapter four doesn't open inside her head I'll be a little impatient.
