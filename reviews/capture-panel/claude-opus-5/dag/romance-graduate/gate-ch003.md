REACTION

Okay so I'm sitting up now. Because I came into this book being told on the jacket that a plan exists and Vee doesn't know about it, and I assumed — from hundreds of books' worth of training — that I'd get the plan revealed in a smug little flashback around 60%. Instead I just *watched it get invented*, in real time, on a walk, on a beautiful autumn day, by two people who are being sweet to each other. He points, she picks. That's it, that's the whole mechanism, and it's a *game*, and it's *fun*, and it made my stomach go cold while my whole chest was warm. I have not had that particular sensation from a romance in a very long time.

The sex at the front — coming up over her while she's still going, kissing her with her own taste still on him so she has to taste herself, and then sliding the *question* in while she's undefended, and her body answering by coming, immediately, helplessly, before she can manage what it means. That's the most genuinely erotic thing I've read this year and it's also, if I'm honest, an *interrogation technique*. He says it himself: "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That line is a *flare*. Because the reader just got a whole chapter of Randi in a locked bathroom deciding never to be seen again, and here he is congratulating himself on reading her truly. He's so good at her and so *wrong* about her, and he doesn't know, and I do, and that gap is the engine and it is *working on me*.

And then she does it again — the move. "Only in a threesome. With you." He clocks it exactly right: she didn't take the wanting back, she *moved the ownership*. Not a woman who hungers for a woman (unsurvivable), a girlfriend giving her man a present (merely sexy). Same act, different owner. That is the exact same maneuver as the face in the mirror and he *admires* it instead of being alarmed by it, and honestly so do I, that's the problem. She's going to get everything she wants by never once being the one who wanted it. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she's outsourcing her desire to a girl she hasn't met yet and he thinks he's doing wellness work.*

The pointing game itself — god. Grading women on a quad like fruit. Too expected. Those two go everywhere together, you'd get both or neither. "God, no," fast, with real feeling, at the one who performs her ease *at* people, because Randi knows that girl, Randi *is* that girl. She's not choosing a woman, she's choosing an *opposite*. And then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bare, having forgotten she can be seen, and Randi's stride drops out from under her and she doesn't look away and says "Yes" with nothing around it. Of course. Of *course* the one she wants is the one who's never learned to hide, because that's the thing Randi has just permanently locked in a bathroom. She's about to eat someone for the crime of being unguarded. "I can't eat that" about the cheeseburger, eighty lines earlier. I see you, book.

The shoulder-check. THE SHOULDER-CHECK. Thirty feet of geometry so she can arrive as the person being apologized to, and it's Vee's hand that flies to her own mouth. That's the single most chilling three seconds so far and it's described like a dance move, admiringly, by a man leaning on a light pole with a warm feeling in his chest. I'm not okay.

And the last line. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Said low, into his chest, happy. He answers "let's go feed you," which is tender, which is the point — he's always feeding somebody. Book, you absolute snake.

Two nags, small: the quad-in-autumn paragraph is the one place my eye slid — the burning maple got two mentions it didn't need, and I don't need the two-centuries-of-composed-sightlines. And I am now three chapters in without one line inside Vee's head, and she's the name on the jacket. I'm not annoyed yet — I think the withholding is deliberate, I think I'm supposed to be ahead of her and dreading — but chapter four had better let me in, because right now Vee is a copper head at the edge of a lawn and I already feel protective of her, which is presumably exactly where I'm meant to be standing.
