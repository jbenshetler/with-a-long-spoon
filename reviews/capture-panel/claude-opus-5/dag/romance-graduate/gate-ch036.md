REACTION

Oh, Cassie. Two pages and she's the only person in this book operating with a full deck of nothing — no folder, no plan, no script — and she's still the one who put a thumb right on the bruise.

"You don't come home school nights." Not a question, like everything she says that matters isn't a question. And Vee *went back and counted* and found her own bed had sat empty more nights than it hadn't, and had genuinely not known. That's the scariest line in the chapter and it's bookkeeping. Six weeks ago this girl paid her own way on a debit card and made her bed in a dorm room; now she has a drawer in a man's bathroom, a credit card with nothing behind it, another woman's shoes under her hem, and no idea how many nights she's slept in her own room. Cassie's the only ledger in this book that isn't being cooked.

And the burn. She touched the side of her breast while she told it without knowing her hand had moved — God, that's good, that's the whole book in one gesture, the body reporting before the mouth decides — and Cassie, deadpan: *there's only one way that burn happens. Not enough on.* She clocked it, she said it dry, and then she let Vee sail right past it. Cassie knows. Cassie has known for a while. She just won't make Vee say it, which is exactly why Vee can't tell her anything, and that is the most painful decent thing in the novel. The one person who would hold it without charging interest is the one person getting the edited version.

But here's what I'm turning over at 1 a.m., and it's the inversion I didn't see coming: with Randi, Vee hands over her body in forensic detail and keeps the heart back. With Cassie she hands over the heart and keeps the body back. She told Randi *he checked* across a tablecloth in the daylight. She told Cassie about tomatoes. And then Cassie asked the real one — *do you love him* — in that flat voice, and Vee, who has not shut up since the door, went *small.* **"Maybe. I think so."** After chapter 35, where she lay in the dark and set the word down plainly in her own chest and said *Peter* into his ear. So she just lied to the honest one. Or — worse, truer — the word only exists in that house, and out here under a cracked window in the cold it shrinks back to maybe. And "it's all so fast," which is her own voice finally saying something nobody coached her to say.

And Cassie *let the maybe stand.* Didn't argue, didn't say *I know.* "I don't get it," kindly. That's a woman refusing to pretend to a wisdom she doesn't have, three chapters after a woman who pretends to exactly that for a living knelt on a bathroom floor in Vee's head. The contrast is doing all the work and the book said none of it out loud. Thank you.

Also: she reached for a word for him and didn't have one and *laughed at herself reaching.* She can't name him either. Everybody in this thing is short a word and nobody will hand one over.

Second quiet one in a row depending how you count 35 — but I'm not restless. This was a held breath with teeth in it. What I want now is Cassie with a number: not "you don't come home school nights" but "where's the money coming from," or "who bought the shoes."
