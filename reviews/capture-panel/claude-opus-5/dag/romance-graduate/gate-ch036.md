REACTION

Oh, she *brought the dress.* That's the thing. She hung it in its bag Wednesday night with the stage still on her skin and then Friday she took it off the rail and laid it across the back seat on her way out to him — no toothbrush, no change of clothes, no bag, because packing one would still mean admitting the plan, but the dress came. Fifty feet of frozen gravel from the man whose hands made it. I got a little wrecked by that, honestly. She crossed a stage in his work for a room of strangers' parents and he was in a hotel in Berkeley, and the only repair available to her was to give him the performance back, privately, in his own lamplight, with the heels he's never seen under it. She's not apologizing for anything this time. She's *returning* something. That's a new verb for her.

And he sat down. That's the other thing. He started up out of the recliner and she put one flat hand out and he *sat back down*, and the wine stayed where he'd set it, and she watched him make himself do it. Every single time she's crossed a threshold in this book it's been his procedure holding the door — may I, would you be bothered, we find out. This time she set the room, she assigned him a chair, she told him he didn't get to guess and didn't get to help, and the man who has never once not been in charge of the pacing sat in a chair for ten minutes and let a twenty-one-year-old run him. His hand came partway up and he put it away again *and she could see what it cost him.* That's the most erotic thing in the chapter and nobody took a stitch off for it.

Which — look. Ten steps down a hallway in four-inch heels and nothing else is not a new image in the history of the genre. I have read that walk a hundred times. What I haven't read is the part where the walk is the *second* costume, offered as a comparison, with an actual question attached that she needs an answer to. The whole thing is built as a question: which do you like better. Because what she's really asking is whether the thing she gave the room or the thing she keeps for him is the one he wants, and he answered correctly and then answered *better* — *the other is for the world, this is just for me.* I did not love "sunrise and sunset," that's him being a jacket-copy for one line, but the recovery was so clean I forgave it inside a sentence.

Small things I'm keeping: her face pink "down to where the mirror gave out," and the pink not stopping there. Hanging the dress *properly*, seams squared on the hanger, because somebody's hands are in it — she's careful with his labor now in a way she wasn't careful with anything two weeks ago. His boots, too big by half, clomping out to the car in a borrowed man's boots to fetch the thing he made to her. And the heels having lived by his bedroom wall since Friday "after, and only after, her bet was paid," which is the most offhand way to carry last chapter's whole surrender forward — she doesn't have to say a word about it, the shoes say it.

What I'm uneasy about: Randi isn't in this chapter at all and the dress she's wearing and the heels she's wearing are both Randi's doing. Randi picked them, Randi knelt and buckled them, and now they're the costume Vee gives Pace in the dark while Randi's across campus being useful to people she doesn't like. Vee thinks she's giving him something nobody else has touched. Every object in that lamplight has Randi's fingerprints on it. And the man in the chair knows it and is going to sit there and take the gift anyway. That's the third game humming under the floorboards and it made the sweetness of the chapter go a little cold at the edges, in the way I want it to.

Also I notice this is the second straight chapter where she does something in his house she has never done in her life, and the escalation ladder is starting to show me its rungs. One more "she had never in her life—" and I'll start counting. But not yet. Tonight she picked the costume and he sat where she put him.
