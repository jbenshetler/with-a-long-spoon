REACTION

Two pages and the whole thing is a knife handed over handle-first.

"You're doing the streetlight thing again. You're a public utility." Cassie gets maybe forty words in this chapter and she wins it. She has *a name for this now*. Not a complaint — an observation she's made often enough to have shorthand for. Which means it's been happening long enough to be a pattern Cassie has sat in the bean bag and watched develop and privately titled. That's a girl keeping a log she never shows anybody.

And then the burn. Vee's hand going to the side of her breast *without her knowing it moved* — God, that's good, that's the best three inches of the chapter — and Cassie, flat as a ruler: "There's only one way *that* burn happens. Not enough on." Cassie knows Vee was topless in that kitchen. Cassie says the funny version instead of the true version, hands it back light, and Vee doesn't even brake, she's already onto how he fussed over it for days. The whole chapter is Cassie reading her roommate off her skin and Vee not hearing one syllable of it. That's the same deafness as the dorm room and the closed window and the cab fare, except now the thing she's not hearing is *affection*, not suspicion, which is worse.

"You don't come home school nights." Not a question, no accusation, and Vee has to go back and *count the week she hadn't counted.* She didn't know. That's the actual horror of the chapter — not that she's gone, that she's gone and has no idea how gone. And "I don't get it" — kindly — "You've had boyfriends. You never went like this over one." The one person in the book positioned to notice the difference between then and now says so and gets *fine, they were fine* and a girl laughing at herself for not having the word.

Then: "Do you love him." Flat, same voice as the rest of it. **Cassie asks it.** Not Randi. Randi, who has had this girl's whole interior on a plate across six brunches, has never once asked that question, because Randi doesn't need the answer and wouldn't like it. Cassie asks in four words in a dark dorm room and gets *"Maybe. I think so."* — and Vee, who said *Peter* into a sleeping man's ear last chapter, cannot get the word out to her best friend at eleven at night. The name she can say. The word she can't. And then **"Cassie let the maybe stand. She didn't say I know."** That's the whole book in one line. Cassie knows. Cassie has known since the dress, since the highlighter, since the *was it a good night — oh, yes.* And she decides, again, tonight, not to make it hard for Vee. She just goes back to her book. *Some of us have class.*

She's still cracking the window. I checked. I will keep checking.

What it cost me: I've now had the cooking inventory maybe six times — the intent, the never out of a box, the no-name spices — and when it comes to me secondhand through a summary of several weeks it goes thin. I don't need another salmon. And I notice I wanted Cassie's side of this and got Cassie's side only as Vee's blind spot. I'm not sure the book intends to ever open that door, and this chapter is the strongest argument yet that it should.

Two pages, though. I don't have a habit for that. It didn't feel like a shortcut — it felt like the whole thing was one question and the author wasn't going to pad the walk up to it. I'd text somebody *she didn't say I know* and nothing else.
