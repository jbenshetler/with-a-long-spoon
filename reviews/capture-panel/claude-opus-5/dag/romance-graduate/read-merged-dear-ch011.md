Okay. This is the chapter I've been complaining didn't exist.

Nine chapters of being *told* Randi is charming and here she finally opens her mouth and I get it instantly. The lacrosse date. "I could have sewn my own mouth shut and he would not have clocked that I'd gone quiet." And then — *and then* — "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She says it like a girlfriend being funny over smoothies and it is the entire thesis of this book, pre-loaded into Vee so that when Vee opens her mouth about Pace she will hear herself describing exactly the thing Randi just defined as the good kind. She told her catastrophe *first*. She told a story about a man who doesn't listen before asking for a story about a man who does. That's not conversation, that's a lede. And she's so good at it that it reads, if you squint, like generosity — like she's going first to make room.

And I got a different thing I didn't know I wanted: Randi running Pace's move. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Then — "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Vee has the dignified line sitting right there, *I made an exception*, and Randi takes it off her and makes her say the smaller true one instead. Same mechanism as the last inch under the tree. Vee has to cross it herself. And then Randi *stops*. Goes no further. "Warm, and let her have it." That's the edge. Both of them do this to her and neither of them ever pushes, and the not-pushing is the hook, and Vee experiences every one of these withdrawals as a kindness. "Vee felt the letting-go as one more kindness in a day that had been full of them." I had to put the book down on my chest for a second.

But here's what actually got me, and it's the thing I'll be chewing on: "Ask and then ravish," a beat late and low, *the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume.* Randi slipped. The whole chapter she's been pitched up in performance register and for one line she drops into the real one, because Vee said *he'd ask, though* with her hands going down her thighs, and Randi is not on the trail with Vee's mountain man, Randi is on a bench, and she knows precisely what the asking does. That's the hottest thing in a chapter where nobody touches anybody but a hand on a table. The want for Vee keeps getting bigger and it keeps leaking out through timing rather than words, and the book trusts me to catch it, which is why I'm still here.

Brooke. BROOKE. She arrives and the silent tall one does a slow circuit of Vee like she's reading a price tag, and hands back "nothing but more smile." I don't know yet whether she's muscle or just scenery with eyes but she *appraised* her. And "We missed you Thursday" — one sentence that puts a whole life on the table that Vee has never been invited into, and Vee immediately goes "very slightly the wrong size for the room," which is such an exact description of that feeling that I felt it in my shoulders.

And then — my notes literally say *Brooke didn't stand up*, about Randi. And in this room, Randi stands up. "Vee's my dear friend," plus the hand, cool and damp from the cup, in plain view, in no hurry. The chapter is called *Dear.* Is it true? One sentence I'd cut: "two small skies where her eyes should have been." That's the book flexing. It doesn't need to; "the sun was on that side. Vee had been fine for two years. This was not fine. This had color in it" is better and plainer and that one's going in my notes.
