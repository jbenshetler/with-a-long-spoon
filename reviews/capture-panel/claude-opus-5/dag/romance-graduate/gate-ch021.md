REACTION

Oh, that zipper. That's the whole chapter and I'd have paid for it alone.

Because this is the third or fourth time this book has done the "Vee edits the story for Cassie" move, and I was starting to feel it as a pattern — and then it stops being a *telling* problem and becomes a *body* problem. She can lift the middle out of the soup and the surface closes over with no hole, fine, she's good at that now, she's practiced. But she can't undress. She has given the green satin away and her own skin is now the unedited version, and she zips back up rather than stand three feet from her best friend with nothing left to say. *It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say.* I actually put my hand over my mouth. She has made herself unable to be naked in her own room. The trade cost something and she paid it on a Sunday morning in her dorm and the book let her figure it out while reaching for a towel.

And "I forgot my." Unfinished, gestured at nothing. She has never once been a bad liar until now and the one place she fumbles is in front of Cassie.

Then Cassie: *You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend.* Said kindly, certainly, wrong — and the book told me it was wrong in three adverbs and did not explain which part. That's the good version of not explaining subtext to me. Boyfriend. She said boyfriend. Nobody in this book has said that word about Pace and Cassie just put it down on the desk like it's obvious, and I sat there thinking: is he? What would Randi say to that word. And then *don't wait up, lover* and the cleats and the door, and she's gone to a game nobody's going to, and that half-second on her face at the very start — *something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name* — is the thing I'm going to be chewing on all week. Because it's the same construction the book uses for Randi's tight smile and the thing that crossed Pace at *parents*. Cassie has an unnamed thing now too. She's stopped being the clean one. Either she's lonely, or she's starting to know.

The happiness is what's frightening, honestly. *She had never in her life been so happy* as a closing line, standing in an emptied cold room alone with her skirt off and nothing under it and a smile she can't get down — I know exactly what that sentence is doing there and I let it work anyway. No brush in the house that would go through her hair. She laughed in his mirror. She couldn't pack a bag because packing a bag would have meant saying it to herself, and that detail is so precisely how a woman like this works that I forgave the chapter for being short.

It is short, though, and it's a bridge, and it's the second quiet-ish thing in a row after the fra diavolo wind-down — but this one earned its keep in one gesture, so I'm not counting it against the book. What I want now is the burgundy silk and Randi's face when she hears the word *dress*. And I want to know what Cassie's face was doing.
