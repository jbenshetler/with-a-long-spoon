REACTION

I had to put the Kindle face-down on my chest for a second, which is a thing I did maybe four times in two hundred books, and three of them were this series.

So the door I've been banging on for fifteen chapters finally opened and I am *inside his head* — and the first thing he does in there is strip a bed. That's the chapter. That's the whole thing. White sheets are his and Randi's, and he carries them to the washer and *pushes them down out of sight* and closes the lid, and only then goes back for the green, because the green are Vee's, her color and no one else's. He is doing laundry as moral administration and the book just lets it sit there like a household chore. And then Randi lies down on the green on purpose. She doesn't wait on the white. She goes and puts her bare body in the other girl's place, on the other girl's cloth, *so that he will punish her for it* — and he reads it in four seconds and gives her exactly that, and then at the end of it all he puts the green in the machine too. Speed Wash. The book rhymes a woman with a bedsheet and does not once lean over and tell me it's doing it, and I could have cried.

And then: **"She's perfect. For your first woman."** I made a noise. He said it *to make her come.* He reached into the thing Randi can't say and the thing Vee can't say and the thing neither of them knows he knows, and he pulled out one sentence that is half promise and half scalpel, and used it like a hand. He has never lied to anybody in this book — she asked *tell me she's better than me* and he wouldn't answer it, because he doesn't lie, he just *arranges* — and so instead of a lie he spent a true sentence that nobody on earth has permission to say yet. He made a plan on Vee's body with Vee not in the room. He's laying track. He was laying track in January with *do you want to sleep with a woman* and the name stepped around like a puddle, and now I've watched him do it from the inside and he never once, not for a flicker, considers telling her. There's no guilt in his POV. There's a clock.

The clock! He spends half an hour catching a clock over her shoulder, easing a woman down from a crying fit with his mind on a highway, and the hour was *never real*. Randi invented it. And when he finds out — "he turned it over once and set it down, because she was warm on him and the fear had nowhere left to live." THAT is the man. Not cruel. *Incurious, when it suits him.* He'll read a stranger's barbell for an uncollared plate and raise hell about it, and he'll be handed proof that he's being managed by the woman in his bed and set it down like a cup. I've spent three books moved by his attention. This is the first time I've seen where he switches it off.

Randi broke my heart in half and I'm furious at her. She came in loud and calendar-posed and cocky — *just put it in me and we'll both be happy* — and she left that bed sobbing into his neck with her hands finally still, clenching around him in the crying without knowing she was doing it, and the book says it straight: it was never about the clamps, those were only the door she went through to get there. She came to be hurt because she needed to be *in Vee's place* and needed to be made to pay for being there, and the only thing he had to give her afterward was his weight. She grieved in that bed in January with her own hand on her own breast as a stand-in. Now she's grieving with a chain between her teeth. She gave the safeword "almost bored, like a thing she had no intention of needing," and then she lay there and needed everything.

And the phone call. God. The phone call. Vee's voice going soft with love — *Randi was so good to me, drove me home, got me out of my fancy clothes, put me to bed, left me water, I don't know what I'd do without her* — and Randi is under the quilt at that exact second taking herself off him with her mouth. Not savoring it. *Erasing* it. Wiping her own night off him trace by trace to hand him back clean for the other girl, putting herself *underneath* her, and he gets harder from it, and the book gives me the sentence and then lets me sit in it. That is the most upsetting thing I have read in this genre and I mean that as the highest compliment available. I would text a friend at 1 a.m. with just *she cleaned herself off him while Vee said thank you* and nothing else.

Also she got her drunk. Four mimosas and a ride home and a glass of water on the nightstand and an afternoon cleared — a kindness and a theft performed in one motion, the way every single thing she does is both, and she could not separate them for you under oath. And *"I have secrets of my own,"* with the grin. She's enjoying standing in the middle. And Vee told her the games. The space princess, the chase, the interrogation, pink to her ears over brunch — so the thing Vee built in that booth in February, the one place she had, she's already handed over, and it came back to her as an afternoon she slept through.

Two things I'm chewing a hole in. One: he buckled microsuede around her wrists and the book did not mention the bracelet. The one that has no clasp. The one that requires tools. It was naked-and-every-toenail-described in that paragraph and the bracelet isn't in it, and either Helen Rivers blinked or she is being very, very quiet, and I have an awful new candidate for who owns the screwdriver. Two: he thought *a girl he cherished.* Cherished. Still not the word. Four letters, still in the drawer, and now I've been inside his head and the drawer stayed shut even in there.

Heat-wise this was the most technically assured sex in three volumes — the whole descending ladder of the spanking heading for the one place left and then the *swerve*, coming around to her face instead, and taking the clamps off so the rebound hits at the exact instant his hand lands between her legs for the first time. That's not a recited scene. Somebody knows what they're doing and knows that timing *is* the eroticism. The sound of the taps going from crack to wet. Fine, yes, I'm a person, that got me.

Closest I came to annoyance: "she hadn't come to be serviced. She'd come to be made to pay for it." I don't need the footnote — except reading people is literally his one skill, so it's him, not the author's elbow in my ribs. I'll allow it. Barely.

Vee is driving out there this afternoon with a surprise and an apology, onto sheets that were in a machine an hour ago, and she doesn't know she's the only one in that house who's ever told the truth badly.
