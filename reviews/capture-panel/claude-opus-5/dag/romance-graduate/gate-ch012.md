REACTION

Okay so the thing that made this chapel-of-nice-girls chapter work on me is the edit. Sunday night Vee told Cassie the truth — *I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me.* Thursday at the gold table she tells it again to Meg and Kayla and the shirt comes off the story. It's the rain, the dry flannel, him standing out there with his back turned, *I kept it* — and then Meg goes "and then he kissed you" and Vee says *No* and hands them a whole different reason: he was shivering, I wasn't going to kiss a man about to go hypothermic. Which is even true! She just left out the part where she knelt up on the bench and unbuttoned all six and held his eyes while she did it. Cassie gets the girl. The dining hall gets the anecdote. Nobody made a point of it, nobody underlined it, and I sat there going *oh, you little liar* with total affection. That's the first time this book has let me catch Vee curating, and it's the most adult thing she's done.

Also she doesn't tell Randi. Randi is the one she hands everything to over lunch, the one who got *May I choose* and *and I liked it* — and the truck, so far, has gone to Cassie and to Meg and Kayla and not to the woman waiting for it. I don't think that's on purpose on Vee's part. I think it's just that she hasn't had a lunch yet. But I'm watching the ledger, because Pace already spent the truck. He gave Randi *her stomach is soft* while she was riding him and withheld how he knew it, same as he withheld the overfilled cup's true sentence. He's accumulating a private shelf of Vee and Randi can feel the shape of the things on it without being handed them. Chlorine in her hair. He smelled her hair.

New things I got and wanted: he lifts first thing, writes six hours, stops, *my writing's no good after that.* A powerlifter who does math and builds furniture, which is three different ways of saying a man who knows exactly how much load a thing will take. And her doing his flat voice for her friends — she's got an impression of him already, she's been collecting him.

Meg is the one who scared me. *Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think.* Then the shrug: *sometimes they even are.* That's the book putting the warning in the mouth of the kindest person at the table and then having Vee not hear it — "it didn't catch on anything" — and for once I didn't mind being told, because Meg is wrong in the exact way that makes her right. He really is different. That's the problem. Kayla's *they're all nice until you don't want them and then they're wonderful* is a BookTok line but it's a good one and it goes somewhere later, I can feel it.

And then. Saturday. Two o'clock. His house. *It's the least information a human being has ever sent me and I have read it a thousand times.* Kayla, God bless her, does the math out loud — there's no meal at two, he wants you for the afternoon, bring a change of clothes — and the whole table starts planning hair. And I am sitting here with *do you think she'll like the bench?* going off like a smoke alarm. That's what the chapter is for. A room full of sweet girls in the last orange light doing a friend's hair for an afternoon at a house that has a bench in it under a table top, built by a man who has already put one woman on it and found something in her she locked in a bathroom afterward. None of them know. Vee doesn't know. He knows, and Randi knows, and Randi isn't at this table. That gold-lit room is the last nice place before the door, and the book knows it, and it didn't say so once.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she edited the shirt story for the normal friends. and her hair appointment is for the bench.*

Where it nearly lost me: the squeal section. "Stop," "You kept his shirt," Kayla going for the phone — that's the Sloane-and-Kinsey hazard, the chorus that exists to react, and for about a page Kayla was a hair color and a volume setting. She got rescued by actually being smart about the two o'clock. But I've had two chapters running now of Vee telling somebody about the mountain, and that's my limit. If chapter fifteen is a third retelling I'm going to start skimming for the house.

Also: "What to Wear." She set a russet silk dress down on a rock and he said *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and didn't pick it up. Now a chapter titled What to Wear. If that dress turns up in a box I will be furious and I will also make a sound.
