REACTION

Okay so I've been waiting nine chapters for the froyo to turn into something and it turned into a girl kneeling up on a bench seat in a cold truck unbuttoning a wet shirt with her eyes locked on a man who does not move a single inch. I take back every impatient thing I wrote. That's the chapter. That's *her* chapter — the first thing in this book Vee has done that nobody steered her into. Randi handed her a picture of being ravished against a tree and Vee took it home and kept it up on the wall and then out on that mountain she did something neither Randi nor Pace scripted: she knocked on the glass and crooked her finger.

And the shame is the engine. That's what's killing me. The mother's voice comes up — *floozy*, in the exact word, *three dates and you'd, your mother would* — and her body answers each hit with heat, and the book just says it flat: she *wanted to be the very thing the voice said, wanted it with him, wanted him watching her be it.* That's the thing I never get in these books. Usually the shame is an obstacle to be cleared by chapter nineteen with a speech. Here it's the fuel line. She's not overcoming it, she's *using* it, and she doesn't know that's what she's doing, and I do, and that gap is the whole erotic architecture of this novel.

And then — and *then* — she doesn't look down for the fourth button. She *could* have taken it by feel. She looks down anyway so his eyes go with hers, so she gets to see what he sees. She staged her own looking. Twenty-one years old, covered to the collarbone on purpose, and she directed that. I had to put the book down for a second.

The restraint on his end is doing something to me that actual sex wouldn't have. He doesn't lean in. Doesn't lift a hand. "His face was want and it was gladness and it was a stillness she had never seen on anyone." For once I believe the stillness because I've watched him do it for twelve chapters — the last inch he made her cross twice, the pencil back in her hand. He does not take. He *receives*, visibly, and lets the receiving be the reward. God, it works.

But here's where I sat up: she reaches for his hand to kiss his knuckles — she's *going*, she's headed for more — and the hand is shaking. And she stops her own seduction cold and pulls his hand under the flannel onto her belly and says *you're freezing*. She chose him over the thing she wanted. That's the first time in this book anyone has taken care of Pace. "He'd been a long way from the question of whether he was cold." And he let his hand stay a beat longer than he had to. That beat is the most naked he's been on the page.

Now. The suspicious part of me never goes to sleep. He read that sky. He said *it's going to come down* and he was right, and I'd love to know how long he'd known. The gym bag behind the seat with the razor and the thin soap and the folded gray briefs and the clean flannel under all of it — that's a bag belonging to a man who sleeps where he isn't expected to, and I'm going to assume it's Randi's house as often as not. And the flannel is now in Vee's room, warm, smelling of soap and him and her own wet hair, and she will absolutely sleep in it, and that's not an accident of weather, that's an occupation. He gave her his only dry shirt *and* the truck cab and went and stood out in it with his back turned, and standing in the rain with your back turned is simultaneously the kindest and the most devastatingly effective thing a man could do, and I've stopped trying to separate those in him. It's both. Both is the horror. I'm just also fully in it now, which is the other horror.

The russet dress. Oh, he's buying that dress. She set it down on the rock — *you put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking* — and he said "that sounds like a beautiful dress" with no weight on it at all and did not pick it up again, which is exactly how this man handles something he's decided about. Silk the red of a brick, an induction dinner, her name read out at the front of a room, girls from where she's from don't get that. The dress is going to arrive. Possibly from Randi's hands so it can be deniable. And I will be furious and I will also cry.

Small things I loved: the pits. She spit one and he said *we take nothing and leave nothing* and picked it up and smoothed the wax paper and put his own pit down beside hers and did not look at her learning. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." That's the cleanest statement of his whole method in the book, and it's also a sentence about class, which this book keeps touching without ever once announcing it. The boots are Meg's. Half a size big. Of course they are.

And the summit — the thing that got me wasn't the colors, it was that the world "did not care that she was standing on the edge of it with her heart going," and his blue shirt was nothing against it. The one place in twelve chapters where he's small. She needed to see something that wasn't him.

The ending. She turns to him with something bigger than words in her mouth, opens it, *Pace—* and a campus cop squawks the siren and takes the rest of her sentence. She's out of the truck before she decides to be, because her body hears a siren and goes. Fire lane, red curb. He pulled up where it was dry for her and got pulled over for it. She never got to say it. I have never been so annoyed and so delighted by an interruption — and it keeps the thing unsaid, which means it has to come out somewhere worse.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she unbuttoned her own shirt and he didn't touch her and I'm unwell. also she came home wearing another man's flannel and CASSIE IS GOING TO SEE IT.*
