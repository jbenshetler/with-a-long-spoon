REACTION

Okay. First: he *left*. He set the beer down on the bar untouched and walked out into the cold, and I sat up. Twenty-four chapters of a man who absorbs everything — who gets out of the truck into the rain, who craned his chin up off his knees, who covered Randi's bowl with a plate and put it in the refrigerator for her — and the one time somebody takes a swing at him he can't metabolize it and he goes. No speech. No "let me explain." Just the good mood draining out of his face and "Yes. Sometimes. When it's required," which is the most devastating thing he could have said because it's *true* and it's *flat* and he didn't dress it up to save himself. And then the untouched bottle on the bar. The book has been putting untouched food in front of me for chapters and now it's his.

The lesson. God, the lesson. I've read a hundred teach-her-something scenes and they're all the same hand-over-hand nonsense, and this one is the first one that was actually about something. *You have a job here. Give me something to push back against. Being jelly doesn't work.* And then — *that's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it, come all the way in, and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours.* I had to put the book down for a second. He is explaining the entire architecture of what's about to happen to her and he thinks he's explaining a three-step. And the book does not come behind him and underline it for me, which after chapter 24's "it's the lift, it's the same face" I was braced for. It let me have it. The moment the guessing stopped happening and *the decision pulled from her somehow* — that's the only erotic thing in the chapter and it's on a concrete pad under cold field lights in borrowed boots and it worked on me better than most bed scenes I've read this year.

Then the Rusty Nail and the count she didn't mean to start, which is the honest part. It's not that she had no reason. She walked into a room where every woman knew his hands. *He's the reason half of us can dance* — delivered as a *gift*, and it lands like a bill. And her mother shows up right on schedule: *a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them.* So when she says the venom thing, I didn't hate her for it, which is rarer than this book probably knows. She heard it going past the point where she could catch it. That's exactly what it's like.

Where my eyebrow went up: the blonde. I know that girl. She's been in forty books I've read. Too-much makeup that'll be wrong by daylight, hand on the forearm, poison in a warm voice, exits before anyone can answer. She's a crowbar, not a person, and the chapter needed her and so she appeared. And then her mirror image, the wise older barmaid who racks glasses and dispenses the exact exonerating facts in the exact order Vee needs them — *wasn't him they were jealous of, honey* — which is a *great* line sitting on top of a very old machine. Two stock women bracketing one real woman's worst night. I noticed. I kept reading, because the thing between them was real: *she'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be.* That sentence is the chapter. She didn't blow up at a cheater. She blew up at evidence of his decency, because decency aimed at other women meant she wasn't chosen, and being chosen is the only thing holding her shame shut.

The shirt. *She'd slept in it and not washed it, rationing the smell of him, until what was left of him went stale and wrong and she'd finally, hating it, run it through the machine. It came back smelling of fabric softener and no one.* That's the one I'd have texted at 1 a.m. with no commentary. That's the whole book in two sentences — the thing she's been living off is a copy, and she ran it through the wash, and now she's lying on top of the covers in a shirt that smells like nobody.

Money: she put four hundred dollars of shoes on a credit card with nothing under it, and six days later she's paying a cab fare that "had been meant for other things" because she wouldn't take the ride and wouldn't wake Cassie. That's not a subplot anymore, that's a countdown.

And Cassie three feet away, breathing easy, the only easy thing in the room, and Vee *does not wake her*, twice, with a reason that's actually airtight: Cassie would make her say what she did out loud. That's not the book keeping Cassie off the board. That's Vee choosing the person who won't make her say it.

Which brings me to the last line, and the last line is why I'm not stopping. *Go to Randi's.* Of course. Her first real wound and she's carrying it straight to the one woman on earth who cannot be handed it — who will hear "I accused him of running a service for girls," and will know *exactly* why that's unfair, and will know it from inside his bed, and will have to do something with her face. I flagged a fourth table scene as my limit three chapters ago. Fine. This one's not a table. This one's a detonation with a napkin on it.
