REACTION

Okay so the thing that got me — and it got me embarrassingly fast — was "May I choose?" Three words. I have read a thousand men order for a woman and every single time it's been a power move dressed as chivalry, the guy who says "she'll have the salmon" while she sits there with her jaw set. This one *asks*. And then he doesn't take it lightly, he actually weighs it, and he asks her the one real question (anything you won't eat), and then he builds her a table of things she can't pronounce. I felt the handoff in my own chest. That's the same move as *may I call you* and the same move as giving her the pencil back, and the book knows it's the same move, and I love that it's not explaining it to me.

And the food. God. This book keeps feeding people, or not feeding them. Randi and her thin honeycrisp slices with the cut sides up, the appetite trimmed down to nothing, and now Vee with a hollow that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon," eating bread with both hands and *not watching herself eat.* That line — she wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped — that's the whole hero-fantasy of this book in one sentence and it's not even a sex scene. I'm a little rattled by how erotic I found a plate of tabbouleh.

The hand. The HAND. "Less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his." I would have rolled my eyes at that in any other book. Here I didn't, because I've watched this man for eight chapters and he's *earned* the capital-H Hand. That's the trick the book is pulling on me and I can see it being pulled and it's working anyway.

But. I am not stupid and neither is the jacket. This whole gorgeous evening was ordered up on a couch three chapters ago by a girl sitting on his lap demanding to be told about it. He *knows* she'll be hungry. He knows exactly what the quiet in the text will do to her — "he'd built the quiet into it without being asked," Vee thinks, and I went cold for a second, because he absolutely was asked, just not by her. Every tender thing in this chapter is also a move, and the book is letting me hold both at once without ever winking. That's the merciless part they promised. The moment Christine comes out and knows him, and he's glad to say her name — I loved him for it and also thought *he brought her here because Christine makes it look like a life, not a play.* I don't know if that's fair to him. I don't think the book knows either yet, which is why I'm still here.

Two small nags. One: this is the second time Vee has lost the transcript of an evening — the Chi Latte hour was "the leaning-in was the whole content" and now dinner goes soft at the edges and she can't reconstruct it. Once was a lovely observation about being dazzled. Twice starts to feel like the book skipping the hard work of writing the actual conversation, and I want to hear these two *talk*, properly, at length, because the one real exchange they've had (econ, the pencil) was the best scene in the book. Two: Randi's absence here is loud. No text, no check-in, nothing — and I wanted, badly, a single line of her waiting to hear about this one, since last time she came over and climbed on him for the report. I assume that's Chapter 10 and I assume it's going to be awful in the good way.

"I'm making an exception" with her feet planted and the hand pulled taut — fine, yes, my thumb moved. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he let her go and she didn't go.*
