REACTION

Oh, this one hurt in the good way. I spent the whole smoothie scene wanting to reach into the page and shake her, and the book knows that, and it does not do me the insult of winking at me about it.

Because here's what got me: Vee handing it all over. Not the kiss — the *May I choose.* She gives Randi the single most load-bearing thing about herself, the thing that is literally the mechanism she's going to be taken apart by, and she gives it over a pink strawberry banana because somebody finally asked. And Randi says *you let him choose for you. And you liked it.* Slowly. Holding it up to the light. Then she lets it go, doesn't push, and Vee reads the not-pushing as kindness. That's the most chilling beat in the book so far and nobody raised their voice. I've read two hundred books where the villain monologues and not one of them made my stomach drop like Randi deciding not to say the next thing.

And then *dear friend.* God. That one landed on both sides at once, which is the thing I didn't expect. Because it *is* a gift — Randi spends real currency in front of Brooke, in a house where Brooke sitting down is a show of force, and she spends it on a girl in a pilled cardigan with her feet not touching the floor. Vee can feel that it cost something and can't price it. Neither can Randi, I think. That's the hook in me now: I came in reading this as a hunt and I'm now reading it as a woman who has found the one person she can say a true thing to and the true thing is *mine, in front of you, yes.* She called her dear in public. That's going to be the thing that wrecks all three of them.

Cassie, though. Cassie is going to destroy me. "Goodbye, Vee" is still sitting in my chest from the coffee chapter and now we get the look going past her to Randi and getting folded away before Vee can see it, and *I'll see you at the dorm,* easy, already going. And then Vee saying *it isn't really her place, the smoothie one* — doing the excluding herself and calling it respecting Cassie's preferences, and then *something turned over, small, and she let it lie.* That's the realest sentence in the chapter and I hated it. I have been Vee. I have let it lie. The window with its two inches of cold Cassie keeps open because she'd rather be cold than cooked — that's the whole friendship in one object and the book doesn't say so, it just leaves it there twice.

Two tiny grumbles. One: I'm three chapters out from anything with real heat in it now, and I can feel the book banking. The *ask and then ravish* beat — Randi dropping into Vee's register for one line, a beat late and low, Vee's hands going down her own thighs — that was genuinely charged and it's the only thing keeping the dial off zero. I will take one more courtship chapter on credit and then I want the floor to open. Two: a couple of times it tells me what it's already shown. *That she had a place now, and that the place was here, next to Randi* — I had that. I had it from the hand on the table. I don't need the subtitle. The deep-water line was perfect; the explanation two paragraphs later was not.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she just told the wolf exactly how she likes to be fed and the wolf said mm and changed the subject.* Also Brooke has filed the name Pace twice now and has filed Vee's face once and I do not think that woman forgets a single thing she's written down. Get in the car, Vee. Wear the borrowed shoes.
