REACTION

Okay so I'm sitting here having been genuinely, physically charmed by a dinner date where nothing happened but bread, and I am also slightly sick about it, and that double thing is the whole reason I'm still in this book.

Because here's what I did while reading: I fell for it. Completely. The damp hair and the razor-flush and the *good boots worn but polished* — I clocked that the way Vee clocked it, that he showered *for this*, and I had the same stupid lift in my chest. The elevator doors closing on her because she forgot to get out. Her hand slapping at them. "If he saw, he had the grace not to show it." I was gone. And then "May I choose?" and the relief going through her bigger than a menu deserves — and I felt the relief too, in my own shoulders, which is the trick, that's the actual trick, because the book has been telling me since chapter one that this man builds things that look like tables.

The food. God. I've read a hundred romance dinner dates and they're wallpaper — she orders a salad, he orders a steak, they banter. This one is a feeding scene and it knows it. She hasn't eaten since breakfast and didn't notice, and the hollow "had been empty a lot longer than since noon," and she stops watching herself eat — *she wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped.* That's the whole book in one sentence and it didn't explain itself to me, thank God. And I'm sitting there going: this is the apple. This is the thin slices with the cut sides up, except Randi was being portioned and Vee is being filled, and both of them are being *managed by appetite*, and he's not even wrong about either of them, which is the horrible part. He's good at this because he's genuinely paying attention. That's not a villain move. That's the move I want from a man. That's why this is working on me.

The right hand thing wrecked me a little. Always the right, never the other, "some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it, one more thing he knew that she didn't." She reads it as charming competence. I read it as: he has a whole interior he hasn't handed her, and she's building a man out of the parts he's letting show. And three chapters ago he had Randi on his lap licking wine into his mouth saying *tell me, tell. Did she make you laugh?* while he decided which parts of the evening were hers to keep and which weren't. He didn't hand over the embarrassing true thing Vee said at the froyo place — "It's hers" — and I noticed that he *does* have a line, and I don't know yet whether having a line makes it better or infinitely worse. Probably worse. A man with no line is just a creep. A man with a line is someone you can't dismiss.

The kiss. I want to be annoyed about "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" because for exactly one second it smelled like a manufactured obstacle, the kind of rule a heroine announces so the hero can dismantle it. And then the book did the only thing that could have saved it: he just *smiled and let it close.* Didn't lean, didn't wait her out, didn't charm — started her walking again. And her feet didn't go. The hand going taut between them. That's the best beat in the chapter and it's not even the kiss, it's the three inches of rope. And then she kisses him small and he gives back exactly what she brought and *eases off and waits*, leaving the last inch where it is — "whatever came next was hers too" — and she stands there and lets herself want it longer than she thought she could stand before she takes it. That's the blurb cashing its check. Choosing *is* the erotic thing. I have read so much alpha-grabs-her-by-the-jaw and it has never once done to me what one withheld inch did here.

And then the grin inside the kiss and him smiling back inside it, and I made an actual noise.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he let her come to him and I'm not okay, also he's going to go home and tell his girlfriend about it and his girlfriend is going to sit on his lap and make him say it out loud.* Because that's the thing. This tender, careful, grown-up date is *material*. It's going to be reported. Possibly tonight. And Vee came home warm with a hand that finally felt like it was where it belonged, and somewhere Randi is waiting to be told how the girl ate.

Also: "Goodbye, Vee." Cassie said the whole word, flat and finished, and Vee smoothed it over, and I have not forgotten it and I don't think the book has either. Cassie saying "That's not a boy. That's a man" after refusing to tease her — Cassie is the only person in this novel standing outside the glass and I'm scared for what she's going to see.

Give me the next one. I want to see what he does with what she chose.
