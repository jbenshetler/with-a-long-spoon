Okay so this is the chapter where I stopped pretending I'm reading this with any critical distance. The whole middle section — the menu, "May I choose?", her saying *yes, please* and then not being able to pronounce a single thing he ordered and liking it that way — that's the most erotic thing in the book so far and nobody took their clothes off. *each soft sound a closed door with something behind it, and she sat there with her mouth watering at promises she couldn't have read aloud.* Come on. That's the whole book's thesis dressed as dinner. He's teaching her to want things she can't name and to hand him the choosing, and she's experiencing it as *relief*, and I am experiencing it as relief, and the two of us are in exactly the same trouble.

And the hunger. The hunger is doing so much work and the book isn't explaining it to me, which I appreciate more than I can say. She skipped lunch for the dress and the other dress and the borrowed shoes, and then it "found her," and then the hollow "had been empty a lot longer than since noon," and I thought about Randi and her thin apple slices in chapter one and the mother's voice doing *that's plenty, that's enough now* — which the froyo chapter already set up and this one pays off by its absence. Two girls, two appetites, one man who feeds people. He fed Randi an apple to the exact thickness she'd accept. He ordered Vee a table full of food she couldn't name and watched her forget to watch herself eat. Those are not the same act of kindness and the book knows it and hasn't told me so. Thank you.

The hand. *less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe.* I put the book down on my chest for a second there. That's the line I'd text. And then the kiss — her rule coming up out of her mouth at the worst possible moment, him not arguing, not leaning, not doing the thing every single alpha on my Kindle would have done, just *smiling like she'd told him something true and fine about herself* and walking her on. And then her feet not moving. The hand going taut between them. God. He left the last inch and made her cross it twice. She kissed him small, he gave back exactly what she brought and no more, and then she had to stand there in it and *want* it before she took it. That's the bench. That's the same architecture as the bench, rebuilt in a tree shadow with clothes on, and Vee has no idea she's on it.

Which is the part that's got my stomach in a knot. Because I'm supposed to be swooning and I am swooning, and the whole time there's Randi on his lap in chapter seven going *tell. tell.* with wine in her mouth, and I know this dinner got reported. I know "she built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop" was a gift handed across a couch. So when Christine warms up at the sight of him, when he knows the block Vee's never turned down, when he does the right hand every time — I'm cataloguing it as tenderness *and* as competence, and the competence is what scares me. He's so good at this. He was this good with Randi too, and Randi went into a bathroom and locked the door and decided he'd never see her again.

Small thing I loved: Cassie. "That's not a boy. That's a man." She doesn't tease about this one. Cassie's the only person in this book not running an angle, and I'm starting to be afraid for her — *Goodbye, Vee* is still sitting in me from chapter four like a stone in a shoe.

The only place I twitched: "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days." I've read that beat four hundred times, the rule declared so it can be broken in the next paragraph, and for a half-second I thought *oh no, here's the one cheap move.* But the book earned it back inside ten lines by making her do the breaking herself, in silence, with her feet, after he'd already let it go. That's the difference between this and the shelf.
