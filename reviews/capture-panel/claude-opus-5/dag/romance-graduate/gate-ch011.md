REACTION

Okay so the thing that got me is that Randi invented a bad date.

She sat there and built a whole fake catastrophe — the tall one, the candlelit French place, Switzerland, lacrosse numbers — and the reason she built it is so Vee will never once wonder whether Randi has a man already. That's the load-bearing lie of the entire chapter and it goes by in thirty seconds dressed up as girl-talk. But she doesn't just alibi herself with it. She uses it to *teach*. "A man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She hands Vee the rubric five minutes before Vee describes Pace, so that when Vee says *I liked listening to him, he goes six years old about a bridge*, she's already scoring him against a test Randi wrote. I actually said *oh, you absolute snake* out loud at the bus stop. In admiration. I'm not proud.

And then Vee says "May I choose?" to her. To *her*. To the woman who has been facedown on the table he built. And Randi doesn't flinch, she just holds it up to the light — "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." — and then, this is the part, she *lets it go*. Doesn't push. Gives Vee the out of "it was just nice, not having to be in charge for once" and lets her keep it. And Vee reads the mercy as kindness. It's the exact move Pace makes: leave the door open, don't shove. They're the same animal and they learned it from each other and I'd very much like to know which one taught the other.

But here's what I'm actually here for: **"Ask and then ravish."** A beat late. Low. At Vee's own volume. Randi has been running the whole hour at performance pitch and then Vee puts her hands on her own thighs and says *he'd ask, though — before he ravished me*, and Randi's voice drops out of the register she does people in. She went up the trail with her. She wasn't running the play in that second, she was *in it*, and she came back a half-second late like someone surfacing. That's the crack. Same late-arriving smile as the quad, same want too big for the face. She's not recruiting anymore, she's got a crush and she's calling it a project.

And then "Vee's my dear friend," with the cold damp hand on the white table, in front of Brooke. I don't know what that cost either, but I clocked what Vee clocked — that it cost. The book is so mean about this: Vee can *feel* the price without being able to read the currency. She thinks she's being defended from a room. She's being claimed in front of a witness, and the witness is Brooke, who already filed the name *Pace* and has now filed the face. And the tall one who says nothing and just runs her eyes over Vee in a slow circuit like inventory. I hate her and I want more of her.

The thing that actually hurt, though, wasn't Randi. It was that **Vee did it herself.** "It isn't really her place, the smoothie one." Randi *offered* — we could have dragged her — and Vee was the one who kept Cassie out, and then reasoned herself comfortable about it in one tidy paragraph about how healthy it is to have your own people. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's how it happens. Nobody pushes Cassie out. Cassie gets left at the top of an aisle with her cap down while Vee doesn't watch her go. And Cassie *saw* the look go past her to Randi and folded it away before Vee could catch it. I'm not going to recover from that one quickly.

Gripes, because I have two. One: this is the second high table in the window with her feet not reaching the floor, and I *think* that's on purpose — the machine running the same cycle, the shelf she gets set up on — but it's also the second chapter in a row of people talking about things I already watched happen. When Vee started recapping the Middle Eastern dinner I felt my eyes slide, because I *was there*, I remember the brass cages. It's redeemed by what the retelling does to Randi, but it was close. Two: nobody has touched anybody in three chapters and I can feel the book saving it up for the mountain. Fine. It better be worth the deposit.
