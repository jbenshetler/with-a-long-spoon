REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where she stops being able to lie to herself and discovers that doesn't help even slightly.

The hand on her stomach. *A lid held down on a pot.* That's the whole chapter in one image and I'd text it to someone at 1 a.m. — a girl lying in a top bunk with her palm flat on her own belly, not because anyone forbade it (and the book is careful to note Pace has specifically told her the opposite, which is its own little knife) but because she knows exactly which face will show up the second she stops supervising. She's not policing the act. She's policing the *name*. That is the entire architecture of this series recited by a twenty-one-year-old in the dark with nobody watching, and she still can't see it.

And the greed on Sunday — four times, every one of them her starting it, *full of him and not, somehow, filled.* That's the best line in the chapter and it's doing something mean: she's using him as a solvent. She did it Saturday night with teeth and she did it Sunday with sweetness and neither one dissolved anything. You cannot fuck a question out of yourself. I've read maybe forty books where the heroine goes to the hero to burn off the Other Person and every one of them let it work. This one has her wake up Monday with nothing in the day and the wanting *still there, low and constant, and a worry wound so smoothly through it the two came as one thing.* Wanting and dread arriving fused, inseparable, as a single sensation. Nobody writes that. That's real.

The closet. God, the closet. Arms caught in a half-on plum sweater, unable to say whether she's dressing to be looked at or dressing to be covered, and *the not-knowing was worse than either answer.* I have stood in that exact paralysis about a person I wasn't admitting to and I have never once seen it on a page. She goes. She leaves the question hanging on the hanger. Of course she does.

And then the hour itself, which is nothing — a knee. A knee against a knee for fifty minutes and the cost of holding her own leg still. *Randi smelled the same. That was somehow the worst of it.* Yes. Yes it is. The horror isn't that something changed; it's that nothing did, Randi is exactly herself down to the uncapped pen, and the entire difference is inside Vee's body where she can't get at it.

The Lifesaver undid me. She's handed Randi one every single class and tonight she forgot clean — because she's so busy managing herself she's dropped the one small ritual that was *hers* to give — and then the fingertip across the inside of the wrist, the same graze as every other week, and her pulse jumping up under it to be found. *She held very still, and it went on not being static.* That sentence. The book letting her try the lie and then just — declining to let it work, in the same breath, no commentary. (And it's the wrist again. This book knows what it's doing with that wrist. Pace parked there. Randi grazes it on the way past and the whole girl lights up.)

Then: *"Having you at the dance was so great. I've wanted to do that for the longest time."* And the half-beat lag, the same lag the word *boyfriend* had in Pace's bed. **Having.** Randi did not mean it that way — or Randi absolutely meant it that way and would swear under oath she didn't, which with Randi is the same thing. And the thing that comes in riding on it isn't even horniness, it's the *size* of it: "something that had already happened and hadn't yet shown its size." That's the first honest look she's taken at it and she takes it sideways, as weather.

And then she does the thing I did not expect and have been waiting four chapters for someone to do: **she turns her mouth away.** On purpose. *Not an accident got up to look like one.* The first physical refusal in this entire friendship, and it's delivered by the girl who says yes to everything, and it costs her so much she has a fake plague pre-built from somewhere on the walk she doesn't remember building. And Randi — Randi doesn't push, Randi huffs *keep your plague, then*, and **doesn't step out of the hug.** Still holding her. That's the tell. She clocked the miss and she just... stayed in contact, and then put her mouth on Vee's neck anyway. *"See? How do you like it?"* Framed as payback for the two neck kisses Vee gave her. Which is deniable, and funny, and also the single most calculated thing she's done since the shoes — Vee refuses the mouth, so Randi takes the neck, and gets to call it a joke, because *ticklish* and *bit* are the only two words either of these women own.

And out of the ringing, with no thinking anywhere in it, Vee says **brunch.** Saturday. She locks herself back in. Two minutes after turning her face away she asks for more, and Randi lights up like she's been handed a present. The pot lid came off for exactly one sentence and what came out was an invitation. I actually made a noise.

Suspicions, in order. One: *"Standards committee — I've got to go discipline a bad girl."* Randi says it as a martyr's groan and I read it as the roof creaking. Brooke runs a ledger. Lauren Pike. Two girls kissed on that dance floor in front of the entire house and the alibi was a boy named Harrison, which technically satisfies Brooke's rule — *for the boys* — and I do not believe that gets Randi all the way out from under it, because Brooke was *there*, Brooke knew Vee's whole history by heart before she'd said her name, and Brooke said *I chose / I can see* with a smile switching on behind it by some exact amount. If Thursday's bad girl is a standards matter and Randi's sitting on the committee judging it while her own weekend is what it is — that's the best irony this book's set up in twenty chapters and it's sitting there unexploded.

Two: Randi is unavailable Thursday for the first time ever. She who is always there first, always ahead. And the replacement Vee proposes is Saturday brunch — which is the *old* shape, the lunches-that-became-dinners shape, moved back into daylight. I noticed the furniture moved and I'm grateful; I'd flagged a third identical loft as the thing that would make me itch and the book skipped the loft entirely and sent them out into the cold instead.

Three: Marsh. Reciprocity, the three obligations, the man beggaring his whole house because *a gift is a debt you lay on someone whether they want it or not* — and the book has her **slide right off it.** Thank God. One more sentence of Vee connecting that to Pace's bread and Randi's shoes and I'd have thrown something. Instead it's a bill left on the table for me and she walks past it, and the chapter is titled Coming Due, and that's as close to explaining itself as this book gets and it still didn't quite.

And Cassie. *"What was that with you and Randi?"* Not flat. A worry under it, and disbelief she doesn't bother hiding. She was out in the cold — she saw something, from somewhere, a window, the path. **The reckoning I've been promised for thirty chapters just put its hand on the door.** And the whole weight of it is in what Vee can't do: she reaches for the easy lie — *sorority stuff, you know how she is* — and there's nothing there to reach for, because the invented cranberry sweater and the shrunk-in-the-wash and the flash-with-no-shoes all required her to *know what she was covering*, and she doesn't. The liar ran out of material because the material ran out of her. Ending the chapter on the plainest question in the book with no answer available is exactly where my thumb wanted it.

Still no Pace. Ten-plus chapters, one chapter from inside him (and it was him inside Randi), and I am moving from "I don't mind yet" to minding. He has now been told *I kissed Randi and I kissed her back* and *nothing, there's nothing else* in the same ten minutes, he watched a parking lot in January, and the book will not open the door. That's the one thing I'm actively impatient about.
