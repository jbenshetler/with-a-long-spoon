REACTION

Oh, there she is.

Forty-something chapters I've been keeping a tally on this woman and the book finally opens the door and the first thing I want to say is: it did not flinch. I was braced for Randi-alone to be a villain reveal — the scheming chapter, the cackle, the plan laid out for the reader who might have missed it. Instead I got her with her feet up on his couch in a short silk going almost black where it moves, quiet, with her machine switched *off*, and the one clean sentence that unmade everything I thought I knew about the premise:

*I didn't. I pointed. I watched your face.*

He didn't choose Vee. **She did.** A year ago, out of a whole set of doors on a Thursday afternoon, and she didn't know she was doing it, and he knew, and he's known for a year and never said, and she came here *tonight* to ask him how he knew because she's finally curious about her own hand. And then her mouth did something and was gone again. That's the third game. Vee is a willing player in two; the third one is Randi being run by her own appetite with Pace holding the stopwatch, and I don't think she can see it, and the jacket told me there were three and I assumed I was the one who'd be counting.

The whole chapter is the brunch again from the other side and it's *devastating*, because I was in that booth two chapters ago. I watched Randi's eyes go bright and unfixed and her breath catch on *like he wants me, with the body attached*, and Vee — sweet, generous, wrong — thought *of course it reached her, these are the ones that get you*, and went warm with pride that a Saturday of hers could do that to a woman like Randi. And now I know. It wasn't the story landing. It was the woman who has been *being* the other way around her entire life, in a cream coat, in the dark, getting hit by it with no warning. And it's not even the one she admits to in here — she gives Pace *the sleeping.* "Twenty-one years old and she found out this autumn that a person can sleep." Her hand closing on the couch arm and letting go. That's the one that got her. Not the sex. The sleeping.

And the pettiness! *She told me that one.* — *Did she? Well. She told me first.* Four words and the entire architecture of her jealousy is standing there naked. She's not competing with Pace for Vee's body. She's competing for being the one Vee *tells.* And Pace, who answers everything with an object, hands her the only object he's got: *she'll give up a Saturday out here to go sit in a restaurant with you. I've never heard her talk about anything the way she talks about being with you.* He says it "because it was true and because she ought to hear it," and she goes warm and thick and I don't know if I just watched kindness or portion control. Probably both. That's what's so good about him and so frightening — he feeds everybody *exactly* enough.

Then the sex, which — fine, I'll say it — is the best scene in this book. Not because of the four times. Because of the **mirror.** She goes up on her knees facing the back of the couch mid-sentence, "as though the position were a thing the sentence required," and the reason she does it is that she's about to say the thing about kissing Vee's mouth and she cannot be looked at while she says it. She puts her face where he can't get at it. *And she forgot the glass.* He had her the whole time. She hid and he watched her hide, and he kept asking — *does she want it* — *that isn't what I asked* — *do you like it* — *I didn't ask who it was for* — the way a man turns off a tap. He does to her, *precisely*, what she does to Vee in a soundproof piano closet with a question like *so, did you deserve it.* And she can't tell. She's the one person in this book who always knows what's being done to her and she cannot tell.

And then she comes on **"you don't have to do it for me."** Said kindly. Hand flat on her belly, holding her still. She comes on being *released* from the lie, and she's still arguing while she goes — *oh, but I'm doing the work* — and then "whatever she had been going to add to her argument did not get added, then or after." I made a noise out loud. That's the most psychologically exact orgasm I've read in years. She didn't come on the filth. She came on being seen.

*God, I want her.* Down into the wood, with nothing at all around it. And then the walk-back, one second late: *for us. When she's ready. That's all I meant.* And *aren't you?* — because she needs him to say yes so the sentence she just said gets to belong to the plan instead of to her. He says *I am*, and it costs him nothing, and that's the scariest line in the chapter.

And then — this is the part I'll be carrying — *say something nice to me.* She asks for it out loud, which Vee has never once been able to do. He thinks about it *because she would know the difference.* **"You are extraordinary."** Not *beautiful*, which is what he gives Vee. Extraordinary. And she says *Mn* in the voice she uses when a thing lands where she wanted it, and then she takes his forearm with both hands and pulls it tighter, and tighter again, and doesn't let go for a long time, and he lies there and "took her holding on for the compliment it was." These two can only talk in positions and objects and they've built a whole marriage out of it with a girl in the middle who doesn't know either of them.

Also, quietly, the thing I did not expect to hurt: *Is she all right? / Is she happy? / She is radiant. / That isn't the same answer.* He's checking the gauge. HE is checking the gauge. And she snaps — *I know exactly what I'm doing. I have always known what I'm doing* — and then *don't ask me in that voice*, and he says nothing, "which was the only thing there was to say." That's a woman who heard the question underneath and is furious because she doesn't have the answer anymore.

What I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: the Saturdays are the only thing Randi gets, and she gets them because Vee *chose* them over him. The one true thing in that friendship is the only thing Randi didn't engineer. And when Vee finds out about the pointing — and she will, this book has never once dropped a loaded gun — what breaks won't be the kissing. It'll be *I still can't get over being the one Randi picked*, which is true, and which is going to turn out to have been true in a way that will take the floor out of her.

Only flicker of resistance in the whole thing: the mirror gets told to me twice ("which gave him her face the entire time" / "and in the glass he had her face the whole time"). I had it. I didn't need the second one. But it's the chapter's whole engine, so I'll let it have the restatement.

Give me the next one. And then give me Cassie, you coward.
