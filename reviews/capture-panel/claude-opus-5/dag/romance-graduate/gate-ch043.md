I put the book down. Not to stop — to physically put it down and say *out loud*, in my kitchen, at eleven at night, "they're TOGETHER."

The deadbolt in the first line. The heat put up two hours ahead of her — the exact thing he does for Vee, done for someone else on a Monday afternoon. And Vee was in that house Sunday night. Vee left that house Monday morning, did her hair at *her* drawer in *his* bathroom mirror, drove to campus with wet hair and his coffee in the steel cup, and Randi — who is *already in his bed by afternoon, on those sheets* — sat down next to her at eleven o'clock and leaned over and tucked her hair behind her ear and told her what she was still full of. And then drove to his house and *reported it to him as foreplay.*

I have been so smug. I wrote down that Randi was dangerous, I wrote down that he had a drawer called Randi, I wrote down "I want to know what she did when she got in that Mercedes," and I still had the shape wrong, because I thought the secret was a past. It's not a past. It's a *staff meeting.* The piano closet. The spanking story with the little man who apologized to the furniture. The Radford boy who never existed and the stranger's bed and the knees wide. The pastry she took one bite of so there'd be sugar on her mouth at the curb. Every one of those was *curriculum.* "Somebody has to get her used to my mouth." I want to lie down on the floor.

And the thing that's going to sit in me for days isn't even the lie, it's chapter forty-two played backward. Vee in that dark green booth saying *like he wants me, with the body attached, God that sounds unhinged* — and Randi's eyes going bright and unfixed and her saying *It doesn't.* I read that as a story landing on a woman who'd never been loved like that. It *was* that. It was also the architect hearing her own building described by the person inside it. Vee went warm with pride that a Saturday of hers could do that to a woman like Randi. I went warm too. We were both wrong in the same direction, me and Vee, and I've never had a book do that to me through a POV switch before. *She told me that one. — Did she? Well. She told me first.* She's jealous of him. Over Vee. She's keeping *score* — "that's more than three," counted out loud into the wood, against Vee's weekend.

But here's why I'm not angry, I'm *fed*: he asks the right question. That's a *real* twenty-two-year-old smart-man morality. The book knows it's not enough and doesn't say so. Nobody explains it to me. God, thank you.
