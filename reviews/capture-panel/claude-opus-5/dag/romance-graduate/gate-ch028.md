REACTION

Okay, so the first half of this had me by the throat and it never took a thing off.

That concrete pad. Field lights, a taped-up boom box, an empty slab outside a dark gym, and this man teaching her a dance by teaching her the exact thing the whole book has been about with its mouth shut: *give me something to push back against.* *Being jelly doesn't work.* *You have a job here.* I actually put the book down for a second at "That's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." That is the thesis. That is the riser, that is the bra-too, that is *nobody measures over a bra*, that is the whole thing, and this time it's said out loud by the man himself and it's ABOUT FEET so it costs nothing and lands everywhere. Nobody in this book explained it to me afterward, either. No little paragraph telling me what the frame meant. Thank you.

And then the moment it takes — "the decision pulled from her somehow" — that's the reins. That's Randi's tomato-soup confession in a different body. *You hold them till your hands cramp and somebody takes them.* Vee just had the safe, clothed, public version of it on a concrete pad in borrowed boots and she doesn't know that's what happened to her. I'm a little dizzy about it.

Then the bar. And look — I have read this. The pretty blonde with a hand on his forearm, the poisoned little sentence, the heroine going off like a lit match. I could have written the blonde's dialogue before she opened her mouth. "He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night." Girl, please. I very nearly checked out right there, because that is *the* manufactured conflict, the one I've quit four books over.

What saved it: three things, and I want to be fair about all three.

One — the book knows she's doing it. "She knew what she was doing, and she did it anyway." "Some part of her wanted to stay that way." It's not a misunderstanding, it's a *choice*, and it's made of her mother, who arrives in the middle of the hugs like a hand on the back of the neck. And it's made of the thing she'd already flinched at earlier in the chapter, in one line I'm still chewing on: *how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join.* That's the most grown-up sentence she's had. She's been curating everyone else all book. She just caught herself curating him.

Two — he *left*. He answered flat and true ("Yes. Sometimes. When it's required."), set the beer down untouched, and went out into the cold. No chasing, no speech, no waiting in the lot being wounded and available. First time this man has not had the right move, and the right move was not having one. Something came off him in that doorway and I want to know what he did with it on that twenty-minute drive past where the pavement quits.

Three — the bartender. *Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey.* Old woman, racking glasses, done being asked for things, and she hands over the correction without one ounce of sentiment. And Vee sitting there in the house lights at close. Good. That's a real scene, and the book let her earn the information instead of having Pace come back and explain himself.

But the thing that actually hurt — the thing I'd text at 1 a.m. — is Cassie. "Cassie was three feet away and would have sat up for this. She did not wake her." She sat in a cab she couldn't afford rather than call the one person who'd come in whatever she sleeps in. And she says it's because of the questions, but the real reason is she'd have to say a true thing out loud, and she's not doing that anymore. She's got a whole sealed room now and Cassie doesn't have the key to any of it. The alarm system isn't muted, it's being *starved*, and Vee is doing it by hand, nightly. Also she's in the washed shirt. The shirt that smells like nobody. Oof.

Suspicious of: that blonde, honestly. Not that she's lying — the bartender settled that — but that she crossed a floor to say it. Who is she to him, and did somebody put her up to it? Probably not. Probably she's just a girl who wanted him. But this book has taught me there's always a hand.

And here's my one real gripe, and it's the shape one I flagged to myself before: the chapter ends pointing straight at *Randi's door.* That's the fourth time in eight chapters the machinery has routed Vee to a table across from Randi. I braced to be annoyed — and then I couldn't be, because this one's inverted. Every previous time she came to Randi glowing, to be admired and named. This time she's coming in the wrong, ashamed, asking how to fix a thing she broke with a man Randi is sleeping with. Randi is the *worst possible* person in the state of Virginia to give this advice and she's going to give it beautifully. If that scene is warm and clever and Randi coaches her to a perfect apology and everybody leaves happy, I'm going to be so tired. If Randi has to sit there and hear Vee be jealous over *her* boyfriend and help her get him back — and something comes off her face — then the book's got me for another twenty.
