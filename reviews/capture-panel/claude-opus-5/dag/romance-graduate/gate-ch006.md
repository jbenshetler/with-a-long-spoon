REACTION

Okay, this one's a wafer. I turned the page and it was over and I actually said "that's it?" out loud — but I'm not mad, because the thing it came for, it got.

The froyo cup. She piles it too high, it's leaning, it's going to drip, and she waits for her mother's voice — *that's plenty, that's enough now* — and it doesn't come. That's the whole chapter. That's the whole *book*, maybe. Nobody told her she could have more, the voice just wasn't in the room this time, and she noticed the silence where it should have been. I've read a hundred heroines with food issues written as a quirk (she eats pizza! she's not like other girls!) and this is the first one in ages where the disordered thing is load-bearing. And the echo of Randi and the thin apple slices in chapter one — the same portioning, the same trimmed appetite, one girl being fed by him at the table and the other girl being fed by him on a sidewalk. He *feeds* women. That's his thing. That's genuinely a little chilling and I like it.

And then "this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo." Oof. I had to put the book on my chest for a second. Because she's *right*, and the awful part is how cheap it was — a man paid for eight dollars of frozen yogurt and listened to the end of her sentences and she's undone. He doesn't even take the comment, doesn't get big about it, just lets it sit and then says her tart's holding up. That is a devastatingly good line to end on and I resent how well it works on me, because I know what I know. I know Randi pointed at her across a lawn. I know there's a bench under a table top in that bedroom. So every gentle thing he does lands double — once as tenderness and once as *technique*, and the book is letting me hold both without telling me which it is. Maybe it's both. Probably it's both, and that's the actual horror.

The bit that scratched me: Randi taking the phone out of her hand. Not asking, taking. And then "he wants to see you naked," grinning, doing the friend-hyping-you-up voice while she's the one who chose the girl. I don't trust a single warm thing that comes out of her mouth and I'm having a great time not trusting it.

Cassie remains the only person in this book with her feet on the ground and I'm getting genuinely worried about her. The healer-in-a-video-game line is the first funny beat any of them has had that's actually funny, and it tells you everything about Vee's history: she spent a semester keeping some business major alive. She's been doing this. She's been the one who waits, the one who's useful, the one who makes the beat land when somebody else drops it. Cassie said *Goodbye, Vee* two chapters ago and I have not stopped hearing it.

What I want, selfishly: heat. Two chapters now of Vee being sweet and lit up and nothing charged happening to her body except a glance at his forearms. I've been running on the memory of the bench. I'll give it one more, because the writing is real and the dread is doing work — but the froyo has to turn into something soon.
