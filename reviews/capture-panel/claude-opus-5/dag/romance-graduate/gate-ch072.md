REACTION

Two pages. I have never read a two-page chapter and I didn't know I was allowed to want one, and then this one ended and I sat there with my thumb hovering because it was *over* and it was also *complete*, and those are supposed to be different things.

Cassie. **Cassie.** Seventy-three chapters of this girl keeping the window cracked two inches and doing skirt arithmetic from a bunk bed and never once being asked a single question about her own life, and she's the one who finally puts the plain question in the room. Not *what happened.* Not *are you and Randi* — nothing with a shape Vee could argue with. **"Is this what you want?"** Which is the only question in this entire book that nobody has asked her, about anything, ever. Not the wax, not the paint, not the photograph, not *roll over for me, hon.* Everybody in Vee's life asks her to say yes to a specific thing with a blank where the reason goes. Cassie asked her what she *wants*, with no object attached, and the girl went and *looked* — "there was a shape there, close and large, and she came up against the edge of it and stepped back off before she could make out its size" — and that's the same gesture as the fingers a breath from her own mouth, the same gesture as *No, not my thing.* Fourth locked door. Her own hand on every one of them.

And then she reached for the plank. *You don't like her. You've never liked any of it.* Of course she did — that's the exact move she pulled on Pace Saturday night, swing the whole weight off yourself and onto whoever's standing closest. She's done it twice in three chapters now and the book didn't underline it for me, which I appreciated. And **Cassie stepped over it without looking down.** God. A lesser book — a book from my Kindle — gives you the fight here. Gives you the roommate who's secretly jealous, or the big tearful airing, or a slammed door and six chapters of silence to pad the middle. This one gives you a woman who recognizes a decoy on sight, declines to chase it, and **changes the subject to dinner.** That is grown-up. That's the thing I came looking for and keep not finding.

"You didn't have dinner." *It wasn't a question, and it wasn't quite a change of subject.* No, it wasn't. It was Cassie saying *I'm not going to make you answer that, and I'm also not pretending I didn't ask.* And then the box, held out. **Cocoa Pebbles.** I laughed out loud and then my eyes stung, in that order, in about a second and a half. Because the kindness in this book has mostly been *expensive* — the loft, the diamonds, the Chambord, the champagne already poured, the skirt off the rail — and here's the one person who's never had an angle on her handing over a box of cereal off the floor beside a bunk bed. And Vee's "Yeah" coming fast and whole, **the first whole thing she's said since she walked in,** because it's a want the size of a bowl and she can actually find it. *That was the size of what she could answer tonight.*

I'm going to be thinking about that for a while. The girl who can't say whether she wants to sleep with her best friend can say yes to Cocoa Pebbles, and the book lets that be the whole ending, and does not add a sentence explaining it to me. (It came *close* — "the way you stack things over a stain" is a hair louder than it needed to be, and "the one they'd agreed a long time ago not to run again" is the book handing me the backstory in a parenthesis. But it earned both.)

Things I'm now carrying: that she thought the neck would *show*. "Cassie could read the whole night off her skin like a postmark." And that Cassie came in from the cold at exactly that moment and we still don't know what she saw, and Cassie is never, ever going to tell her, because Cassie doesn't trade in information. The one person in this book who could blow it up is the one who won't.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she asked her IS THIS WHAT YOU WANT and the girl went and looked and couldn't see the top of it. and then she offered her cereal. I'm fine. I'm completely fine.*

Also: Randi has standards committee Thursday and has to go *discipline a bad girl*, which is sitting in the room like a loaded gun with a bow on it, and Saturday brunch is on the books, asked for by Vee, out of a ringing neck, by a mouth that got there before any part of her had voted. Two nights ago she bought her way out of Randi and tonight she bought herself Saturday. She's not retreating. She's rationing.
