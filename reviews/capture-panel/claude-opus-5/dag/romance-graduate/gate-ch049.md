REACTION

Okay so this is the first two-pager I've ever been handed and I was braced for it to be a cheat — a page of mood because the author didn't have a chapter. It isn't. It's one image executed and then stopped, and the stopping is the whole thing. Six pages of this would have been a girl feeling sad about a shirt. Two pages of it is the shirt getting taken apart in front of me.

Because *that's* what happened. "And it was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." I actually said something out loud. Four chapters ago she knelt on his bathroom floor and handed him that flannel and said *I need you to wear it for me, the whole weekend,* and I thought it was the most naked thing she'd done all book, and he put it on over the shirt he was already wearing and I melted. And now she's lying in it in the dark doing an inventory — the things he makes with his hands, the rooms he keeps warm, the plate he sets down once he's built the hunger in her — and she can name every single part of him in that cloth and *none of it answers the question.* He answers with objects. He's answered with objects since the lining and the plate over the bowl and the dozens of scrunchies with no note, and I've been charmed by every one of them, and this is the first time somebody held one up to the light and found it empty. The object can't be asked a follow-up question. She finally asked one.

And then she takes it off and lies there bare in a cold room and *stays in it.* Doesn't put it back on. Leaves it in a heap beside her, not folded, not back on the pillow — the ritual undone on purpose. That's the only thing she's done in two chapters that wasn't managing him or managing herself. It's self-harm in a very small key and I believe every inch of it.

The phone. God, the phone. Every book on my Kindle would have had her send that text, and then there'd be ninety pages of the wrong reply. This one has her hold her thumb over the empty field and work out that *there is no message that makes it come clear* — they'd all be answered small and warm and leave her exactly here. She's right. She's completely right, and she knows it in under a minute, and she puts the phone facedown. That's a grown woman's read on her own situation and it's also the trap closing, because knowing that is how she talks herself out of asking again.

What's got me actually sick, though: Cassie is three feet below her breathing slow and easy with the window cracked at her two inches. Vee is naked and awake above her and will not say a word. And this is now the fourth person in this book holding something back out of kindness — she didn't tell Randi what MIRA stood for, Pace won't say the word, Randi says *God, I want her* into the back of a couch where Vee can't hear it, Cassie didn't say *I know.* Everybody in here is being so careful with everybody else that nothing is going to get said until it detonates. And the shirt over the shirt "so Cassie might look and wouldn't have to say anything, a girl too far gone in a boy" — she's costuming for her roommate now. In her own bed.

Still in my stomach: she couldn't eat the nachos, she can't sleep, she's lying in the cold on purpose, and the clothes have been going loose since chapter thirty-something. Nobody has said a word about it. I don't think the book's forgotten. I think it's waiting.

Small ledger items: Thanksgiving happened entirely offscreen. Sheri at his table, the pie she'd pretend she made, the two of them with nowhere else to be — I was promised that in forty-one and I got a cafeteria debrief instead. I noticed. And he still hasn't said one word about the photograph.
