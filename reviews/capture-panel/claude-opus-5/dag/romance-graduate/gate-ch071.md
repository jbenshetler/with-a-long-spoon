REACTION

Okay so the thing I want to text somebody about at 1 a.m. is **"Standards committee — I've got to go discipline a bad girl."** Said martyred, said light, said as the *reason she can't do Thursday.* Thursday. The Thursday. The one I have in my notes as the night she had him. She just cancelled dinner with Vee, to Vee's face, with a groan and a joke, and walked off into the dark on quick heels, and Vee — who is standing there with her neck still ringing — hears nothing but a girl with a committee meeting. And the joke has two floors under it and I can see both and she can see one and Vee can't see any. If that Thursday is a county road and a white truck I am going to put the book down on the table and just sit with it for a minute. And if it's actually Brooke's committee, and the bad girl up for review is the one whose panties the whole room saw on Brooke's own floor — with Randi sitting on the panel — then this book is cheerfully loading two guns at once and I hate how much I want to watch.

The wrist. God. She has handed that woman a Lifesaver every class since the term started and tonight she forgot, which is the small true thing — the ritual went out of her head because her head is full — and then Randi's fingers come in on the *inside of the wrist* on the way to the candy and Vee nearly drops the roll. *Static.* Everyone's skin is thin there. It meant nothing. It went on not being static. That's the whole book in six lines and nobody had to explain it to me, and I noticed I was holding my own breath over a graze, which after seventy-one chapters and a man with a paintbrush is genuinely impressive.

But the move that actually got me is that **she turned her mouth away.** On purpose, not dressed up as an accident, and the book says so. First thing Vee has ever refused Randi. Three volumes of saying yes with no floor under it and the one time she holds a line it's against the thing she wants most, because she wants it that much — *the wanting was the very reason she couldn't let herself take it.* That is a twenty-one-year-old's logic and it is also exactly right and I felt it in my chest. And then Randi — Randi, who is *not sorry at all* — reaches around the refusal and puts her mouth on the hollow of her neck. The exact place. Twice-weaponized, per my notes, and now I know Randi kept count, and I know she told him, and Vee has no idea she's been audited. *"See? How do you like it?"* Delighted. Punitive. A woman getting even for two kisses the other girl doesn't know were logged. That's not flirting, that's a receipt being presented, and the goosebumps went out over me too.

And then Vee's voice, out of the ringing and not out of any thinking part: *Brunch. Let's have brunch this weekend.* She spends the entire chapter building an escape and then, one second after the escape works, buys herself a whole Saturday. I love her so much. She can't stop reaching. She doesn't even get to decide anymore — the mouth goes first and she catches up.

What's underneath all of it now is colder than it was, though. She has a lie in her, the first one, and it's still sitting there, and this chapter is what the lie *costs* in ordinary minutes: the hanger in each hand and not knowing whether she's dressing to be seen or to be covered, the hand flat on her stomach like a lid on a pot, being late on purpose for the first time in her life. She can't even sit down to a dinner anymore. The rot is in the Tuesdays now, not in the dramatic nights.

The reciprocity lecture went right past her and right into me, which I can live with — the book let her slide off it instead of having her think *oh, like us!*, which is the version that would have made me close it. But the chapter is called **Coming Due**, and that's the title doing the elbow-nudge the scene politely declined to do. One notch too helpful. I'd have called it *Static.*

And then Cassie. Of course Cassie. Coat still on, cold still coming off her, worry under it and *a thread of disbelief she didn't trouble to hide* — the one person with no angle asks the one plain question and Vee reaches for the easy lie and **there's nothing there to reach for.** Not "she lied." Not "she told the truth." She opened her mouth and found she didn't know. That's a better cliff than any kiss and it's the first time in seventy-two chapters somebody standing in front of her has asked her for something she can't manufacture.
