REACTION

Okay so this is the debrief and I knew it was coming and I mostly didn't mind it, which surprises me, because I *just* read chapter seventeen and normally being walked back through a scene I was inside of an hour ago makes me start thumbing. Chapter thirteen did this same thing after the mountain and it was two pages and it earned its keep. This one's longer and it's doing something the first one didn't, so fine.

Here's what actually got me: she doesn't tell Cassie the real thing. She tells the bra, the box, the being seen, the grinding — she tells the *funny* version, the version you can hand a friend and both laugh at — and the thing she was actually dying of, the soaked satin and the thumbprint and the minute with her eyes screwed shut waiting to be found out, doesn't come out of her mouth at all. She goes right up to the edge of it — "he was on his knees and looking up and I kept waiting to feel wrong about it" — and then she puts it down. She keeps the center of it. That's the second time in this book someone has kept back the one true piece from the person they're closest to, and the first time it was Pace keeping the froyo line from Randi, and I wrote that down as where this goes wrong. Now Vee's doing it too, without knowing she's doing it. I don't think she's hiding it from Cassie. I think she doesn't have the sentence yet. But the effect is the same: the real thing lives only in her.

And the heat-turned-up beat landing here instead of there was smart. In seventeen it was one clause, a thing she noticed with her skin. Here she has to *say it out loud* to another person — "He turned it up hours before, probably. As soon as he knew I was coming" — and Cassie goes very still and says *Oh, Vee*, and the second-person version of it is worse, in the way that wrecks you. That's the whole trick of this chapter, actually: the retelling isn't redundant because saying a thing to someone else makes it mean more than feeling it did. She interrupts her own narration to go back and put it in. *I have not meant to say this part yet, or maybe not at all.* God.

*Are you sure he's not gay* — I laughed. Out loud, alone, at eleven at night. Because that is exactly the friend logic: two shirts off, zero sex, a tape measure, a man who *sews*, of course that's where Cassie lands. And Vee's answer being not a defense but a brisk clinical pat of her own ass, indicating it on a diagram — and then the actual confession underneath it, *I was grinding on him*, which is the one piece of the afternoon she volunteers that costs her something. She gives Cassie the wickedness and keeps the shame. Tells you everything about what she thinks is safe to be.

But Cassie. Cassie is my whole problem with this book right now and I mean that as the compliment. *You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?* — that's a joke and it's also the only clear-eyed sentence anyone has said in six chapters. She's not warning. She's letting Vee have it. Her hand on Vee's knee, once, and taken back. She *let the pivot happen* — the book names it, she's good at that — which is Cassie choosing, again, not to make the sad version bigger. And she put the highlighter down before Vee got a word out. My notes said Cassie is the currency and she's going to get spent and Vee will let it happen while feeling chosen, and this chapter is the last good day of that friendship and I think I'm supposed to know it. The shirt folded on the pillow at the top of the bunk, in Cassie's room, the whole time they're talking. Vee didn't even decide to put it there.

What I'm suspicious of: nothing new, which is its own thing. No Randi in this chapter, no Pace, no crack widening. The book gave me the biggest chapter it's written and then a soft landing, and I'm ready for that to be over. I want the sewing weeks to *cost* Randi something — he's got a woman's measurements written down in his square hand and he's spending two weeks on a dress for her while his girlfriend leaves right after and doesn't stay, and nobody has said the two things in the same room yet. And *he'll call me when it's ready* means there's a fitting, and a fitting means the box again, and now she knows what she is on that box.
