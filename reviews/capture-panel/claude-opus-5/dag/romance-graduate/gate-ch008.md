REACTION

Oh, this one surprised me. I braced for filler — a pool chapter, no Pace, no Randi, right after that wine-on-the-lap scene where Randi drank and Pace didn't and I was genuinely a little unwell about it — and instead I got the first thing in this book that made me put my hand over my mouth for a reason that had nothing to do with sex.

The walk past the bleachers. God. "You won't need floaties with *those*" and then "Legs for days," and the thing that gutted me wasn't the catcall, it was that she had *already put her face on* before she started walking. Chin level, shoulders open, towel carried down at her side instead of hugged to her chest, "she took it like an entrance." She pre-loaded armor for a fifty-yard walk to a swimming pool. And then Cassie says "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman," low, kept for Vee, not pitched up into the bleachers even though she could have — and Vee laughs way too hard, "fifty yards of held breath going out at once." That's the whole interior life of being a woman with a body in a room, in like forty words, and nobody stopped to explain it to me. This book keeps not explaining things to me and I keep loving it for that.

Then underwater. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." I'm going to be thinking about that line for a while. The room switching off, the scull her hands remember, the light coming down in sheets, "nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything." And she *grieves a little* — for the girl in the green-and-white suit from when she was sixteen and there was less of her. That's the starving the blurb promised, and it turns out it isn't only about wanting men or wanting Randi, it's about having been taken out of her own body and parked up on the deck with the rolled towel. "The body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck." Come ON. That's the best sentence in the chapter and it's the last one.

And now I'm nervous in a new way. Because the person who gets Vee out of that grief isn't Pace and isn't Randi — it's Cassie, from ten feet off, without taking a single step closer, with a cupped-hands splash. Cassie who doesn't ask, because asking makes the sad version bigger. Cassie who said "Goodbye, Vee" like a whole word, flat and finished, and then didn't fix it. Cassie who read Pace's look across the coffee shop perfectly and said so out loud. Cassie who will never know the love of a woman — *who says that*, by the way, Cassie, sweetheart, you said that about the guys in the bleachers and I heard it land somewhere else entirely. She's in a navy suit older than their friendship that does her no favors and it doesn't bother her one bit, which in this book's grammar means she's the only person we've met who isn't performing. Randi has a face. Pace has a method. Cassie has a locker key on a stretchy wristband.

So what I'm suspicious of: this chapter is the control group. It's showing me what Vee's life is actually like, and what love without an agenda looks like, right before Pace calls. It's the book laying a cost on the table so I can watch it get spent. Randi and Pace have "planned the best year of Vivienne Thorne's life," and I now think the price of that year is the girl standing in the shallow end like a person, arms along the surface like a woman leaning on a fence, who isn't invited to Chi Latte and declines without a sulk.

No heat, no Pace, didn't miss him. That's a weird thing for me to say about a book I bought for the bench.
