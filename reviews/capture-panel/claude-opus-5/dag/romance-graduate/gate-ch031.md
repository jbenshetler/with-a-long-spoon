REACTION

Oh, she did it to herself. The whole book has been building a room that turns to look at her and then it handed her a warm dull kind room full of somebody's parents and navy dresses and nobody watching anybody longer than the walking took — and she had to *make* the turn happen. The second step. I actually said something out loud at "She took a breath, and made the second step long." That's the best sentence this book has given me and it's five words of nothing. Six weeks ago that girl kept her hand over her mouth when she laughed. Now she's bare under four hundred dollars of silk in a departmental ceremony and she chooses, mid-stride, with her own body, to give the room her leg on purpose and let it travel down the rows. Nobody dressed her for that part. Randi did the shoes, Pace built the dress, and the long step was hers.

And then — *nobody came.* Not her mother, not Pace, not Randi. I have been bracing for that induction like a car crash for ten chapters: the gown, the mother, the slit, the shoes she can't afford, the whole pin. And the book just... emptied the room. Flight cost more than they had and the bright voice her mother keeps for things she can't give her, which gutted me in one line. Pace in Berkeley, sorry in the quiet way with no performance in it, missing the wearing the dress was built for. I came in expecting to be flayed and instead got the cleanest thing that's happened to her all book: she was alone in there with it, and she didn't shrink, and the only witness she wanted afterward was Cassie.

CASSIE. "You look—" and didn't finish it, which from Cassie was a standing ovation. Taking the phone out of her hand to hold. Eyes gone bright at the corners *which she'd deny to the grave.* "Somebody had to document it." "Come on, streetlight." I'm not okay. Cassie has been quietly losing a bidding war to a woman who cares about nothing but clothes, and the one day it counted Cassie walked her to the door, held her phone, took the picture, and bought her a terrible sandwich. If this book hurts Cassie I'm going to be insufferable about it for a week, and I've said that before, and I meant it more this time.

But the thing that actually made me sit up — the thing I'd text at 1 a.m. in all caps — is the photograph. She sends Pace the whole frame, uncropped, and she *knows to the heartbeat what second it is*, and "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." That's the first time in thirty-one chapters Vee has kept something. She's been routing her entire interior to Randi and her entire body to Pace and now she has a private room with one thing in it, and the thing is her own appetite. She didn't tell Randi either. The book didn't even mention Randi in the aftermath, which I noticed like a missing tooth. Vee walked offstage with a secret and went to get a sandwich with her roommate. Good. *Good.* That's her first dollar of actual equity.

What I'm suspicious of: Pace is gone three days. Which means Randi is loose for three days with a girl who just discovered what being looked at does to her and has a photo on her phone she already knows is dirty. Saturday is coming. The book put him on a plane for a reason.

Also — the dress worked. Not "looked nice." It did what it was built to do: he put the doubled oyster lining over the exact two places she's braced her whole life, and she walked into a public room with nothing under it. That's the lining being load-bearing. He armored her and then wasn't there to see her use it, and she sent him the picture anyway. I'm a little in love with how little that chapter had to say about any of it.

One wobble: the last line explains her to me. Pride, or the same heat, or whether by now they're the same thing — I had already gotten there on the long step, and I didn't need the book to put a hand on my arm about it. That's twice now it's reached for a closing summary of the thing it just did perfectly. Trim that and this chapter is airtight.
