REACTION

*Fed.* The title's doing work and I clocked it before I was three paragraphs in, because Randi opens by announcing she's starving — "I'm running on fumes here" — and then sits in the good light and eats an hour of Vee describing her boyfriend in bed. She *asked for it.* "How was he?" She built the table, pointed at the girl, and now she's sitting across from her asking for the report and calling it living vicariously. And Vee gives it: patient, gentle, hungry, *singular.* Unique. Not just tonight. To the woman who has him. Who has never once, in twenty-two chapters, told Vee she has him.

And the second fabricated man! I caught it instantly and that's the pleasure and the problem both. Last time it was the tall one dressed like money with the lacrosse numbers, invented so Vee would never wonder whether Randi had somebody. This time it's a beautiful ex who was fast and nervous and over in a hurry — invented, I'm certain, and invented *downward*, because she needs her own night to be smaller than Vee's. She needs to be the poor thing running on fumes so Vee will feel safe pouring. That's not vanity, that's the oldest move there is: make yourself the hungry one and the other person will feed you. And she got her whole meal.

The two beats I'll keep: the polish and the sheet. The polish is beautiful — she picked frosted plum in an Ulta aisle to wake up a plum that lives inside a burgundy silk she's seen exactly once, and she cannot say that out loud, so it comes up in her face, and Randi reads the face and says *that face isn't about a color, is it.* Got the shape without the content. Again. That's the third or fourth time this woman has taken the outline of Vee's secret and left the secret in her. And then Vee's one-word *no*, and the thread of fear showing before she could catch it, and Randi's hand coming down over the painted one: **"You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky."** Cold as the bottom of a lake, delivered warm, and *true*, which is what makes it unanswerable. She sealed the only crack Vee's had in two chapters, in one sentence, with love.

The sheet: Vee getting halfway to seeing herself from the outside — a girl in a man's sheet with nothing under it at midnight — and Randi *getting there first* and framing it for her. "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." She's curating what Vee is allowed to remember. Hanging it on the wall so Vee can't turn it over. Absolutely sinister and also I'd have said it too, because it *is* the best thing in the chapter — the oil ticking, the black window, neither of them talking, nothing missing.

Then "you keep saying *he*, like you just stood there and let it happen to you," and the toes, and the bought underwear — *that's not nothing, that's a campaign* — and the landing: **"I wanted it. And I took it. For once."** *Good.* Hand over hand, left there. That's the whole novel in four lines. This woman is walking Vee out of her mother's voice and into her own appetite and it is genuinely the kindest thing anybody has done for her and it is also the exact machine that eats her. I have never been this unable to root for or against somebody.

What I'm less happy about. Twenty-one was the Cassie debrief of Friday night. Twenty-two is the Randi debrief of Friday night. Back to back, same night, second table — and the book *quotes Cassie's lines into this scene* so I can compare, which is either confident or nervous, I genuinely can't tell. I said in my notes that if the next chapter was her telling somebody across a table about the night I'd just read, I'd skim, and I did skim, a little, through the recap of the fra diavolo, because I was there. And there's a paragraph near the end that flatly explains Randi to me — the person you got to *tell*, the friend she'd wanted at fifteen — after a whole scene that had already shown me that better than the summary does. I don't need the book's hand on the back of my neck.

Also nobody paid. Nineteen made an entire quiet monument out of Vee buying her own tomato soup, and here she drinks a second mimosa she doesn't price and the check never comes. I don't think that's sloppiness, I think it's the slippage — she's stopped counting, and that's the first thing Randi's world took off her. But I want the book to know it did that.

What I actually want, still, and didn't get: Randi in a room by herself. Pace anywhere. A folded green pair of panties in a house she has a key to. She fed this chapter and she did not break, and I'm starting to suspect the book knows I'm waiting and is making me wait, which is fine, but the waiting room has three tables in it now.
