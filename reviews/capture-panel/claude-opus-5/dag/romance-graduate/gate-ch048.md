REACTION

Oh, this one got me. Two pages and it got me.

I've been waiting the whole book for her to do something in private that wasn't a confession, wasn't a performance for Randi or a step down a runway or a photograph composed by someone else's eye, and here it is: a girl alone on a top bunk at 3 a.m. doing the one thing nobody taught her, which is sitting in it. The empty text field is the best image in the chapter. She doesn't send a stupid message. I braced for the manufactured-conflict text — this genre has trained me to flinch at a thumb hovering over a keyboard — and instead she *looks at the lit rectangle until it's only a lit shape* and puts it face down. There's no misunderstanding to be engineered. There's no answer that can come back as the thing she needs. That's a grown woman's realization in a twenty-one-year-old's body and the book earned it.

And then the shirt. God, the shirt. Chapter 44 she handed it to him to fill, and he wore two shirts in his own kitchen for her, and it was the single warmest thing in the book, and now that same shirt is the thing that fails her. The move from *over her own shirt, so it hides what it is* — worn for the hall, for Cassie's eyes, deniable — to pulling both off and putting his straight on her skin because she needs it closer. And it works, for a while. It always works. And then the knife: *it was warm because she was warm.* The heat is her own, given back. He isn't in it. She's been holding a smell in the dark. That's the whole relationship in one sentence and the book doesn't explain it to me, which after forty-seven chapters I have learned to be grateful for.

Then she takes it off and she doesn't put it back on. That's the beat I'll carry. Naked in a cold room on purpose, choosing worse over comfort she's decided is a lie, and the shirt left in a heap where a dropped thing lands — not folded, not on the pillow, where she'd have put it any night in the last two months. That's a woman beginning to stop. Not a decision. The body making one ahead of her, the way her body always does in this book — she still can't name a thing but her hands know.

What's scaring me is the trajectory. Ch 46 she said her one good sentence and he gave her *I love how good we are together* and she filed it. Ch 47 she got everything but the word and lay awake. Ch 48 the last comfort object has gone hollow and she's lying bare in the cold. Three chapters of the same wound and the author knows it's the same wound — she's escalating the *object*, not the complaint, which is why it isn't repetitive. But I'll be honest: I'm one more sleepless-dorm-ceiling chapter from restless. The ache needs a door now. Randi's in it, the jacket promised me she's in it, and Vee's lying there with every breath of him and a silence at the center — the person she should be reaching for in that moment is the woman who said *you're not greedy, you're finally hungry out loud.* The fact that she doesn't even think of Randi here is either the most disciplined thing in the chapter or the author holding the fuse.

And Cassie. *Below her Cassie breathed on, slow and easy and asleep.* The window still cracked its two inches. Vee stripping naked three feet above a sleeping girl who has clocked every single thing about her all semester and never once said *I know.* I hate how loaded that bunk is now. She's wearing a man's shirt specifically so Cassie won't have to say anything about it, and that's the saddest piece of care in the book, going the wrong direction — Vee managing Cassie's comfort instead of asking her for a single thing.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she took the shirt off and left it on the floor and lay there naked in a cold room on purpose and I'm not okay.* Two pages. I didn't think two pages could do that. I came in suspicious of the short chapter as a shortcut and this one's a held breath, which is the only thing that length is actually good for.
