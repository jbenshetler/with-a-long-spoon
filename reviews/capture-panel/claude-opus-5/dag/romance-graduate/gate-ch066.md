REACTION

Two pages. Two pages and I'm sitting here with my hand over my mouth.

I have been asking for Cassie for thirty chapters. I wrote in my own notes that the book keeps setting her a place and not seating her, and that I'd stopped being impatient and started being sad about it. And this is what she gets — not a confrontation, not the reckoning I was bracing for, no *you lied to me about a sweater*, no *I'm worried about you*. She gets four sentences and she wins the whole volume with them.

*Is this what you want?* God. Nobody has asked Vee that. Not once. Randi asks her *did you take it all* and *how does he act* and *tell me I'm wrong* — questions with the answer already folded inside them, doors built and held open. Pace asks *will you* and *do you want to sleep with a woman* and then steps around the name like a gentleman around a puddle, which I clocked at the time as courtesy and am now rereading as architecture. Cassie asked the one question with no shape in it. No exits. No floor. Just: *is this what you want.* And the most frightening sentence in the chapter is that Vee **actually went and looked** — "because Cassie had asked it so simply it seemed like a thing a person would just know about herself." She reached, she came up against the edge of something close and large, and she stepped back off before she could make out its size. That's the same four inches of air from the dark room. Same reach, same stop. The book is going to keep handing her that distance until somebody closes it and I'm now almost certain it won't be her.

And then she did the shabby thing. She grabbed the plank — *you don't like her, you've never liked any of it* — the oldest argument, the one they agreed not to run. Deliberately laid it on the floor to walk out on. And Cassie **stepped over it without looking down.** That image is going to live in me. Vee has gotten so good at swinging the weight off herself and onto someone else — she did it to Pace Saturday night, *you're the one who wants this, you want Randi* — and it worked on him, he actually defended himself, and she read that as the sweetest thing he'd ever done. It did not work on Cassie. Cassie is the only person in this book who will not be managed. That's why nobody tells her anything.

*You didn't have dinner.* Not a question and not quite a change of subject. Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles. I want to say something snide about how a box of cereal is doing the work of an intervention but I can't, because it's exactly right and it undid me. Cassie watched her roommate come in with her neck ringing and a lie half-stacked in her mouth, decided the big question wasn't going to get answered tonight, and offered her the only thing she could actually accept — *a want the size of a bowl.* That's love administered at the dose the patient can tolerate. Everybody else in this girl's life gives her more than she can hold and calls it generosity. Cassie gave her less, on purpose.

And it's *hers.* "Some of my." Cassie's two inches of window, Cassie's cereal, Cassie's lamp — Vee has been spending this girl's small possessions as cover for a month, shutting the window to have a private hour and throwing it open again to air out the evidence, and tonight Cassie handed over the box unasked. I don't think the book is being cute about that. I think it knows exactly what it just did.

The other thing I noticed: she still doesn't know what Cassie saw. The chapter opens inside that — how close did she pass, how long did the hug look from a few feet off, did she hear the sound. Vee is now keeping books on four people and the ledgers don't reconcile, and the panic isn't about love, it's about *coverage.* And the line that actually scared me is "it seemed to her, absurdly, that it must show — that Cassie could read the whole night off her skin like a postmark." She isn't afraid Cassie will be hurt. She's afraid Cassie will *know.* Brooke's rule, running inside Vee's own chest now: the act is survivable, the word is what kills you. Nobody had to teach it to her. She picked it up off the floor of the house.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the roommate offered her cereal and I cried. two pages. TWO PAGES.* And then immediately: *she asked the only real question in 70 chapters and got lied to and didn't even make her pay for it, I'm unwell.*

I came into this chapter braced for a short one to feel like a shortcut and it didn't. It felt like the book finally letting the quietest person in it speak at her own volume. Brunch is Saturday, Randi's off disciplining a bad girl Thursday, and there is now one person in Vee's life who has the question on the table and the patience to leave it there. Go.
