REACTION

Okay so they ran the same hour twice and I'm not over it. Randi in Gstaad warm only along one seam, carrying her own cold into a faultless room; Vee in a twin bed her legs outgrew, warm only where a shirt gave her back her own heat two chapters ago. Same night. Same clock. Same text thread — and *both of them read their own message twice for anything that gave them away before sending it.* Randi: *every word of it true, none of it the thing.* Vee: *she read it twice for anything that gave her away, found nothing.* Two women lying to each other in matching tenderness about the exact same wound. I'd have texted a friend a screenshot of just those two lines side by side with no comment.

And the one Vee's keeping is MIRA. She never said it, not then and not after, and the book just quietly confirmed she's been sitting on it the whole break — and then in the same breath she envies the chalet *knowing whose money bought it.* "Even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had." That's such an ugly, honest, twenty-one-year-old sentence. She's not above it. She's just broke and alone in lavender plugin air.

The room. God, the room. The ribbons in their bunting, the little gold synchro girls frozen mid-lift with their toes pointed — *of course* she was a synchronized swimmer before she was a lap swimmer, of course the body that's now the treasured thing used to be a thing that got scored. The clarinet in the closet since senior year. The faces on the corkboard who haven't texted in a year. "The room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." I have been in that room. Everybody's been in that room. And then — "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone." I let that one sit.

The cropping is the knife. She sent Pace all of it and took the scissors to her own leg for her mother, *the exact thing that had made her feel like herself* — "same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it." She's been doing surgery on herself for everybody in her life and she's just now noticing she holds the scissors. Her dad pricing the dress. Her mother touching her own collarbone, then Vee's, with the nurse face on, asking if she's making herself sick. (Which — hello, Randi's one neat bite and black coffee. This book keeps putting food next to love next to withholding and never once explains it to me, and I'm so grateful.)

But the thing that actually got me, the move I didn't see coming: she reaches for her best memory to get warm — the top sheet, the fra diavolo, the garlic in the oil, the sheet slipping and him seeing and her not fixing it — and *it turns on her inside the memory.* The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, and the blonde she invented out of spite sits down in Vee's own chair in the wine light. She can't keep her own night. That is the single most accurate rendering of 11:52 p.m. jealousy I've read in a spicy book. It isn't a misunderstanding plot. It's a woman losing custody of her own good memory. And then the bartender's lines and Randi's *he leaves you the room* going straight through her hands, because reassurance only loads if you know you're the one — "the floor was gone." Yes. That's it. That's exactly how that works.

And *he leaves you the room* flipped from virtue to abandonment in one clause: "a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That's the whole indictment of Pace in eight words. His restraint is indistinguishable from indifference from the inside, and he will never know that, because he hands people the authorship and then calls their silence consent.

Also noted, with my hackles up: he said nothing about Christmas. A whole week she can't see into. He cooked for Sheri because *she's alone* — and Randi was alone in Gstaad with her parents and a stranger in white tie, so that's not where he was. The book is being very careful about that week and I don't trust careful.

The ending re-ordered the triangle on me and it's the first time it's happened from Vee's side. She's got a date circled for Randi — term starts, a class, a face that lights up and calls her gorgeous — and *nothing at all* for Pace. No hour she can name, no room he'll surely be in. The man gets the void and the woman gets a Tuesday at seven. Then *and what about Pace?* and nothing comes after it, and she falls asleep still reaching. That's the right place to end a year.

What I'm less happy about: this is the fourth time I've been walked around the same no-name lap. 45 in his bed, 46 with the shirt, 47 at the door, now 49 in Ohio. Each one is beautifully done and each one costs a little less than the last. The paragraph re-litigating the fight — "she turned it over and it would not come out even" — I already had. The beige-days inventory went on a beat past where I needed it. I don't need another chapter of Vee discovering she has no word; I need somebody to hand her one, or to take something away.
