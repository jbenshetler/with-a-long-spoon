REACTION

Oh, the *title*. Cropped. She cropped it for her mother and the version they have stops at the waist, and the version Randi has doesn't stop anywhere, and I had to sit up in bed about that. Her mother gets less of her than her best friend does. Her mother gets less of her than the uncropped one she made such a production of being brave enough to hand across a brunch table. And I don't think Vee has noticed she ranked them. That's the whole book in one gesture and nobody said a word about it, which is the only way I can stand it being said.

HE MADE THE DRESS. He *sewed* it, on a machine on his sun porch, because she mentioned once — once — a dress in a magazine she couldn't afford. I have been carrying that burgundy dress around for ten chapters as Randi's heels and Randi's scarf and the second step long, and it turns out the thing she gave the room her whole leg in came off a man's sewing machine in the woods. And she has never told a living soul. She told me before she told her father. And then: *my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it.* That's the funniest and the saddest line in forty-five chapters and I'd text it to someone at 1 a.m. with no context at all.

But the thing that actually got me — *I told them he was my boyfriend. I think I put a question mark on it. Out loud.* She doesn't know what she is to him. She has a drawer, she has a dress he built with his hands, she has whole nights of sleep she didn't know she had in her, and she can't say the noun in her parents' kitchen without her voice tipping up at the end asking permission. And he has still never said the word. Cassie asks *so what is he, to you* and Vee has nothing that fits in the frame. Of course she doesn't. Nobody's ever told her what shape she's standing in.

And then: *Cassie didn't push at it. Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not.* There it is. That's the same ache as five nights ago when some unreasonable corner of her wanted him to fight for the nights and he just held the door open instead. Everybody in this girl's life is being so beautifully, carefully gentle with her that she is starving for one person to grab her wrist. Pace doesn't ask. Cassie doesn't push. Randi — Randi doesn't push either, and Randi is the only one who gets everything, and now I understand *why* she gets everything. Not pushing is the lever. Randi figured that out and the other two came by it honestly. The book has been showing me the same gun three ways and this little two-page chapter is where it finally pointed at me.

Two pages, and it paid for itself. I've never read a chapter this short and I half expected to feel short-changed; instead it felt like a photograph. Also, quietly: the mother. She cried on the phone and cried again at the table and printed it out and carried it in her purse and took it out for people who'd already seen it — and still nobody has said out loud why she wasn't in that room. I'll accept that it's a plane ticket. I notice the book won't just say so.

What I'm suspicious of now: Thanksgiving happened off-page with Sheri at his table in his house with a pie she'll pretend she made, and Vee was fifteen hundred miles away being proud of a cropped photograph. The book skipped that day. Books don't skip days.
