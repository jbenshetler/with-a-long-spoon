REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where the book hands me the knife and makes me hold it, and I'm not entirely sure I forgive it.

Three chapters of Randi's interior — the face, the bench, the deadbolt going over in her chest — and now I'm inside Vee, and she's *lovely*. That's the cruelty. She's not a dim ingénue. She reads a room in granular detail, she clocks the sage quarter-zips and the mauve one and exactly where she sits in the hierarchy, she has the cardigan-over-the-better-shirt calculus down to a science, she has her mother's voice installed in her mouth calling girls floozies and then a half-beat too long spent looking at the sprinter's stomach. That's real interiority. She's got the same architecture Randi has — a managed surface, a shame engine, a thing under it that wants — and she has no idea she's being handled by a professional.

And the *handling*. God. Randi comes in the back door so Vee can't see her arrive. She takes the aisle seat so Vee is boxed between her and Cassie. She turns the full wattage on Cassie too — total, undimmed — which is exactly what makes it invisible, because a woman who only shone at Vee would be suspicious, and a woman who shines at everyone is just *warm*. "I'm going to copy off you all semester" gives Vee an office, a reason to be needed. "I've decided" removes the burden of choosing. And *love you, girl* — the sorority nothing-phrase that costs Randi absolutely nothing and lands in Vee like a key in a lock. I have read hundreds of seductions and I have never watched one done this way, in daylight, with a latte, where every single move is something a nice girl would do.

The blank transcript wrecked me a little. "She had not been keeping the words. She had been keeping the feeling." That's so true it's almost unfair — I have had that hour, that friendship-crush hour with a girl who was brighter than me, and I came away with nothing but the glow. To watch it used as a *mechanism* is genuinely sinister.

And Cassie. CASSIE. "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word, flat and finished. Vee smooths it over, calls her weirdo, forgets it by the time Randi's holding the row open — and I did not forget it. That's the best beat in the chapter and it's four syllables. Cassie saw something. The ball cap, the four words a day, the thing that passed over her face that Vee didn't try to name — she read Randi in under ninety seconds and declined to get in the car. And she said goodbye because she knew she was losing her friend today. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the quiet girl in the ball cap is the only person in this book with functioning instincts and she just left.*

What I'm suspicious of: nothing, which is the problem. There's no plot twist coming that I don't already know. The jacket told me and chapters one through three showed me, and this chapter is the machine engaging. My dread is structural now, not curious. Which means the book has to keep being *this good sentence-by-sentence* to keep me, because I can't be surprised anymore — only implicated.

The one thing I'll note: this chapter had no heat in it. Not a complaint, exactly — the want is there, "flat helpless want," and Vee not looking straight at either fact about her own body is doing real work. But it's the first chapter that didn't touch me physically at all, and I felt the temperature drop. It read as a bridge, a beautifully built one, and I know what the shape means: the next one had better move. I also notice I now have two chapters in a row without Pace in a room with anyone, and he's the one I have the most unanswered questions about — Daphne, the door he didn't respect, why he needs a woman's *real* please so badly. Randi's project has momentum. His has gone quiet.

Still. I'm not putting it down. I want the stats class where Vee and Randi and Pace are all in the same air, and I want to know what Vee does the first time somebody sees her the way Randi got seen.
