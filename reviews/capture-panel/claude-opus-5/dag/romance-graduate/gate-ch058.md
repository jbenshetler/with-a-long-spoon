REACTION

Three warm chapters in a row now and I clocked it — but I didn't put the book down, because this one is doing something sneaky and I only caught it on the second pass.

She told the truth. That's what got me. Cassie got a *shrunken cranberry sweater* — an entire invented laundry accident with dye bleed and everything, delivered with the "eep" and the hands going — and Kayla and Meg get "I flashed him on his front porch," flat, into a loud room, while she sets the flute down. Same weekend, two completely different women telling it. She's been cropping Pace for her father, her mother, Cassie, and now she's cropping *herself*, and the cuts aren't about modesty at all — she gave the dirtiest version to the girls who'd scream about it and the clean lie to the one who'd look at her a beat too long. Cassie would have asked the next question. That's the whole reason.

And then the line that actually made me sit up: *she never had to say one word about the drive.* Kayla's riffing about socks and underwear for three sentences and Vee just lets her run — because the drive is the part with her hand in her lap on a county road and a cruiser's lights coming up behind her and the mark she left on the lining of her good coat. That stays hers. Fine, I respect it. But then she gives them "we talked all night. We're good, we're better than we were" — and they did not talk. They did not talk at *all*. He never said the word, he said *I'm sorry, I never meant to hurt you, I missed you so much,* and she was so full of pancakes and relief that there was no room in her for wanting what he hadn't given. She just told a table of people she got the conversation. She's started doing to her friends exactly what he does to her: hand over the true-shaped thing that isn't the thing.

Meg's maple bacon landed like a dropped glass, too. *Sometimes you have to make it right in their language* — said kindly, said by a woman who was a hag for five days and bought a dozen donuts, and it files Vee's December under "I was awful and I made it up to him." She asked that man what she was to him. She was *right*. And now her best read on it, from the outside, is an apology. Nobody in Vee's life has the shape of this. Nobody. The one person who could give it to her is Randi, who has her own reasons.

Also: she asked "it's a little expensive, is that okay?" before anybody got in the car, and then at the end they split it three ways on the little terminal with nobody letting anybody cover anybody, out loud about the tip buttons. After fifty-some chapters of paying her own check at Randi's places and never saying the number. This is the one table where money is just money, and the book put it right there without a word about it.

But the actual reason my thumb is already moving: **Wednesday.** Pace is handling it. Pace, whose nervous system came apart over nachos under four screens, is choosing a venue for eight loud college kids — "tables have ends, somebody ends up at one" — and he asked for *Theo* by name, and Cassie's coming, and **Randi is coming.** Randi and Pace at the same table with Vee in the middle, performing near-strangers who said nine words about math at a coffee shop. Randi who said *God, I want her* into the wood. Randi who heard about the coat because it was her idea and her shoes. Fifty-nine chapters and the three of them have never been in a room. I have been waiting for this since the pointing game, and now it's got a *date* on it, and Vee's walking into it beaming because her two worlds finally get to find out about each other.

She has no idea they already have.
