REACTION

She lied. Out loud, fluently, with *jokes in it* — the wool, the hot water, the dye going into the good pants, the whole cranberry sweater that does not exist — and she built it in about four seconds flat under the one question she couldn't answer. That's what this chapter is and I don't think the book thinks it's a small thing either, because it put that one sentence at the bottom of the laugh: *the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody.* She didn't crop this time. Cropping is what she does to her mother. This was manufacture. And she was *good* at it, which is the part that made my stomach turn over a little, the man-reporting-from-the-front bit dropped in as garnish on a fabrication.

And Cassie knew. *A silence about the length of a held breath.* Cassie sat there with her feet folded under her and did the arithmetic — the girl came home Sunday night in a man's sweats and going-out heels and a coat, and now the sweater, sure, the sweater — and then she put her head back and gave Vee the full laugh, the rare one she won't spend on her own jokes. She chose it. She bought the story at full price in front of the witness so her friend wouldn't have to stand there holding it. I have been grinding my teeth about Cassie's window for twenty chapters and this is the widest it's been open and Vee *still* doesn't know what she's being given.

But then the card. Oh, the card. Vee sent Cassie's mother a card and never mentioned it to anybody including me, and Cassie's mother wants to know when she gets to see her again, and Vee's hand stopped halfway to the towel and went to her collar instead. Closed her robe. Over *that*. Because a woman she's met once wants her at a table for no reason and with no condition attached and Vee's whole body doesn't know where to put it — *warm and specific, like a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck* — and she gave back "tell her I said hi," which was, as the book says, not the size of it at all. Fifty-eight chapters of this girl being managed and listened to and handled with exquisite non-coercion by everyone who wants something off her, and the thing that nearly undid her was Cassie's mother saving boxes and asking after her. That's the first door in this book nobody staged.

The other thing I'll keep: the shower. Three conditioners on the little ledge, the toilet an arm's reach past the curtain, the polite trickle — *less pressure, less private, less hers* — and she was happy in it the whole time. That's the first time she's come back from out there and the comparison hasn't cost her something. Last time she came back to this room she lay naked on purpose in the cold with his shirt in a heap on the floor. Now she stands in a draft on purpose because she missed the draft. I'll take it.

What I'm uneasy about, and it's specific: *"We talked. Friday night. I drove out there and we — Cass, we* talked*."* They did not talk. She opened a coat. He went to his knees on a freezing porch and said sorry and said he missed her and never once said the word she drove out there over, and the next morning she decided there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given. And now she's narrating it as a conversation, to her roommate, in good faith. That's not a lie she told Cassie. That's the one she's telling herself, and it's gone onto the pile with the rest, and I remember what the pile has done to her every single time. Also — and I notice she's done it again — she left a bag in a car that never had a bag in it: *"There wasn't anything important in it anyway."* She's lying in reflex now, in small denominations, to nobody.

And still, fifty-eight chapters in, not one syllable about the weight. The girl who's been eating like she's starving in one house and nothing in the other, whose clothes have been going loose since autumn, sat on a bean bag in a robe tonight in front of the one person who looks at her properly, and nobody said anything. I'm not going to stop noting it.

The ending got me. Bunk one girl wide, arm out across the covers, cold painted cinderblock a hand's width off, and she left her hand there. No pang, no begging, just a wall that's only a wall because he's out there in the wide bed and Friday's coming. That's the right note to end a nothing chapter on. I'd text a friend: *she invented an entire sweater and her roommate laughed on purpose and I'm not okay.*
