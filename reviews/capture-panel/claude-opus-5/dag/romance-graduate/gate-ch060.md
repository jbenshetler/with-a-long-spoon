REACTION

Okay so I need to say the structural thing first because it's been sitting on my chest for three chapters: the author put chapter 61 — Pace in Randi's bed, holding her open above him, *you liked it*, *she kisses me first now*, two people passing a girl's name back and forth like a plate — and then immediately handed me the taproom and the hangover. The two warmest chapters in the volume. That is not an accident. That's a man being bought amnesty with a cinnamon raisin loaf, and it's the *second* time in ten chapters, and the worst part is it works. I was fully in his chest by the time she came around that counter. I'd be angrier about it if I didn't think the book knows exactly what it's doing to me.

Because it did land. God. *My one friend. There isn't anyone else.* He said it plainly, flat, no door left for himself, in a kitchen, in rolled sleeves. Fifty-some chapters of a man who won't spend a syllable and he spends that one like it's a weather report. And the reason — both reasons — are the most honest accounting of a male life I've gotten out of a romance in years. *Men make friends by competing. The window opens once. That door's on a timer.* I have never once seen that written down and it's true and it's devastating and he delivers it like a derailleur spec. And then *the ones already there, when I was small among them, either weren't safe or weren't welcoming.* No name. The book didn't give me a name. Thank you. Sheri gave me *went bad with his daddy, hasn't been home since he was sixteen* last chapter and *less than staying* is already a rock in me from a black window in January, and this chapter just set the third stone down and let me do the geometry. That's the good version of this book.

And then it did the bad version for about four paragraphs. The whole *she set Pace down at eighteen beside all of it* passage — the cafeteria trays, the tables you fought your way onto, freshman English at nine on no sleep, the forty to a dorm floor — I had already done that math. I did it the second he said *I went to college at fifteen*, which, by the way, I've known for two volumes. The book stood there and showed me its work and then showed me its work again, and the line that actually mattered (*he would not have sat in a single class with one person his own age*) was buried in the middle of an essay I didn't need. My eye slid. This book's one standing sin is explaining its own subtext and it relapsed here, and it relapsed in the exact chapter where it had otherwise shown the most restraint.

But Daphne. *Daphne.* Four volumes' worth of crater and the first actual light got into it here, from the only angle that could do it: not what she did to him, but what she *was* to him — all four of Vee's people in one person, in a building with nobody his age in it, and then she went, *sensibly*, and there was no one left on earth to bring the heartbreak to. And Vee holding both versions stacked and unreconciling, *the best two years he'd had* and *the ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue and that she kept pressing back down*. She still won't say it. She's never going to say it. But she looked at it head-on and it hurt to hold, and that's the closest this book has come to naming what happened to him, and it did it without naming it. That's the trick I came for.

What nobody in that kitchen clocked, and what I'll be chewing on all week: his one friend on earth is a woman who can never want him. *The only kind, she was sure now, the world had ever quite let him keep.* And Vee believes that sentence completely. And it isn't true, because there's a second person who knows him, and she's not safe, and she wants him, and she wants *Vee*, and she was in his bed on Thursday saying *she's not the only one in love.* Vee asked *I'd love to meet your other friends* and got a true answer to a question that has a hole in it the size of a redhead. He didn't lie. He never lies. He just lives in a house where the true answer is a lie, which is the whole man.

And now Tuesday. Vee is going to walk into that booth and lay Sheri on the tablecloth — five foot nothing, two colors of hair, *gay, so I don't have to be jealous* — and hand Randi the win with both hands, and Randi is going to have to sit there and be *surprised.* Randi, who had the sentence *I've met Sheri, she's five foot nothing with two-tone hair* in her pocket in January and swallowed it because spending it meant explaining how she knew. Vee is about to say that exact sentence *to her*, cheerfully, as a gift. I actually sat up. That's the best-loaded scene the book has set in thirty chapters and it loaded it by accident, in a hangover, over eggs.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he put the water glass on a folded paper towel so it wouldn't mark the wood.* That. And *she got the hug she gave him from Sheri, three steps ago, and used it.* That's hau. That's Marsh's whole lecture, performed in a kitchen by a girl who got a hug two nights ago and gave it back to the man it belonged to in the first place. The book didn't tell me that one. It let me have it.
