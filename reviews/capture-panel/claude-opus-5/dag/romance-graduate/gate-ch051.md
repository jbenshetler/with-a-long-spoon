REACTION

Oh, you absolute *bastard* of a book. Fifty chapters I waited for Vee to walk out of that house into the cold and the very next thing you hand me is a different woman on a different continent in a dress the color of a glacier. And I wasn't even angry for a full page, because this is the best chapter Randi has ever had and possibly the best chapter in the book.

The breakfast. *Eating her revulsion.* That's the first time she's ever said what she thinks of him in a word I can hold, and it's not *cheating bastards* aimed near-but-not-at, it's point blank at her own father over steak and eggs. The mother "all edges and points against her" — I felt that hug in my own ribs. The label on the belt. Central Florida, *and did not mention it.* So it's new money and she knows exactly how new it is and she's spent her life sanding the accent off it, which is the same skill set she uses on everything else. And she invents boys for him. *The invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table.* Always. Since she was a girl. She's been running a cover story at that table since before she could drive, and now I understand where the technique came from — she didn't develop it for Vee, she was *raised* in it.

And then the thing I can't put down: **the plain white-gold bracelet, locked on her wrist, kept out of the light.** It *never comes off.* It catches on the heel of her hand when John turns her out. The book names it three times in one chapter and explains it exactly zero times, and after fifty chapters of this author I know precisely what that means. Her mother's tennis bracelet plays with the light and Randi hides hers. I have theories and all of them make my stomach drop. Is it his? Is it older than him? Is it the one object in her life she didn't choose and won't take off?

John. God, John was *fun*, and I resent how fun he was. The fake marriage in the lift line, three children at ski camp, he gives the number and she gives the names and neither of them misses a beat — that's two professional liars recognizing each other and having the time of their lives. The hand under the elbow she didn't need and took anyway. And then the run, and the whole paragraph that made me sit up: he was better, and she knew it not because he passed her but because *he had the run to spare and spent none of it proving it.* That's the first man in this book who isn't Pace and he's not a strawman, he's genuinely wonderful, which is the only way this chapter works.

And then the dance floor did the thing and I actually said *oh* out loud. His lead is firm and unmistakable and her body could follow it in her sleep. *Pace's lead was the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for and good enough to catch, and it left the rest of the way to her.* THAT'S THE BOOK. That's the entire architecture of this man in one sentence about a waltz — the pressure so light you have to be the kind of person who can feel it, and the rest of the way is yours. Same as the doors he opens and waits behind. Same as *she consents every time.* And she thinks tonight she's done with the work, easier is better, and I knew from the word *easier* exactly how the night was going to end.

*Pace had never assumed anything in his life. Pace made her say it, out loud, every time. She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked.* And four paragraphs later she's lying in a perfect room with a perfect man going under beside her, waiting to see what the holding would develop into, and **nothing developed.** The good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon. I have read ten thousand sex scenes and I don't think I've ever read one deliberately drained like that, where the body does everything right and the person isn't in the room — the ring of her own lipstick left on him *where she had felt her own mouth do it from a long way off.* And then the mirror: the mouth gone, the face thinned, **"The eyeliner had held. Of course it had. Nothing had happened to her eyes."** I'm not over that line. It's the funniest and bleakest thing in the chapter at once.

And the cold. The cold is the *shirt*. Two chapters ago Vee put on his flannel and found the heat in it was her own, given back, and took it off and lay naked in a cold room on purpose. Tonight Randi is warm only along the one seam where she touches him and *the cold did not belong to the room, it was hers, she had brought it in and would carry it back out* — and then she gets up, fixes her face, and lies back down **in the warmth she could borrow and could not make.** Same object, two women, two chapters apart, nobody explains it to me. That's the thing I came to this book starving for and this is the third or fourth time it's fed me.

But here's what actually got me, and it's not John at all. *The flaw was hers.* She lies in that faultless room and concludes the defect is in herself — a faultless man, nothing to point to, therefore she's broken. She has no idea she's just a woman who wants a woman, raised at a table where the real ones were never safe to mention. It never even occurs to her as an explanation. And then *Pace on his worst night reached further than this man at his best. She wished it wasn't true.* And then Vee, on a nothing Tuesday, across a table, and this room couldn't counterfeit one thread of it.

And then the con, laid out in her own head in full daylight, which is the only reason I can still stand her: she *could* tell her. She can run every scenario to the end — she's always been able to — and the one she cannot run is Vee's face. *She was not brave enough to lose her. She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be.* **So let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were. As long as the word kept its cover.** *Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways.* That is a manipulation and it is also the single most understandable thing anyone in this book has done, and I hate that I'd do it too.

And *Vee was her gorgeous.* Excuse me. Excuse me.

Then the texts and I had to put the book on my chest. Vee is in Ohio two days (?) after walking out of that man's house into the dark with nowhere to sleep, and what she sends is *i'm here if you want to talk. or not.* — offering Randi the ear, saying nothing about her own wreck. And Randi sends back the chalet and the mountains and *i'm never coming home.* **Every word of it true. None of it the thing.** Both of them cropping, in both directions, at the same hour of the same night. These two are going to kill me. And the one real line she lets herself have is *miss your face most though, gorgeous* — then she turns the phone facedown, which is the most honest gesture in the chapter.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she compared the sex to a sandwich and then said nothing had happened to her eyes and I have not recovered, also the bracelet DOESN'T COME OFF, also Vee is somewhere sleeping in her childhood bed and nobody has asked her a single question.*

What I'm cross about: I was promised the cold and I got Gstaad. That's twice now — Thanksgiving with Sheri happened offscreen, and now Vee's worst night happens offscreen. I will forgive it if I get her next chapter and not a third party. And the dinner — the bubble, the smoke, the foams in colors she had no name for — I skimmed it. I've skimmed every food paragraph since chapter forty and I'm telling you so you know: the truffle landed, the foams did not.
