REACTION

Okay so I went in braced. First two paragraphs are a man moving furniture and Steely Dan playing and I thought, here we go, twenty minutes of a guy being quietly competent in a well-heated house. I gave it to the apple. And the apple is where it got me — he sliced it thin *because she won't eat thick slices*, cut sides up *because she eats faster when the cut sides show*. That's not a man being thoughtful, that's a man running surveillance and calling it care, and the book knows it, because two lines later we get her mother on egg whites and the sorority scale and "he was sorry about it." He's sorry about it and he's still using it. I sat up.

And then the bench. God, the way she reads it — piece by piece, face cradle, handholds, and then *no place at all, an open middle where a surface should have been*. Watching a woman assemble the purpose of an object out of its negative space, feeling what her own body would be doing in it before anyone says a word. That's the most erotic paragraph I've read in a year and nobody's undressed yet. "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you." I actually made a noise.

The heat delivered. It's slow and it's *specific* — the hands going up her sides and turning back before they arrive, three times, until she calls him on it and he does it again. The wet fingers laid flat on the small of her back, her own heat returned to her in the wrong place. The heels coming off mid-scene, him holding her bare foot in both palms. It's explicit without being a parts inventory, which I did not think anyone was still doing.

But here's what's actually got me at 1 a.m.: the *please*. That whole business of "it was still her please, the please she says to a waiter" and him refusing it — refusing the performance, waiting her out until the real one comes up out of her — that's a genuinely new idea about consent and control in a book like this. He's not denying her orgasm for the standard brat-tamer reason. He's denying her the *script*. "She could not pout her way back. The bench was not there." I've read four hundred spankings and I have never read one where the point was that the woman's entire social technology had been removed from the room.

And then the name lands. *There you are, Randi.* I had to put the Kindle down. Because I read the back of this book — I know who Randi is, I know she's the one who's going to hand a twenty-one-year-old named Vee to this man like a party favor. So now I know the whole game is a woman's escape hatch. She got seen once, on that bench, and decided in a locked bathroom that she is never coming out again — *not tonight, and this arrived without words, quiet as a deadbolt going over, not ever* — and her solution is going to be to go find him someone else to look at. That's not a plot twist, that's a *character*, and it's ruthless.

The bathroom mirror section is the best writing in the chapter and it's not close. "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." The face taking the being-wanted and passing it inward in amounts she decides. The smile sitting *on top of* the face that had begged and her seeing the disjunction. And the detail I can't shake — the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail, slow, into the nail bed, and she doesn't know she's doing it. Nobody comments. It just sits there. That's the tell that this book isn't going to be sweet about any of it.

What I'm suspicious of: it's almost entirely his POV, and he's *very* pleased with himself. "He thought that this had been a remarkably good evening." He's sitting there feeling moved about the apple and the hunger while she's in the next room bricking herself up, and the book is letting me see the gap he can't. That's the whole engine and it worked on me once — but it's a trick with a shelf life, and if chapter after chapter is a man reading a woman correctly except for the one thing that matters, I'll get tired. Also: Daphne. One sentence, a door he didn't respect, cost him more than he knew anything could cost. I want that and I don't want it doled out in single sentences for three hundred pages.

The thing I'm most curious about isn't even Vee yet. It's that Randi decided in that bathroom, and now I have to watch her build the escape.
