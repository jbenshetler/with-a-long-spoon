REACTION

Okay. So I bought this book for Vee and chapter one doesn't have Vee in it, and I didn't notice until the last word of the spanking scene, and when I did notice I actually said something out loud in my apartment. *There you are, Randi.* I had spent nineteen pages building a girl in my head — I assumed the sorority bag and the blue eyes and the apple slices were Vee, because why wouldn't I, the blurb told me a twenty-one-year-old college junior walks into a mathematician's path — and the book let me do that. It let me do all that work and then handed me the name and the floor went out. Because now I know what this chapter *is*. This isn't the prologue to a romance. This is the origin of a plan, and I just watched the exact minute it was conceived, before the woman conceiving it even had words for it. *Not tonight — and this arrived without words, quiet as a deadbolt going over — not ever. He could have the face.* She's going to give him somebody else. She's going to find him a girl who *can* be seen, and hand her over, and call it a game, and she will never once have to come out from behind the smile again. I am sick about it and I am absolutely not putting this down.

And the awful part — the genuinely good, awful part — is how much I liked him for most of it. He's not an alpha. I've read nine hundred alphas and he is not one. He's a man who sliced the apple thin because she won't eat thick slices and laid the pieces cut-side-up because she eats faster when the cut sides show. That detail did more to me than any bicep in my entire Kindle. It's love and it's also a dossier, and the book doesn't tell me which, it just sets it on the table. He backs off after the hello kiss so she has to come get it. He turns his chair when she circles her finger and admits to himself he likes being directed and keeps it off his face, *mostly*. He takes her hands out of his hair — twice — because he wants them empty, and then she puts them behind her back herself and the book calls it an offering without ever saying *submission* at me. That's the register I've been starving for. Somebody who knows that the whole charge is in the half-inch of distance.

The bench. I've read a thousand pieces of furniture and not one of them has ever worked on me like that, because he doesn't describe it — she *reads* it, part by part, in the order a frightened body would find them. Face cradle, so that's a massage table. Handholds at shoulder width — a massage table doesn't have those. Then the open middle where a surface should be, where everything a surface usually covers would hang free. *Her hands closed on nothing.* I had my whole stomach in my throat before anyone touched anyone. And "where I've been laying my underwear?" — "Yes." That's the single best line in the chapter and it's four words long.

The sex: yes. Finally. Explicit, unhurried, and *about something* the entire time, which I had started to believe was not a thing that existed. The edging worked on me, which is annoying, because I know the choreography. What got me was that he's not withholding to be cruel or to win, he's withholding because her *please* is still the *please* she'd use on a waiter. That's the most specific thing I've read about a person in months. And when the real one comes out it's described as *lower, broken, almost ugly* — and he doesn't flinch from how much he wanted to hear it. He stays hard the whole night and never takes anything and I believed it, because the thing he wanted wasn't that.

Then she's crying on the leather and doesn't know she's doing it, and he says *there you are*, and I thought I was in the afterglow. And instead: the water bottle cap jammed under her thumbnail, slow and steady, into the nail bed, where it would hurt. No commentary. Nothing. Just a girl who has to put a known pain somewhere because an unknown tenderness just got in. And then he says *you're beautiful* and she **flinches**, and that is where I understood this book is not going to be kind to me. He's a man holding a woman he has just gotten all the way to the bottom of, thinking he's had a remarkably good evening, moved by the apple, deciding whether to offer dinner — while four feet away she is locking a door she has never locked and deciding to go dark forever. He is *so proud of himself*. He has no idea. I'm ahead of him and it's unbearable.

Also: Daphne. One paragraph, no explanation, *it had cost him more than he had known, then, that anything could cost* — and that's why he doesn't knock. His one hard-won piece of wisdom is the exact thing that loses her. If he'd knocked, if he'd been a worse man about it, she might not have gotten all the way behind the face. He respects the door and the door closes forever. I need to know what he did to Daphne and I need to know it soon.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the apple slices are cut side up on purpose* and then, twenty minutes later, *IT'S NOT VEE.*

One place it pushed its luck — the mirror section is a little in love with its own thesis near the end, *the face was lying, the face was good at lying, the face had been lying for years*, which I'd already gotten from the flinch and the thumbnail. I don't need the book to underline a thing it just did perfectly in gesture. But it's one paragraph and it's in her voice, so I'll allow it.
