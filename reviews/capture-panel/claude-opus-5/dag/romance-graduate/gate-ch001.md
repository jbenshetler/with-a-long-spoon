Okay so I opened this expecting to meet Vee, because the blurb is all Vee, and instead I'm in a warm house with a man slicing a honeycrisp thin because she won't eat thick slices and laying the cut sides up because she eats faster when the cut sides show — and I actually sat up. That detail did more for me than every "he knew her body better than she did" line I've read in the last two years, because it's not about her body, it's about the *machinery in her head that polices food*, and he sees it, and he's sorry about it, and he works around it without saying a word. That's the grown-up thing I keep begging for. That's a man paying attention with a cost attached.

And then the whole chapter is her and I don't know her name. I spent the entire bench scene calling her "the blue-eyed girl" in my head. "There you are, Randi." I made a sound. Out loud. Because I'd already agreed to like her before I knew she was one of the two people who are going to do something to Vee, and now I've been handed the thing the jacket promised — being ahead of the girl — except the trick is it's not Vee I'm ahead of yet, it's *Randi*, and she's the one who thinks she's holding the cards.

The sex is what I came for and it is not coy for one second. I want to be specific about why it worked on me, because I've read four hundred spanking-bench scenes and most of them are furniture porn. This one was *slow* in a way that had consequences. The hand passing up her ribs and turning back before it arrived, three times, until she forgot the bite in her mouth — that's foreplay with a joke inside it. Him taking her hands out of his hair with two fingers under each wrist, no grip, and her then putting them behind her back *herself*, and the book noting that it became an offering without her deciding it — I had to stop. The edging sequence went exactly as long as it needed to and the distinction between the two *pleases* is the best idea in the chapter. Her *please* in the voice she'd use asking a waiter for water. God. I know that woman. I have been in rooms with forty of that woman.

And then he gets it wrong. That's the part that has me. He's sitting there afterward feeling quietly, decently pleased — *the hunger had been in her all along, all I did was answer it* — genuinely moved by the apple slices, still hard and setting it down because *that's not what tonight was for*, which, fine, I'm a little in love with him. Meanwhile she is twelve feet away in a locked bathroom deciding **he can have the face** and *not ever*. Permanently. Quiet as a deadbolt going over. He hears the lock and reads it as respecting her privacy — he even has a whole dead-serious reason, Daphne, a door he once didn't respect — and the thing he's respecting is her walling herself up forever. The best restraint of his life, used on exactly the wrong door. That's not manufactured conflict. That's two people being their most admirable selves straight into disaster and I want to watch it happen.

The water bottle cap. The sharp inner edge under her thumbnail, pressing down into the nail bed, *and she did not seem to know she was doing it*. Nobody writes that. That's the detail I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. — that and "she had built her whole adult life around being looked at for them."

Suspicions: I don't trust Pace's serenity. A man that regulated, that pleased with himself for how far he took her, who narrates his own tenderness as technique — the book keeps the pleasure of mastery visible in him ("he heard himself want to hear it again") and I don't think that's an accident. And I don't trust one word of the jacket's framing of who's running the game, now that I've seen Randi decide in a bathroom mirror that being seen is unsurvivable. A woman who has just concluded she can never be known again, who then goes out and finds a girl for the man who knew her — yeah. I see what's being built. I want it.

Gripes, small: it's almost entirely his POV, and the single best passage in the chapter is hers, in the mirror. I want more of that and I want it soon or the heat starts feeling curated *at* her. There was a stretch in the middle — strike, soothe, strike, check-in, strike — where I felt the rhythm start to become a rhythm and my eye sped up. And the chapter is long and is one continuous scene; if the whole book runs at this length-per-beat I'll need the next one to move differently.

But I read every word. I never do that anymore.
