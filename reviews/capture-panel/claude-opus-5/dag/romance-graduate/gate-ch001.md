REACTION

Okay so I went in braced. First page is a man alone in a house adjusting furniture and a woman coming in the door and I thought *here we go, the competent dom, the perfectly calibrated house, he's going to know her better than she knows herself for two hundred pages and I'm going to be bored by page forty.* The apple thing almost lost me — the slicing, the cut sides up, the noticing-how-she-eats. It read for a second like the book flexing how observant its man is.

And then the book *used* it. That's the whole trick, isn't it. Every one of those creepy-competent details — the water bottle left out in advance, the too-warm house, the cashmere blanket she brought in week two — turns out to be building toward a man who is genuinely paying attention and is *still going to be completely wrong about what just happened in his own bedroom.* I have not had that done to me in a long time. The last two pages I actually sat up.

Let me talk about the bench, because I came here for heat and the heat was real. She reads the thing before he explains it — the face cradle, the handholds, and then *no place at all* where a surface should be — and I read it with her in the same order, and my stomach went. That's a better reveal than most books get out of an actual plot twist. And then the scene itself is *long*, and slow in the way I always claim I want and rarely get given: the edging, the coming around to her face to check on her, *hi*, the taking off her heels in the middle of it like it was the most tender thing available. Nothing faded, nothing coy, nothing "and then he took her to heights she'd never known." Wet on his knuckles, a thread of it breaking, the smell of her coming up through the perfume. Written, not recited. Thank you.

But here's what I'd actually text somebody at 1 a.m.: **it's not Vee.** Two hundred words into the jacket I have a whole Vee-shaped expectation and the book spends its entire first chapter taking apart the *other* woman, and doesn't tell me her name until the last beat of the scene. *There you are, Randi.* I put the book down on my chest for a second. Because the jacket already told me Randi is the one running the game, the one who steers Vee into his path, laughing, as if by accident — and Chapter One shows me Randi sobbing facedown on a bench he built for her, unable to get her own face back on. The glamorous friend who plans the best year of a girl's life is a woman who, three steps into her own boyfriend's bedroom, could not pout her way out of anything. I am *so* much more interested in her now than I would have been if I'd met her charming and in control first.

And that bathroom sequence. God. *He could have the face.* She has the single most alive experience of her life and her immediate, silent, deadbolt-quiet decision is *not ever again.* That's not manufactured conflict, that's not an unsent text — that's a person choosing her armor over the best thing that's ever happened to her, in real time, for reasons the book has already made me understand. And the cap of the water bottle pressed under her thumbnail, *slow and steady, down into the nail bed.* She doesn't know she's doing it and he sees it and doesn't name it. I felt that one in my teeth.

The flinch at *you're beautiful* is the line I'll remember. She takes a spanking, she takes being seen, she takes begging out loud in a voice she hasn't used since she was a child — and the thing that makes her body jerk away is a compliment. Yes. That's exactly right and I've never seen it put that way.

Suspicions: Daphne. One paragraph, dropped in like a stone down a well, and the book doesn't explain it — *it had cost him more than he had known, then, that anything could cost.* That's a loaded gun and I want it. And I'm now reading the whole premise sideways: if Randi is going to hand Vee to this man as a *game*, and she made that decision from behind a face she put back on because he got too close — then the game is her way of feeding him someone else instead of herself. She'll give him a girl before she'll give him her. That's horrifying and I need it confirmed.

The one thing I'm watching warily: he is a little too right about everything, and the prose has a tic — *He did this. He did that. He waited.* — that could get monotonous over three hundred pages. Right now the flatness is doing work, it's holding the charge down so the sounds she makes land harder. If it's still doing that in chapter six, great. If it becomes a rhythm I can hear instead of feel, we'll have a problem.

But I'm not putting this down. Someone finally wrote the sex *and* the woman.
