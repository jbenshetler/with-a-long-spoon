**REACTION**

Okay. *Okay.* I need to say the thing first: I got all the way to "There you are, Randi" before I knew who I'd been reading. And I want to be annoyed about being played like that — I've had a hundred books try a withheld-name trick on me and it's usually just coyness in a trenchcoat — but this one *earned* it, because the blurb already told me Randi is the friend who steers Vee into Pace's path. So the floor dropped out twice: once at the name, and again about four seconds later when I did the math on what that means for the girl who isn't even in the chapter yet.

And the heat. Thank *God.* Nobody blinked and cut to the ceiling. The apple on her lap in the kitchen — bite, kiss, bite, and his hands making those slow passes up her ribs that keep *not arriving* — that got me before a single button was undone, which is a thing maybe three books have managed for me in two years. The bench, discovered in pieces — face cradle, handholds, then the gap where a surface should be, then the hips — she reads it as anatomy and so did I, and I was warm before he closed the door. The spanking-then-stroking-then-stopping, the two *pleases* that aren't the same *please*: "It was still being said in the voice she said *please* in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water." I put the book down for a second at that line. That's not a sex scene, that's a *diagnosis*, and it's hotter for it.

Then the second half turned the temperature to something else entirely and I wasn't ready. She's crying without knowing she's crying. She takes the water bottle cap and presses the sharp inner edge under her thumbnail — *he notices and says nothing and neither does the book* — and I actually sat up. He says "you're beautiful" and she **flinches**. And the lock going over on the bathroom door, which she has never once done in his house. And in front of the mirror she doesn't come apart, she *reassembles*: he can have the face, the one he found is going back behind it, not tonight, not ever. Quiet as a deadbolt going over. I am unwell.

What's killing me is the double blindness. He's out there on the bed thinking about the thin apple slices, thinking *she finally let someone feed her*, quietly happy about her, deciding whether to offer dinner — this generous, careful, unhurried man who was right about her body in every particular and is one hundred percent wrong about what just happened to her. She's curled into his neck lying with her whole face. Two people in one warm room, one of them glowing, one of them sealing a door, and I'm the only one who can see both. That's the best thing in the chapter and it isn't the sex.

Suspicion, at 1 a.m., texted to Jen in all caps: *Randi doesn't bring Vee to Pace as a game. She brings him Vee because she can't be the one he looks at like that again and she needs someone else in the room.* The blurb calls it a bet between two people and says it got real for both of them, and now I think the "game" is a woman building a decoy out of a twenty-one-year-old. Also: **Daphne.** One paragraph, a door he didn't respect once, and it "cost him more than he had known, then, that anything could cost." Put a pin in her, I'll be waiting.

The one thing making me squint: Pace is *very* good at everything. He built the bench, he sliced the apple cut-side up because he noticed she eats faster that way, he knew to leave the water, he knew not to knock. A man that frictionless is usually a fantasy vending machine and I get bored of those fast. But the ending saved it — he read her body flawlessly and her *self* completely wrong, and his little private pleasure at "having taken her further than he had thought she would go" has a faint cold draft under it. So I'm not filing him as a wish. I'm filing him as someone I don't fully trust yet, which is much more interesting.

Middle stretch did go on — a run of strike/soothe/check-in where I started to feel the rhythm as rhythm and skimmed a couple of the "how's your back" beats. Worth it, but it was the one place my attention went loose.
