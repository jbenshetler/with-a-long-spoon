Okay. Okay okay okay. I need to text someone.

I went in thinking I knew the shape: chapter one, hero and heroine, bench, spanking, we're off. And I spent the entire thing reading a woman I assumed was Vee, filing away details — the sorority, the blue eyes, the apple she's allowed to eat — and then he says *There you are, Randi* and the floor moved under me. That's not a twist for its own sake. That recontextualized every single thing I'd just read. She's the one who's going to hand Vee over. And she's already in it up to her throat.

The apple. God, the apple. He slices it thin because she'll eat thin slices and not thick ones. He lays the cut sides up because she eats faster that way. He's *sorry about it.* That's four sentences of food-restriction backstory delivered without a single word of exposition and it told me more about her mother, her house, her whole nervous system than most books manage in a chapter of flashback. And then at the end he thinks of it again — "a girl who had been portioning herself" — and it's tender and it's also, if you tilt your head, exactly the appetite-language the whole series is named after. He's thinking about her hunger the way a man thinks about a project.

The water bottle cap under the thumbnail. I actually sat up. She sets the sharp edge under the nail bed and presses, slow and steady, where it will hurt, and *she does not seem to know she is doing it.* That's the kind of detail that makes me trust a writer completely. Nobody puts that in unless they know what they're doing.

And the bathroom. That's the real scene, isn't it. All the bench work was foreplay for four pages of a woman standing at a mirror discovering that she has a face and the face is not her and she just let someone get behind it. "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." I've read a thousand heroines described as "guarded" and not one of them has been *explained* like that. The bit where she tries on her photograph-smile and it "sat on top of the face that had begged" — and then the decision, quiet as a deadbolt going over: *not ever.* He can have the face. She's going back behind it and she isn't coming out. That's a whole novel's worth of dread planted in one paragraph, and it's her own hand that plants it.

The dramatic irony is what's got me. He's sitting out there on the bed feeling *quietly happy about her*, thinking he found her hunger and answered it, thinking he'll do it again as often as she wants, and she's in the bathroom sealing herself shut. Two people who just had completely different evenings. I'm going to be thinking about that at work tomorrow.

Heat-wise: yes. Genuinely yes. The edging, the *wrong* please and then the right one — "It was still the voice she said please in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water" — that's the most psychologically precise description of submission I've read in ages and it's hot precisely *because* it's precise. The wet warmth of his fingers pressed back against her spine in the wrong place. The bench built and disguised as a table she'd been laying her underwear on. "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you" — I made a noise.

My suspicions, because I have them. Pace is very close to too good. He anticipates everything: the water, the blanket she brought, the lights, the safeword check-ins, the exact thirty seconds of scalp-scratching. There's a version of this man who is just the dom-fantasy with better prose, and the Daphne line — the door he didn't respect once, the thing that cost him more than he knew anything could cost — is the only crack in him so far, and I want that crack pried open hard. Also: he's the one *watching* for the whole first half. Page after page of his hands cataloguing her and her having no thoughts at all. I trust it now, because the bathroom pays it off — the whole point is that she's all surface until she isn't — but that's a long check to write before cashing it.

And the thing I can't stop turning over: the blurb promises me a girl who never knew there was a plan, and chapter one hands me the woman who will make the plan, already cracked open, already deciding to lie about it. Whatever these two do to Vee, Randi is doing it from behind a face. That's so much better than I expected.
