REACTION

Okay. *Okay.* I got to "There you are, Randi" and I actually said something out loud in my empty living room.

Because I spent that entire bench sequence assuming I was watching Vee. The blurb put her in my hand — twenty-one, starving, walks toward him because everything in her has been waiting to — and the chapter hands me a sorority girl with an overnight bag and lets me fill in the name myself, and I did, happily, for twenty minutes of reading. And then it's *Randi*. The friend. The one who's going to steer the girl into his path, laughing, like it's an accident. The architect of the game is the woman who just got taken apart on a woodworking bench and decided, in a locked bathroom, that she will never let it happen to her again. The *size* of what that does to the premise. She's not going to hand him Vee out of generosity or kink or even cruelty — she's going to hand him a girl because she can't be the one on that bench as herself ever again, and someone has to be. I'm not even sure the book has told me that yet. It just put the pieces where I could reach them.

The heat worked on me, which is the part I don't take for granted anymore. I've read nine hundred edging scenes and I've skimmed most of them, because they're choreography — insert, deny, repeat, heroine says "please" in italics, done. This one wasn't about the denial, it was about the *voice*. He's not withholding to make her desperate, he's withholding until the please stops being the please she uses on a waiter. That's a genuinely new reason for a man to say "not yet" and it made my whole chest tight. And the apple. God, the apple — thin slices, cut sides up, because she eats them faster that way. He clocked her disordered eating and built a workaround into a snack bowl and said nothing about it. That's hotter than the spanking and the spanking was hot.

Things I noticed and am turning over: the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail. It goes by in one clause, nobody comments, and it's the single most frightening thing in the chapter. She was being held and she needed to hurt somewhere she controlled. And the flinch at "you're beautiful" — she can take being wanted all day, that's what the face is for, but she cannot take being *liked*. Also: Daphne. One paragraph, a door he didn't respect, something it cost him more than he knew anything could cost. I want that. Not explained, just — eventually.

And Pace. I'm suspicious of him in a way I'm enjoying. Not because he's sinister — because he's *wrong*, warmly and completely wrong, and the book lets him be. He ends the night pleased with himself, moved by her hunger, planning to answer it as often as she brings it to him, and she's in his arms with the smile arranged, already gone. He's so good at reading bodies and he just catastrophically misread a mind. A man whose whole appeal is that he sees you, failing to see the one thing that matters. That's a real engine, not a misunderstanding-over-an-unsent-text.

The one place I bristled: the mirror section goes on to explain its own machinery to me — the script, the *wanted* position, what the face is for, the whole thesis stated in clean declaratives. I'd already *felt* all of it in the flinch and the thumbnail. It's the one moment the book didn't trust me. I forgave it because the writing is genuinely doing something — "quiet as a deadbolt going over," and she locks a door in a house where she has never once failed to lock the front door behind her. That's the kind of thing that buys back a lot.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *it's not the girl from the blurb. it's the FRIEND. she's on the bench in chapter one and by the end of it she's decided to disappear and I think that's where the whole plot comes from. also he slices her apple thin because of her eating disorder and never mentions it. I'm so in.*
