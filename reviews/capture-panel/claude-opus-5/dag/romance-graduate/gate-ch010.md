REACTION

Oh, that was a knife wrapped in a bathrobe.

Two pages of two girls on a bunk bed and I sat there with my jaw tight the whole way through, because Cassie is asking every single right question about the wrong man. *How much did he give you to drink.* *What do I tell the state troopers.* *Is he rich.* She's running the full best-friend safety protocol — and the protocol assumes the danger is the stranger from the coffee shop. It isn't. The danger is the girl who walked into Vee outside the dining hall on purpose, who has already been sat on a man's lap in a warm house drinking wine out of his mouth asking *tell me, tell me,* who said *do you think she'll like the bench.* Cassie can smell something and she's pointing her nose at the one person in this who, so far, has actually been decent to Vee's face. And Vee hands over the address like it's proof of safety. *See. I know where he lives and everything.* Off six-fourteen. Past where it goes to gravel. She's building a dossier for a kidnapping that's already happened and didn't need a road.

And the line that actually got me — *Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.* She's so proud of it. She's right, too, which is the thing. That's the whole jacket delivered in a girl's happiest voice to her best friend in a cold dorm room, and the book keeps doing that — putting the sentence in her mouth when she's at her most radiant and letting me hold both ends of it. I'm not being manipulated into dread; I'm being handed it and asked to enjoy it. I do. I feel filthy about it.

The spin was nearly too much for me — the arms-up, knocked-the-closet-door, *look at me* — I braced for cute, and then it saved itself by being clumsy and over before it was a dance and the room too small to hold it. Fine. I'll allow it. Because she's twenty-one and she kissed a man under a tree and she's never done that before, and I remember that specific unbearable need to physically move the joy out of your body.

What I'm suspicious of: Cassie. Not of her — *for* her. "Goodbye, Vee" has been sitting in my chest since the lecture hall and now she's the only person in this book with a functioning alarm, and books do not keep functioning alarms around for long. She kept the insult off the bleachers rather than fire back. She asked about the drink and then believed her. She says *borrow good ones* and goes back to her page. I will be furious when Randi edges her out, and Randi is going to edge her out, and the awful part is Vee will let it happen while feeling chosen.

No heat in this one at all and I didn't want any — a debrief is exactly what a girl does after a first kiss, and letting me hear the version Vee *tells* against the version I watched is the point. But I'm keeping count: I've now had a pool, a dinner, and a bunk bed, and the last time Randi's appetite was on the page was the wine. Give me the hike, give me that house with the long table in the bedroom, and give me Randi and Vee in the same room again, because the thing I'm most impatient for is the one the jacket promised and the book keeps holding just off-screen.
