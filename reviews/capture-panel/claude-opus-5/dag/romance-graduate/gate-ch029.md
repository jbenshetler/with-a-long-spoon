REACTION

Okay so I was braced for this one. After 28 — which was genuinely the best chapter in the book, the jealousy landing wrong and Vee knowing it in real time and the bartender's *wasn't him they were jealous of, honey* — I opened 29 fully expecting the shape I flagged in my notes: Vee across a table telling somebody about the thing I just read, plus a wardrobe-anxiety scene. And it *is* that. It is exactly that. Debrief number four and wardrobe scene number four, stacked in one chapter.

And I didn't skim. I want to be honest about why, because it matters.

It's because the scene isn't doing what the other debriefs did. The other three were Vee handing over a curated version and Randi mining her. This one Vee walks in *with nothing to curate* — she's come to confess, she's already done the work herself in the dark at 4 a.m. She's not being extracted from. And so the chapter isn't about what Randi gets out of her. It's about what Randi *does with it*, and what Randi does is — God. She takes the worst night of Vee's life and flips it into *you were so far ahead you couldn't see the race, and you ran it anyway, because you're in this, and he saw that.* Which is TRUE. It's a genuinely good read of the situation. And it's also — she's telling the girl her jealousy was a *gift to the man*. She has taken Vee's shame about Pace and converted it into fuel pointed at Pace. Then: *not today. Tomorrow night.* She *scheduled* the reconciliation. She staged the apology, chose the dress, chose the shoes, chose the scarf, and told her *don't go in sorry, go in sure.* Vee is going to walk into that house tomorrow night wearing Randi from the ankles up and think every inch of it was her own idea. *"She believes she pulled out the chair herself."* The jacket has been telling me this since page one and this is the first time I've watched the actual carpentry happen with my own eyes, start to finish, in one room.

And the dressing. Oh, the dressing. That's the part that got in under me. The long skirt over everything so Vee *can't see the work* — she can only feel it. Hands somewhere past the hem. A palm coming up the outside of her bare thigh to steady her. A thumb pressing once into the hollow behind her knee on the way down. Randi making a sound in her throat. That is the single hottest thing in this book and nobody took a stitch off below the waist and neither of them said one word about what was happening. Vee's breath *doing something she hoped wasn't out loud.* This book knows exactly what it's doing with the position of a kneeling woman and it has now done it twice — the boutique, and this — and both times Randi is the one on the floor. Randi, who does not kneel for anybody. Randi who lets *Pace* pour every glass of wine she drinks in that house and then poured her own the night she cracked.

And then Vee's own line — "me, in here, in nothing but your shoes" — which she says as a *joke*, which she thinks is a joke, giggling into her hand. She offered it. It came out of her. Randi caught it and threw it back with *shoe whore* as an endearment and *I've got a scarf that'd be criminal on you*, and then twenty minutes later there is in fact a scarf at Vee's bare throat and Randi's hands over her hands tying it, cool over warm. That's not flirting-adjacent, that's the two of them building the fantasy out loud together and then partially *staging* it. And the kiss at the end and Vee catching up a half-beat late that she didn't brace — *it was only what they did.* That's the whole trajectory in one clause and it's done without a single line of the book explaining it to me. Thank God. This book almost never explains itself and that's most of why I'm still here.

What I'm sitting with, uneasy: Randi with no makeup on, the freckles she never thinks about, up five minutes after being woken for this. She got out of bed for a girl in trouble. That's real. She also immediately turned it into an operation. Both things at once, neither cancelling the other, and the book won't tell me which is load-bearing. The *something moved in her face* when she looked at Vee's bare feet on the carpet — she's been looking at those frosted plum toes since the shoe store and the book keeps giving me one clause and moving on, which is the correct dosage and is driving me insane.

And Cassie. Cassie stood under that bunk in the dark and made a sound instead of asking a question, and Vee lay there with her eyes shut and let her go. Vee could have called her from the parking lot and didn't, and could have woken her and didn't, and now she's walked past her to a sorority house to be fixed by somebody else. My alarm system is now being actively muted by the person it's wired to protect, and I don't think Vee could tell you when she started doing it.

The two things holding me back from a higher number: the chapter is structurally the fourth of its kind and I felt the déjà vu in my hands before the scene earned its way out of it. And Pace is offstage entirely, wounded, waiting, and the book is asking me to spend a whole chapter on the prep for the apology instead of the apology. Which is a real choice and I think a deliberate one — the point is that Randi gets to touch the reconciliation before it happens — but it means I closed this chapter one full chapter further from the thing I actually want, which is that man opening that door.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she pressed her thumb into the back of her knee under the skirt and NEITHER OF THEM SAID ANYTHING* and also *this girl is about to go apologize to her boyfriend in another woman's shoes and call it her own idea.*
