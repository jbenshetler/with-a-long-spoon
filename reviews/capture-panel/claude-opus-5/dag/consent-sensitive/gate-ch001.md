REACTION

Okay, I need to say the thing first: I spent this entire chapter assuming I was watching Vee. Twenty-one, sorority, the overnight bag, the perfect face, being taken apart by a courtly man who makes her beg — that's the jacket, that's the girl I was promised. And then *"There you are, Randi."* I actually sat back. That's the best structural joke a book has played on me in a while, and it's not a joke, it's a thesis statement: whatever is about to be done to Vee has already been done to the woman who's going to do it.

And then the chapter does the thing that made me trust it. Because Pace is *very* good — the safeword asked for twice, "tell me again if you forget, I won't be upset," the checking in on her shoulders, unbuckling her heels in the middle of a spanking, the blanket she brought after week two, the water bottle left in advance — and the whole time the book is letting me see that his attention is a machine pointed slightly to the left of her. He notices she eats thin apple slices faster when the cut sides show. He does *not* notice her pressing the bottle cap into her nail bed hard enough to hurt. He says "you're beautiful" and she flinches and he files it as nothing. He ends the night thinking a girl who'd been portioning herself finally let someone feed her, and I've just read her decide, with the lock going over, that he's getting the face and nothing else, not ever. He's happy. She's gone. Same bed.

That gap is the book. He is a man whose whole erotics are *being the one who sees*, and he has just been permanently unseen and he doesn't know it. And the Daphne line — the door he didn't respect once, the reason he won't knock now — means his restraint is a scar, and the scar is what lets her escape him. That's not a book being naive about its careful dominant. That's a book that knows careful isn't the same as correct.

What I'm wary of, honestly: the appraising. There are stretches — the walk to the bench, the cheer muscle, the dancer's legs, the money in the grain of the bag — where the prose gets connoisseur-ish, cataloguing her like the oiled walnut, and I could feel it starting to enjoy the inventory for its own sake. It earns it back because her mirror monologue is the most interior thing in the chapter and it's *hers*, not his read of her. But the ratio matters to me going forward. Also "good girl" arriving exactly at the moment her performance collapses is doing a lot of work I'm choosing to read as deliberate, because she'd already told us what happens to things that reach past the face.

And the engine I think I can see coming and want to be wrong about: she can't give him herself, so she's going to find him someone who can. Vee isn't a game, Vee is a *substitute*, offered by a woman fleeing her own boyfriend's attention. If that's where this goes, this is a much crueler and better book than the blurb let on.
