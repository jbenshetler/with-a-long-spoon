REACTION

Okay, so the last word of the chapter is the whole chapter. *There you are, Randi.* I actually sat back. I'd spent forty pages assuming I was watching the jacket's Vee get undressed under the lamps, and instead I've been handed the origin wound of the woman who is going to go out and *recruit* a twenty-one-year-old for this. That's not a twist for its own sake. That's a thesis. Randi got cracked open on that bench, decided in a locked bathroom that it would never happen again, and — if the blurb is telling the truth — her solution is going to be to find someone else to put under that gaze. I would text a friend at 1 a.m. about that structure alone.

And here's what makes me willing to stay: the book is not asking me to find the ending sweet. He is sitting there radiating contentment — *she finally let someone feed her*, *I'll answer it again as often as she wants to bring it to me* — while she has just concluded, in language as final as a deadbolt (the book literally says deadbolt, twice, front door and bathroom), *he can have the face, I'm not coming out again.* Two people in the same warm room, one of them completely wrong about what just happened, and he's the one who reads everything. That's the awareness I needed and I got it in chapter one. He knows she eats thin apple slices and not thick ones. He knows her mother's egg whites. He knows she braces because every man before him was in a hurry. And he does not clock the bottle cap. She's pressing the sharp edge of a plastic cap down into her nail bed hard enough to hurt and doesn't know she's doing it, and he notices that she drank more water than he expected. That detail is the best thing in the chapter and it's the most frightening thing in the chapter, and the book put it there on purpose.

The consent mechanics are genuinely careful — safeword asked twice, *tell me again if you forget, I won't be upset*, checking her back and hands and shoulders mid-scene, taking her heels off with both hands like they mattered. I have no complaint about the physical scene; she wanted this, she'd been thinking about it since she opened her eyes that morning, she said *show me*. What the chapter is quietly admitting is that none of that covers the thing he actually did. She consented to being spanked on a bench. She did not consent to being found. He went past the face and pulled a sound out of her that she hadn't made since she was a crying child asking her mother for something she wasn't going to get — and then said *you're beautiful* to the person he'd found, and she *flinched*, and the book let the flinch be the truest response in the room. He built the bench for exactly this. He withheld the answer to *please* until the *please* changed registers. That's a man engineering a psychological state and calling it generosity, and the prose knows the difference even while it's enjoying itself.

The Daphne line did a lot of work in three sentences. He learned once, expensively, not to go through a door he can hear through — so he sits on the bed and respects the lock. Which is decent of him and also precisely how he misses the only thing he needed to see tonight. His restraint costs him her. I like a book where the virtue is the failure.

Things I'm side-eyeing: he is very fond of himself. The interiority is all connoisseurship — the laminate she disliked, the wood she couldn't price, the sorority girl asking about the lights "the way a sorority girl asked about an inconvenience." He appraises her the way she appraises his furniture, and I can't yet tell whether the narration shares his taste for that or is setting it up. Also the middle stretch — hand down the spine, heat of her skin, salt at the nape, hand down the spine again — I did glaze a bit. There's about a page and a half of sweat inventory where the sentences stop doing anything the previous sentence didn't. Cut a third of the touching and the *no* would land even harder.

But she's crying on the leather and doesn't know it, she's smiling in the mirror and knows exactly what the smile is for, and she told herself *not ever* in the same breath as admitting she has never been so alive. I want to watch her walk that out into the world. God help whoever she picks.
