REACTION

Okay so the first thing I did was flip back to the jacket, because I was thirty pages into what I assumed was Vee and then he said *There you are, Randi* and I actually made a noise. That's a hell of a move — open Book One not with the girl the plan is about but with one of the two people who made the plan, and show her getting taken apart. Before I know anything about the game, I know that the woman who is going to hand Vee over is herself somebody's project, and that she cried about it in a locked bathroom.

And the bathroom is the whole reason I'm staying. Because for most of this chapter I was reading Pace with my instruments out and the needles twitching. Thin apple slices, cut sides up, *because he had noticed that she ate them faster when the cut sides showed.* The water bottle placed in advance. The bench built in secret, disguised as the table she'd been setting her underwear on for weeks. "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you." That line is the book putting its thesis on the counter where I can't miss it — his method isn't lying, it's declining to correct, and the jacket has already told me he's going to run it on a twenty-one-year-old who doesn't know there's a plan. A lesser book would have given me all that as swoon. This one gives me the choreography from inside the choreographer's head and then — crucially — cuts to her, and she is *not where he thinks she is.* He's holding her thinking *remarkably good evening, the hunger was in her all along, I'll answer it again.* She's decided, quiet as a deadbolt going over, never to come out again. He got it completely wrong about the one thing he prides himself on. That gap is the author's hand raised, saying: I see him. Good. I'll stay.

The tells I'm keeping: she flinched at *you're beautiful.* She dug the bottle cap under her thumbnail hard enough to hurt and neither of them noticed. He heard her crying through the door and chose not to knock — and the Daphne flashback frames that as his hard-won ethics, which it is, and also means his great lesson about boundaries is *the ones people announce,* while he's been quietly managing her plate and her aftercare and her language for weeks. A man who will not open a door he can hear through, and will absolutely build you a bench and let you call it a table. I don't think that contradiction is an accident. If it turns out to be one, I'll know fast.

The heat worked on me, and it worked because of restraint that was actually about her: the hands going up her sides and turning back before they arrive, three times, and her forgetting the bite of apple in her hand. Undressing her from one knee. Stopping mid-scene to unbuckle her heels and hold her bare foot. Taking her hands out of his hair because he wanted them empty, and her putting them behind her back herself and it becoming an offering *without her deciding it* — that sentence is the book in miniature. The long edging stretch is the kind of thing that can go mechanical, and this one didn't, because the target was never her body, it was her *voice* — the waiter-please versus the real please. That's an astonishingly mean and precise thing to want from someone, and the chapter knows it's mean; it lets him narrate her losing the script for the *wanted* position and having nothing underneath it, and I could feel him enjoying the demolition. "He heard himself want to hear it again" is the one moment his composure cracks toward appetite and I circled it.

Closest I came to bristling: he clocks the thing behind her face policing her food, is *sorry about it*, and then feeds her the exact slices engineered for compliance. Sorry-and-proceeding is the oldest move in the book. Plus "good girl" landing three lines after her whole performance apparatus collapses. I didn't leave, because the bathroom scene told me the author is tracking the cost — but that thread is on notice. If we get twelve chapters of her appetite as a lovely gift he unlocked and nobody ever returns to the thumbnail, I'm out.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he thinks he found her and she's already gone, and neither of them has even met the girl yet.* Also: if this man builds Vee a piece of furniture I'm going to scream.
