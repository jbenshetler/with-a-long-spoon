Two pages and it's the whole cost, laid out so gently you could miss it.

Here's what I actually did while reading: I went back in my head to the bean bag chapter. That night Cassie asked how much he'd given her to drink. She asked for his name *for the state troopers*. She asked for an address and got "off six-fourteen" and didn't get more. And tonight? "No windowless van." "I had your blood type ready." Cassie's whole alarm system has been converted into a running gag. It's still *technically* the same instrument — she's still asking where Vee went and whether she came back — but it's been metabolized into a bit they do, and a bit you do is a bit nobody has to answer. Vee didn't have to deflect this time. There was nothing to deflect. Three chapters ago Vee had to work to route Cassie away from the address; tonight Cassie routed herself. And the chapter doesn't editorialize about it, which is the part I respect — it just lets me notice that the last line of defense in this book has become a punchline about blood type.

And then. **"Cassie was rapt."** Three words and I put the book on my knee. That's the book saying it out loud. Cassie — four words a day, ribs-deep in the pool, won't go in past where she can stand — is sitting in her sweats being *told a love story* and she is *into it*. Not suspicious. Rapt. And of course she is. Look at what she's being handed: he knelt in the dirt and fixed her foot and didn't make it weird, he gave her his only dry shirt and then got out into freezing rain and stood with his *back turned* so nobody would be looking at her, and then she called him in and undressed in front of him and he didn't touch her, he just was glad. There is no version of that story that sounds like anything but a good man. It *is* a good man, that's the trouble. The whole horror of this book is that the evidence is genuine. Cassie's not being fooled by a lie. She's being fooled by facts.

Then: "Vee." Soft. *Not "Oh, Vee." Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break.* And Vee says "I know." And I have read that exchange four times now and I still can't fully close it, and I think that's on purpose and I think it's the best thing in the chapter. What does Cassie mean and what does Vee know? Two readings and they're both alive. One: *you are gone, you are so far gone, and I'm not going to be the one who says it.* Two: *that's the most beautiful thing I ever heard and I'm frightened for you and those are the same sentence.* Either way Cassie has decided not to put it into words, which is exactly the thing Pace does — the decline-to-say, the door left closed — and now Cassie's doing it too, out of love, and it costs Vee the same thing it always costs. Nobody in this book will say the hard sentence and everyone has a good reason.

The other thing I'm chewing on: Randi isn't in this chapter and Vee doesn't mention her *once*. Not "Randi told me to go full nature girl," not "I told Randi about the dinner." And two chapters ago Vee kept Cassie out of the smoothie place on Cassie's behalf — *it isn't really her place.* So Vee is now maintaining two separate rooms with two separate versions of her life in them, and nobody made her do that. The architects needed her compartmentalized and she's doing it herself, for free, out of tact. That's the scariest structural fact I've got right now and the book laid it in without a single line of comment.

Small ones I loved: "My legs are going to file a complaint."
