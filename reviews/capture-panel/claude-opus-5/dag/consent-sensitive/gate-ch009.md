Okay so this is the chapter where I was checking the instruments, and the instruments are fine. Better than fine. This book knows exactly what it's doing and it kept doing it with no heat to hide behind, which is the test I actually care about.

The thing that got me — really got me, cold down the back of the neck — is the food. Because I've read chapter one. I watched this man slice a honeycrisp thin, cut-sides up, because he'd *noticed* Randi ate them faster that way, and the slicing was tender and it was also surveillance. He feeds women. That's the move. With Randi he trimmed the portion to what her policing would permit; with Vee he ordered everything and let her not watch herself eat. Same hand, calibrated differently. The book didn't underline it once and it didn't have to, and I sat there going: oh, you're doing it again, and you know you're doing it.

"May I choose?" — three words and I made a noise. It's the same formula as *may I call you*, the courtly thing that makes you swoon, and what it *does* is take the choosing. And she feels the relief of handing it over go through her "more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would," which is the book saying the quiet part in Vee's own vocabulary without making her understand it. That's the whole engine: she gets to describe the mechanism accurately and never see it. I love and hate it.

And then: "she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong... that low background readiness she carried everywhere, the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there." Friend. There IS a bill. It's enormous. It was drawn up on a lawn while a man pointed at her laughing into the sky. Her vigilance going offline is being written as the most restful thing that's ever happened to her, and the book absolutely knows that's the scariest sentence in the chapter. It didn't blink and it didn't reassure me. That's the framing carrying awareness.

The kiss is where it's most merciless and most honest. He doesn't push. He doesn't do a single one of the six things I was braced for — no lean, no charm turned up, no patient waiting-her-out — he *lets it close*, and then she stops walking and breaks her own rule herself and calls it an exception. Also the right hand, never the left, "one more thing he knew that she didn't" — Vee files it as charming mystery; I file it as the structural fact of this entire novel, and I clocked that the book let her say it out loud in the wrong key.
