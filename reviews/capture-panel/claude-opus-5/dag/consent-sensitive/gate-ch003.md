REACTION

Okay. So the first thing that happened to me is that Chapter 1 retroactively changed shape, and I had to sit with that for a minute. I read the bench chapter as the book's opening move on its heroine and it wasn't — that was the *recruiter* being taken apart. Randi on that bench, deciding in a locked bathroom that she would never come out from behind the face again, and now here she is four days later selecting a girl off a lawn like fruit. The book built that sequence on purpose. The woman who just learned exactly what it costs to be seen is going to go do it to someone else. That's not a clumsy structure. That's a thesis.

And the beat I keep going back to: *he asked her mid-orgasm.* "He had decided this beforehand." He gets inside her while she's still pulsing and undefended, and *then* asks would she like to taste another woman, and when her body answers before she can dress it up, the narration says — flatly, with no hedging — "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." I stopped dead on "He usually did." Because that is the whole problem with this man stated in four words, and the book put them there, and it did not soften them. He then congratulates himself at length about wanting her "gathered back into herself when he asked, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did" — sir. You extracted it while she was coming. You already have it. The re-ask is theater and the book lets the gap sit there wide open for anyone who's looking. That's the awareness I needed and it's the reason I'm still here. If that paragraph had read as sincere and the prose had agreed with him, I'd have shut the book.

What I love, uncomfortably: Randi's "only in a threesome, with you," and Pace clocking exactly what she's done — moved the wanting to a survivable owner, made herself the generous instrument instead of a woman who hungers. That's a real observation about how people who can't own an appetite launder it. And then the pointing game gives her the appetite back a piece at a time — "I point. You pick" — and she thinks she's being a good sport and she's been shopping for a quarter mile. Him watching her learn she's allowed to want something. It's tender and it's completely instrumental and the book knows both at once.

The shoulder-check is the most frightening thing in the chapter and it's three sentences long. Randi closes thirty feet on angles nobody could name, takes the hit at half a head and enough weight that *Vee* is the one whose hand flies to her mouth, *Vee* is the one apologizing — and then the hand on the arm staying a half-beat past what the apology needed. Ten years of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually teaches, folded into three seconds. I got genuinely cold. That is a grooming mechanic rendered with total precision and zero applause. And Pace at his light pole feeling "a deep warmth in his body" watching it. God.

"Do you think she'll like the bench?" I said something out loud. That's what I'd text at 1 a.m. — she has a name and a stats class and a scheduled appointment with furniture, and she doesn't know any of them exist yet. The jacket told me and I still wasn't ready for how fast.

Where I'm wobbly: this whole chapter Vee is scenery. Beautifully written scenery — the head thrown back, laughing like someone who'd forgotten she could be seen, which is *exactly* the quality they're hunting — but she's an object in it, graded on a lawn alongside five other women who get graded on their bodies and their airs. The prose gives each of those women one true human detail (the split earbuds, the faceup ignored phone) which is the book flinching on their behalf, and I noticed and appreciated it. But I need Vee's inside, and I need it soon. If this book stays in the predators' heads and lets me tour her like a property with them, that's the naive version and I'm gone. The jacket promises interiority. I'm holding it to that.

Also filed: they *checked* whether Randi was in his section. Brooke checked, Randi answered fast, everyone exhaled. Nobody has checked about Vee. That's a loaded gun on the mantel and I saw them load it.

And the cheeseburger. "I can't eat that," automatic, no glance back. Then "let's go feed you." He feeds her thin apple slices and points her at a woman built on a lavish scale who is unmistakably allowed to eat. I don't think that's accidental either.
