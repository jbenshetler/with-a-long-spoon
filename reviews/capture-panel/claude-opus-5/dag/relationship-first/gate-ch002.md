REACTION

Okay, so this is the chapter that tells me what kind of book I'm actually in, and I liked it a lot more than I expected to after last night.

Because last night was a *reveal* chapter — I spent the whole thing thinking I was watching Vee, the girl on the jacket, get taken apart on a bench, and then he says *there you are, Randi* and the floor moves. Which was a good trick. But a trick is a trick. This morning is the bill for it, and the bill is where I find out if the author can write people or only set pieces.

She can write people. Four minutes in a common room and I have the whole ecology. Brooke not standing up — *the not-standing was the move* — god, yes. I have sat in that chair and I have stood in front of it. The seat by the window every Saturday, the laptop she keeps clicking so the interrogation looks like multitasking, the "I kept thinking you'd said *Chase*." She's filing him. And the guess — *engineering, isn't it* — lands him a notch down without her ever having to reach, and then "oh — math" absorbs the correction without giving the notch back. That is so precisely how it works. Nobody says anything unkind. Everybody is warm. It's warm the whole way through and it's a strip-search.

And the *section* question. She goes right at it — are you in his class — and Randi's fraction-of-a-second gets wider, and then Brooke laughs at herself for going there and apologizes. That apology is the knife going back in the drawer where everyone can see where it lives. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. just about that apology.

Then: "Anyway, I'm sure you want to shower. Sorry to grill you. Go." And it's over because Brooke ended it, and Randi has three lines built and loaded in her mouth that she now has to swallow. The book calls it the cleanest hit, and it's right, and I almost wish it hadn't said so — I'd already felt it. That's my one real gripe with this writer so far and it was worse last night: she keeps landing a beat and then putting a little placard next to it. The held-breath thing at the end — "she had gotten so good at it that it no longer registered as an act, the way a held breath stops feeling held. The breath was held." I had it. I had it at "it felt like there being no door." The placard didn't add anything, it just made me aware of a hand.

But then the duvet. Oh, the duvet. She lies down in last night's clothes with her shoes on and she pulls up this light synthetic thing and the sentence goes *It was not — it was a duvet.* And I know exactly what almost got said, because I was there last night when he pulled the cashmere blanket off the foot of the bed, the one she brought over in week two because she gets cold after. She can't even finish the comparison to herself. That's the whole chapter. That's the book not explaining, and it's so much better than the explaining that I want to shake the author.

What's getting me is the structure of it. I'm two chapters in and I have not met the girl on the back cover. The jacket promised me Vee, and instead I'm being handed, very deliberately, the *reason* — the exact temperature of the morning after the night Randi decided she was never coming out again. She's going to build that plan out of this. A woman who has just discovered she can be reached, deciding on the spot that she will never be reached again, is a woman who needs somebody *else* to stand in the doorway. And the jacket already told me the invitation started as a game between the two of them. So I'm being walked backwards into the motive before I meet the target, and I can feel the shape of it, and it's a shape and not a stall. That's the thing I need from a book and I'm getting it.

Suspicious of: Brooke. Not the plot-suspicious, the social-suspicious — she's collecting Pace's name for a reason and the reason may just be that collecting is what she does, which would be scarier. Also Sloane's laugh, "the laugh that meant nothing on its own and was therefore available for anything." That's a great line and a great girl and I want her in more rooms.

And Kinsey saying he's *short-cute*. Randi matching her pitch too fast because she didn't get to choose the pitch. He's short! Nobody told me that last night, because last night we were in his head and men do not narrate their own height. Wonderful.

Two chapters, one of them nearly wordless with a man's hands on a woman, one of them nothing but women being pleasant to each other, and the second one scared me more.
