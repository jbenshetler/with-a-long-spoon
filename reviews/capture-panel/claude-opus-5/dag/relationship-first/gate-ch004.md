REACTION

Oh, *Cassie*. Cassie is the one I'd text about at 1 a.m. Four words a day, the wrong textbook then the right one, a lab at noon and no particular feelings about the yellow place on Draper — and then she doesn't take the bit. That little dead spot in the room where Randi's name routine is supposed to get its answer and instead just gets "Cassie," one word, pencil back up. I sat up. Somebody in this book finally didn't play. And then *Goodbye, Vee.* The whole word. I read it twice and got cold. Three chapters of this book teaching me to watch for the fraction-of-a-second and here's a girl in a ball cap who's already done the reading. Whatever she saw across that row, she saw it in about ninety seconds and she declined and she didn't fix it when it landed wrong, and Vee smoothed it over, laughing, *it's coffee, weirdo.* That's the saddest line in four chapters and it's about a latte.

And I loved being on Vee's side of the glass at last. I'd been two chapters up in Randi's head where every gesture is a move, and now I'm inside the girl who gets the gesture and feels only warmth, and the warmth is *real* — that's the horrible part. The right pen. That's the one that got me. Nothing praise, praise she didn't work for, and it lands like she did. I know exactly what that is. I have been twenty-one and had someone better-lit than the morning tell me I had good taste in a nothing object and floated home on it.

The blank transcript is the smartest thing the chapter does and also the thing it says three times. She can't reconstruct a word of the hour; she kept the feeling instead. Beautiful — I'd have had it from the parking-ticket story and the cup going down. But then I got told the words were gone, and told she'd been keeping the feeling and not the words, and told the transcript was blank, and by the third pass I was being handed back a thing I already held. Same with the close: the *later* doing in her chest what the right pen had done and the high table had done and the whole sweet hour had done — I'd already felt all three, I didn't need them lined up and totaled for me. This book keeps footnoting its own best effects.

What I did not expect to love: *Floozies*, arriving whole and in her mother's voice before she'd finished forming the picture. That's the shame the jacket promised and it's done in one word, and then underneath it the longer beat of looking that the word was there to cover — the cut of the leaner one's stomach, how low the waistband sat. She's grading those girls and wanting them in the same glance and the cardigan stays where it is and the room doesn't know it's a choice. That's the real thing. That's the book knowing more than it's saying. And then she looks at Randi and "all Vee could do was look at her with a flat helpless want" — which is the same helplessness Randi had on the lawn, the *yes* that arrived before she got to it. Two women wanting each other in the identical grammar, one of them being paid to steer. Merciless is right.

Though — small thing I've now noticed three nights running: this book inventories bodies constantly. Randi's frame, the whole pointing-game parade, now the entire banquette graded by leggings and scrunchie. It's plainly the point, it's how these women *see*, I get it. But it's starting to read as the book's only way of entering a room, and I could use one room where nobody's scale gets assessed.

Suspicion ledger: the stats class was actual coincidence, which is a relief — the plan needed one break and got it honestly. But Brooke filed *Pace* like a name going into a drawer, and I have not forgotten it, and Randi sits on Standards, which is an outrageously good joke to be sitting on this quietly. And I keep turning over that Vee is being given the thing Randi cannot have: an unguarded hour, being liked out loud, *love you, girl.* Randi is going to hand this girl to Pace and watch her get the tenderness she locked herself out of in his bathroom. I'm not sure Randi survives that. I don't think she knows that yet.
