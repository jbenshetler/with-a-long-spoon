REACTION

Oh, this is the one I've been waiting for — the girls' room after, the door shut, the cold line off the window, someone on the bean bag telling it. Three pages and it did more for me than the whole restaurant did, and I loved the restaurant. The book finally let Vee *narrate* her own night instead of living inside it, and the difference between how she tells the brass-cage part and how she tells the tree part — voice dropping, words coming out one at a time so Cassie has to lean in — that's exact. That's how you actually tell it. You do the funny part loud and the real part quiet.

"How much did he give you to drink." God. I laughed and then I sat up. Because Cassie asks it flat and it's the right question — it's the question a friend asks — and Vee's answer is the thing I think this book is actually about: *nobody did anything to me, I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.* She's so proud of it. She should be; it's true as far as she can see. And the jacket already told me the sentence I'm supposed to hear under it, and hearing it is awful and delicious at once. I'm being handled and I know I'm being handled and I'm not mad about it yet.

The spin. The clumsy whole-body turn that nearly takes out the closet door, the room too small to hold it. That's the best thing in the chapter. That's a twenty-one-year-old.

But here's where I got a small cold spot, and it's not the plot, it's the machinery: Cassie keeps getting handed the ominous line. "Goodbye, Vee." "That's not a boy, that's a man." Now "what do I tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain," and then Vee cheerfully reciting *off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel* like a dossier — which is not an address, and the chapter knows it's not an address, and has Cassie say so. Three chapters running, Cassie is the instrument the book uses to tap me on the shoulder. She's too good a person to be a smoke alarm. Give her a Tuesday. Give her something she wants that isn't about Vee's love life. Right now she has a lab at noon, a rec-season pair of lungs, and an unerring nose for wrongness, and I'm starting to be able to hear the author breathing behind her.

The "is he rich?" run felt like the author needing certain facts placed on the table — the house exists, Vee hasn't seen it, the road is remote — and dressing it as banter. It's good banter. I still saw the hand.

What I notice, and I don't think it's a flaw: this is the woman-talk chapter and it is still entirely about the man. Which is honest at that age and in that bloom. But I'm now genuinely hungry for Vee and Randi alone, because *that's* the conversation the jacket promised and the one the book keeps deferring. Randi wasn't in this chapter at all and her absence was loud — I kept waiting for Vee to say *and Randi says*, and she didn't, and I don't know yet if that's the book being careful or Vee simply having two separate rooms she lives in. The second one would be better. The second one is how it actually works.

Also: the last person who heard Vee's full unguarded joy sat on a man's lap and drank wine out of his mouth while she got it secondhand. Cassie's getting the original. Cassie doesn't know what she's got.
