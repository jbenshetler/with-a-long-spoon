REACTION

Oh, they're *shopping*. I had to put the book on my knee for a second when I got it. The jacket told me and I still didn't feel it coming — I thought Randi steering Vee toward Pace was going to be a piece of machinery I'd be told about later, and instead I'm on a quad on a nice Tuesday, holding a warm hand, watching a woman get selected off a walkway like a melon. And the thing that actually turned my stomach in the good way is that Vee apologizes. *She's* the one whose hand flies to her mouth. She walks into an ambush and takes responsibility for the collision. That's the whole book in three seconds and nobody had to say a word about consent for me to feel it.

And then the last line. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I read that and I heard the bathroom lock click in chapter one. Because I know what Randi decided in that mirror — the face, the not-ever, the key turning — and now she has an answer to the problem of being seen, and the answer is: give him somebody else to look at. She doesn't know that's what she's doing, or she half knows, and the book didn't explain it to me, and I got to arrive at it myself in the two seconds after "Let's go feed you." That's the first time this book has really withheld something with a shape instead of narrating around it, and it bought a lot of goodwill from me. Whatever the jacket means by "a game between the two people now closest to her," I now think Pace is *not* the one holding the plan. He thinks he invented this today. "It was the best idea he'd had in a month." That's a man being played by the woman he thinks he's reading, and I would very much like to watch him find out.

Which is my problem with him, and it's growing. He is never wrong. Ever. Every flinch, every half-beat, every "she has moved the wanting, she has not taken it back" — he clocks it, and then the prose clocks it a second time to make sure I did. "The same act. A different owner." Great line! It did not need the paragraph before it or the paragraph after it. And "If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her, good enough that the good feeling and the wanting became one fact" — that's not a man thinking, that's the author leaning over my shoulder with a laser pointer. The effect is that Randi only reaches me secondhand now, translated. Chapter two, with her alone in a cold room under a synthetic duvet with her shoes on, told me more about her than all of Pace's flawless reading here. I want the book to stop trusting him so much. I want him to misread her once, badly.

The heat: the opening worked on me, the timing of it — coming up into her while she's still going, asking the question into that, her body answering before she can make it safe. And the kiss on the path with her own taste still on his mouth, in daylight, in front of everyone, right after she said "maybe." That's the kind of thing I read this shelf for. But I'll admit that by the fourth candidate I was skimming the outfits. Five women, five paragraphs of leggings and scrunchies and the correct trainers — the grading is doing thematic work, I get it, Randi's whole eye is a scale, the apple is right there — but it got listy, and the one I actually wanted her to pick (beanie, wire glasses, back on her elbows, taking up her room) got a "maybe" and walked off, which I suspect is the point and still.

And the food. Third chapter, third feeding beat: the thin apple slices, "I can't eat that," "Let's go feed you." I love that thread and I'm now officially counting, which means the book has one more free use of it before I start to feel handled.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she picked the girl who laughs like nobody's watching, because that's the exact thing she can't do anymore, and she doesn't know that's why.* I need the next chapter to be Vee's. If the next forty pages are Pace correctly interpreting women to me, I'll get restless. If I get Vee in a kitchen with Randi talking about anything at all, I'm here for the rest of the volume.
