REACTION

The concrete pad is the best thing in the book since the fitting, and it's the same scene, and I don't mind that at all — a man standing a woman up on a slab of concrete under cold field lights and putting his hand in one exact place on her back and saying *give me something to push back against*. That's the whole novel in a sentence and I got a little shivery when it landed. *Being jelly doesn't work.* And then: "That's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." I would have taken forty more pages of that. Just the two of them and the tape warbling up to speed and *there's no sorry unless you take off a foot.* The boom box with the electrical tape on the handle. The short-sleeved shirt in October because he dressed for the dance and not the weather — that's the kind of detail this book is genuinely good at, a man's whole priority shown in one wrong sleeve.

And then the book brought a plot in, and I felt my shoulders come up.

The blonde is a device. She's a device with a drink in her hand and makeup that's "doing a lot of work" and she exists to say the poisoned sentence and walk off. This book named a nameless woman on purpose once and I've been carrying that for ten chapters as evidence of how careful it is, and here it hands me a walk-on whose only function is to load the gun, and then hands me a *second* walk-on — the bartender, older, economical, racking glasses — whose only function is to unload it. Two women used as machinery in one chapter. The bartender's "wasn't him they were jealous of, honey" is a good beat and it's also the book leaning over the table to tell me what to think. I can feel the hand on my shoulder again, the way I felt it with the MRI patent.

But. *But.* Here's where I got genuinely unsettled, and it's the reason I'm not putting the book down: Vee's suspicion is wrong in every particular and dead right in its shape. She looks at him and thinks *how much of this is Pace and how much is the Pace I've been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly I can't find the join* — that is the single most awake sentence Vee has had, and it goes by in half a line and the floor eats it. Her instinct fires. It fires at the wrong target, she is humiliated by how wrong she was, and she spends a sleepless night in a laundered shirt that smells of no one teaching herself never to do that again. *She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be. She'd done it to a good man.* That paragraph is the book absolving him at full volume, and I know what he's got in a drawer, and I know what the two of them arranged before she was anything to either of them. So either the book doesn't know what it just did to her, or it knows exactly, and the last line tells me which.

Because the last line is: she's going to Randi's.

I put the page down at that. She has been hurt by a suspicion that was structurally correct, she has decided the lesson is that she was wrong, and the person she is taking that lesson to for polishing is the other half of the thing she was right about. *Cool embarrassment was just information.* Oh, sweetheart. Vee's one immune response went off and Randi is going to be the one who teaches her to read it as a misfire. That's not a romance misunderstanding. That's a training session. If the book delivers that scene with any honesty it's the best chapter it will ever have, and if it delivers it as warm girl-talk and a squeeze of the hand I'll know what I've been reading.

Other things I'm holding: she didn't call Cassie. She stood outside with the phone in her hand and didn't, *because there'd be questions and she'd have to put words to what she'd done.* And then three feet away in the dark, "Cassie was three feet away and would have sat up for this. She did not wake her." That's the fourth time Vee has personally shut that door and the book counted it this time, out loud. Also: borrowed boots again. Meg lent her boots that didn't fit on the hike; now it's Dana's ropers, and Meg dressing her with a keyhole shirt and *well, that's settled.* Meg keeps being the only person who hands Vee something without an angle on it and the book keeps giving her thirty seconds.

The cab fare "was more than she'd guessed, and the money had been meant for other things" — after last week's credit card with nothing under it. The book is keeping a ledger on that girl's money and I trust it entirely.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she got it RIGHT and got punished for it and now she's going to go apologize to the woman who set the whole thing up. I'm going to be sick.*
