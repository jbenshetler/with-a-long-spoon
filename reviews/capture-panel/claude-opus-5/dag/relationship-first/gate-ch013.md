REACTION

Two pages and I read them twice.

What got me is that this is the third debrief in four chapters and it's the first one that isn't a performance. At the smoothie place Vee was *telling* — she had a version, she had a dignified line ready and lost it, she was watching Randi's face to see it land. Here she just says it. She sits down like an old woman, makes the joke about her legs filing a complaint, and then hands over the actual thing: I called him back in, I took my shirt off, he didn't touch me. No shaping. No checking. And Cassie, who has spent this entire book at four words a day, goes *rapt*. The book says the word flat out and I believe it.

And then it doesn't say the thing. "Vee," Cassie said finally. Soft. Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break. And Vee says *I know.* Neither of them finishes it and I don't know which of the two possible sentences it was — is it *Vee, you're gone* or *Vee, I'm happy for you* or *Vee, be careful* — and the book knows I don't know and is leaving it there. That's the withholding-with-a-shape I said I needed. Then "Borrow real boots next time," "I'm keeping the shirt," "Keep the shirt," and we're out. She gives her permission for the one thing Vee actually asked permission for without asking. God.

Also: Vee told Cassie the truck. She has not told Randi the truck. Not yet, anyway. Which of them gets it first, and in which register, is now the thing I'm sitting up about, because the jacket promised me lunches too intimate to name and the smoothie one was intimate but it was also *managed* — Randi asked the leading question about being chosen for and let it go when Vee flinched. Cassie didn't ask anything. She just got told.

And the flannel. She's wearing it in the room. She kept it, she didn't ask, he didn't ask for it back. That shirt lives in a gym bag behind the bench seat of a truck Randi has absolutely sat in. Somebody is going to recognize it and I want to be there. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she's wearing the shirt around the dorm and neither of us has said out loud yet who else knows that shirt.*

Small thing I loved: she said *and then we ate* the way other girls say *and then we kissed*. Rye and horseradish and the pits on the wax paper. That's the whole girl in one line — she's been hungry her entire life and a man packed her a sandwich.

What I'm still holding against the book: Cassie's opening is the bit again. Blood type, windowless van. She has been the state-troopers joke three times running now. But then it drops the bit inside four lines and lets her be a person, and "Look at you," she said, and meant it — that's the first thing Cassie's said in this book that's about Vee's life instead of Vee's danger. I'll take it as a down payment on a Tuesday of her own.
