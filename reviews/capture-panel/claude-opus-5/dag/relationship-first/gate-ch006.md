Oh, the froyo cup. That's the whole chapter for me and it's three sentences long: *Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, that's plenty, that's enough now. Because I have already watched this man slice an apple thin because she'd eat it thin, and thick-slice her nothing, and I have watched him say *let's go feed you* to a woman who won't eat a cheeseburger. And now here's a second girl with a second mother in her head, and the mother goes quiet in his presence, and she builds a cup too big and leans it and bites her lip and doesn't care. He is feeding women who ration themselves. That's not a coincidence the book stumbled into; that's the shape. And it's the first time I've felt genuinely uneasy rather than just wary, because it works on me too — I *want* the mother to shut up, I was glad for her, and about four seconds later I remembered Randi locked in a bathroom deciding never to come out again.

What I actually loved: "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again." A whole ex-boyfriend, a whole year of her life, a whole *kind* of girl she's been — the one who keeps the business major alive until two in the morning — in one deadpan line from the girl in the ball cap. Randi taking the phone *out of her hand.* Not asking for it, taking it.
