REACTION

Okay. I did not expect the name to be withheld and I did not expect it to be *Randi*, and I sat back in the chair when it landed. "There you are, Randi." I'd read the whole chapter assuming this was Vee — the jacket trains you to — and the reveal doesn't just flip a card, it re-poisons everything behind it. The woman who is supposedly running the game with him has already lost it, in chapter one, on a piece of furniture he built for her. That's a better opening than I bought this book expecting.

The apple. God, the apple. Bite, kiss, bite, and the hands going up her sides and turning back before they arrive, three times, and her losing the thread of the bite she's holding — that's the hottest thing in the chapter and she's fully dressed for all of it. Whoever wrote that knows that anticipation is the whole instrument. And the apple is doing four things at once: it's foreplay, it's him having noticed she eats thin slices and not thick ones, it's her mother on egg whites "a thing she had told him with a knife in it," and it's a man feeding a woman who has spent her life portioning herself. The book never says *eating disorder.* "Apples were permitted." That's the line. That's all it needs.

And then the water bottle cap under the thumbnail. Nobody comments. She doesn't know she's doing it. He sees it and takes the bottle and pulls her closer and the book just moves on. I got a chill. That's a book that knows more than it's saying.

What I'm actually going to be thinking about all day is the bathroom. That's the chapter — not the bench, the bathroom. The lock turning over, which she has never done in his house. Her face in the mirror, and the *smile sitting on top of the face that had begged*, and the decision arriving "quiet as a deadbolt going over." He could have the face. I have read a hundred scenes of a woman being undone and maybe four where the undoing is immediately, consciously, permanently sealed over by the woman herself while the man in the next room is quietly congratulating himself. The gap between his closing paragraphs and hers is *vicious*. He's thinking about how he found her hunger and answered it. She's thinking about exit strategy. And he's not a fool — he's attentive, he noticed the bracing, he noticed the flinch at "you're beautiful," he let the locked door be hers — and he still reads the night exactly backwards. That's the cruelty the blurb promised and I didn't think it would arrive this early.

The flinch, by the way. He says "you're beautiful" and she flinches. After all of it — after she's begged, after she's cried into the leather — the only thing that actually hurt was the tenderness. That's the whole thesis and it took four words and a body.

My hesitations, because I have them. Pace's POV spends a fair amount of energy admiring itself. He is patient, he is not in a hurry, he has learned not to interpret things for her, he built the mechanisms himself and they are silent and well-oiled. Some of that is character — a man whose self-image is *craftsman* — but there are stretches where I could feel the book deciding I should find him extraordinary before I'd finished deciding on my own. What saves it, and I think saves it on purpose, is that final page where his read of the evening is flatly wrong. If the book knows that's what it's doing, I'm in for the long haul. If it doesn't, I'll be able to tell within two chapters.

The other thing: the prose sometimes hands me the meaning of a beat I'd already received. "It was not really a question. It was a request." I *knew*. The bathroom monologue has a paragraph — "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for" — that is genuinely good writing and also the novel putting its own X-ray up on the light box for me. I'd have preferred to keep the flinch and the deadbolt and figure out the rest myself. It's a small tax and I paid it, but I noticed.

And Daphne. One paragraph, no explanation, "it had cost him more than he had known, then, that anything could cost." That is the right amount. Don't tell me for two hundred pages.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the girl who's supposed to be running the con is the one who got caught in chapter one, and she just decided to never let him see her again while letting him hold her. also she's going to hand him another girl, isn't she. that's what happens next. she's going to find him someone she doesn't have to be.*

Sup with him with a long spoon, indeed.
