REACTION

Oh, this one got me, and I'm a little annoyed about how easily.

It's a meal chapter. Of course it's a meal chapter — this is exactly the thing I stay for, two people at a table with food between them and the whole evening happening in the space around what they're actually saying. And Rivers does the smart thing and doesn't tell me what they talked about. Vee can't hold the transcript, again — same as Chi Latte, the leaning-in was the whole content — and I noticed that's twice now the book has put her in a room where the words evaporate and only the feeling of being chosen stays. That's not laziness, that's the pattern being laid down: Vee doesn't remember content, she remembers who made her feel like this. Somebody is going to be able to do anything to this girl.

The hunger. God. She hasn't eaten since breakfast because she spent the afternoon on the dress and the other dress and the borrowed shoes, and then the door breathes garlic at her and the hollow that's been opening since noon reaches up. And then: "it went down into the hollow and the hollow only opened wider." And then: "it kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." I felt that in my own chest and I also went cold, because I've already read the apple chapter. Pace sliced a honeycrisp thin, cut-sides up, because that's the only way Randi will take food — a woman who has portioned herself down to nothing. And here he is ordering for a girl who has no lid on her at all, building it as he goes, bright thing and dark thing, one to wake you and one to fold you under. Two women, two appetites, the same man reading both and feeding both exactly to spec. He's *good* at this. That's the whole problem.

"May I choose?" Three words and I went straight back to the lamppost on the quad — *I point. You pick.* He is running the same instrument on Vee that he ran on Randi, only inverted: with Randi he pointed and let her choose so the wanting would be hers; with Vee he takes the choosing away, because what Vee's starving for isn't permission, it's someone to stop asking her to hold the wheel. "He let her carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could" — no, "She let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could." That line is the most frightening sentence in the chapter and it's dressed up as the most restful one. She loses the thread of what he's saying and nothing bad happens and *that's* the relief. She stops watching herself eat. The background readiness for the bill to come due just isn't there. Reader, the bill is coming due, it was drawn up before you met her.

The hand. That's the beat that actually landed on me and not on my suspicion. Not something beginning but something resolving — her hand wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly. I've been handed a lot of first-touch paragraphs and most of them are weather. That one had a shape.

Where I got itchy: the kiss. The rule stated out loud — *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days* — the walking-away, the hand going taut, the "But. I'm making an exception." I saw every stone of that path from four feet off and it's the one place in the chapter where I felt the author steering rather than Vee moving. It's a romance beat and I don't have the contract for it; the small kiss, then his restraint, then her taking it — it's charming and it's a bit of a dance number. And the prose does the thing I always catch: "It was too much for what it was. And underneath the too-much, stranger, was the other thing:" — the sentence before it already gave me the feeling, this one just labels the drawer. Same with Cassie's "That's not a boy. That's a man," which is doing theme work at me in a girl's mouth. Cassie has been so good precisely because she says four words and lets them be flat.

What I want, badly, is Randi. She wasn't in this chapter and her absence was loud — because I know she's on the other end of it, waiting to be told, straddling him on the couch and drinking wine she won't let him have any of, with that want too big for her face. Vee had the nicest night of her life and somewhere across town it's going to become a story someone gets off on hearing. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he asked to order for her and I gasped like it was a knife.*
