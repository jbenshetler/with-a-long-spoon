REACTION

So this is the fourth time I've been handed the flannel shirt. Once live in the truck, once to Cassie, once to Meg and Kayla in the gold dining hall, and now the no-tag reveal comes back around with Cassie saying *He sews* like she's setting a fact down on a table. And I want to be annoyed about it and I'm only about forty percent annoyed, because the book clearly knows what it's doing: Vee is a girl who doesn't become real to herself until she's said it out loud to somebody, and the retellings are the actual mechanism of her life, not padding. Fine. I see it. But I've now got a shape in my head — thing happens, thing gets told — and I could feel myself skimming Cassie's recaps to get to the next new line, and skimming is how books end for me.

What saved it: Vee doesn't tell her the whole thing. She tells Cassie the box, the bra, the kneeling, *like I was supposed to be there* — and she does not tell her about the satin. The entire engine of the last chapter, the thumbprint going to a patch, the dying-of-it on the couch with the tea, and she keeps it. That's the first thing Vee has kept from anyone, and she keeps it from the one person in this book who isn't running anything, and I don't think the book will let that go. She gave Pace her body in daylight and she's giving Cassie the story with one panel cut out of it. That's a girl learning to have a private life. It's also a girl learning to edit, which is how you become someone who can be handled.

And *I* was grinding on *him* is the best laugh the book has gotten out of me. The patting-her-own-ass-like-a-diagram bit, "grave as a witness" — I made a noise. Cassie asking if he's gay is exactly what a friend says at that stage of a story that's gone on too long without sex, and the powerlifter-not-bodybuilder correction coming out of Vee automatically is perfect, she's absorbed his taxonomies already.

*It was like his eyes asked, May I look.* Okay. That one got me in the chest. And Cassie saying *He asked* before Vee has worked out that he did — Cassie hearing the shape of it faster than Vee does, again — and *No. I think I have it.* Cassie has better instruments than anyone in this book and she's using them on her roommate's boyfriend for the fourth chapter running and I am still waiting for her to get a single want of her own. The hand on the knee, once, taken back. She's so careful. She is going to be the one who gets hurt by something she didn't touch.

What I actually noticed, though: Randi isn't in this chapter. Not mentioned. Not once. The girl who called her *dear friend* in front of Brooke and killed the wait-a-day rule and hung the whole *ravish* picture up in Vee's head — and Vee comes home from the most naked afternoon of her life and goes to Cassie with it. So either Vee has instincts she doesn't know she has, or the Randi version is coming later and will be a different edit, and I badly want to compare the two. And I keep thinking about chapter 14, Randi on top of him saying *tell me,* and now there's a whole afternoon of Vee half-naked on a riser waiting to be told. *Her stomach is soft* nearly took Randi apart. What does *I measured her bare* do. What does *I'm building her a dress* do, when the dress is the one thing Randi never once asked him for.

Also, quietly: he still hasn't opened the bedroom door. Two chapters, and the only closed door in the house stayed closed, and the book made Vee notice the satin she'd worn for it. That withholding is either the most careful courtship I've read or the most patient construction, and my problem is I can't tell, and I think the book knows I can't tell.
