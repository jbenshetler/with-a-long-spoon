The boot. He didn't ask. That's what I'm going to be chewing on all night — three chapters of *may I choose*, *may I call you*, the whole courtly apparatus, and then he crouches down on the trail and just takes her boot off and peels her sock down and has her bare foot in his hand before she's finished being horrified about what the sock might smell like. No permission. And the book lets her stomach clench and then lets the clench go without ever naming it, and I sat up, because that's the first inch he's taken. I don't think it's sinister. I think it's *correct* — asking would have turned a blister into an event, and he knew it, and he was right again, God help me. But the jacket says nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over and I've now watched him take a small thing and be thanked for it, and the book put that scene forty pages before she unbuttons her shirt on purpose. That's not an accident. That's a shape.

The dress. Oh, the dress is going to arrive. Russet silk, the color of a brick, *like the one* — she says the wanting out loud and then puts it back on the shelf herself, the way she's been trained to, and he says *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and doesn't pick it up. Flat, no weight, and I heard the drawer close. He now has two things in that locked drawer: the nicest-a-person-has-been-to-me, and the dress. He is building a file on this girl out of the things she's ashamed of wanting, and he calls the not-mentioning-it decency, and it *is* decency, and it is also inventory. I would text a friend at 1 a.m.: he's going to buy her the dress and it's going to be the most romantic thing in the book and I'm going to want to throw it across the room.

But the truck. The truck got me completely. The flannel handed over with no speech attached, and then him just getting out and standing in the rain with his back turned — and Vee taking a second to even understand what she's looking at, *his shirt and then the truck, both* — and then her mother's voice coming up and her body answering each accusation with heat, *wanted to be the very thing the voice said.* Yes. That's the actual engine of this girl and the book finally wrote it plain. And then she doesn't wait, she doesn't get ravished against a tree the way Randi hung it up for her — she knocks on the glass, she crooks a finger, she puts his eyes on hers and holds them so he can't look down, and then she looks down *on purpose* so his eyes have to follow hers to her own body. She directed it. Vivienne Thorne, who apologized for a collision someone else engineered, ran that. And then his hand is shaking. That's the turn. She reaches for it to start the next thing and it's *shivering*, and he'd been a long way from the question of whether he was cold — which is the closest this book has come to letting him be caught out, and it's not him being wrong about someone, it's him being overtaken, which is not the same thing and I'm noting the difference. She put his hand under the flannel against her own belly, and that's the first time in twelve chapters anyone in this book has put someone else's body ahead of their own appetite, and it was the girl everyone's been handling who did it. I'm a little wrecked about it. He left the hand there a beat longer than he had to. That beat was the whole chapter.

The gym bag I liked precisely because it *isn't* planted: razor, thin soap, folded briefs, a bag that lives behind a seat because he stays places. Where does he stay, I wonder.

No Cassie. And last chapter Vee decided the smoothie place *isn't really her place* and moved on to feeling picked, and now she's spent a whole Sunday not thinking about her. Still waiting on that.

Mostly what I want now is to watch Randi get told about this.
