So she cut the part where she took her shirt off.

That's the whole chapter for me. Three nights ago I sat in that truck with her while she unbuttoned by feel with her eyes on his, and then she went upstairs and gave Cassie all of it — *I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me.* — and Cassie went still and said her name like setting something down where it wouldn't break. And tonight, at a gold table with Kayla and Meg, the story runs: rain, shirt, he got out, I kept it — and then *I was about to kiss him and he was shivering, so I had him drive us home.* She put a kiss where the undressing goes. She edited herself for the audience, smoothly, mid-flow, while Kayla had both palms flat on the table. And nobody in the book underlined it. I had to catch it myself, and I caught it, and I actually said *oh* out loud on the bus.

Which means Cassie is the one she tells the true version to, and Cassie was not invited to dinner. They're eating in the athletic dining hall — Kayla's whole theory about walking in like you've always eaten there — and the actual athlete she lives with isn't at the table. Two chapters running now Vee has decided, warmly, generously, on Cassie's behalf, where Cassie wouldn't want to be. *It isn't really her place, the smoothie one.* That's how it goes, isn't it. Nobody drops a friend. You just keep booking the room she wouldn't like.

I'll admit the new girls irritated me for about a page. Chapter fifteen is late to be handed two named friends with hair and a fork and a boyfriend named Danny, especially when what I've been waiting for since the jacket is Vee and Randi alone with a lunch between them and no man in the room. This was a chorus, not a conversation. But then Meg — who I liked instantly for eating like she had all night — said *everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think.* And then: *sometimes they even are.* That's the first time anyone in this book has put a thumb lightly against Vee without being either a mother's voice or a person running a game, and the book let it not land. *Vee let it go by. It didn't catch on anything.* I've been that girl. Nothing catches on anything at twelve days.

Twelve. She didn't have to count. God.

The powerlifter thing is where I nearly put it down. Short, courtly, builds furniture, never wrong about anyone, lifts before dawn because it clears his head, writes exactly six hours and stops because after that it's no good — this man is getting buffed with every chapter and I can feel the book's hand on my shoulder again, telling me what to admire. I don't need him to be impressive. I need him to be wrong once, out loud, and the book to know it. That's still the load-bearing question and it's now four chapters since I asked it.

Kayla says there's no meal at two, he wants you for the afternoon, bring a change of clothes, and she means it as a scream at a slumber party, and I'm sitting here knowing exactly what she's right about and how she's wrong about the shape of it. *That's not a message, that's a ransom note* is funnier than the girls know. I've been ahead of Vee since page one and it's never cost me this much. Saturday at two, and the last thing she says about it is that her heart is doing something quick and light and stupid and she lets it.

I want Saturday and I'm scared of it, and I'd text a friend right now: *she told the sanitized version. she told the roommate the truth and the pretty girls the edited cut and the book didn't even stop to point at it.*
