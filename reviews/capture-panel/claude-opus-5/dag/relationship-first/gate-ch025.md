Oh, the stats class. Twenty-something chapters of latent variables going nowhere and then the man at the front puts up two seasons of baseball and says *same numbers, nobody touched one of them* and I actually sat back. Because that's it. That's the whole book laid on the board in a flat cereal-box voice while three women in row eight aren't listening. Vee gives Cassie a true version, gives Randi a true version, gives Kayla and Meg a true version, and every single one is accurate year by year, and put together they are a lie the size of her life. And the book won't even nudge me. It puts the red dot on the number and lets us go.

And then — *Cassie had never gone down into them at all.* That's the best Cassie has ever been. She didn't look at the professor once. She doesn't care about the dress, she has worn the same fleece through two winters, the only part of any of this Cassie has ever been able to see is her friend. "Was it a good night?" "Oh, yes." And something eased in her face and she picked up the highlighter. God. She asked the one question that was actually about Vee and got the one answer that was actually true, and went back to work. Meanwhile Randi is reaching into the gold of the day and finding the one snag in it inside of ninety seconds — *I'll bet you've had the whole outfit planned* — and turning it into a date. The eyebrow going up about shoes and coming back down is Cassie watching herself get out-bid for the tenth time and not saying so.

*You lucky slut,* landing lower than Randi meant it to, onto a body still keeping the account from eight that morning. That's a small perfect nasty thing.

But the foot. The foot. Randi down on one knee on a pale floor in her own beautiful clothes, saying *I've got her* to the woman with the box, and then not reaching for the box — holding Vee's foot in both hands and turning it to the light to look at the frosted plum that Vee painted for a man, and saying *Mm*, and running a thumb once across the big toenail, and Vee's floor tipping and her hand going out to Randi's shoulder to stop the room swinging. And Randi looks up and *smiles and says nothing at all.* That is the most erotic thing in this book so far and it is a toenail. She didn't even have to pull the thread; she touched the varnish and the whole girl rang. And she *knew*, and she let it be nothing, and went back to the buckle. That's the same door she shut so gently at the green velvet booth — *you don't have to tell me* — except this time she opened it a half inch first to see if it was still there. It's still there.

And then — same shoe size. *God help us both, it's practically a marriage.* With the white-gold band sitting right there on her wrist where nobody in twenty-five chapters has said one word about it. The book made a marriage joke over a plain wedding band nobody is allowed to see. I'm not going to be told, am I. I'm going to find it out the hard way, at the worst possible moment, and I will deserve it for laughing.

The cards. She stood there with the debit between her fingers — *the card she paid her own way with, the one that only ever spent what was already behind it* — and put it back. Every lunch, every coffee, she has paid her own, and Randi watched her do it and never once reached again, and that was Vee's whole floor. She just took it out from under herself, on purpose, with her face perfectly still, because *the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch.* There is nothing under that number. It will be sitting there next month. That's the first thing this has actually cost her and she handed it over gladly, which is the jacket's whole sentence and I felt it arrive as a *joy*. She's not being robbed. She's spending.

And the cruelty of the staging: Randi beside her, buying loafers that cost several times what Vee just went into debt for, not looking at the number, talking the whole time about a woman who ruined a coat so that Vee doesn't have to watch her not look. Is that mercy or is that the demonstration? Both. It's always both with her. And then she walks out in them, because shoes are a thing you trade mid-afternoon when you pass better ones, and Vee carries her box against her chest with both arms like it might get up and leave her. *You can put it in the trunk, it's not going to spoil.* That one went in under my rib the way it went in under hers.

But here's what I'm really carrying out of this chapter. Randi went still before she said anything about the dress. Just looked, the way you take in a thing you're going to have to account for. *He didn't make this for you. He made it to you* — and she's right, and it's the best line anyone in this book has produced about Pace, and she produced it about a dress her man sat up all night making for another girl. And then Vee says *he was up all night finishing it, I watched him sleep,* and Randi's hand stays on the shoulder seam and she goes somewhere behind her own face — and **Vee's heart goes out to her.** Poor Randi, with her beautiful boys in a hurry, nobody who ever sat up a night over anything of hers. That's the most merciless thing this book has done and it's done with pity, pointed the wrong way, from the wrong woman, with all the warmth in the world.

Also: three chapters ago she asked him *how long are you going to take* and called him selfish and wouldn't eat the stew. And now she is personally putting this girl in four-inch heels that will make her stop a room. She's not waiting anymore. She's dressing her.

And the laugh. The real one, the one that costs her something — Vee got it, in Vee's own store, on Vee's own ground, with a clearance flat one brisk walk from becoming a sandal. That's the first time Vee has had something Randi doesn't. It bent Randi over the bench. If this book has a heart it's in that aisle, two women being stupid about shoes while an old couple watches and sees nothing at all.

The dress has still been shown to not one living soul. Her mother is coming to that induction and her father isn't, and Vee's private daydream is to walk in *late, on purpose,* bare-shouldered, slit to the hip, and let the room turn. I want that room. I am afraid of that room. I want it anyway.
