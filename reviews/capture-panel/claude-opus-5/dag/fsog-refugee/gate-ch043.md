So there it is. Forty-three chapters and she finally opens the hood, and she does it in the one room I never thought to be afraid of.

I had to put the book face-down on my knee at *I pointed at her and watched your face.* Not because I didn't know — the jacket told me, my own notes told me, I've been writing "the floor was poured without her" for weeks like a woman warding something off. I knew it in the abstract. What I didn't know was that she was *chosen off a lawn*, in daylight, in the second week of term, as the last and best item in a game of this-one-not-that-one, and that the choosing was so hot to the two of them he'd have had her on the quad for it. Vee's whole spring — the drawer, the forty scrunchies, the juice held to her mouth — starts as a man reading a woman's breath change and pointing.

And here's the part that actually took the floor out from under me, and it's not the quad. *You taught me. Not with anything you said. Your hand went cold on mine at the two that went everywhere together.* He read her off her *body*, not her words. That's his method. That is the exact method that produced *"This?"* in the kitchen and *"Slow. For me."* and the hand that went away the instant she said *don't* — he watches, he reads the answer off her skin, and then he hands the choice back to her as though she'd authored it. I have spent two volumes calling that the most beautiful consent I've read in this genre and I still think the moments are beautiful and now I can see the machine shop they came out of. He said it himself about Randi: *your face said yes before the rest of you got there. That part I didn't do. That part was all you.* That's his whole ethics in one line, and it's *almost* right, and the place it's wrong is the only place that matters. The wanting being real doesn't settle who set the table. He thinks it does. He genuinely thinks it does.

And then — *somebody has to get her used to my mouth.* I had to get up and walk to the kitchen. That kiss is the thing I've been tracking since the practice room. I wrote "the third kiss. The one Vee's arms are slow coming down from." I wrote that the body had the noun before the mouth did and I wanted the word to be *hers.* And now I'm hearing it reported from the other side of the couch as a conditioning schedule with a progress note — *she wouldn't let it end. She came after me. She took it* — delivered like a technician's log, except it isn't, except Randi's coming on her own words while she says it. The sidewalk I called briefly luminous was an installation step and a woman's private disaster at the same time, and I don't get to have one without the other. I'm furious and I was also *reading faster.* That's the trick this book keeps playing on me and I keep walking into it.

But — and this is why I'm not throwing it — the book is not winking. It does two things in this chapter that buy it everything. One: *"Does she want it?"* and then *"That isn't what I asked."* **He asked the big one.** He asked it balls-deep in the woman who built the whole thing, and she dodged it twice, and he came back for it. He's been asking Vee about three inches of underwear for forty chapters and he asked *this* out loud about her to the only other person who knows, which means the question exists in him, which means it is coming. Randi turned her face to the back of the couch on purpose so he couldn't have it, *and she forgot the mirror,* and the author gives it to him anyway, the whole time.
