Oh, that hurt, and it hurt in the good way, and I want to say out loud that the person who did the hurting in this chapter is *Vee*.

Because look at the first page. Cassie's already up, cap on, and the look goes *past* Vee to Randi and comes back folded away. "I'll see you at the dorm." And Vee — my Vee, the one who reads a room instantly and smooths every rough beat in it — doesn't read that one. Doesn't even try. And then thirty seconds later, when Randi actually offers, *we could have dragged her*, Vee is the one who shuts the door. She could have stopped at "she's got practice," which was true and clean and would have kept the invitation alive for next week. She didn't. She added the leggings, the matched hair, the *not her thing*. She built Cassie a little box labeled DOESN'T BELONG HERE and set it down herself, so nobody would have to. I said in my notes I thought Cassie would get edged out and it would hurt. I did not expect the hand on the wedge to be Vee's, or for the book to be kind enough not to underline it — just that one line: *underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie.* That's the sound of a girl choosing, and the book is going to make her pay for it, and she'll deserve it, and I'll still be on her side.

And then the chapter goes and earns Randi's side of it too, which is the part that makes me crazy. Randi opens with the bad-date story and I clocked what it was within three sentences — she's laying the ruler down before she measures. *A man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else.* Ma'am. MA'AM. That is not about a lacrosse boy from Friday. That's a woman who got taken all the way out on a bench she asked for and then locked herself in a bathroom, saying the true thing sideways in a smoothie shop to a girl who has no idea she's hearing a confession. She's telling Vee what to want. She's also telling herself what she had and gave away.

The extraction was beautiful and I hated watching it work. *You let him choose for you. And you liked it.* Not a question, said slow, held up to the light — and then she doesn't push, she lets Vee have the cover story ("it was just nice, not having to be in charge"), and the not-pushing is the technique. It's Pace's technique. Same hands. Two people running the same play on the same girl from opposite sides and neither of them has to do anything crude, they just have to keep leaving the door unlatched. And then Vee says *I made an exception* — except she doesn't, she can't, it comes out as *because of him*, because Randi got there first with *because you couldn't not.* Put the truer smaller word in her mouth and she'll say it. I have read a hundred books where a man does that and it's called mastery. Watching a girl do it over beet-and-wheatgrass at a window table is somehow worse and I could not look away.

And the high chairs. Feet not touching the floor, the whole bright room laid out in front of her, the cardigan going pilled at the cuffs in a place full of bare arms — *underdressed before she had looked at a single person.* I felt that in my actual body. I have been that woman on that stool.

Then Brooke arrives and the temperature drops ten degrees without anyone being rude, which is Brooke's entire skill set and I did tell you I'd watch her run a room. *We missed you Thursday* — one friendly sentence that puts a whole week Vee isn't invited to onto the table. And the tall one who never speaks, who finishes her circuit of Vee and hands back nothing but smile, and at the end gives her *the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over.* That's appraisal. That's someone checking the goods. I don't know yet whether the house knows what Randi's doing or whether they're just sorority girls being sorority girls, and the not-knowing is doing excellent work on me.

But here's the line that got me, the one I'd text at 1 a.m.: **"Vee's my dear friend."** With the cool damp hand closing over hers on the white tabletop, in plain view, in no hurry to let go. And Vee doesn't know what it cost — *she knew only, without being able to say how, that it had cost something.* God. Because it did cost something, and not the thing Vee thinks. Randi just claimed her out loud in front of the women she has organized her entire life around being seen by, and the thing is — I don't think that was a move. Or it was a move *and* it was true, and Randi can't tell the difference anymore either. My notes said I couldn't find the seam. There is no seam. That's the horror and that's the romance.

And then she says *love you, girl* on the sidewalk, ten lines after the other girl tossed a *love you* over her shoulder on the way out the door, and the book just sets those two next to each other and says nothing. Cheap version, real version, identical sounds. Cruel. Correct.

So: the chapter I'd been waiting for, sort of. Randi and Vee in a room with the wanting grown — and the wanting is *all over* this, Randi with her face turned to Vee the whole walk, Vee's cheek going hot on that side and blaming the sun, *two small skies where her eyes should have been*, the hug she leans into like it's already a thing they do. Vee doesn't know. Randi may not fully know. The jacket told me and I'm sitting here holding it.

What I'm missing, and I'll say it plain because I promised myself I'd track it: **Pace has not been in a room with Vee since the dinner.** He's been a subject at lunch twice now. I love what the book does with him as a reported object — she can't repeat the words he ordered in, she can only tell Randi the *shape* of him — but I've now had two consecutive chapters of Vee describing a man to a woman who is also his girlfriend, and the pattern is starting to show its bones. The hike was promised. Real shoes. Off six-fourteen. I want the house and the long table with the top on it and I want to see whether the *may I* holds when the stakes are her body. If chapter twelve is another lunch I'm going to be short with it.
