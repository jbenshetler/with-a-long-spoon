Okay. I have to say the chair first, because it's the thing I can't put down. He comes through to meet her and sees *the chair by the door with nothing on it, and set that aside.* She didn't bring a bag. She had already decided, before the tires hit the gravel, that she wasn't staying — and the evidence was sitting in his hallway in plain sight and he looked right at it and filed it. That's the whole book in one sentence. The most attentive man I have read in five years, the one who noticed how she slices apples and laid the cut sides up, noticing and *setting aside.* I want to shake him.

And then the other refusal, which is worse. *"Stay. I'm starving. I'll cook. Are you hungry?" "No thanks."* Bright, from inside the camisole going over her head. He fed her once and it pulled the real voice out from under the face and she went and locked the bathroom door over it, and now food from his hands is the one thing she will not take. That's not a mood. That's the deadbolt, still over. She turned his deadbolt going in and she turned it going out and she wouldn't eat. I noticed the chapter opens with him hungry and ends with him eating alone at the table and then making *more*, and I don't think that's an accident, and it made my chest hurt for a man I don't trust.

The sex itself — God. The lowering-herself-an-inch-at-a-time with her eyes on his face and not on what she's holding, making him watch, making him say her name, *she had earned it and she knew it* — that was genuinely hot, bodies that know each other, nobody asking permission because they don't need to and that's its own kind of intimacy. And then *Tell me*, and it curdles in my mouth while I'm still enjoying it, which I suspect is exactly the arrangement this book has made with me. Gardenia and musk, warm on her since morning. Shampoo-clean hair and *subtly, underneath it, a hint of chlorine. Like a pool.* That's the thing I flinched at. Not because it's a violation of the rules they've set between them — they set them — but because that's Vee. That's the girl who loved the water before she had a body to be a problem, and her chlorine is being inventoried in a dark bedroom as fuel, and she's three miles away in a borrowed flannel she didn't ask to keep, radiant, telling Cassie she got up a mountain on her own legs. The book is not winking. It let me feel it go sour. That's the only reason I'm still here, and it's reason enough.

But: *Her stomach is soft.* And then — *He did not say how he knew. That she had given him herself, in the truck with the rain coming down, and he kept it.* He kept it. Again. He kept her embarrassing true line before and now he's keeping the truck. He's started running a private account that Randi isn't on, and he doesn't know he's doing it, or he does, and either way that's the crack the whole thing goes down. And it's being built out of *decency.* He's not lying to protect himself. He's holding something back because it was hers. I don't know how a man gets out of that honestly and I am going to read every page of him trying.

And then *I want us.* Torn out of her at his ear, and I have been sitting here for ten minutes deciding what "us" means and I don't think she knows either. The three of them? Or the two of them, said in the voice of a woman who can feel something being lost? Because her belly went tight as a board under his hand and he took it for want, and he was wrong before when he thought she'd finally let someone feed her, so I don't believe him now. She clenched and held and went silent with her eyes shut *long enough that he said her name* and then drove like she was trying to get somewhere before the thought caught up. And she skipped the one part she keeps for herself — going slack on his chest after — and that's the tell. She has given up the thing she actually likes in order not to be lying still and unguarded in a room with him.

The Blondie, too. He had Steely Dan on in the front rooms before; tonight it's the disco one, cool voice over the pulse — and if that's the one I think it is, the one about the heart of glass, somebody in the author's chair is being very funny and very cruel.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she didn't bring a bag and he SAW THE CHAIR and did nothing* and *she won't eat his food anymore, that's the whole plot, that's it, that's the knife.* Also: I am now genuinely frightened of what happens when Vee finds out her shampoo was on a report. I signed for this. I didn't know it would feel like this.
