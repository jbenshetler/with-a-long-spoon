REACTION

Okay so I'm texting you at 1 a.m. because this book just did the thing.

The opening — him coming up over her *while* she's still going, kissing her with her own taste still on his mouth, and then asking the question into all that softness — I had to put the book down on my chest for a second. And then she came from the *question*. Not from anything he did. From being asked. That's the kind of writing I left the whole genre looking for and couldn't find: heat that's about what's happening between two people's heads, not an inventory of body parts. And then — this is the part that got me — he doesn't pounce on it. He waits for the heat to leave the room. He wants her "gathered back into herself" before he asks for real, because he wants her able to actually choose, and he says outright that mattered to him more than the answer did. Reader, I nearly cried. That is the exact man I have been looking for through about forty bad books.

And then the other shoe. "Only in a threesome. With you." She didn't take the wanting back — she moved it. She made herself the generous girlfriend instead of a woman who wants a woman, because the second one is, what did it say, unsurvivable. And he *sees* it, and he lets her have the costume, because he thinks he's being gentle with her. He thinks he's handing her a gift. He's handing her a better hiding place. One chapter after she stood in his bathroom and decided *not ever* — she's found a way to want something enormous without ever having to admit it's hers, and he's helping. He's helping so lovingly. I don't think he knows. That's what's making my skin crawl in the good way.

"I point. You pick." God. That line is the whole reason I'm still here. He's not choosing a woman for them, he's teaching her she's allowed to choose, and watching her learn it is the thing turning him on. That's dominance that makes room for her and it's *exactly* my drug. And the walk is so good — the way she starts out doing bits, *I'm being a very good sport about your little game*, and a quarter mile later she's hunting ahead of him, her hand asking *that one? now?* before he points. She was shopping. She'd die before she said so.

Then Vee comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown all the way back, laughing like someone who forgot she can be seen. And Randi's stride just drops out from under her and she says "Yes" with nothing around it. Of course. *Of course.* Randi has been curated since she was thirteen, and the one woman on that entire quad who pulls her up short is the one who isn't performing. Not the mirrors, not the matched pair, not the blonde who runs the room — the one who's forgotten the wheel. That's not a type, that's a diagnosis.

And here's where I went cold. The shoulder check. The thirty feet of geometry so clean it looked like weather, and *the redhead's hand flies to her own mouth.* Vee apologized. Vee stood there stricken and sorry and Randi's hand settled warm on her arm and said no no, my fault. And Pace is leaning on a light pole finding it beautiful, "a thing to watch," and I know he's right that it's beautiful and I hated knowing it. That's the first thing in this book actually done *to* someone, and the book let me enjoy the craftsmanship of it before I caught up. The jacket warned me it would do this. It did it anyway and I still fell for it.

Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Said light, said into his chest, after she kissed him twice — once in daylight-voice and once in bed-voice. I know what that bench did to her. I watched it take her apart and I watched her lock a bathroom door and decide the woman who begged on it was never coming out again. And her very next move is to put someone else on it. She's not recruiting a third. She's finding someone to carry it for her.

The one thing nagging me: Pace has now been right about everything for three chapters. Every flinch read correctly, every costume seen through, every pause understood. He's never once wrong, and a man who's never wrong is the thing I used to swallow and don't anymore. I need this book to cost him something. And I need Vee from the inside — right now she's a copper head at the edge of a lawn, which is precisely what they've made her, and if the book keeps her that way I'm out. But the jacket says she's the one we follow, so. Turn the page.
