Oh, they're *hunting*. That's what the first two chapters were — not a prologue to a love story, a fitting. He built her a bench and got her to beg on it, she locked herself in the bathroom and swore never again, and now four weeks in he takes her out into a beautiful Tuesday and teaches her that she's allowed to want a woman. And the whole time I'm sitting there with the jacket in my head going: I know who the redhead is. I know her name before Randi does.

Let me do the part that upset me first, because it's the part I keep chewing. He asked her *while she was coming*. "He had decided this beforehand." He came up into her while she was still pulsing and undefended specifically so the question would land before her defenses got back from wherever they'd gone, and he took her body's answer as the true one — "He usually did." And I have such mixed feelings, because on one hand: that's a man reaching past someone's guard on purpose, and I have read four hundred books where that's the move and the book calls it insight. But then he *asked again*. Clothes off still, but heat gone, her breath all the way down, and the text says out loud that he wanted her gathered back into herself, free to weigh it, and that mattered to him more than the answer did. That's the thing I never find. That's the exact thing. He got the answer he wanted from her body and then went back and asked the person. Most books don't know there's a difference.

And then she lies to him — beautifully. "Only in a threesome. With you." Handing the appetite over so she can be the generous girlfriend instead of a woman who wants a woman. And he *sees* it, and he doesn't correct her, and I don't think that's cowardice, I think it's the same courtesy as the locked bathroom door. He lets her have the wrapper. God, that's a good man to be terrified of.

The walk. Okay, the walk is one of the best things I've read in this genre in a while. "I point. You pick." She laughed before she could decide whether to — yes, that's it, that's the sound of a rule arriving that you like more than you're prepared to admit. And I loved watching her taste reveal itself against her own grammar: no to the mirror of herself, no to the matched pair, a real considering for the round-faced girl on the steps with the beanie and the split earbuds, and then a *fast* hard no to the blond who runs rooms — because that one's performing, and Randi knows performance from the inside, and she doesn't want another face. She wants someone who forgot she could be seen. Which is the one thing Randi has never once done in three chapters.

And then Vee comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown back laughing and Randi's stride just drops out from under her. "Yes," with nothing around it. Every other woman she released; this one held her where she stood. I got a chill. Not a sexy chill — a *this is how it starts* chill.

Then the shoulder bump, and my stomach went cold. Thirty feet of geometry so it looks like chance, and *Vee's* hand flies to her own mouth, *Vee* apologizes for being walked into, and Randi's hand stays on her arm half a beat longer than the apology needs. That's the moment I understood exactly what book I'm in. She's so good at it. A decade of dance and three years of sorority folded into three seconds, and the girl thanks her for it. And Pace leaning on a light pole a hundred feet back, feeling "a deep warmth in his body" watching it — he's not even the one doing it anymore, he just wound her up and let her go, and she's the one who turned out to be an instrument.

And then she skips. Actual happy footwork on the brick she didn't decide to do. High squeaky "*Yes*" and then the low one, the bedroom register in daylight, which she *never* does. That's the tell that undoes chapter 2 for me: she swore in that mirror that the one he found was going back behind the face and not coming out again, and three days later the face is cracking open on a lawn over a redhead and she doesn't even notice she's doing it. She's not keeping her vow. She's just moved what's underneath it onto someone else.

"Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I am not okay. That's the coldest line in the book so far and she says it with her head on his chest, all cuddled in, and he answers it with *let's go feed you* — which is tender, which is genuinely the tenderest thing in the chapter, the man who slices the apple thin and notices she can't eat a cheeseburger — and those two things sit in the same breath and the book doesn't blink. That's the merciless-tenderness thing the jacket promised and I thought was marketing.

What I'm suspicious of: Pace's serene confidence that answering hunger is the same as loving someone. Also Daphne, from one paragraph in chapter one, sitting there like a loaded gun. And I want to know what it costs him when the thing he's building gets away from all three of them, because the jacket says it does.

Small honest complaint: the parade of strangers graded on body and "proper scale" got slightly airless in the middle — I understood it was Randi's ruler, not the book's, but I was ready to be done with the inventory a woman or two before the chapter was.

And I need Vee. Three chapters and the heroine I bought the book for got a name in the last two paragraphs. I'm greedy for her POV — I want the inside of that laugh, I want to know what she thought of the pretty girl who bumped into her, I want to be ahead of her and hurt about it. Give me that.
