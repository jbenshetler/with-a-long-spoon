REACTION

Okay so the first four pages of this chapter are the best sex I've read in a long time and I'm furious about why.

He comes up into her *while she's still going* — while she's still clenching around nothing from his mouth — and then kisses her with her own taste still on him, and she takes it off his lips and kisses him harder for it. I had to put the book down on my chest for a second. That's the thing. That's the exact thing I keep going back to the shelf for. And then he asks the question *into* that, into the undefended part, and her body answers before she does, and he takes the body's answer for the true one. The line "She had been performing, the first week. She was not performing now" did something to me. Because I watched her performing in chapter one. I watched her lock a bathroom door and practice a smile. So when the book tells me this woman's *yes* was real, I believe it, and that's rare.

And then he gets dressed and takes her out to go shopping for a person.

I have been sitting here for ten minutes trying to work out why I'm not angry at him and I think the answer is that I *am*, a little, and the book knows it, and that's the only reason I'm still holding it. Because every single move he makes toward Randi in this chapter is the move I want from a man. He asks the second time *after* the heat has left the room, specifically so she's gathered back into herself and free to weigh it. He lets her move the wanting into a shape she can survive — "only in a threesome, with you" — and he doesn't make her stand naked next to the truth, and he doesn't correct her, and he sees exactly what she did and finds it *beautiful*. "I point. You pick." The choosing is hers. He's not picking a woman, he's teaching her she's allowed to want one. On its own that's the most generous thing a man in one of these books has done in fifty chapters.

Except there's a third person and she's a hundred feet away with her head thrown back laughing and she doesn't know the laugh is being graded.

The grading was the part that nearly lost me. "Built to the proper scale, the scale Randi approved of and maintained" — a whole quad of young women sorted by shoulder width and shoe. I know it's Randi's eye and not the book's, I know the chapter is *about* how narrow that eye is, I know the red-haired girl walks out of the dining hall and blows the whole grammar up. I still felt it on my hands. And the pointing in daylight, the two of them discussing a stranger as a dish — the book lets Randi feel the social wrongness of it for exactly one beat and then lets the wanting eat it, and the honesty of that is the only thing that kept me from closing it.

But God, when Vee came out of those doors. I know she's Vee before Randi does — the jacket told me, I'm supposed to be ahead — and still, that paragraph. The hair going copper every time the wind lifts it, the laughing with her whole body, *laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen.* Three chapters of women managing how much of themselves the day gets — Randi deciding at her vanity that the afternoon gets the ponytail and not a strand more — and then here comes a girl who forgot. Of course Randi's stride dropped out from under her. Of course the one word came out alone. That's not a type, that's a *hunger*, and Randi doesn't know it's hunger yet, and I think I'd follow this book quite a long way just to watch her find out.

And the shoulder-check. Reader, the shoulder-check. Thirty feet of geometry arranged so it looks like chance, half a head of weight, and it's *Vee's* hand that flies to her own mouth, *Vee* apologizing — and the hand on her arm staying a half-beat past what the apology needed. That's the scariest three seconds in the book so far and nobody in it raised their voice.

Then she comes back across the lawn doing happy footwork she didn't decide to do, squeaking *yes* into his hair, and I liked her. I liked her so much right there. Which is appalling.

"Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I actually said *oh my God* out loud. At my own kitchen table. That's the line I'd text at 1 a.m. — that and his answer, which isn't an answer, it's a kiss on the top of her head and *let's go feed you*, and he's been feeding her all chapter, the apple slices and the cheeseburger she can't eat and now this. He's so tender. He is so, so tender, and the tenderness is the mechanism, and the book put that on the cover and I bought it anyway.

What I'm suspicious of: I'm three chapters in and I have not been inside my heroine once. The jacket says this is Vee's book and so far Vee is a hundred feet of brick away and rendered entirely through two people deciding about her. Chapter one and two gave me Randi's insides and they were extraordinary — the mirror, the unbearable hum, lying under a thin duvet in last night's shoes not-crying. Chapter three took that away and gave me Pace reading her from outside instead, decoding her like a problem he's good at, and he *is* good at it, but I missed her. If the next chapter is also Pace being right about everyone I'll start to feel managed myself.

What I want, badly, is to be inside Vee's head before anybody touches her. Give me her walking up that path thinking about nothing, give me the stats class, let me have her *first* — because the only way this book earns the thing it's promised is if the girl who never knew there was an invitation turns out to be a whole person with an appetite of her own, and not the prize at the end of a pointing game.
