REACTION

Oh, they're *hunting*. I got about four paragraphs into the walk before my stomach went cold and hot at the same time and I had to put the book down on my chest for a second.

Because here's the thing — I came into this book knowing from the jacket that Vee gets chosen, that it starts as a game between two people. Knowing it and watching it happen are not the same. That afternoon is so *pretty*. The maple that's gone over all at once, the wind turning the leaves pale, the brick walk, her hand in his. He even thinks it — he didn't plan the maple but he's glad of the maple. And underneath the gorgeous light two people are shopping for a person the way you'd shop for a chair. "Too expected." "You'd get both or neither." The girls get graded like produce and the book doesn't flinch about how ugly that is and *also* doesn't stop being sexy about it, and I hate how well that works on me.

And then Vee comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown all the way back laughing — "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That line put a fist in my chest. Because that's exactly the thing they're going to take. Randi has spent two chapters teaching me what it costs to be seen and what she'd rather die than do again, and the first woman she wants on sight is a woman who doesn't know she's visible. Of course. *Of course.* She didn't pick the mirrors, she didn't pick the polished ones, she picked the one who isn't guarding anything. That's not a type, that's a wound picking.

The shoulder-check. God. Three seconds of choreography so clean the redhead apologizes to *her*. A decade of dance folded into a fake accident. I texted nobody but I wanted to: this woman is a professional and the boy watching from the light pole thinks he's watching her bloom.

Which is my actual obsession right now, and my actual worry. Pace is still doing the thing I read for — he asks, he waits, he checks, he lets her keep the wrapper she needs ("*only* in a threesome, *with you*") instead of forcing her to stand naked next to what she wants. He *sees* that she's moved the ownership of the appetite and he lets her, because making her name it would hurt her. That's tenderness. That's genuinely the thing I've been hunting for years.

But: *"He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did."* I sat up at that. That's the sentence every bad book in this genre leans on — her mouth said no but her body said yes — and Helen Rivers just laid it out flat as this man's operating principle and didn't defend it. And I don't think the book is winking. I think the book is *loading a gun*. Because I know something he doesn't: three nights ago this woman locked a bathroom door and decided never to come out again, and he thinks the evening was "remarkably good," and he thinks he's found her hunger and is answering it. He's reading a body that has been trained since age thirteen to say whatever keeps the face intact. He's the most attentive man in the genre and he is being *played*, gently, by someone who loves him and has just finished deciding he can never have her. The flinch. He clocked the flinch and misread what it meant. That gap is the whole novel and I can feel it under the floorboards.

And then — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Sweetheart. She's a *person*, you haven't said forty words to her, and you've already got her facedown. He doesn't answer it. He says "let's go feed you," and it's tender, and she said "I can't eat that" back on the quad, and he's been feeding her thin apple slices since chapter one, and I don't know whether to be moved or to shout.

What I actually feel: complicit, and a little sick, and I want the next chapter immediately. I want Vee. I've now seen her for one paragraph — freckles, copper, the laugh — and I am already braced on her behalf, which I suspect is precisely the trap the jacket promised me ("lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does"). Fine. I'm in it. But the book is on notice: if Vee turns out to be furniture — if she's the thing this gets done *to* and not a woman with her own appetite in her own head — I'm gone. Everything rides on whether she gets an inside.
