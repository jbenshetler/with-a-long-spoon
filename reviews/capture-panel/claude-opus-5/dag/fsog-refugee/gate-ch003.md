REACTION

Okay. I need to say the thing first: the opening in bed is the best argument this book has made for itself, and it made it on exactly my terms. He comes up into her *while* she's still coming, before he kisses her, and he's decided that in advance — and then he asks her a question, and her body answers it so fast and so hard that she comes again off nothing but the question, and he *does not take that as her answer.* He waits. He waits for the heat to go out of the room. He lets the afternoon get ordinary. He asks her again when she's gathered back into herself, so she can actually choose. "That mattered more to him than the answer did." I underlined that in my head. That's the man I've been looking for in this genre for about six years. Nobody writes that. Everybody writes a man who takes the gasp as the yes.

And then he hands me the other half of the bargain in the same chapter, because what he's being so careful about is picking out a girl on a sidewalk.

I know. I *bought it* knowing. The jacket told me there was a plan and that I'd be ahead of her. But knowing and feeling it are different and this chapter is where it bit. The walk is so pleasant. The maple, the undersides of the leaves going pale, the frisbee, the bass arriving half a second late — it's a *lovely* afternoon, and under it two people are window-shopping a human being, and the book just lets the afternoon be lovely. "I point. You pick." God. That line is hot and I resent that it's hot. He's not choosing a woman, he's teaching her she's allowed to choose one, and he's right that that's the gift, and the gift is *a person.*

Randi is the most interesting thing happening to me right now. Two chapters ago she locked a bathroom door and decided, flat, deadbolt-quiet, that the one he found was never coming out again. And here she is, forty-eight hours later, leaking want all over a quad, and she can't help it, and the whole chapter is her trying to re-dress it fast enough. "Only in a threesome. With you." That maneuver — moving the appetite into his hands so it's a gift from a girlfriend instead of a hunger of her own — I understood it instantly and it made my chest hurt a little. She can't own wanting anything. She couldn't own an apple. She says "I can't eat that" about a cheeseburger without so much as glancing back. And he sees all of it, every move, and lets her keep the costume, which is either the kindest thing in the book or the most frightening.

Then the bare "Yes," with nothing around it. The stride dropping out from under her. That's the second time this book has gotten under her face and both times I felt it in my own stomach.

The shoulder-bump is the moment I went cold. It's so *good.* A decade of dance folded into three seconds, and the engineering is that the redhead ends up apologizing to *her.* That's not flirting, that's tradecraft. And the thing I'd text a friend at one in the morning is the last line — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — because Vee has had a name for ninety seconds and she's already been assigned a piece of furniture. And he doesn't answer. He says *let's go feed you*, which is tender, which is him feeding the starving girl again, and I'm supposed to be charmed and I *am*, and that's the trick of this whole book, isn't it.

My one actual complaint, and it's the thing that will decide whether I'm here in four chapters: Vee is the heroine on that jacket and I have now read three chapters without her. She's been a body on a walkway, inventoried — scale, hair, freckles, the laugh with her chin thrown back, clothes that fail at an impossible task. Lovely writing. Still an inventory, taken by two people shopping. I need her from inside, and I need it next, because "a woman who exists to be acted upon" is the exact door I walk out of books through, and right now the only thing keeping it shut is the jacket's promise that the book knows this and is doing it on purpose. Give me her head. Then I'll go anywhere with these people.

Also tracking, quietly: the water-bottle cap under the thumbnail. The egg whites. Nobody's named that yet and I don't think it's decoration.
