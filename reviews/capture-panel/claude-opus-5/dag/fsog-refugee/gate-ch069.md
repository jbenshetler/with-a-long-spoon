Okay. So here's what that chapter actually did to me: it made stillness the hottest thing in the book. *Still meant the brush. Moving meant the brush going away.* That's it. That's the whole mechanism, and it's a feedback loop built entirely out of her own nervous system, and he never once raises his voice, never ties her to anything, never punishes her — he just stops, and waits, and the stopping is unbearable because she wants it so much. And then she tries to be good at it, tries to *hold the pose* by sheer will, and that fails, because gripping is still moving. That's the thing I came back for. That's the whole thing.

And then he wrecked me twice in ways that have nothing to do with the paint.

One: the scar. He's spent the whole book not making her explain herself. She just paid him back in his own currency, and I don't think she knew she was doing it.

Two: the photograph. He took it, he picked the frame, he said *that one*, and then he **gave her the phone and never asked for a copy.** Didn't reach for his own. Didn't say send me that. On the entire shelf I walked away from, a picture like that is a leash — it exists so a man can hold it. This one exists because she said *take a picture* and he handed it back. And the book knows exactly what it's doing, because she lies there in the dark afterward counting it up: nobody on earth has seen it but her. That's the rarest thing in this novel and it's rendered as an afterthought.

But. The hole in the ritual. He is still, months in, managing a squeamishness she has never once mentioned to him, steering a whole evening's architecture around the one flavor she balked at, so smoothly that she gets to not-ask inside his design. She's chasing a ghost he's erasing on purpose. That's the tenderest sabotage I've ever watched. He will take her apart with a paintbrush and he will not let her find out what she is.
