REACTION

Two pages and I'm sitting here with my hand over my mouth.

Because here it is — the thing I've been begging this book for since the roadhouse — and it doesn't come from Randi and it doesn't come from Pace. It comes from a girl in her coat on a lower bunk who's been outside in the cold. **"Is this what you want?"** Not *what happened.* Not *what is she to you.* Not *you need to choose,* which is what Brooke gave her and which is a management question. Cassie asked her the one question nobody in two volumes has thought to ask, which is the question about *Vee*, and she asked it so plainly that Vee — and this is the part that got me — **actually went and looked.** She reached for it. She came up against the edge of something close and large and stepped back off it before she could make out its size. That's the same body of water she wouldn't swim in at the salon, the same breath's width she couldn't close with her own fingers in her own locked room. The book keeps walking her up to it and she keeps not looking, and I'm not angry at her for it, I'm scared for her, which is different and worse.

And then she did the ugliest thing she's done all volume and I loved the book for making her do it. *You don't like her. You've never liked her.* Laying the old argument down on the floor **like a plank to walk out on** — and Cassie stepped over it without once looking down. God. Years of that fight, and Cassie just *declines it*, because she's not having an argument, she's worried about her friend. That's the first time in seventy-three chapters somebody has refused Vee a cover story. Randi hands her one every single time. Pace hands her a puddle to step around like a gentleman. Cassie let the silence stay open to see what Vee would put in it, and Vee put nothing in it, and Cassie didn't fill it either. The only woman in this county who doesn't manage people.

And then: **"You didn't have dinner."** / **"Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles?"**

I'm not going to be normal about that. Because look what it is — it's the same move Pace makes. It's the folded paper towel under the glass, it's the lock instead of a sentence, it's the loaf of bread while she slept it off. A thing instead of a word, given to somebody who can't hold a word right now. Except Cassie asked the hard thing *first*, and only when Vee had nothing did she scale the question down to something Vee could actually carry. **"A want the size of a bowl."** And it came out of Vee fast and whole — *yeah, yeah, thanks* — the first whole sentence she's said since she walked in the door. The relief of being handed a question you can answer. Cassie didn't let her off. She just made it smaller until Vee could hold it.

And she is being *lied to.* That's what's sitting cold under all of it. The shut window, the uncorrected story about the shrunken sweater, *you can stay* given to a man on half the facts, and now the plainest question there is, and Vee stood in the doorway in her coat and **could not answer her oldest friend.** Vee's noting of it is the best line in the chapter and the loneliest: *her oldest friend had asked her the plainest question there was, and she could not answer it.* She's not even lying anymore. She's just empty. The pipe is capped at both ends and now there's a third end.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: THE ROOMMATE. It's the roommate. Cocoa Pebbles out of the box beside the bunk at nine at night is the single most loving thing anybody has done for this girl in three hundred pages and nobody got naked for it. And also — she thought Cassie might have *seen it.* The hug, the mouth on the neck, the sound she didn't catch in time. **Like Cassie could read the whole night off her skin like a postmark.** We still don't know what Cassie saw, and I don't think Cassie's going to tell us, and I think that's going to matter enormously by Saturday.

Saturday brunch, which Vee asked for out of a ringing neck and not out of any part of her that was thinking. Circled. Coming.
