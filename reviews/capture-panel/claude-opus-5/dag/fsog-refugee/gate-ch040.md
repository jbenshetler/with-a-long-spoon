REACTION

Oh, I needed this and I didn't know it.

Forty chapters and this is the first room nobody put her in. Not his house, not Randi's foyer, not a boutique floor with another woman on one knee holding her foot up to the light. A Tuesday pool at four with the bleachers folded against the wall, and the third lane in from the wall — *she'd taken it her first week back and kept taking it*, and the butterfly man and the woman with the good flip turn arrange themselves around her without a word. She has standing somewhere. She didn't get it from either of them. She's had it since before they existed.

And she's been swimming since she was twelve. Nobody told me that until now and it reorganized her for me. All this book she's been a girl who got handed things — a bolt of cloth matched to her face, a mirror screwed to a wall, a scarf tied at her throat by somebody else's hands, a pair of heels she can't pay for — and underneath the whole spring there's been seventy-five minutes on a clock and a black line on the floor that she's been doing alone since she was a kid. *You were just already in it, any more than she had ever once decided the second verse of a hymn.* God. That's the Ohio in her used for once as a strength instead of a wound.

*Her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here. It only worked.* I sat with that one. This is the woman who said *floozy* to herself in a mirror on reflex, who's spent her life apologizing for exactly the two places he lined that dress at, who flinches at her own wetness on a washcloth — and there is one place she goes where the body is not a subject at all, it's just an engine, and the book put that in without making a single speech about it. *The green-and-white suit had nowhere left to go on her and hadn't for years. She looked at the water instead of at that.* One sentence, no lesson, and I know everything about how she feels standing on that tile.

But the thing I'm actually going to be carrying is four strokes long. *She could not tell, for about four strokes, whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good.* And then the wall came, and she rolled, and it was gone. That's the first real doubt she's had and it got exactly four strokes and no more, and the chapter is honest enough to let the wall take it away from her. I don't think she's wrong about him, by the way — he's the most carefully *good* man I've read in this genre, I'd still take his number — but she's never once asked herself that question with her eyes open, and the water let her ask it and then let her off the hook. Of course it did. That's what she goes there for. *It never answered anything, the swimming. That was the whole use of it.*

Which is the quiet, awful thing in this chapter. She brought two questions into the lane. *Do you love him* came in off the third wall in Cassie's voice with a *dread riding it* — and she has the answer, she said it to his sleeping ear, *Peter*, barely a breath, and it came out of her mouth as *maybe* to the one person who'd have sat up for it. And by the sixth piece she doesn't have the words anymore, only him and the ache and the count, and then only the count. She came here to get the words taken off things. She went up the hill with her head as quiet as it's been all week and *did not go looking for it.* That is a girl using a lap pool the exact way she's been using Randi's dinner table and Cassie's half-told stories — to be relieved of having to know. Happier than she's ever been and still editing, still at it, and now even the one thing that's purely hers is in service of not finding out.

Small snag that itched: *did he mind* was *nothing at all* by the fourth piece and she's been turning it over since Saturday — but he already answered it. He emptied the top drawer. That's the single best thing he's done since the mirror and it should have come in off a wall and warmed her all the way down the lane. Either I've got the days crossed or she has, and I half suspect the point is that his answers don't stick to her the way the questions do.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *forty chapters in and she finally got a chapter where nobody is doing anything to her and she spent it successfully not thinking.* Also — the pastry glaze on her lip is still on her lip as far as I'm concerned. Nobody's mentioned it since. Randi said *sticky fingers* out loud in a sunlit room and then handed her a pastry, and this chapter is a pool, and I am not fooled: that bill is coming and it isn't coming as money.
