REACTION

Oh, that zipper. Half down and stopped — that's the whole chapter and it's the best thing this book has done with Cassie yet. She's stood in that room a hundred times pulling her clothes off mid-sentence and it's never meant anything, and now the skirt comes off and there's nothing under it, and nothing under it is *a fact*, and facts can't be ladled around. She can edit the telling. She cannot edit the floor. So the towel goes back on the hook, more or less, and she stands there lying with her body in a room where she's never had to.

And Cassie — *God*, Cassie. "Was he worth painting your toenails? In October. I have eyes." She sees everything and she's still pointed at the wrong thing, and then she does the worst kindest thing in the book: she feels the seam and doesn't pull it. *"He cooked. A man made you dinner." "Mm." And she let it stand.* That's a friend deciding not to make you pay for the shape of your own story, and it's also the moment the door quietly latches. Vee's going to remember this as Cassie being sweet about it. What actually happened is she got away with it and the getting-away got easier. "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend" — said *kindly, certainly, wrong.* I felt that sentence land in three different places.

The bit I keep turning over is the ladle. The book names it out loud — the surface closes without a hole, and she tells herself it's a kindness, and it *is* a kindness, and she believes it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep. That's the most honest sentence about Vee anyone's written so far, including Vee. She is building the second version of her life with her own hands and she's doing it out of love, not shame, which is so much harder to argue her out of.

No brush in the house that would go through her hair. Face bare. Drove home with nothing, because packing a bag would have been saying it. She laughed in his mirror. The smile came back at every red light. I'm not made of stone — I liked her this happy. "She had never in her life been so happy" is a beautiful line and it is also the loudest alarm bell the author has rung yet, sitting right at the end of a chapter about what she's learned to leave out. She's happy *and* she's already alone with it, in the one room where she used to not be.

What I want, though: he wasn't in this chapter, and the last two people to be alone with Vee in a room were Cassie and a bowl of cereal. That's four chapters now that end in somebody being told about it. This one earned it by being about the telling *failing* — but I've noticed, and I said I'd notice. Also: she drove home bare and the green is in his house, folded in thirds, in a house with two towels on the rod. I have not stopped thinking about where that object ends up. Randi still doesn't know about the dress.
