REACTION

Oh, this chapter is *mean*. In the good way — the way that made me put my phone down.

Because I've been sitting with Randi for three chapters. I watched her beg on that bench and then lock a bathroom door and decide, with that awful quiet little click, that she was never coming out again. I watched her lie on a bed in last night's clothes with her shoes on, not crying. And then yesterday I watched her stop dead on a brick walk at the sight of a redheaded girl laughing, and say *yes* with nothing around it. So Randi, to me, has been the one with the wound.

And now I'm inside Vee's head for the first time and it's — she's so happy. That's the thing. She's *so happy*. The right pen. The high table. The joke on the sign she'd walked under forty times and never been let in on. *Love you, girl.* I know exactly what the warmth coming off Randi is costing to produce and what it's aimed at, and Vee is standing there holding an empty cup, spinning, going home the long way. I wanted to put a hand on her arm. I also — and this is why I'm not stopping — I *felt* the hour. The spoons ringing. The leaning-in being the whole content and the transcript blank. I have had that hour. I have been twenty-one and been picked by somebody shinier and come home not able to repeat a single sentence. That's a real thing to render, and it got rendered from the inside, which is all I ever ask of a heroine.

And then Cassie. *Goodbye, Vee.* Whole word, flat, finished. Oh, that landed cold. The book put a hand up and said *you understand what this is,* and I do, and Vee smoothed it over and laughed *it's coffee, weirdo,* and went. The one person in the room not being charmed is the one who saw it. I'm going to be thinking about Cassie's face doing the brief thing Vee didn't try to name.

What I notice, and what's keeping me honest about this book: Vee isn't a blank. She reads the room in detail, she clocks the floozies and then clocks herself looking a beat longer than the word accounted for, she knows she has more than any of them under that cardigan and she keeps the cardigan on and lets the room not know it was a choice. That's a girl with an interior and a mother's voice installed in her throat, and the shame-into-heat thing the jacket promised is already wired up and waiting. The want she aims at Randi is hers — "flat helpless want" — and the book doesn't make her name it. Good. Both women are doing the same trick, from opposite ends: wanting a woman and moving the wanting somewhere survivable. That's not an accident and I'm here for it.

What I'm uneasy about isn't the ethics — I knew from the blurb I'd be ahead of her, that's the deal I signed. It's that Pace wasn't in this chapter, and I can feel the gap opening. The last time he and Randi were alone in a room, something cracked in her that she decided to hide from him forever, and he's walking around pleased with himself and *doesn't know*. That's my actual tension right now, and it's a love-story tension, not a conspiracy one. I want him back. I want to see whether the man who sat outside a locked door and didn't knock is going to notice that the woman who came back out was the face. And I want to see him with Vee, because I haven't met them together yet and the whole book is supposedly built on that gaze.

Zero heat in this chapter and I don't mind. The hunger is all social and it's the same hunger — Vee's *yes* arriving in her chest fully formed and her making it wait one beat so it wouldn't come out the size it actually was. That's the identical mechanism as Randi's *please*. The book knows.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the roommate said GOODBYE. the whole word. I'm not okay." And: "I think the girl being hunted and the girl hunting her are the same girl and nobody in this book has figured that out yet."
