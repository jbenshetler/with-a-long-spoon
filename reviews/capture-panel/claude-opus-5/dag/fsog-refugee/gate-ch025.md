And it's not decoration — *same numbers, nobody touched one of them.* That's the whole book said out loud in a cereal-box voice by a man with a laser pointer, and the two women who engineered this are sitting on either side of her not looking at the board because they already know the trick. And then the thing I'd actually text at 1 a.m.: **she put it on the credit card.** She stood there with the debit between her fingers — the card that only ever spends what's already behind it, the card of a girl who paid her own check in a velvet booth and tipped well — and she put it back. That's the first thing in twenty-five chapters that got *taken* from her. Not her body; she's handed that over gladly and I've been cheering. Her solvency. The one unassailable piece of her, the thing she had instead of money, which was *never owing anybody.* And look how it was done: Randi didn't push. Randi said the truest, loveliest sentence in the chapter — *a dress a man sat up nights making doesn't want to go with everything* — and it's **right**, it's so right I'd have followed her out of that store too, and it cost Vee a number she doesn't have. And then Vee did the rest herself, in her own head, in Randi's grammar: *the shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed.* Honey. You just outsourced your wanting to an object so you wouldn't have to be the one who wanted it. That's the only time in this whole book she's talked herself out of owning her own desire, and it's the one time it bankrupted her. And beside her Randi bought loafers for several times that without a glance, mid-sentence, about a coat.

She'll be carrying that in November. Nothing under it. The book made me feel the two cards with my own fingers.

Also — the *to*. "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." I made a noise. That's the word for the fitting, for the riser, for the chin tipped back, for the whole thing, and Randi handed it to her clean the way Randi always does, and Vee's throat went because someone finally named the thing she cried about and couldn't say. That's Randi's actual gift and it's not fake. That's what makes her so dangerous — the naming is a real service.
