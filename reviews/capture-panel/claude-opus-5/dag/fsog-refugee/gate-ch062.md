Oh, this is the chapter. This is the one I'm going to be thinking about in the car tomorrow.

He isn't even in it. Not one line of dialogue, not a phone call, not a truck in the drive — and he's the whole material of it, and the book just proved its own thesis without him in the room: *He had looked at the whole slick fact of her as if it were true, as if it were fine. As if the plenty of her required no pardon.* That's what she took home. Not the orgasm, not the compliment, not *boyfriend* said six times to six measuring faces — she took home the look. A girl in a borrowed flannel in a dorm room metabolizing the fact that somebody drank what she's been apologizing for since she was fifteen. And she *wants the look again.* She's not chasing the hand. She's chasing being looked at like that.

And then the reach. God. A breath's width. *She could not close the distance.* I have read probably two hundred books on this shelf and I don't think I've seen a writer do that — give a woman the whole private room, the locked-enough door, the wanting fully present, no one watching, nothing forbidding it, nothing in the room telling her why — and then let her *not be able to.* Every door in this volume she's walked through on her own nerve. The coat, the shoes, the county road, the truck, the finger wagged at a man with wet hands. This is the first one she got her hand on and couldn't push. And it isn't fear of him or of ruin or of being caught. It's a voice installed so early it has no author left. *Nothing in the room told her why.* That sentence is the quietest horror in two volumes and it's about a woman's own mouth.

And the smell. She braced for the stink the boys' jokes promised and it wasn't there — *warm, damp, alive, a hothouse with its doors shut.* Hers. Unmistakably hers. Whoever wrote that knew exactly which decade of a girl's life to go get. That's not titillation, that's reparative. And the shining strands between thumb and finger, the *excessive, the shameful plenty of her made visible again* — and then, at the end, the correction administered by her own body: *Greedy. Her mess. Hers to lie in.* She lay down on the wet spot on purpose. On *purpose*, with a blanket to her chin. I want to put a flag on that line. Four months ago she couldn't say *slutty* out loud and tonight she chose her own mess as a place to sleep.

But here's what's got the hair up on my arms. Twice: *A mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers.* And the second time it's the last thing in her head before she goes looking for the damp patch. No *obviously.* No *that was about Pace, all of it, about Pace.* The machine she installed in a restaurant bathroom four chapters ago didn't even switch on. She lay there in her boyfriend's shirt, came to his face, and finished with her mind on another woman's fingers at another woman's mouth, and nothing in her objected, and nothing in her explained it away. The wall is coming down and she isn't the one taking it apart.

And look what did the work. Six words at a dinner table. *I don't.* *It was powerful. It turned me on.* Level, mild, cost her nothing, took up almost no room — Randi said the opposite of a settled thing and declined to argue it, and twenty-four hours later it's inside the girl's body doing surgery. No lecture. No bow. Just an opposite, set down and left. That woman can move a person with less material than anybody I've ever read, and I cannot for the life of me tell anymore how much of it she means and how much of it is the only move she knows. The *pleasant as a hostess* question — *how does he act?* — and she got *enthusiastic* out of Vee's own mouth and then ate the bread. Then this chapter: *Enthusiastic.* *Careful.* *Randi hadn't minded. She had done it herself.* Her own two words and Randi's three, running in parallel while she works herself. Randi didn't hand her a verdict, she handed her a doubt, which is the one thing that keeps working when you're not in the room.

Also — the window. She shut Cassie's window. The two inches that have been open all winter, Cassie's one standing arrangement, closed so the heat would hold while she did this in Cassie's absence, under Cassie's lamp burning over nobody and Cassie's anatomy book tented facedown where she stood up mid-sentence. And then she shoved it open afterward and stood in the January air in nothing until the room smelled like hot dust and winter, so there'd be nothing to come home to. It's tiny. It's also the third thing in six chapters she's cleaned up before Cassie got back. The cranberry sweater, the weekend they didn't talk, and now the air. I don't think the book put that window there twice by accident.
