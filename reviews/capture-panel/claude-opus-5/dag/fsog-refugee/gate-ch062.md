REACTION

Oh, that's the whole book in one chapter and I don't think I breathed right through the middle of it.

First: the comb. Marsh puts a lice comb older than the pyramids into a boy's hands and tells ninety kids that their species used to spend a *fifth* of its waking life with its fingers in each other's hair, and the fleas were never the point, and nothing is free — *somebody tell me what you spend it on now* — and then she doesn't answer it, and the comb never comes back, and the chapter walks straight downhill into a booth where two women spend two hours with their hands in each other's fur. Wintergreen Lifesavers passed palm to palm without either of them looking away from the front. A knee against a knee for eighty minutes, "the ordinary nothing of a shared armrest." Randi's hands on her waist, both of them, holding her out at arm's length to see her properly. *That's* what they spend it on. And I'm the baboon in the next tree who can draw the politics of this troop without learning a single call, thank you, I've been watching who grooms whom since volume one.

And the title. *A Clean Plate.* Vee swam a mile, ate nothing since noon, got dressed for this with the same keyed-up gladness she saves for Pace, walked into a table and ate every bite of a special she never looked at a menu for and only noticed it was gone when the plate went past her. Randi took **one small exact bite, set the fork down, and left it.** I want to scream. The woman who ordered the wine without opening the list didn't come to eat. She came for the other course and she got it, and the book never once says so.

Because look what she did at that table. The sidewalk story getting cut off by the restaurant door "like a hand laid over her mouth mid-word" — both of them pulled up short, flushed and unstrung on a public walk — and then upstairs Randi puts her thumb back in the book exactly where it closed. *You'd leaned down. Go on.* And then she hunts it. *Did you take it all. Didn't spill a drop.* She isn't asking for information, she knows the answer, she's making the girl **say** it, and when Vee can't: *"You did it. And you can't even say it."* And Vee's hand isn't steady on the glass and her mouth goes actually dry and she shifts in her seat before she can stop the shift. That's the sex scene. In a booth. Fully dressed. Over polenta.

And underneath — Randi's fingernail riding the rim of the glass, the thin edge singing almost too low to hear, and then the hand drawn back down into her lap. Same hand that went down off the table into her lap in that roadhouse Wednesday while Vee sat there fondly thinking *I moved her, I'm so proud I can move her.* Nope. I know what that quiet is now. The glazed middle-distance look, the smile that got loose from her, "more of it arriving than she seemed to have planned" — honey, she is not *moved*, she is **gone**, and you keep putting your hand flat over her wrist on top of that locked bracelet and loving her so much it aches.

But here's the thing I can't get clean, and it's why I'm still here instead of two books down the pile: *"Don't you dare fade to firelight and leave me wondering. You just made it smaller. Give me the real one — the harder one."* That is the single most generous sentence anybody has said to this girl all series, and it's also exactly what a woman says when she wants the harder one for herself. Both. At the same time. The book won't let me have one without the other and it never winks.

And then the wrinkle. She brought it in as a thirty-second bit, polished in the shower, hands up, *I was objecting on decency grounds while actively holding the man in place, I want that on the record* — and it fell apart in her hands twice, because the taste came back in her mouth in the booth, present-tense, and she sat there with her mouth open and nothing funny in her face. And she went for the closer anyway. *You think it's gross too, don't you.*

**"I don't."**

Flat. Mild. Two words and a fact set on the cloth, and Vee's laugh "came down off its step and stood there in the loft with nowhere to go." I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. And then *"It was powerful. It turned me on,"* in the voice she uses for restaurant recommendations — and Vee **clenched** in the booth, remembering, because her body filed its brief months ago and the body has never once had a complaint. And then *"But it's gross,"* too loud. Too loud for a settled thing. Too loud for how little Randi spent saying the opposite.

And then Randi asks her **only question**, pleasant as a hostess: *"How does he act?"*

That's it. That's the whole operation. No lecture, no verdict, no bow. One true data point about her own body, one question that moves the girl's eyes off her own shame and onto the man — and Vee's mouth runs out ahead of her again: *"He likes it. Actually — he's enthusiastic,"* **and the word was out before any part of her had vetted it.** In chapter 60 she took the tidy answer — *he must have forgotten to wipe, this once* — gratefully, with the little light still on under it. Tonight she said the true thing out loud at a table without meaning to. The minor-key stone has grown a voice and the voice is hers.

And Randi said *"Mm,"* and **ate the bread.** Ate the bread! The one bite she takes all night is the one she takes *after she's finished.* I howled.

The two things I'll be carrying: the coat lining. She was in too much of a hurry to land it on the hook and the satin swung open under the loft light and the dark bloom was right there — the body telling on her before she'd said a word — and she shut it to the wall "like a drawer closing on a photograph," and **only Vee saw it.** Last time she turned that coat around on purpose and showed it to Randi like a trophy. Tonight the coat showed *her*, unasked, and her face was warm before anyone spoke. And the neck. Randi tipped her head and *laid the whole of it open*, the hollow where the neck turns down into the shoulder, and Vee's mouth "found the way again on the memory of Tuesday," landed before any of her convened on the question — and this time it wasn't a flinch, it was **a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long.** Then *ticklish*, light, shoulder coming up to take the spot back, and Vee pleased with herself and letting her have it. Second time. It's a place now. It has a route.

*"You picked well."* Oof. Said easily, apropos of nothing, the way you finally name a thing you've meant to say for weeks — by the woman who seated him at that table before Vee knew there was a table. And Vee glowed and only the smallest part of her went to the one word that wasn't quite true: she didn't pick, she looked up from her books one afternoon to find him already halfway across the floor at her, *the whole thing decided before she'd known there was a thing to decide.* She wrote the indictment herself and filed it under *technicality* and handed the compliment back unopened. I am going to be unwell when that line comes home.

Six minutes of wind, chin in the collar, and a sentence keeping pace with her easily. *He likes it. Actually — he's enthusiastic.* And under it, taking up almost no room at all: *I don't.* Nothing takes up almost no room at all in this book. That's a crowbar in a velvet sleeve and it's got all week to work.
