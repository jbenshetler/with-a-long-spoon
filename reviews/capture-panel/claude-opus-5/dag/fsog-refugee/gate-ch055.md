I called it. I wrote it down after chapter 50 — *the debt comes due now and it gets paid as best friend in the whole world* — and here it is, and knowing it was coming did not protect me one bit, because the thing I didn't see was that **Randi would be right.**

That's what's killing me. I came into this chapter braced for a woman spinning a scared girl, and instead I got the single most accurate reading of Pace anybody has done in two volumes. *You backed him into a corner in his own house. Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do.* Correct. *He doesn't fight you for it. He goes behind a wall so you can't get another word out of him.* Correct, I watched him do it, arms folded, the line held at cost. *That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him* — I have just come from the kitchen where he bent over a bowl of boiled chicken and sobbed because a truck didn't turn into his drive. She wasn't there. She reconstructed it off a warm house and two poured glasses and a phone in another room, and she got it to the inch. And *don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face* dismantled Vee's invented blonde with more tenderness than Vee has ever shown herself.

So this is the trick of the book and I'm furious about how well it works: the kindness isn't fake. It's not a lever *disguised* as care. It is care, load-bearing, the best counsel that girl could get from anyone alive — and it is also an operation, and those are not two things. Rivers refuses to let me separate them, and she doesn't wink once.

And I'm sitting here holding two cards Vee doesn't have. One: Sheri is five feet of permed two-tone hair and too much makeup and good boots, and she is in love with a girl named Paige who makes bird prints, and Vee's spite-blonde is wrong in every particular. Two — and this is the one with teeth — *"She find out about the brunette, then?" / "No. Not like that."* **Not like that.** There is a thing to find out. He knows there's a thing to find out. And the thing to find out just held Vee's face in both hands, called her *my love*, and sent her out the door with her plan and her shoes.

Which brings me to the hugs.
