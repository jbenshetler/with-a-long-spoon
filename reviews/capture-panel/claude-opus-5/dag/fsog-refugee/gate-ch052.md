REACTION

Oh, that's *cruel*, and it's the best kind of cruel, because Helen Rivers made me read these two chapters in this order on purpose.

I sat through Gstaad. I watched Randi get the whole approved life handed to her on a silver tray — the tailcoat, the sleigh, the truffle, a man who never once asked and whom she *liked* not asking — and feel absolutely nothing, and lie there warm along one seam and cold everywhere else and think *something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of*. And then I turn the page and here's Vee, under a shelf of little gold synchro swimmers, doing the time-zone arithmetic and building Randi a devastatingly handsome billionaire and *a weekend so wild Vee wouldn't recognize half of what they did*, and envying her. Envying a woman who, at that exact minute, was at a mirror putting a mouth back on because she'd left all her lipstick on a man who meant nothing. Both of them cold in a dark room on the same night, each one certain the other is warm. That's the whole machine of this book in one diptych and I want to lie down.

And the message. I *watched* her write that message. I watched Randi read Vee's text and see the warm kitchen and the parents who love her out loud, and decide to give her the fairytale because that's what Vee needed to see — *every word of it true, none of it the thing* — and tack on the one line she meant. And now I've watched Vee compose her half with the same care, reading it twice *for anything that gave her away*, making sure it demanded nothing, and then putting the phone facedown and turning it back over to check. Two women drafting each other, both editing out the one true sentence, both landing on an emoji. And the only honest word that crossed the Atlantic that night was *gorgeous*. God.

The thing that actually hurt, though. *He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now.* Fifty-two chapters. That is the bill coming in on everything I have loved about that man. The not-pushing, the *take the time you need, I'll be here*, the unhurried patience I have been swooning over since the sun porch — turns out it has one failure mode and she's in it, alone, at Christmas, for a week and a half, with the field empty. He doesn't come after people. He doesn't go back. And she walked out a door and his entire method has no move for a door, so there's just... nothing, for ten days, and she's lying there not knowing *whether they were still anything at all.* Maybe he thinks it's over. Maybe he thinks nothing happened. She can't even tell which.

And then she couldn't keep her own night. That's the detail that got under my ribs and stayed. She reaches back for the best memory she has — the top sheet, the fra diavolo, the garlic going into the oil, the plate set down only once she was aching for it — she goes *into it to be warm*, it's hers, it's the one thing nobody can take — and it turns while she's inside it. The hair goes gold. The leg goes tan. The sheet slips off a body *that had never in its life needed building for*, and Pace at the stove is looking and smiling and the tomatoes go in and the kitchen smells right. She built the other woman out of spite from a face at a dance hall and then couldn't evict her from her own best evening. I have done that. Not with a spite-blonde at a stove, but I have had an imagined woman repossess a real memory at one in the morning and there is nothing you can do about it but lie there. And the supports all failing at once — the bartender, *wasn't him they were jealous of, honey*, Randi's *he leaves you the room* — *they had only ever held her up while she was the one.* "The floor was gone." She has no standing to ask him anything, because he never gave her the word that would make it standing. The missing noun from chapter 50 is now a structural absence in her entire ability to be jealous, to inquire, to *exist* as a person with a claim. That's the cost of *words are cheap*, laid out in a twin bed.

And Sheri. *About Christmas he'd said nothing at all — a whole week she couldn't see into.* She's looking straight at the loaded gun and calling it a blonde from the dance hall. He cooked for Sheri in November because *she's alone*, and it is now the most alone week of the year, and the book is just letting me sit here with it. I don't even think he did anything. I think the horror is that Vee has invented the wrong woman and will be relieved by the wrong denial.

The small thing I can't stop turning: *her mother's question in her own mouth by then.* So the sentence she stood on the mat and asked — *what am I to you* — came out of Ohio, out of ten days of *how's your young man, is it serious*, out of a door she kept shut and smiled in front of. That doesn't make it less true, it was the truest thing in the book, but it tells me where the pressure came from and I notice the book handing Pace a sliver of cover I don't want him to have. And the crop, again: *same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it.* She scissored off her own leg. Nobody made her.

What I'm actually frightened of is the last paragraph, and it isn't Sheri. *She'd have Randi back the day the term started — the one sure thing the new year held, a class, a face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it. And Pace came with none of that: no hour she could name, no room he'd surely be in.* There's the ledger. One of them is a date she can circle and one of them is a maybe, and the girl is cold, and *you poor thing* is coming off a plane with its arms open. Randi decided in a hotel room in Gstaad not to tell her, not in any way that would make Vee *decide what to do with it* — *let Vee arrive at it on her own* — and then Vee lies down in Ohio already reaching for her. Randi doesn't even have to run the play. The play is running itself. And she's not doing it to recruit a girl for a threesome anymore, she's doing it because she's a coward in love, which is so much harder to hate and so much worse for everybody.

Tuesdays and Thursdays, seven to eight-twenty, no Cassie. Term starts. I'm already there.
