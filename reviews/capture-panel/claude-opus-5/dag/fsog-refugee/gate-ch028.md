REACTION

Okay, the pad behind the dark gym under the field lights, the taped-up boom box, and a man teaching a girl to dance — I did not expect to be undone by that and I was undone by that. *Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead.* *Being jelly doesn't work.* He's not asking her to go soft and be moved, he's asking her to be *solid* so she can receive him — she has a job, the resistance is the instrument, and that is the entire argument I have been trying to make about this genre for six years and this book put it in a man's mouth on a slab of concrete in October. And then: *That's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours.* I put the book down. The thing that hurts you is the distance you're holding for safety. Sure, Helen. Sure. Say it about dancing.

And *there's no sorry unless you take off a foot.* I'd take that man's number.

Then the Rusty Nail, and my shoulders came up around my ears, because I know this scene, I've read it ninety times — the bar full of women who've all had a piece of him, the pretty blonde with the drink and the poison, *he's so good about getting a girl home safe.* That's the oldest machine on the shelf and for about four pages I was tired. Except the book didn't let it stand. The bartender with the glass rack, flat, not doing Vee any favors: *Leaves her at the door. Doesn't take advantage — I'd have heard otherwise. Not that plenty haven't tried, hoping he would.* And then the knife: *Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey.* The blonde wasn't warning her, the blonde was *bleeding.* So the chestnut turns out to be the setup for a correction, and the correction is: he is exactly the man he appears to be, and more people know it than she does, and she is the one he brought.

What got me is that the jealousy wasn't from the blonde. It was her mother. *A man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them* — that voice has been in this book since chapter one and this is the first time it's steered the car. She didn't get lied to. She got handed a straight answer, *yes, sometimes, when it's required*, and it *wouldn't have mattered what he said, she was already angry and some part of her wanted to stay that way.* That's the truest sentence in the chapter. And then three dates and *spread her legs like a slut* — her own mouth, her mother's word. Everything he built in that fitting room, the burgundy, *he made it to you*, and one blonde with a drink undid it in ninety seconds, because it was never built on solid ground, it was built over a hole.

Him leaving — oh, that cost me. He set the beer down *untouched* and went out and let the cold in. No scene, no explanation, no chasing. She said *I don't need you to get me home safely, I'll handle that myself* and he took her at her literal word, the way he takes everything at her literal word, and that is both the most consistent and the most devastating thing he could have done. He has never once taken anything she didn't hand him. Tonight she handed him *go*, and he went. I sat there half furious at him for not saying one sentence and half knowing that the one sentence would have been him deciding for her, which he doesn't do. So she sat on a stool turning down men until the lights came up, and paid cab fare that was meant for something else — on top of shoes she put on a credit card with nothing under it. The money's getting loud. That's a bill coming.

And then the part that actually scared me, more than the fight: *Cassie was three feet away and would have sat up for this. She did not wake her.* Not because she couldn't. Because Cassie would *ask.* And then, in the morning: *go to Randi's.* There it is. The person who'd cost her the truth got skipped and the person who collects is the destination. She thinks she's choosing the friend who understands; she's walking the whole rupture — Pace's side and hers — straight into the hands of the one woman in this book who has both ends of the string. Randi is about to be handed the most valuable thing she's ever been handed, by a girl who thinks she's asking for help, and Randi *will* help, genuinely, warmly, and it will still be the third game. I want to reach through the page and shake her. Wake Cassie. Wake Cassie.

Also — the small dark thing I keep turning over: she went looking for the bill herself, mid-dance. *How much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join.* She's smart. She's asking the right question about the wrong man. The join she can't find isn't in him. It's in the woman who drives the Mercedes.
