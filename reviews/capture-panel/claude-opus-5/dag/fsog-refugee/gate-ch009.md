REACTION

Oh, *that's* the chapter. That's the one I've been hunting for years and mostly not finding, and this book just handed it to me in a restaurant with eight tables.

"May I choose?" Three words and I made a sound. Because look what it actually is — she's drowning in a menu she can't read, she's *asked for rescue without asking*, and he doesn't do the thing every other man in every other book does, which is start explaining. He doesn't perform his knowing at her. He sets his own menu down and *asks for the handoff*, and then — this is the part that got me — he doesn't take it lightly. He looks at the page longer than he needs to. He weighs what he's choosing *for her*. And he asks the one question that matters, is there anything you won't eat. He asked permission to take control and then treated the control as a responsibility instead of a trophy. I have read four hundred books where a man orders for a woman and it's a flex. This one it's *service*, and it's still dominance, and both things are true at once with no strain. That's the thing. That's the exact thing.

And she's *hungry*. God, the hunger in this chapter. The hollow that had been opening since noon that she didn't notice, and then "it kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." I felt that in my sternum. The jacket said *quietly starving* and I read that as a metaphor and here it is literal and metaphor at the same time — she eats without watching herself eat, which after Randi and the thin apple slices lands like a bell. Randi portions herself. Vee just got fed. The two of them are being mirrored and I don't think Vee will ever know.

The kiss. I want to lie down about the kiss. She says her rule — *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days* — and he **doesn't argue**. No lean, no charm turned up, no patient little wait for her to change her mind. He smiles like she's told him something *fine about herself* and starts them walking. He lets it close. He gives up the inch. And then she's the one who stops, and the hand goes taut between them, and she takes it. And even then — she kisses him small, and he answers *no more than she brought*, and eases back a breath and waits, because "whatever came next was hers too." He made her choose twice. He made choosing the erotic act. The blurb told me that would happen and I still wasn't ready for how hot a man declining to take something could be.

Also: she reached for Randi's bit at the coffee shop and here she's shedding it — "Vee, people call me Vee" is still doing its work in me. And the thread she drops at dinner and *doesn't pick back up*, the constant maintenance labor of being a girl going quiet — and then the rule arriving "at the worst possible moment, with its rule already in her mouth," the same reflex coming back. That's a woman rendered from inside. I believe her.

Which is why I'm sick about it. Because I was on that couch two chapters ago. I watched Randi come in hot and say *tell me* with the wine in her mouth, and I know that "she built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop" was reported back like a dispatch, and now I'm inside the froyo cup from the other side and the tenderness is *real*, that's the horror, it's not fake, he genuinely didn't hand over the embarrassing true thing she said on the walk — he kept it, *it was hers* — and he also kept her because Randi pointed at her across a lawn and said *yes* before she'd finished looking. Every single thing I want in a man is in this man and he was handed to her as a gift by a woman who wanted her first. The book is not winking. That's why I'm still here. If it winked I'd be gone.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he asked before he ordered her dinner and then he asked before he kissed her and then he made her close the last inch herself and I am NOT okay, also I think he's going to be good to her for a hundred pages and it's going to cost her everything.*

Small things I loved: the boots already lived-in versus the boys buying new shoes to look like they'd lived. Christine knowing him. The brass cages on the tea glasses so you can hold what's too hot to hold — come on. The elevator doors closing on her because she forgot to get off. The soft foreign words she can't repeat, *each soft sound a closed door with something behind it, wanting things she had no names for.* That's the whole book in a sentence and it's sitting in a hummus order.
