REACTION

Okay so I'm annoyed at how well that worked on me.

"May I choose?" Three words and I put the book down on my chest for a second. That's the thing. That's the exact thing I have been digging through this genre for years trying to find — not *let me order for you*, not the smug maître d' move where a man demonstrates command by talking over a woman's head to the waiter. He *asks*. He waits for the yes. And then — this is the part that got me — he doesn't treat the yes as a formality he's already banked. He takes it *seriously*. He looks at the page longer than he needs to because now he's choosing on her behalf and that's a weight. And he asks the one real question, is there anything you won't eat. That's it. That's dominance that makes room. A man taking the wheel *because she handed it to him* and then driving carefully because it's her car.

And the food. God, the food. She hasn't eaten since breakfast because she spent the afternoon on dresses and borrowed shoes, and the hunger finds her in the doorway, and then he orders things she can't pronounce and she sits there with her mouth watering at "promises she couldn't have read aloud." That's the whole book in one line and I know it. Wanting things you have no names for. Content for once not to know. I felt that in my sternum.

The thing that actually undid me, though, was smaller than the kiss. It was: *she lost the thread of what he was saying and let it go and nothing bad happened.* Because I know that girl. That low constant labor of keeping up your end, catching the thread before anyone notices you dropped it, staying watchable. And she just — stopped. "She stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong. She didn't notice she'd been waiting until she wasn't." I have never seen safety written as an absence before, as a thing you only detect by the noise that isn't there. And then *"she stopped doing any of the things she normally did and was not, for once, anywhere except exactly where she was."* I'd text that one at 1 a.m. with no context.

The hand. The hand undid me too. "Less like something beginning than something resolving" — her hand had been wrong all its life for want of his exactly. That's absurd, that's a twenty-one-year-old's overclaim, and it's *right*, it's exactly the size the feeling is at that age and the book doesn't apologize for it or wink at it.

And then. The rule. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days." I braced so hard. This is the hinge where ninety percent of this shelf loses me — the lean, the charm turned up, the *patient little wait for her to change her mind*, which is coercion wearing a cardigan. And the book names all three of those and he does *none* of them. He smiles like she's told him something fine about herself and he lets it *close*. He gives it up. He starts walking. And then she doesn't move, and the hand goes taut between them, and she gets to be the one who closes the distance *he had already surrendered*. "I'm making an exception." I made a noise. That's the whole thesis of what I want out of a book — she is not overcome, she is not talked past, she *chooses*, and the choosing is the erotic event. And the chapter had already taught me she still has the reflex ("it came back now, here, at the worst possible moment, with its rule already in her mouth") and she overrode it herself. Nobody overrode it for her.

Now. Here's why my stomach is upside down.

I know. I've known since the blurb and I've known worse since chapter 7. I sat through Randi straddling him on the couch, drinking his wine, hissing *tell* into his ear, getting off on a report about a girl building too big a froyo cup. So every single tender thing in this chapter arrives doubled. He showers and shaves and polishes the boots *for this* — and I love him for it, and I also know this evening was pointed at from a lawn. He builds the quiet into the text so she doesn't have to answer that night — which is the single most considerate thing a man has done in any book I've read this year — and it's also *good technique*, and it was honed on a woman who locked herself in his bathroom and decided never to come out again. That's the trick this book is running on me and it's working. I'm being handed exactly the thing I say I want and made to watch it be aimed.

What keeps it from souring: I don't think he's faking. That's what's merciless about it. He's *not* performing care — he really does pick the tutor's path, he really does leave the closed door alone (Daphne, whoever she is, cost him that lesson), he really does let her have her rule. He is genuinely this man. He just also selected her off a sidewalk like a dish, and the two facts are not going to cancel, and the book knows they won't. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." Yeah. I get it now. I hate it. Keep going.

Small things I'm turning over: always the right hand, never the left, "some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." That's planted. I don't know what it's for and I'm watching it. Christine, who *eased* when he walked in — somebody has known him a long time and is glad about him, which is the first outside testimony we've had, and I want more of that. Cassie saying "that's not a boy, that's a man" and *not teasing her about this one* — Cassie is the only person in this book with clean hands and I'm scared for her, because she said *Goodbye, Vee*, the whole word, and I have not forgotten it. And Randi is starving on apple slices while Vee eats bread with both hands in front of the man they share, and nobody in the book has noticed that yet but me.
