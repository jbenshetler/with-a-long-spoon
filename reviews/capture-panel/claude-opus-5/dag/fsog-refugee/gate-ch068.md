Oh, this one's titled *Coming Due* and then Marsh stands up and lectures on **reciprocity — the three obligations, to give and to take and to pay back, a gift being a debt you lay on someone whether they want it or not** — and Vee *slides off it.* Sits there with her leg held still against Randi's knee and lets the entire operating manual of her own life go by in the air over her head. Sixty-odd chapters of Randi's gifts — the skirt off the rail, Sondra on Draper Street, the bottle ordered without the list, the cover story handed over warm every single time the truth got close — and the man at the front of the room is saying *that was never free* and she can't hear a word of it because her wrist is still ringing. I had to put the book down on my chest for a second. This book is not even hiding anymore. It's just confident I'm the only one in the room awake.

And the Lifesavers. **She forgot.** Every class so far she's handed one over, and tonight Randi had to lean in and *ask* — the ritual reversed, the giver gone absent at her post — and then Randi's fingertip dragged across the inside of her wrist on the way to taking it and *her pulse leapt up under it to be found.* That's the booth, nine weeks later, with the wiring exposed. And Vee calls it **static**. *The skin is thin there, everyone's is.* Girl. You have a whole file of these now.

But the thing I'm actually going to be carrying: **"Having you at the dance was so great. I've wanted to do that for the longest time."** And the half-beat lag — *the same lag it had had in his bed* — and the word coming in behind the woman who'd already turned to her coat. *Having.* That she is something Randi *gets to have*, and has wanted to have, and for a long time. And then the line that took the top of my head off: **in his bed there had been him for it, the whole solid fact of him under her to pour the heat into, to set it against and call it his. Here there was no him.** That's the whole problem of this volume in two sentences. She's been laundering every single feeling about Randi through Pace's body — Saturday night on the county road at one in the morning was a *laundering run*, that's all it was, she said so herself, *to crowd the other thing out of her head* — and tonight the heat arrives in a warm cluttered classroom with no one to charge it to and it just sits there in her lap being hers. Unassignable. No receipt.

And **she turned her mouth away.** On purpose. *Not an accident got up to look like one.* After dozens of them — after bringing her face up with her eyes already shut and *waiting on the hand* — she took the miss deliberately, and handed over a plague she doesn't have. The first thing she has ever withheld from Randi, and she withheld it because she wants it too much: *she wanted it so badly that the wanting was the very reason she couldn't let herself take it.* That is the most accurate sentence about desire in the entire series and it's about a two-second goodnight kiss on a dorm step.

And then Randi took the neck anyway.

I want to be careful here because I keep getting played by this woman, but: Randi read the turned mouth. *Drew back an inch, reading her.* She accepted the cover story — **"Keep your plague, then"** — and did not step out of the hug, and sighed Thursday into her hair, and then put her mouth on the exact spot, the hollow where the neck goes down into the shoulder, the one she'd been told not to weaponize, **and made it wet**, and got the sheet of goosebumps and the sound Vee couldn't catch, and laughed, *delighted, not sorry at all:* **"See? How do you like it?"** Payback. Framed as a joke. On the night the lecture was about the obligation to repay. She collected. And she got the hug she was denied twice over, and inside ten seconds she had **brunch Saturday** out of a girl who'd walked the whole cold mile planning to escape her. *Lit up like she'd been handed a present.* The refusal cost Randi nothing and earned her a weekend.

And the brunch came out of **the ringing, not out of any part of her that was thinking.** Her mouth asked for more while the rest of her was still trying to get out of the building. That's the engine of this volume: her body files the request and her head gets the carbon copy.

Also — *"Standards committee. I've got to go discipline a bad girl."* I'm sorry, no. You do not get to drop that in a hallway with your mouth in a girl's hair and have me treat it as scheduling. That's a woman saying something out loud to see whether it lands and having a committee to hide it behind if it does. The loophole is always pre-built. Always.

And the long bad quiet of Monday, which I loved more than the kiss: **the hand flat on her stomach, a lid held down on a pot**, not because anybody forbade it — *if anything the message ran the other way, and Pace had made a point of telling her so, in the dark, in the way that stuck* — but because she knows exactly where her mind goes the second she stops minding it. *As long as she didn't give it the thing that would make it true, it could stay only wanting.* She's rationing her own hand to keep a name out of a room. That's the inch she couldn't close in January turned inside out and used as a dam.

Sunday I liked less, and I noticed myself liking it less. Four times, every one of them started by her, *each time something in her let go for as long as it lasted, and then, after, took itself back up*, and she drove home **full of him and not, somehow, filled.** It's honest and it's a little awful, because he's being used as an appliance by a girl who told him a lie in the dark thirty hours earlier and is now topping it off hourly. And it's all summary. He's offstage as a *person* for this whole chapter — he's a resource. I know that's the point. It still means the two of them haven't been in a room together in any way that counts since she lied to his face, and that lie is sitting down there *cold, under all the rest of it*, and growing.

And then the last four lines. **Cassie in her coat, cold still coming off her, book on her knees: "What was that with you and Randi?"** *Not the flat way she said most true things — there was a worry under it, and a thread of disbelief she didn't trouble to hide.* She was *there.* She came in behind them, or she saw the doorstep, or she saw something. The only instrument in this book that reads true, finally pointed at the thing, asking out loud, worried — and Vee **opens her mouth for the easy thing and there is nothing to meet it, because she doesn't know.** That's not a cliffhanger, that's a trapdoor. Her oldest friend asked her the plainest question there is and she has no lie left and no truth yet.
