She put the skirt back. Four words and I knew exactly what this chapter was going to cost her. Black pants, crewneck to the collarbone, pearls, coat done up to the collar — *in all the brunches since October she had never once dressed for Randi to see less of her* — and then she sat down in that armor and told that woman about the brush going into her and handed her a photograph of her own open body. The clothes did nothing. The clothes were never going to do anything. She dressed like a church pew and then let a woman's stockinged foot sit in her lap for ten minutes and rubbed her thumb back and forth across the arch of it without noticing she'd started. *She couldn't have said since when.* God.

And the gesso telling. Third time Randi has run a whole sex scene across a tablecloth with a fork in her hand and I fell into it again. The questions are what undo me — not *what happened* but **"What did it feel like? There. Inside."** / **"Was it a soft brush?"** / **"Where?"** / **"You're going to have to clue me in"** — a woman who knows precisely what the answer is making the girl say it in her own mouth, in a booth, at noon, over coffee the waiter was too smart to deliver. And the foot pressing *once, gently,* right at the exact bare place under discussion. That's not a tease. That's a woman putting her foot on the thing she wants and calling it a joke, which is the only way she has ever touched anything.

But the photo. The photo. I wrote in my notes four chapters ago, in her own words, *she would not be telling her about this* — too much, God, no — and she **showed her**, and the reasoning she used to get there is the most frightening thing in the chapter: *if the photo stays in the bag, it stays there because of last Saturday, because I've made my best friend into somebody I have to hide things from. A friend would just hand it over.* She used the fact that it would mean something as the proof that it had to mean nothing. She reasoned her way across the last line she had by arguing that having a line was itself the sin. And then **"Randi would look at it and know everything... and the thought of that went through Vee low and hot and would not leave."** She wanted to be known like that. That's the whole transaction and she dressed it as friendship.

And Randi didn't take it so much as **draw it in — her fingers closing over Vee's and the phone together.** Both hands. Her own side of the table. And then her face did *nothing at all, which was not like her face*, and the thumb didn't move. And **"I see why, Miss November"** in the hoarse voice. And then she recited the colors back — walnut, parsley, grape, cashew, parsley, cantaloupe — *she'd been holding them in order* — and asked the one question that matters: **"So what did he give you for the gesso?"** She went straight to the hole. She found the missing seventh taste faster than Vee found it in four days of lying awake. Because she's been looking for that hole since the polenta.

**"Not a bit. I'd hang that over my bed."** And the knees pressing together under the table, hard.

And then — **"If it doesn't mean no anymore, honey, maybe you should tell him."** Which is the single most useful sentence anyone has said to this girl in three volumes, and is also a woman unlocking the door she wants to walk through. Both at once, no wink, and I cannot get it to resolve and I've stopped expecting to. And **"You were curious — and he listened"** — she gave Pace the credit, out loud, in his absence, for the thing that's starving the girl. She is the only person in this book who tells Vee true things, and she only ever tells her the ones that serve her.

Then the ladies' room, and that's where I went up in smoke. It started as him — the brush, the mouth, the long cleaning, his face coming up shining — and then **"Don't wipe it," she thought. Forty feet from the woman, with her own phone still in that woman's hands, with the picture open on it. Her knees went.
