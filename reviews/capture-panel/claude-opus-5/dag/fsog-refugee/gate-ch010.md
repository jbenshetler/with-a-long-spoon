REACTION

Oh, this one got me in the throat, and it's barely four pages long.

Because here's the thing — I *wanted* this chapter. After a first kiss, the only thing I want is the girl running home to tell someone. That's half of why I read these books. The event is the event, but the telling of the event is where it becomes real, where she gets to hear herself say it out loud and find out what she thinks. And Vee comes in still warm and the cold off the sill can't touch her and she's already talking before her coat's off. Yes. That's exactly it. I have been that girl in that doorway.

And then she slows down at the tree, and her voice drops, and Cassie leans in the last inch to get it. That's the whole chapter for me. "He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me." She's not telling Cassie about a kiss she received. She's telling Cassie about a thing she *did* — and that's the line I've been starved for in this genre for about five years. Every book I left had a heroine reporting what had been done to her. This one came home with a story about her own hands.

And then Cassie — God, Cassie. "How much did he give you to drink?" I actually put my phone down. That is a best friend who has heard a different version of this story before, from this same girl or from some other girl, and who checks first and celebrates second. And Vee gets to say *nothing, nobody did anything to me, I wasn't talked into it, I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose* — and she gets to say it out loud, to a witness, with the words in the right order. The book put a consent check in a girls' dorm room at midnight and made it the emotional climax of the scene. I don't think I've ever seen that. I'm a little undone by it.

The spin in the bean bag chair. The room too small to hold it. "Look at me." I'm so happy for her and I feel sick.

Because the other half of this chapter is the dread, and the dread is enormous. Cassie says *what's his name so I know what to tell the state troopers* as a joke, and Vee throws a pillow, and then Vee counts off her dossier — off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel, a trail somewhere, he'll have water — and Cassie says, flat, "That's the whole address." And Vee *laughs*. "See. I know where he lives and everything." She doesn't know where he lives. She's never been to the house. She's going out past the gravel with a man whose house she hasn't seen, and the one person in this book who smells something is sitting in a desk chair with her feet off the floor saying *borrow good ones.*

And I know what's in that house. I've been in that bedroom. I know there's a table in the middle of it that isn't a table, and I know the last thing Randi said about this girl was *do you think she'll like the bench.* Vee's little dossier is funny to her and it's a cold hand on my neck, because the thing she's missing isn't the address. It's Randi. She's listing the risks of a stranger and the stranger isn't the stranger.

And Cassie said *goodbye, Vee* in a lecture hall two chapters ago. The whole word. I noted it then and I'm noting it harder now, because this chapter is Cassie on the edge of the bunk being the warmest, driest, most present person in the book, and the book keeps putting her slightly outside the frame — declining the coffee, not reaching far enough with her feet, going back to her page. She's the one who'd catch it. She's being walked out of the story and she can feel it and she's not going to make a scene about it.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the best friend asked if he got her drunk and the answer was no and I cried a little, also I think they're going to lose her and the only person who'd notice is the one nobody invites.*

Also, quietly: four weeks in, no one has been alone in a room with Vee and Pace since the shadow under the tree, and I don't need them to be yet. The froyo, the dinner, the dorm room — the care is on the page in all of it. But I want that house. I want to see her walk into that house. And I am frightened of the chapter where she does, in a way I haven't been frightened by a book in a long while, because I'm not frightened *of* him exactly — he gave her back the pencil, he left the last inch where it was — I'm frightened of what the two of them decided about her on a lawn in October while she was walking back to her dorm not feeling her feet.
