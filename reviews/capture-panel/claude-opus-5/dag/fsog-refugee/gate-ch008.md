REACTION

Oh, this one snuck up on me. I opened it expecting to be a little impatient — no Pace, no Randi, a pool — and instead I got the first chapter that's actually *Vee's*, alone, with nobody working on her, and it turns out that's the chapter I needed. Everything else so far has been Vee being lit up by other people's attention. Here she's just a girl in a swimsuit that doesn't fit her anymore, doing the long walk past the bleachers with her face on.

The catcalls made my jaw tight. "You won't need floaties with *those*." And the way she's already pre-armored — rolled towel carried *down at her side, not up against her*, because holding it against her chest would admit something. God, that detail. That's a girl who has had years of practice. And the book doesn't play it as cute or as flattering; it lets it be what it is, which is a room full of boys taking something off her on her way to the water. I noticed the writer didn't let her have a comeback either — just the long blink, the half-purse, the eyeroll at the ceiling. That's exactly right. You don't get to answer. You just keep walking.

Then the underwater bit and I put my phone down for a second. *She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem.* That's the whole book's thesis about her in one line and it's the first time I've felt genuinely tender toward her rather than fond-and-worried. The scull, the figure-eight her hands remember, the bright moving ceiling, nobody up there can see her and nobody down here wants anything — and then she *grieves, a little*. For what? For the body that used to just be a body. I'm going to be thinking about that. It also reframes the Chi Latte chapter for me, retroactively: the cardigan over the better shirt, her mother's voice saying *floozies*. This is where the shame lives, and now I've stood in it with her instead of watching her manage it.

And Cassie. Cassie, Cassie. She could have pitched it up into the bleachers, she had the lungs, *she'd kept it for Vee* — that's love, and it's the only uncomplicated love in this book so far. The splash from ten feet off, no step closer, never asking because asking makes the sad version bigger. Cassie in the navy suit she doesn't like and doesn't care about. Standing at rib depth like a woman leaning on a fence. I would read a whole book about Cassie.

Which is also what's scaring me. Because I'm holding the jacket in my other hand, and I know there's a plan, and Cassie is the only person in this cast who isn't in on it — the flat patient look at Randi thumbing her phone, declining Chi Latte, and that *"Goodbye, Vee"* in the lecture hall, the whole word, the wrong word, which I have not stopped chewing on. Cassie knows something, or senses it, and this chapter just made me love the person who is going to get edged out. That's what this quiet pool hour was *for*, wasn't it. Establish the one safe room so the loss has weight. I see you, Helen Rivers. It worked anyway.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the swimming chapter. no sex, no men, and I'm the one who's wrecked. also I'm now genuinely afraid for the roommate." And underneath it all I'm aware I haven't had Vee and Pace in a room together since the frozen yogurt, and I *want* that — not because this was slow, because it wasn't, but because now that I've been down in the green with her I want to see her get looked at by the one person who's been looking *past* the face instead of at the chest. That's the appointment I'm keeping.
