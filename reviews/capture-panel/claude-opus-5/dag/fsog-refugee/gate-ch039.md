REACTION

Oh, Cassie. *Cassie.*

I've been waiting for someone to be in a room with Vee without an angle, and here it is, and it's the bean bag and a book turned facedown. "You're doing the streetlight thing again. You're a public utility." That's love. That's the whole register of a person who has watched you be this happy before and is going to let you have it anyway, with one dry shot across the bow first.

And then — *"There's only one way that burn happens. Not enough on."*

I put the book down and laughed out loud. Cassie knows. Cassie has known, probably for weeks, and she's never once made Vee say it, and the first time she lets on it's a one-liner about a tomato and she's already letting Vee talk right past it. Vee's hand goes to the side of her breast without her knowing it moved — God, that detail, the body telling on her while her mouth is busy being charming — and Cassie clocks the location, does the arithmetic, and spends it on a joke instead of a question. That's the opposite of Randi. Randi would have gone *tell me everything* and leaned in and taken it out of her an inch at a time and Vee would have left the table wet and ashamed. Cassie takes exactly one bite and sets it down. (There's a sentence I didn't mean to write that way, and I'm leaving it.)

But here's what's got me cold under the warm: Vee came home *lit up* and she gave Cassie the food. The tomatoes, the cooking with intent, the house staying warm. Not the kitchen. Not the bet. Not the kneeling at the edge of the bed, not the shoes she can't pay for, not the woman who says *sticky fingers* and hands her a pastry she can taste on her own lip afterward. The edit is still running. It's just so much smoother now that it doesn't even feel like editing — she thinks she *told* Cassie, she talked for twenty minutes, she was generous, she was glowing. And every single thing she handed over was something Pace cooked.

What she can't edit is her own absence. *"You don't come home school nights."* Not a question. Vee opens her mouth to argue it and goes back over a week she hadn't counted and finds her own bed sat empty more nights than it didn't. Nobody else in this book is counting. Randi's not counting — Randi's scheduling. Pace isn't counting; Pace gave her the top drawer. Cassie is the only person in Vee's life keeping an honest tally of where Vee actually is, and she's keeping it *kindly*, and she has no idea she's the last one who doesn't know.

And then she asks it. *"Do you love him."* Flat, same voice as the rest of it. Randi read it off her face in a mimosa glass back in the fall and moved on and never asked. Pace hasn't asked, hasn't said. Cassie asks. And Vee — who hasn't shut up since the door — goes small. *Maybe. I think so.* And then *it's all so fast*, which is the truest thing she's said out loud all book, because it has been fast, and the speed is not an accident, and she doesn't know that.

She whispered *Peter* to a sleeping man last chapter and couldn't say the word in daylight to anyone. Now she's said *maybe* to the one person who will carry it without using it. Cassie didn't say *I know.* Didn't argue the maybe. Went back to her book. *Some of us have class.* That's the most restraint anyone has shown Vee since page one.

The "fine" thing wrecked me a little. The boyfriends before were *fine* — and she went along thinking fine was the whole of what there was to have. That's my people. That's exactly the sentence I'd have written at twenty-one if I'd had it, and it's why I read these books at all. She's not comparing him to a fantasy, she's comparing him to the actual thin gruel she'd been told was dinner.

Two things sitting crooked in me. One: she's lost weight and the book keeps telling me so, gently, in bra bands and loose clothes, and I don't know yet whether that's *happy* or whether it's the first item on a bill. Two: she's happier than she's ever been and she has now, in one chapter, been seen clearly by the only person who sees her clearly, and said *maybe* instead of yes. The gap between what she's living and what she can say is the widest it's been, and the only room where it narrows is the one she sleeps in two nights a week.

Also I noticed: it's been a whole chapter and change since Randi, and I'm not relieved. I'm waiting for her with my shoulders up.
