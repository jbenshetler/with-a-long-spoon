REACTION

Oh, this one's cruel in the way I came back for. I'd *just* been in that chalet. I know what the eyeliner looked like at the mirror. I know the cold didn't belong to the room. And now I'm lying under the gold swimmers in Ohio watching her refresh a feed and envy it — "even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than I have" — and the text lands and she reads *you ANGEL 😇* as proof that glamorous wild Randi took time out of something amazing for her. She's envying the loneliest room in the book. Rivers put the two sides of that exchange back to back and didn't say one word about it, and I had to sit up.

And the part I'll keep: the blonde. She goes looking for her own best night — the top sheet, the fra diavolo, the wine she hadn't drunk earlier, the garlic blooming, the sheet slipping and him seeing and her not fixing it — she goes in *to be warm*, and it turns on her while she's inside it. The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, somebody else is in her chair in the wine light and he's at the stove smiling. **She could not keep her own night.** That's the first time this book has taken something *back* from her, and it did it with her own hands. And then all the reassurance she's been living on — the bartender not looking up from the glasses, *Pace don't bring dates*, *he doesn't sulk, he leaves you the room* — "they had only ever held her up while she was the one," and they went through her hands. *The floor was gone.* She's standing where he's been standing this whole time. Same trapdoor, other side.

The *he always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now* is the knife. His whole grace, every bit of it, converted to silence by one unanswered night. He's across town being exactly who he is and it reads as indifference now, and nothing about him changed.

What I'm genuinely sick over: *She'd have Randi back the day the term started — the one sure thing the new year held, a class, a face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it. And Pace came with none of that: no hour she could name, no room he'd surely be in. A day she could circle for the one and nothing at all for the other.* There it is. The hole he left is the exact shape of what the other one does for free, and Vee has now done the arithmetic herself, in the dark, without knowing it was arithmetic. And then — *and what about Pace? and nothing came after it.* She can't even finish the sentence about him anymore. Two nights a week, seven to eight-twenty, no Cassie, starting in January. The man's going to lose her to a scheduling conflict and a woman who says the word.

Also noting, because it's hers and nobody made her: she's the one who scissored the leg off the induction photo for her mother's phone. *Same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it.* She keeps doing the editing herself and then grieving the edit.

The only thing that wore on me — this is the second time I've watched her hold a thumb over an empty message field in a cold bed with somebody breathing or sleeping nearby. The bunk bed did it harder and did it first, and the shirt in a heap was a bigger ending than this one's got. I didn't need the repeat to understand her. But *are you*— deleted letter by letter, and being glad he's not a man who lives on his phone so he'd never see the dots start and stop? Fine. Fine, I'll allow it.

Two chapters now with no Pace in a room. I can feel it. I want him on the page.
