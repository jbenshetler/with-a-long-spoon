REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter the whole book has been standing behind, and I felt it in my teeth.

Because here's the thing — I know. I watched them pick her. I watched Pace point at a redheaded girl laughing with her head thrown back and say *her*, and I watched Randi go still and say *yes* in that bare voice before she could dress it up. So I came into this chapter already sick with it. And then the chapter has the nerve to give me the single most *me* sequence in the book so far. He gives her back the pencil. He gives her back the pencil at the exact line where she can finish it herself. He asks what her professor said instead of telling her what he'd say. He writes the ugly formula in the margin and then says *don't, nobody should have to carry that around* and makes one little crossed mark instead. That's not seduction, that's — it's the thing. That's a man whose competence makes room instead of taking it up, and I have been looking for that specific move in this genre for about six years. And he's doing it *on purpose, as a technique, at a girl who was selected for him across a lawn.* I'm furious and I'm charmed and I refuse to pick.

"May I call you." I heard it the way she heard it. I actually sat up.

What's doing the most work, though, is Randi's hands. The book never once tells me what Randi's feeling — it just puts the cup in her hand and has her hold it too long, and bring it to her mouth twice without sipping, and Vee notes it and files it as *she's bored, I'm talking too much, wrap it up.* Oh honey. And Randi gives him her name as one flat word — no bit, no adjective, no parents-with-a-sense-of-humor — the same flat one-word thing *Cassie* did to Randi in the lecture hall, which I noticed, which means something I can't name yet. Jealousy? Of him? Of Vee? Of what she's set in motion and now has to sit at the table and watch? I think she wanted this and is finding out it costs, and she has no apparatus for that, same as the bathroom mirror.

And then Cassie. Cassie is the only honest instrument in this book. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." Said right in front of Randi. Dead flat. Cassie keeps handing Vee the truth in four words and Vee keeps smoothing it over — *it's coffee, weirdo* — and I am starting to be genuinely afraid for Vee in a way that has nothing to do with sex. The *goodbye, Vee* from last chapter has not stopped ringing. Cassie knows something or senses something and nobody is listening to her.

Also: the title. Substitution. Turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do. Put two things that won't give on their own at right angles to each other and suddenly they're one thing that's easy to name. That's the chapter, that's Pace and Randi, that's Vee. The book just told me its whole method in a margin note and let her thank it.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he gave her back the pencil and I've never been more scared of a man in my life.*

Weak spot, small: I did feel the author's hand a bit in how thoroughly Vee catalogues his unreadable clothes — the shirt-too-good-for-a-handyman beat went one pass longer than it needed. But she's an econ girl who reads rooms for a living, so I'll allow it.
