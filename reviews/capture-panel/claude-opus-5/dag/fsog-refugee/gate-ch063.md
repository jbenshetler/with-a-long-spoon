Okay. Okay. I had to put the book face-down on the arm of the couch for a second.

First: she did it. Six chapters of me yelling into my notes about what's owed, and Randi walked into her own house's party with Vee on her arm and said **"I chose"** to the woman who handed her the bill, out loud, in the room, with her arm already around Vee's waist. And then the chapter spent the next twenty minutes showing me she'd found the loophole instead.

Because Brooke's terms were *play all you want, privately, or for the boys, and not one girl in this house will say a word.* And what did Randi do? She found a boy across the room — Harrison, who exists for exactly one reason and has no other lines — pointed at him, said *let's put on a show, make him wish he were me*, and kissed Vee like that in the middle of a floor full of ninety girls who now have a frame to file it under. She got the thing. She got it in public. And she paid nothing. That is the most Randi move in two volumes and I did not see it coming because I was busy being proud of her.

The kiss itself. God. The held inch. Randi's hand in her hair, drawing her *down* — Vee has to lean, has to give herself to the angle, and the book makes a point of that — and then Randi does *nothing at all* and lets the inch become its own event, and Vee's eyes go over her face the way they've wanted to all autumn and weren't allowed, and the perfume arrives "from everywhere" and turns in her **like a key**. That's a consent beat. That's the whole asking-is-the-heat thesis done in silence, a woman holding still a breath away and letting the other one come the last distance. Vee's lips moved first. Her hand went up into Randi's hair on its own and she *did not take it back*. The desire is hers, rendered from inside, no ambiguity, and it is molten.

And it's built on a lie. That's the part that's sitting in my chest like a swallowed ice cube.
