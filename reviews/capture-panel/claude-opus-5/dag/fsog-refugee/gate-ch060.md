REACTION

Okay so I went in expecting a soft landing chapter — water on the nightstand, two brown pills, a man who baked a loaf of cinnamon raisin bread while a girl slept off three ciders in his bed — and instead the floor came out from under me in a kitchen with nobody raising their voice.

The front half is pure balm and I ate it exactly the way she ate the toast. *A folded square of paper towel under the glass so it wouldn't mark the wood.* That's the whole man in nine words. The gap where the walk from the truck to the door should have been and wasn't — the undershirt pulled down over her head, the smell of his soap, being steered by the shoulders, and then **his mouth on her forehead in the dark after he thought she was already gone.** That's the one that got me, because it's the only kiss in this book nobody was supposed to receive. He didn't do it for her. He did it because he couldn't not.

And then she asks for his other friends.

I have been complaining in writing for sixty-six chapters that this man will give her everything except a sentence, and this chapter he gave her four paragraphs. Unasked. About himself. **"Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else."** And then he stood against his own counter with his arms crossed and *explained the architecture* — fifteen years old off the end of every scale men measure on, never once in the middle of the pack, so never once able to lose to somebody and come out the other side friends; and then eighteen, when his own cohort finally arrived, and he already had the patent and the money and the house and was finished with the thing they were starting. **"The window opens once. That door's on a timer."** Flat, no heat, a man handling a tool he's used a long time. And *"people underrate it"* — *what are you doing for Christmas, your folks coming up, you want to bring anybody* — **"Every one of those has a gap under it, for me."** Friendships get built out of the ordinary questions and every ordinary question is a trapdoor for him. I have never once thought about it that way and I'm going to be thinking about it in the car tomorrow.

And **"It isn't tragic."** And Vee clocking that *he set it in good order and the good order was the tell.* Same as "the two years with Daphne were luck." The man files his amputations.

The one that went coldest: **"and the ones already there, when I was small among them, either weren't safe or weren't welcoming."** No name. Doesn't need a name. And Vee lining it up against *less than staying* at a black window and Sheri's low voice across the taproom — *went bad with his daddy, he hasn't been home since he was sixteen* — and the pieces making a thing she will never be shown and doesn't need to be. I didn't need to be either. A fifteen-year-old in a building of grown men, home not an option, nobody his age in one single room of that entire starting year.

And then she does the arithmetic I've been doing for her all along and she does it *better* than I did, because she does it out of her own life: Meg's closet, Kayla's fry tax, Cassie saying *oh, Vee* in the voice that already caught the part she hadn't said, Randi helpless on a shoe-store carpet. **"One person couldn't be all of that. Nobody could hold that much of somebody."** And he'd had the one. And the one was Daphne. And **she'd had that much of him only because of the thing about it that was wrong.**

That paragraph. She got right up to the word and would not set it down — *the ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue and that she kept pressing back down* — and held both versions at once, his best two years and the wrong shape it actually was, **stacked and unreconciling, both true, and it hurt to hold them both.** That's it. That's the honest place. The book didn't make her say it and it didn't let her off either, and it told me plainly that the refusing is a choice she's making out of love for his memory of it. I'd have thrown the book if she'd shrugged. She didn't shrug. And then: Daphne left *sensibly*, and **there was nobody left in the world to take the heartbreak to.** No Randi to bring it to. God.

And then she didn't say sorry. *Because sorry would have asked him to hold still and be comforted for a thing he'd handed her as plain fact.* She came around the counter — the counter she'd kept between them the whole conversation because she was assembled out of spare parts this morning — and gave him **Sheri's hug.** Both arms, cheek flat to his chest, whole weight committed. She learned it off another woman in a doorway twelve hours earlier and she brought it straight to him. And his arms came around her *after a moment. Not fast.* And his chin on her head and his eyes out past her at the middle of the morning, **not happy and not sad, somewhere else with it**, and he let both of them just stand there and didn't try to fix anybody's feelings. That's the strongest thing the two of them have done together without taking anything off.

And now the part that's sitting in my chest like a swallowed ice cube. Because I've been in that other bedroom. I know what she doesn't.

*Women misunderstand it. The friendship. It gets awkward.* Sir. **Sir.** He said that in his own kitchen to the girl who loves him, about the one woman he can't be friends with, while the woman who can never want him is the only friend he's got — and across town there's a third one who is neither, who he is in bed with, who said *I want her to know exactly what she does to me.* He has exactly one friendship in the world that didn't go one way or the other, and the reason it works is that Sheri could never want him. He said the true sentence and it indicted him and he didn't hear it.

And Vee sitting there warm over her eggs, planning Tuesday. **"Randi was going to be insufferable about having been right, and Vee was going to let her, was going to lay the whole thing out and hand her the win with both hands."** *I don't have to be jealous* — tried from every side, and it held, and it's *true about Sheri and only Sheri.* The faceless blonde got downgraded and retired and she's going to walk into that loft and hand the trophy to the actual answer. The jealousy had a correct address the whole time and Randi personally talked her out of ever checking it.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he has ONE FRIEND and the reason she's the only one who stuck is she can't want him, and he said that out loud, and the girl across the counter is going to tell the other one about it on Tuesday, and I think I might scream.*

I'm not even annoyed there was no sex in it. I'd have been annoyed if there had been.
