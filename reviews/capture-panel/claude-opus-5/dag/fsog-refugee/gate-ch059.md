REACTION

Okay so I was braced. Two chapters of warmth in a row and then "There's somebody I'd like you to meet" at the sink, and the name, and I felt the floor go out from under *me* the way it went out from under her in Ohio — because I know what that invented blonde cost her, and I know the book knows, and I was absolutely sure we were about to spend a chapter paying for it. We did not. We spent a chapter getting it refunded, and I'm a little wrecked about it.

The blonde "didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand." That's the sentence. A month of a woman built out of self-loathing in a childhood bed, dissolved by five feet of two-tone perm coming off a stool. And then — God — the *look.* Vee braces for the pricing look, the placing look, the one she's been fluent in since high school, and instead the look takes the bare January leg with its time fully taken and gets to the induction heels and *sticks*, and the mouth goes round, and Vee realizes it was enjoying her the whole languid trip down. And she laughs out loud at nothing in the middle of a taproom and a month leaves her ribs in one breath. I put the book down on my chest for a second. That's the first time in this entire series a woman has looked at Vee's body in public and handed her something with no bill attached — no coaching, no *hmm*, no quarter-inch adjustment to her wrap. Just: nice, and have a cider.

And I had it filed wrong! The redhead. All this time I had "she doesn't care what the redhead says" down as Sheri bristling at *Vee*, and it was a standing invitation to her — *bring your opinions* — issued in December, before they'd ever laid eyes on each other. Sheri's been saving a quip about Randi like money and holding a dance for Vee like a seat. The fuse I thought I was watching was pointed the other direction entirely.

Then the blush. *Then the blush.* "And you get to motorboat them things?" and the color comes up his neck past anything deniable and he looks at the table and rubs his neck. Sixty-five chapters of a man I have watched be unflappable in a dark bedroom, in a doorway with a naked girl in a coat, on a dance floor with a hall watching — and a short woman with crooked eyeliner turns him red in one line. I loved it so much. It's the first time he's been *outmatched* by someone who isn't Vee, and he wore it the way he wears everything, without fighting it.

And "He tells me you get the philosophy of his food." That landed on me harder than it landed on Vee. He *said that to somebody.* He's been carrying her around out loud in a place she wasn't, for months, in the one room where he talks. All those nights at his back and he was also talking about her off a tailgate to a woman on a bicycle. That's the man who won't say a sentence to her face saying it everywhere else, and I don't know whether to be moved or to put my head in my hands, so I did both.

Sheri's story gutted me and then got up and ordered another round. Came out last March, lost the mother, the Marine Corps father who doesn't retire, the brother, the church, the Bible study girls, all inside a month — "whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish." And *"strangers will say that for free. Easy words, for a hard thing. The ones who owed me words weren't spending any."* That's this book's whole thesis said from the other side of it: words are cheap when they're cheap, and the ones who owe them don't spend them. She cried in the middle of a two-step at the one building left with her name on it, and he drove her home and asked nothing, and came back the next morning with coffee and donuts and *fixed the lock the landlord had been promising since August.* He gave her a working lock instead of a sentence. He always does. It is the most him thing I have ever read and it is also, exactly, the flaw in its best coat — again.

"I could tell he understood it, and I didn't ask how." And then the thing she dropped on the table and refused to decorate: *went bad with his daddy, he hasn't been home since he was sixteen.* **Sixteen.** I've been treating Daphne as the pin in everything and now there's something underneath Daphne, and it's a father, and a boy who left at sixteen and never went back, and *my mother stays home*, and the scar. And she handed me the dish and said *I'll tell you the rest another time — you don't praise a man at his own table*, which is the most elegant IOU this book has written. I will absolutely be there for that.

And the ending. He puts the truck in park and holds the headlights on her door until the porch light comes on and says nothing about it, and Vee sits there thinking *bacon cheeseburgers* at the window, and then rides home against him watching his hands at ten and two, steady as a table, "like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling." She's revising him in real time. She drove out in December to get a word out of him and got nothing and decided there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given — and now she's being shown, by a third party, that she's one entry in a long private ledger of people this man quietly keeps alive. Which is the best possible answer to December *and still isn't the answer.* The lock, the donuts, the headlights, the drawer, the bread timed to her waking. All unsigned.

Also, and this is the cold spot I can't get out of the warm bath: chapter 64 happened. I have been in his bed with Randi saying *I want her to know exactly what she does to me* while he says *she makes me happy*, and now I'm at a taproom where Sheri — who was looked at once like a harem applicant by a woman who never learned her name — says "You've got him happy. That's the whole book, far as I'm concerned," and she means it, and she's wrong, and she's the only honest witness in the county. The one person who cannot be managed is now in the same frame as Vee, likes her on sight, has a standing date at a dance hall in a week, and has opinions about the other one. Something is going to come across that floor and it won't be a two-step.

I'd text a friend: *she met the other woman and the other woman is a tiny gay cyclist who checked out her legs for eight seconds and then told her the one true thing about her boyfriend, and he BLUSHED, and I have never been happier to be wrong about a book.*
