Okay, I opened this expecting a doorbell and I got a bicycle. First paragraph, "They rode most Saturdays" — past habitual tense, a brand-new woman at his left elbow — and I went *no. No no no, she's getting dressed, she's putting on another woman's shoes, go back.* I actually checked how long the chapter was. Three pages. Fine. Fine.

And then he started talking about her and I forgave most of it. Because he can't *stop.* She asked him one question about a joint in a chair arm and he's still carrying it around weeks later — she ran her thumb down the grain, she pulled a math book off the shelf and *didn't put it back like it had burned her.* That's what he kept. Not the dress he built, not the night. The fact that she touched his work and wasn't frightened of his books. "He heard himself going and didn't stop." This man who fences off everything, who filed what she said at the bar under *his, not mine to hand over* — he just opened his mouth on a cold road and poured her out to a stranger-to-me on a small bike. And Sheri, God bless her, puts the exact right two words on it: *Listen to you.* And he doesn't know what there is to listen to. He genuinely does not know. That's the first time in this book I've been ahead of him about himself and it's the best feeling I've had since the mirror.

Sheri I like immediately and I'm suspicious of immediately. Bleached ponytail, blonde, remarks on women the way other people remark on weather, has been at the Rusty Nail — I think she's the blonde with the drink. The one who leaned in with *he's so good about getting a girl home safe* and lit the fuse. If that's her, then the woman who poisoned Vee's whole night is the same woman who then stayed and watched her glare at the floor and nurse one drink and *leave alone*, and reported it back like a scout. Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that. I laughed and then I didn't like it, and then I decided that's just her register and the book isn't winking — she's not being offered to me as sexy, she's being offered as a woman who says what she sees across a handlebar for two years and has never once been managed.

*You want me to not like her?* — said at the top of a climb, soft-pedaling, not as winded as would have suited either of them. That's a threat and an offer in one breath and she means both. And he says *she couldn't take your heat*, which is, I notice, not a no to the question underneath it.

What chilled me, in the good way: *She knows. She likes her.* / *And the redhead?* / *Not yet.* Flat, two syllables, no flinch. He's got the whole asymmetry in a drawer labeled *not yet* and he said it out loud on a road to a cyclist before he's said it once to Vee. And Sheri — who knows nothing, who has no stake, who is just running the arithmetic anybody could run — hands him the only prophecy in the chapter: *If that's not already complicated, it will be.* Then, because she's generous: *But I bet it's worth it.* And he doesn't answer. He never answers the ones that count.

But I'm going to say the other thing too. I have now gone: dress-hanging, dance lesson, bar, Randi's bedroom, Randi in his bed, and now a bike ride with a fourth woman — and I cannot remember the last time Vee and Pace were alone in a room together with nothing between them. I can feel that gap in my hands. She's supposed to be on his step tonight in the black dress with Randi's scarf at her throat and I have been promised that for two chapters now and the book keeps putting other women in the frame instead. That was *close* to costing me something. What saved it is that she was the only thing in his mouth the entire ride.
