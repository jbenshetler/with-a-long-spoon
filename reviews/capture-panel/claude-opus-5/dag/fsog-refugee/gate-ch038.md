REACTION

The drawer. Oh, the *drawer*.

I want to be careful here because I almost got had twice in one chapter and he saved it both times. First the mirror — her arms up in her hair, him folded around her from behind, and his eyes going down to the counter and *holding there a second.* I sat up. I actually braced, because I have read eight hundred books where that look is the first crack, where the man who seemed kind turns out to need the counter wiped and the girl smaller. And he looked back up and smiled and put his mouth back on her shoulder and said nothing, and she carried the little tail of worry out of the room with her, and I carried it too, which is exactly what the chapter wanted. Then the next Friday the scrunchies are gone off the counter and the bottom goes out of her morning *and mine* — and it's because he emptied the top drawer. The easy one. The one your hand goes to first. And crushed his blades and his comb and all his careful ordered ranks into the drawer below, pressed in too tight, built to hold half of it.

And no note. There's never a note. God, that's his whole grammar: he does the enormous thing in the dark and doesn't sign it. The mirror screwed into the sun porch wall with the screw heads still bright. The lining at the bodice and hips and not one word about why. Dozens of scrunchies so she'd never be without, no note, because the plenty *was* the note. He keeps telling her she gets to take up space and keeps doing it in a language she has to translate alone.

But. *But.* I can't have the drawer without the other thing in this chapter, and I noticed the other thing, and it put a cold hand on me while I was being warmed. She said it. Not to him — into her own chest, where she couldn't take it back. She loves him. And then she lay there in the dark and did the arithmetic *for* him: the door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the warm tomatoes, the coming back to her, a man doesn't do all that for a girl he's passing a season with, he *has* to feel it, he says it in everything he does and nothing he says — and she decided she could live inside the everything-he-does and not go hungry for the saying. That is a twenty-one-year-old talking herself out of needing the one sentence, and she's doing it beautifully, and she's doing it because he has never offered it, and *I know why he hasn't.* He chose her with another woman before she was anything. He can't say it. Not because he doesn't feel it — I think he does, I think he's drowning in it, that's what the drawer is — but because the one true sentence would drag the rest of the truth up behind it and he knows the proof doesn't close. So she's building a cathedral out of his silence and calling it faith, and he's letting her, and he'd pass a polygraph.

And then *Peter.* Again. Breathed into a sleeping man's ear, where he can't hear it and can't take it back off her. That's the second time she's done that and both times it wrecked me. She's got one piece of him nobody else uses and she keeps it in her mouth like a stone. And she still hasn't asked about the scar — she's "decided to keep" not asking, and she frames that as love, and it is, and it's also her editing again. She edits Cassie, she edits her mother, and now she's agreed in advance not to need what she isn't given. This girl has made not-asking into a virtue and the book knows it and it is so quietly brutal about it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he gave her the top drawer and crushed his own stuff into the bottom one and didn't tell her and I am not okay.* And then, about forty seconds later: *he still hasn't said he loves her and she's already decided she doesn't need him to, and I think that's the loudest thing in the book.*

The one place I rolled my eyes — and it's small but I felt it — is the paragraph about her clothes going looser. She eats like a field hand at his table now and she's *lost* weight, her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, bands riding higher. Come on. This book has been so patient and so honest about her body, the two places she's spent her life apologizing for, the shame stopping in front of the mirror — and then it hands her the free gift anyway. That's the first time I've felt the author's thumb on the scale to make her a little more reward-shaped. I don't need her thinner. The dress already did the work and it did it without taking an inch off her.

Also noting, because I always note it: Randi was not in this chapter at all. Not a text, not a name. That's the first clear air Vee and Pace have had to themselves since the bar, a week apart and then a whole weekend alone, and the chapter spent it on a drawer instead of a plot. I'm not mad about it. I needed it. But Randi is somewhere right now with a pastry she took one bite of and a thread of sweetness she left on another woman's lip, and the quieter this house gets the louder she gets by being absent.

He's accumulating, still. He didn't say anything about the counter *out loud.* He never does. The pile is getting tall.
