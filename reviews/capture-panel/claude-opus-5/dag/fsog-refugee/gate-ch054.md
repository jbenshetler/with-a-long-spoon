Oh, I have *so* much to say about this chapter and most of it is me being furious at how good it was.

First: she kissed her. *Vee* did. Four months of Randi leaning in and doing "the work" and getting a girl who holds still and is sweet about it, and Vee got her hand on the latch and couldn't walk out with only thank you, so she turned around and put both hands on that woman's face and *stayed*. And Randi's breath stopped. And her hands shut on the back of the coat — not on *her*, on the coat, like she didn't trust herself with anything closer. I read that twice and then I read the whole back half once more.

And then the thing that actually undid me is what Randi did with it: hand to her own mouth in an empty foyer, a thing she hasn't done since she was fifteen. One short idiotic laugh out loud in a hall with nobody in it, and *heard herself do it, and did not care.* A sentence trying to assemble itself with *means* in it and she wouldn't let it finish because she couldn't survive it finishing. She wanted twenty minutes and a warm mug and to *hold on to this.* That's the woman who has engineered two volumes of other people's wanting, standing in a hallway being happy like an ordinary person for about ninety seconds.

Ninety seconds. Then Brooke. God, Brooke. I need to say out loud how frightening that scene was, because nobody raised their voice and nobody was unkind and the warmth never moved once, not one degree, and it was a blade the whole way through. *You always say goodbye under the composites.* She runs a house of ninety and she knows to the inch what every room in it is worth, and she's been doing the arithmetic on Randi for months while being lovely in the front hall. And she started with *I like her, I want you to know that first* — which is how you set a girl down gently before you break her arm. And then *if she looked at me like that, I might be tempted myself,* and Randi said **"Don't,"** which is the single most naked syllable that woman has produced in two books, and Brooke rolled right over it with the same warm face. And then the permission, which is the cruelty: *play all you want, privately, or for the boys.* For the boys. You can have it as long as it's a performance for somebody else or it's invisible. The one thing you can't have is for it to **mean** something. And then, bright, curious, the *can I ask* face: whether she'd like to keep her position. And *Randi heard herself say that she did.* Heard herself. She wasn't even in the room when she gave the answer.

Then she put the mug back in the cupboard. She never poured anything into it. She put her own twenty minutes back in the cupboard and went up and shut her door.

I have spent fifty chapters watching Randi decide she'd never have to be brave, and the one afternoon something fell into her lap that she didn't arrange, a woman with a clipboard priced it for her inside of five minutes. I'm not going to pretend I know what that does to her. But I'll say this: the kiss and the threat happened in the same building within ten minutes of each other, and whatever Randi does next is going to be done by a woman who's just been told the exact cost.

Now. The coaching. I came into this chapter braced, because I wrote it down — the bill isn't coming as money, it's coming as comfort, at exactly the moment the girl has nowhere else to put her face — and here it was, on schedule, and it was *worse* than I expected because almost all of it was **true**. He isn't with another woman. His phone *is* in the other room. He *was* set up for a beautiful evening. He *did* go behind the wall because there was no answer that would keep her. I've read chapter 54; I watched that man's head come up off the fork at the sound of an engine on the highway and then sob into a bowl of boiled chicken. Randi read him dead right from across campus without a single piece of evidence Vee didn't hand her, and she did it in about four minutes, and she gave the girl back her floor.

And — this delighted me — she dismantled the blonde. Vee built a rival out of spite in a twin bed in Ohio, tan and model-built, and Sheri is five feet of two-tone perm who eats a double cheeseburger in nine bites and had a girlfriend named Paige who made bird prints. The one woman Vee can't see is the one who leaned across an amber tabletop and told Pace *from this seat, that feels pretty shitty.* Vee invented a threat and the real Sheri is the only person in the county arguing her case. I'd text a friend about that at 1 a.m. with no words, just a screenshot.

But. **"You don't do it with words. Words are the whole war."**

That's the knife and it's got a ribbon on it. Randi — the woman whose entire existence depends on a word never being said out loud — just told the girl standing in front of her that asking for words is the problem. Vee's one legitimate, unanswered, *unanswerable* question gets reclassified as "the fight," and the fix is to show up and settle it with her body, which is the fourth machine she's got for being relieved of having to know. Her orgasm drowned the real question on his bed in December. Now she's being sent across town to drown it on purpose, in heels Randi handed her.

The heels. The *heels.* "Just the thing for tonight," pressed into her hands, and Vee took them because *Randi always knew the shoes*, and she'll walk into that house wearing nothing but a coat and another woman's shoes. Randi will be in that room. She'll be the only thing on the girl.

And here's what keeps me honest about it: the book did not wink. It wrote **"a thing being decided for her and by her at once"** and put that sentence right in the middle of the floor where I'd trip over it. It made Vee say the ugly word herself, out loud, flushed to the ears — *it's such a slutty thing to do* — and then it let the shame light the wanting, which is the truest sentence about Vee's body in this whole book, that the mortification makes it sharper. And it gave her *she was going to do it, she'd known it since the coat.* She got there first and Randi only walked her past the part of her that would rather die. If I'd been given one wink — one *good girl*, one nudge at the reader — I'd have shut it. Instead I got Randi telling her to go sleep eight hours and shower because *no man opens that coat for a woman who needs a shower first*, and the raccoon line, and a wet laugh out of a girl who'd been crying for twenty-four hours. That's care. Mercenary, interested, load-bearing-for-Randi care, and also the real thing. It's always both. That's been the trick the whole way and I keep paying for it.

What hurt in a smaller way: Vee sitting there taking the Gstaad story *sideways.* Naming children on a chairlift on the first day. *The kind of dress you tell your daughter about.* A man who builds the whole future out loud inside an hour and isn't scared of one word in it. She's listening to the exact thing she asked for and got folded arms over, and the book doesn't even make her say so, it just has her look down. And Randi stopped at *we went up*, and Vee read it as discretion. The one thing Randi can't narrate is the night she felt nothing. *Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in.* She made the emptiest night of her life into a punchline and got the laugh she needed out of a girl who'd been crying.

And *"Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face"* — from the woman who's been inventing faceless men on a weekly schedule for two volumes, one week ahead of every time she needed one.

Where I actually am: I want that doorstep so badly I can taste it. Because he's not where she left him. She thinks she's walking into the house of a man who let her go and never texted, and he's a man who's been living on boiled chicken and listening to the highway. She's bringing a peace offering made of her body because a word is too expensive, and he's the one who needs to spend one, and the whole horror of it is that her plan is going to **work**, gorgeously, and work is exactly the problem. She's going to get the best night of her life and the question will go under again, and this time she won't even notice it happening because she'll be the one who put it there.

Also, nobody has explained the table in his bedroom and she's about to arrive at that house wearing a coat.
