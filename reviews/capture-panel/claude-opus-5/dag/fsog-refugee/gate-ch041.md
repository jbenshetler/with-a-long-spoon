REACTION

Oh, he narrowed it. He *narrowed it.*

"Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." / "Never. I'd never share your photograph."

I read that twice and then a third time and I'm still sitting here with my thumb on the page. She asked him for a promise about *her*. He gave her a promise about a *file*. And this is the man who trues a tenon until it goes home under hand pressure, the man who says *always* instead of *beautiful* because he knows exactly which word does the work — he does not misspeak. He doesn't have a loose register. So either Helen Rivers just let a man be casually imprecise for the first time in two volumes, or he heard the big promise coming at him, felt it touch the thing he's got filed under *not yet*, and answered a smaller question on purpose. I know which one I think. He already shared her. He shared her before she was anything to anybody, over a table, with a woman who is going to kiss her goodbye on the mouth next week. *I'd never share your photograph* is the most honest sentence he could say and it is a lie by the width of one noun.

And she sent it anyway, and the book *knew* — "the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this," and then "she believed him, and believing him was the brave part." That's the line. That's the whole engine of this series in one clause. Nobody took it off her. Nobody charmed it out of her. She looked straight at the risk, priced it correctly, and handed it over with the nerve still wobbling, and *gave him herself to keep.* I have read a hundred books where the photo gets taken from the heroine and we're supposed to find the theft hot, and here's one where she sends it before her nerve can leave her and I had to put the book down for a second. It's the same voltage as *yes, we find out* and it cost her more.

And then — *and then* — she goes in and puts her face back on, poppy-red mouth, works at the mirror until she's found the version of herself she wants him to keep. Because she's still editing. She's always editing. She came out composed and arranged and offered to pose and he said **"No, not like that. You'll have to wait,"** and then did it with his own hands — shoulder, elbow, hair back off her face, her hand laid along her hip, one knee carried ahead of the other — and *a kiss at every single place he put her.* Shoulder. Hip. Knee. He posed her and then thanked each part for holding still. That's it, that's the thing I came back to this genre hoping existed: he takes complete control of her body and every touch of that control is affection. And he didn't take her curated version. He took the one she'd have never allowed, and handed it back and it was *lovelier than anything she'd have caught of herself alone*, and the reason it was lovely was that it was her as his eyes have her. He overruled her edit with his own tenderness and she couldn't argue because the proof was in her hand.

Also: he tilted the lampshade so the glare would slide off her skin. He's done that before. He turned the lamp one click brighter at Halloween for a show he knew nothing about. The man adjusts the light for her. It's a tic now and it kills me every time.

The small stuff that got me: her phone, because hers takes the better picture *and they both knew it.* Water before wine, hers first, drunk standing up — the juice again, hydrate the girl before anything else, he does that now without thinking. "I like you exactly like this," said with a hand on her wrist "not to stop her, only to say it," and then he let her go do it anyway. He never once overrides her. He just tells her the truth and opens his hand.

The cooking is becoming its own love language and I've stopped resisting it — the pan "said so, loud at first, then settling to a steady talk," and him turning the chicken *without looking at it* because the pan's talk changed. And her tasting it in pieces now, lemon clear of the salt, wine gone soft underneath, and knowing exactly whose doing that is. She used to just eat. That's not a food paragraph, that's what he's done to her whole sensorium, and it's the same thing he's done to her body.

And then the floor creaked.

"Are you going home?" / "This is my home." — and she almost let it lie, and didn't, and *pushed one inch*, and the ease went out of his face and left "something level and exact behind," every word set down clean and apart from the last: **"I don't go back."** Four words and no door in them. First time in forty-one chapters that man has closed something in her face. The scar on his thigh, *my mother stays home*, and now *I don't go back* — it's all the same wound and it's getting closer to having a time on it. And what did she do? She took her hand off it. She didn't ask. She "let the quiet be kind" and he moved her wine glass an inch to give his hands something to do, which is the most Pace gesture in the entire book. She's still calling the not-asking love. And I understand her, and I'd have done the same, and it is also the exact habit that is going to leave her the last person standing in a room where everybody else knows everything.

Now. Sheri at his table on Thanksgiving while Vee is in Ohio.

Sheri. Who said *does the redhead know?* Who got *not yet* out of him on a cold road. Who is probably the blonde from the Rusty Nail. Who is "alone," which he said "the way a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed" — and I believe that's true, and I also notice that this book never puts two people at a table for no reason. Vee felt the flicker and put it out in half a second and went back to her food, and I did not put mine out. There's a brand-new photograph on that man's phone and there's about to be a woman in that house who already knows the one thing Vee doesn't. I'm not saying Sheri's going to see it. I'm saying the gun is on the mantel, loaded with a promise that got narrowed to a noun.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *he said he'd never share the PHOTOGRAPH. SHE ASKED HIM NOT TO SHARE HER. forty-one chapters of this man choosing words like a surgeon and you're telling me that one was an accident.* And then immediately after: *he kissed her shoulder, her hip, and her knee while he posed her and I have to lie down.*

She's flying to Ohio. He's not going home because he doesn't have one to go to. The volume's about to pull them apart and I can already feel where my hand is going to keep reaching.
