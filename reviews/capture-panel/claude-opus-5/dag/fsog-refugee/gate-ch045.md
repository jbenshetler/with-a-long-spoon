Oh, she titled it *Cropped.* Of course she did. The photo stops at the waist for her mother's purse and the whole of her went to Pace and then the whole of her went to Randi across a brunch table, and now here's Vee at a cafeteria table doing the same thing with her life out loud — handing out the version that fits the frame of whoever's holding it. And this is the first time I've watched her *know she's doing it.* "I told Dad the parts that fit." She says it like a logistics problem. She has a whole taxonomy now: what Dad can hold, what Mom will touch her collarbone over, what Cassie gets.

And she gave Cassie the dress. She actually gave her the dress — that he *made* it, on a machine on a sun porch, because she mentioned a magazine once. That's the biggest true thing Vee has handed Cassie in forty-five chapters and she handed it over as a joke about her father. "My dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it." That's a *great* line and it's also a woman shoving the real thing out fast under cover of comedy, same as the flannel speech last chapter, same as she's done every time something costs her. She gets it out at speed so nobody can look at her while it lands.

The question mark. She told her parents he was her boyfriend and she *heard herself put a question mark on it, out loud.* That's the whole no-I-love-you thing surfacing from a direction I didn't expect — not in his kitchen, not in the dark with him asleep, but at her mother's table where she had to produce a noun for a man who has never given her one. She needed a word and she didn't own it, and she asked her own parents to ratify it. That's twice now the word she needed wasn't hers: *boyfriend* at the Thanksgiving table and the other one, the one for Randi, that she still doesn't have. A whole novel of a girl reaching for nouns.

And then Cassie. God, Cassie. "So what is he. Not to your dad. To you." And when Vee has nothing: **"Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?"** That is the cleanest knife in this book and she delivers it without a bit of pressure behind it. Nobody else in this story asks a question they're willing to get an honest answer to — Randi asks questions the way a locksmith asks about a door. Cassie asks and then *lets go of the rope.* And Vee says *both,* which is the single most honest sentence she's said since "she handed me a lie and I picked it up," and then the chapter closes on the line I'm going to be carrying: **She was grateful. She was also, a little, not.**

That's the crack. That's the first time Vee has felt the cost of her own cropping instead of mistaking it for sovereignty. She *wanted* to be pushed. She wanted Cassie to make her say it, because saying it under duress wouldn't be the same as choosing to tell, and choosing to tell means admitting she's been choosing not to for months. And Cassie — who deduced a bra from a tomato burn, who knows more than she lets on and has decided her friend's dignity is worth more than her own curiosity — gave her the dignity, and the dignity is the thing starving her.

What I notice in the negative space: not one word about Randi. Not the Saturdays, not the Mercedes, not the shoes, not the kiss on the mouth on a sidewalk that Vee is the one leaning up into now. She told Cassie the man made her a dress and left out the woman who buys her shoes she can't pay for, and she wouldn't be able to tell you she left it out. The dress she's "not ashamed of, the opposite if anything." The other thing doesn't even make it to the list of things she can't say — it isn't on the page of the inventory at all.

Also: her father pricing the dress and concluding his daughter is in deeper than he pictured. He's wrong about the money and dead right about the depth, and neither he nor Vee can see that the actual bought-and-paid-for items in this arrangement — the cognac heels, the scarf, the cab, the card with nothing behind it — came from someone nobody at that table has ever heard of. The bill's still out there. He sniffed at the wrong garment.

Two sentences at a cafeteria table and I'm more rattled than by the whole pizza night. Text at 1 a.m.: *she said BOTH. she knows. she knows she's hiding and she's lonely in it and the one person she could tell is the one person she's furthest behind with.*
