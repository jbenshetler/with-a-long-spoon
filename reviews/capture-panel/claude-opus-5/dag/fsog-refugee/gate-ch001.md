REACTION

Okay so I spent the whole first page assuming this was Vee. The jacket told me a girl named Vee gets walked into this man's path, so I'm reading a woman come in the front door and lock the deadbolt behind her — *first thing, before anything else*, which, oh — and I'm settling in for the heroine, and then he says *there you are, Randi* and I actually sat up. Book one, chapter one, and the very first thing it does is put me in the room with the conspiracy before the conspiracy exists. That's the whole jacket promise — "the reader, ahead of her from the start" — cashed on page one. I love a book that trusts me like that.

And then the chapter itself. God. This is the thing I have been looking for, and I want to be careful saying it because I've been burned so many times: a man who asks. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." He asks about her back, her hands, her shoulders, and when she says *they're fine, Pace* with that exasperation he just smiles and says *just checking*. He crouches down so his face is level with her upside-down face and says *hi* like they're at a bus stop. He takes her shoes off in the middle of it — takes her foot in both hands and holds it a second — and that undid me more than any of the striking did. The kissing between. The "you're doing so well. You are. You are doing so well." His control is not a cage, it's a floor she can stand on, and the whole time the book keeps showing me him choosing not to take: hands going up her sides and turning back before they arrive, the hand off her the second she's slick because *greed now would spend the night early, and there was better ahead of her, and he meant her to have it*. He's hard the entire chapter and it never once becomes the point, and he literally sets it down at the end — *that's not what this night was for*. Do you know how rare that is. I have read forty books where a man's erection is a deadline.

The edging worked on me like it worked on her, which is embarrassing and also exactly what I pay for. The two *pleases* — the waiter *please* and then the real one — that's such a precise thing to build an entire scene toward. Not humiliation, not breaking her, just *her actual voice*. And it's given from inside her too, which is what saved it for me: the script for the *wanted* position, turning the heat up as proof of his desire, and the bench simply not being in that room. She can't pout her way out because there's nothing to pout at. She's never been wanted in a way that didn't run through the face. That's not cruelty, that's a door.

And then the mirror. *The face had just begged.* I have not read a post-scene interiority that good in years. She wasn't hurt. She wasn't degraded. She had the best moment of her life and it terrified her because it came from being seen, and she went in that bathroom and locked the door — which he clocks, *which was not a thing she had ever done before in his house* — and decided, quiet as a deadbolt going over, that he can have the face and he will never get her again. Never ever. That's the horror. Not the bench, the smile that "settled." She walked back out and curled into him and he sat there thinking what a beautiful evening, thinking tenderly about the apple slices, and I wanted to scream.

The water bottle cap under her thumbnail. *She did not seem to know she was doing it.* I went cold. And the flinch at "you're beautiful" — the one thing he says that isn't a question, and it's the only thing that lands wrong, because it's aimed at the face and she knows it. He's so exact about her body and so wrong about her in that one syllable.

Things I'm side-eyeing, because I'm not a total sucker: the apple. Sliced thin because she won't eat thick, cut sides up because he noticed she eats faster that way. He's *sorry* about what polices her eating, and he's also optimizing around it without ever saying a word to her about it. That's tenderness and it's also engineering, and this is a man who builds mechanisms, silent, well-oiled, his own design. "He felt the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go" — that's a craftsman admiring a finish. So the book has already told me what the jacket told me: the same attention that makes him wonderful is the attention that will make a plan out of a girl named Vee. And Daphne. He put a whole ruined thing behind that closed bathroom door and didn't explain it, and I want that.

The thing I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the friend who hands her over is the one who couldn't survive being seen*. Randi is about to go find a girl who can take what she can't. That's not a plot twist, that's a wound with a plan attached, and I'm gone. I'm completely gone.
