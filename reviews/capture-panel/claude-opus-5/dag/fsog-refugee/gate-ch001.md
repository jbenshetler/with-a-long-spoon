REACTION

Okay. I did not expect to open a book about Vee and spend the entire first chapter watching a man take a different woman apart on a bench he built himself, and then get her name in the last line like a hand closing on my wrist. *There you are, Randi.* I actually said something out loud. Because I'd been reading her as some unnamed girlfriend, a warm-up act, and the whole time she's one of the two people on the jacket who are going to do this — whatever *this* is — to Vee. And now I've watched what it looks like from inside, before I know there's a plan, and I already understand why she'd want to hand it to someone else.

The consent is *there*. It's actually there. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." How's your back, hands and shoulders, tell me — and she snaps *they're* fine*, Pace* and he just smiles at her upside-down face. I have read so many books where a man decides what she can take and the book calls that mastery. This one had him lifting her hands off him with two fingers under each wrist, no grip in it, and putting them back at her sides — and she then put them behind her back *herself*, and the book was careful to say it became an offering "without her deciding it." That's the distinction I've been hunting for years and almost never get. He's not extracting. He's building a room and waiting to see what she does in it.

And the withholding — God. I've read a hundred edging scenes and none of them have been about what this one is about. He's not teasing her to prove he can. He's taking away the script. She can't pout, she can't turn the heat up, "the maximum was below her, where she was," her hands worrying the grips looking for a handle that has always been there. That's the most erotic thing in the chapter and it isn't a body part. When the second *please* came and the book said *it came from below* — that landed in my chest, not just lower down.

But then. Then the bathroom, and the floor dropped out. Because he's sitting out there *happy*. He's thinking about the thin apple slices and how she finally let someone feed her, and he's so pleased with himself, and she's in the mirror deciding *not ever*. He can have the face. He gave her the best hour of her life and she's sealing the door with the deadbolt of it "quiet as a deadbolt going over" — same deadbolt she turns the second she walks into his house, which, now that I see it, she's been doing every single time. She locks herself in and she locks herself away and he thinks it's intimacy.

And the water bottle cap. The sharp inner edge under her thumbnail, pressing down into the nail bed where it would hurt. She doesn't know she's doing it. And *he takes the bottle when she sets it down.* He saw. He didn't name it. That's the moment I got scared of him a little — not because he's cruel, he's the least cruel man I've read in this genre in a year, but because he's so good at watching and so certain that watching is the same as knowing. He respects the locked door because of Daphne, whoever Daphne is, and his hard-won honorable restraint is *exactly* what lets her get away from him. The one man who wouldn't force the door is the one man she needed to force it. That's not a flaw in him. It's worse than a flaw. It's a fit.

What I'm suspicious of: he does a lot of grading. The counters she dislikes and never mentions, the furniture she "had not really heard" him say he built, the calculation "behind her face that policed her relationship to food." He's tender about all of it and he's also above all of it, and there were a couple of pages where I felt the book inviting me to find her a little small. I don't want that. I want her to get her own chapter — and the bathroom was so good that I think I'm going to get it.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the whole first chapter is the OTHER woman and it's the best consent scene I've read and she ends it deciding to never be real with him again and HE HAS NO IDEA and I think she's going to solve it by putting someone else on that bench.* Which, if that's where the Vee thing comes from — Randi needing a third person in the room so nothing is ever aimed at her again — then this book is doing something I've genuinely not read before. She said she was going to have to figure out what she was going to do about this. I know what she does. I know her name's on the jacket next to his. Give me the next chapter.
