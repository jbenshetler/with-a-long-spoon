REACTION

Okay so I sat down expecting Vee and I got — not Vee. I was maybe two pages in going *wait, black hair, blue eyes, sorority, this is the friend, this is Randi,* and then the book made me wait an entire chapter to confirm it and dropped the name on the last beat of the scene like a coin in a glass. "There you are, Randi." I actually said something out loud. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the book opens inside the conspiracy, not outside it, and the woman who's supposedly running the game is the one getting taken apart on a bench.*

And God, the bench scene. This is the thing I've been hunting for years and mostly not finding. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." Twice. And then he *keeps checking* — how's your back, hands and shoulders, and she gets snippy about it — "They're *fine*, Pace" — and he says "Just checking" and smiles at her upside-down face. That's it. That's the whole thing. The control does not need her smallness to exist; it makes room. He crouches down to her face level and says "Hi" in the middle of a spanking. The transition where he just holds her cheek for thirty seconds and the book *notices* it's longer than it's supposed to be. I was gone.

The apple. The apple undid me before anything else did. Sliced thin because she won't eat thick ones, cut sides up because she eats faster that way, and he never says a word about any of it. The attention is the seduction. Then she straddles him and circles a finger for him to turn the chair and he does, and *being directed by her pleased him* — dominance that can be handed the wheel for a minute without flinching. Yes. That's the man.

What I'm chewing on: the whole middle stretch lives in his head, and his head is an appraiser's. The long inventory of her body under the lamp — beautiful writing, genuinely, the oiled-wood light, the hairs the razor missed that only his hands find — but I felt the gap where she wasn't. And then the bathroom gave it all back to me and then some. Her interiority hit like a dropped plate. *The face had just begged.* The smile sitting on top of the face that begged. "He could have the face. The face was very good." And the deadbolt image — quiet as a key turning in a lock — closing back over the exact door she locked behind her when she walked in. That's a woman writing her own escape while still warm in his arms and I have not stopped thinking about it.

The bottle cap under her thumbnail. He sees it. He takes the bottle. He says nothing. I don't know yet whether that's the finest piece of care in the chapter or the first crack in him.

Because here's my suspicion, and it's the good kind: he is *so* pleased with himself. "The small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go." "The hunger had been in her all along. All he had done was find it." He is sitting there holding a woman who just decided to never be real with him again, and congratulating himself on how well the night went. He read her body perfectly for two hours and then missed her completely in the last five minutes. The flinch at *you're beautiful* — he feels it run through her against his chest and just... lets it go. He respects the locked door, which is enormous and which I loved and which comes with a whole history I want (Daphne — noted, filed, want it) — but respecting the door is also how he ends up never knowing what's behind it.

And I know from the back cover what these two are about to do to a twenty-one-year-old. So the tenderness is real and the ground under it is not, and the book seems to know that, which is the only reason I trust it.

I want the next chapter immediately. I also want to know whether Vee exists yet.
