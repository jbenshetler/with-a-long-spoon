REACTION

Okay so I spent this whole chapter thinking I was watching the heroine and then he says *"There you are, Randi"* and my stomach dropped out. I actually flipped back to the jacket to check. Randi is the *friend*. Randi is one of the two people with the plan. And I just spent twenty minutes being completely in love with how this man handled her.

And here's the thing — I *was* in love with it. This is the chapter I left the genre looking for. He asks. He gives her the word before he touches her and then tells her *tell me again if you forget, I won't be upset, tell me anytime*, which is the first time in years of reading this stuff that a safeword hasn't felt like a legal disclaimer the author inserted so we could all get on with it. He checks her back, her hands, her shoulders, and when she snaps *they're* fine*, Pace* he just says "just checking." He crouches down to her upside-down face and says "hi." He takes her shoes off in the *middle* of the scene — stops the spanking, buckle by buckle, holds her bare foot in both hands. That detail wrecked me a little. And the apple. The thin slices, cut sides up, because he *noticed* she eats more when they're cut sides up. That's the whole thesis of the man in one bowl of fruit.

The edging worked on me, I'll be honest. The thread of her stretching between his fingers and breaking. Taking his wet hand and laying it flat on the small of her back — her own heat returned to her in the wrong place — I made a noise. And "greed now would spend the night early, and there was better ahead of her, and he meant her to have it," and then at the end he's still hard and just *sets it down*: that's not what this night was for. A man whose control makes room for her instead of erasing her. That's literally the thing I wanted. It's on the page.

But the book is doing something meaner than turning me on. Because underneath all that care, the person he's being tender *to* is pressing the sharp edge of a water bottle cap into her nail bed while he thinks she's resting. She flinches when he says "you're beautiful." She locks a door she has never locked. And then the mirror section — *he could have the face, the one he found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again* — quiet as a deadbolt going over. God. She had the single most alive experience of her life and her response is to seal it in concrete and walk back out smiling. And he's sitting on the bed happy for her, thinking *she had finally let someone feed her*, and he is **so wrong**, and he's wrong because he's the one person decent enough to respect the closed door. His virtue is exactly what blinds him. That's a hell of a trick to pull in chapter one.

The Daphne thing. One paragraph, no explanation, *it had cost him more than he had known then that anything could cost*. I want that. I'm filing it.

What I'm suspicious of: the jacket says a game between these two, a secret choice made about a girl who doesn't know. So I now have to sit with the possibility that the hands that unbuckled her shoes with that much attention are going to be aimed, by arrangement, at a twenty-one-year-old who has no idea. And I think the book knows that's the knife. The dread isn't that he's cruel — it's that he isn't, and it might not matter.

Also: Randi has decided to never be seen again, and the next thing she's going to do with that is hand him somebody else. I think that's the plan. I think she's building a door out of Vee.

Would text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the heroine isn't even in chapter one and I'm already scared for her, and the man is the most careful lover I've read in five years, and I think that's the problem.*
