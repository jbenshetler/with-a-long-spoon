REACTION

Okay. I opened this expecting Vee — the jacket sold me Vee — and instead I got twenty-one pages of a man undressing the *other* woman on a bench he built for her, and I did not once want to skip ahead. That's the first thing I'd text somebody at 1 a.m.: *it's not even the heroine and I'm wrecked.*

The thing I have been hunting for years is in this chapter and I want to be careful about how much I say so. He asks. He doesn't ask in that limp way where the asking is a formality the book is embarrassed by — "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." And then he keeps *coming back around the bench to her face*, crouching down to be level with her, "Hi." "You with me?" "How's your back? Hands and shoulders?" and she goes *they're fine, Pace*, half exasperated, and he says *just checking*. I actually made a noise. That's the whole thing. That's the thing every other book fakes. He interrupts his own scene to unbuckle her heels and hold her bare foot in his palms for a second. He is hard the entire night and never once makes it her problem — *that's not what this night was for* — and I want to hand that line to about forty authors.

And the hands. God, the hands. The slow passes up her sides that turn back before they arrive, three times, four, until she says *you're doing that on purpose* with apple in her mouth and he says *doing what* and does it again — I was more turned on by that than by the entire bench. Bite, kiss, bite. Her forgetting to finish the slice. Her taking his hands and *putting* them where she wanted them, and him letting her, in no more of a hurry than before. He takes her hands out of his hair, twice, with two fingers under each wrist and no grip in it, and then she puts them behind her back herself and it becomes an offering without anyone naming it. That's control that makes room for her instead of erasing her. That's the exact thing.

But then — and this is why I'm not just happy, I'm *unsettled* in a way I'm going to be chewing on all night — the whole thing is told from inside his head, and inside his head it's an engineering project. He sliced the apple thin because she won't eat thick slices. He laid the slices cut-side up *because he noticed she eats them faster that way*. He left the water bottle out in advance. He noticed the bracing in her shoulders the first night and let it pass and didn't comment. That is the most attentive man I've read in this genre and it is also, if I tilt my head one degree, a man running a very long experiment on a woman who does not know she's in one. I felt the flicker. I let it pass, because everything he does with what he notices is *kind* — he's sorry about the thing behind her face that polices her eating, he doesn't interpret her feelings for her, he doesn't knock on the bathroom door. But the flicker happened, and I think the book wants it to.

And then the bathroom. The bathroom is the best thing in the chapter and it's the thing I'd actually call a friend about. Because the scene works — she begs, and the second *please* is real, it comes from under the voice she uses to ask a waiter for water, and he hears it land and says *there you are, Randi* and I got chills — and then she goes and locks a door in that house for the first time ever and stands at the mirror and *decides never to come out again.* "He could have the face. The face was very good." Quiet as a deadbolt going over. The size of a key turning in a lock. I am not fine.

Because he's sitting out there on the bed, happy, having read her completely wrong. "A girl who had been portioning herself... who tonight had finally let someone feed her." No, sweetheart. She was more alive than she's ever been and it *terrified* her and she's going underground, and you don't know, and you said *you're beautiful* and she flinched, and you saw the flinch and didn't understand it. That gap — him tender and satisfied, her sealing herself shut three feet away — is the cruelest and best thing this book has done so far, and it's page one.

The water bottle cap under the thumbnail. Pressed slow and steady into the nail bed where it would hurt. She doesn't know she's doing it, he doesn't read it, and I had to put my phone down. That detail told me more about her than the blue eyes did.

So now: this is the woman who is going to hand Vee to him. This is the "game between the two people now closest to her." And the jacket says it got as real for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew — and after this chapter I think I understand what that's going to cost Randi, and she doesn't yet, and she absolutely cannot be given a girl to be the architect about without it going somewhere awful. Also: who is Daphne. He built a door once and didn't respect it and it cost him more than he knew anything could cost. I want that. I want that badly.

If I have a worry it's that this man is close to too perfect at reading bodies, and that a book this good at the mechanics of care could eventually use them as a magic trick instead of a relationship. But I have zero interest in worrying about that tonight. I'm going to the next chapter.
