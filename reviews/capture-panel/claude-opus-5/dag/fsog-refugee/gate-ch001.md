REACTION

Okay. I did not expect to get thirty pages into a chapter, convinced I was watching Vee's first night, and then have him say *There you are, Randi* — and feel the floor go out. I actually sat back. Because the jacket told me Randi is the *friend*, the one who steers, the one who already knows there's a plan. And here she is before any of that, facedown and crying and begging in a voice she hasn't used since she was a child. The book put me inside the thing I was told to watch from above and then told me whose it was. I'd text someone about that at 1 a.m. just to make them read the last line of the section cold.

And God, the man. This is the thing I left the genre looking for and stopped expecting to find. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." He stops mid-scene to ask about her back, her hands, her shoulders, and when she says *they're fine, Pace* half-annoyed, he says *just checking* and goes back to work — he didn't make a production of it, he didn't break the spell, he just checked. He crouches down so his face is level with hers and says *hi*. He takes her shoes off in the middle of it because she's been in heels for hours and he noticed. None of it deflated a single degree of the heat. That's the whole argument I've been wanting a book to make for years: that the asking IS the dominance, that a man paying that much attention is more frightening and more exciting than a man who grabs. Every captive-bride book I threw across the room thought the attention was optional.

The kitchen before the bench killed me, honestly, more than the bench did at first. The apple sliced thin because she won't eat thick slices. Cut sides up because she eats faster when the cut sides show. *He was sorry about it.* He has clocked her mother and her sorority and the arithmetic running behind her face and he just quietly built a plate that gets past it. That's not seduction, that's feeding someone. And then she straddles him and he keeps his hands light at her hips and lets her run the kiss as long as she wants it — and the teasing up her sides, the hands turning back before they arrive, three times, until she takes them and puts them where she wants them herself. I was squirming. Clothed, fully clothed, and I was squirming.

The bench scene itself is as good as I've read. The slow build — he will not be hurried, and the not-hurrying is the weapon. The line about her bracing because every man before him was in a hurry, and him just letting the bracing pass without comment, no speech about it. The edging until she runs out of the apparatus of performance. *It was still her please. It was still being said in the voice she said please in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water.* That's the most precise sentence about a person I've read in this genre, and it's doing erotic work, not literary showing-off. He's not withholding to be cruel, he's withholding because he can hear that she isn't there yet, and I believed that distinction completely. When the real one came out of her I felt it in my chest.

But.

Two things are sitting in me like cold stones. First: the water bottle cap. She takes the sharp inner edge and presses it under her thumbnail, into the nail bed, where it will hurt — and he doesn't see it. He's holding her, he's stroking her hair, he's *proud*, and she's quietly hurting herself under the blanket. I read that twice. Second: he says *you're beautiful* and she flinches. He said the one true tender thing and it landed like a hand on a burn, because beautiful is what the face gets, and he'd just gone past the face. He doesn't know that's what he did wrong. He thinks it was a remarkably good evening.

And the bathroom. *Not tonight — and this arrived without words, quiet as a deadbolt going over — not ever.* She is going to put the face back on and keep it on forever, and the hum in her ribs is the size of a key turning in a lock, and I know from the jacket what she does with that key: she goes and finds a twenty-one-year-old girl and hands her to him. She can't survive being seen, so she'll bring him someone else to see. I understood the entire premise of this series in one paragraph and it made me feel slightly sick in the way I want to feel.

So am I suspicious of him? A little, and I want to be honest about it. There's a watchfulness in him that's one degree off from study — the noticing of how she eats, the inventory of what she's never said about his cheap countertops, the small warm pleasure at how far he took her. In another book that's a man building a file. In this one he respected a locked door he could hear crying through, because of someone named Daphne and something he did when he was young and thought love was permission. That one paragraph bought him a great deal of credit with me. I want to know what he did to Daphne. I want to know it soon.

What I'm left with: he thinks he found her hunger and answered it and will answer it again as often as she brings it. She has already decided never to bring it again. They are lying in the same bed having two completely different evenings, and I am the only one who knows. That's an unbearable place to end a first chapter. Give me the next one.
