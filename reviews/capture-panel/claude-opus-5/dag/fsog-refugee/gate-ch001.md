REACTION

Okay. Okay. I put the book down after "There you are, Randi" and just sat there for a second, because I'd been reading that whole scene assuming it was Vee — the blurb told me Vee, I came in loaded for Vee — and then the name lands and the floor goes out. That's the second thing that got me. The first thing is that I've been hunting for this exact man for about six years.

Because — *he checks*. He asks for her word. He says "tell me again if you forget, I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." He comes around to her face mid-scene to ask about her back, her hands, her shoulders, and when she snaps "they're *fine*, Pace," he doesn't punish her for the tone, he smiles and says "just checking" and kisses her forehead. And none of it deflates the heat. That's the thing I keep being told is impossible. Every book that wants to be hot tells me the asking would kill it, so it gives me a man who just *takes* and calls it intensity. This one made the asking part of the temperature. The thirty-second pause with his palm on her cheek, "longer than a spanking transition was supposed to be" — I felt that in my chest more than anywhere else, and then two paragraphs later I was squirming.

The apple. God, the apple. He sliced it thin because she'll eat thin slices, cut-sides up because she eats faster when the cut sides show. That's the whole book's thesis in a fruit bowl: this is a man who *watches*. And I loved it and it also made the hair on my arms stand up, because watching that closely is not neutral. The line where he clocks "the thing behind her face that policed her relationship to food" running its calculation — he sees an eating disorder, notes it, feeds her around it, and doesn't say a word. That's the closest I came to disliking him. Is that tenderness or is that management? I genuinely can't tell yet and I don't think the book wants me to.

And then the ending. The *ending*. She goes in the bathroom, locks the door — which she's never done — and the book flips into her head and it's not afterglow, it's a woman discovering she has an inside and deciding to brick it up. "He could have the face." I made a noise. Because from where he's sitting on that bed this was the best night of their relationship; he's out there feeling "the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he thought she would go," thinking about how he'll answer her hunger as often as she brings it to him — and she has just quietly decided to never bring it again. Two people in the same warm room having completely incompatible evenings. That's the cruelest thing I've read in ages and nobody in it did anything unkind.

The water bottle cap under her thumbnail. She doesn't know she's doing it. He doesn't comment. I went back and read that twice.

Also — Daphne. He closed a door once and shouldn't have, and it cost him more than he knew anything could cost. That's a whole other novel sitting in three sentences, and it's *why* he lets Randi's lock stay locked. The care has a scar under it. I want that story badly.

What I'm suspicious of: the blurb says this started as a game between him and Randi, a secret choice made about Vee. So the woman who just decided she will never be seen again is the one who's going to hand a twenty-one-year-old to this man. And Vee's going to get the version of him I just watched — the palm on the cheek, the word, the waiting — which means the best man I've read in years is going to be *aimed*. I think I'm about to fall for him exactly the way the jacket warned me I would and I'm annoyed at how willing I am.

The only thing sitting slightly wrong: the narration's confidence about her. "It was not the right *please* yet. It was still her *please*." He's grading the authenticity of her begging, and somewhere in there is a man who's decided he knows what's real in a woman better than she does. The book seems to know that. If it doesn't know that, I'll find out in about four chapters.
