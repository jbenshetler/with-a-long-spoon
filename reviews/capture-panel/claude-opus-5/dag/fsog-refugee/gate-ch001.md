REACTION

Okay so I read the back cover and then I read this and I had to flip back to the blurb twice, because for the first six pages I was certain I was reading Vee. The overnight bag, the sorority, the undressing — I was settling in, thinking *oh good, we're starting at full heat.* And then "There you are, Randi," and I actually said something out loud in my kitchen. She put the name at the end of the crying. Not as a gotcha exactly — more like the floor dropping two inches. Because the woman I just watched get taken apart on that bench is the one who's going to walk up to some twenty-one-year-old in a hallway and steer her, laughing, as if by accident, toward *this man*. And now I know why. Or I think I do, and the thinking-I-know is doing something horrible and delicious to me.

The sex itself: this is the thing I've been looking for and failing to find for about four years. He asks. He *asks* — "You okay?" "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset." And then he asks about her back, her hands, her shoulders, and she gets exasperated — "They're *fine*, Pace" — and the exasperation is what sold me, because that's a woman who's been asked enough times that it's become a small domestic annoyance rather than a ceremony. Nobody writes that. Everybody writes the one grand negotiation scene and then never mentions it again. And the control in him never once needed her to be smaller. The edging was brutal and it was brutal *for* her, there was a thing he was trying to get to and it wasn't his own orgasm — he's still hard at the end and he sets it down like a fact, *that's not what this night was for*, and reader, I felt that more than I've felt most actual sex scenes.

The apple. God, the apple. The thin slices, cut sides up, because she eats them faster that way. That's where I came closest to pulling back, honestly — there's a stretch early on where he is *reading* her so completely and so silently, cataloguing the thing behind her face that polices her eating, and I couldn't tell if I was being invited to find that loving or predatory. It reads as both and I don't think that's an accident, which is either the best thing about this book or the trap it's setting for me. I'm going to keep an eye on it. Same with the water bottle cap under her thumbnail — he sees it, he takes the bottle, he says nothing. He *saw that* and didn't name it. I noticed. I'm not sure he did, fully.

And then the mirror. That's the part I'll be thinking about tomorrow. "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." A woman who has been giving herself away all her life at zero cost because none of it reached past the surface, and then one man looks *past* it and the aliveness is ecstatic and terrifying in the same instant, and her response — quiet as a deadbolt going over — is *he can have the face, and I'm not coming out again.* That's not a plot beat, that's a knife. She was more alive on that bench than she's ever been and she's going to spend the rest of this series making absolutely sure that never happens again. Which means — and this is where I got the chill — the plan about Vee is a door she's building. She's going to hand him a girl who *can* stay out from behind her face, and tell herself it's a game.

And he thinks he had a remarkably good evening. He's sitting there quietly happy about her, thinking he'll answer her hunger as often as she brings it to him, and he has no idea she just left and isn't coming back. He respected the locked door — the Daphne thing, which I want all of — and the respecting cost him the one thing he needed to know. I don't think I've ever seen consent *done right* and have it be the mechanism of the tragedy. That's a genuinely new move on me.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: "chapter one isn't even about the heroine and I'm already sick about it. the man is GOOD. she's the dangerous one. and the bench gets a tabletop put back on it in the morning like nothing happened."
