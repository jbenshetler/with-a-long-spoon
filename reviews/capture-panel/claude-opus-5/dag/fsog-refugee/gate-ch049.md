REACTION

Three pages and I'm sitting here with my hand over my mouth.

*It was warm because she was warm.* That's the sentence. That's the one. She's been doing this for weeks — the shirt, the whole ritual of it, carrying him home in a flannel and rationing the smell like it's medicine — and the thing she finally works out at two in the morning is that the heat in it is her own, handed back to her. He's not in it. He's across town sleeping easy *as a man with nothing grinding in him.* That line is a knife and she doesn't even throw it at him. She just notices. Twenty-one years old, alone on a top bunk, doing the math on her own comfort and finding out it was a loop.

And the escalation of it, which is so quiet I almost missed what the chapter was doing to me. First the shirt over her own shirt — the public version, the deniable one, *it hid what it was.* Then she takes them both off and puts his back on bare, the private version, skin to cloth, the way she wears it when no one can see. That's her whole method in miniature: the edited one for the hall, the real one for the dark. And then it *doesn't work*, for the first time ever, and she takes it off too and lies there with nothing on her in a cold room and **stays in it.** She chose the cold. She picked the worse thing on purpose because at least the cold wasn't lying to her. That's the first honest thing she's done since the porch.

And the shirt left in a heap. Not folded, not back on the pillow. *down in a heap where a dropped thing lands.* After forty chapters of this woman treating every single object that came off that man like a relic — the scrunchies, the drawer, the dress with the seams squared. She let it fall and left it. That's the loudest thing she's ever done and there's nobody in the room to see it.

The phone, too. The empty field, thumb hovering, waiting for words that don't exist — *the ones she thought of were all small, and would be answered small and warm, and would leave her exactly here.* She knows. She's finally clocked the mechanism: that he will always give her the warm small answer and she will always end up back in this bed with the center hollow. She said her best sentence. She got *I love how good we are together.* I am still not over that, and I'm glad the book isn't either — I was genuinely afraid 47 was going to get smoothed over by good sex in 48 and then forgotten, and instead it's metastasized into this.

And the catalogue broke me a little. She goes through the shirt and names every part of him in it — the things he makes with his hands, the rooms he keeps warm, *the plate he set down once he'd built the hunger in her* — all of it true, all of it his, all of it real, **and none of it told her what she was to him.** That's the indictment of the whole beautiful wordless grammar I've been swooning over since the drawer. The drawer was gorgeous and the drawer is not an answer. He does the enormous thing in the dark and doesn't sign it, and I called that his whole grammar and loved him for it, and now I'm watching a girl lie naked in the cold because an unsigned life doesn't have a name you can say to your mother.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she took it off. she took the shirt off and lay there freezing and left it on the floor. and Cassie is RIGHT THERE breathing four feet below her and she won't wake her and that's the actual tragedy of this book.* Because that's the part that got me sideways — she's bare and awake all night and the one person who'd have sat up and said *streetlight, what's wrong* is asleep underneath her, and Vee has spent two volumes carefully making sure Cassie has no idea. She built this solitude herself, brick by brick, freely, every time she handed over the tomatoes instead of the truth. Outlier number two, in everything that comes after.

The thing I'm scared of now: she's going to go back Friday and he's going to open the door and lift her off the ground and she'll put all of this away again, and the pile she doesn't look at will get one layer thicker. And somewhere out there Randi is on a couch saying *God, I want her,* and the floor under all of it was poured without Vee, and this girl's crisis is that she can't find a *word* for him. She doesn't even know what she doesn't know yet. The window went grey. Nobody came.

Give me the next one. I'm not going anywhere.
