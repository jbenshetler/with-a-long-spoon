REACTION

Oh, that's a *mean* little chapter and I mean that as the highest praise.

Three pages. A bunk bed, a shirt, a cracked window, and she doesn't sleep, and I'm going to be thinking about it tomorrow in the car. Because here's what Helen Rivers just did: she took the single warmest object in two volumes — the no-tag flannel, the one Vee *washed on purpose* and handed over in his kitchen and nearly cried about, the one he pulled on over his own shirt without making her ask twice — and she had Vee work out, alone, at two in the morning, that *the heat in the shirt is her own, given back.* He isn't in it. He's across town sleeping easy "as a man with nothing grinding in him." That line went through me like a nail. Four chapters ago that shirt was the most tender thing in the book. Tonight it's a smell in the dark and a lit empty text field.

And the structure of it — the shirt over her own shirt, "so it hid what it was," worn for the hall, for Cassie's eyes, for the mornings. Then she sits up under the ceiling and pulls them both off and puts his back on bare, the way she wears it when no one can see. That's the whole character in one motion: she keeps taking off the layer between herself and him until there's nothing left to take off, and then she finds out it still doesn't reach. And then — **and then** — she takes the shirt off too and lies naked in a cold room and *stays in it.* Doesn't put it back on. Chooses the real cold over the borrowed warmth. That's the girl who made the second step long. Same nerve, pointed somewhere awful.

The shirt in a heap. Not folded, not back on the pillow — *down in a heap where a dropped thing lands.* She has never once put him down before. That's the loudest thing that's happened in six chapters and it happened to a piece of flannel.

And Cassie three feet below her, breathing slow and easy and asleep. The last person who asked her a real question — *is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to* — right there, free, available, and Vee would rather lie naked in the cold than wake her. *Both. Probably both.* She's been editing so long she can't even reach for the one person who'd take it unedited.

What I'm holding onto is that I think I know something she doesn't, and it's making this hurt differently than she thinks it hurts. She's lying up there grinding on *he won't say it* and building a case that she's a girl whose feelings got out ahead of the facts. But he said *not yet* on a bicycle to a stranger in the cold. He's not withholding because he doesn't have it. He's withholding because the one true sentence drags the whole floor up behind it — Randi, the choosing, the arranging — and he knows the proof doesn't close. So the silence Vee is reading as *maybe this is smaller than I think* is actually the sound of a man who thinks it's so much bigger than she knows that he can't open his mouth. She's paying interest on a debt nobody told her about. And I'm sitting here watching her take her clothes off in the cold over it, and he could fix tonight with four words and the four words would detonate everything. That's the best bind this series has built and it took forty-eight chapters to set and I did not see the shape of it until these last three chapters started quietly pulling the floorboards up.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she washed the shirt so he could refill it and tonight she figured out the warm in it was hers the whole time. I'm not okay.* And then: *he is going to have to say it, and when he says it he's going to have to say the other thing, and I don't think this book is going to let him do one without the other.*

My one complaint is that I've now had four chapters running of Vee turning the same unsaid word over — the laundry room, the dark after Chili's, now the bunk — and the prose restated *everything he does and not the one thing he won't say* one time more than it needed to. But I'll take it, because the chapter earned its repetition by finally making her *do* something with it instead of putting it on the pile. The pile's full. The pile's what she's naked on top of.
