WHO'S WHO

**Pace.** The man. Math PhD, nearly done, seems young for it. Lives west of town off 614, past where the road goes to gravel — a small cheap-countered house he keeps too warm, Steely Dan always going low, and the furniture *he built* is the only good thing in the room. Quartersawn oak cabinet. A walnut spanking bench with a hinged top that disguises it as a table. Drives a white pickup. Wears soft washed work shirts and boots he's kept rather than bought. Courtly in an old-fashioned way — *may I call you*, *may I choose*. Asks. Checks. Stops. Gives the pencil back. Also: notices everything, catalogs everything, and acts on the catalog. Somewhere behind him is **Daphne** — he went in through a door she'd built and it cost him more than he knew anything could cost. One paragraph, chapter one. Loaded gun.

**Randi.** Twenty-one-ish, sorority, Standards board, single room, black hair, *startlingly* blue eyes she has built her entire life around being looked at for. Eats thin apple slices and nothing thick. Mother lives on egg whites. Pace's girlfriend, four-ish weeks in. On the bench she begged in a voice that wasn't the face's voice, cried without knowing she was crying, and then locked his bathroom door — first time ever — and decided at the mirror: *he can have the face, the one he found is not coming out again.* Not ever. Since then she has been executing that decision by pointing him at another girl.

**Vee / Vivienne Thorne.** The heroine the jacket sold me, finally here around chapter 3's last paragraph. Junior, econ, good at applied math, likes the structure under the numbers. Tall, red hair going copper in sun, freckles, built lavishly and dressed to cover it — cardigan over the better shirt, on purpose, most mornings. Her mother's voice lives in her head and says *floozies*. Was a synchronized swimmer at sixteen; the suit still fits and gives her nowhere to hide. Reads rooms instantly and smooths every rough beat in them, for everyone, always. Starving — for being chosen, for being waited on, for permission to want things.

**Cassie.** Vee's roommate/best friend. Ball cap, shorts, yesterday's shirt, four words a day, boxy and doesn't care. Won't go past rib depth in a pool. The only person in this book performing for nobody. Loves Vee plainly and quietly — kept the *tossers* line low, for Vee, instead of pitching it at the bleachers.

**Brooke** — chapter president, warm voice, never stands up, runs a four-minute interrogation entirely out of smiles. **Sloane, Kinsey** — house furniture. **Meg** — Vee's floormate. **Christine** — the woman at the little restaurant who was *glad* it was him walking in.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Chapter one is Pace and Randi on the bench he built for her, and it took her further than either of them expected. Aftercare, blanket, water bottle, *you're beautiful*, and she flinched — then locked herself in the bathroom and sealed over. He heard her cry through the door and did not knock, because of Daphne. He came away thinking he'd fed a starving girl. He is wrong and doesn't know it.

Next morning she walks into the sorority house in last night's clothes and gets smiled at until she bleeds. Then she goes upstairs and lies down in her shoes and doesn't cry.

Then: sex at his house, and mid-orgasm he asks *would you like to taste another woman* — decided beforehand, asked past her guard on purpose. Her body said yes so hard it took her. Then, later, clothed and cooled and gathered back into herself, he asked her *again*, properly. She moved it — "only in a threesome, with you" — making herself the generous girlfriend instead of a woman who wants a woman. He let the wrapper stand.

Then the walk on the quad. *I point. You pick.* A whole afternoon of women graded against Randi's ruler, and then Vee comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown back laughing and Randi's stride drops out from under her. Bare *yes*. Pace says *go meet her.* Randi engineers a shoulder collision so clean that Vee apologizes for it, gets a name, a class, a schedule. Comes back skipping. *Do you think she'll like the bench?*

Wednesday: Randi takes the seat next to Vee in stats — "*There* you are," which is Pace's bench line, used on the girl. Coffee at Chi Latte after. Cassie declines and says *Goodbye, Vee*, the whole word. Vee floats home unable to recall a single sentence Randi said.

Then Pace crosses a coffee shop to Vee's table with a calculus problem in front of her — Randi sitting right there, holding a cup she keeps not drinking from — and helps her without solving it for her. Euler's, the little cross in the margin, *you already know every piece of this*, the pencil back in her hand. *May I call you.* He forgets to buy his coffee, which Randi points out to Vee as evidence.

Froyo that afternoon. She overfills the cup and her mother's voice doesn't come. Says the embarrassing true thing; he lets it stand and gives her back *your tart's holding up*.

That night Randi comes over hot, straddles him on the couch with one glass of wine he never drinks from, and pays him in kisses for a report on Vee. *Did she eat all of it?* He keeps back the one true line Vee said on the walk — that was hers. *She's fun. You'll like her.*

Pool with Cassie: bleacher catcalls, then Vee underwater in the green where nobody wants anything, grieving a little.

Saturday dinner, the first real date. He comes to get her, showered and shaved and in his own good worn things. A tiny place off a block she'd never turned down. *May I choose?* — and he takes the handoff as a duty, weighs it, asks the one question, orders in soft words she can't repeat. She eats without watching herself eat, into a hollow much older than lunch. Loses the thread and lets it go. Brass cages on the tea glasses. He takes her hand on the street and it feels less like something beginning than something resolving. Under a tree she says her rule, he lets it go without one second of pressure, starts walking her on — and she stops him, pulls him back by the hand, and kisses him. Then he only gives back what she brought and waits, so she has to choose it twice.

Home: she tells Cassie everything, spins until she nearly takes out the closet door. *Nobody did anything to me. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.* Cassie's one safety check — *how much did he give you to drink* — comes back clean. Hiking is next. Real shoes. Off six-fourteen.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

**Pace** — I want him and I'm scared of my own wanting. Everything I've hunted this genre for is in him: the word given back and re-given, *tell me anytime*, the interrupted scene to unbuckle her heels, hard all night and never once making it her problem, the locked door respected, control handed over as service and not as trophy. Ten chapters and he has not once taken a thing that wasn't put in his hand. And he is also running a very long quiet operation — the apple cut-side-up, the water bottle set out in advance, the question asked deliberately while she was coming. He has a real line and it's in a strange place: he'll trade Vee's *body* across the couch as an aphrodisiac but he won't hand over the one vulnerable true thing she said. That's a conscience about the part that costs him nothing. Not nothing. Not enough. I'm watching it.

**Randi** — the villain I feel sorriest for. She doesn't get to be seen, so she's procuring. She's tender and she's a closer — *the rule's for boys you're not sure about, you're not unsure* — and I can't find the seam and neither will Vee. The cup she holds and doesn't drink from. The thumbnail under the bottle cap. She's in worse trouble than anyone in this book and she's the one causing it.

**Vee** — I love her and I'm braced. I keep waiting for the book to make her a thing that gets acted upon, and so far it keeps refusing: the kiss was *hers*, the yes was hers, the choosing was made the erotic thing. But she has no idea there was an invitation, and her proudest sentence — *nobody did anything to me* — is factually true and is also the trap. Oh honey.

**Cassie** — the only clean vision in the book and she's being managed out of the frame in real time, partly by Vee's own reflex to smooth things. I'd read a whole book about her. I am afraid for her.

**Brooke** — nobody I need to like, but I'd watch her run a room any day.

Owed: Vee is owed the truth and no one intends to pay it. Randi owes Pace a self she's decided he'll never get again. Pace owes Daphne something he already paid.
