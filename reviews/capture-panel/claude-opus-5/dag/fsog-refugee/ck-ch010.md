WHO'S WHO

Pace — the mathematician. Mid-to-late twenties, finishing a PhD, lives out west of town off 614 past where the road goes to gravel, drives a white pickup, keeps his house too warm, plays Steely Dan low in the front rooms. Builds things with his hands — the quartersawn oak cabinet, the table that isn't a table. Wears soft washed shirts that tell you nothing, good boots he's kept rather than bought. He asks. *May I call you. May I choose. Tell me your word.* He gives the pencil back at the exact line where she can finish it herself. There's a woman named Daphne somewhere behind him — one paragraph, no detail — whose door he once went through uninvited, and it cost him more than he knew anything could cost, and that's why he will now sit outside a locked bathroom and not knock. He is the most careful lover I've read in five years and he is running a game.

Randi — black hair, startlingly blue eyes, the flat oval face with the permanent small surprise. Twenty-one, sorority, sits on Standards, has a single room because of it. Pace's girlfriend, four or five weeks in. Eats thin apple slices and not thick ones; her mother lives on egg whites and tofu and told her so with a knife in it. On the bench in chapter one she begged in a voice that came from under the face, cried without knowing she was doing it, and then locked his bathroom door — a thing she'd never done — looked in the mirror, and decided: *he could have the face, the one he found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again.* Quiet as a deadbolt going over. She is the one with the plan, and the plan is a way out of being the one who was seen.

Vee / Vivienne Thorne — my actual heroine, finally. Junior, econ, good at applied math, too early to class, the right pen. Tall, built lavish, red hair that goes copper in wind, cinnamon freckles, and a one-piece from when she was sixteen that fits her now and gives her nowhere to hide. Loved swimming before she had a body to be a problem. Wears the cardigan over the better shirt on purpose and lets the room not know it was a choice. Her mother's voice lives in her throat and says *floozies.* Gives herself away — a whole semester up till two keeping a business major alive as a healer. Nobody has asked her anything in a long time.

Cassie — Vee's roommate and the only honest instrument in this book. Ball cap, shorts, yesterday's shirt, navy swimsuit older than the friendship, feet that don't reach the floor from the desk chair. Says four words and they're the right four. Declined Chi Latte without a sulk. Won't wade past her own ribs. Didn't shout the catcall down into the bleachers — kept it for Vee.

Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey — the house. Brooke is chapter president and doesn't need to stand up to stop you walking. Four minutes of smiling that did more damage than the bench did.

Meg — the girl Vee was walking with when the shoulder-check happened. Nothing yet.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Randi came to Pace's house, he'd built her a bench, he spanked her and edged her until the voice came out from under the face and she begged for real, and then he gave her what she asked for and held her while she cried and said *there you are, Randi.* She went into the bathroom, locked it, and decided never again. He has no idea. He's sitting there thinking she finally let someone feed her.

Then in bed a few days later he asked her, into the undefended part, whether she'd like to taste another woman, and her body came before she could make it mean anything safer. He asked again later, cooled off, gathered — and she moved the wanting into a shape she could live in: *only in a threesome. With you.* Handed her own appetite to him as a girlfriend's gift. He saw exactly what she'd done and found it beautiful and let it stand.

Then the pointing game on the quad. *I point. You pick.* She rejected the mirrors of herself, the matched pairs, the one who ran the room — and then a redheaded girl came out of the dining hall with her head thrown all the way back laughing, and Randi's stride dropped out from under her and she said *yes* with nothing around it. Pace said *go meet her.* Randi engineered thirty feet of geometry into a shoulder-check so clean that Vee apologized to her. Came back across the lawn doing happy footwork she didn't decide to do. *Do you think she'll like the bench?*

Since: Randi has installed herself in Vee's stats row, taken her to Chi Latte, called her *love you, girl*, said *see you later* like a scheduled fact. Pace crossed a coffee shop to Vee's table, helped her with an integral in a way that left the solving hers, asked what was *drawing* her to econ, and asked *may I* for her number. Texted inside twenty minutes. Froyo, where she overfilled the cup and her mother's voice didn't come. Then Randi, that same night, on his lap, drinking the one glass of wine he poured for her and saying *Tell* into his ear and going tight in the thighs at the answer. Then Saturday dinner at Christine's little place — he chose for her, she ate like she hadn't in years, lost the thread of conversation and let it go — then the hand, then the tree, then her rule said out loud, then him letting the kiss go entirely, then her feet not moving and the hand going taut between them, and she came and got it herself. Twice. Grinning inside his mouth.

Home to Cassie at midnight. Hiking next, out past the gravel, to a house she has never seen.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace — I cannot get my footing and I've stopped trying. Every single move he makes is the move I left a whole shelf looking for: the safeword given before a hand was on her and *tell me again if you forget, I won't be upset.* Taking her shoes off mid-scene, buckle by buckle, holding her bare foot in both hands. Still hard at the end and just setting it down — *that's not what this night was for.* The pencil. The *may.* Leaving the last inch on the ground. And all of it is aimed, by prior arrangement, at a girl who was pointed at across a lawn. He kept Vee's one embarrassing true line back from Randi — *it was hers* — and that's either his conscience or the start of the thing that destroys everybody. He has a line about Vee's interior while serving her exterior as an aphrodisiac. I don't trust him and I want to be in every room he's in.

Randi — I feel sorriest for the villain, which is a hell of a place to be put. She was more alive on that bench than she's ever been anywhere and she's sealing it in concrete, and the wanting she has for Vee is enormous and real and growing and she's dressing it as a present for her boyfriend because the alternative is unsurvivable. The water bottle cap pressed into her nail bed. The flinch at *you're beautiful.* She's steering Vee with closer's lines — *the rule's for boys you're not sure about, you're not unsure* — and she means the warmth too, which is worse. She owes Vee something she doesn't know she's borrowing.

Vee — I want to put a hand on her arm. She is not a blank and that's what's saving this book for me: she reads rooms, clocks herself looking a beat too long at the two half-dressed girls, knows she's the most of what the room is selling and the one with the most fabric on. Her desire is hers. She came home and said *nobody did anything to me, I did it on purpose.* She's owed the truth by both of them.

Cassie — the only uncomplicated love in the book. She is owed being listened to and she isn't going to get it.

Randi and Pace together — I still believe they adore each other, and that's the merciless part.

WHAT I'M CARRYING

*There you are, Randi.* The deadbolt in the bathroom. The apple sliced thin with the cut sides up because he'd noticed. His wet hand laid flat on the small of her back — her own heat returned to her in the wrong place. The thread of her stretching between his fingers and breaking. *He had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else.* The wine glass he poured for one. *Did she eat all of it?* *Do you think she'll like the bench?* The cup Vee overfilled while nobody said *that's enough now.* *Your tart's holding up.* The hand going taut between them under the tree and him turning back — not hopeful, attending. *May I choose.* Brooke not standing up. *Goodbye, Vee.* The whole word. The green under the surface where nobody up there could see her and nobody down here wanted anything, and *she had loved this before she had a body to be a problem.* Cassie keeping the comeback for Vee instead of pitching it at the bleachers. Cassie's feet not reaching the floor. *That's the whole address.*

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

The house. I want to see her walk into that house, and I want to see what she does with a long table in the middle of a bedroom that she has no reason to look at twice. I'm frightened of that chapter and I'm going.

Whether Pace ever figures out that the woman who came back out of his bathroom was the face. That's my real tension and it's a love-story tension — he's the one man careful enough to respect the closed door and that's exactly what's blinding him. And what Daphne was. One paragraph is not going to be the last of it.

Whether Randi ever stops pretending her mouth on a woman would be a gift to her boyfriend. The book is withholding that scene superbly.

What Cassie saw on those stairs. Whether anyone listens to her before she's walked out of the frame — declining the coffee, going back to her page, *borrow good ones.*

And the thing I actually want: for Vee's yes to go on being hers all the way down. The jacket says every one was freely given and that was the problem. I signed for that. I want to find out whether this book can hand me the heat and the asking and the real consent, and still make the cost land — without ever once winking.
