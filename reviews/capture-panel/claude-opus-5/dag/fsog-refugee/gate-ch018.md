REACTION

Oh, I needed this. After that fitting — after standing on the box with her for a whole hour of held breath — I needed exactly this: Cassie on the bed with her knees up and the highlighter put *down*, which is Cassie's whole love language, and Vee not able to stay in one place in the room. She sets the bag down, picks it up, puts it on the chair, turns around. That's a girl with a body full of something she can't hold.

And I was so relieved Cassie is still here. My notes say I was braced for her to get edged out of frame, and she does not get edged out — she gets the whole story, she gets it *first*, and Randi doesn't appear anywhere in this chapter, which I clocked hard. Vee took the smoothie-table version of her life to Randi and she took *this* to Cassie. That's the sorting instinct still working even if Vee couldn't tell you she has one.

The "are you sure he's not gay" bit killed me. Genuinely laughed. It's the exact thing a best friend says, and it's also the book being smart, because it lets Vee say out loud what she'd never say straight: he has had her half-naked twice and picked up a tape measure. And then her answer — the brisk clinical pat on her own ass, grave as a witness, *definitely not gay* — and then the actual bottom card, which she has been sitting on the whole conversation: *I* was grinding on *him*. Falling backward onto the bed laughing. That's the sound of a girl who found out she has a power she was never issued and is still ringing from it. I would text a friend at 1 a.m. about "he's a bodybuilder who makes dresses" alone.

But here's what got me and it's quiet. She doesn't tell Cassie about the panties. The whole engine of that afternoon — the soaked satin, the eyes screwed shut, the count of three breaths, opening them to find him smiling up at her with his chin tipped back because he never once looked down — none of it makes it into the room. She gives Cassie "and then the hem," and "he was on his knees and looking up," and then she stops and lets the geometry say itself, and Cassie says nothing, which the book tells us is the right thing. And then: *like I was supposed to be there.* That's the whole afternoon compressed to five words with the shame surgically removed. She kept the true thing. Same as Pace kept her true line back from Randi across the couch. This book keeps showing me people holding one thing back and it keeps meaning something different each time — his was a conscience, hers is that it's too big to hand over yet, even to Cassie. Maybe *especially* to Cassie, who'd understand it.

And the beat that actually got my eyes hot, same as it got Cassie's: the heat. Vee pivots into it before Cassie can ask — *and the house was warm* — like she has to get it out. He turned the thermostat up hours before, as soon as he knew she was coming, so she wouldn't be cold standing there with her bra off. She noticed it walking in and filed it under the small mysteries of him and understood it only later, standing on the box. *Oh, Vee,* Cassie says, and her face goes soft, and I made the same face. That is the thing I read this genre for and almost never get: the care that happened *before*, offstage, at a cost to him, that she was never supposed to find out about. Same species as standing in the rain with his back to the truck. He keeps paying in advance.

And the shirt. She's been sleeping in it all week, wore it to breakfast Tuesday, turned it inside out looking for a tag the first night and found nothing and *gave up on the riddle*. A week with the answer in her hands. That's the whole book's shape in miniature and I don't think it's an accident — she's holding the evidence, she went looking once, she stopped looking. Which is exactly what she's going to do with Randi. The jacket promised me I'd be ahead of her and I am and it is starting to ache a little.

I'm still not scared of Pace and I keep waiting to be. Eighteen chapters and he hasn't taken one thing that wasn't put in his hand. But I haven't seen Randi since the smoothie shop and the last time I saw her she was on top of him saying *tell me,* and he said *her stomach is soft,* and he did not say how he knew. That bill is out there. So is the fact that Randi didn't get called about the dress.

Also, Cassie clocking it — *you keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any* — is the book's own tension acknowledged out loud, and I'm fine with it, because the fitting *was* the sex. But I want the dress finished and I want him to call and I want to be in a room where somebody finally says the true thing.
