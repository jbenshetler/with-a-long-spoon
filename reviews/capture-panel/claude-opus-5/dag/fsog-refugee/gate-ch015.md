REACTION

Third time I've heard about the shirt. Cassie got it, and now Meg and Kayla get it, and I was there for the actual thing — I was in the cab with the rain on the roof and her on her knees on that bench seat with her eyes locked on his, which is one of the best scenes I've read in years, and now I'm getting it in dining-hall paraphrase with "Stop" and "You kept his shirt" in the margins. I felt the skip. I nearly put it down at *you keep getting to it and going soft and skipping the middle*, because honey, so does the book, we all know what she's skipping and it's the only part I wanted.

And then. Two things bought the chapter back and I'm annoyed at how cheaply I was bought.

One: **twelve**. She didn't count. She already knew. *Once. One time, the whole time.* I have been sitting here with my own private tally — four chapters past the froyo, one dinner, one trail, no room — and the book reached out and said *I know you're counting, so is she, it's twelve days and once.* That's the book telling me the ache I've been carrying is the intended ache. And then Vee says the thing out loud, the actual thesis, in her own mouth for the first time: *he asks me first. The small things. And he waits for the real answer, and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it. It sounds like nothing. I can hear it sounding like nothing.* And she's climbing the walls over a man asking what she wants for dinner. That's *me*. That's the whole reason I left the mafia shelf and came back for this. Fifteen chapters in and the heroine has articulated why being asked is the hottest thing in the book, and she knows she can't make it sound like anything to anybody else. Nobody at that table gets it. Meg says *everybody's different at the start, sometimes they even are* — which is the kindest, most ordinary, most quietly chilling line in the chapter, because Meg is measuring him against Danny and the scale is wrong by an order of magnitude in a direction Meg cannot imagine.

Two: **the text**. And this is where I got cold in a way the girls got giddy. *Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock.* That's the least information the most meticulous man in this book has ever handed anyone. He laid the apple slices cut-side-up. He set the water bottle out beforehand. He asked *may I choose* over a menu. He asked *may I call you.* This man has never once given less than he could — and now he sends fourteen words with no *for what* in them, to a girl he knows will read it a thousand times, and I don't believe for one second that's absentmindedness. It's the title. It's a polite invitation, and the politeness is that it's empty; he's making her walk through the door on nothing but wanting to. Which is, technically, consent theater of the highest order — nothing is being concealed except everything. And the walnut table with the hinged top is sitting in the middle of that bedroom right now.

Kayla, God love her, says *he wants you for the afternoon, bring a change of clothes*, and she's right about everything except scale.

Texted at 1 a.m.: *she's doing her hair on Saturday and I know what's in that room and she doesn't and I feel sick and I cannot wait.*

Also noted, filed, and not letting go of: the russet silk dress she set down on a rock and he didn't pick up. He said *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and let it lie there and I have never trusted a man less while wanting him more. That dress is coming. And the induction dinner with her mother in the room — the woman whose voice says *floozies* — is going to be in the same book as whatever happens Saturday.

Last thing: the house-invite landed at a table with Meg and Kayla on it and no Randi. Randi asked her for a full report in a lecture hall two chapters ago and got one. She is going to hear about Saturday, and she'll hear it from Vee, gladly, and I want to watch her face while she does.
