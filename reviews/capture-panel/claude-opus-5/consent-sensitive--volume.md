TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — it's the one I've been complaining doesn't exist. A conspiracy romance where the reader is in on it from page one and the book is *not* confused about what that makes it. The jacket says "every yes was freely given, that was the problem," and it means it: every single yes in there is real, hers, hot as hell, and every one of them is standing on a foundation two people poured in secret before she knew there was a plan. And the author keeps her hand on the instruments even in the warm stretches — there's a line where the girl edits her best friend out of a story and the book says flat out that she believes it's a kindness *because believing it lets her keep what she wants*, and there's a lecture on Simpson's paradox running on a blackboard behind her head while she sits happily between the two people running her, same numbers, nobody touched one of them, answer reverses when you put the halves together. Chapter one is a rug-pull I won't spoil. Chapter twenty-seven is where I stopped bracing: he hands over a two-year relationship he had starting at fifteen with a nineteen-year-old and calls it luck, in the flattest voice in the book, and the twenty-one-year-old listening does the arithmetic, feels the ugly word form, and holds it behind her teeth because it isn't hers to say — a naive book gives him that as texture, this one gives it as the origin of his whole method. The fitting scene (dressed man, undressed girl, hour of being looked at) is the single best thing in it and would have been the exact page I quit on in worse hands. Now the bad: the man is a walking Swiss Army knife — math PhD at twenty-two, a patent, powerlifting, cabinetmaking, couture tailoring, cooking, *teaches country dance* — and there are stretches where the book is plainly enjoying him more than it's examining him. The shame-voice-in-her-mother's-register beat gets run maybe four times past where it needed running. The clothes-and-food cataloguing goes long. And fair warning: the best friend with no angle gets quietly starved out over the back half, which is on purpose and will still make you want to throw the book across the room. Also it ends with the bomb still unexploded, so know going in that you're signing up for volume two. Read it. Text me when you get to the kitchen.
