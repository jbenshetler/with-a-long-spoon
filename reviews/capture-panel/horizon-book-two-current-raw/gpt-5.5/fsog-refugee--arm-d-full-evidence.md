Only-if, but it is a strong only-if.

**F3 — Does Book Two Earn The Delay?**

The current material earns the delay better than the jacket alone. Much better. Not because it excuses the delay, but because it makes the delay morally legible.

The scenes that decide it:

`Missed a Spot` earns Vee’s agency. She is not being dragged into appetite; she is discovering command, play, pleasure, and her own boldness.

`A Clean Plate`, `The Reach`, and `Gesso` make her conflict about tasting herself and wanting Randi feel interior and hers, not just engineered from outside.

`On Her Floor`, `Boyfriend`, and `Coming Due` are the big ones. Those make the Randi want undeniable, frightening, self-deceiving, and emotionally costly. I believe Vee’s confusion. I believe her denial. I believe the wanting is real.

`Grace` is dangerous in the best and worst way. Pace’s tenderness there is so real that I stay vulnerable to him, which means the betrayal still has teeth.

`In Her Place` is the scene that keeps me on probation. It confirms Pace and Randi are not just passively letting things happen. They are using Vee’s absence, Vee’s symbols, Vee’s green sheets. That scene makes me angry in the correct direction, but it also raises the debt enormously.

`Vee on the Bench` is the other deciding scene. It gives Vee a transformation that feels profound and chosen in the moment. But if the book uses that transformation to launder the hidden frame, I will turn on it hard.

**F4 — Book Three Reveal / Fallout**

The planned late reveal can answer the accountability problem, but only because of the no-road-back ending.

If Vee discovered the truth and then stayed, or forgave them because she has become freer and hungrier, I would be furious. That would retroactively poison everything. But the planned ending — recognition, reconstruction, shame, anger, no repair conversation, no romantic reunion, Cassie debrief — tells me the book is not asking me to clap for the manipulation.

Is the delay too long? It is almost too long. Late Book Three is a very long time to live inside compromised consent. The only reason I accept it is that the ending promises not catharsis but consequence.

Book Two has to keep letting the wrongness leak through. Not melodramatically. Not with neon signs. But enough that I never feel the book is winking with Pace and Randi against Vee.

I need Vee’s accurate perceptions to matter: the scrunchies, the found hair, the odd gaps, the things she almost knows and then talks herself out of. I need the narrative to respect those flinches.

And Vee’s desire for Randi must remain genuinely hers. If it starts feeling like Randi has simply trained her into it, I am gone.

Also, Pace needs to remain tender without being exonerated by tenderness. That is the needle. His care cannot become a moral alibi. Randi’s love cannot become a moral alibi either.
