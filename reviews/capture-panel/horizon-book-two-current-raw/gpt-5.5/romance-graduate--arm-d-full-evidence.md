F1 — **Commercial Verdict**

Yes, I keep reading through Book Three.

Not happily-trusting, not relaxed, but absolutely yes. Book Two has me by the throat now because the charge is no longer only “when will Vee find out Pace and Randi arranged her?” It’s also “what has Vee become while not knowing?” I’m furious in advance, but I’m buying. The no-road-back promise matters a lot. If I believed this was headed to a cute triad healing circle, I’d be gone.

F2 — **Jacket Contract**

The revised jacket is fairer than I expected. “Randi and Pace chose her together,” “does not yet know how much… has been arranged,” and “shadowed by the game still moving around her” tells me Book Two is escalation-without-reckoning.

Would I still feel tense at the curtain? Yes. But not misled by the jacket. The book is openly selling the knife under the table. I may still be yelling, but I knew there was a knife.

F3 — **Earned Delay**

The current Book Two earns the delay much better than the jacket alone.

The scenes that decide it for me:

**“Missed a Spot”** proves Vee’s joy is not fake. Her pleasure princess logic is ridiculous and erotic and psychologically real. She is not a puppet on strings in her own experience.

**“Covering,” “A Clean Plate,” “The Reach,” “On Her Floor,” “Boyfriend,” and “Coming Due”** make the Randi want grow from inside Vee, not just from Randi pushing buttons. That matters. I need the wanting to belong to Vee or the whole structure turns ugly in the wrong way.

**“Among Friends”** gives me the public boyfriend word and Cassie’s conditional blessing. That buys Pace time with me. Not forgiveness, but time.

**“Hangover”** and **“Another Round”** make Pace’s loneliness and his life with Sheri emotionally costly enough that I still believe in him as a person, not just a manipulator with good pancakes.

**“In Her Place”** is the big danger scene. It is hot, yes, but it also proves Pace and Randi are actively inside the con. That scene cannot exist unless the eventual bill is massive.

**“Vee on the Bench”** earns a lot because it is Vee’s transformation at full force: shame, appetite, self-revelation, surrender, power. But if the added act-three revision lets her name wanting Randi while still not knowing the shared arrangement, then the book has to be very careful. That’s delicious. It is also where my trust starts making little warning noises.

F4 — **Reveal/Fallout**

The intended Book Three ending answers the accountability problem for me, narrowly, because there is no road back.

Vee recognizing Randi through the kiss, reconstructing the whole year, freeing herself, refusing both of them, leaving without giving them a repair conversation — yes. That is the consequence I’ve been waiting for. The Cassie debrief also matters because Cassie is the person outside the spell, and Vee choosing her as the final witness feels right.

Is the delay still long? Yes. Late Book Three is a long damn time to carry this debt. But if the crash-out is truly permanent and not secretly foreplay for reunion, I’ll accept the long fuse.

F5 — **Remaining Book Two Must**

Book Two has to keep letting Vee’s unease stay alive.

I need more anomalies like the scrunchies, the hair, the drawer, the little delayed recognitions — not clues in a mystery way, but reality pressing through the fantasy. Vee can misread them, but the book cannot smooth them over for her.

Cassie has to keep existing as moral gravity. Not scolding, not solving it, just seeing Vee get further away.

Randi needs private cost. “Inversion” and the social-price beats are necessary. If she’s only gleeful mastermind and never lonely, afraid, or paying for what she’s doing, I stop believing the book knows her.

Pace needs moments where his care and his complicity sit in the same room without one cancelling the other. I do not want villain Pace. I also do not want “poor wounded man, look how tender” used as legal defense.

And Vee must not be made stupid. Innocent, hungry, avoidant, self-protective, yes. Stupid, no.

F6 — **Early/Mid Book Three Must**

Before the reveal, Book Three has to keep the dread moving. I need the secret to become more structurally unstable, not just more erotic.

I need Vee’s agency to sharpen. Let her ask for things. Let her author things. Let her become more herself in ways Pace and Randi did not fully script. That way, when she leaves, it is not just escape from harm. It is self-possession.

I also need signs that Pace and Randi know the moral temperature has changed. If they are still congratulating themselves on how beautifully Vee is blooming while withholding the core truth, I need the prose to know that is damning.

F7 — **Concrete DNF Line**

I DNF if the reveal is delayed past the point where Vee has explicitly asked a question that Pace or Randi answers with a direct lie and the book treats that as sexy suspense.

I can tolerate omission for a while. I can tolerate self-deception. I can tolerate dramatic irony if the erotic and emotional work is this good. But if she reaches for the truth and they knowingly shut her hand around a falsehood, and then I’m asked to stay swoony with them for another hundred pages, I’m out.

F8 — **Final Condition**

I’ll follow this all the way to the crash if Vee gets the truth, keeps her anger, and leaves them with nothing; if the book tries to turn the betrayal into the price of her awakening, I’m gone.
