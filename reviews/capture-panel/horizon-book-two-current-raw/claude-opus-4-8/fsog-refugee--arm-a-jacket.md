A1 — The jacket is honest in a way I did not expect, and I want to give it credit for exactly that. It doesn't smooth him back into the perfect careful man. It says the words out loud: "Randi and Pace chose her together." "She does not yet know how much of that wanting has been arranged." "shadowed by the game still moving around her." "the shape closing around her." That is the folder I've been holding, printed on the back cover. So it prepares me fairly for *escalation with the con still live.*

But you asked the real question, and here's the real answer: this jacket prepares me for the trap staying sprung through Book Two. It does *not* promise the bill comes due in Book Two. "Not every door has been opened" is a Book *Three* sentence dressed up as a Book Two sentence. And I clocked "This spring" — this is a season, not a reckoning. So no, I wouldn't feel *misled* if Book Two ends unresolved, because the jacket is telling me plainly it's a middle. What I'd feel is *taxed.* I said Volume One earned exactly one more volume of benefit of the doubt to see the other half come due. This jacket is quietly asking me to extend that to a volume *three.* I can see it doing it. That's not the same as being lied to — but it is the moment I start counting.

A2 — Yes, I buy it. Same reason as before, unchanged: to collect the debt, not because I trust him. But my terms have hardened, and the jacket is why.

What I'm expecting it to pay *this* volume: I'm no longer willing to let the emotional reckoning defer entirely. Not the full blowup — I'll grant that's Book Three's — but I want one beat that *lands and stays landed.* Someone with standing names the arrangement to a face that can't deflect, and it isn't muffled by love inside a paragraph. Volume One rang every alarm softly and then smothered it. If Book Two does the identical move for another whole book — Sheri gets close, Cassie asks the right question, canary goes back to her plate — then the muffling stops being the subject and becomes the *method,* and that's the author withholding, not Pace.

What I'll let it defer: the full exposure, Vee standing in complete knowing, the play detonating. Those can be Book Three. Also my literal list — the scar's "not tonight," the panties, whether Vee ever spends MIRH. I'll wait on the props. I will not wait on someone paying a real cost for the concealment. Randi's the one this jacket tells me is going to hurt, and I'll take the down payment in her.

A3 — Half and half, and the seam between the two halves is exactly where my worry lives.

The wound language is there and it's good: "shadowed," "the shape closing around her," "the closer she gets, the less she can see." That's a coming bill, and it's addressed to *me,* over Vee's head, the way this book has always worked. I believe the dread.

But the sentence right in the middle of the copy — "The lunches are dinners now. The jokes have teeth." — is being sold to me as heat, as *flavor.* And Randi is the person I ache for most, the one bleeding on her own knife. So when the jacket takes Randi's want and frames it as spice for Vee's "spring of her own appetite," I get a cold feeling that the book might do to Randi's grief what Randi did to "the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — flatten the truest, most costly thing into something consumable. If Book Two spends Randi as *seasoning* on Vee's awakening rather than treating her arrangement as the wound it is, that's the wink arriving on Randi's face instead of Pace's. So: it reads as coming-bill for the plot, and it reads as flavor for Randi, and the second one scares me.

A4 — This one: **"Everything she gives is hers."**

That's the load-bearing sentence, and it's the exact promise Volume One actually kept — the "Don't" and the hand gone palm-up, the desire rendered from inside her, the heroine who held the question on the mat and got out into the cold. If that stays true through a volume where her appetite "runs in more directions than one," where a second person she wants is one of the two who arranged her — if her wanting is still *hers,* rendered from inside, and not something the game hands her and calls her idea — then the book is still doing the harder, colder thing. The whole difference between this and what I left *Fifty Shades* over lives in whether that sentence survives contact with Randi.

A5 — Two, and they're close cousins.

First: if the jacket had promised the reckoning *this* volume and I already suspected it wouldn't deliver — but it didn't overpromise, so that's not the trap here. The actual not-buy move on *this* page would've been the wink: any sentence that framed the arrangement as lucky, as devotion, as "chosen" being romantic — "the two who love her most conspired to give her everything." That would've been disregard sold as a gift, and I'd have been gone. This jacket doesn't do it. "arranged," "the shape closing," "she does not yet know" — it stays cold and on Vee's side. Good.

So the move that *would* stop me is the one it flirts with: if the copy had made Randi's want purely Vee's playground — if there were no shadow on the Randi thread at all, only teeth-jokes and dinners and heat — I'd read that as the book deciding Randi is a device, and I'd pass, because the one person I trust the book to *hurt honestly* would've been turned into flavor. It half-does this. It's the reason I'm buying with a hand on the exit instead of both feet in.
