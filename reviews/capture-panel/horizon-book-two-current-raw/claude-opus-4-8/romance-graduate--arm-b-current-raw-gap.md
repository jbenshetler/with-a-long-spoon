B1 — Yes. Grudgingly, completely yes, and I want to be honest about why, because the "warm ending" is exactly the thing I told you in Arm A I'd feel *managed* by — and here it is, and it still earns the next book. It earns it because the drafted prose did the one thing the jacket wouldn't promise me: it put the fuse in. "My Pleasure" and "In Her Place" aren't mood-of-menace, they're mechanism. I have *seen* the arrangement operate from inside — Randi under him saying *I want her to know exactly what she does to me*, Pace laundering a whole afternoon off his own body with his mouth so Vee walks into a clean house. That's not "how much of that wanting has been arranged" as atmosphere. That's the machine, running, with its gears showing. So when Book Two ends with Vee naming the want and still not knowing, I'm not being sold deferral-as-flavor anymore. I'm being sold a bomb with a visible timer, and I will absolutely pay to watch it go off. The "yet" I staked everything on in Arm A — it's being honored. She's moving toward the knowing. The staircase is real and it climbs.

B2 — Productive hunger, still — but it's standing right at the line, and I can tell you the exact material that keeps it on the right side: the counter-voice. As long as the book keeps letting me see what Vee can't — Cassie's *is this what you want*, the drab little relief-stone when the closing spared her, the *what is wrong with me* into the dark — my anger is aimed *with* the book, at the trap, not *at* the book for building one. The instant it stops giving me that, the hunger curdles into distrust. What would cross me over: if Book Three's discovery scene turns out to be as withheld as the arrangement itself — if I get another three hundred pages of dread with no daylight. The concealment is bearable *because I can feel it costing her*. Distrust starts the moment the cost goes decorative.

B3 — They show enough. This is the answer I didn't expect to be able to give, and it's the whole reason my trust survived the packet. "On Her Floor" is the tell — Vee alone, hand caught stealing, telling the dark *I have a boyfriend* like a rail on a stair. "Boyfriend" — the whole confession scene — is a woman handing over every piece of the truth *except the one that's actually true*, and the book knows it, narrates the knowing: *it wasn't the doing that made you a cheat, it was the keeping.* That's not the lie made comfortable. That's the lie's exact architecture laid open for me while Vee stands inside it blind. And the Hangover chapter — Pace's one-friend confession, Sheri's whole tragedy — that's the book quietly showing me the loneliness *engine* under a man who arranges people. It's building the why. My one worry: the sex scenes are so good, so total, that there's a real risk the heat *becomes* the argument that the deception was fine — the Arm A curdle-line. "Vee on the Bench" is a masterpiece of surrender and I noticed I was almost persuaded by it, which is the danger.

B4 — Most keeps my trust: "In Her Place." It's the bravest thing in the packet. It could have been a titillating three-way tease and instead it's Randi *sobbing*, undone, punishing herself into the thing she wants, and Pace saying *she's perfect — for your first woman* with Vee's name in the room and a clock he thinks is real ticking over Randi's shoulder. It refuses to make the arrangement sexy-without-cost. Everyone in it is bleeding a little. That's the jacket promise Arm A said the copy had sanded off — and the *prose* put it back. Most threatens it: the Grain vignettes, especially "The Restoration" and "The Found Hair." I understand the design — Vee learning to distrust her own accurate readings, filing the hair under Sheri. But there's a version of that thread that tips into the reader watching an idiot miss the obvious, and idiot-plot is its own DNF. Right now they're calibrated as *trained* blindness, not stupid blindness. Keep them there.

B5 — They have to keep the pressure touching the outside world and keep the cost visible without spending the reveal. Specifically: the concealment has to keep *costing Randi* on the page — "Inversion," the Standards docket, Randi alone with the bill — because if only Vee pays and Randi just wins, the book has agreed with the arrangement and I'm out. Cassie has to stay a live wire, not a decoration; "Cassie — May," the untellable coin, the friendship going quiet from both sides — that's my proxy for the world being real. And at least one crack in the glass, the way Arm A demanded: the hair, the scrunchie form, *something* that says the machine leaves marks even where Vee won't look. What they must NOT do: manufacture a fresh external melodrama (Cassie's-dad-fraud energy) as a substitute for the internal reckoning. The reckoning is the spring. Don't swap it for plot.

B6 — Concrete DNF line entering Book Three: if the discovery gets deferred a *second* full volume. Book Two honored the "yet" by moving her toward knowing; Book Three has to move the knowing into the room, or at minimum crack it — Vee catching one thing she can't file away, one reading she can't make herself distrust. If Book Three is another three hundred pages of appetite-and-blindness with the truth still sealed at the last page, then "yet" was a con after all and I'll know I was farmed. Second line, faster: if the book ever *blesses* the arrangement — if the heat is offered as proof the deception was fine, if Randi stops bleeding and just purrs, if Pace's loneliness becomes an excuse the narrative endorses rather than a wound it shows. Either one and I'm gone, and I won't feel bad about it.

B7 — Both, and I'm not going to pretend it's clean. I'm invested — I've read this much, I want the plate to break, I've spent real attention on Vee and I want to see her come up whole out of the wreck the way she came up whole off that bench. That's trap. But it's not *only* trap, and that's the part that matters: the prose is genuinely written, the interiority is real, the sex is both hot and load-bearing, and the book has repeatedly chosen the harder, costlier version of every scene when a lazier one was right there. That's earned trust, not just sunk cost. So I'm buying because I believe this writer knows exactly what they built and intends to detonate it honestly — and if Book Three proves me wrong on that, the investment won't save it. I'll drop it at the page where I feel the con instead of the wound.
