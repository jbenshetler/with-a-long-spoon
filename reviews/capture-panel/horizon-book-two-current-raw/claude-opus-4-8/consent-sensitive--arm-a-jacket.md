A1 — The jacket is honest about the shape, and that's the only thing that keeps it from being a trap. "She does not yet know how much of that wanting has been arranged." "Not every door has been opened." "The shape closing around her." That's not coy — that's the book naming its own mechanism to my face, the way Volume One did. So as *preparation*, yes: it tells me this is escalation, that the spring is the honeymoon of the cage, and that the arrangement stays hidden and moving under the pleasure. I'm not going to be able to say I wasn't warned.

But here's the sharp part, and I want to be precise because you're asking the exact right question: *fair preparation for escalation-without-reckoning is not the same as permission to end without reckoning.* The jacket prepares me for Book Two to *withhold* the detonation. It does not license Book Two to be *another whole volume of withholding with no gun fired at all.* If A Warm Reception ends with the arrangement still fully hidden, Vee still not knowing, nothing crossed from inside to outside — then the jacket didn't mislead me about Book Two, it revealed the series is a stall. Two volumes of "the shape closing around her" and zero of the shape being *seen* by the person inside it isn't a slow burn, it's a pattern of a book that only knows one move. So: fairly prepared, yes. Still furious if it ends unresolved — also yes. Those aren't in tension. The jacket buys the deferral; it doesn't buy it forever.

A2 — I buy it. Same terms as last time, thumb still on eject, but I buy it — and the honesty of *this* jacket actually firms up the purchase rather than softening it, because it didn't try to sell me the arrangement as romance. It called it "arranged." Good. That's the seam showing, which is the thing I've trusted this book to keep doing.

What I expect it to pay: at least one gun. I don't need the whole reckoning in Book Two — I need *consequence to cross the membrane.* Somebody has to come to *know* something they didn't. Ideally Vee touches the edge of the arrangement herself — not the full picture, but a fact that won't fit the story she's been handed, and the book lets her sit in that dissonance with her own interiority intact instead of soothing it. Randi's the likeliest fuse: the jacket puts her want in the open now ("the want Vee can no longer keep safely unnamed"), and Randi is the one who can't perform the true thing. If her real feeling collides with her role as co-arranger *on the page, with Vee near it,* that's the membrane breaking. That's what I want paid.

What I'll let it defer: the *full* exposure, Pace's total accounting, the MIRH file, the photo, the public reckoning. Those can wait for Book Three. I'll let the apparatus stay mostly intact. I will *not* let another volume pass where the only thing that happens is more exquisite private weather and nobody outside Vee's own skull learns a thing.

A3 — This is where the jacket is doing its most important work, and mostly winning. "The want Vee can no longer keep safely unnamed" — that word *safely* is a wound, not flavor. "Everything she gives is hers. Not every door has been opened." — that back half is a bill; it's telling me her agency is real *and* partial, that she's spending freely inside a room whose walls she can't see. "The closer she gets, the less she can see" — that's live. That's a coming detonation, and it's framed as menace, not as spice.

Where it wobbles toward flavor: "The jokes have teeth. The lunches are dinners now." That's the register of a sexy-complication love-triangle blurb, and if I only had that line I'd be nervous the book was going to play the threesome as the *reward* and the arrangement as seasoning. But it's immediately disciplined by "how much of that wanting has been arranged." So on balance: live wound, with a couple of sentences that flirt with flavor. The structural sentences are load-bearing and they hold. If those sentences were cut and only the "teeth"/"dinners" register remained, my answer would flip to flavor and I'd be much colder.

A4 — "She does not yet know how much of that wanting has been arranged."

That's the whole contract in one line. It confirms the arrangement is *ongoing and hidden* — not resolved, not confessed, not softened into "they chose her out of love." It keeps the operator's hand in the frame during the pleasure. As long as the book stands next to that sentence, it still knows what it's depicting, and I'm still in hands I can read. The moment the *book itself* forgets that sentence — starts treating the arrangement as sweet, as the two of them having "chosen" her in a way I'm meant to find romantic rather than chilling — the contract's void.

A5 — The move that loses me instantly: if the jacket (or the book) reframed the arrangement as the *gift* — "the two people who love her most planned her happiness," the arrangement recast as devotion I'm meant to be warmed by rather than watch with dread. This jacket pointedly *doesn't* do that, which is why I'm buying. But adjacent to it, the thing that would make me not buy: any sentence promising the spring as pure arrival — "she has never been more free," "everything she ever wanted," with no shadow-clause behind it. Volume One's entire integrity was that the liberation line *always came from a handler's mouth with the seam showing,* never from a clean narrator. If the jacket had let the narrator itself declare Vee free and happy and full, unqualified, I'd read that as the author finally believing the con — and I'd close the tab and not open Book Two. The "shadowed by the game," the "shape closing" — those clauses are the price of my money. Take them out, sell me an unshadowed spring, and I'm gone.
