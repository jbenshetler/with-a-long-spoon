C1 — Yes, it changes it — and in the direction I needed. My whole Arm B anxiety was that the reckoning was on credit and I couldn't tell if the author *meant* to pay. This packet tells me she does. Points 4, 5, and 6 are the exact answers to my DNF line: no road back, no compressed forgiveness, Vee gives them nothing, she keeps the no. That's not the "the two who love you most conspired to give you everything" framing I said I'd walk out on — it's the inverse. So structurally, yes, the trilogy *can* hold the reveal until late Book Three, because the thing being held isn't a warm payoff, it's a detonation. I can wait for a bomb I've been shown the wiring of.

But — and this matters — "can hold it" is not "has held it." The packet is intent, not prose. I believed the *plan* in Arm A too. What I don't have yet is the drafted proof that Book Two doesn't spend Vee's uninformed body for three hundred pages of comfort and then hand the whole bill to a single late chapter. The plan de-risks the destination. It does nothing for the middle, which is where I actually live while reading.

C2 — It mostly answers it, and answers it better than I expected. The thing I was afraid of was Pace and Randi *getting away with it* — the con paying off and being called love. This ending refuses that: no resolution for them, the door "quietly and permanently shut," Vee walking out looking at the objects instead of the people. That last image is the one that convinces me most, honestly — a woman who won't even give her betrayers the dignity of her eyes. That's not a woman being operated. That's ownership.

Where it's still short: "getting away with it" has two meanings and this only closes one. They don't get *Vee* — good. But do they get *punished*? The packet says everybody gets the truth and nobody gets resolution, which means Pace and Randi mostly get... quietly left. Randi betrayed her best friend across an entire year; the plan gives her loss-of-Vee but no scene where she has to *bleed* on the page for it the way "In Her Place" let her. I said in B5 that Randi has to stay a wound, not a garnish. The ending as sketched risks letting her off with an offscreen ache. I can live with "no melodramatic comeuppance" — that's more honest than a punishment scene. What I can't fully tell yet is whether the friendship-betrayal gets weighted as heavily as the lover-betrayal, and for me it's actually the worse crime. The delay isn't too long *if* the reveal reorganizes the friendship too, not just the romance.

C3 — Yes. This is the clearest yes in the whole packet. Retrospective contamination — the reveal "reorganizing the year behind her," Vee reconstructing every scene I loved and finding the strings — is *tolerable and even the point* precisely because the ending is terminal and ascendant rather than reparative. If the reveal recontaminated the year and then she *forgave into a reconciliation*, the contamination would be a cruelty the book performed on me and then asked me to swallow. Contamination followed by ascension is different: it means the beauty was real *and* the theft was real, both true at once, and she gets to carry both out the door. That doubleness is the exact thing I read this genre hoping to find and almost never do. So yes — I'll accept that the gorgeous Vee/Pace scenes get poisoned in retrospect, *because* she doesn't stay in the poison. The terminal ending is what earns the right to have made me love the lie.

C4 — Book Two is where I'm still most exposed, and the packet doesn't fix it — it just raises the stakes on it. Three things, and they're the B3/B5 notes with the ending now bearing down on them:

Give Vee's counter-voice teeth about the *concealment*, not just her body. If her only unease across Book Two is "why do I taste like this, why does he like it" and never a splinter about what she saw, then the reveal has nobody to land on — you can't betray a woman who never had a single accurate suspicion she was made to bury. At least one grain has to *stick*. She needs to have been *close* to it once, and talked out of it, so the reveal is a return, not a surprise dropped from the ceiling.

Keep Randi bleeding, so the friendship betrayal has mass before it's named. "In Her Place" did this. Book Two needs to keep doing it, or the year-long betrayal is abstract when the door slams.

Don't let the "Vee names wanting Randi" beat play as clean self-discovery. This is still my sharpest fear and the ending doesn't neutralize it — it *depends* on it being handled right. If Book Two frames the installed want as Vee's brave awakening with the book cheering, then the Book Three reveal has to un-teach me a triumph the previous book sold me straight. The undertow has to be in the framing now, so the reveal confirms a dread rather than contradicting a celebration.

C5 — Before the crash-out, Book Three has to not coast on the fact that it *knows* the ending is coming. Specifically: don't let the pre-reveal stretch become victory-lap heat now that the plan's locked — the dread has to keep tightening, not idle. And the reveal mechanics in the packet (kiss, recognition, sight) are good, but the "retroactive reconstruction" has to actually be *rendered*, not summarized — I need to feel her re-reading the year in real time, because that reconstruction is the entire payment on the debt I've been carrying. If that gets compressed into a paragraph of "she understood everything now," I've been shorted at the register. And the threesome-under-clean-terms setup is the riskiest single thing in the book: it has to be staged so her surface consent reads as genuine and her deceived consent reads as the violation it is, simultaneously. If the book gets a frisson out of the deception itself in that scene — winks at the trick — it undoes everything the ending is trying to earn.

C6 — Only-if. I keep reading through Book Three, but the condition is this: the reveal must render Vee's retroactive reconstruction and her walk-out on the page in full, with the friendship betrayal weighted as heavily as the romantic one — if that reckoning gets summarized, softened, or routed toward any flicker of repair, I'm out and I'll feel conned as the reader in exactly the way the book spent three volumes conning her.
