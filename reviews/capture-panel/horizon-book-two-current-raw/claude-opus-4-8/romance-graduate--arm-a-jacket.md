A1 — It prepares me for the escalation honestly and I respect that it didn't lie to make the sale. "Not every door has been opened," "shadowed by the game still moving around her," "how much of that wanting has been arranged" — that's the jacket telling me straight that Book Two is the trap tightening, not the trap sprung. Good. I'm not going to feel misled about the *premise*.

But here's the thing: prepared for escalation is not the same as satisfied with deferral, and the jacket is doing something slippery I clocked immediately. It promises me *more* — warmer, fuller, bolder, more honest in the body, now with Randi's mouth on the menu too — and it promises me I'll *see the shape closing*. What it very carefully does not promise is that anything closes *on the people who built it*. "The closer she gets, the less she can see" — that's a sentence about Vee's blindness deepening, not about the bill coming due. So no, I wouldn't feel misled if Book Two ends with the arrangement unresolved, because the jacket honestly reserves the right to keep it hidden. I'd feel something worse than misled. I'd feel *managed* — the same way Vee's being managed — and I'd be furious at myself, not at the book, which is exactly the trick and I see the gear turning.

A2 — Yes, I buy it. I told you last time it was a yes with a knife on the table and the knife's still on the table.

What I'm expecting it to pay: the outside world has to touch the airless room. Volume One was hermetic — one cop siren swerving past was the only external thing allowed to land. You cannot hold a book airless for two volumes; the pressure has to find a wall. So I'm expecting at least *one* of the live wires to fire — Cassie's dad and the fraud, the photo now loose in Randi's hand, the collision Sheri named. And I'm expecting the Randi-Vee line to actually *cost* something, because the jacket just made Randi a lover and not only a puppeteer, and you don't get to run the same machine on the girl from inside her bed without the machine leaving a mark.

What I'll let it defer: the full reckoning. Nobody has to *learn the gesture was built* in Book Two — that can be the Volume Three spring. I'll let the discovery wait. I will not let the *pressure* wait. Deferring the explosion is fine; deferring all evidence that an explosion is coming is not. Give me one crack in the glass this volume and I'll wait for the shatter.

A3 — Honestly? Right now it reads closer to sexy complication than coming bill, and that's the jacket's one real weakness. Look at how the arrangement gets phrased: "how much of that wanting has been arranged," "the game still moving around her," "the shape closing." It's atmosphere. It's a *mood* of menace, not a *mechanism* of consequence. Nothing in the copy has a fuse on it. Compare it to Volume One's jacket, which handed me "every yes was freely given, that was the problem" — that was a *thesis with a blade in it*. This one gives me "not every door has been opened," which is a tease, not a wound.

The line that's supposed to be the threat — "Randi and Pace chose her together... She does not yet know how much of that wanting has been arranged" — lands as delicious rather than dangerous, because it's framed entirely from the seduction side. Nobody's bleeding in this copy. Randi bled in Volume One — "like he wants me, with the body attached" — and that crack is what made her tragic instead of decorative. This jacket sanded that off. So it feels like flavor. I want it to feel like a bill.

A4 — "She does not yet know how much of that wanting has been arranged."

That's the sentence carrying my whole trust, and it's carrying it on one word: *yet*. "Yet" is a promise that the knowing is coming — that this volume moves her toward the discovery rather than just deepening the dark she's kept in. If Book Two honors the "yet," I'm with you. If "yet" turns out to be decorative — if she ends Book Two knowing exactly as little as she starts it, three hundred pages of appetite and no daylight — then that word was a con and I'll know I was sold the con on purpose.

A5 — Two moves, and they're the same two lines I've held since chapter one.

First: if the jacket-or-book starts *agreeing with them*. If Book Two tips into "look how good the three of them are together, so what does it matter that it was built" — if the arrangement gets retroactively blessed by how much pleasure it produces, if the heat becomes the argument that the deception was fine — I'm out, and I'll feel stupid for the goodwill. The jacket flirts with this: "Everything she gives is hers" is doing a lot of exculpatory work, and if that sentence turns out to be the book's actual moral position rather than Vee's comforting lie, that's the curdle.

Second, and this would make me not even *start* it: if it signals the same gears. If I get the sense it's another volume of the hanging sentence, the recipe aria, Vee circling one unsayable word for another three hundred pages — I'm gone on boredom, not principle. The jacket doesn't tip that either way, which is why it's still a yes. But "fuller, bolder, more honest in the body" better mean the machinery *changed*, not just got louder. I came back for heat *and* an argument at one in the morning. Give me the same argument twice and it stops being an argument. It's just a loop with good lighting.
