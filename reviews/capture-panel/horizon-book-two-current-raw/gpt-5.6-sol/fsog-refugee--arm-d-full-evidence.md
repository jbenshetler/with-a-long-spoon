**F1 — Commercial verdict: only-if.**

I will keep reading through Book Three only if the finished Book Two repairs the consent problem created by “Grace” and “Vee on the Bench.” I still desperately want Vee’s awakening, and I now know the eventual betrayal will actually cost Pace and Randi everything. That makes me want the reveal.

But the concealment is no longer my only concern. Pace repeatedly treats Vee’s spoken “no” as a lie her aroused body authorizes him to defeat. That is precisely the genre bargain I came here to escape. A safeword does not automatically make every ordinary “no” erotic theater, especially when they have never explicitly agreed that it is. If those scenes remain framed as proof that Pace understands her better than her words do, I may not reach Book Three.

**F2 — The jacket sets a fair contract for Book Two’s delayed reckoning.**

“Randi and Pace chose her together,” “she does not yet know how much…has been arranged,” and “the game still moving around her” tell me plainly that Book Two escalates the trap rather than exposing it. If I buy after reading that, I cannot fairly claim the curtain itself deceived me.

The warm endpoint—Vee finally naming that she wants Randi while still not knowing the deed—is also consistent with that contract. I would finish Book Two angry and frightened for her, but not commercially misled.

**F3 — The material earns the delayed reveal better than the jacket alone, while making the ethical danger much worse.**

What earns the delay:

- “Missed a Spot” gives me Vee’s appetite from fully inside her. Her pleasure-princess realization is joyful, funny, and genuinely hers.
- “Among Friends,” “Another Round,” “Hangover,” and “Grace” make Pace’s tenderness particular and devastating. He remembers her people, makes room for them, cares for Sheri without claiming credit, gives Vee a key, and wants her because she is Vee.
- “Covering,” “A Clean Plate,” “The Reach,” “On Her Floor,” “Boyfriend,” “Coming Due,” and the planned “Gesso” trace Vee’s desire for Randi with exquisite specificity. I feel the wanting emerge rather than being told the plot requires it.
- “My Pleasure,” “In Her Place,” the scrunchie restoration, and the found hair make the hidden relationship active rather than merely postponed exposition. The betrayal is accumulating evidence and consequence.
- The planned Book Two addition in which Vee herself names that she wants Randi is essential. That gives her authorship over the desire even while Pace and Randi conceal the deed.

What nearly destroys that earned patience:

- In “Grace,” Pace “runs clean out of asking” while Vee is blindfolded. The prose explicitly understands that he has crossed from asking into taking and makes her thrill at having caused it.
- “Vee on the Bench” is a much larger problem. Her mouth says no; Pace calls the no a lie, uses her arousal as the supposedly truer answer, makes pleasure conditional on retracting or disproving her words, and is delighted when her body “wins.” That is not the consent-aware dominance I trusted him for in Volume One.
- “Barely Stings” also exposes how readily Vee treats an uninformed yes as irrevocable: she believes she has no lines left because she agreed before understanding the service. The scene recognizes the problem, but I need the book to reject that logic, not merely use it as part of her erotic psychology.
- “In Her Place” makes Randi’s manipulation especially ugly: she incapacitates Vee, lies to Pace about the available hour, occupies Vee’s green sheets, and then lets Vee praise her caretaking while secretly erasing the evidence. That is powerful betrayal writing, but it places the book very close to luxuriating in the con.

So the answer is both: the people and desire earn the delay much better than the jacket alone, but the execution has created a second debt that the jacket never warned me about.
