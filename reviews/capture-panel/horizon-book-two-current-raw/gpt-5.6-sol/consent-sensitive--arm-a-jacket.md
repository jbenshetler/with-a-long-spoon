A1

The jacket fairly prepares me for escalation without immediate reckoning, but not for indefinite non-reckoning.

“Shadowed by the game still moving around her” and “Not every door has been opened” tell me plainly that Book Two will deepen Vee’s desires while Pace and Randi continue withholding the structure behind them. So I would not feel misled merely because most of the book sustains that concealment.

But if Book Two ends with Vee still ignorant and no meaningful rupture in the arrangement, I would feel misled by the narrative promise, even if the jacket is technically accurate. “The closer she gets, the less she can see of the shape closing around her” promises movement toward a crisis. A second entire volume ending in another erotically satisfying reset would cease to feel like escalation and start feeling like stalling. Volume One bought one postponement. This jacket might buy another book’s worth of pressure, but it does not buy another consequence-free ending.

A2

Yes, I buy Book Two. Less happily than I bought Book One, but immediately.

I expect it to pay in pressure, evidence, and irreversible change. Vee does not necessarily need every fact early, but the hidden arrangement must begin damaging the relationships in ways the book refuses to soothe away. She needs to notice seams, ask sharper questions, catch at least one of them managing her reality, or make choices that the conspirators cannot neatly absorb into their plan. Pace and Randi must become less able to tell themselves that her genuine desire makes their methods benign.

I am willing to let the complete disclosure and final relational reckoning carry into Book Three if this is structurally a trilogy. I am also willing to let Vee’s conscious desire for Randi arrive before the truth. That could be emotionally devastating in exactly the right way: Vee finally names something genuinely hers, only to learn that the conditions around its emergence were curated.

What I will not defer is consequence. By the end of Book Two, somebody must lose control of the arrangement. A lie must become visible, trust must materially crack, or Vee must cross a threshold from unsuspecting participant to active knower. I will not accept another ending where sex, tenderness, and domestic warmth restore equilibrium while the secret remains perfectly protected.

“Everything she gives is hers. Not every door has been opened.”

That is the promise keeping me in the chair. It preserves Vee’s sexual agency without pretending that individual, authentic consent cancels informational control. It tells me the book understands both truths simultaneously: her desire is real, and Pace and Randi are still withholding facts that matter to what she believes she is choosing.

If Book Two keeps faith with that distinction, I can follow it somewhere very dark.

Any move suggesting that Vee secretly wanted this all along, and therefore the arrangement does not matter, would make me refuse the purchase.

For example: “Two people who know her desires better than she knows herself finally lead Vee home.” Absolutely not. That would recast surveillance, selection, and engineered encounters as superior romantic insight. Likewise, promises such as “surrender to their plan,” “discover she was always meant to be theirs,” or “trust them to give her what she needs” would tell me the book wants me to admire the very machinery Volume One taught me to fear.

The current jacket makes me buy because it calls the enclosure an enclosure. Make it sound like destiny, and I’m gone.
