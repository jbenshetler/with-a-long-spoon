REACTION

Oh, thank God. After that brunch — after the glaze on her lip from a pastry she never touched, after I sat there feeling Randi eat her one confession at a time in full sun — I needed exactly this: a door swinging open before she reaches it and her feet off the ground. The porch light wheeling once behind his head. That stupid bright sound she'd have died of anywhere else. I made it too, a little, reading it. A week apart and they didn't make the hall, and even then he was *slow* — "he never went straight at his own finish" — and the book doesn't render the sex, it renders her still not being used to it, and that's the hotter thing. "She hadn't known a man would want to." That's the sentence I left the whole genre looking for, and it's sitting here in the dark with the salmon pan soaking.

"Peter." To the sleeping shape of him. Barely the breath of it. She's keeping the real name the way she's keeping the scar — the two things he handed her that nobody else got — and she says it when he can't hear, because she's not ready to be seen holding it. I had to put the book down on my chest for a second. And she's counting the ways he says it without saying it, and deciding she can live inside the everything-he-does — and I believe her, and I also know what she doesn't know, which is that there's a woman who ate his couscous to a ruler line and pulled him off by the hair, and that woman kissed her at a curb four days ago. So the happiness is real and I'm holding it like something with a crack in it.

The drawer. The *drawer*. He saw the sprawl in the mirror — I watched his eyes go down to the counter with her, and I felt that guest-on-good-wood flinch in my own stomach — and he said nothing, and she carried that little tail of *had he minded* out of the room, and I carried it too, for a whole week of her life, which was three lines of mine. And then the scrunchies are gone and the bottom drops out and she opens the top drawer and it's hers. Pins in a dish. A clear run of liner with room for more of her. His whole careful self crammed into the second drawer, the one your hand doesn't go to first. No note. There's never a note. He didn't tell her it didn't matter; he went and *built* her a place, which is what he does, which is what the bowl was and the plate over the bowl and the dress in the window. I was crying at a drawer. I'm not ashamed. This is the man. This is the whole argument for him in one wiped counter.

And still — "he never said." She clocks it twice. The chapter is so gentle about it that it almost slides by, but she's lying awake in a house where everything is said in walnut and salmon and nothing is said in words, and I know from the sink scene that he *has* words, extravagant ones, and that he's spending some of them on Randi. The weight thing snagged me too: her bras riding higher, "her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him," told as a good secret. Maybe it is. But the book just spent five chapters showing me a girl being dressed into someone else's shape, and now her own shape is changing and she "never asked it to," and I'm not sure the book wants me to read that as only sweet.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: he gave her the top drawer and she still doesn't know whose clothes were folded on that table. He made her a place in the one room in his house that Randi hasn't been written into yet. I want it to hold. I don't think the book means to let it.
