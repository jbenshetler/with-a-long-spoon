Oh. So that's what's on the shelf.

I've been asking since — God, since the fire and the robe, chapters I can't even draw anymore — *what's the reason*, not the parents, the *reason*, and the book just set it down on a laminate counter next to a bowl of boiled chicken. Daphne. Tutor, best friend, first, first, first. He said it to her and she left and "there had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told." I read that sentence three times and I'm not okay. That's the loneliest thing in this book and it's not even about Vee. And then — "he had taught himself that the words were the difference." He built a rule out of a wound at eighteen and he's been running it on every woman since like it's a proof. Of course he has. He's a mathematician. He found the one variable that was present when it hurt and absent when it didn't and he *controlled for it*. That's the method. That's "extraordinary" and "how good we are together" — a true sentence to each that isn't the sentence. I called it a method in my own notes and I thought I was being cold about him. I was being exact. He'd appreciate that.

So: can't or won't. I've gone back and forth every chapter and the answer is *can't that is actually won't*, and he knows it. "He had chosen the smaller truth." "At the moment she had asked him to be brave he had been weak." He doesn't let himself off. Not one inch. Nobody in this book has ever been as hard on Pace as Pace is in this chapter, and I've been pretty hard on him.

And he said it. He finally said it. *He knew he loved her.* He said it to a bowl of broccoli and a hangover and me, and she's in Ohio in a bed her legs outgrew, building a blonde out of spite. I got the declaration. I did. The reader got it and the girl didn't, and I'm sitting here holding it like a letter that came to the wrong address. I want to drive it out to her myself. I want to put it in the field she keeps opening and closing.

The truck. Oh, the truck. His head lifting before he knew he'd moved — the fork stopping — "his body gathered itself toward the narrow cut in the hill." That's the stoop. That's her car nosing in and her face finding him, the smile she gives only him, and I didn't know he'd been *watching for that*, I only ever had it from her side, her hopping out of her jeans in his hall, and now I know he was standing on that stoop waiting for the headlights every single time. The engine didn't slow. And this man — fork squared to the table edge, two breaths before any sentence, "I don't go back" with every syllable set down separate — bent over a bowl of prison food and sobbed until he couldn't hear the truck anymore. "And for a few breaths after." I had to put the phone down. I'm texting you at 1 a.m.: *he cried over the chicken.*

Sheri did that. Not Vee, not Randi — Sheri, with her two-color hair, who stole his fries and said "from this seat, that feels pretty shitty" and didn't soften a thing. He went home from that diner and drank Whiskey River until he threw up and mopped his own bathroom floor, and that's what it took. I love her. The chill that takes her after — I know that chill, I watched him throw a blanket over it on the couch, I said in my own notes I'd throw a blanket over her myself. The third chair is still at the table even while he's weeping for the first one. And here's what I can't get out from under: the whole chapter is a man finally understanding that keeping the words back is what hurt her — and *Randi* is also a thing he's keeping back, and the chapter doesn't put those two withholdings next to each other, and I don't know yet if the book knows that or just Pace doesn't. "She find out about the brunette, then?" "No. Not like that." Sheri knows. The waitress at the Rusty Nail knew. The food. "Everything a body could need and nothing his mouth wanted."
