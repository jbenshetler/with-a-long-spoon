REACTION

I had a whole chapter dreaded in my notes — "Randi will sit there knowing exactly why he couldn't answer and say nothing" — and it came, and it wasn't what I braced for, and I'm more unsettled by the version I got than the one I was afraid of.

Because she *didn't* say nothing. She said the truest things anyone's said to Vee since "hungry out loud." The fear's a liar and you haven't slept. Set each thing down where Vee can see it — the heat up hours ahead, the wine poured, the chicken going. "What did his face do — right then?" God, that's good. That's a woman who knows him, making Vee look at a man instead of a wall. And "a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — I read chapter 54, I watched him sob over a truck that didn't slow, I *know* she's right. She read him dead on. She read Vee dead on too: "you built her at three in the morning," and Vee said "I've seen her. In my mind," small, and I wanted to put my hand over my own mouth. Every one of those lines I'd have texted a friend at 1 a.m. as *this is how you talk someone down.*

And then: "You don't do it with words. Words are the whole war."

That's where I put the book down for a second. Not because it's wrong — it's practical, it'll work, he'll open the door and he won't say no. But Vee held the stall-line in his house in December. She wouldn't let his hands answer a question her mouth asked, and I wrote *good girl* in my own notes, and tonight the woman who knows exactly why he can't say the word has talked her into going out there and answering with her body, no words, nothing kept back. Randi just turned Vee into Pace. "Believe what I do." She's protecting him. She's protecting the arrangement — and she's doing it with such warmth, such real tenderness, and Vee feels it "settle over her like a thing being decided for her and by her at once," and that sentence is the whole book. The half-inch again. Randi sets the coat down and stops talking and lets Vee find the end of the sentence, and Vee finds it, and believes it's hers. And it *is* hers — the wanting came up under the shame the way it always does with her, and I believe she'd known since the coat. Both things true. I've stopped being able to separate them and I think that's the point and I hate how much I admire it.

The shoes. Randi always knew the shoes. She knelt and buckled the heel at the induction and now she's hooking black heels on two fingers and sending my girl to his door in them, and "for us" is sitting right under it, and Vee's too wrecked to look at anything.

And then Randi told the fairytale and I watched her lie by omission to the one person she's never lied to, and watched Vee eat it — "the kind you tell your daughter about," soft, looking down, because that's the exact thing Vee doesn't have, a man who'd name children in the first hour. Vee envying the easy lead. Vee envying a night that left Randi cold everywhere the fur didn't reach. "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — the one true sentence hidden inside the joke, and Vee laughed, really laughed, and I didn't.

Then the composites. Fifty years of sisters looking slightly to the left, and Vee turned around and went back. Both hands on Randi's face. *She* did it. Four months of Randi leaning in on a schedule and calling it work, and the girl who held still for it took her face in both hands and kept going because she wasn't finished — and Randi with the back of her hand against her own mouth, fifteen again, laughing one idiot sound in an empty hall. I've been so hard on Randi for so long and that paragraph undid me. She wanted twenty minutes with a mug of matcha nobody drinks just to *hold* it. That's not a lever. That's a woman who got kissed.

And then Brooke, warm the whole way, took it off her in under a minute. "You always say goodbye under the composites." "Play all you want, privately, or for the boys" — and I felt the floor go out from under Randi the way it went out from under Vee in December. "I'm asking whether you want to keep your position." Randi heard herself say that she did. Put the mug back in the cupboard. Never drank it. Didn't get to hold on to the thing for twenty minutes. The chapter's called "My Friend Randi" and the last time the word *friend* gets said in it, Randi's using it as a cover under ninety sets of eyes, the exact thing she decided in Gstaad: let the word keep its cover. She kept it. It cost her the one thing she'd just been handed.

Where I am with them: Pace is five chapters out of the room and sobbing over chicken, and he's about to open his door to the answer he couldn't give, and I don't know if I want him to get it for free. Vee's about to walk through the cold naked under a coat to hand him everything, and it's brave, and it's hers, and someone else chose the shoes. Randi got the thing she wanted and was told the price inside of ten minutes, and I'd throw the blanket over her tonight without being asked. Cassie's side of the room is stripped bare and I noticed that more than I expected to.

Nobody did a thing to anybody they didn't hand over. And I still want to shake Randi and say *tell her*. And I still know she won't.
