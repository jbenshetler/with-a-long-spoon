REACTION

Cassie. Finally. Four silences and then the hands come out of her lap, and what she does with them is hold out a box of Cocoa Pebbles.

I've been begging this book for one person to ask Vee what *she* wants with nothing riding on the answer, and I'd about decided it was never going to happen — Randi arranges the room so the yes is the only door, Pace asks "will you" and never "why," and even Saturday night in his bed, "anything else you want to tell me about Randi" was a question *he* needed answered, with his own appetite folded in under the gentleness. "Is this what you want?" is the first clean question anyone has put to her in seventy-three chapters. No glass of wine behind it, no inch of him held back as the toll. Just a girl in her coat on the lower bunk who'd like to know if her friend is all right.

And the thing that got me — Vee went and *looked.* She actually reached for it. She's been talking herself around that shape for two chapters now, hand flat on her stomach like a lid on a pot, "I'm not gay," "it didn't mean anything," and the one time somebody asks her straight she goes in after the answer like it ought to be a thing a person knows about herself. And comes up against the edge of it and steps back. Her neck said yes. That's as close as she's gotten, and it took the plainest person in the book to get her there.

Then she did the thing. "You don't like her." Oh, Vee. The plank laid out on the floor, the old fight, the ugly swing at the one person in the room with no stake in her — that's exactly what she did to Pace Saturday night, "you want Randi," grab the nearest thing and throw the whole weight of it off herself. He defended himself, that once, and it was the sweetest thing she'd ever seen him do. Cassie just stepped over it without looking down. I don't know which I love more, but I know which one is scarier for Vee, because Pace giving her a wall to lean on is a comfort and Cassie refusing to be one is a mirror.

"You didn't have dinner." I actually put the book down for a second. *Sit, you need to eat before you decide how you feel* — his line, Sunday morning, with the bread. And Randi's whole courtship is a table. The series is literally called With a Long Spoon and here's Cassie, no cloth, no candle, no leaning in to draw the next thing out of her, just a cereal box across a dorm room. A want the size of a bowl. Everybody in this book feeds this girl and Cassie's the only one who isn't going to want anything back for it. "Some of Mine." Not all of it. Some. She's offering to share, not to serve.

I'm so relieved and I'm so worried. Because the question is still open on the desk — Vee said so herself — and Cassie didn't close it, she just let Vee answer the one she could. That's mercy and it's also a clock. Cassie's seen *something* — the hug, the neck, the sound — and she's got it filed now with the sweater and the skirt and "Randi's date," and the next time she asks it's not going to be about cereal. Thursday Randi's off disciplining a bad girl (I heard that, Helen Rivers, I heard it), Saturday is brunch that Vee asked for out of a ringing neck, and Sunday he gets whatever's left of her. She's got a week to find a floor. She won't. She'll find a bowl.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: the roommate's the only one who's never kissed her and she's the only one who asked.
