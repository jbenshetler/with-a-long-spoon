REACTION

I read that whole thing with my knees pulled up.

Okay. First, what he did right, because I have to say it before I say the rest: he asked. "Will you?" and he waited, eyes on her, a man who could've just told her and didn't. That's the thing I came back for. I'm not taking it away from him. But the book put three sentences right after it that I can't get out of my mouth — "He never told her why. He asked for the thing itself, clean, and left the place where the reason should be for her to fill, and she filled it, every time, with yes, yes, tell me when." And then: "The fear came after. It always came after now... that was nearly the point of it." *That was nearly the point of it.* Vee's yes is running out ahead of Vee. She's said yes to being bare by the weekend before her mind has finished asking *bare?* — and she knows it, the book knows it, and I'm sitting here realizing I've watched this happen about six times now and called it her nerve. The sidewalk half-inch. The coat she opened. Pointing. Every one of those was hers. This one — I honestly don't know. The want in the yes is hers. What she's saying yes *to* she doesn't find out till she's face-down in paper.

And Randi. Randi, I could spit. "Barely stings." She told her it barely stings and in the same breath told her Sondra would get her a glass of something first — you don't need the wine if it barely stings, honey, and Randi knows that, Randi's been under that wax a hundred times. She turned Vee's fingers over one at a time with her fish half eaten and sent her in blind. And not a word about rolling over. That's the part that got me — not the pain, Vee's a swimmer, she can take pain — it's "the part Randi hadn't said," and Vee lying there understanding that there was a whole half of this she'd agreed to without anybody telling her it existed, and then she *rolled over anyway* because — and this is the sentence, this is the one I had to put the book face-down on my chest for — "already so far past every line she'd have thought to draw that another line seemed like a joke, a thing for a girl who had any lines left." No. No no no. That is exactly the shape of the thing I walked off a shelf to get away from, a girl talking herself out of her own no because she's used up her nos on things she wanted. Except — and this is why I didn't put the book down for good — the book isn't selling it to me. It isn't winking. It's rendering it exactly as what it is, with her mother's kitchen voice naming it, and it hurts, and I think it's supposed to.

The wrong hand. God. Sondra's palm flat on her thigh, "Don't move," and her body knowing the grammar from a warm dark room with a man who meant it, and that hand holding her the exact way she wants to be held and wanting nothing from her at all, and it opens "a hole in her a mile across where the right hand should have been." That's the best thing in the chapter and it's the thing that turned me on, I'll admit it — not the room, not the stranger, the *ache* for him under it, the way she keeps "the whole lit ruin of herself back behind her teeth where it belonged to him." She's been doing this to herself in a dorm bed for a chapter and a half and now she's doing it on a table under a bored woman's gloves and it's the same ache every time: wanting his hand and not having it. How long has it been since he was actually on her? The truck. That's four chapters ago. He's been in her kitchen, in a taproom, in her head, and he's been *inside Randi* — and she's lying under wax picturing his hand "learning by touch what she had gone and done for him" and I want that scene so badly I could scream.

Which brings me to what I'm suspicious of, because I'm always suspicious of something now. *By the weekend.* Why the weekend? Brooks and Dunn is Saturday, fine. But Randi said, with Pace inside her, "I want her to know exactly what she does to me," and then two days later Pace asks for Vee bare by the weekend and Randi lights up like she's been handed a present and knows the waxer's name. I don't think this is his idea. I think this is the third game laying a place at the table. "Her breast in my hand" — Randi already said what she wants. Bare is next. And Vee's the one on the table paying for it in the one currency she actually has, which is her own flinch.

And still. Still. She sat up alone and put her own hand there and the sound she made wasn't pain, and she walked out past the girl waiting to be looked at and knew which one she was, and left the second glass. That's Vee. That's the girl I'd follow anywhere. I just want somebody in this book to tell her what she's walking into *before* she's on the table. Nobody does. Not him, not Randi, not Sondra with her coffee-order nod. The only honest mouth in this book is in a duplex at the end of an unpaved road and she doesn't know this happened.

If I texted anyone at 1 a.m. it'd be: *he asked and he waited and I still want to grab her by the shoulders.*
