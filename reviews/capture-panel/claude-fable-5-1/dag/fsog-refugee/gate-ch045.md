**REACTION**

Five nights and she called it. Her. For school, for the paragraph she'd read three times, for her own life — and I sat up a little, because that's the heroine I asked for in chapter one, a girl with a to-do list that isn't him. And then the book did the thing it does to me every time: "Take the time you need. I'll be here." No fight, no cold, no cost. And she *wanted* the fight. She wanted him to want the nights badly enough to say so. I read that sentence twice because I've been wanting the same thing for forty chapters and here's Vee wanting it out loud in her own head, and the book knows it, and still won't give it to either of us. He opens the door and waits on the far side. That's his whole vocabulary. It is a good vocabulary. I am so tired of translating it.

The hall. God, the hall. The jeans that were sensible in the car and the enemy now, hopping one-legged, the socks off with a hand flat on the wall while he just *stands there* enjoying himself, and the bra he holds and doesn't pull, so she has to unwind herself out of it like something off a spool — that's the man I came for. Doesn't take it. Holds it and lets her come out of it herself. She lost and she knew it and she put her mouth back on him anyway. I laughed out loud, in bed, alone, and then I didn't laugh, because his hand went flat over the green and found the proof of her and *stayed*, and she gave it a moment she never decided to give it, and I felt that in my own spine. Then her throat published the number. Five nights coming out of her in one rush, her body reading out the tally her mouth would never. That's desire from inside. That's hers. Nobody harvested that yes.

And then the shirt, and I'm done, I'm wrecked, I'd text it at one in the morning with no words after. She washed it. She slept in a dying shirt every night she wasn't there and one day it smelled like nothing and she *washed* it and drove it out to him with her nerve going on the highway. And his face — puzzlement, and then the start of a hurt, because he thought she was giving it back. The book has never once shown me that man can be hurt by her. Forty-five chapters of his still hands and his small smile and *wonderful* quieter each time, and the first crack in him is a girl handing him a clean flannel. "He decided not to ask. He was good at not asking things." Oh, I know he is. I watched him ask Randi six questions in a row on that couch — *does she want it, that isn't what I asked, do you like it* — relentless, in that voice. He asks *her* everything. With Vee he waits. Two people in a warm kitchen who will not ask each other the plain thing, and she gets to it sideways through a shirt, and he puts it on *over the one he's wearing*, right then, no second ask, and stands there in two shirts because she told him to. "Smelling like me." That's as close as he's come. It's closer than *always*. It isn't the word, and it's the best not-the-word I've ever read, and I cried a little, and I'm not sorry.

Now the part I can't put down, because the book handed me chapters forty-three and forty-four and I can't give them back. "He always had a slowness left in him when she had none." I know where that slowness lives. I watched him go four in a day two weekends ago on a white oak couch with the other one, counting out loud, and now I'm asked to feel the slowness as a gift to Vee, and I *do* feel it, and I also feel the couch under the bed. And this — "she could not have said, watching him, which of them got the larger share, and found she didn't mind." Him and a ball of dough. The book put that sentence there for me, not her. His attention is a thing that divides and she's just told me she doesn't mind the dividing, and she thinks she's talking about pizza. And the thong landed green on "the long, unusually proportioned table that stood in the middle of his bedroom." She never looked to see where it went. I looked. That's not a table for eating. I've been in this genre long enough to know a prop when a book plants one, and that one's standing in the middle of the room he sleeps in with both of them, and Vee threw her underwear on it blind. The third chair's a table. I think I just saw it.

So where I am: in love with the two shirts, uneasy about the table, and watching the other woman's father sit in a government database that Vee closed with one keystroke and kept to herself. Vee's carrying Randi's secret for her now, out of kindness, not knowing Randi's carrying three of Vee's. The ledger is so uneven it makes my teeth hurt. And I'd still read the shirt scene again right now. I'd reread it before the next chapter if I weren't already reaching for the next chapter.
