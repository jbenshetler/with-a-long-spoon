**REACTION**

Okay. The pencil.

I've read a hundred meet-cutes and I have never once had my stomach drop over a man handing a girl a *pencil*, but that's what this chapter did to me. He asked for it with his eyes first. He wrote the ugly formula and told her *don't, nobody should have to carry that around*, made one small mark, and then put the pencil back in her hand at exactly the line where she could finish it herself. "She hadn't known she was allowed." That's it. That's the whole thing I've been hunting for since I put down Fifty Shades feeling slightly sick — a man whose whole competence is pointed at making *room*, and a woman feeling the room open under her before she has words for it. It's the bench again, in miniature, in daylight, with clothes on. I had to put the book down and just sit for a second.

And *may I call you.* She heard the may. I heard the may. I'm a grown woman and I heard it in my body.

Which is exactly why I'm scared, because I know what Vee doesn't. Randi wasn't texting "some boy." Randi was texting *him*. The Wilson place is the place she lied to Brooke about. The whole scene — the cute guy in line, the four steps across the floor, the sitting down without dragging the chair — was staged, and Pace walked in and played a stranger to a girl's face for twenty minutes. He's the man who let Randi believe a bench was a table and then said *you said it was a table, I didn't correct you.* He's built on not-correcting. And I found it the hottest chapter in the book so far, and the book knows I did, and that's the "that was the problem" from the jacket landing on *me* instead of on Vee. I don't feel winked at yet. Nobody has run over anyone's no; there isn't a no to run over. But I've started keeping a little ledger, and the first entry is: *he lied to her about who he was, courteously, and I liked it.*

Randi, though. Randi is who I'd text about at 1 a.m. The cup coming up to her mouth twice and no sip happening. Asking about Bessel's correction — a question she plainly knows the answer to — just to keep the stranger act standing, and then holding the cup a beat too long when he answered her "the way you'd answer anybody." She's the one who pressed a bottle-cap edge into her own nail bed on his bed. She's the one who said *do you think she'll like the bench* with her face in his shirt. And now she's sitting three feet away watching him look at Vee the way he looked at her upside-down in the face cradle, and she's *selling* it — "a man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call." She closed the sale on her own boyfriend for her new friend, warm as anything, and I don't think she knows yet what she's paying for it. I ache for her more than for Vee right now, which I did not expect.

Cassie sees everything. *Goodbye, Vee* last chapter, "the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" this one. She's the only person in the room not performing and she's going to be the casualty and I already hate that.

Small tender thing: Vee borrowing Randi's name-bit — *yes, like the university* — and then wanting her own name back a second later. A girl trying on a skin and feeling it fit and not knowing whose it is. That's the book being kind to her while it sets her up, and it's the kindness that keeps me in.

No one's been alone in a room for two chapters now. I feel it. But the care between them is on the page in the way a man reads a problem twice before he touches it, so I'm not restless yet. I want the phone to light up. I want to see what he does when it's just the two of them — whether the may holds when there's no audience.
