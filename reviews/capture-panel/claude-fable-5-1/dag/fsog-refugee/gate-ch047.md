REACTION

Oh, Vee. Oh, honey, no.

I read the first half of this chapter holding my breath with her. The porch, the moon, the second cup already cooled to drinking temperature — he set it there before she woke, the way he does, the way he does *everything*, and I thought: this is it. She's going to say it sideways and he's going to hear it straight. "I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal." That's the best sentence she's ever built. It's the stall-yes and the long step and the top drawer all in one, and she *made* it, over weeks, carved it the way she trimmed HUMBLE PIE with the good scissors, and she carried it to his porch and set it in the air. And his hand stopped. Two breaths. Two responses started in his palm and put down. I had my own hand over my mouth at "I love how good we are together," because I knew before she did. *How good we are together.* Not *you*. We. Sex. The thing we do. The man who reads Randi's hand going slack on a lawn and knows which girl she wants before she does — *that* man heard "you fed me a meal" and answered about chemistry. He didn't miss it. He can't have missed it. He heard the word she wasn't saying and he stepped around it with a kiss to the top of her head held one beat too long, and he thinks the beat was an answer.

And here's what gutted me: the book let me know the whole time that he isn't lying. "I never had this either." That's true. "I'm glad you feel it too." True. He did the thing he always does, which is give her the real thing he can give and nothing new, and I have spent forty-six chapters calling that control-that-makes-room and tonight it made a room she walked into alone and sat down in and waited, and the moon came up, and he held her, and the *wait* behind her breastbone went where waits go when the quilt is warm. I know that place. Every woman reading this knows that place. The one where you file the evidence you're not sure of the weight of, and you don't look at the pile, and the pile is not empty.

Chapter 43 is sitting under this like a stone under water. He said *you are extraordinary* to Randi, on the couch, because she asked for something nice, and he thought about it because she'd know the difference. He didn't think about it for Vee. He produced the sentence a man produces when a woman says something *larger* and he needs to get them both past it warm. And Vee can't test it, and she knows exactly why: "the words went through him first." That's the most precise thing she's ever thought, and she thought it on a folding table in a dorm laundry room with other people's socks going round. She's not stupid. The book has never let her be stupid. She lands on *I do not know what just happened* and then — this is the part I'd text at 1 a.m. with no words after — "The cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech." She retired it. She put her best sentence back in her body. He'll never know she took it off the table.

I'm angry at him and I'm not sure I'm allowed to be. He hasn't done a cruel thing. He's done the kind thing exactly one size too small, and the book is making me feel the size. That's what I asked for, isn't it — I wanted a book where the disregard isn't dressed up as romance, and here it isn't dressed up at all, it's a man in two shirts who can't say the word and a girl who's learned she's not permitted to say it first. There's no wink. The quilt was warm. The sky went gold on the porch floor. And it still hurt her, and I felt it in my own chest, and the book knows it hurt her, and that is the difference between this and every shelf I left.

What I'm also hearing, and I don't want to: in forty-three he asked "Is she all right?" and Randi said "radiant" and he said "that isn't the same answer." He knows the difference between happy and all right. He checks the gauge on her for Randi. He didn't check it for himself this morning. Or he did, and the gauge read fine, because her body did know what to do with being held, and he trusts bodies — that's his whole instrument. The one thing his instrument can't read is a sentence about crackers.

I'm not leaving. God, I'm not leaving — I want the next chapter the way she wants Friday. But I'm reading on with a different thing in my hands than I had last night. Last night I would have taken him. This morning I'd still take him, and I'd make him say it, and I'm not sure anyone in this book is going to make him.
