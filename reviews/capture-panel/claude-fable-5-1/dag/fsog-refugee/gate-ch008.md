REACTION

Okay, I did not expect to get wet-eyed over a pool chapter, and yet.

The underwater bit. *She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken.* I read that line and had to set the book on my chest for a second, because I know things she doesn't. I've got the jacket. I've been in Pace's bedroom with the bench. I've sat on his couch while Randi drank wine and made him *tell*. So when this girl hangs in the green with nobody wanting anything from her and thinks the water is the one part that's never been taken — and then *grieves, a little* — the book is basically leaning over my shoulder going, *yes, and you know what's coming for the rest of her, don't you.* I do. And the awful thing, the thing that's got me actually unsettled, is that I *want* it for her. I want Pace to look at that body the way he looked at Randi's — properly, lights up, hands on her not to move her but to take the feel of her — and I want her to stand there and let him, and I want it to be the first time somebody's looking at her didn't cost her anything. And I'm also the reader who's spent years leaving books because a woman's yes was arranged for her before she got to say it. So I am sitting here wanting a thing and side-eyeing myself for wanting it, which I think is exactly where this author wants me, and I resent how good she is at it.

Cassie, though. Cassie is the one I'd text a friend about at 1 a.m. *They will never know the love of a woman* — kept low, for Vee only, when she's got the lungs to put it up in the bleachers. That's love. That's the person who knows her. And then the splash-from-ten-feet-off, not stepping closer, not asking, just *fixing* it — I laughed out loud and then went a bit quiet, because Cassie is the one who said *Goodbye, Vee* in the lecture hall like she already knew the shape of what was coming up the aisle in an oat-milk latte. She's in the shallow end standing *like a person* while Vee goes off the edge. I'm scared for Cassie in a way I can't quite justify yet. The book keeps putting her in the frame and then having everyone look past her — Pace looked at her like a great-aunt, Randi's warmth swings over and gets nothing back — and I think she sees more than any of them. And the irony of *the love of a woman*, said to Vee, by the friend who actually loves her, when the woman who's coming for her is Randi — oh, that's mean. That's well-aimed mean.

The bleacher boys didn't bother me the way they'd bother me in a lesser book, because the book isn't winking at them. It gives Vee the long blink, the eyeroll at the ceiling, the game face — and then it lets us watch fifty yards of held breath go out on Cassie's joke. That's the register I'll take a quiet chapter in. Her body is being acted upon by every room she walks into, and the book *knows* that's what's happening, and gives her an inside.

What I noticed, and what's got my foot tapping: the heat in this book has all been Randi and Pace. Kitchen chair, bench, couch, wine in his mouth. Vee's had a froyo walk and a hand that gave her back a pencil. I'm one chapter out from the two of them alone and it was a short chapter, and this one has neither of them in it — it has her *body* in it, waiting up on the deck with the towel, and I'm now genuinely tracking how long before somebody who wants her gets to be in a room with her. Pace has her number. He said *talk soon.* He has never once in this book been in a hurry and I'm starting to feel the not-hurrying in my own hands.

I'm not tired of anything. I just want the phone to light up.
