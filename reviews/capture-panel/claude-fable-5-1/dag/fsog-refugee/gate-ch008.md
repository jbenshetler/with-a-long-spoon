REACTION

Okay. I've been waiting for this one since the coffee shop, and it paid up.

"May I choose?" I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. Because that's the whole thing, isn't it — that's what I've been hunting across a hundred books. She's lost in a menu she can't read, she's handing him the rescue without quite asking, and he doesn't take it. He *asks for it*. And then — this is the part — he takes it seriously once she gives it. He looks at the page a beat longer. He asks the one question, is there anything you won't eat. The relief she feels handing it over is the relief I feel reading a man who understands that being given the choice is a gift and not a conquest. Compare that to the first chapter, Randi's "what are we doing tonight" — same surrender, same man, and he receives both of them the same way. Carefully. It's the same Pace in both rooms. I keep checking for the contradiction and it isn't there.

And the kiss. God, the kiss. She says the rule — I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days — and I braced the way she braced, because I know what this scene looks like in the books I left. The lean, the charm turned up, the little patient wait that isn't patient at all, the "come on" dressed up as romance. He just smiles. Lets it close. *Starts walking.* Takes her hand along with him like the no was a complete sentence. And then it's her feet that stop. Her hand that pulls taut. Her exception, her inch, her mouth to his, and he answers *no more than she brought* and eases back a breath and waits again. She has to decide twice. She gets to decide twice. The second one, where he finally meets her all the way and she's grinning inside it — I felt that in my own face. That's the hottest clothed scene I've read in a year and nobody so much as unbuttoned anything.

The food got to me more than I expected. She ate without watching herself eat. That line — "she didn't notice she'd stopped" — sat right next to the apple slices from chapter one in my head, the thin ones cut-side-up, Randi's whole trimmed-down appetite, and now here's Vee going down and down into a hollow that's been empty longer than since noon, and he just lets her. Doesn't make a thing of it. The bread, the bright one and the dark one, "one to wake you and one to fold you under" — I think that's what he's doing to her, honestly, and I think she half knows it, and I think she's right to let him. The right-hand thing I clocked too. It's some rule he lives by that she doesn't know yet. One more closed door with something behind it. I want to know what's behind it almost as much as she does.

Now the part I'd text at 1 a.m. Because I'm not Vee. I know where this started. I know there's a couch and a glass of red waiting, and Randi's going to straddle him and say *tell* against his ear, and he's going to tell her about the hummus and the exception, and I can already feel how wet that's going to make Randi and I don't know what to do with the fact that it makes me a little wet too. But — he kept the froyo thing. The nicest-a-person-has-been-to-me line. He didn't hand it to Randi. "It was hers." So what does he keep from tonight? The elevator doors? The rule she broke? I'm watching for what he holds back because I think that's where the book is going to tell me whether this is real for him or whether she's a game they're both playing with their hands on each other. The jacket promised me it gets real for all three. This chapter is the first time I believed Pace might already be there before Randi is.

Cassie's "that's not a boy, that's a man" — I'd have said the same thing. And Cassie's "goodbye, Vee" from the lecture hall is still sitting in the back of my skull like a splinter. Cassie sees it. Cassie's the only one in this book who sees it and I'm scared for her and for Vee both, and I'm turning the page anyway, which is exactly what the blurb said I'd do. Fine. Fine. I'm in.
