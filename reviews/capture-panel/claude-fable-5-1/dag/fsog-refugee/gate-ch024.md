REACTION

The lesson undid me before anyone had a drink in their hand. "If you want to, I'll teach you" — she repeated it to Meg word for word and I understood exactly why, because I'd have done the same. And then he puts her in the frame on a cold concrete pad under the field lights and says the thing this whole book has been saying for twenty-seven chapters without ever saying it: *give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead. Being jelly doesn't work.* That's it. That's the man I came back for, in one dance instruction. He can't lead a woman who isn't there. The dominance literally needs her weight. I sat with that a long minute. "Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." The distance is what gets you stepped on. Oh, Helen Rivers, you absolute menace.

"There's no sorry unless you take off a foot." And then he laughs *after* she laughs, the gravity cracking. "There it is," quiet, and she says nothing because she's busy. I was grinning like an idiot. HEAT in this chapter is a hand at a shoulder blade firming "a degree and no more" and the word "Again," and I felt it lower than most of the bedroom chapters.

So of course the book takes it away from me. The hugs, the count she didn't mean to start, her mother arriving flat on the tail of it. The blonde. I will say — I saw that blonde coming three paragraphs out, with her makeup doing too much work and her little speech practically gift-wrapped for the plot, and that's the one place I felt the author's hand on the back of my neck. But what Vee did with it was *hers*, and the book didn't flinch from it. "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" I actually said *no* out loud. And the thing that got me — she knew. "She heard it the way you hear a glass go past the point where you can still catch it." She wanted to stay angry and the book let her, and then it made her sit in it until the lights came up. The slut word, in her own head, about herself — three dates. That's her mother's voice finally landing a clean hit, and it hurt because the book has spent twenty chapters getting that word to stop closing her.

And him. "Yes. Sometimes. When it's required." He didn't defend himself. He didn't follow her across the room. He didn't do the thing every alpha in every book I quit does — grab her arm, growl *you're coming with me*, make her anger into foreplay. She said *I'll handle that myself* and he took her at her word and set the beer down untouched and walked out into the cold. Here's where I'm split right down the middle, and I think the book wants me split: part of me is going *you left her at a bar out of town with no car, Pace*, and the rest of me knows that if he'd stayed and managed her, I'd have liked him less. He honored a no that cost her a cab fare out of money that had other jobs — I felt last chapter's credit card slip sitting right behind that line. That's what real consent looks like when it isn't pretty. It isn't always a man kneeling. Sometimes it's a man leaving when she tells him to, and the leaving being the respect.

The bartender was the mercy. "Leaves her at the door. Doesn't take advantage — I'd have heard otherwise." "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." I needed someone in that room to tell her, and I loved that it was a tired woman racking glasses who was done being asked for things.

Then the shirt. She washed it, hating it, and it came back smelling of fabric softener and no one, and she put it on anyway. That's the saddest sentence in the book so far and I'd text it to someone at 1 a.m. And Cassie three feet away, breathing easy, and "She did not wake her." Cassie would have sat up for this. Cassie would have *driven* for this. I'm storing it. The ledger against Vee on Cassie's behalf is getting long.

And then the last line. Not *go to his house*. Not *call him*. *Go to Randi's*. My stomach dropped. Vee spent an entire night in a fury about *the girls* — all the girls, which one knows what his pillow smells like — and the one girl who actually does is the one she's going to for comfort in the morning. The dramatic irony is so thick I could cut it. And one more thing I'm holding onto: on the floor she asked herself how much of this was Pace and how much was "the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join." That's *my* question. I've been asking it since the pool. The book put it in her mouth and then turned her so she'd lose it. And on the pad — "guessing stopped happening, the decision pulled from her somehow." I underlined *pulled*. I don't think that word is an accident in this book.

He didn't fight dirty. She did. The book didn't make either one sexy. I'm shaken and I'm in.
