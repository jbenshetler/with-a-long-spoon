REACTION

She walked out into the cold. "Vee," once, and the door, and I turned the page with my whole chest tight to find out whether he went after her — and I got a breakfast order. Smoked salmon on one slice of dark rye, no butter, lemon, capers, English breakfast tea, ice water. I read that sentence and genuinely thought, I don't care what she ate, where is my girl. For about a page I was reading the way you read when you're waiting for a chapter to be over.

Then the bracelet. Plain white gold, *locked* on her wrist, and she keeps it out of the light at her father's table. That's the shelf. Twenty chapters I've been asking what's on it, and it's been on Randi's wrist the whole time, under a cream coat, at brunch, across from Vee, kept out of the sun. He put that on her. Nobody has to say so. And her father — the point on the graph, the MIRA man — carving a rare steak and asking how many hearts she's broken, and she *invents boys for him* because the invented ones have always been safer at that table. "Eating her revulsion." I had her as the predator in the practice room and she's a girl making up a life to feed her father over eggs. Both. Both are true and I hate that they're both true.

The John thing — okay, I laughed. Three children at ski camp, he gives the number and she gives the names. It's a good meet-cute, it's the kind I used to buy whole series for. Tall, law school, assumes the table, conjures the sleigh, takes her up the hotel steps without ever asking where the night was going. And then the line that made me actually brace: "She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked." Because that is the *exact* sentence the books I left used to hand me, with a wink, as the sexy part. I was ready to be angry. And the book didn't wink. It let her have him — faultless, practiced, good — and then it gave me the sandwich. "Her body had spent itself on him and she remained unmoved." The ring of her lipstick on him and her own mouth bare in the mirror, and her eyes fine, because nothing had happened to her eyes. Everything done to her and done well and not one thread of her found. I have been trying to explain to people for years why the man who doesn't ask gives you *nothing*, and here it is, in a perfect room in Gstaad, with the safe man made perfect on purpose so there'd be nothing left to blame but the not-asking.

"Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." She had the whole vocabulary — the counting, the mouse, the welcome — and he still made her say it. That's the sentence I've wanted since the practice room, and it came from the one person who'd know. And "he was a better dancer than Pace" — so she's danced with him, of course she has — and Pace's lead is the lightest pressure, a thing you have to be listening for, and it leaves the rest to you. That's the top drawer. That's the empty palm at "Don't." That's what Vee has in her hands right now and walked out of a house over because it never came with the word. And Randi, gliding on marble, decides she's done with the work — and the work is the only thing that has ever once reached her. "Pace went straight to her. That was the whole terror of him." She wishes it wasn't true. Me too, honey.

And then — Vee's text. From Ohio. With minutes left in the year. To Randi. Not to him. She left his house crying two chapters ago and the person she reaches for at midnight is the one who's been harvesting her yeses, and she does it in the most Vee way there is: *i'm here if you want to talk. or not.* The *or not*. She leaves the door. She always leaves the door. And Randi sends back the fairytale — every word true, none of it the thing — and one honest line, and turns the phone facedown next to a man who means nothing. I had to put the book on my chest for a minute.

Here's what I can't settle. The brightening smile in the practice room was the worst thing anyone's done in this book, and I've now seen the inside of the woman who did it, and it isn't strategy. It's terror. "She was not brave enough to lose her." She's never had to be brave, she built a whole life so she'd never have to be, and Vee is the one thing she'd have to be brave *for*. That I understand down to the floor. But "let the thing keep the shape it was already taking" — she is still steering. She has just stopped calling it that. Let Vee arrive on her own, keep the brunches exactly as they are, keep the word under cover. That's the same deniable wink, just lonely now. I don't forgive the steering. I forgive the cold. I didn't know you could hold both at once until this chapter made me.

And the cold *is* the thing. Two chapters ago Vee pulled his shirt off in a dorm room and lay bare in it and it was worse and she stayed in it. Tonight Randi is warm along one seam and cold everywhere else and the cold is hers, she brought it in. Two women in the same book, a week apart, lying awake in the cold, and he's asleep in his own bed both times, gone down easy, a man with nothing grinding in him. He's never said it to Randi either. Has he ever said it to anyone? Because if the answer to Vee's question is that he doesn't spend the words on *anybody*, that's not a comfort, that's a worse question.

Where is he. What did he do with New Year's. Did he pick up the phone once. Did Sheri bring the pie. I need the next chapter to be him or her and I'm a little afraid it'll be the three of them.
