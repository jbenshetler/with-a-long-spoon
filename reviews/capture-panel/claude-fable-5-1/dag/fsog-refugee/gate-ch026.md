I put the book face-down on my chest at the kneeling and had to breathe for a second before I picked it back up. That's the whole chapter for me, that one image. He kneels every time — I've got that in my notes in his column, *kneeling every time* — and now she goes down onto one knee on the pale floor in her beautiful clothes and takes Vee's foot in both hands and doesn't reach for the box. She *looks at the toes.* And she knows what the toes are. She got that out of Vee across a mimosa four days ago — "you paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands," she said that, I remember it because it was true and it made me flinch — and now the feet are bare in *her* hands and she runs her thumb across the plum and says "Mm" and nothing else. And Vee's hand shoots out to her shoulder because the floor tipped. Sitting down. The floor tipped while she was sitting down.

And here's what I can't get past: I felt it too. That's the thing this book keeps doing to me that I told myself I was done letting books do. The thumb across the nail went up the length of me right alongside her, and I know exactly whose move it was, and I know she knows, and she didn't ask. Not with words, not with a silence that did it — he leaves a silence and the silence is honest; she leaves a silence and then bends back to the box. Vee had a hand on her shoulder and Randi smiled at her and *said nothing at all.* That's not the same thing as what he does, and I've spent twenty-six chapters learning the difference, and the book knows I have. I'd text a friend at one in the morning: *she did his thing to her in a shoe store and I wanted it and I want to throw the book across the room and I'm not going to.*

The credit card is the part I'm actually sick over, though, more than the foot. The foot was hot. The card was just true. I watched her stand in the first store — *her* store, she knows which racks lie — and find the tan one, the one that'd hold her a whole night, and talk herself into it out loud, and it was the most Vee she's been in three chapters. And then "same feet, baby, practically a marriage," and the tan heel is still in her hand when Randi says "which is exactly why you're not buying that," and there goes Vee's whole ground out from under her, and *she doesn't even answer.* She just follows her out. And then the boutique, where she stands "holding a quiet the exact shape of what was in her account," and Randi's beside her before it can set — and I've read that sentence in this book before, Randi getting there first, Randi got there first at the brunch too, on the sheet story, before Vee could hear the other version of it. And the debit goes back in the wallet. *The last careful thing in her give way.* She has nothing under it. She knows that while she signs. And then the sentence that made me put it down again: "The shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed." That's not her arithmetic. That's Randi's arithmetic, in Vee's head, in Vee's voice, and she can't hear the accent on it. The jacket told me — *nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over* — and I nodded like I knew what that meant, and it meant a twenty-one-year-old putting a month she can't pay on a card because a woman in a Mercedes said the word *lover* and knelt.

And Randi buying the loafers. A multiple, and never looked at the number, and wore them out the door. I don't think that was cruelty. I honestly don't. I think she's so far inside her own life she can't see that the girl beside her is white-knuckling a box she'd have to work a month to fill. And that's worse than cruelty, or it's the thing I'm always afraid is under the glamour when I read these — that the money isn't a flex, it's just weather to her, and Vee's standing out in it with no coat. "You can put it in the trunk, it's not going to spoil." God. And Vee *laughed*, the real one, bent at the middle, and thought she'd never had a friend like this, and I believe that she believes it and I believe that Randi wants it to be true, and neither of those things helps.

The shoe-store bit in the middle was so good it hurts in retrospect. The yeti shoe. Six-inch pink rhinestone platforms, "do these go with burgundy," grave and strained. The flat one brisk walk from becoming a sandal — and that's the one that got Randi, folded her over, "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give." I know that laugh, the book's shown me the thumb on the water glass and the bottle cap enough times that I know when Randi's not holding anything, and for that minute she wasn't. Two girls being silly over shoes and an older couple watching. That's the friend Vee wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting. It was on the page and it was real. And then the size, and it turned, and I watched Randi *gather* it — "gathering her bag and her sunglasses and the whole afternoon with them" — and I thought, she doesn't even have to plan it anymore, she just picks it up and carries it wherever she's going and Vee comes along.

"He didn't make this for you. He made it to you." I hate that she's right. I hate that Randi's the one who put the word on it, the word Vee cried over on the sun porch and couldn't find, because now the word's Randi's, it came out of Randi's mouth, and every time Vee thinks it she'll be hearing Randi say it. That's how Randi does it, I've watched it since the smoothie shop — she doesn't tell Vee things, she names things Vee already feels, and then she owns the name. "Ravish." "Lucky." And now "to." And she read the cut with two fingers up the seam to the bare shoulder and left her hand there, and Vee held still under it "the silk pressed where Randi had settled it." He measured her on a box in the brightest room in the house and she wasn't found wanting. Randi's reading the same seam in a cinderblock double and Vee holds still the same way. I notice the book making me notice that. I'd like to stop noticing.

Cassie's bed, made square, nobody home. Randi's eyes went over it first. That's all I get of Cassie this chapter and it's the only thing in the room that Randi's light didn't make smaller — it just made it *empty.* I keep waiting for four words from her and instead I get her corners.

And underneath all of it: I'm still holding the bowl with the one line through the sauce. Randi told him he was selfish, on your schedule, and he said *she chooses every step or it isn't anything*, and Randi poured her own glass. And now here's Randi not waiting on his schedule. Here's Randi with her thumb on the toe he was supposed to have. "Lover" — she handed Vee that word like the shoes, like a thing Vee couldn't afford and would take anyway. I don't think he knows about today. I don't think she'll tell him the way he'd tell it. And the induction's coming, the daydream Vee never said out loud to a living soul — walk in late, stop the room — and Randi just said it to her, word for word, easy, and Vee's face went warm because someone else had it. Nobody gave it to Randi. She just *knew*, or she built it, and I can't tell which, and that's the whole book in one sentence.

I'm going on. I'm going on with my stomach in a knot, which I think is the honest place to be reading from.
