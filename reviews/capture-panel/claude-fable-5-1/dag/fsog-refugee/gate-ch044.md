REACTION

I came into this one still shaking off the couch. "God, I want her." "For us." "Somebody has to get her used to my mouth." I'd put the book down on that and gone and stood in the kitchen for a while, and I thought the next chapter would either be Pace alone with her so I could breathe, or Randi across a table doing the thing she does, and I'd braced for both. Instead it's a cold lecture hall and the professor clicking the slide forward and back and I actually felt my shoulders drop. *Fine. A breather. Project week.* And then the book put four letters on a trackpad and I was not breathing.

MIRA. I read it a half-second before Vee did, I think, because I'd been handed "the loans kept people on, that's what the money was for" at the coffee and heard it as a girl repeating her father, and the tag came up and I went cold the way Randi went still. Not a motion — the withdrawal of motion. I know that stillness. That's the practice room stillness, the bistro stillness, the one she's always done *to* Vee while a story landed, and here she's the one it's landing on, in a glass room, with the door latched, and she can't get her face out of the way of it. "Cheating bastards." Two words aimed too near. That's the first thing Randi has said in this whole book that she didn't build first.

And here's what I'd text at one in the morning: Vee saw it. Vee saw the whole thing go across her and said nothing and slid the laptop over and gave her a stack of logistics to hold. The girl who's been drawn out, a confession at a time, over mimosas and glaze and "did you deserve it" — she's the one who listened this time, the way nobody ever has, and she listened by *not asking*. She kept Randi's secret from Randi. She didn't tell her then or after. I've been waiting forty chapters for someone to see Randi, and it turns out the one who does is the one Randi picked off a lawn with Pace's hand in hers, and she does it with more care than either of them has ever shown her. It's the kindest thing anyone's done in this book and the person it's done to has no idea she was looked at. That sentence — *Randi had no idea she'd been looked at* — I read three times. That's the whole series turned inside out in nine words.

And God, the irony is almost unbearable, and the book doesn't wink once. Randi told him two nights ago, flat as a hand on a table, "I know exactly what I'm doing. I have always known what I'm doing." And then her father's name sits over the worst point in the county on a government page and the thing she was going to prove — home, the bright counterexample — is the thing that proves Cassie right. The daughter of the money. The green Mercedes, the brunches she keeps reaching for the check on, the heels she knew to the quarter-inch — I'd never asked where it came from and now I have the short column of numbers and so does Vee, and Vee doesn't ask that either. She thinks *dealt a rotten thing by a man she'd had the luck to be the daughter of, no hand in it* — and that's Vee, that's exactly her, extending to Randi the grace of being an outlier you lift out and account for. She's doing the professor's method as mercy. And I'm sitting here knowing Randi's running a game on her with a man who went hard on a public lawn, and that *I* can't lift that out, and that Vee just paid into an account she doesn't know exists.

Cassie. Finally, Cassie and Randi in a room on the page, and of course Vee's between them, and of course Cassie can't see the screen. "My dad got laid off in 2020." No handle left out. She was right the whole way and the proof got closed with one keystroke and the only thing she got for it was a nod and "that's just the one that's easy to see." She doesn't know what she proved. She doesn't know whose father. She does the grunt and knows it and drinks nothing. I'm starting to think Cassie's the one person in this book who'd tell Vee the truth in the first register she had, and she's the one person nobody tells anything.

The gap: that's two chapters now with no Pace and Vee in a room, after the one where he asked "Is she all right?" and got "radiant" and said *that isn't the same answer* — which, honestly, is the one line keeping him in column one after that couch. And I felt the gap. I did. But not as a complaint. This chapter had the care on the page, it just ran the other direction for once, out of Vee and toward the person who least deserves it and most needs it, and that counts. I'll give the book one more without him, but it's cashing that check now.

What I'm scared of: Randi came back brighter than she'd been all afternoon. I've seen her do that. The beam coming up full after the catch in her breath. Now I know what's under it, and I know she'll go out to the gravel road and sit with her feet up and let him be the one to talk, and I don't know if she'll tell him about MIRA or whether she'll tell him Vee gave her the write-up — and if she tells him that, if she says *she saw and she didn't say*, I think that's the thing that moves the two of them. The jacket promised it. I think I just watched it start.
