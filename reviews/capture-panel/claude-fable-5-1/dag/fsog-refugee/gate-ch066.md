That's the whole man in one gesture: he took care of the thing *and* the thing under the thing, at whatever hour, while she was passed out in his undershirt, and he'll never mention it. Then the forehead, once, after he thought she was gone. He does it again. He spends when she can't see. "I missed you so much" was the biggest spend of his life and he made it with his voice cracked on one knee, and the rest of his spending is a kiss on a sleeping girl and a loaf of cinnamon raisin bread that didn't exist when the world ended. I'd text a friend: *he baked while she slept it off. BAKED.* And then I'd sit there with the phone in my hand feeling the catch in it, because I know what the bread is instead of.

Sheri's look got its encore and I loved it twice. "She can be like that" — flat, no flicker, and Vee laughing with her hand on her chest at the memory of being enjoyed. She's going to hand Randi the win with both hands and keep the look for herself, and I'm fond of her for it and I also watched her build the package in real time, which is what she does now — she crops, she arranges, she saves the best card. Then the kitchen. "I'd love to meet your other friends." And he says it *to the window.* He always says the costly ones to the window — "bring Theo" into the sink, "Sheri is my friend" to the glass. And then he turned around, which he doesn't do, and said it to her face: "My one friend. There isn't anyone else."

I put the book down on that line. Not to leave — to breathe. Because I was in that bed two chapters ago. I watched a woman kiss the center of his palm and say "she's not the only one in love" and he couldn't tell her she was wrong. There's a blanket on a chair in the room Vee just woke up in. And he looked at Vee across his own counter and said *there isn't anyone else*, and the terrible thing is I can't call it a lie. Randi isn't his friend. That's the honest hole in it. Whatever Randi is to him, it doesn't fit the word, and so he could say the sentence with his whole plain face and mean every syllable, and it's the biggest omission he's made in sixty-six chapters and he made it right after she'd put her cheek on his chest about Sheri. Vee heard "one friend" and her heart broke for him. I heard "one friend" and thought: *then what's the redhead, Peter.* And then he said "women misunderstand it, the friendship, it gets awkward," and I nearly laughed out loud, because one woman very much did not misunderstand it, she understood it so well she picked out a girl for the two of them.

And here's what's actually eating me. Watch what Vee does with "women misunderstand it." She finishes it for him. *Your* friendship. She fills the gap, sharpens it herself, decides what the missing word was, and then crosses the counter. That's the exact machine Randi runs on her — the sentence stopped at "you've got that good long coat" so Vee will finish it. She's been trained to complete other people's unfinished sentences in the most generous direction available, and now she does it for him unprompted, and he didn't even have to leave the sentence open on purpose. She's doing their work for them. I don't think she knows that's a skill she's acquired. I think she thinks it's love. It might be both.

The hug. Sheri's hug — whole weight committed, cheek flat to the chest — Vee gave him Sheri's hug because she learned last night what one looks like. And he didn't make it better. So I'm sitting here warm all through and angry in one spot, which is how this book likes to leave me. He gave her more of himself in one kitchen than in the last ten chapters combined and left out the one living person who's inside both their lives. Vee thinks she got the whole picture this morning. Tuesday. Randi, who's about to be insufferable about the blonde and then go quiet with her hand in her lap when Vee says "he has one friend." I want to see her face do that. I want to see whether she hears what I heard. I think she will. I think it'll cost her something, and I think she'll put her face back on.
