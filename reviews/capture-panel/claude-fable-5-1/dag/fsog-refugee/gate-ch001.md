REACTION

Okay. Okay. I put the book down after "There you are, Randi" and had to actually walk to the kitchen and stand there for a minute.

Because for about thirty pages I thought I was reading Vee. The jacket told me Vee, so I was reading a nervous girl with an eating thing and a mother and a sorority, and I was falling for Pace on her behalf — and then the name lands and it's *Randi*. The glamorous friend. The one who's supposedly going to steer Vee toward him "laughing, as if by accident." So this is the two of them, before there's a plan, before Vee is even a person in this book. And the last thing I see is Randi standing in a bathroom mirror deciding she is never coming out from behind her face again. That's the woman who's going to take Vee to lunch. Oh, I'm scared. Good scared. I feel like I've been handed the murder weapon in the first scene and told to hold it for four hundred pages.

But let me talk about him, because he is the reason I read this genre and the reason I keep being disappointed by it, and this chapter gave me the thing. He backs off after the hello kiss. *That was what he did, he backed off and let her decide.* He asks for her word before he's touched her. "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset. Tell me anytime." I have read so many men who take a safeword like it's a formality, a box to tick before the real story starts, and this one keeps circling back — *How's your back. Hands and shoulders. Tell me.* — and she's *exasperated* with him about it, and that exasperation is the sexiest thing in the chapter, because it means the checking is real enough to be annoying. And then the bathroom. She locks the door, which she has never done, and he sits there and doesn't knock, and he doesn't knock because of a woman named Daphne whose door he once went through, and it cost him. I didn't expect a backstory in one line to hit me that hard. A man who's already learned the thing most of these heroes never learn.

And he's hard the entire night and sets it down. *That's not what this night was for.* I actually said "oh" out loud.

The bench itself — the reveal, the way she reads it with her hands before she understands it, "Where I've been laying my underwear?" — that undid me a little. The whole scene was in my body, not on the page. The apple slices cut thin and turned cut-side up because he noticed she eats them faster that way. The pink strap marks fading on her shoulders. Her heat returned to her "in the wrong place" on her spine. The thread of it breaking between his fingers. I don't usually get pulled this hard by a scene where she's facedown and can't see him, because that's often where the coercion sneaks in, but she *asked* for this — "What are we doing tonight," "Show me" — and he kept coming around to her face. Kept crouching down to her upside-down face and saying hi. Every time he did that I relaxed a little more.

The one place I braced: when he decides her "please" isn't the right please yet. The waiter-voice line. A man grading the authenticity of a woman's begging is exactly the move that, in a worse book, turns into "I know what you really want." I felt my jaw go. But she'd handed him the evening, she had the word, she didn't use it — and what he wanted wasn't obedience, it was *her*, the one behind the face. And she knows that's what he found. That's why she's in the bathroom. That's why the thumbnail thing. God, the thumbnail thing. Pressing the bottle cap into her nail bed without knowing she's doing it. I want to reach into the book and take the cap.

So here's what I'd text at 1 a.m.: I think I just watched the best sex scene I've read in years happen to the wrong woman. Or — to a woman who can't survive it. He found her and she flinched at *you're beautiful* and locked the door and decided he can have the face forever. And then the bench "waiting for the next time." And somewhere out there is Vee, twenty-one and starving, who maybe *can* survive being seen — and Randi's going to hand her to him. Is that the game? Is Randi going to give Pace the girl who can take what Randi couldn't? Because that is a devastating premise and I don't know yet whether to be furious with her or heartbroken for her, and the fact that I'm already both is why I'm not putting this down.

Also I need him to tell her he likes her feet. He hasn't yet. That was such a small, specific, tender thing to withhold and I'm going to be waiting for it.
