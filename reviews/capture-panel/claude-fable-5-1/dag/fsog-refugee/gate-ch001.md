REACTION

Okay. Okay. I read that in one breath and then I went back and read the kitchen again, because I needed to check whether I'd imagined how good the kitchen was. I hadn't. A man who slices the apple thin because she'll eat thin, and lays it cut-side up because he *noticed* she eats faster that way, and is *sorry* about the thing behind her face that polices it — that's the whole genre I came here for, done right, before anybody's taken off a stitch. He sees the egg-white mother in one line. He turns the chair when she circles her finger and it *pleases* him to be directed and he keeps it off his face. I was already gone by the apple.

And then the bench. I've read fifty versions of the furniture reveal and every one of them has the man standing there smug while she's frightened, and the book wants me to find the fright hot. This one — she turns around with her nose crinkled and says "Show me." *She* says it. And he still doesn't rush: the undressing on one knee, the cardigan folded, the blouse folded, him pressing his face to her stomach through the silk before he touches a button. Then "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset." The whole scene I kept waiting for the moment where the book would wink at me and it never came. He checks her back, her hands, her shoulders, she snaps "they're *fine*, Pace" and he says "just checking" and kisses her forehead and I honestly laughed out loud at midnight because that's it, that's the exact note, exasperated and safe. He takes her heels off *mid-scene*. He crouches at her upside-down face and says "Hi." Jesus.

The edging killed me. I want to be honest about that — the "No" when she said please the waiter-way, the way he explained to himself that it wasn't the right please yet, and then the please that "came from below" — I felt that in my own body in a way I haven't from a book in a long time. The whole thing about the *wanted* position being a script, and the bench not being there, and her hands worrying the grips looking for a handle that had always been there — that's the most exact thing I've ever read about why dominance works on a woman who's spent her whole life being the one looked at. And it never once became cruelty. He wanted to hear the sound again and he admitted it to himself and that admission is the hottest line in the chapter.

Then he said her name and the floor dropped out of my stomach. *Randi.* Not Vee. This is the glamorous friend. This is the woman who's going to steer Vee into his path laughing, "as if by accident." So the two people with the plan are lovers, and this — this is what they are to each other, and the book has handed it to me first so that I'll know, for the whole rest of the volume, what Vee doesn't. I knew the jacket said I'd be ahead of her. I didn't know it would feel like *this* to be ahead of her.

And the bathroom. God, the bathroom. She locked the door — first time ever in his house — and I was so braced for him to knock, and the Daphne paragraph came and he *didn't*, and I think that's the moment I decided I'd follow this man anywhere, which is of course exactly what the book wants and I'm fine with it. But then her at the mirror. "He could have the face. The face was very good. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." Quiet as a deadbolt going over. I actually said "no" out loud. He's sitting out there deciding between *do you want to stay* and *do you want dinner first*, thinking it was a remarkably good evening, and she's already gone. He got all the way in and she's never letting him in again, and he doesn't know, and she comes back out smiling the photograph smile and he smiles back *warm, unguarded* — I can't. And the bottle cap under her thumbnail. That one scared me. Not the bench; the bottle cap. That's a girl who's done that before.

So here's what I'm suspicious of, and here's what I'd text you at 1 a.m.: this is why Vee. Isn't it. Randi can't be seen, so she's going to hand him somebody who can be. She's going to sit across lunch tables from a starving twenty-one-year-old and watch her get fed, and tell herself it's a game, and the jacket already told me it stops being one. And I'm going to watch Pace do *this* — the apple, the folded cardigan, "there you are" — to a girl who doesn't know a woman she loves has arranged it. The jacket says every yes was freely given and that was the problem, and I believe it now in a way I didn't on the shelf. I'm not sure yet whether I'm going to be able to forgive these two. I'm very sure I want to find out.
