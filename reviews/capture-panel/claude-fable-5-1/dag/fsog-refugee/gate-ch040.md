**REACTION**

Oh, she swims. Since she was twelve. Three hundred-odd pages and I didn't know that about her, and the way it comes in — "any more than she had ever once decided the second verse of a hymn" — I believed it instantly, that this is older than Pace, older than Randi, older than the dress. There's a lane she took her first week back and the butterfly man and the flip-turn woman arrange themselves around her without a word. She has a *place* nobody made for her. Nobody built it, nobody cleared a drawer for it, nobody knelt to buckle it. She just took the third lane in.

And here's the thing that got me, that I didn't expect to need: for the first time in this book her body isn't out ahead of her. It's not drawing up under thin jersey while Randi talks, it's not telling him what she won't say, it's not soaked over a story in a piano closet. "Her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here. It only worked." I read that line and let my breath out like I'd been holding it since the practice room. I didn't know how tired I was of watching her body get read by other people until she was in a lane with the black line going under her and nobody reading it at all. That's hers. The green-and-white suit that's got nowhere left to go on her — she looked at the water instead, and I loved her for not making it a scene.

What she took in with her: *Do you love him.* Cassie's. Flat, with the book on her chest. I've been begging for Cassie's four words for ten chapters and it turns out Cassie already said them and I didn't count them because they were a question. But look — it's the thing Vee's carrying. Not his hand, not Randi's mouth with glaze on it, not the drawer. The one person telling the truth at her volume finally got something through, and it rode her for six hundred-pieces. And she knows the answer. She says so — "the answer sitting plainly in her, easy to reach, easy to say" — and it came out *maybe*. She whispered *Peter* to a sleeping man and said *maybe* to her awake roommate. I'd text that at 1 a.m. with no words after it.

And then the four strokes. Around the seventh piece, in the good quiet, she asks it again — is he that good or did she build him out of wanting him, out of two months of being looked at like that. She asked this on the dance floor and let the floor take it. Now she asks it in the water and the wall takes it. Four strokes. Twice now the book's let her almost put her hand on the question and twice something came and rolled her off it, and I don't think that's accident, I think that's the book telling me she's got the thread in her fingers and keeps dropping it, and that someday she won't. The thing I'm suspicious of is that she's going to finish that thought on a day she can't afford to.

*Did he mind.* By the fourth piece it was nothing. Good. It was always nothing; he gave her the top drawer. But I noticed it was the first to go and *do you love him* was the one that stayed. That's the right order.

She walked up the hill in the dark to her own bed on a Tuesday, thinking about a sandwich, with her head quiet and not going looking for it. No Pace, no Randi, no heat, nobody's hand on her anywhere — and it's the first chapter in a long while where I wasn't frightened for her. Two chapters since the drawer. I can feel the gap starting, faintly. I'll give it one more.
