Oh, the morning after. I'd forgotten how much I love a morning-after chapter when the book has earned it, and this one earned it last night with the sheet on the floor and the satin folded in thirds. Her hair its own wild country, no brush in the house that'd go through it — I laughed out loud at that, because of course there isn't, he's a man who sews and owns one hairbrush. And the smile coming back at every red light. I know that drive. I've made that drive.

Here's what got me, though, and it's small and it's the whole chapter: she didn't pack a bag. *To pack a bag the night before would have been to say the thing to herself, plainly.* That's Vee to the bone. She can stand on a box in green satin and press her breast to his cheek on purpose and say *trade* — but she can't tell herself on Thursday night what Friday is for. The body goes first and the mouth catches up a beat late, same as "famished," same as "ask and then ravish." The book has been so consistent about this that I trust it the way I trust a friend's tells.

And then the zipper. God, the zipper. Half down, and she stops, and I knew before she did — *nothing under the skirt*. Three feet from Cassie. The story she's been ladling out clean, surface closing without a hole, and the next thing off wouldn't be a story. I held my breath there, honestly, more than in some of the sex scenes. That is the most Vee sentence in the book.

She clocked the plum polish, which Vee bought for a reason she wouldn't look at, and Cassie looked at it for her and didn't say the reason.
