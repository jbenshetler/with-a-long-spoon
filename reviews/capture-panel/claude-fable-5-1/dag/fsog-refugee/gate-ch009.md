REACTION

Okay. Okay. *That* is the chapter. That's the one I've been circling this book for.

"May I choose?" — three words over a dog-eared menu and I actually put the book against my chest for a second like Vee with her phone. Because that's it, that's the thing I keep trying to explain to people who think this genre is all Christian Grey buying you a laptop: he doesn't order for her. He asks if he can order for her. And when she says yes he *doesn't take it lightly* — he goes back to the page and weighs it and asks the one thing he needs to know. The control makes room for her. The whole meal is him running things and her handing it over and it costs her nothing, it gives her something, she eats without watching herself eat for maybe the first time in her life. I've read fifty versions of the man-orders-for-the-woman scene and this is the first one where I felt her get *bigger* under it instead of smaller.

And then the kiss. God, the kiss. She says the rule out loud — *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days* — and I braced the way she braced, because I know this genre and I know what's supposed to come next: the lean, the low voice, the "are you sure," the patient little wait that isn't patient at all. And he just smiles like she told him something nice about herself and *turns to walk her home.* No sulk. No pressure disguised as respect. He gives up the inch completely. And then she stops him. Her feet, her hand pulling taut, her "but." And even then — even when she's the one who closed the distance — he leaves the last inch. She kisses him small and he answers exactly as small and eases back and waits. Twice she chooses him with him standing there doing nothing but being available to be chosen. I have been hunting for that scene for years. It's a no that mattered, and then a yes that was hers, and the yes was hotter *because* the no was real. I'm not even embarrassed about how I'm feeling about a closed-mouth kiss under a tree.

Here's what's doing my head in, though, and this is the 1 a.m. text: I know where he was "somewhere in himself" in that lobby. I know because two chapters ago Randi was on his lap drinking his wine and saying *tell* into his ear, and he told her. About the froyo. About the cup Vee built like nobody'd told her when to stop. So every tender thing in this chapter — the mint tea, the bread torn with care for the heat, the hand — I'm reading it with that couch behind it. He's going to go home. She's going to lock the deadbolt and straddle him and say *tell me* and he's going to say "she said she doesn't kiss a man she's known two days, and then she did." And I don't know whether that ruins it or makes it worse in the exact way the jacket promised — *merciless in its tenderness.* Because what I'm holding onto is this: last time, he *didn't* hand everything over. He kept the froyo line — the "pretend I didn't say that" — he thought *it's hers* and didn't give it to Randi. That's the thread. If he keeps something of tonight back, I'm still in. If he narrates the kiss to Randi like the cup of yogurt, I'm going to feel it in my stomach.

And the feeding. I can't stop noticing it. Randi gets the apple sliced thin, cut sides up, because she'll only eat what's permitted. Vee gets a table full of things she can't pronounce and a hollow that's been empty "a lot longer than since noon." He feeds hungry girls. Two women starving in different ways and one man who's figured out that the way in is the mouth. That's either the most tender thing in the book or a method, and the honest answer is it's both, and the book knows it's both, and the book is making me sit in it. Christine knowing him, softening when he walked in — that got me too. He's a man other people are glad to see. Randi's sisters don't know his last name.

Small things I loved: the elevator doors closing on her because she was busy looking at him. Cassie — "That's not a boy. That's a man." — the one person in this book who says exactly what she sees and never a word more, and I'm starting to feel that "Goodbye, Vee" from chapter 4 like a stone in my shoe. Whatever the right-hand thing is, I like that Vee doesn't know either. I like that she lost the thread of what he was saying and nothing bad happened. "The bill of a thing coming due" — that line. That's the whole girl.

I'm keen. I'm scared for her. I want the next one right now, and I want it to be Pace's front room with the deadbolt going over so I can find out what kind of man he is when the one who's asking is Randi.
