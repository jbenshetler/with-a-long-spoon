REACTION

Okay. *May I choose.* I actually put the book down on my chest for a second and looked at the ceiling, because that's it — that's the thing I've been hunting through this whole shelf for years and mostly not finding. He doesn't explain the menu to her. He doesn't do the little tour of what she doesn't know. He watches her be funny about her own ignorance and lets her enjoy it, and then he offers to carry it, and he asks *first*, and she gets to say yes. And then — this is the part — he doesn't take it lightly once she's said yes. He goes back to the page and weighs it. He asks the one question that matters. The consent isn't the checkbox before the fun; it's the fun. I could feel the relief go through her when she handed it over, and I felt it go through me too, and I'm a little annoyed at how much a plate of hummus did to me.

And the eating. God, the eating. I didn't expect the food to be the sexiest thing in the chapter, but it was — the bright thing and the dark thing, one to wake you and one to fold you under. She's been starving in every sense since page one and he just... feeds her. Doesn't comment. Doesn't watch her eat. Lets her not watch herself. She loses the thread of what he's saying and nothing bad happens. After eight chapters of this girl doing the constant small work of keeping up her end, the line about how restful it was to not follow the words went straight into me. That's what I want from these books and can never quite say — not just being wanted, but being let *rest* inside the wanting.

Then the kiss. She says her rule, and he doesn't lean. No charm turned up. No patient little wait for her to change her mind — the book actually names the three moves I've hated in fifty other books and has him do none of them. He smiles like she's told him something true about herself and starts them walking. And *she's* the one who stops. Her hand goes taut. She makes the exception. I have read a hundred versions of that scene where the man's "respect" is a tactic with a timer on it and you can feel the author winking, and this isn't that — or at least Vee's body doesn't experience it as that, and I'm inside Vee's body, so neither do I.

Which is exactly the problem, and I know it. Two chapters ago I watched Randi straddle him on the couch with a mouthful of wine going *tell, tell,* getting wet hearing about this girl's frozen yogurt cup. I know what the bright thing and the dark thing are for. I noticed the right hand — always the right, never the other, a rule he keeps without thinking — and I don't know what it means yet but I know it's a tell, and Vee's filing it as one more charming thing about him. And Cassie's *goodbye* is still sitting in my ear from chapter 4. So I'm holding two things that don't fit in one hand: this is the most cleanly consensual courtship I've read in years, and every yes she gives is being collected. The jacket told me that was the point. Fine. It's working. It's making me a little sick and I'm turning the page anyway.

What I'm watching for: whether the book lets Pace's tenderness *be* the tenderness or whether it's going to make me pay for having believed it. Because right now I believe it. He shaved for her. He wore the boots he already looks after. Randi's face on the couch, the smile a beat late over a want too big for it — that moved me too, and I didn't expect to feel for her after the bench. Three people and I'm already fond of all of them, which means somebody's going to hurt me. At 1 a.m. I'd text: *she said no and he just walked, and then SHE stopped him, and I don't know if I've ever wanted a kiss to land more, and I'm scared of what it costs her.*
