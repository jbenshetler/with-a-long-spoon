Okay. Okay. I'm putting the phone face-down because if I text anyone right now it's going to be in all caps.

This is the chapter. This is the one the whole shelf I walked away from kept promising and never delivered — a woman standing in nothing but the panties she chose with hope, in a room a man arranged for her hours before she got there, and the thing he does with all that power is *write a number down and step back*. Closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil. He built a rhythm out of asking without asking and the book made me feel it from inside her skin — the withdrawal was the part that undid me, exactly like it undid her. While his hands are on her she has a job. When he walks away to the pad she has nothing to do but be seen. I've read fifty "he looked at her hungrily" scenes and not one of them understood that being looked at from across the room by a man with a pencil is worse and better than being grabbed.

And then the panties. I was dying with her. Really — I was lifting the blanket with her and looking down and bargaining, *not at conversational distance*, and I hated her mother for the word and I wanted to shake Vee and tell her he's going to be glad, he's going to be GLAD, and I didn't actually know that, I was scared too, because the book had set me up so well for the "kind careful nothing" that I couldn't rule it out. And when she opened her eyes and he hadn't looked down — he'd been on his knees with his chin tipped all the way back waiting for her face — I made a noise. Out loud. He gave her the one thing she's never had, which is a man choosing her eyes over the evidence, and then the evidence stopped being shame and started being hers. Her chin came up. She rose on her toes. *Hmm*, and the little frown at her heel, and she came back down laughing. That's the whole book's promise paid in one page: the shame turning into heat, not because he told her it was fine but because he never once treated it as something that needed telling.

The pre-aroused line. I laughed with my hand over my mouth. She made him laugh and she stumped him *standing there like that*, and she knew it, and watching her figure out she has a kind of power she was never issued — and then spend it, badly, gloriously, the breast against his cheek on purpose, "Oops. Sorry" — I was so proud of her I could have cried. And him going red. Him *fighting the grin and losing*. A man who can take a woman apart on a bench and he goes red because a girl pressed her breast into his face. That's the contradiction I keep hunting for between tender and dominant and once again it just isn't there. Then the pin, the bead of blood, him kissing it before he'd decided to — and "Keep still," low, a beat late, after a silence she *watched him spend deciding*. That's the hottest two words in the book so far and nobody's naked but her. He chose the dress. He chose doing right by her over the yes she was all but pouring into his hands. And then he let himself have the small things — the palm down her spine, the finger at the back of her knee, the long warm breath below her navel — and the book is honest that those weren't about posture and that they both knew it. I'll be honest too: that's the one stretch where he isn't asking. He's reading her instead, and he's reading right, and she started it, so I'm not calling foul. But I noticed. I always notice.

Now the cold part, because there's always a cold part with this book and I love it for that. The heat turned up early — I *knew* that detail from the other side of the wall; I knew it before Vee walked in, I knew why the house is always too warm. The riser in the corner, finished like fine furniture, "of course he'd built it." Of course he had. And Vee up there thinking he picked her, that he walked right past Randi, *Randi!*, without even looking — and me knowing Randi pointed. That line went into me like the pin went into her ribs. She's never been found wanting and she's standing on a box that was built for her by two people, and she thinks it's one. The next time Randi says *tell*, what does he keep? He kept the truck. Does he keep the satin? Does he keep her chin coming up? I'm reading his silences now and I need the next one.

Also, quietly: he said "Do you want to get started?" and waited for her hands to move, and when she stood there he let her stand there. That's the consent I left the genre over. It's on the page. Every yes was freely given — I know, I know that's the problem, the jacket told me — but God, it's been a long time since a book let a woman give one.
