Oh, that's a small chapter and I'd have read it twice. I did read it twice, actually — the last three lines.

"Your tart's holding up." I'm not okay about that line. She hands him the most exposed thing she's said to anyone in the whole book so far — *this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time* — and she's already wincing, already asking him to let her take it back, and he does neither of the two things a man does with that. He doesn't pretend it away and he doesn't swell it up into a Moment where he gets to be the wonderful one. He just walks. He lets it sit there and be true. And then he gives her a way to look down instead of at him, and what she sees is that the thing she built too big, the thing she didn't police, is still standing. That's the whole book in a cup of frozen yogurt and I know it and I don't care, it got me right under the ribs.

And the mother going quiet. *That's plenty, that's enough now* — and today, nothing. She lets the cup get away from her and bites her lip and then finds she doesn't care. After the apple in Chapter 1, after Randi's egg-white mother and *I can't eat that* on the quad, I felt that like a hand opening. Two women in this book who don't let themselves have, and one of them just did, by accident, on a first date, and nobody made her. That's the appetite the jacket promised and it's arriving as a leaning cone of froyo and I'm a little embarrassed how much I felt it.

He's doing the thing I hunt for. *May I call you* last chapter, and now this — asking like her grandfather asked, waiting through the whole sentence, not taking his turn. She "wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on." God. I've read three hundred books where the man is the thing she waits on. This is the inversion I actually want and it's so quiet you could miss it.

Now the part I'd text you at 1 a.m., because I can't just be happy and you know that about me: Randi took the phone out of her hand. Just took it. And I know — I *know* — who she was texting under the table last chapter, thumbs quick, "some boy." She's reading Pace's message to Vee having probably already read the draft. *He wants to see you naked* — she's not guessing, she's grinning at a thing she built. And *do you think she'll like the bench* is still sitting in my head from that lawn, and now Vee is walking the long way in the gold light with the man who built it, thinking she's the reason his smile crossed the room. She is. She's also the third person at a table for two and doesn't know it. The book is doing exactly what the jacket said — I'm ahead of her, and I'm falling anyway, and I resent how well it's working.

Here's what's keeping me on the right side of it, for now: nothing Pace did in this chapter is a move. Nothing. He paid before she found her wallet, he held the door with his back, he waited out her sentences. If there's a plan, the plan is apparently *be genuinely good to her*, and I don't have a rule against that. What I'm watching for — what I'll leave over — is the first time the plan needs her not to know something in order to keep working, and she says yes to a thing she'd have said no to with the full picture. That hasn't happened. Randi's silence is the shape of it, though. I can feel where the door is.

The gap I track — I'm counting. Vee and Pace have now been alone twice, both in public, both with all their clothes on, and I want the door to close behind them. Not because I need the heat yet — the warmth is carrying it — but because I need to know what he is when it's just her. Chapter 1 told me what he is with Randi. I want to see if he asks Vee the same way.
