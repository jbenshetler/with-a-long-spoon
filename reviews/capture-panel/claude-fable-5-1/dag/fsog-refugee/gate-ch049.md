She took the shirt off.

That's the text I'd send at one in the morning with nothing after it, because four chapters ago I watched her put that flannel into his hands and say *wear it for me, give it back Sunday smelling like you*, and he pulled it on over the shirt he already had on and I nearly cried in a kitchen that isn't mine. That was the warmest thing in the book. And this chapter walked it back one layer at a time — over her own shirt for the hall, then bare under it the way she wears it when nobody can see, then off, down in a heap where a dropped thing lands — and every layer was true and I felt every one of them come off. "It was warm because she was warm." God. The heat in it is her own, given back. He's across town gone down easy. She's holding a smell in a cold room. I've done that. I've done exactly that with a man's sweatshirt and I'd forgotten until this page made me remember.

The phone. I was braced for the small text — *you up?*, something she'd hate herself for — and she looked at the empty field and knew there wasn't a message that gets the shape of what you are to someone out of them, and put it facedown. That's her. That's the girl in the stall who wouldn't say yes over a story. She won't beg in a text box either. I'm proud of her and it doesn't help her at all.

And here's what I can't stop hearing, because I was on the couch in chapter forty-three and she wasn't. "Easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." She thinks that. She thinks the silence at the center is just a man who can't say a word. I know the silence has a shape in it — I know what he said with his hand flat on Randi's belly, *she consents every time or I'm done*, I know *for when she's ready* — and I know he gave Randi "you are extraordinary" after thinking about it because she'd know the difference, the exact same way he gave Vee "I love how good we are together." He hands each of them a true sentence that isn't the sentence. I've watched him do it twice now and the second time I understood it was a method. Vee is lying naked in the cold because she can feel the method and can't see it, and I can see it, and there's nothing I can do from here but turn the page.

Cassie breathing below. Two inches of window. Vee wore the shirt *over* her own so that if Cassie looked in the morning there'd be nothing to see — she's hiding the shirt from the one person who's been counting the bed since October. And now the bed's full and she's the one not sleeping, and Cassie doesn't know that either. The book keeps setting those two a foot apart and not letting either of them reach.

The only thing pulling against me: I have now read "everything he does and not the one thing he won't say" in three chapters running — the porch, the dark after Chili's, and this. This is the best of the three by a mile, because it's done in cloth and cold and not in her head, but I felt the sentence arrive and thought *I know, honey, I know*. The book's earned the grind. It can't keep sitting in it. Someone has to say something to someone — her to Cassie, Randi to her, him to anyone — and I'd like it to be soon, because the girl in the top bunk is running out of layers.
