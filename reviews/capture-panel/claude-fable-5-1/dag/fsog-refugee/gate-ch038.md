Oh, she swims. Since she was twelve, in a suit that gave up on her years ago, third lane in from the wall without anybody arranging it. Forty chapters and the book just now hands me the thing she does with her body that isn't for anyone — and I read the first hundred and thought, that's his squat bar. "Her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here. It only worked." That's the face. That's the exact face he told her he makes at the bar and makes with her, and she's got one too, in chlorine, and neither of them knows the other has it yet. I'd text a friend that at 1 a.m. and she'd say *it's a girl swimming laps* and I'd say you don't understand, he lifts and she swims, they've both got a room where the noise stops, and the book put them in it on separate pages.

And the *maybe.* She knows. She lay in bed after Cassie and had the answer "sitting plainly in her, easy to reach," and it came out of her mouth as maybe, and she doesn't know why, and I do: because she said *Peter* to a sleeping man and he's never said anything to a woman who was awake. She can live inside the everything-he-does, she told me last chapter, and not go hungry for the saying — and then Cassie asked one flat question and the hunger showed. I felt that in my own chest. That's how it goes. You're fine until somebody asks.

Here's the thing that got me, though, and it's a trick of where the book put this chapter. She's swimming with *did he mind* in the lane with her, the counter, the brush and the pins and the earrings, "which she had been turning over since Saturday." And I already know. I've been in that bathroom the next Friday. I've opened the top drawer. I know he folded his whole ordered self down into the one drawer and gave her the easy one, the one your hand goes to first, and she's out here on a Tuesday doing a two-thousand-yard set over a question that's already been answered with a dish for her pins. I wanted to reach into the water and tell her. That's a kind of ache I don't get from this genre often — not will-he-won't-he, but *he already did, honey, you just haven't opened the drawer.*

And the four strokes. "Whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting." She gave it four strokes and the wall came. I sat up at that, because that's *my* question about Randi — whether Randi's building a Vee that Pace never fell for — turned around and pointed at him by Vee herself. She's not stupid. She knows two months of being looked at like that can make a man out of the looking. I don't think she's wrong to ask it and I don't think she's right to worry, and the book let her ask it and move on, which is exactly what you do with that question in a pool. You can't hold it on the stroke. It gets taken off, word by word, "until what was left was the shape underneath."

Nobody touched her this chapter. No Randi, no sugar on anyone's lip, no hand anywhere — and I noticed I was grateful. After the brunch I needed her alone somewhere Randi's eyes couldn't follow, and the book gave her seventy-five minutes in a lane where "nobody was counting but her." The last time somebody counted in this book it was Randi, over a knee, out loud. This count is hers. Sixty-one, sixty-two. Hand in front of the shoulder.

She went up the hill thinking about a sandwich with her head quiet and didn't go looking for it. That's the most at-peace I've seen her since the cherry pits. I don't trust it to last — Randi hasn't been in a room since the pastry she never touched, and the string's still got two ends — but I'll take the quiet. It cost me nothing. The care's on the page even when he isn't.
