REACTION

Well, that's a cold shower, and I think it was supposed to be. I came out of chapter one still warm — the water bottle he'd left in advance, "I won't be upset, tell me anytime," the fifteen minutes of just being the thing she sat against — and then the book walked me straight into a sorority common room at nine-fifty on a Saturday and made me stand there while Brooke did to Randi, with a smile, what I have watched women do to each other my whole life. I know that room. I've been the girl on the stairs. "How's the paper coming?" and the not-standing and "I kept thinking you'd said *Chase*." Nobody raised their voice, nobody said anything you could quote back at them, and Randi got demoted a notch and a half in under four minutes and then *dismissed*, and the dismissal was the worst part. I actually felt my shoulders come up. That's the part that's funny in a not-funny way — chapter one had a man with his palm coming down on her and every second of it felt safe, and this chapter had three girls with coffee and I wanted to get her out of there.

And she lied. Of course she lied, and she lied *badly*, a beat late every time, and I felt that too, because last night she ran out of the apparatus and it hasn't come back online yet. Watching her try to boot the face up in front of an audience that can tell — Sloane "watching without watching," Kinsey's laugh above pitch — after we'd just seen what the face costs her, that hurt more than I expected a scene about a spreadsheet to hurt.

Then the room. I have to be honest about the line that got me, and it's not the one I'd have guessed. It's the duvet. "It was not — it was a duvet." She had the cashmere at his house, the one *she brought*, because he keeps the house too warm and she still gets cold after, and she went to a cold room and pulled up something adequate and lay there with her shoes on. That's the whole book in one object and I want to shake her.

Now the thing I'm suspicious of. "*He's hard to say no to*, she heard, in her room, in a different key." I sat up when I read that. Because I have read so many books that would use exactly that sentence to retroactively turn a night like the bench into something done *to* her, and I would put this book down if it tried. It didn't — not yet. She said "show me." She gave him her word before he asked her to use it. She can't sing in that key and the book says so. But I'm watching. That sentence is a door and I want to see which way the author walks through it. If Randi starts telling herself, or telling someone else, that she was steered — I'll know it's a lie, because I was in the room, but I'll need the book to know it too.

And that's the other thing, the 1 a.m. text. I've got the jacket. I know Randi is the "glamorous new friend" who steers Vee toward Pace, laughing, as if by accident. Two chapters in there is no Vee, and I'm not complaining, because I think I've just watched the plan get born. She went behind the face "not ever," and she's lying under a duvet that isn't his, and the next move — I can feel it — is to find someone she can hand to him so she never has to come out again herself. Give him a girl to see, so he stops looking at *her*. That's not glamorous. That's a woman running. And I already like him too much for what's coming to be easy.

One chapter without them in a room together and I noticed. That's new for me this early. Small, but I noticed.
