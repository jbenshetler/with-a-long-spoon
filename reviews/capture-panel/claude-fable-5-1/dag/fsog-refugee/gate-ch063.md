REACTION

Okay. So the book heard me. Three chapters of me writing "the salt" and "the small light left on" in my notes, and it gives her a Thursday night alone in his shirt with the window shut and lets her go all the way to one breath from her own mouth. And then it doesn't let her. And I'm not even mad, because that stop is *honest* — it's the mirror again, the thing she couldn't finish on purpose in December, only this time it isn't on purpose. "Nothing in the room told her why." That's the truest sentence about shame I've read in this whole series. The wanting's there, the smell's there, his face is there, calm and glad, and her hand just won't travel the last inch. Her mother's voice didn't even have to speak. It's in the hinge.

The way it's built from inside is why I'm still here. The clench with nothing inside her — "her body had given the same answer twice" — the shine running to the knuckles and the old verdict landing *on the amount*, not even the act, the amount. Excessive. The shameful plenty. God. I know that girl. Every woman I know knows that girl. And then she lifts her hand expecting the stink the boys promised and it isn't there — "a hothouse with its doors shut" — and I actually said *oh* out loud, because that's the whole book in one gesture: she was told what she'd find and she went and checked and the telling was a lie. She just can't do the last step yet. "Her mouth was still empty." I'd text that to a friend at 1 a.m. with no context and she'd know.

And the flannel. Thin by Thursday, "inside the collar there was still enough of him to find." Every button open. She wears him to touch herself and the face she builds is his — not the mouth, the *look*, "no apology in him, no recoil," the man who looked at the whole slick fact of her like it was fine. That's the thing I've been saying about him since the stop in 56. He read which no was which and wiped once and said okay, and now she's alone in a room running that "okay" against twenty years of her mother and it's *winning*, slowly. He's not in the chapter and he's doing more for her than he's done in some chapters he was in. That's what I mean by the care being on the page even when they're apart. His face is the permission and she's learning to take it from memory.

Here's what I'm suspicious of, though, and it's the thing that made my stomach drop a little: whose fingers are in the last paragraph. Not his. "A mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers." She's in Pace's shirt, coming on Pace's face, and the question she falls asleep on is what Randi's looked like. "Had Randi's looked like this? Sticky too. Shining." I was that woman once — dressing for a Tuesday, going over in a stall on a face and calling it his — and I know how long that holds, and this is the chapter where it stopped holding. The *I don't* did this. Randi laid down one flat true card across the cloth and it followed Vee home and got into bed with her. I'll give Randi credit I didn't think I'd give her — that was the straightest thing she's said in this book, no sentence left for Vee to finish. But it's also the first time Vee's wanting for her has gone somewhere Vee can't crop. She can't call *that* about Pace.

And speaking of cropping — the window. She shut it so the room would hold everything, and then she opened it so Cassie wouldn't smell her. She aired herself out for her roommate. Like the photo. Like "we *talked*." The one friend who knows and let her keep it, and Vee stands in January with the flannel buttoned to respectable and waits until the room smells like hot dust again. Then goes back and finds the wet patch and lies down *on* it. "Her mess. Hers to lie in." That turn — greedy from the truck, greedy as shame, greedy as *mine* — is the best thing the book's done with a single word, and it's also exactly the trap I wrote down three chapters ago: she'll own it to herself in the dark and show everybody the aired room.

What I want now: for her to ask *him*. Out loud. Not Randi at a rim of a glass — him, with his mouth wet, "why do you like it so much." The book let her reach tonight. Next time I want the reach to be a question in his kitchen. And I want Pace and Randi on that couch before whatever Thursday-after-Thursday brings, because Randi's winning ground in Vee's bed by telling the truth, and he's losing it by keeping a word in his pocket, and I don't think either of them knows the score.
