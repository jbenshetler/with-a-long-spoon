REACTION

"Paul?"

I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. There it is. Ten chapters I've been waiting for the moment the plan needs Vee not to know something, and I thought it would be big — the bench, the couch, some scene. It's two fingers snapping and a wrong name. She sat on his lap with wine in her mouth saying *tell*, and she's snapping her fingers going "Paul?" And then — "tell me he is nothing like that" — she *built the mirror*. The whole disaster-date story, the lacrosse boy who wouldn't listen, that was set dressing so Vee would walk into the frame and see herself as the lucky one. I don't even know if the French place exists. I don't think it does. She invented a man so my girl would feel chosen by comparison.

And Vee handed it over. All of it. "I stopped keeping track of myself." That's the truest thing she's ever said out loud and she said it to the one person who's going to carry it up the gravel road and put it in his ear. I watched Randi's smile go up a notch at "I felt good just sitting there listening to him" and my stomach went with it, because I know what that notch is. That's not a friend being happy for you. That's someone hearing the answer she came for.

But the one that really got me — the one I'd text you about at one in the morning — is smaller than that. Vee had the sentence. *I made an exception.* Her sentence, the one from under the tree, the one that made me love her. It was right there, dignified, ready. And Randi said "because you couldn't not, because of him" in that tender agreeing voice, and Vee let go of her own words and took Randi's instead. "…Yeah. Because of him." Do you see what just happened? The book took her *choice* and reframed it as *compulsion*, softly, warmly, in her friend's mouth, and she thanked her for it. That's the thing I've spent years leaving books over — the heroine's yes gets rewritten as helplessness — only here it isn't the man doing it and it isn't the author winking. It's the woman who wants her, and the author is watching me watch it. I don't know whether to be furious or impressed. Both. I'm both.

And then Brooke walks in and I forgive Randi everything for about a page. Because "Vee's my dear friend," the hand on the table, damp from the cup, in front of the woman who demoted her without standing up — that cost something real. I believe it cost something. The girl who stopped dead on the quad is still in there, and she just spent her standing on a redhead in a pilled cardigan, and Vee felt the weight on it without knowing what the weight was. I felt it too. That's what's so awful about this book: the tenderness isn't fake. She wants Vee. She's also selling her. The hand is both. I can't pull them apart and I'm starting to think I'm not supposed to.

The thing I'm not forgiving is what Vee did to Cassie, and it wasn't Randi's doing. "It isn't really her place." Vee said that. Randi actually offered — "we could have dragged her" — and Vee closed the door herself, with a little hand motion for the leggings, and then thought comfortably about how healthy it was. I saw Cassie's look go past Vee to Randi and get folded away, and that's the second time the book has shown me that face and taken it back. She's being left at the door of the brighter life, and it's Vee's hand on the door. That hurt worse than "Paul."

Nobody was alone in a room this chapter. Pace isn't in it except as a story being told about him, and I felt that — one chapter since the tree, and already I'm hungry for him to walk in and be exact at someone. But I'm not restless. I'm scared. Everything Vee said at that high table is going to end up on his couch, and I need to know if he keeps any of it back the way he kept the froyo line. If "I stopped keeping track of myself" gets narrated in his ear while Randi's thighs go tight, I'll feel it for a week.

She walked home feeling picked. She was.
