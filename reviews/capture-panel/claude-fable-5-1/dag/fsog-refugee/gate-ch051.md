I said last time I wasn't reading for him anymore, and then the book sat me down across from him in a double-wide with Elvis under the glass and made me watch him choose not to bring Sheri down this week. The decision before the door swung shut. Of course that's the first thing we see him do. He's been deciding things can keep since before I met him.

Sheri. God, Sheri. "Don't waste the good stuff on me." Five feet of her, drawing the shake across by its base like something owed, and then — "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you, from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." I actually said *thank you* out loud to an empty room. Fifty-three chapters and someone finally put the thing in front of him in a daytime voice and didn't soften it, and he looked down. He *looked down*. He didn't look down for Vee. And then she stole his fries and closed the folder, because she's the only person in his life who wants nothing off him, and I think that's why she's the only one who gets to say it. "Her name is Vee." I didn't expect that to get me and it did — the one correction he's willing to make, to the one person who'd never need it. He'll spend a sentence defending her name to Sheri and won't spend one on her.

"The philosophy of your food." "Lord almighty. Get over yourself." The laugh — the first full one — and I laughed too, and then I resented laughing, because I know exactly what the book was doing. It was warming me back up for the recliner.

And the recliner. The lamp off. Pictures in order, "like reading." Vee ruining his coffee with sugar. I was soft by the second glass, I'll admit it. Then — "a girl with brown hair and glasses who laughed with her hand over her mouth." Daphne. Further down than he'd been in years. The scar door cracked, finally, and it's a woman, and "there had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told." I'm fairly sure she didn't just leave him. I think he's been running the house of a man whose first love died and who decided the words were the thing that killed him. "He had taught himself that the words were the difference." That's the whole book in one sentence and I believe it. I believe every brick. "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." Yes. Now say it to *her*.

But here is where I put the book down and went and stood in the kitchen for a minute. He changes the album and Randi arrives, and he's smiling in the dark, and then: "Not whether she wanted it — she wanted it, and a man would have to work at it to miss her wanting it." He has *decided*. From brunches she dresses for. From a sidewalk kiss *Randi told him about with her mouth at his ear while he was inside her* — and it "still worked on him now." He's holding Vee's one chosen half-inch as evidence, gotten secondhand in bed from the other woman, and he's counting it as her yes. And the picture — "further along than last time, because he had been adding to it for weeks." Vee in his sheet with too much honey, Randi with her feet on the lacquered seat. He has been building that kitchen in his head for *weeks* while she sat in a cold bunk not knowing if they were anything. "Whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her" — he asks it. He asks it in the dark, drunk, to no one. He asks everything about her except to her. Same man. Same horror. The whiskey didn't even change that.

The morning I'll give the book without a fight. Bleach reaching the bedroom. The mop with the crust of foam. And "Randi's cashmere blanket, the one for the chill that took her after, had slid off the foot of the bed" — he *folds it and puts it back where it lives*. A woman's blanket lives in his bedroom. Vee was never told that blanket exists. He folds it with the hangover hands before he even showers. The chicken and the broccoli in their columns, "enough in there to go on like this a long time" — I know what that freezer is, it's how a man eats when he's decided nothing's going to touch him. Prison food. She was laughing. He laughed with her. Nothing altered.

And then the truck. I knew it wasn't her. He knew it wasn't her "at once and whole." His head lifted anyway and the fork stopped and the house gave her to him for one second coming small around the bend, and it went past, and he bent over the table and sobbed. I did too, a little, and I'm angry about it, because he's crying in the exact empty kitchen where he could pick up his keys. He's got the words. The chapter proved he's got the words — "I love how good we are together" was a *choice* and he knows which one it was. And the chapter ends with him eating anyway. Eating anyway is what he's done since Daphne. It's not a turn. It's the pattern with the lid off.

So where I am: I finally have him from inside and it's exactly the man I'd built from the outside, which is a terrible kind of satisfying. He loves her. He knows it. He's never going to be told by anyone but Sheri. And the three-of-them kitchen is sitting in his head half-furnished and Vee's a chair in it he's already placed. The book knows the difference between him missing her and him going to her; I'm sure of that now. What I can't tell yet is whether *he* does — whether the next thing he does is drive, or whether he waits on the stoop for a car that has every reason not to come.
