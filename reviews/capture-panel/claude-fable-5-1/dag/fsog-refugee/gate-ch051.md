That breakfast. The bracelet she keeps out of the light. "Eating her revulsion." Making up boys for her father because the invented ones are safer at that table — I knew in one paragraph why she slides phones across brunch tables instead of saying things, and I didn't want to know, because knowing it makes it harder to hate her for the lawn. And then John shows up over her shoulder in the lift line and I actually laughed with her, and I liked him, God help me — the "you're already in it, you're whispering," three invented kids by the top of the queue, the hand under her elbow she didn't need and took. The stair. Him with both hands crossed over his heart. Her watching it happen to him — that's the picture, that's the thing I'd have cut out and kept if I were her. The pale blue dress that gives her eyes to the room. She buys it for a daughter who doesn't exist yet. I was *in* that chapter. I was in the sleigh. I didn't notice until later that she was warm only on the side he was on and the book had told me that twice before anything went wrong.

He assumed it like he assumed the table and the sleigh, and she said *what kind of girl do you think I am* with a hand on her chest like a girl in a movie, and — this is what got me — she *liked* it. "Pace made her say it, out loud, every time. She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked." I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. And then the book lets the night finish and shows me exactly what easier is worth: a good sandwich eaten fast. Her lipstick ring on him and her mouth doing it from a long way off. "Everything done to her and done well." Nothing happened to her eyes.

That's the argument. That's the whole argument of the book, made in one woman's body, and I didn't have to be told. The man who asks — the one who makes room, who leaves the rest of the way to you — he's the one who reaches you, and the price is that you have to be *there* to be reached, and Randi has arranged her entire life so she never has to be there. I'm so scared for my girl. Nobody in this book is cruel to her. That's what's going to break my heart.
