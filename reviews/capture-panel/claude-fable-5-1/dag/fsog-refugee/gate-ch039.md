REACTION

Four words. She got them. I've been asking for Cassie's four words since the apple and the *Hrm* and the three nights not woken, and when they came they were "Do you love him," flat, in the exact voice she'd used for "you don't come home school nights," and I had to put the book down on my knee for a second over a *bean bag*. Of course that's how she'd say it. She doesn't do the lamp coming on. She does the window cracked two inches and the book turned facedown and then she asks the one real question in the whole dorm.

And Vee gave her a *maybe*. That's the thing I'm turning over. Last chapter she put her mouth to a sleeping man's ear and said *Peter* and let the word be true in her chest where she couldn't take it back — and the one person in this book who'd tell her the truth straight back gets "Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast." She tells the sleeping man. She tells her own ribcage. She tells Randi the spanking, every piece of it, face down in the covers. And Cassie gets the baked tomatoes. The ladle again — I wrote that down twenty chapters ago and she's still doing it, still closing the surface so there's no hole. Except this time Cassie saw the hole. "There's only one way *that* burn happens. Not enough on." I actually laughed out loud. She told us in October she has eyes.

"It's all so fast." Oh, honey. It isn't fast. It's on *schedule*. It's been on somebody's schedule since before you were anything to either of them — I know that from the back cover and she doesn't know it from inside her own life, and her saying *fast* to Cassie like it's weather she walked into is the sharpest the irony's cut yet. She thinks the speed is hers. The yes is hers. Every single yes is hers, I'll die on that, and the speed was set by two other people and she's apologizing for it to her roommate.

The burn, though. Her hand going to the side of her breast while she talked, without knowing it moved. The cold cloth before she'd got the first swear out. Fussing for days over a thing the size of a thumbnail. That's it, that's the whole column one, that's what I left the genre to find — a man who treats the small hurt like a big thing because it's *hers*. I didn't need him in the room for this chapter. He was in her hand going to the spot.

What I noticed in the shape she gave Cassie: no Randi. Weeks of nights, and the woman who tied the scarf and knelt to buckle the straps doesn't appear once. Either Vee's started keeping Randi from Cassie the way she keeps Vee's things from — no, the way *he* keeps things, in drawers — or Randi's just gone quiet, and I realize I haven't seen her since the glaze on her lip. "Give her a day," she said. It's been a lot of school nights. Where is she, and what's she doing with the empty ones.

And Cassie "with no face on at all." Fifth time. I think I finally read it. It isn't about him and it was never about him. She's been counting the empty bed across the room and not saying, and when she finally says it she says it *kindly* — "I don't get it," which is the most honest thing anyone's said to Vee in this entire book, because she's the only one who has data on who Vee was before. "You never went like this over one." Nobody else can say that sentence. And then she let the maybe stand. Didn't say *I know*, didn't push, went back to the book. That's a kind of love too and I'm not sure Vee has the instrument to measure it yet.
