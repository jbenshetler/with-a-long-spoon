REACTION

He stopped the kiss. That's where I sat up. She drove twenty minutes of gravel with that kiss decided, she took it, and he met her all the way and then *he* drew back — and the book let Vee notice what I noticed, that she has never in her life been with a boy who was the one to stop. That was always her job. And here's a man who takes it off her hands and she doesn't even know yet what to do with the want he's left lit. I've been reading that beat for years in the other direction — the man who won't stop — and this one is the thing I came back for. He can want her that much and still be the one holding the line. No contradiction. I keep checking for it and it isn't there.

And then he gave her the house. The whole thing, and I knew that house before she did, which is the trick this book keeps playing on me. Too warm — she nearly says *did you leave the heat on* and I'm sitting there going, honey, he keeps it that way for bodies. Two towels on the rod. Two. Who's the second one for, and did it get used last Sunday night while the gravel was still crunching, and I don't think Vee will ever ask and I'm asking for her. The shut bedroom door and her leaning at it in the satin she wore out there on purpose — and he said *my bedroom* like he'd said *the kitchen* and turned her back to the light, and something dropped in her and something dropped in me, and then I thought: no. That's exactly right. That's him not taking the easy one. He's not walking her into the room where Randi begged. He's going somewhere else with her. Then the porch. The long table. I wrote in my own notes — *the long table she'll walk past without knowing* — and she didn't walk past it, she walked straight up to it, and there's a bolt of wine-colored silk lying on it and I had to put the book down on my chest for a second.

The error-correcting codes thing undid me more than it had any right to. *Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it.* She said the dress once. Offhand, tired, high up, cherries in her hand, and let it go in the same breath, and he said *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and took another cherry and I thought nothing of it, same as she did. He heard it the one time. And he didn't buy the magazine red — he went and found the red that was hers. He matched *her*. And he *made the shirt* — the shirt with no tag that she's been wearing into the ground, I'd clocked the no-tag and let it go too, and the book just sat on that for chapters and handed it over at the exact second it would land hardest. I actually said "oh my God" at the wall. That's the 1 a.m. text, in full caps: HE MADE THE SHIRT.

And now the part I have to be honest about, because I'm the one who left the whole genre over this. He built a room with the best light in the house and put the silk on the table in it and he asked for the one thing standing between her and the dress, which is her standing in front of him in her underwear. He stacked it. He stacked it beautifully, down to the sunlight. For a second — "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light" — I felt the old flinch, *so this is the move*. And then I read the rest of the sentence. *If you want it.* He tipped his head at the glass and left it open and didn't fill the silence. And more than that: she wore the satin out there. She leaned at his door before he did anything. The wanting is hers, it was hers in the truck, it was hers under the tree, and all he's done is build a reason she can say yes *to* that her mother's voice can't argue with. That's the difference between this and every book I quit. Those men manufactured the wanting or ran over it. This one just noticed hers and made it a dress. I know the jacket already told me *every yes was freely given, that was the problem*, and I know I'm being worked exactly as hard as she is, and I'm going anyway. I'm the girl in the spread.

What's chewing at me underneath, because I'm reading his silences now: the little door that shut at *parents*. Not Daphne — something else, something older. And Randi. Randi is going to say *tell* on his couch and he's going to have her measurements in his hand, and I want to know what he keeps. He kept the truck. Let him keep this. And Randi — Randi, who told her *ask and then ravish* with her own voice dropping to Vee's volume — is sitting in that sorority somewhere not knowing there's silk on the table, and I don't know yet which of them that's going to hurt.

She said yes three times. Not a waiter-glass-of-water yes. The book cut on it before the tape came off the table and I actually swore at it.
