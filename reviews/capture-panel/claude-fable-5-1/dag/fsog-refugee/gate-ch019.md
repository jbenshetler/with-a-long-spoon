**REACTION**

I put the book face down on my chest after the fire lane and lay there for a minute.

Okay. First: she did it. The thing I wrote in my own notes as the shape of the danger — *the first time the plan needs Vee not to know something in order to keep working, and she says yes to a thing she'd have said no to with the full picture* — it happened, and it happened over tomato soup, and there was no bench in it, no bedroom, no man in the room at all. Vee told Randi about the bra. Vee told her about the left hip. Vee was one syllable from telling her about the satin. And she'd have told none of it — not one word — if she'd known the woman across the table was going to be on Pace's lap in a week saying *tell.* That's the whole book in one lunch, and I felt it exactly where I said I would, in my stomach, and I still didn't want to stop.

Because here's what got me. Randi's *tell* — I've watched her do it to him. Wine in her mouth, hot in his ear. And now I watched her do it to Vee across a velvet booth, the same rhythm, and the difference is Pace knows what he's being asked and Vee thinks she's being loved. "Which hip?" "The left one." "All the best ones start at the left hip." I actually said *oh no* out loud. That line means nothing and it lands like a hand on the back of your neck, and Vee walked down it one *yeah* at a time, and I was furious and I could not look away, because the book wasn't winking. It let me see the thumb working the base of the water glass. It let me see the bottle cap again.

And then — "You don't have to tell me." I don't know what that was and I've been turning it over for twenty minutes. Was it mercy? Randi did the same thing Pace did on his knees: she didn't look down. She left Vee the last thing. Or was it that she couldn't *stand* it — that she's been hearing this girl secondhand off his tongue for weeks ("her stomach is soft," I haven't forgiven him for that yet) and hearing it firsthand, from the girl's own mouth, dreamy, *he looked at me and I was fine* — was too much for the face to hold? Because Randi flinched at *you're beautiful.* Randi locked the bathroom door. And here's Vee, seventeen days in, saying being looked at made her *fine.* Of course something went tight behind the smile. Of course she closed the door softly and said *big day* and went looking for the check. I ache for her and I want to shake her and those are the same feeling.

The tickling story. I don't believe there was a beautiful idiot. I think that was Saturday night at the house on the gravel road, after Vee left, dressed up as a stranger so she could hand Vee the lesson without the name on it — *a little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right.* "Do you think she'll like the bench?" She's *prepping* her. And the awful part is it worked on me too; I felt Vee's heat at the picture — Randi tied and laughing and cracked open — and I felt my own, and then Vee asked "weren't you scared, though?" in that small voice and I wanted to reach into the book and say *yes, baby, ask that one again, louder.*

The kiss. I've been waiting for the Randi-wanting thread to stop being subtext and it did, in the fire lane — the same fire lane the cop barked Pace out of, and nobody barked this time. Soft, in no hurry, and then *not ending.* And Vee's two fingers on her mouth and *it's a French thing* — God. She had it explained away before her lips were her own. And then the windows. Counting the dark ones. Her mother's voice found a new thing to be ashamed of before she'd even gotten inside, and that's the truest paragraph in the chapter and the one that broke my heart.

Small things I'm keeping: Vee putting her debit card down on her own folder because she would not let Randi buy her seat — and Randi *not reaching again.* That's respect and it's the first thing all lunch that didn't have a hook in it. The white-gold band, heavy, no stone, never off — I'm not saying what I think it is yet. The scuff on the left pump. Randi reading only the left side of the menu. "Overdue reserve." Vee's heat at Randi's chest turned into the thought she "had a use for."

No Pace in this one and I didn't miss him, which tells me something about what this book is actually about. But I need his side now. I need to know whether Randi drives out to the gravel tonight and hands him *she loves being looked at* like a present, and whether he takes it. If he does, the ledger tips. If he asks her what she's doing — if anyone in this book asks Randi what she's doing — I'll cry.
