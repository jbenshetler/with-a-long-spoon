REACTION

The door opened before she got to it and she was off the ground, and I made the sound she made. A week. I'd been counting it under the brunch chapter without knowing I was — the longest they've gone — and the book knew I was counting and paid it off in the first paragraph with her feet swinging out and the porch light wheeling. And then the thing I read this whole genre for, said plainer than I've ever seen it said: he never goes straight at his own finish. "She hadn't known a man could do that. She hadn't known a man would want to." That's it. That's the sentence I've been hunting since I put down the mafia books. She's inside her own wanting and he's building around it and it comes around to her first, every time, and she's stopped bracing for the catch.

And then he picked up a spatula and lied to her face and I had to put the book down on my chest for a minute.

"Short brunette?" Her hair is *black*. He's had it in his fists. He knows exactly what that woman's hair looks like spread on his pillow and he looked over his shoulder at Vee and made a little puzzled face. "Only good things, I hope" — from a man who gets the full report in his own bed. And then she told him about the kiss — the kiss-kiss, the one with "a little more in it" every time, the one I've watched her not brace for — and he did the air-kiss beside each cheek, *very grand*, eyebrows up, and I felt sick. Not because he's a villain. Because he's the man who turned the tap off, who went "gone" when she said don't, who kept the bar — I have built my whole reading of this book on him not spending what isn't his — and he performed not-knowing with a cooking utensil and then walked over and kissed her so she'd stop talking. "It's French, I guess." "Not *that* kind of French." *His grin spread.* Oh, Pace. She sat in your kitchen in your shirt and handed you the lie Randi planted — stumbled into her, small world, next morning she sat right down — and you nodded along and turned the fish. This is the first time she's ever brought Randi to you in words and you steered. That's not "not yet" anymore. That's a hand on the wheel.

And she didn't tell him the bathroom. I noticed that too. She's got "Randi, have you ever—" still unfinished in her mouth and she said "girl stuff, sometimes we even talk about boys" and let that be the whole of it. He's got the biggest thing in the house hidden from her and she's got the second-biggest hidden from him, and they're both doing it in his kitchen with the wine open, smiling at each other. The book made me watch both at once and I don't know who I'm more frightened for.

Then she lay there in the cooling sheet and said "I love him" in a chest where he can't hear it, and "Peter" to a man who was asleep. Twice now she's used his real name only when it can't land. She counted all the ways he's said it without saying it — the door, the spin, the salmon, the coming back — and decided she could "live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying," and God, that's beautiful, and it is *word for word* the thing the jacket warned me she'd do. Everything but a word for what they are. She's made peace with the silence and the silence is where Randi lives.

The weight thing stopped me a beat — eating like a field hand and her bras riding higher, "burning down whole weekends of him," the racks that never believed a build like hers. It's tender and it itemizes her body the way the book itemizes her money, and I filed it next to the credit card bill. Somebody's keeping accounts.

And then the morning, and the bag. She *brought the bag*. I said I'd know when there was a toothbrush and there was a whole counter of her, face cream open, pins scattered, thrown across the one clean surface in a clean man's house, and I watched his eyes go down to it in the mirror and hold and I held my breath with her. He said nothing. Mouth back to her shoulder. "He never said." And a week later the form's gone and the bottom drops out of her morning and out of mine, I genuinely thought — he minded, he's tidied her away — and then the top drawer. Her scrunchies. Her pins in a little dish. *Room for more of her, laid out like something prepared and waiting.* And him folded down into the bottom one, everything pressed too tight, his whole ordered self made to fit half the space so she could have the one your hand goes to first.

No note. There's never a note. He said it in furniture again — the lamp, the tap, the bed turned down, and now the drawer — and I cried, actually cried, over a man rearranging a bathroom. "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead." That's the whole of him. That's also exactly the problem. He makes her a place and he won't tell her who else has a key.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: he gave her the top drawer and I don't know if I've ever loved a fictional man more, and the same man said "short brunette?" about a woman whose hair he has in his sheets, and I can't make those two be the same person and the book won't let me pick. Also — where's Randi? Two Fridays, no brunch on the page, no gravel. The costumes still haven't been told. Something's being held back and it isn't by Vee.
