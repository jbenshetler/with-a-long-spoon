REACTION

The door swung open before she got to it and he had her off the ground and I made a noise on my couch. A week. The book made me feel the week — I'd been counting too, since the brunch, since the bathroom stall and the glaze on her lip, and nobody had been alone in a room with anybody and I was starting to itch. And then the porch light wheeling behind his head and her feet swinging and the bright stupid sound she'd have died of anywhere else. That's the sound I read for. That's the whole thing, right there, and she's not ashamed of it in his doorway.

And he was slow. Even then. Clawing at him inside the door and he still went around to her first. "She hadn't known a man would want to." Fifty Shades never once gave me that sentence — it gave me a man who'd do it as a technique, not as a wanting — and this book keeps handing it to me like it's nothing, like salmon turned once.

Then the kitchen, and I stopped breathing for a different reason. "You remember Randi? From the froyo day." "Sure." *Sure.* She sat on the rung of his chair in his shirt and told him the stumble outside the dining hall — the stumble they built — and he turned the fish. She told him "she still can't find what she wants, you'd think she'd have her pick," about the woman who had his second kitchen drawer without looking, and he said "Only good things, I hope." I put the book face down on my chest for that one. Not because it's cruel. Because it's so *easy* for him, and the easiness is what I can't forgive yet — he's not lying with effort, he's lying in the voice he uses for everything, and she can't hear the difference because there isn't one to hear.

And then the kiss-kiss. He did the grand cheek-kiss mime and I thought oh, he's going to play it as a bit, and she said "No, on the mouth," and her tongue went to her lip — she *still has the glaze on her*, she doesn't know she's licking it — and his grin left and the bed look came. She put her hand down the front of him and found him hard and said "umm, yeah" like it was her. It was partly her. I know what else it was. He got hard hearing his girl describe his other girl's mouth on her and she thinks she's teasing him about a European custom. "Not *that* kind of French." Honey. And he made her dinner after.

The weight thing I'm filing. Bras riding higher, she's "eating like a field hand," and the book says it like good news. I don't know if I think it's good news. A girl who was taught shame starts eating at a man's table and gets smaller — I'm watching that.

Then the dark. Him asleep with his arm flung up, the scar she's decided to keep unasked — I'd have asked three chapters ago and I'm starting to think her patience is better than mine — and she lets it be true in her chest. She loved him. And then the inventory: the door, the spin, the salmon, the coming back to her. "He felt it in everything he did and said it in nothing." That's the jacket copy, verbatim, from inside her. And she decides she can live inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the word. I believed her. I also know she's lying on top of a man who said "the lamp" to the wrong woman and the word he won't give her isn't withheld from everyone — it's being spent elsewhere. "Peter," to the sleeping shape. She's keeping that one. Randi doesn't have it. I'm holding onto that like a railing.

And the counter. God, the counter. She finally brought the bag — she said it to the mirror weeks ago and I said I'd know something had changed when there was a toothbrush, and here's her whole kit thrown across the one clean surface in a clean man's house. His eyes in the mirror going down to it and holding. Her waiting to see what he'd do. He did nothing. Smiled, mouth back on her shoulder. And she carried out the smallest tail of it — *had he minded?* He never said. He never says. That's the sentence this whole book is about and she just said it over a bathroom sink.

Then a week later the form's gone and the bottom fell out of her morning and out of mine. I was sure. I was so sure he'd tidied her away. She opened the drawer and it was hers. Pins in a dish. Room to spare, laid out like something waiting. And his — the whole ordered self of him — folded down into the lower one, pressed in too tight, because he gave her the top. The one your hand goes to first. No note. There's never a note. I cried, actual tears, on a Friday night over a bathroom drawer, and I'm not embarrassed.

But here's what I'd text at one in the morning: he gave her a drawer the way he gives her everything — in silence, in his house, in the one room Randi doesn't have a drawer in. Randi has the kitchen. Vee has the bathroom. He's made two women a place without saying one word to either of them about the other, and he's doing it so beautifully that I keep crying at it instead of screaming. The drawer is the truest thing he's done and it's still a thing done instead of said, and the jacket told me that's the whole game, and I'm playing it anyway.

Also: a week. Where was he for the week? Who came up the gravel?
