Oh, she turned the lamp up. No — *he* turned the lamp up. One click, by the little switch, while she was walking away from him down the hall with the bag, and then "Take your time," once, and then nothing. No feet in the hall. No voice at the door. He doesn't follow her, he doesn't pry, he doesn't ask what's in the bag — he just makes the room brighter for whatever she's bringing back into it. I've read a hundred books where the man goes to the door and listens. This one sat in the recliner because she told him to.

And she told him to. That's the thing. "Get your wine and go to the den. Wait for me in the recliner." Thirty-six chapters of him being the one who says *leave them on* and *slow, for me* and *not yet* — and tonight it's her hand out flat when he starts up out of the chair, and he sits back down, and "she watched him make himself do it."
