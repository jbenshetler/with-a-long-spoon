REACTION

Cassie got her chapter. Not a big one, not the one I've been bracing for with the cracked window and the granola bar — just Vee sitting backward on a desk chair with her arms along the top, talking, and Cassie turning her book facedown and saying "Go on, then." And I realized how long I'd been waiting for Vee to spend any of this on the girl three feet away. She finally did. She gave Cassie the tomatoes and the cold cloth and the warm house. She did not give Cassie the bet, or the finger in the lamplight, or "Sticky fingers?" — all of that went across a tablecloth to Randi last Sunday, and Cassie gets the cooking. I notice who gets what. Vee doesn't.

"You don't come home school nights." Not a question. That's the Cassie I've been trusting since the bunk — she doesn't accuse, she just hands Vee the count and lets her do the arithmetic herself, and Vee goes back over the week and finds her own bed empty. And then "Do you love him," flat, and the girl who said "Peter" into his sleeping ear a week ago, who let it be true in her own chest where she couldn't take it back, says *maybe*. To the one person in this book who lies to nobody. That stung more than it should have. Not because I think she doesn't — she lay awake afterward with the answer sitting right there, easy to reach, and no idea why it came out as maybe. I know why. She's said the full sentence to a sleeping man and to herself, and never once to anyone who could look back at her and hold her to it. Cassie could. Randi — I don't think she's told Randi either, and I wonder what Randi would do with it. I'm scared of what Randi would do with it, actually, and that's new information about me.

And then the pool, and oh, I didn't expect to love it this much. This book has been about Vee's body being looked at — his lamp, Randi's mirror, the stage, the recliner — and here's seventy-five minutes where nobody's looking and it just *works*. The suit that's got nowhere left to go on her and she looks at the water instead. Breathe every third, hand in front of the shoulder, the black line coming under her. "Her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here." That's the line. That's a girl who's been taught to be ashamed getting an hour where the body is just an engine and asks her for nothing, and she's had it since she was twelve, and nobody gave it to her. It's hers before him and it'll be hers after. I needed to know she had a room like that.

The four strokes. "Whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting." That's the jacket, isn't it. That's the reader's dread, the step-ahead thing, and the book let her put her foot right on the edge of it for four strokes and then the wall came and she rolled. I held my breath. I'm the one who knows he told the wrong woman she's the lamp in the room, and here's Vee underwater half-asking herself if she built him, and she's *wrong* — he is that good, that's the whole cruelty of it, he's exactly as good as she thinks and he's lying to her anyway. Four strokes was all it got and I don't know if I'm relieved or sick.

And "did he mind" went to nothing by the fourth piece, which is the right proportion, and I know it's nothing because I've already read Friday — the top drawer, his whole ordered self folded down into one — so the chapter put me a step ahead of her even on the small thing. She's walking up the hill in the dark with her head quiet and a sandwich to think about and a drawer waiting for her she doesn't know about, and that's the kindest dramatic irony this book has managed. The other kind is still coming.

Small things: "there's only one way that burn happens, not enough on" — Cassie's whole heart in a dry line. The cold cloth before she'd got the first swear out. He fussed over the burn for days. Of course he did. He fusses over a tomato burn and keeps the quad. Both true, both him, I've stopped expecting them to cancel.
