The water glass got me first. Not the bread — the paper towel folded under the glass so the sweat wouldn't mark the wood. That is the whole man in one square of paper, and I'd forgotten, after the truck and the Randi chapter, how much I like being in a room where somebody does that. Then the undershirt pulled down over her head to the knees, the forehead kiss after he thought she was gone — he does his tenderest things when nobody can collect on them, and the book keeps letting me catch him at it, and I keep falling for it, and I'm not sorry. "You need to eat before you decide how you feel." I'd take that on a plaque.

And then she asks about his other friends and he says *one* to the window, and then he turns around and says it to her face, and I put the book down in my lap for a minute. One. Said plain, no door left open, the way he says things he's already decided. And here's what I couldn't stop hearing underneath it: he was inside Randi two chapters ago telling her Vee kisses him first now. So what is Randi, if she isn't a friend? Every word he said at that counter was true — I believe the pack, the timer on the door, *weren't safe or weren't welcoming* — and it's still the most honest lie in the book, because the actual answer to "why don't you have friends" is standing in his Monday with a bracelet on. "Women misunderstand it. It gets awkward." Vee hears that and thinks *they fall for him.* I hear it and think *he sleeps with them.* Daphne. Randi. Sheri's the one he kept because Sheri's the one who can't be either thing, and Vee gets all the way to that conclusion herself — *the only kind the world had ever quite let him keep* — and she's right, and she's holding the wrong end of it.

Which brings me to Daphne, and I have to be honest about my own head here. I'd have sworn Vee had never heard that name. I have it in my notes as a thing I was waiting for. And now there's a January night by a black window — *less than staying*, *I was not a good fit where I was* — that she's carrying like a rock, and I don't have it. Either I lost it or the book slid it under the door while I was in Gstaad, and I genuinely can't tell which, and that's an uncomfortable feeling to have about a book I'm this far into. But what landed harder than the gap is what she's doing with it: *the ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue.* Oh. A grown woman and a boy of fifteen in a building full of men. "The best two years he'd had." I'd been holding Daphne as his one clean love, the person he would've told, and Vee just turned it over and showed me the underside, and he's standing there calling it luck the way he calls the broccoli dinner. *It isn't tragic.* He set it in good order and she clocked that the good order was the tell. She's reading him the way I read him. I don't know if I've ever been prouder of her or more worried.

Because she's going to Tuesday with both hands open. She's going to lay the real Sheri on the white cloth — five foot nothing, two colors of hair, gay — and say *I don't have to be jealous* and hand Randi the win, and I could feel it coming the way you feel a glass start to go over. Randi, who made her say *blonde, tan* out loud and asked how she knew. Randi, who's the one with a face. And the little thing I almost missed: "That way too. She can be like that," about Sheri's look, said without a flicker. He's getting her used to being looked at by a woman like it's weather. Last chapter it was "she looks at you, too." He doesn't miss, and he didn't miss there.

And still — the end. She came around the counter. She didn't say sorry because sorry would've asked him to hold still and be comforted, and that is the most grown thing she's done in sixty-six chapters. She held him the way Sheri held him, whole weight, and he put his chin on her head and looked at the wall and *didn't try to make either of them feel better.* First time she's been the one holding. First time he's let her. I've been waiting for him to say the word; this was better than the word, and it's also exactly how he gets out of saying it, and I know that, and I was still a mess in a kitchen with a loaf cooling on the rack.
