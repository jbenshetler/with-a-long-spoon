Okay, that's a small chapter and I still read the "And I didn't go" paragraph twice. "I made *him* stop. And I kissed him. Me." That one-word *Me* is the whole reason I came back to this genre after swearing off it. She's not telling Cassie about what he did to her. She's telling her what *she* did, and she needs Cassie to understand that nobody did anything to her — "I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." I've read a hundred morning-after debriefs in these books and they're always about him — his hands, his jaw, how he took her. This one is a girl claiming her own yes out loud to the one person who'd catch her if it was fake. It's such a quiet thing and it did more to me than most of the sex scenes in the books I quit.

And Cassie. God, I love Cassie. "How much did he give you to drink?" — that's the question I'd ask, that's the question every woman who's read these books would ask, and the book lets her ask it flat and lets Vee answer it clean. Then "what to tell the state troopers," and "Off six-fourteen. That's the whole address." She's doing the work. She's the one person in this book who isn't inside the warm bath, and I notice I'm leaning on her the way Vee leans on her, because I know what Vee doesn't. Vee says "He'll have water, he's not going to walk me off a cliff" and I actually winced. Not because I think he will. Because I've seen the bench. I've seen Randi straddling him on the couch saying *tell*, drinking wine while he describes how much frozen yogurt this girl ate. Vee's building a dossier out of two facts and a gravel road and calling it plenty, and I'm sitting here holding the rest of the file.

That's the ache of this book for me right now and I can't decide if it's the good kind. Everything Pace does *with* her is exactly what I want — the walking away under the tree, the inch he left, the hike asked for after and not during. He's asking. He's watching for the answer. The spin that nearly took out the closet door — that's the texture I'd screenshot for a friend. Her body can't hold it. "Look at me." Yeah. I'm looking too. And I'm already thinking about her walking into that house with the heat turned up and the long table with its top on, and her not knowing what she's walking past.
