REACTION

Okay. The truck. I'm going to be thinking about the truck for a while.

Because here's what got me, and it wasn't the shirt coming off — it was him getting *out*. He hands her the only dry thing he owns and then he walks back into the rain with his hands in his pockets and his back to the glass so she can change without being looked at. He doesn't say anything about it. He doesn't make it a gesture. And for a second it makes no sense to her, and then she looks down at the flannel in her lap and understands, and I understood with her, and I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. That's the thing I left the genre looking for. Not a man who takes. A man who builds the room she gets to decide in, and then stands outside it in the cold.

And then she knocks on the window. *She* calls him back. She's the one on her knees on the bench, she's the one holding his eyes, she's the one who takes the buttons by feel so she never has to look away from him — and he just sits there and *receives* it. Doesn't lean, doesn't lift a hand. "His face was want and it was gladness and it was a stillness she had never seen on anyone." I had to reread that. The restraint isn't a withholding — it's not the Christian Grey thing where control is a wall between him and her. He's glad. He's being handed something and he knows it and he lets her see that he knows it, and he still doesn't reach. That's the whole bench chapter turned inside out: there, his control broke Randi open; here, his stillness lets Vee open herself. Same man. No contradiction. I've been waiting years for someone to write that man and not flinch.

And then — God — she goes to kiss his knuckles and his hand is shaking. He's been freezing the whole time and he was so far inside watching her that he didn't know it. And she stops the next thing. She puts his hand against her belly under the flannel and says *you're freezing, we should go, please.* She takes care of him. She chose heat and then she chose him over the heat, and that's hers, that's her desire doing something with itself, not just something being done to her. I felt that more than I'd have felt a sex scene.

The mother's voice is doing exactly what I'd have wanted it to do — *floozie, three dates, what kind of girl* — and the book doesn't argue with it, it just lets Vee feel the heat *under* it. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." That's the truest thing about shame I've read in one of these. She wants to be the thing the voice says, with him, watching. That's not the book winking. That's her, from inside.

Small things that undid me: the sock. Her panic that he's going to see a sock that's been in a boot forty minutes, and he just does it, and the panic lets go "without her catching it go." The cherry pit — "we take nothing and leave nothing," and then he spits his own pit onto the wax paper beside hers and doesn't look at her doing it right. The way he lets her catch him watching her face instead of the valley. And "That sounds like a beautiful dress." Four words, no weight on it, and I know exactly where that dress is going to show up, and I know it's going to wreck me when it does.

Now the part I'd text at 1 a.m. and then delete and retype: I know what he and Randi said on the quad. *Do you think she'll like the bench.* I know he went home Thursday night and told Randi about the froyo cup with Randi straddling him drinking wine. And I'm watching Vee stand under that overhang in his flannel saying *I kept the shirt, I didn't ask* with her whole face open, and I feel sick and I can't look away. Because every single thing he did on that mountain was real. Nothing in the truck was a move. And it's still inside a plan she doesn't know about. The jacket told me — *every yes was freely given, that was the problem* — and I thought I understood what that meant and I didn't until this chapter. I keep trying to find the place where he's being cruel so I can be angry at him, and he isn't, and that's worse, and that's the book.

The campus cop taking her sentence away — fine, a little cute, I'll allow it. She was about to say something bigger than she had words for anyway. And Cassie. Cassie going still. "Vee." Just the name. "Keep the shirt." Cassie knows something's coming and loves her too much to say it, and I'm starting to think Cassie's the only one in this book who's not lying to anyone, including herself.

Heat-wise: nobody touched anybody and I'm a wreck. That's a three on a different scale than the bench; I'll call it a two because the charge stayed in her body and his eyes and never crossed the bench seat. But the bond moved — this is the chapter where she stopped being a girl he's dating and started being a person he's actually taking care of, and the book let me watch him not realize that's a problem for him yet.
