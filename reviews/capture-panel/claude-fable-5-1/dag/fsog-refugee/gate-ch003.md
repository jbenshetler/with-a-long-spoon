REACTION

Okay. So that's where she comes from. Two chapters of me thinking I'd picked up the wrong book — where's Vee, the jacket said Vee — and it turns out I've been sitting in the room where she gets chosen. I'd been reading the prologue to somebody else's life and didn't know it. I feel a little played, and I'm aware that's the point, and I'm also aware I'm annoyed in the way you're annoyed when a friend tells you a secret that's going to make the next six months extremely interesting.

The first part, in bed, did the thing I read for. Not the mechanics — the *asking*. He asks her the question with himself inside her and her body answers for her, and then he does the thing I have waited years for a man in one of these books to do: he says to himself, I know her body's answer, I'm not going to take it for her answer. He waits until she's come down. He asks again when she's herself. He gives her the room to dress it up as a gift to him if that's the only way she can say yes, and he *lets her*, and he knows exactly what she's doing and he thinks it's beautiful. That line — "The same act. A different owner." — I had to put the book down for a second. That's the most precise thing I've read about how a woman like Randi survives wanting something. I know that move. I've watched friends do that move. I've done it.

And then the walk, and I'm sitting here feeling two things at once and not loving that I feel both. The game itself is genuinely sexy — "I point. You pick" is the exact shape of dominance I want, where his control is the frame and the choosing is hers, and the kiss on the quad with her own taste still on his mouth after she said *maybe* — that went straight through me, in daylight, fully clothed. Her stopping dead on the walk when she saw Vee. The *yes* with nothing around it. I was in it. I was shopping with her.

But I was also watching Vee laugh with her head thrown all the way back, having no idea two people on a wall were deciding things about her, and the back of my neck knew what my hands didn't. I have read books where this walk ends badly. Where "go meet her" is the first move of something the book will later ask me to call romance. The jacket swears every yes is freely given, and so far this author has been scrupulous to the point of fetish about consent with Randi — he waits for the lock, he waits for the gathered self — so I'm extending credit. But I'm counting it. The bench line at the end was so cold and so funny and so wrong that I actually laughed, and then I didn't like that I'd laughed. Randi locked a bathroom door two chapters ago. Now she's asking if a stranger will like the thing that broke her. That's not a girlfriend being game. That's a woman handing her own fear to someone else to carry.

And Pace. I want to be fair to him: he's tender, he reads her like weather, "let's go feed you" after the cheeseburger she "can't" eat — he's still trying to feed her, that got me. But he was imagining his hands full of two of her before he pointed at anyone. He wanted the blonde who chaired things *himself*. He let Randi's camouflage stand because it was gentle and because it got him what he wanted, and I can't tell yet which of those he'd say if you made him pick. I've been burned by men in these books who were perceptive right up until it cost them something.
