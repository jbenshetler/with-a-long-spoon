REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I had to put the book face-down on my chest for a second after the first page, because *would you like to taste another woman* — asked into her, with his mouth still wet from her, while she's still coming down — and her body answers before she does? I felt that in my own stomach. That's the thing I've been chasing since the Red Room and almost never get: a question that's also a touch. And then — this is the part that got me — he doesn't take her body's answer as the answer. He waits. He lets the heat go out of the room, lets her get her head back on her chest, and asks it *again*, plain, into her hair, when she's whole enough to lie to him if she wants to. And she does lie, a little, and he sees it and lets her keep the costume. "I point. You pick." I underlined that. I actually underlined it. That's a man whose control makes room for her instead of standing in it.

There was one line that pricked me — "He had decided this beforehand." The idea that he'd planned to put the question into her while she was undefended. My old alarm went off: that's the move, that's the thing the mafia books do and wink about. But then he asks the second time, on purpose, for exactly the reason I'd want him to, and the alarm went quiet. I don't think it was a trick. I think he wanted to know what her body knew, and then he wanted *her* to be the one who said it. Those are different, and this book knows they're different.

What I can't stop thinking about is that I know something Pace doesn't. I was in that bathroom with her in chapter one. I heard the deadbolt go over: *not ever.* And now here she is, four weeks in and sure the face is sealed, and the face fails her *twice* on one walk — the bare "yes" with nothing around it, and then that squeaky daylight "yes" into his hair, the bed voice out in the open. She's not back behind it. She thinks she is. He's reading her body right and reading her wrong at the same time, because he doesn't know there's a lock. That's going to cost somebody.

And the cheeseburger. "I can't eat that." Then "Let's go feed you" at the end. He's still slicing the apple thin for her. That's the tenderness I'll forgive a lot for.

But — Vee. The jacket told me, so I'm not surprised, but God, actually watching it happen. Watching Randi *drift* into her shoulder so cleanly that the redhead apologizes. Watching her laugh with her head all the way back, not knowing two people on a brick wall just said *her*. I was turned on and I had a cold spot at the base of my neck the whole time. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — I said *oh no* out loud. Not because I'm leaving. Because I think I'm going to fall for all three of them and the girl with the freckles has no idea what's walking toward her, and the book is going to make me want it anyway. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." I'm going to be holding the book to that sentence for the next twenty chapters. I'll know if it cheats.

I want her chapter. I want to be inside Vee's head when she thinks that collision was her own fault.
