REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I read this one twice, and the second time I read it with the jacket open next to me like a cheat sheet, because I needed to check that I was allowed to feel what I was feeling.

The bed first, because that's where my body was. Him sliding into her while she's still coming — "he had decided this beforehand" — and then asking the question *into* her, "would you like to taste another woman," and her body answering before she can get a hand over its mouth. God. That's the thing I read for. Not the act, the being-known: a man who has been paying such close attention that he can put one sentence in exactly the right place and watch her whole self tell on her. I felt that one in my legs. And then he doesn't take his own — again. Two chapters, hard the whole time, and both nights he sets it down like a cup. In Fifty Shades that man would have been getting his. I don't know yet whether Pace not-taking is devotion or a kind of hoarding, and I've decided I like not knowing.

But here's the line I stopped on: *He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did.* That is the exact sentence this whole shelf uses to excuse everything — *her body said yes* — and for a second my stomach went cold, because I've left books over that logic. And then he does the thing I have spent years waiting for a man in one of these to do. He waits. He lets the heat leave the room. He wants her "gathered back into herself" before he asks with words, and he says outright that her being free to choose matters more to him than the answer. He asks the *same question twice*, once to her body and once to her. Reader, that's the whole thing. That's the fantasy I couldn't find. So I stayed. But I want it on record that the book made me flinch on purpose, and then reassured me on purpose, and I'm not sure I trust a book that knows where my flinch is.

Randi's move — "only in a threesome, with you" — I actually said *oh, honey* out loud. Because I watched her lock the bathroom door in chapter one, and I watched her lie under the wrong duvet with her shoes on, and I know what she's doing: she can't be the woman who wants a woman, so she'll be the girlfriend who gives her man a gift. Same hunger, different owner. And Pace *sees it* — "because it was beautiful" — and lets her keep the costume. That's tender. That's also the first time I've watched him let her get away with something, and I noticed.

The pointing game turned me on more than I expected and I'm a little embarrassed about it. Not the pointing — the "I point. You pick." The way the choosing is hers, the way she goes from good sport to *shopping* without ever admitting she crossed the line, the way he kisses her on the path with her own taste still on his mouth so she can't pretend she's not in it. And then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her whole throat and Randi just — stops. "Yes." The bare word. I got chills, and then the chills turned, because I know who Vee is. I bought the book for her. She's the one on the jacket who "never knew there was one," and I just watched her get chosen from across a lawn by a girl who then *walked into her on purpose* so gently it looked like Vee's fault. That collision is the most frightening piece of choreography I've read in ages and it's described as a thing of beauty. It is a thing of beauty. That's the problem.

And then — "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I put the book down on my chest for a minute after that. Because I understood it. Randi can't go back on the bench — she said so, in the mirror, *not ever* — so she's going to put someone else on it. She's going to give him a girl who *can* be seen so she doesn't have to be. That's not a threesome, that's a substitution, and I don't think Pace has clocked it, and I'm not sure Randi has either. What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the plan isn't a sex plan it's a hiding plan and the hider is the one who cried.*

I'm uneasy. I'm also completely in. Nobody has been cruel. Nobody has been disregarded — yet. Everything between the two of them in this chapter was asked for, waited for, chosen. It's the girl on the far edge of the lawn who hasn't been asked anything, and the jacket promised me every yes will be freely given, and I want to see how the book is going to make that true.
