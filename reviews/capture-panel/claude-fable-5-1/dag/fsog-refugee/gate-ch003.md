REACTION

Okay. Okay. I had to put the book down on my chest for a second after that last line and just lie there.

First — the opening. He asked her *that* question while he was inside her and she came on it before she could decide anything about it, and I felt that in my own body, honestly, that thing of being asked something true at the one moment you can't arrange your face. And here's where I was watching him like a hawk, because that's exactly the kind of moment the books I left would've used as the whole answer — "her body said yes, so." And he doesn't. He says it in so many words: he knows her body's answer and he does not take it for *her* answer. He waits until the heat's gone out of the room and she's got her head on his chest and he asks it into her hair, plain, in daylight basically, and lets her flinch, and lets her dress it up as a gift for him, and lets that stand. That is the man I've been looking for since I was twenty-six and furious at Christian Grey. He asks. He watches for the answer. He leaves the room for her to choose, and then — "I point. You pick" — he makes the choosing *hers*, and she lights up like she's never been handed a wheel before. The library-steps kiss with her own taste still on his mouth, in public, so she'd know he'd been keeping it for her — I'm sorry, that's the hottest thing in the chapter and nobody's clothes came off.

But.

I kept hearing Randi under the duvet with her shoes on. "He's hard to say no to," in the wrong key. She turned a deadbolt on him in that bathroom in chapter one — *the face he can have, the rest of me is not coming out again* — and he doesn't know it. He thinks tonight she met more of herself. He thinks it's the best idea he's had in a month. So I'm watching a careful, attentive man be careful and attentive to a woman who has already decided to lie to him, and all his reading of her — the flinch, the kitten voice, the seam in her laugh — he reads *beautifully*, and it still doesn't tell him she's gone. That's the thing that scares me more than anything he did. His control makes room for her. She's filling the room with a performance. And he's so pleased.

And then Vee. Walks out of the dining hall with her head thrown all the way back, laughing like she's forgotten she can be seen, and the whole chapter had been building toward her without me clocking it — the cheeseburger Randi "can't eat," the apple slices from before, the "let's go feed you" — and I understood it with a little sick drop: this girl is the thing Randi won't let herself have. Appetite, walking. And Randi engineered a shoulder-bump so clean the *redhead* apologized, and came back across the lawn glowing and did a little happy footwork, and I was glowing too, I was, and then — "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I read that line three times. He *kissed her head* and said let's go feed you. He didn't answer. Did he hear it? Does he hear what that sounds like — a woman who's just had the realest night of her life, and locked it away, already assigning it to someone else? The jacket told me Vee doesn't know there's a plan. Well, I've just watched the plan get made, by two people talking about a stranger's body the way you'd talk about whether a dress would fit her, and the book is very good at making me enjoy it, and I resent that a little and I'm not stopping.

What I need from this book now: that when it gets to Vee, she gets what Randi got — a man who waits for the second *please*, the real one — and not a man who's already decided what she'll like. And I need someone, at some point, to notice that Randi is running on a smile.
