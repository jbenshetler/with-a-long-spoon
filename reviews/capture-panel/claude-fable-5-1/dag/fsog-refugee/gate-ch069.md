REACTION

Okay. So that happened.

I've been waiting for Randi to kiss her for — what, since the composites? Since the foyer with the back of her hand on her mouth? — and I thought I knew what I'd feel when it came. I didn't. I felt it the way Vee felt it, which is the book's whole trick and I'm sick of being good at falling for it: the held inch, the smell arriving from everywhere, "turned in Vee like a key," and then the kiss that started like the goodbye and didn't stop where goodbye stops. Her hips pressing once before she could stop them. *Asking.* I was gone. I was Vee, leaning down into it, and I did not want Harrison or the floor or the room, and I'd have told you right then this was the most honest thing anyone has done in seventy chapters.

And then "I almost believed it."

That's the line I'd text you at 1 a.m. Not the kiss. That. Because Randi came up out of it a moment behind, didn't speak, shook her head blinking — she was *wrecked*, the book let me see her wrecked — and then she reached for the cover the way she reached for it at the top of the stairs, the way she reached for it in Gstaad, and this time the cover wasn't "after driving out to see Pace." This time it was handing Vee the whole night back "neat and folded" with the one thing Vee actually did inside it erased. Vee moaned into her mouth. Vee's hand went into her hair and pulled her deeper. And Randi told her she did a great job pretending. I wanted to throw the book. I wanted to go through the page and shake her — you said it to him, on top of him, "I want her to know exactly what she does to me," and then you got your mouth on her and you made *sure* she wouldn't know. You sent her back to the party with it ringing and nowhere to put it. That's not a cover anymore. That's taking something off her.

Which — fine, here's where I have to be honest about what I'm uneasy with, because it isn't just the one line. Look at the whole evening from the top. The skirt out of Randi's own closet that Randi *knew* — she went straight down the rail, no hunting. "Not that short," twice. Cassie doing the math from the bunk, "that hem's not going to stay put," and being right. Then the floor: Randi's hands on her hips moving her wider than she'd have gone, and then up her arms to her wrists, and *holding* them there, so the skirt rides and Vee can't get a hand to it. Vee's thong shown to a room of faces who weren't dancing. Nobody asked her. Randi arranged it so there was nothing for Vee to choose except letting go of her hands, and Vee didn't let go, and the book counts that as her choice, and — I half do too, that's the thing. "She did not let go" is Vee's, same as opening the coat on the porch was hers, and the shame turning over into heat was hers, and I believed every word of it from inside. But Pace *asks*. He asked her bare. He waited for the answer. That's the whole man. Randi has never asked Vee one thing. She builds a room where the yes is the only door and then congratulates Vee on walking through it, and I've known that since the shoes and I keep forgiving it because I can see she's scared, and tonight I can't quite. "She's here with me" to the boy, "I chose" to Brooke — she can say the true thing to everyone in the building except the person it's about. Brooke heard it. Brooke said *I can see.* Vee had to ask what she chose and got "as my date," which is the sidewalk kiss told at his ear all over again: every word of it true, none of it the thing.

And still. The French braid like lacquer, nothing soft on her anywhere, "the other Randi, the one the ordinary days never got" — I know what that is now, that's armor, that's a girl who's never had to be brave putting on the whole suit to do the one brave thing and then losing her nerve a breath after she's done it. The bracelet is called Love and it requires tools to come off. I sat up at that. Locked on, kept out of the light since Gstaad, and now I know its name, and Vee laughed and filed it with the glamorous things. I don't think it's glamorous. I think somebody put that on her. I don't know who yet and I want to very badly.

Small things that got me: Brooke running forty-dollar Chambord through a trash can so you'd think she spent nothing, and Vee — who can price a department store from the door — not able to taste a dime of it. That's this whole book in a cup. Vee's been drinking the expensive thing for months and it's been served to her like party punch. Cassie's eyebrow at "tonight I'm Randi's date," half an inch, held, said nothing. She let the sweater stand; she's letting this stand; the one person with clean hands keeps choosing silence and I'm starting to think silence is her tell too.

And Pace. Not in the room, and in every inch of it. He said "she looks at you too" while he was inside her. He *knows*. He gets Vee Sunday, and Randi gets him Monday, and the kiss is going to arrive in his bed from one direction or the other, and the thing I can't stop turning over is that Vee has no idea she's the only one of the three who doesn't have the map. She was so happy to be chosen. "Nobody tells me anything." Baby, no. Nobody does.

Where I am: hot, moved, angry, and not at the kiss. The kiss was true and Vee owned it from inside and I'd read it again right now. What I'm angry at is the fold afterward, and the book isn't winking at it — it put me in Vee's body "found out twice over" and then let me watch Randi lie to her face, and I felt the lie land as a blow, not as cute. That's the only reason I'm still here. If the book had let "I almost believed it" be a flirt I'd be done.
