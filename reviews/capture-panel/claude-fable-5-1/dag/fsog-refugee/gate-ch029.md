REACTION

He wasn't there. I read the line about Berkeley twice to be sure I hadn't skipped something, and then I sat with it, because I'd been carrying "tomorrow night" and "the induction" as the same lit room for a week of chapters, and the book just quietly walked him out of it three days early with a conference that "wouldn't move." I rolled my eyes at that. Of all the things on earth that move, a math conference is one of them. It smelled like the author needing him gone so the girl could do the thing alone — and then she did the thing alone, and I stopped minding, because that was the point and I'd been slow to see it.

Nobody's in the room. Not him. Not Randi, who knelt and did the ankle strap and then isn't there — no face doing nothing, no Brooke at the front, none of the collision I'd braced for. Not her mother. And that's the line that got me, the one Cassie couldn't be handed: *nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of.* He said it weeks ago at the sink, "somebody taught her," and Randi went still, and I didn't know who. It's her mother. The book doesn't say it and it doesn't have to. The bright voice she keeps for the things she can't give her. "A little relieved. Which is a horrible thing to be." Vee, honey, it isn't. It's the most honest thing you've said since "I wanted it and I took it."

And then the second step. I was in the chair with her, hand at the hip, the slit lying shut, every careful small step up to the stage, and I actually said *go on* out loud. Because this is the one I've been waiting for — not whether she walks in sure in Randi's scarf tied Randi's way, but whether there's anything under the sure that's hers. The practice room scared me. She sat on her own hand in that bathroom and said *not over a story*, and I thought, she's going to let Randi's voice tell her what she wants. And here she is in a room full of someone's parents with no one's voice in her ear at all, and she decides it a half-second before her head is sure, and the heat comes up to meet it. Hers. Her leg, given a step at a time, and she didn't make herself small. Thirty-four chapters and the desire is still hers, and this is the clearest proof the book's given me, precisely because nobody she loves was watching.

The photo, though. I keep turning it over. She sends him the whole frame, uncropped, and keeps the one thing that's in it for herself. "That part was hers." I love that she has a part that's hers. And I'm counting — the face she hid in his neck, the slap she filed, now the bare walk — the pile of things she doesn't say to him is getting as tall as his pile of the day and the bottle and the bowl. These two keep each other's secrets *from* each other and call it tenderness, and the book knows it, and I don't know yet whether it's going to make them pay. He'll open that picture in a hotel between sessions and see the dress do what he built it to do, and he won't know. Six weeks of him seeing everything, and the first time she's really beautiful at someone on purpose, he gets a jpeg.

Cassie. Finally, Cassie. "You look—" and didn't finish. She took the phone so Vee's hands would be empty. She was the one who caught the step — the only person in the building who had the camera up at the right second, and of course it was her. "Honor student," eyes bright at the corners, and "streetlight," and a terrible sandwich. If I'd been there I'd have been Cassie, and I'd have known there was something in the frame, and I'd have asked "was it a good night" and taken "oh, yes" and gone back to my highlighter. The ledger on her behalf just got longer and she doesn't even know she's keeping one.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she walked across a stage with nothing under the silk and nobody made her and nobody she loves saw it and I don't know if that's the saddest or the hottest thing this book has done.* Both. By now the two come to the same thing, the chapter says, and I think that's true of me reading it, too.

Three days. He's gone three days. I want to know what a man who won't text does with a photograph.
