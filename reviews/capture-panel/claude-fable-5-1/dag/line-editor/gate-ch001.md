REACTION

Well. I bought this for Vee, and Vee isn't here. I spent the whole first stretch assuming the sorority girl with the monogrammed bag *was* Vee — the jacket said starving, and here's a girl for whom apples are "permitted" — and then "There you are, Randi" landed and I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. That's the shape the jacket promised: I'm ahead of her from the start. So this is the game before the game, and the glamorous friend who's going to steer Vee toward Pace is the one who just begged facedown and then locked a bathroom door and decided, "quiet as a deadbolt going over," never to come out again. That's a good, cold place to start a book. The withholding has a shape. I trust it, for now.

The apple is the best thing in the chapter and it's on page two. Sliced thin because she won't eat thick, cut sides up because he'd noticed she eats faster when the cut sides show. That is a man paying attention in the way the kitchen-novels I love pay attention, and it did more to make me believe he sees her than any of the later sentences that told me he sees her. Her mother on egg whites and tofu, "a thing she had told him with a knife in it" — yes. The bite-kiss-bite game with his hands turning back higher every pass, her forgetting to finish the slice — that was the hottest stretch for me, honestly, hotter than the bench, because it was two people playing and one of them losing the game she'd started.

The bench itself: I asked for the door open and the door is open, wide, for pages. It's slow and it's specific and I was in it — the sweat darkening at the nape, the thread of slick that stretches and breaks, the flinch when he says "You're beautiful," the thumbnail she presses the bottle-cap edge into and the book *doesn't explain*. That thumbnail is the moment I knew the book knows more than it's saying. But God, the prose keeps stepping in front of the scene to caption it. "It was not really a question. It was a request." I *had* that. "The leaning was genuine." I had that too. And then the paragraph about "the script for the *wanted* position" — that's the book sitting me down and telling me who Randi is before I've finished watching her be it. Pace "had learned not to interpret things for her." The narrator has not learned this. The irony was not lost on me and I don't think the book meant it.

Tics I'll be watching for across nights: "small" is everywhere — small kitchen, small house, small economy, small bag, small adjustment, small sound, small breath, small high arches; I started counting and stopped because it was making me mean. "He waited." recurs like a metronome. And the lock: she deadbolts the front door first thing, she locks the bathroom, the decision arrives "quiet as a deadbolt," the hum is "the size of a key turning in a lock." That's a motif I like, done one time too many in a single chapter. The bathroom section — "The face. The face. The face." — mostly earns its anaphora, and then keeps going three sentences past earning it.

Still. The flinch. The lock she's never turned before. Pace still hard and setting it down like a fact — "That's not what this night was for" — which is either the most tender thing in the chapter or the first sign he's managing her, and I genuinely don't know which, and that's why I'm staying. What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the friend is the one who broke first and she's going to spend the whole book making sure it happens to someone else instead.* Bring me Vee.
