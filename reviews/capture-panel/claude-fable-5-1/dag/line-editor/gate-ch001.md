REACTION

Okay. I did not expect to be handed the two conspirators first, and I certainly didn't expect the woman I'd filed as "the glamorous friend" to be the one facedown on a bench crying into the leather with her name withheld until "There you are, Randi." I actually said "oh" out loud at that line. I'd been reading her as an unnamed girlfriend — the sorority bag, the honeycrisp politics — and the book let me, and then it gave me the name at the exact moment she stopped being able to hold her face together. That's the book knowing more than it's saying. That's the thing I need and I got it on the first night.

The apple is the best thing in the chapter and it isn't close. Sliced thin because she won't eat thick, cut sides up because he's noticed she eats faster that way, the mother on egg whites "a thing she had told him with a knife in it." That's forty pages of a kitchen novel in three sentences. And then the bite-kiss-bite game with his hands going up her ribs and turning back — I was fully inside that. The whole first movement is about a man who pays attention to what a woman will let herself have, and that's my kind of erotic, and the book knows it's mine. The pale hairs at the backs of her calves that only his hands find. The blue toenails. Yes.

The bench scene itself: I'm not a kink reader, I don't know the contracts, and I was still there for most of it. What kept me wasn't the spanking, it was the *please* — the waiter's please versus the one "from below." That's a real observation about a certain kind of girl and I've known her. "Good girl" made me wince a little, honestly, because it's the one moment he sounds like he's read the same books my friends lend me. But then she flinches at "You're beautiful," and the flinch is the truest thing in the chapter, truer than the orgasm. The bottle cap pressed under the thumbnail — I loved that, and then the book told me "She did not seem to know she was doing it," which it had already told me about the crying, and I'd already known both times. That's the tic I'm going to be watching for. The bathroom passage does it worst: "The face was lying. The face was good at lying. The face had been lying for years." I felt the first sentence. The other two are the author standing behind me saying *did you get it.*

And here's my suspicion, texted at 1 a.m.: this man is being *sold* to me. The water bottle left in advance. "He had learned not to interpret things for her." Then Daphne gets dropped in — one paragraph, a door he once forced, so that his not-knocking reads as wisdom rather than as a man who'd rather sit and feel good about his evening than go find out. I haven't decided about Pace and the book has. Meanwhile it's letting Randi decide the opposite about herself in a locked bathroom, and her decision — he can have the face, the one he found is going back behind it, "not ever" — is genuinely chilling, and I believe it. The deadbolt at the start, the lock in the bathroom, the key turning at the end. That's a shape. If I'm right about the shape, the whole novel is: she can't be seen, so she'll hand him someone who can. I want to watch that girl arrive. I want to see what Randi's face does at lunch.

No women talking without the man yet — the only woman's interior we get is through a locked door, alone, and I think that's on purpose. I'll allow it once.
