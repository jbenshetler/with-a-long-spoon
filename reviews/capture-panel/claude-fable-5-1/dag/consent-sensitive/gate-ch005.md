REACTION

Okay so I was braced for this chapter. The pointing game left me with that specific dread — the redhead laughing with her throat bare to the sky, "do you think she'll like the bench," and then the book handing me *Vee's* head for a whole chapter with her not knowing a thing. I've read this chapter badly written many times. Girl gets noticed by the room's best man, she glows, we're supposed to glow with her, and the author has no idea the glow is the crime scene.

This isn't that, and the reason it isn't is the pencil. Vee *sees* what he did. Not fast enough — "her body got ahead of her mind by what felt like a full second," the thought "did not finish," twice — but she sees it: he asked what her professor said instead of saying what he'd say, he made a margin note not a rewrite, he handed her the pencil back at the exact line where she could do it herself. She reads his tutoring like an econ problem and gets it right. And I'm sitting there having watched him do the identical move on a woman face-down on a bench four chapters ago. "He backed off and let her decide." "I point, you pick." It's the same hand. The book knows it's the same hand, because it let Vee describe it in the language of her own discipline — "the gap between what they say they're doing and what they're doing" — which is either the author winking at me or Vee winking at herself, and at this point I don't care which, I'll take it.

Cassie. God, Cassie. "That cute guy in line is looking at you" — flat report, no delivery — and then "the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She said it in front of Randi. She named the exact thing Vee half-filed and didn't finish filing. Cassie is my person in this book. She said "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter with the whole word, and this chapter she's the only one at the table not selling anything. Every time she opens her mouth the book is telling me it has an instrument that isn't rigged.

But the thing I'd text you at 1 a.m. is Randi. Randi went *flat*. One word, "Randi," no adjective, no parents-had-a-sense-of-humor — she gave him the bare name the way Cassie gave it to her, and I felt the temperature drop through the page. The cup going up to her mouth and coming down with no sip, twice. Vee reads it as boredom and I nearly laughed out loud, because Randi is sitting there watching the man who found her — the one she's decided will never find her again — aim the exact same patience at the girl she picked out on the lawn. She texted him in. She rang the bell. And now she's holding a coffee cup with both hands like it's the only thing in the room that's hers. That's not a villain. That's a woman who ordered something and is discovering she has to eat it. I don't know if I'm scared for Vee or for Randi, and that split is what's keeping me in the chair.

What actually turned me on, honestly: the forearms, the cuffs rolled back two turns, and "May I call you." I heard the *may* the way Vee heard it. I know what it's for. The jacket told me what it's for. It still worked on me, and that's a good sign for the book and a bad sign for my judgement, which is the trade this book is offering and I knew that when I bought it.

Where it got its fingers under my skin: Vee borrowing Randi's bit. "Yes, like the university." She tried on Randi's line and it fit, and she felt good in it, and then wanted her own name back "quickly." That's the whole seduction in two sentences — she's already wearing the other woman's clothes before either of them has touched her. The author saw that. I'm certain the author saw that, because the chapter's called Substitution.

The close is Randi shutting the sale — "a man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call" — and that's the line that made my jaw tighten. She knows exactly why he left without coffee; he never wanted coffee, he came in on a text. It's a lie told warmly to a friend to move her toward a man Randi's sleeping with. But Cassie's great-aunt line is sitting right there in front of it, undercutting it in the same room, and Vee — bless her — doesn't turn the phone over. She *lets herself want it.* The book gave her that verb. As long as it keeps giving her verbs, I'm here.

What I'm watching for: the "seemed young to be finishing a PhD" thought she set down without opening. She keeps setting things down. If she never picks one up, that's when this becomes the other kind of book.
