REACTION

Okay. I put the book down for a second after this one and just sat with it, because I did not expect *this* to be the chapter that got me.

Seven chapters of the machine — Pace and Randi and the bench and the pointing game and the wine, all that careful managed heat — and then the book hands me Vee, alone, underwater, in the suit she wore at sixteen when there was less of her. And it just lets her be a person. Nobody's engineering anything. Nobody's watching her from a light pole. The bleachers are doing the thing bleachers do and she takes it like an entrance because that's how you survive a walk like that, and then she goes under and the room switches off, and — "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." I had to read that line twice. That's the whole book in one sentence, and Vee doesn't know it. *The one part that had never been taken.* Because we, the readers, know exactly what's coming for the rest of her.

And here's what I'm really feeling: the book is telling me it *knows*. That's the thing I was waiting on. After "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I was sitting on a hair trigger, because that line is either the author being deliberately chilling or the author thinking it's cute, and I couldn't fully tell yet. This chapter tells me. You don't write Vee grieving at the bottom of a pool — "and grieved, a little" — you don't write her body waiting up on the deck with the rolled towel, unless you understand that what Pace and Randi are about to do is going to happen *to that body*, the one that's been a problem since she was sixteen, the one she covers to the collarbone. The blurb promised "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" and I have been, frankly, skeptical of that promise. This is the first chapter where I believe it.

And *Cassie.* God, Cassie. "Goodbye, Vee" back in chapter 4 has been sitting in my stomach like a stone and this chapter tells me why: Cassie is the one person in this book who loves Vee and wants nothing from her. She kept the joke for Vee instead of pitching it at the bleachers. She saw her friend come up "with her face on wrong" and didn't ask — "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger" — she splashed her. From ten feet off, without moving a step closer. That's real love, the unglamorous kind, and it's the only love in this book that isn't being *managed*. And I'm scared for her, honestly, more than I'm scared for Vee right now, because I've read enough of these to know what happens to the plain friend in the navy suit who said "goodbye" instead of "see you later." Randi's "*see you later*" and Cassie's "*goodbye*" — the book put those two words a page apart and I'm only now getting the full weight of it. One of them is a promise and one of them is a farewell, and the wrong one is the promise.

The bleachers thing landed harder than I'd have guessed, too. "You won't need floaties with those" — it's small, it's ordinary, it's every pool I've ever walked past at seventeen. But it sits right next to Pace and Randi in a way I can't unsee: the boys in the bleachers want her body loudly and stupidly, and the two people closing in on her want it quietly and beautifully and with *manners*, and the book has already shown me which one Vee finds harder to defend against. The floaties guy she can eyeroll at the ceiling. "May I call you" she can't. That's the trap, and it's not hidden from me.

What's bugging me — and it's a good bug, the kind that means I'm awake — is that I don't yet know if the book is going to let Vee keep the water. That's the question now. Everything she is has been either mocked or *chosen for her*; the pool is the one place she's hers. If this book takes her all the way inside and doesn't let her hold onto the underwater version of herself — if it's just something she loses to become Randi's project — then I'll know what kind of book this is. If it lets that self be the thing that eventually sees, then this is the book the jacket promised.

Text to a friend at 1 a.m.: *the pool chapter. she grieves underwater. the friend in the ugly suit splashes her so she doesn't have to talk. I'm so scared for both of them and it's the first time the book has felt like it's on their side.*
