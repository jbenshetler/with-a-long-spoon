REACTION

The yeti shoe. I didn't know this book had that in it. Two women in a discount shoe store off a bypass, wrecking the clearance wall, one of them six inches taller in pink rhinestones asking gravely whether they go with burgundy — that's my genre, that's the kitchen scene, that's two people being stupid together for no reason and I couldn't put it down. And the book knew exactly what it was handing me, because it gave Randi *the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give*, and I've been waiting since chapter 3 to see that woman cost herself anything in front of Vee. I got it over a flat whose seam was grinning off the sole. I'll take it. I'd take it over the bed in 24, honestly — I've been saying that for a hundred pages and I keep being right.

But I read this one the night after Portion, and I can't unread Portion. Three days ago in book-time I watched her ride him with her eyes shut and not say *tell me* and roll herself in cashmere with her back to him, and now she's on one knee on a pale floor in her own beautiful clothes with Vee's foot in both hands, looking at the plum. She already knew the plum. She asked what it was called at brunch. So what is she doing down there — checking his work? Doing his gesture back? He knelt at that riser and cut his eyes at Vee's heel and said *hmm*; Randi holds the foot to the light and says *Mm*. Same syllable, same posture, and I sat up in bed. They share a vocabulary of one sound and Vee's the only one in the room who doesn't know it's a vocabulary. And Vee's hand shot out and found Randi's shoulder to stop the floor tipping, and the book let it go by her *again* — "a small swing of the room though she was sitting down" — and I've stopped asking whether that's a shape or a hole because this time she didn't check the windows. There was a saleswoman standing right there. That's the book answering me without saying so, and I respect it more than I'd respect a sentence.

*Charmeuse.* Not to Vee, to the dress. *And a good weight.* She reads a bolt of silk like she reads the left side of a menu, and there's exactly one way a sorority girl learns to name charmeuse by touch at the hip, and it's standing on that riser while a man with a pencil says *keep still.* I've been asking who was up there before Vee. The book just told me in one word and let me do the rest. That's the withholding-as-shape I keep asking for. And then the coat story — a woman who ruined a coat doing the tailoring — another fake person with a true sentence inside her, and this time the true sentence is *ruined it doing the tailoring*. Randi is scared of what cutting a thing to fit does to it. She's watched him do it twice now.

The credit card is what actually got me, though. Not the foot. The debit between her fingers, the card that only ever spends what's behind it, going back in the wallet. "There was nothing under it." I've been writing in my notes for weeks that this is a book about a ledger — that when the telling comes she'll read it back and find she paid for every line — and here it is made literal, and she paid with money she doesn't have, for a thing she reasoned herself into as *what the dress was owed*, with the woman who steered her there standing at her elbow buying loafers for several times the price and never once looking at the number. The book cut so I can't see whether Randi saw the card switch. That cut is the meanest thing in the chapter. Because either Randi respected the girl who pays her own seat, or Randi watched her go under and picked up a shoe. And the fact that I can argue both is the fact that I'm still here.

Things I noticed across nights, since you asked. *Baby* — five times in one chapter, from a woman who's been *honey* and *sweetheart* and *gorgeous* till now. If that's a shift in Randi, I'm for it, it's an intimacy she's granting herself; if it's the author's ear drifting, I mind. I'll know next time. *Lit* — Randi is lit, Vee's face is lit, Pace rearranges into something *adult and lit*, the shoes are lit on their blocks — this book has one word for wanting-in-good-light and it's wearing it out. And *made to you* comes three times, once from Randi's mouth, which was perfect, and then twice more in Vee's head and at the boutique, which is the book explaining the tears it had already made me cry. Same with "the shoes were not a want she had to own as a want" — I'd *had* it, two sentences earlier, watching her look at the dress on its hook. Stop handing me the thing I'm already holding.

And Cassie's bunk. Randi's eyes went over it — made tight, nobody home — and came back to Vee. That's all. Cassie's been *nobody home* two chapters running while the other one is in the room, and I'm keeping that the way I'm keeping Meg's sentence. And the phrase "never once in her life had a friend like this" — that's the second time, it was *the friend she'd wanted at fifteen* at brunch, and the repetition isn't sloppy this time, it's a girl saying grace before a meal somebody else cooked.

Text I'd send at 1 a.m.: *she bought the shoes on credit and the other one bought loafers and neither of them flinched and I think that's the whole book in one counter.*
