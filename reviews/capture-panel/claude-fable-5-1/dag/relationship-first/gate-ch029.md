REACTION

So that's the single room. Twenty-six chapters I've been asking for it and the book hands it to me at nine in the morning with the bed still holding the shape of her — and of course it's small, of course the order is so quiet it reads as calm, of course she has freckles she never thinks about while Vee spent ten minutes burying hers. No makeup, robe, sky-blue toes. I knew those toes before Vee did; I saw them from Pace's side of the lamp two chapters ago, "the blue of her toes the last composed thing left of her," and now Vee's sitting three feet from them in a desk chair and doesn't know she's looking at something I've already seen undone. That's the book knowing more than it's saying, and doing it with nail polish. I'll take it.

And Randi is a *stranger* in this chapter — the narrator said so. "A stranger's silk at her throat." Vee would never say that; the book said it over her head, once, and let it go by. Same with "watched her like her own good work." Two places where the prose tipped its hand about who's holding the top, and Vee was too happy to go looking. I'm not.

Here's what I'd text somebody at one in the morning: the bar scene came back wrong. I read it last night. There was no glass of water. Vee didn't say the "don't need a man" line to the bartender — she asked, small, "does he give girls rides home." The bartender never said anything about a *chair*. And "left him standing there holding both our drinks" — she set hers on the ledge, I watched her do it. Vee has been sorting every story by who's hearing it since the Smoothie Bird and this is the first time I could lay the two versions side by side with the original still warm, and she's moved every detail an inch toward a better story — braver in the bar, wittier with the bartender, him more abandoned. If the author did that on purpose it's the best thing she's done with Vee's mouth. If she didn't, she's lost track of her own bar. I genuinely don't know which, and that's the thing I'll be chewing on tonight instead of sleeping, and it's a compliment to the book that I'm giving it the benefit.

"He can dance, the bastard." Warm, fond, thrown away — and Randi has never been said to have seen him dance. "That *bitch*," with feeling, about a blonde in a bar Randi's never been to. "He doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." She said it like a diagnosis and it was a confession — that's her sentence about her own nights at his table, the plate covered and put in the fridge for her. And Vee took it as friendship. Every true thing Randi says still comes out wearing somebody else's story; she's just stopped bothering with the Swiss lacrosse boy.

*Not today.* That's the line that turned me cold. Not "go, he's waiting" — an instruction, and Vee stopped half out of the chair. Randi now sets the day and the dress and the shoes and the knot at the throat. Vee is going to walk into Pace's house tomorrow night in Randi's shoes, and he's going to know them — same size, "practically a marriage," he's had that foot in his hands — and she'll think the light coming off her is the morning turning out survivable. She's been dressed for one of them by the other, and she's calling it getting ready.

The kneel. I noticed it was the second one in four chapters — boutique floor, now closet floor — and I'd usually mind being shown a scene twice, but this one *escalated*: thumb on the toenail last time, and now a hand up under the skirt on the outside of her thigh, and the thumb pressed once into the hollow behind her knee on the way down, and Vee unable to see any of it, "somebody kneeling to tend to her" with a whole skirt of dark between her and what's happening to her foot. Her breath did something she hoped wasn't out loud. That's the hottest thing Randi's done in this book and it's fully clothed and Vee has still not once, in twenty-nine chapters, thought about the fire lane. She caught up to something half a beat late at the door — "she hadn't braced" — and the book let it be that small. It's only what they do. I read that line three times. That's not nothing going by her; that's the whole door raised and her walking under it without ducking.

Small things. "Move in." "Shoe whore," and that the worst words are always the endearments between them. *Evil Queen* — she named herself and Vee laughed. Brooke with a folder on a girl who's told the house nothing. Edna Mode got me the way it got Vee, and then I minded a little that it was so cute; the book can be funny without making the funniness out of a movie. "A surrender wearing the coat of a defense," "ablutions," "with the conviction of the converted" — the narrator's been at the sherry again, and there's a run of it in the first third that had me counting instead of reading. And where are the strappy black heels she put on a credit card with nothing under it? She stood in Randi's closet saying all she had was block heels. Either she's saving them for the burgundy and the book trusts me to know that, or the author forgot the purchase she made a whole chapter about, and after the bar scene I'm running low on trust and high on attention, which is a good place to keep a reader if you meant to put her there.

I'm worried for Vee for the first time in a way that isn't about Pace at all. And I want to see his face at the shoes more than I've wanted anything since the panties.
