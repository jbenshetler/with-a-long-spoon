REACTION

Okay, so I knew. That's the strange pleasure of this one — the jacket told me, and the last two chapters told me, and so I sat through the bell over the door already braced, the way Randi braced on the bench before the first strike. Randi's thumbs going on her phone, "some boy, Vee thought, amused" — I actually said *oh, honey* out loud to my kitchen. The whole chapter is me watching a girl walk into a room that's been set for her, and the book is clever enough to make me want her to walk in anyway. Because the math scene is *good*. It's the kind of thing I came to this book for: two people at a table, a page between them, and the entire seduction is that he gives the pencil back. Euler's formula, the little cross in the margin, "You already know every piece of this" — I got a small unearned thrill from that myself, the same thrill she got, and then I caught the book giving it to me and felt handled, and then I decided I didn't mind. That's the exact experience the jacket promised. Falling for it exactly as she does. Fine. I did.

But here is what I actually can't stop thinking about, and it isn't Vee, and it isn't Pace. It's the coffee cup. Randi's cup coming up to her mouth and going back down, twice, no sip. Randi giving him her name as a single word after two chapters of the adjective bit. Randi asking about Bessel's correction — the most transparent "notice me" question I've read in a long time — and getting the great-aunt answer, and then holding the cup a second too long. She set this up. She *chose* Vee, on the lawn, with that bare "yes." And now she has to sit at the table and watch him do to Vee, in daylight, with a pencil, the thing he did to her with his hands — read her like a problem he's meeting for the first time. The title's doing the work: she's substituting Vee for herself. She decided in that bathroom mirror that the one he found was never coming out again, and this is how she keeps the promise. Give him a redhead who *will* come out. I don't know if she knows that's what she's doing. I don't think the book is going to tell me, and that's the shape I need — the withholding feels like a shape, not a stall. Pace, meanwhile, walked out of chapter one "quietly happy about her," completely wrong about what had happened, and I like him a good deal more for being wrong. He's not the omniscient dom the register keeps flirting with. He misread the woman he loves. He's about to read Vee beautifully and I suspect the reading is the trap for *him*, too.

And Cassie. God, I love Cassie. Four words a day, and every one of them true. "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter, "great-aunt" this one. She's the only person at the table without an angle, and the book is smart to keep her at the *next* table, headphones in, laughing at things two tables over — she hears everything. What the women say when he leaves is the best page in the chapter: Randi selling ("a man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call" — the coffee he never ordered, Randi, because he came in *for her*, and you're the one who texted him), Cassie flatly refusing to sell, Vee not turning the phone over. That's three women and three completely different relationships to the truth in six lines. I'd read forty pages of that.

What bothered me, and it's the thing that always bothers me: the book doesn't trust me to have caught it. The pencil-handing-back is *shown*, cleanly, and then I get the whole "The way he had read it... The way he had asked... The way his pencil-mark had been..." paragraph, six "the way"s in a row, explaining the feeling the scene already gave me. Same with "she felt, absurdly, *chosen*" — I know, I saw her shoulders settle two sentences ago. And I'm starting to count "small" — the small lift, the small *oh*, the small busy-ness, the small *got it* nod. The book has a tic about bodies getting a half-second ahead of minds, which was gorgeous once in chapter two and is now on its fifth or sixth outing. Also, everyone in this book has one line about Pace that amounts to "nothing about him should be impressive and yet." I've decided about him already; the narration can stop lobbying.

Heat: cuffs rolled twice and a half-second too long on his forearms, and "what he did with his hands besides math." That's a simmer and I'll take it. What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she's texting him under the table WHILE HE'S WALKING IN and the book wants me to feel bad for HER and I do??*
