**REACTION**

Okay. I'm annoyed at this book and I'm not putting it down, and I want to be honest about both.

The dinner got me. I'm the kind of reader who'll stay for a meal, and this was a real one — the bread coming last and hot, the green sour thing that wakes your mouth and the smoke-dark thing that folds you under, Christine easing when she saw who walked in. And Vee eating. God, Vee eating. After the pool chapter, after the mother's voice in the froyo shop that *didn't* come, after Randi's thin apple slices laid cut-side up so she'd eat faster — I sat there watching this girl not watch herself eat, and I felt it in my own chest, that thing where the hollow is older than lunch. That's the book's real subject, I think. Not sex. Appetite, and who gets to portion it. And then I remembered *let's go feed you* at the end of the pointing game, and the warm thing went cold on me, because I've now watched him feed two women and I know what feeding is, in his hands. It's a technique. Vee thinks she's found the one person who doesn't count her bites. I know he's counting everything.

Which is the whole trick the jacket promised and it works — I'm ahead of her and I'm falling anyway, and I hate that a little and I also kept reading. Every time she felt *chosen* tonight — the text left with her "like it could keep," the *may I choose*, the hand taken with no question in it — I heard Randi on his lap in Chapter 7 saying *Tell.* He's going to walk home from that moonlit tree and put this whole evening in Randi's mouth with the wine. The kiss under the shadow is going to become a story on a couch. That's the thing the book knows and isn't saying, and it's the reason I'll be back tomorrow night. The withholding has a shape.

But. Here's my 1 a.m. text to a friend: *the book did the blank-transcript thing AGAIN.* At Chi Latte it told me Vee couldn't remember a word Randi said, only the feeling, and I let it go because it was clearly about Randi's particular spell. Now it's dinner with Pace and "she couldn't, later, have laid out what they talked about." Twice is a pattern, and I can see the argument — she goes soft and wordless around both of them, that's the point — but I read for the *talk*, and twice now this book has told me the talk was wonderful and declined to hand me a single line of it. If two people are so good together across a table that she loses the thread and lets it go, show me the thread. I'll decide if it's worth losing. The book keeps deciding for me — *the most restful thing she had felt in as long as she could remember* — and every time it tells me how a beat felt, I feel it a little less.

Same with the hand. "It should have been nothing, and it wasn't. It was the first time." Yes. I had that. Then "It was too much for what it was" — I'd already had it. Then the resolving-not-beginning sentence, which is lovely and would have landed clean if it hadn't been the third sentence doing the same job. And the right-hand thing — I clocked it, I know what it means, and the little wink of "one more thing he knew that she didn't" felt like the book being pleased with its own plant.

Cassie, though. Cassie's the person I'd actually want at a kitchen table. She said *Goodbye, Vee* five chapters ago, the whole word, and now she's on the bunk saying *that's not a boy, that's a man* — and I can't tell if that's her being honest or her being taken in, and I like that I can't. She's the one woman in this who talks about Pace when he isn't in the room and doesn't sell me anything. I want more of her and I'm suspicious the book is going to spend her.

The kiss itself was good. The rule out of her mouth at the worst moment, him letting it close without a flicker, her feet not moving. *I'm making an exception.* Him leaving the last inch. It's the same choreography he did with Randi at the door — back off, let her lean in — and I'm supposed to find it beautiful, and I do, and I also see the machine. The mint in both their mouths. Fine. I felt it.
