REACTION

Well. I bought this book for Vee and the first thing it does is not give me Vee. It gives me the two who made the plan, in a small hot house, before there's a plan at all — and it doesn't even tell me her name until he says it into her hair after she's come. *There you are, Randi.* I actually put the book down on my chest for a second. That's the jacket's "ahead of her from the start" done as a trick on me, and it worked, and I'm a little annoyed at how well it worked.

I want to be honest about the heat first because it's the reason I kept reading past the apple. The apple is where I nearly relaxed into liking this man too much — cut-side up because she eats faster that way, and he's *sorry* about the calculation he watches happen behind her face. Her mother on egg whites. That's the detail that got me, more than anything on the bench: a girl who portions herself, and a man who has noticed and is quietly feeding her thin slices. And then the bench, and the long, long not-touching — the passes up her sides that turn back before they arrive — that is the exact thing I want from a book that leaves the door open. It's not the strikes. It's that the book can write waiting. I was there, on the leather, with my shoulders a quarter-inch up. I could smell the oiled walnut. The moment his wet fingers go flat on her spine — "her own heat, returned to her in the wrong place" — I said something out loud.

And then the "please." The book knows the difference between the waiter-please and the other one, and it made me hear it. When she comes and is just *leaking*, not sobbing, tears crossing the bridge of her nose to pool on the leather — that's real, that's written, that isn't a closed door and it isn't explicitness pretending to be writing.

Here's what I'm suspicious of, and I'm telling you because it's 1 a.m. and you'd ask. Pace. The book loves him a little too neatly. The Daphne paragraph — he learned, a closed door is hers not his — arrives right when I need to be reassured that he won't knock, and it's a bit like being handed a character reference. He holds his own hard-on "the way you hold a fact." He's a saint with a spanking bench. *Except* — the book also let me hear "he heard himself want to hear it again, and he soothed her and waited and hit her again to hear her make it again," and that last line, the bench "full of what it was, waiting for the next time," is not a saint's line. So I think the book knows more than the Daphne paragraph is admitting. I'll give it that. For now.

What I noticed, because I always notice: the repetition. "The face" — I counted, then stopped counting, and mostly it earns it; it becomes a mask she's tapping on. But the bathroom mirror runs long and starts explaining what the crying and the bottle cap already gave me. That bottle cap under the thumbnail, pressing where it would hurt, not knowing she's doing it — that's the whole scene, in one gesture, and then I get three paragraphs of "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." I'd already felt it. The book didn't trust me for a minute there. And "the bright sorority-house voice," "the script for the wanted position" — there's a sneer in the narration toward the sorority girl that I hadn't decided on yet, and I resent being told. She locks the front door first thing every time. She brought her own blanket by week two. She's not a type; she's a person who gets cold. Let me find her.

But the thing that's really got me is the ending, because it's not a warm ending — it's a woman deciding, "quiet as a deadbolt going over," that he can have the face and the one he found is going back in and never coming out. And he's holding her, *quietly happy about her*, not knowing. That is the shape. If she's the friend who's going to steer Vee toward him, laughing, as if by accident — then I think I already know why, and I think it's terrible, and I think it's about her and not him. That's a book that knows more than it's saying, and I'll stay for that a long time.

I do wish there had been one woman in a room without him. There are two women in this story and the one I bought the book for hasn't shown up yet. Give me a lunch.
