REACTION

Oh, this is the chapter I came for. Two girls in a dorm room, the window cracked its two inches, one of them narrating a date to the other. This is my whole shelf. Forty pages of this and I'd be fine. The brass tea cages — Vee can't explain them right and doesn't need to, because the not-explaining-right is the point, she's *trying to hand Cassie the evening* and the evening won't fit through the door. I know that voice. I've been both people in that room.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. "How much did he give you to drink?" — the flat dry floor under the gladness, exactly. The book's been letting Cassie be the only person who sees straight since "Goodbye, Vee" in the lecture hall, and here she is doing it again with a highlighter and her feet not reaching the floor. "What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers." It's a joke. It's also the only person in this book asking the question. And then Vee *answers* it, proudly, like a dossier — "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" — and Cassie says "That's the whole address," and I put the book down on my chest for a second. Because I know the house. I've been in the house. I've heard the deadbolt go over from the other side, twice now, in Randi's hand, first thing, before anything else. Vee says "I know where he lives and everything" and she doesn't, and the woman who bought her a lavender latte does. The book didn't underline that. It trusted me to hear it. That's the withholding-as-shape I keep asking for.

The line that actually got me, though, was the one Vee most wants Cassie to have: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." She's *right*. That's the awful thing. Every word of it is true and I still felt sick, a little, hearing her say it with that much certainty into a room where nobody's told her there's a game. The jacket warned me — every yes freely given — and I didn't expect the book to make me feel the warning this early, in a scene this warm, with a girl spinning in a room too small to spin in.

I noticed the rhyme, too, and I liked it: Randi in chapter two lying on her bed in last night's clothes under a duvet in a cold room, not crying; Vee here lying in a bean bag, the cold line coming in over both of them, and she "did not feel it." Same posture, opposite temperature. Two girls in single rooms after a night with the same man. If the book keeps doing that quietly I'll follow it a long way.

Small things. "Is he rich? Rich people hike. Poor people walk." — I laughed, and then I thought about Randi's eyes going over the cheap laminate and saying nothing, and I wondered what Vee's eyes will do. I want that scene. The one place the prose leaned on me was "it went into the pile of things she'd get to find out, which was the best pile there was" — the sentence before had already given me the feeling and the tag on the end is the book patting it. Once. I'll allow once.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *Cassie is the only adult in this book and she's twenty and she can't reach the floor from her chair.* And then: *she's going to borrow the good shoes and I'm scared for her and I can't tell if that's the book's doing or mine.*
