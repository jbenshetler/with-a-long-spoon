REACTION

Where have these two been? That's my first thing. Fourteen chapters of a girl who's "quietly starving," whose friendships have been "fine," and it turns out she's got a corner table in the good dining hall with a Meg and a Kayla and a whole semester of sneaking in, and a Theo who asked her to the fall thing and took no like a gentleman. She laughs with her whole body here. The next table looks over and wants in. That's the laugh Randi chose her for, and it turns out it isn't rare — it comes out of her any evening the light's right and nobody's watching her from a high chair. So either the book's been hiding a perfectly good life from me to make the hunger land, or this *is* what fine looks like and fine looks pretty good, and I'm meant to feel that little unease. I do feel it. I'm just not sure the book put it there on purpose. And Meg is the boots. Meg's been in the story since the trailhead and I only get her face now, chin in her hand, following Pace "in installments like her shows." I liked that. I'd have liked it more a chapter earlier.

But this is the chapter I keep saying I want — women at a table, no man in the room — and I'd be a liar if I said it didn't work on me. What I noticed most is what Vee doesn't say. Cassie got the whole thing: the knock on the glass, the shirt off, on purpose, *me*. Meg and Kayla get a version where she nobly declines to kiss a hypothermic man. She cut the truck out. "She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care" — that's the book letting me watch her edit, and it's the most honest thing in the chapter, because it tells me the ranking. Cassie is where the truth goes. These two are where the story goes. And Randi — Randi got May I and the tree and "because of him," and I don't know yet which of those Randi is. That's the map, and Vee doesn't know she's drawn it.

Meg's the one that got me. "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think." And then: "Vee let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." Oh, I felt that in my teeth. That's the coldest true sentence anyone's said to her in this book and it's said by the calm one with cheese on her fork, not unkindly, and it goes straight through her without touching anything. The book knows. That's the shape I keep asking for — it set the sentence down and walked away from it. Same thing with the phone: he texts, and I'm sitting there thinking, who wrote that. Two lines, no reason, two o'clock. That's the least information a human being has ever sent her and I know exactly which woman on a couch with wine in her mouth would find that funny. Randi isn't in this chapter and she's all over it. I couldn't stop feeling her under the table.

Small things. Vee sits between them — between Randi and Cassie in stats, between Meg and Kayla now; she's always the girl in the middle of two women and the book keeps seating her there and I don't think it's an accident. The glass turned a slow half-turn, and turned again: that's the cold cup at the Smoothie Bird, that's her tell now across nights, and I'm glad the book gave it to her and slightly worried it'll wear it out. "Didn't bother keeping the pride out of it" after "didn't bother to hide" last chapter — that's a phrase settling in where it shouldn't. And I've now had the truck three times. Lived it, told to Cassie, told to these two. Each telling's different and that's the point, I know that's the point, but around "You have to tell the shirt" I felt myself pulled back through a chapter I'd already been wrung out by, and I wanted a new room. The powerlifting is a good new fact, "my writing's no good after that" is a good line, and the man who lifts first thing and writes six hours and stops is exactly a man who'd build a bench and hide it under a table top. I noted that. I did not enjoy noting it.

And then: the house. The one thing I've been waiting for since the laminate counters and the deadbolt and "that's the whole address." Two o'clock, not a meal, Kayla clocked that faster than I did — the one time he isn't feeding her. Bring a change of clothes. The shirt's folded on her pillow, she's got her own tell, she's got Meg's line going by her unnoticed, and Saturday is a door. If Randi's on the other side of it I don't know how I'll breathe. If she isn't I don't know what he told her. Nobody got up to go. The light went thin. I'd have stayed at that table too.
