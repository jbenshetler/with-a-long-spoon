REACTION

There it is. The lunch. I've been asking since chapter 3 for Randi and Vee with nobody else at the table and the book made me wait sixteen chapters and then gave it to me in a green velvet booth where you could say anything, and what got said was worse than anything I'd been dreading, because it was all so *kind*.

"Which hip." "The left one." "Oh, all the best ones start at the left hip." I put the book down on my chest at that line and stared at the ceiling. That's the whole book in three exchanges — a meaningless sentence that lands like a blessing, and Vee going *yeah, yeah, yeah* one step down at a time, and me knowing, the way Vee doesn't, that she's being walked. And then the thing I didn't see coming: Randi opens the door all the way, gets Vee to the very edge of the one thing she never told Cassie, the thing she folded smallest — and shuts it. "You don't have to tell me." I think that's the cruelest line so far and it's said with love and I still can't decide who it was cruel *to*. Because Randi already has it. Of course she has it. "Tell me," in chapter 14, with him inside her — she's got the truck and the stomach and I'd bet she's got the satin. So what was this? Checking his version against hers? Or she wanted to hear Vee say it in Vee's own voice and then, at the last second, couldn't stand to. The thumb working the base of the glass. The muscle held behind the smile. Vee reads it as *she's moved, she's happy for me* and reaches for the kinder thing and I wanted to shake her, and I also thought: Vee's not entirely wrong. Randi *is* moved. It's just not about Vee's happiness. It's the same thing that went still on his lap at "she's easy to listen to." She's grieving something in that booth and she's doing it with a Rombauer in front of her and a girl across the table she can't stop looking at.

And she slipped. Did anyone else catch it? Vee said *he had me take my top off*. She never said platform. Randi said "with you up on the little platform. The pedestal." Randi knows about the riser. Because he built it and told her, or because she's stood on it herself, in that same light, and I honestly can't tell which is worse for Vee, and the book let it go by without a flicker and let *me* catch it. That's the withholding being a shape. That's the thing I stay for.

The tickle story — I don't believe a word of it. Randi doesn't go home with beautiful idiots, she told us so at the smoothie place, she left the lacrosse boy because he couldn't listen. That story was *built*. A scarf and a nobody, so Vee could hear "a little helpless can be a very good thing, done right" without hearing where it was coming from. It's the bench, pitched. And it worked — the heat came up in Vee and it wasn't the man in the picture, it was Randi with her wrists tied and her surface cracked open, and Vee has no shelf for it and I've been watching her not have a shelf for it since the coffee shop. "Weren't you scared, though?" was the truest thing she said all lunch and she got a speech in return. But here's what got me: the speech was true. "The only time some of us ever put them down." That wasn't a lie told to Vee. That was Randi telling the truth about the bench through a story about a scarf, and it's the closest she's come to the single room I keep asking for.

The money was the part I'd have texted about first if the kiss hadn't happened. The right side of the menu, reading it like a road in fog. One glass of wine costing more than her whole lunch. The pumps with the scuff she'd tried to rub out that morning, tucked under the booth. And then the card out before Randi's hand got there, because "the one thing she would not do at this table was let Randi buy her seat at it" — that's exactly right, that's the girl whose father says *it didn't come for free*, and Randi "did not reach again," and I couldn't tell if that was respect or a note taken. The book knows exactly what a Corolla with clean cloth seats means. I trust it more for that than for anything with a tape measure in it.

Then the fire lane. The *same* fire lane — the overhang, the red curb, where the cop chased him off before she got her sentence out — and Randi stops there like fire lanes happen to other people, and finishes the thing the siren interrupted, except it's her. Soft, and then not ending, the second press with something in it. And what does Vee do first? Not feel her mouth. Check the windows. Count the dark ones. That's her mother without the word for once, that's the whole scan-the-room ledger turned on herself for wanting a woman, and "French, it's a French thing" is the fastest anyone in this book has explained their own body away. I felt sick and I felt turned on and I'd like the book to know both were on purpose.

Small things. The window dropping half an inch to meet the door and rising again to seal her in — "tucking itself in around her" — good. The bracelet: white gold, plain, heavy, never off, and the book pointed at it twice, so it's not a bracelet. The Sinatra doing "Witchcraft" is the author winking at me and I'd have taken the wink out. And "It was gentle. It was kind. It was a door closing very softly" — I only needed the third sentence, I'd already felt the first two, and "the just-us register, the one Vee would have followed anywhere" is the book telling me what I'd just decided on my own. The word *warm* is carrying too much freight in this chapter and has been since chapter 11; I've started to hear it as a tic. None of that matters much against "which hip."

What I'm afraid of now: Cassie got the grinding story and the pillow fight, and Randi got the thing Vee never told her. Different currencies, and I know which one Vee thinks is worth more, and I know it's the wrong one. And I want the next chapter to be Randi driving home to the gravel with that kiss still on her, and him at the door with the deadbolt already turned, and I'm braced for the book to give me Pace instead of the single room again.
