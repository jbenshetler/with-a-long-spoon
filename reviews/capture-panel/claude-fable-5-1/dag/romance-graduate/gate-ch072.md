So that's a two-page chapter. I didn't know what I'd do with one and it turns out what I do is read it twice, because the first time was over before I'd sat down into it and the second time I was looking for where the money went.

It's in "Is this what you want?" Not *what happened*, not *what is she to you* — the same flat register Cassie used for *do you love him*, however many chapters ago, and the book made sure I remembered. And Vee *goes and looks*. That's the line I'd text at 1 a.m.: she actually went and looked, because Cassie asked it so plainly it seemed like a thing a person would just know about herself. Three people have asked her a version of this now — Pace with his hand still on her back, Randi with *how do you like it*, Cassie in a coat — and this is the first time she went for the answer before she went for the exit. She came up against the edge of the shape and stepped off. But she touched the edge. *Her neck said yes.* I'll take it.

Then the plank. *You don't like her. You've never liked any of it.* I sat up for that because I've seen that move four days ago on a county road — *you want Randi* thrown at Pace when the floor went — and here it is again at Cassie, same hand, same swing. What I noticed is who catches it. Pace, for once in his life, defended himself and she took it for a blanket. Cassie stepped over it without looking down. He gave her a wall to lean on; Cassie gave her nothing to push on and then handed her a cereal box. I know which one of those is love and I'm not sure Vee does yet.

And the Cocoa Pebbles — God. *You didn't have dinner.* Of course she didn't; she bought her way out of the loft and bought a walk home instead and got her neck kissed for her trouble. *Yeah.* First whole thing she's said since the doorway. A want the size of a bowl. I felt the book lean on that a hair — this series's whole spine is appetite and it knows I know — but it got out before it became a speech, and the thing underneath it is real: she can answer a hunger that fits in two hands, and the one on the desk she can't even size. The room is Cassie's room. The window's two inches open. The lower bunk. That's the only space in seventy-three chapters that nobody arranged for her, and it's the one place she can't lie, so she doesn't; she just runs out. *She never ran out.* I believed that.

What's got me at the edge of the chair is smaller than any of that. She said *brunch, Saturday* out of her ringing neck in the last chapter, and Saturday night is the hall on 460 with Sheri and Pace, and she's going to carry Randi's goodbye kiss — or the second turned-away one — straight from a loft to a dance floor where the one person who's seen the brunette is going to look her down keyhole to shoes. Nobody in this chapter mentioned it. That's why I'm still up.

The second quiet chapter in a row. I'm counting and I'm not resentful yet; this one was the held breath after Cassie's question and it needed its own page so the question didn't get swallowed by a scene change. But I'm counting.
