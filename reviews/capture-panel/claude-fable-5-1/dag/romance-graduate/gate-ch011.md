"Paul?" — snapping her fingers. She couldn't drink her coffee while he looked at Vee, she straddled him with the wine to hear about the froyo cup, and she sits down in a lecture hall and pretends she can't retrieve his name. I actually put the Kindle down on my chest for a second. That's not a lie, it's a whole performance of not-caring, and it's for an audience of one who has no idea she's an audience. The book keeps doing this thing where I'm the only person in the room who knows what I'm watching, and it should be exhausting and instead it's the reason I'm still here.

And then the bad-date story. I'll be honest, I nearly checked out on "My love life is a disaster. Friday. A date." — I have read the bestie's comic-relief bad-date monologue four hundred times, it's filler, it's the sound of a book stalling. Except by the third sentence I realized it's not a story, it's a sales brochure. "A man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She's not telling Vee about lacrosse boy. She's telling Vee what to value, so that when Vee says "he listened" she's already been handed the scale to weigh it on. Was there even a Friday date? I don't think there was a Friday date. I think Friday she was on her back on his bed with a wine glass.

The thing that actually got me, though — the thing I'd screenshot and send to a friend at 1 a.m. with no caption — is "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Vee has the dignified version *sitting right there*, "I made an exception," and Randi says the smaller truer thing *for* her, in a tender voice, like she's agreeing with something Vee hasn't said yet, and Vee just... takes it. Says yeah. Same play as the smoothie order — she says oat milk because Randi's standing there and "let herself not look at that either." Ten chapters ago her mother was the voice in her mouth. The mother's gone quiet and I was so happy about that over the froyo, and now I can see exactly who moved into the vacancy. Randi's just running the same play Pace runs, isn't she — hand back the pencil at the line she can finish herself. He does it with a menu. She does it with a sentence about the man. "The same act. A different owner." I wrote that down two chapters ago and didn't know it was going to be about this.

And then the heat finally, *finally* crossed the aisle, and it was one line. "Ask and then ravish." A beat late, low, at Vee's volume for the first time all day — and Vee's hands are already running down her own thighs on a high chair in a room full of matched girls and she's "half up the trail still" and "felt her there in it beside her." Vee thinks she's picturing Pace. I'm not sure who she's picturing. I'm not sure Randi is either.

The hand on the table. "Vee's my dear friend," fingers cool and damp from the cup, held there in plain view of Brooke and the tall one who drank her coffee and *inventoried* her. I know Vee read it as Randi spending something on her, and she's right, it did cost something — but I also know Randi does nothing in front of Brooke that isn't a move, and Brooke's smile "took it all in and did not change," which is the scariest sentence in the chapter. Brooke already filed "Pace." Now she's filed the hand. Vee walks home feeling *picked* and I'm sitting here thinking about what it means to be picked by a woman who locks every door first.

Cassie. God. "I'll see you at the dorm." Easy. Already going. I have been that friend who let it lie. I have also been the Cassie. That whole exchange took maybe a page and it hurt more than anything Brooke did.

But I'm putting the book on notice anyway. The hike is next. She's going out past the gravel with a man who asks and a picture Randi hung in her head, and if I get another chapter of sweetness with a hand-hold at the end I am going to feel played in a way that isn't the fun kind.
