REACTION

Okay. Okay. So that's how it starts.

I've been reading this book knowing — because the jacket told me — that there's a girl named Vee and two people who planned her, and for two chapters I've been sitting with Randi on a bench and under a duvet thinking *where is she*, and then this chapter just... walks me up a brick path for three pages and hands her to me laughing with her head thrown back. And I actually gasped. Not at anything sexual. At a redhead coming out of a dining hall. Because I knew what I was looking at and Randi didn't, and that's the whole trick the jacket promised and I didn't believe a book could actually pull off, and it pulled it off on *me*.

Back up, though, because the opening. The thing where he asks her — mid-stroke, with her own taste still in the kiss — *would you like to taste another woman*, and she comes before she can answer. I have read that question in probably forty books and it's always a negotiation, a Talk, somebody sitting cross-legged on a bed being Very Open. Here it's a question that gets answered by a body before the person it belongs to has caught up, and then he *doesn't take that as her answer*. He waits until she's dressed-in-herself again and asks it properly, and she lies, beautifully — "only in a threesome, with you" — and he sees exactly what the lie is for and lets her have it. That's the Pace I signed up for in chapter one. The man who leaves her the room to choose and then watches what she does with the room. It made me a little sick with how good it is, honestly, because I also know what he's doing with that information.

And that's the thing I'm turning over at 1 a.m. He's changing. Chapter one, he sat there hard and decided *that's not what this night was for*. This chapter he's got the sprinter undressing Randi from behind in his head, he wants the blazer girl *himself*, and when the redhead laughs he's already got her mouth at Randi's breast and himself there "to watch her find out." He's got an appetite now. He's not the pure attentive instrument anymore. And I don't hate it — I think it's the most honest thing the book's done with him — but the gentleness is starting to look like a technique that happens to also be gentle, and I don't know yet which way the book wants me to fall. I think it wants me not to know. Fine. Mercilessly tender, the jacket said.

The pointing game itself — I was grinning like an idiot. "I point. You pick." Randi going from offended propriety to *shopping* in a quarter mile, him feeling it in her hand like a dance follow leaning into a turn before it's called. And the kiss on the library walk, where he hasn't wiped his face and she tastes herself in public — that got me more than the bed did. The bed was good. The bed was summarized a little, honestly; after the bench in chapter one, three orgasms in a page and a half felt fast, and I wanted more of her and less of his assessment of her. "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it" — I rolled my eyes a quarter-inch. Every romance man knows that difference. But then he does something with the knowing that no romance man does, so I let it go.

The collision. God. Randi walking across a lawn and arranging an accident so clean the *other girl* apologizes — a decade of dance and three years of sorority "folded down into three seconds." That's the first time I've seen Randi aim the whole apparatus at someone and I understood in my stomach why Pace is the one watching from the light pole. He's not seducing Vee. He's going to let Randi do it, and he's going to watch Randi become someone while she does. And Randi comes back *glowing*, with her feet doing a happy thing on the brick, and says *yes* in the bed voice in daylight — and then, into his chest, "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I put the book down for a second there. Because that's the girl who locked herself in his bathroom two chapters ago, who had the key turning in the lock, who was never coming out again. And here she is, out. Lit up. For *this*. The book just told me what Randi's going to do with what happened to her — she's going to do it to someone else — and it told me with a joke and a kiss and a man saying *let's go feed you*. (The cheeseburger. "I can't eat that." Same girl. He's feeding her something she'll eat.)

I'm scared for Vee and I've known her for one paragraph of laughing. That's absurd and it's working.

Small suspicion: three chapters, two of them in Pace's head, and I still haven't been inside the protagonist. I'm trusting the book that it's sequencing this on purpose — show me the planners, then drop me into the planned — and the jacket backs that up. But I'm noting it. If chapter four is more Pace noticing things, I'll start to feel managed.
