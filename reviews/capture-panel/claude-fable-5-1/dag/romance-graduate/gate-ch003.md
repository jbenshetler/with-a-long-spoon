REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I put my phone down twice during this one and picked it back up both times.

First — the sex at the top. He asks her "would you like to taste another woman" *while he's inside her* and she comes on the question. Not on anything he's doing. On the question. I have read that scene attempted probably forty times in my life and it's always a man's fantasy wearing a woman's body, and this isn't that — the book stays with him watching her face do something she didn't authorize, and then the line that made me sit up: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." That's the whole thing, isn't it. That's why I trust this man even though the jacket has told me flat out not to.

And then the "only in a threesome, with you" move. God. The book nails exactly what she did — "the same act, a different owner" — and I knew it before the sentence told me, which is the one place I twitched: this book does like to turn around and explain the subtext it just dramatized perfectly. It does it maybe once a page. I'm forgiving it right now because the subtext is actually good, but I'm watching it.

The walk. I did not expect a chapter of two people looking at strangers on a quad to be the hottest thing I've read this month, and it's a *clothed chapter*, and I'm a little annoyed about it. The way the game turns under her — "she had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." The kiss by the library steps with his face still unwashed from her so that she tastes herself while she says *maybe* about a girl in a beanie. I actually said "oh no" out loud at that. And "I point. You pick." — that's the courtly thing from the jacket made concrete, and it's also a trap, and both are true at once, which is the register I've been begging for.

Then Vee comes out of the dining hall and I felt it go through *me*, which — that's the trick, right? The jacket told me I'd be ahead of her from the start and I am, and I still fell for it. The redhead laughing with her head thrown back, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten she could be seen," and Randi — who has built her entire self around being seen correctly — can't look away. The bare "yes." And then the shoulder-bump that Randi makes look like the other girl's fault. Three years of sorority and a decade of dance folded into a three-second con. Jesus. I'm a little in love with her and a little scared of her and the book wants both.

Here's what's turning in my stomach though, and I mean that as praise. Chapter one ended with Randi deciding, behind a locked bathroom door, that the real her was going back behind the face and never coming out. Chapter two, she can't cry. And now in chapter three she's glowing, doing happy feet on the brick, squeaking *yes* into his hair — and I genuinely don't know whether that's the real Randi getting out again despite herself, or whether this is the face finding the perfect project: someone else to be seen so she doesn't have to be. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is the most chilling line in the book so far and it's said in the voice she uses in bed. I think she's going to hand Vee to him so she can stop being the one on the table. I think she doesn't know that's what she's doing. I think Pace, who sees *everything*, is going to miss it because he's too busy being happy.

And the apple. "I can't eat that," automatic, not even a glance at the cheeseburger. "Let's go feed you" as the chapter's last line. He's going to keep feeding her and she's going to keep eating thin slices and she's going to find a girl who laughs with her whole throat and — I don't know what yet, and that's the point.

Texting a friend at 1 a.m.: "the girl from the blurb just showed up and the predator fell for her on sight and I think I'm the predator??" Three chapters in, this is the first book in a year where I can't predict the next one.
