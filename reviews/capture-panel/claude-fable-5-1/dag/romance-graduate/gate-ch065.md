The paper towel. Folded square under the glass so the sweat ring wouldn't mark the wood. I read that and put the phone down on my chest for a second, because that's him entire — he'll fix the lock, he'll stand the headlights on the door, he'll put a square of paper towel under the water he left you, and he will not say the word. I know what the water costs him: nothing. That's why it's perfect and that's why it's not enough, and the book knows I know, and it set it down anyway and let me drink it. Then the undershirt pulled down over her head to the knees and the kiss on the forehead "after he thought she was already gone" — the one thing he does when nobody's awake to receive it. I've been reading this man for sixty-six chapters and he's still only honest in the dark.

And then he made bread while she slept and I laughed, because of course he did, and then "I like her so much" twice, drunk and then ruined, and that was sweet and I was happy for her for about a page. She tried the sentence *I don't have to be jealous* "and it held from every side." Honey. It held because you were looking at the wrong woman. Technically true, maybe — Randi isn't a *friend*. That's the whole trick of him. He never lies. He just leaves the room and lets her furnish it.
