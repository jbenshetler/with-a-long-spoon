REACTION

Nobody came. That's the thing I'm sitting with. The man who built the dress is in a hotel in Berkeley, the woman who knelt and did the ankle strap isn't in the room, and the mother whose word is *floozy* is "right there on the phone, honey" in the bright voice — and the book just let the chair stay empty. I'd been waiting on this scene for thirty chapters with a whole cast in my head turning to look, and it gave me a dull warm room full of somebody else's parents and navy dresses and a girl with her hand pinned at her hip holding a seam shut. And then it gave me the second step.

God, the second step. "She took a breath, and made the second step long." That's the whole book in one sentence and it didn't need a single word after it. Nothing under the silk, and she *gave the room her leg* — not because he was watching, not because Randi told her to, not even to spite her mother, because none of them were there to perform for. She did it for herself in a room that wasn't going to turn on its own. "The sound a dull room makes when it wakes up." I felt that in my chest, clothed, three rows from a microphone, and I'm counting it as hot because it is.

And the relief. "I'm a little relieved. Which is a horrible thing to be." Then the line she can't hand to Cassie — nobody in the room who ever taught her what to be ashamed of. The book got close to tapping me on the shoulder there, and I'll allow it, because it's in her head and she's half-trying to say it out loud and failing, which is exactly how that sentence lives in a person. What I won't quite forgive is the last line, "whether, by now, the two came to the same thing." I *know*, book. I've known since the kitchen. You don't have to narrate the thesis.

But Cassie. Finally Cassie in a scene with her, and look at what she did with it: took the phone out of Vee's hand so she couldn't hide behind it, "You look—" and didn't finish, eyes bright at the corners, and then *took the photo*. "Somebody had to document it." Cassie's the one with the camera. Cassie's the one who was in the room. And — "streetlight." Pace told Randi that Vee comes into a room like somebody found the lamp, and Cassie has never heard that sentence in her life and she landed on the same image on her own, by the bad coffee, because she has eyes. In October. I nearly cried at a nickname.

Then the thing I'm suspicious of, in the best way: she sent him the whole frame, uncropped, and "that part was hers." That's the second chapter running where Vee ends holding something back — the kitchen sat in the room unnamed, hers; now the photo, hers. She's learning to keep a private room. She's learning it from two people who are experts, and she doesn't know that's where she learned it. And I notice she didn't send it to Randi. Randi dressed her for a door again — new heels, four inches, "dear," two fingers on the ankle strap — and the picture went to the man in Berkeley. I want to see Randi's face when she finds out she wasn't on the thread.

Four chapters without those two in a room together now and I'm not restless, because the book keeps handing Vee the room alone and she keeps doing something with it. But I'm ready for the collision. He opens that photo between sessions. He'll see the dress do what he built it to do. He won't see what she saw. And someone, eventually, is going to show it to Randi.
