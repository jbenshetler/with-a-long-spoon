REACTION

Two pages. First one of those I've ever hit and I've been braced for it since the back cover — and it turns out it's one beat, the zipper, and the book had the nerve not to pad it. It opens on cereal milk gone brown and ends on "never in her life been so happy" and I read the last line with the jacket in my other hand, which is what the jacket was for. If it had tacked this onto the top of the next chapter I'd have lost that cut. So, provisionally: technique. I'll be watching to see if it does it again when it has nothing to cut on.

The zipper. God. She can tell Cassie the apron and the Chianti and the garlic and lift the whole middle out "clean as a ladle" — and then she reaches for the skirt and the next thing coming off is not a story, it's on the floor, and there's nothing left to say. That's the wire, not the cure. Last night the shame ran the whole length of her and didn't close her; this morning it's back and it's got a new face and the face is *Cassie*. Not her mother — she says the word can come and she doesn't mind it, and I believed her. It's being bare three feet from the one person who'd actually look. And I know the jacket's pointing me at a different woman, but the book just had Vee unable to take her skirt off in front of her roommate and I'm not going to pretend I didn't feel that in a place the book may not have aimed for. Or did. I can't tell with this one anymore and that's why I'm still here.

Cassie. That half second crossing her face "before it found a name." "In October. I have eyes." She saw the plum toenails that Vee hid in flats, she saw them weeks ago probably, she's been *waiting* with that line. And then she doesn't pull the seam. She hears the fra diavolo and says "a man made you dinner" and "Mm" and lets it stand — and gets up for her game that Vee doesn't ask one question about, and calls her *lover* on the way out, and the book doesn't underline any of it. Cassie's getting the edited cut and the word "boyfriend" and she said it "kindly, certainly, wrong" and I wanted to climb into the room and tell her. That's the second time Vee's set her down without noticing and I'm starting to think Cassie's the one who's going to be standing there when this comes apart, holding the version she was given.

Small things that got me: no brush in his house that would go through her hair. Two towels on the rod, we know that, and no brush — he planned for her and didn't think of that, and I don't know whether that's sweet or whether it tells me which woman that bathroom is usually for. She drove home bare. The green is folded in thirds on his counter and she's walking around with the smile that came back at every red light and she doesn't know who else has a key.

One flinch: "It was a kindness. She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." It's her voice, it's earned, but the book said the lie twice and the second time was for me. I noticed. I'm still here.

She hasn't thought the name. That's a week, a night, a morning, and a whole chapter now. I've stopped calling it burial.
