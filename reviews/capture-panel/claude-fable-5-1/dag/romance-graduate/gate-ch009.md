REACTION

Okay so I've been waiting for this one — Vee's first real evening with him — and I went in bracing, because I know what the bench is and she doesn't, and I know Randi drank wine off his mouth to hear about her froyo cup. That's the thing this book keeps doing to me: I'm not reading a date, I'm reading a girl walk into a room I've already seen the furniture in. And it's *still* sweet. That's the trick. I sat there warm and a little sick the whole time.

The lobby bit got me first. Her having him to herself for a second before he sees her — the razor-blush on his jaw, the boots he already takes care of instead of new ones bought to look lived-in. I've read five hundred "he was wearing a suit that cost more than my rent" paragraphs and this is the first time a man's outfit on a date told me who he *is* instead of what he spent. And then the elevator doors closing on her because she forgot to get off. Love her. She's so much more of a person than she thinks she is.

"May I choose?" — I actually put the book down on my chest. Because I heard it twice. Once as Vee hears it, this old-fashioned courtesy, the relief of handing something across the table. And once as the reader who watched him stand at a bench and say *Tell me your word.* It's the same man doing the same thing at a dinner table with bread, and she's saying *yes, please* just like Randi said it, and she has no idea she's rehearsing. That's not a metaphor the book is shoving at me. It's just there if you've been paying attention, and I have.

And the eating. God. This whole book is about who's allowed to eat. Randi and her thin apple slices and *I can't eat that*, and now here's Vee hollowing out since noon and tearing bread and not watching herself do it, for maybe the first time in her life, and he doesn't make anything of it. The hollow going down into "something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." I felt that in my own stomach. Contrast with Randi, who's on his couch pressing his hands to her breasts to get him to *tell* — the two women are being fed completely different things by the same man and I don't know yet which one is being fed better. Probably Vee. Which is why it's going to hurt.

The right-hand thing — always the right, never the left — she filed it and so did I. I don't know what it is. I don't know if it's a culture thing about the food or a Pace thing or nothing, and I like that the book let her notice it without explaining it to me. Same with the mint tea in the brass cages. Nobody explained it. He just knew and she just let him.

Now the honest part. This is a quiet chapter. So was the pool. So was the froyo. The only real heat since the bench has been Randi on the couch with the wine, and that was two pages. I'm not skimming — the writing's too good and the dramatic irony's too loud for me to skim — but I'm *aware*. The stretch in the middle where the food comes and the evening goes soft at the edges and she loses the thread of what he's saying — it's beautiful, and it's doing something (she stops managing, she lets him carry it, that's the whole seduction in miniature) — but a small unromantic part of me was going *okay okay I get it, she's melting, when does someone touch someone.* That's the reader I've been trained into, and I don't fully trust her, but she was in the room.

Then the kiss saved it. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — I groaned, because that's a line I've read a hundred times and it usually means fifteen pages of manufactured restraint. And he just *smiled and started walking.* No lean. No patient wait. He gave it up completely, and she's the one who stops him with the hand. She's the one who says "I'm making an exception." She kisses him small and he gives her back exactly as much and not one inch more, and then she has to decide *again*, and she does. That's the jacket copy doing what it promised — *choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done* — and it is erotic, a closed-mouth kiss under a tree is more charged than most of the sex I've read this year, because I know what his restraint costs and what it's for.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she thinks the space he's leaving her is respect. it IS respect. it's also the whole trap. i can't tell if i want to warn her or watch.* And the thing I'm scared of isn't Pace. It's Randi. Randi who dropped her stride on the quad, Randi who can't hold her face over how much she wants this girl. Vee's going to be fed and fed and fed and she's going to be so happy and I'm going to be sitting here knowing that Randi's coming to the table too.
