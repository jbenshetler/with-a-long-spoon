He took two breaths. I read that line and put the Kindle face-down on my chest for a second. She built that sentence for *weeks* — I've been watching her build it, she said *Peter* to a sleeping man and *love* to no one and *maybe* to Cassie, and this is the thing she made out of all of that — and she handed it to him in his own language, the only language he's ever given her, crackers and a meal, and his hand started two answers and put both down and he breathed twice and then he said *I love how good we are together.* What your body does. He heard her. That's what the two breaths are. He heard exactly what she meant and he answered the sentence she said instead, and the thing that makes me want to throw something is that it's the same move as "thank you for telling me what she said" — he's never once lied to her and he's never once given her the thing. Courtly and exact. He's exact at her. And then "I never had this either," which is the closest he's come to anything, and which I'll be turning over for a week because I don't know if it's true or if it's the kindest available deflection, and neither does she, and the book's not going to tell either of us.

And I noticed — because of course I did, I was in that room on his couch four chapters ago — that Randi said *say something nice to me* and got *you are extraordinary*, and Vee said a sentence about love and got a sentence about how good they are in bed. He gives each of them precisely what they put on the table, measured, nothing over. Randi asked for a compliment and got a perfect one. Vee asked for nothing out loud, deliberately, and that's what she got. He's not cruel. That's what's killing me. He's *accurate*.

The porch half is the chapter. The quilt that smells like him, the cup already cool enough to drink, the reaching and the not-moving, her face doubled in the glass — she's been thinking about the sentence so long it comes out like a memorized poem, and then the *wait* behind her breastbone that goes "where small *waits* go when the quilt is warm." That's the whole fall in one paragraph. She's been putting things in that place since the practice-room bathroom and the book just walked into the room and named it — the pile, the small repair work — I've been calling it the shelf for ten chapters and I actually said "oh" out loud when the book put its hand on it.

Where it dipped: the laundry room. The dryer bit is fine as a beat — two days later, her body doing the thing before her mind — but then it keeps going, and by the time I hit *She had said her best sentence. He had not said the sentence back* in italics I was being handed what the porch had already put straight into my chest. I don't need the book to underline it. The two breaths were the underline. I forgive it because the last line earns its way back — *the cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech* — and because of what that line actually means, which the book, to its credit, doesn't spell out: this is the first thing she's not going to carry to the booth. Every Saturday she hands Randi all of it, uncropped, the toes, and now there's a sentence that isn't going anywhere. Randi won't hear it. Pace won't hear it twice. Cassie's the one who'd ask it flat and Cassie's not getting it either. She's got a secret from all three for the first time, and it's a secret made out of being *received warmly*.

Chapter title. Seconds — the second breath, the second helping she didn't get, second best. Fine. I saw it. It's still good.
