Twenty chapters. I said one more walk home and I'd be skimming, and the book heard me, and it went and put the Chianti out to breathe.

"Famished" — and she hears it a beat after he does. The green bought "for the color." The bra "because it would be silly." Sure, honey. The flats so the polish is hidden. She dressed for the floor of his bedroom and wouldn't say so even to the mirror, and the book let me sit next to her in the not-saying without once elbowing me.

And then it delivered. Not fade, not euphemism, not the closed door I've been suspicious of since Randi's hand went out in chapter ten. Him on his knees at her hips, the satin peeling, the strings coming with it "obvious and excessive" — I put the phone down for a second. What actually got me, though, wasn't the acts. It's that the shame doesn't get cured. I've read a hundred of these where the right man's mouth dissolves the mother's voice and she's *healed*, and I've never believed one of them.
