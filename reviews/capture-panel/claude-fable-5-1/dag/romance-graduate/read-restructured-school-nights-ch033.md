REACTION

Cassie. Finally. I've been holding a place for her for ten chapters and the book finally walked her into the room and let her be the person I knew she was — book facedown, *you're a public utility*, and then the dry look giving over, *glad*. That one word did more for me than Randi's whole "best friend in the whole world" speech with the sugar on the lip. And then she just asks it. Flat, with her book on her chest, no setup, no fishing. *Do you love him.* The one question nobody in this book has asked Vee out loud, and the one person who asked it is the one who gets nothing out of the answer. Randi would've gotten there in six moves and a mimosa. Cassie got there in four words and went back to her reading.

And Vee said *maybe*. Three nights ago she lay in his bed and let the word be true in her own chest and whispered *Peter* to a man who was asleep, and when the one person who'd just hold it asked her straight, it came out as *maybe, I think so, it's all so fast.* I actually put the phone down on that. Not because I didn't believe it — because I did. That's exactly what she'd do. She can tell Randi *he checked* in a sunlit room full of strangers and she can't tell Cassie she loves him in the dark of her own room. The confessions all go one way and it's the wrong way, and she doesn't know it, and the book didn't explain it to me, it just let her say *maybe* and let Cassie let it stand. That's the thing I keep coming back to this book for. It trusts me.

*Not enough on.* Cassie read a tomato burn on the side of a breast and knew Vee was topless at his dinner table and said it like weather. She has eyes, she has always had eyes, and she's never once been cruel with them. I want her to meet Randi in a room and I'm scared of it.

Then the pool, and I'll be honest, I almost skimmed. I saw *four hundred easy to start, then the long middle* and thought, oh, we are going to swim the whole set, aren't we. We did. And it got me anyway, because the writing is real — *she never had to decide what to swim any more than she'd decided the second verse of a hymn* — and because the swim did the thing the swim was for. It took the words off. *Do you love him* went to a pressure with dread riding it, *did he mind* went to nothing, and then there was just the count. And in the quiet part, four strokes' worth, she wondered whether he was that good or whether she'd built him that good out of wanting. Four strokes. That's the first time Vee has had my doubt. I've been the one counting since chapter one and for four strokes she counted with me and then the wall came. I don't know if I'm relieved or sick.

Here's what nags: I already know the answer to *did he mind*. The book put the drawer on the thirteenth and this on the ninth, so I've been carrying his answer around since last chapter while she's still swimming on the question. Either that's the jacket's promise working — I'm a step ahead and feeling the degrees — or the book shuffled its own calendar and didn't notice it let the air out. I'm going to be generous because the drawer was worth it, but I noticed, and I noticed that *did he mind* had no weight for me when it should've had some.

And I noticed this is the second quiet one in a row. Last chapter's heat was all in the rearview — *they hadn't made the kitchen* told from the cold side of the sheet — and this one has nobody in it but Vee and her roommate and a lane line. That's fine. Twice is fine. The best scene in this book might still be two people talking. But I'm counting now the way Vee was counting empty nights, and if the next one is Vee thinking about a sandwich I'm going to start thumbing for Randi's name.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: she told the one honest person *maybe* and she tells the liar everything. And she has no idea which is which.
