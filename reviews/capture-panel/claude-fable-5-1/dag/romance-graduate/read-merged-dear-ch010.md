REACTION

Okay, so this is the chapter where the dramatic irony finally got its teeth into me and I didn't love how it felt — which I think is the point. Vee, glowing, in the bean bag: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." Girl. I know. That's exactly the problem. The jacket told me that line in so many words and the book had the nerve to put it in her own mouth as her proudest moment and NOT underline it. No wink, no narrator leaning in. It just let me sit there knowing what I know about a bench in a bedroom off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel. I actually put the phone down for a second.

And Cassie. Cassie is doing the thing I keep hoping someone in these books will do — she's the only person in Vee's life with no angle, and she's clocking it. "How much did he give you to drink." The state troopers joke that's not entirely a joke. "Borrow good ones." After that flat "Goodbye, Vee" back in the lecture hall, I'm reading every dry line out of her as a woman who can smell something and has decided not to be the one who ruins it. The exchange about the address — "Off six-fourteen. That's the whole address." — is Cassie saying *you don't know this man* in the only register Vee can hear it in. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: Cassie is the heroine of this book and nobody has told her yet.

What I don't love: this is a short retelling of a chapter I just read, and for a page I was getting the brass cages again, and I noticed my thumb. I know what it's for — I needed to see Vee *own* the kiss out loud, and the spin into the bean bag is the realest joy she's had on the page — but it's the third Vee chapter in a row where the heat is somebody else's memory. The hike is on the table, the house is on the table, and I've been carrying what's in that house since chapter one. Pace has had this girl's number for what, four days? I'm not bored. I'm *impatient*, which is different, and this book has earned enough trust that I'll let it have its ninety seconds of girl talk. But I want the gravel road now.
