REACTION

Okay so this is the Cassie chapter, and I have to admit I've come around on Cassie. Three chapters ago she was the flat one, the "goodbye, Vee" weirdo, and now she's the only person in this book asking the right questions and getting sitcom answers. "What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers." "That's the whole address. *Off six-fourteen*." "Is he rich?" — and Vee doesn't know. Vee doesn't know anything. She has a road and a trail and the word "lovely," and she's counting it off like a dossier. I have been Vee in that bean bag. I have said "I know where he lives and everything" about a man whose last name I could not have spelled.

And the line that actually got me, the one I'd screenshot: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." Girl. GIRL. I know about the bench. I know about the wine in Randi's mouth. I know about Pace standing at the light pole watching Randi bump into you and *making it look like you walked into her*. And here's the thing that makes it nasty in the good way — she's not wrong. She did pull him back under that tree. He was walking away. Every yes freely given, the jacket said, that's the problem, and I thought that was a tagline and it turns out it's the whole knife. I felt a little sick reading her say it so proudly. I also felt happy for her. Both at once, which is the trick this book keeps doing to me.

The little echoes are doing a lot of quiet work too. Hiking — Randi listed hiking as one of Pace's unnamed things, back in the pointing game. Now Vee gets the same itinerary. "Real shoes" and I immediately thought of Randi's heels coming off one at a time on the bench. Cassie's "is he rich?" and I'm thinking about the cheap laminate counters and the quartersawn oak cabinet — no, Cass, he's not rich, he *builds* things, and that's so much worse. The book trusts me to carry all that. I like being trusted.

Now the honest part. This is the second quiet chapter in a row — really the third without any actual heat on Vee, if I'm counting the pool. Chapter 9 earned its length; it was a slow burn that actually burned, that kiss under the tree was a whole meal. This one is a two-and-a-half-page debrief. I still don't know if these short chapters are a technique or a shortcut — this one feels like the author wanted the "nobody did anything to me" line to have its own room and nothing else in it, and fine, I'll grant that, it works as a room. But I'm noticing the pattern now: Randi's chapters are where the heat lives and Vee's are where the sweetness lives, and I've been promised for ten chapters that these are going to collide. "Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" — that's the house. That's the bench. The next chapter had better take her there, or at least most of the way.

Also, small thing, and maybe it's just me — the "off six-fourteen" back-and-forth ran one beat too long. I got it the second time Cassie repeated it. That's the moment I felt the chapter padding a scene that's already thin.

But Vee spinning in a room too small for it and nearly taking out the closet door — that I'll keep. She's so happy. I know what's out past the gravel and she's borrowing shoes for it.
