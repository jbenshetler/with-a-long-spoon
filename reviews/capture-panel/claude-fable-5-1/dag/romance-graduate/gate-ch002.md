REACTION

Okay, so I closed Chapter 1 thinking I knew what the shape of this was — the bench, the begging, "there you are, Randi," the bathroom lock. I knew *who she was going to become* after that. And then Chapter 2 doesn't go to Pace. It doesn't go to Vee, who's supposed to be the protagonist of the book I bought. It goes to a sorority common room at 9:50 a.m. on a Saturday, three girls and a laptop, and I thought, *fine, bridge chapter, show me her re-armoring.*

What I did not expect was for Brooke to be the hottest scene so far in terms of pure dread. That's four minutes of small talk and I was holding my breath the whole way. "Engineering, isn't it" — landing him a notch down without reaching for it — I have *been in that room*. Not a sorority, but I know that woman. The one who doesn't stand up because she doesn't have to. The one who asks about the paper you lied about and lets you answer, and then asks *where*, and lets you answer that too, and then — "I was going to ask about it last night and you'd already left" — oh my god. And the release at the end, "Sorry to grill you. Go." Randi doesn't even get to say the three lines she'd prepared. That's the one that got me. Brooke decided the conversation was over, and the chapter calls it "the cleanest hit," and it is. I've watched a hundred fictional villains monologue and none of them scared me the way that *go* did.

And here's what I love — the chapter never tells me Brooke knows. Brooke might know everything. Brooke might just be Brooke. Randi herself is Standards, she sits on the board that polices this stuff, and the book lets me hold that irony without underlining it. Nobody explained it to me. Thank you.

The matching laugh. "She had not had time to choose the pitch." That one line did more to tell me what Randi is than the whole bathroom-mirror sequence last chapter, and I liked the bathroom mirror. Because last night she decided, with a deadbolt-turning finality, that the face would go back up and stay up — and this morning the face is *a fraction late*. Twice. She's not as good at it as she was on Friday, and she doesn't know that yet, and I do. That's the jacket promise — "the reader, ahead of her from the start" — actually paying out.

Then the room. The closet she opens and can't remember why. The duvet that is "adequate" — *light and synthetic and adequate*, it is not, it was a duvet — against the cashmere blanket she *brought to his house* because she gets cold after. The book doesn't say "she wanted his blanket." It just lets the duvet be a duvet. I felt that in my chest. The shoes still on, the cardigan buttoned, the bed made because she never slept in it.

"He's hard to say no to" heard in a different key. "It was not a key she could sing in." Okay, Helen Rivers. Okay.

And this is where I want to be honest about the gap. HEAT is near zero this chapter, and I'm somebody who said depth alone won't hold me. But — I'm not bored. I'm not restless. The not-crying at the end is written as "not a door, there being no door," and that's exactly the thing I keep reading romance for and never get: a woman's interior shown at the exact resolution she experiences it, not a diagnosis. The hairline fracture in her is ten times more charged for me than another orgasm would have been right now, *because* I watched what it cost to get there.

What I'm suspicious of: this is two chapters, and Vee hasn't appeared. The jacket sold me a girl who's being invited into something, and so far I'm reading the two people who do the inviting. I know that's the design — I'm being shown the hands before the card trick — but if Chapter 3 is *also* interiority with no body in it, I'm going to start feeling the pattern. I'm not there yet. This is the one quiet chapter I always grant. I'd just like a pulse in the next one.
