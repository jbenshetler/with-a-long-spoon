REACTION

Okay, so that's the cold shower after chapter one, and I mean that as a compliment, mostly. I went in wanting to know what Randi does with the thing that happened to her on that bench, and the book's answer is: she does the walk of shame past three sorority sisters at 9:50 on a Saturday and gets vivisected by a girl who never stands up from her laptop. I have *been* in that common room. Brooke's "Engineering, isn't it" — knocking him down a peg without even reaching for it — and then the smile "absorbing" the correction to math PhD without conceding the notch. And "short-cute." God. Kinsey's laugh coming in a hair above pitch and Randi matching it before she's chosen to. That whole scene is the most socially exact four minutes I've read in a romance in a year, and nobody took their clothes off.

What got me was the duvet. "It was not — it was a duvet." She can't finish the sentence because finishing it means *cashmere, at his house, that I brought there myself,* and she is not going to think that. And the "He's hard to say no to" replayed in a different key — that line gave me a little chill, because in the kitchen it was a flirty what-could-I-do, and in her cold room it sounds like something a woman says about a thing she can't consent her way back out of. I don't think the book is telling me he's dangerous. I think it's telling me she can't tell the difference between being wanted and being *found*, and the second one feels like a trap to her because she's never had a door for it. Which — the deadbolts. She locks his door first thing. She locks his bathroom. Last night's "not ever" went over "quiet as a deadbolt." And this morning the sorority door is unlocked and she closes it *quietly*, which is wrong for her. I'm noticing the author keeps handing me locks and I'm not sure I'm supposed to be noticing yet.

Here's the 1 a.m. text, though: I reread the jacket. Vee's the girl. Randi's the "glamorous new friend" who steers her at Pace. So these two chapters are the prequel Vee will never get to read — I'm inside the private thing between the two people who are about to make a game out of somebody else. And Randi just decided, in a mirror, that the one he found is never coming out again. So what does she do with a man who *sees* like that and whom she can't stand to be seen by? She finds him another girl to look at. She hands him Vee so she can keep the face and keep him too, sort of, from one chair over. I don't know if that's right but if it is, it's cold and it's sad and I'm completely in. The jacket said the reader would be ahead of Vee from the start and I feel that now like a hand on my back.

The one place I twitched: "She walked to the vanity. She did not sit. She walked to the bed. She did not sit." That's the rhythm literary books use when they want me to feel Meaningful Nothing, and for about two lines I thought, oh no, here it comes. Then the duvet line landed and I forgave it. Also: this chapter's short, and it's the first quiet one, and it moved her more than an inch, so I'm fine. But I was told wickedly explicit and I got one chapter of it. Chapter three needs either Vee or Pace's hands, preferably both.
