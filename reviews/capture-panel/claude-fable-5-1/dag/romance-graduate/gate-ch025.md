Okay, so I texted nobody at 1 a.m. because who would I text — "the glamorous friend just put the college girl on a credit card and I felt it in my stomach"? But that's where I am.

Start with the room, because the book started there and I'll admit I got a little itchy. Both girls flanking her, the made bed as evidence, Cassie having "cracked it hours ago" — I love that Cassie reads the bed and not the dress, that the forty seconds Vee spends being a student Cassie spends watching Vee's face because the friend is the only part of this Cassie's ever been able to see. That's the dog again, and she's still not barking, and "Oh, yes" easing something in her face made me want to lie down. But then the professor put Simpson's paradox up on the board and I actually said "oh, come on" to my Kindle. Each year alone one man wins, together the other does, nobody touched a number. I get it. Two of them in the plan, one of her, and the total isn't what the parts say. The book didn't underline it, I'll give it that — it just left it on the board and walked off — but it's the first time this thing has set a metaphor on a table and pointed at it, and I've DNF'd books for the third one of those. This is the first. I'm counting.

Then the dorm, and this is where it got good, and where it got cold. Randi in the doorway making the Stevie Nicks poster visible again — that's exactly how a certain kind of woman enters a room you've stopped seeing. And Vee couldn't just hold up the hanger. Of course she couldn't. She had to go put it on, because she wants to be *looked at* now, that's who she is since the box, and Randi's first look was "the way you take in a thing you're going to have to account for." Account to *whom*, Randi. She knew it was charmeuse. She knew the weight. She read the bias with her fingers up to the shoulder like she was checking his work — she knows his tables and his hands and I'd bet she knows the sound the machine makes at three in the morning — and then she handed Vee the word for it: *to*, not *for*. Randi always hands Vee the word. That's her whole function in this girl's life, and it's also, if you tilt it, a woman labeling something. And then "he was up all night finishing it, I watched him sleep" and Randi going somewhere behind her own face, and Vee's heart *going out to her*. Poor Randi with her boys in a hurry, nobody who ever sat up a night over anything of hers. Vee. Baby. He made her a pot of chicken in white sauce and she drew one line through the top of it and left. I know that and you don't and it's awful to hold.

The shoe store was the best scene in the chapter and I want to say why, because it's the one where Vee was *winning*. Her store, her racks, she knows which aisle lies about sizes. Yeti shoe. "One brisk walk from becoming a sandal." And she got the laugh — the real one, "the one that costs her something to give" — I believe that laugh completely, and I think Randi did too, and I think that's the "as real for the two who started it" thing on the jacket starting to bite. Vee made Randi laugh for real on Vee's own ground, and then Randi said "same size, same everything, it's practically a marriage," and then she said "which is exactly why you're not buying that," and took her off her ground. I saw it happening and I couldn't grab Vee's arm.

Because the boutique. God. The price on the sole in pencil. The "quiet the exact shape of what was in her account" — that sentence is the whole book to me, she's been right-side-of-the-menu since the tomato soup and now she's standing in a room where she can't afford the door. And Randi kneeling. In her own beautiful clothes on the pale floor. Foot in both hands, turned to the light, "Mm," a thumb once across the plum on the big toe — that's *his* move. That's the mountain, bare foot in his hand in cold air, *hmm*. Randi's doing Pace's play, the exact gesture, and Vee's hand shot out to her shoulder because the floor tipped, and I'm not going to pretend it didn't get me too. It did. Clothed, in a shoe store, one thumb. Then the shoe *holding* her, "being stood up by one," and the arithmetic switching drivers mid-sentence: the shoes aren't a want, they're what the dress is *owed*. Every yes freely given. That was the problem. She put the debit back and took out the card with nothing behind it and *smiled*, because the one thing she wouldn't do in that room was flinch, and I've been that girl at that counter, and the book didn't stop to tell me it was sad, it just let Randi buy loafers for several times that much without looking down and walk out in them. That was the cruelest beat in the book so far and there was no blood in it. I said last time I wasn't sorry for Vee yet. This is the first pang. Not for the sex — she's getting fed better than anyone on this shelf — for the *money*. She'll have that number next month and neither of the people who planned her will.

"You can put it in the trunk, it's not going to spoil" — and she laughed, and it was a real laugh, and she thought she'd never had a friend like this. That's true. She hasn't. That's what I keep not being able to file.

Where I am: that's the debrief I said I'd take one more of. It was a good one, funny and mean and Randi's hands did something new. But the pattern's now date-debrief-date-debrief with one Randi/Pace kitchen in between, and I'm reading this book for that kitchen. Randi has the toenail, the "lover," the credit-card number, and a girl whose feet are her size. She's going to drive up the gravel with all of that. If the next one isn't that or the induction, I'll notice.
