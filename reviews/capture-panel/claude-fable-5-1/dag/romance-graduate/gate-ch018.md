Vee sitting on "just the edge" of Cassie's bed. Cassie's hand on her knee, once, and taken back. Here's what I actually sat up for, though. The title. "Turned Up." Vee tells it like it's the most tender thing anyone's ever done — he thought of her hours before, he set the thermostat for her bare skin — and Cassie goes still over it, and I'm sitting there going: honey, he turned the heat up for Randi in chapter fourteen. Henley, jeans, thermostat. He keeps that house too warm for whoever's driving up the gravel. The same act, a different owner — the book said it about the bench and now it's saying it about the radiator, and Vee's building a cathedral on it. That's the psychologically merciless part and I felt it in my teeth.

I'm ahead of both of them and it isn't fun being ahead. It's like watching someone check the wrong lock.

What Vee kept back is what I keep turning over. She gave Cassie the box, the bra, the kneeling, "I was grinding on him" — she made the whole afternoon a comedy with a punchline. She did not give Cassie the satin. Everyone in this book keeps the true thing back from the person they're telling — Pace kept the truck from Randi, Randi kept the mirror from Pace, and now Vee's learned it too. Nobody taught her. She just knows now what's too good to hand over. That's growth and it's also exactly what a plan needs.

Heat-wise this was a comedown from seventeen, which it had to be. One simmer, all of it in Vee patting her own ass and "*I* was grinding on *him*" with her whole face going wicked. Fine. The dress needs sewing, which means weeks, which means somebody's going to fill those weeks, and I know who has a car that sounds expensive on gravel.
