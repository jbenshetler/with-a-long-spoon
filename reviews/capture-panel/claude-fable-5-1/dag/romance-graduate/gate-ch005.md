REACTION

Okay so I knew it was coming. The jacket told me, chapter 3 told me, Randi's thumbs on her phone told me — and I still sat up when the bell over the door went. That's the thing this book keeps doing to me: I'm ahead of Vee the whole way and it doesn't cost me anything, it *adds*. I'm watching a girl get handed the best afternoon of her semester and I know who paid for it, and I'm not sure whether to be sick or to want in.

The part that got me wasn't the math and it wasn't even him. It was Randi's hands. The cup coming up to her mouth twice and no sip. "Randi" as one bare word — the exact flat thing Cassie did to *her* in the lecture hall, and now she's doing it to her own boyfriend across a coffee-shop table because she's got to sit there and watch him give Vee the look. The look she got on the bench. The "chosen" look. And Vee's brain files it as *she's bored, wrap it up* — I actually said "oh honey" out loud. Randi is watching the man who made her beg do the thing he does to somebody else, and she chose this, she set it up, she texted him in, and she still can't drink her coffee. I don't know if that's jealousy of Pace or jealousy of Vee or both and I don't think Randi knows either, and that's the first time I've felt genuinely sorry for her since she locked the bathroom door.

Then Cassie. "The way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." I cackled. Four words a day and she spends them like that. Vee thinks Cassie can't see anything and Cassie's the only person at the table with clear eyes — she's clocked Randi, she's half-clocked Pace, she said *goodbye* last chapter like a girl who's already lost. I would read a whole chapter of Cassie. I'm scared for her.

And Pace — fine, yes, the forearms, the cuffs rolled back two turns, the "may I call you." I felt the *may* the same second Vee did. But what actually turned me on was the pencil. He asked for it with his eyes, marked the margin, and handed it back at the exact line where she could finish it herself. That's the same man who wouldn't touch her until she'd put her own hands behind her back, the same man who said "not yet" until the *please* was real. He runs one play. It's a very good play. And the crossed-mark thing — two things that won't give on their own, put them at right angles and they turn into a single plain thing — I'm not stupid, I know what that's a picture of, and I'm a little unnerved that the book made me feel it as *hot*. Substitution. Who's being substituted for whom, Helen.

Vee borrowing Randi's name-bit was the wince of the chapter. Trying on the poise like a jacket off the back of a chair. It fit. That's the awful part — it fit, and he liked it, and she took her own name back one second later. She's already becoming someone. Nobody told her.

What I'm holding against it: this is two quiet chapters in a row, and I'm counting. Chapter 4 was coffee, chapter 5 was coffee. I trust this book because chapters 1 and 3 showed me it will go all the way into a room and stay there — but I want the phone to light up and I want it to lead somewhere with lamps on. The Bessel's correction stretch was the one place I felt the math tutorial going on a beat past me, and I don't want to feel that again next chapter.
