REACTION

Okay so I know. I know the whole thing. I watched him build a bench for her and I watched her ask "do you think she'll like the bench," and now I'm sitting in a coffee shop on Wilson watching a girl get her integral solved by the nicest man in the world and my stomach is *clenched*. That's the whole trick of this book and it keeps working on me: Vee gets to have the best hour of her life and I have to have it with her and I can't warn her and honestly I'm not sure I'd want to. "Pardon me. It sounds like you're having trouble with math." I actually said *oh no* out loud. He gave up his place in line. He had never been in line. Randi was texting him from three feet away — "some boy," Vee thinks, *amused* — and I had to put the Kindle down for a second.

The hand on the coffee cup. That's the thing I'd text you about at 1 a.m. Randi's hand goes to the cup and no sip happens, twice, three times, and Vee's brain helpfully files it as *she's bored, this is going on too long for her*, and I know — from the water bottle cap under her thumbnail last chapter — what Randi does with her hands when something's getting past the face. She is watching the man who got her to beg sit across from the girl she picked and be *kind* to her for five minutes about econ. She wrote the script and she's not enjoying it the way she thought she would. Or she's enjoying it in a way she didn't budget for. I can't tell yet and I love that I can't tell.

And Vee. God, Vee is so much more interesting than I was braced for. The borrowed bit — "yes, like the university" — she tried on Randi's little swerve and it *fit*, and she wanted her name back a second later. That's a real girl. The forearms, the half-second past where she'd have chosen. The "may." The way she could read how he handled the problem — a tutor's path, not a mathematician's — with her econ eye, and then *couldn't finish the thought* twice. The book keeps doing that: showing me her seeing it, then showing me her not letting herself see it, and that's the exact move Randi did in the bathroom mirror. They're the same kind of girl in different fabric. Chapter 1 told me that at the dining hall doors and now I believe it.

And the math is a love scene. I don't say that lightly, I've read a hundred "he taught me to shoot pool" scenes. Two things that won't give on their own, at right angles, become one plain thing you suddenly know what to do with. He wrote her the ugly formula and then told her not to carry it. He asked for the pencil with his eyes. He gave it back at the exact line where she could do it herself. That's the same man who waited for the right *please* on the bench — he's doing to Vee's brain what he did to Randi's body, leaving her the room, and I am turned on by a man handing a girl a pencil, which is embarrassing and I'm not taking it back. Is that the title? Substitution — Vee for Randi, Randi for whoever Daphne was, or just the thing where you swap in what you already know and it goes clear? I don't know. I'm chewing it.

Cassie. "The way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She's wrong and she's the only one in the room telling the truth as she sees it, and Vee hears it as Cassie naming the look that slides off Cassie — which, ouch, and also that's the second time Cassie's said goodbye in a key nobody's listening to. I think the book is going to make me cry about Cassie and I'm not ready.

Where I got twitchy: the long paragraph where Vee itemizes *what he had done* — the margin note not a rewrite, gave back the pencil, didn't perform. I had already seen all of that; I didn't need the inventory. It pulled back from the ledge by cutting itself off mid-sentence, and I'll allow it because it's her brain doing it and not the author's, but I felt the explain-the-subtext hand hover for a second and I don't love that hand.

This is the second clothed chapter in a row, and I clocked it — Chi Latte, now Wilson, two coffee shops. But I'm not skimming. The charge in this one isn't on the page, it's in what I'm carrying into it, and that's a different kind of heat than I usually get and it's working. I do need the next one to put somebody's hands on somebody, though. The book has earned exactly one more chapter of this before I start wondering.

What I actually want right now isn't the phone lighting up. It's the walk back from Wilson. Randi and Pace alone, after. What does her face do when she's done being Vee's friend for the day.
