The glass of water with the paper towel folded under it so it wouldn't mark the wood. That's where he got me, first paragraph, before anything. Not the bread — the bread's a flex, the bread is Pace doing what Pace does — the paper towel. He thought about the ring the glass would leave *while he was putting a drunk girl to bed.* And the forehead kiss after he thought she was gone. I know this man says nothing and I've been angry at him for it for sixty chapters, and the book keeps handing me these little receipts in the currency he actually spends, and I keep cashing them, and I'm so tired of being right that it works on me.

And then I noticed the title. *Hangover.* He had one over Christmas — Sheri across a laminate counter, prison chicken, "then not saying it matters more to you than having her" — and now she has one, and he's the one at the counter, and instead of putting a hard sentence to her he feeds her and then gives her the hardest thing he owns. The book mirrored it and didn't point at the mirror. Good.

"Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else."

I sat up. Because — Randi. Randi, who was riding him two chapters ago saying "she's not the only one in love," whose feet were up on the kitchen chair in the picture he's been building for weeks, who *chose Vee with him* according to the back cover. Not a friend. Not anyone. He just told Vee the truest thing about his life she's ever gotten out of him — the fifteen, the pack he was never in, "weren't safe or weren't welcoming," his father without the word *father* — and he hid the biggest lie in the book inside it like a pit in a peach. And I don't even know if it's a lie in his head. Maybe Randi isn't a *friend* in his taxonomy. Maybe that's exactly what she is to him — not-a-friend — and that's its own confession, if you know what you're listening for. Vee doesn't. Vee hears "women misunderstand the friendship, it gets awkward" and fills in *your* and thinks of Theo, and I'm sitting here thinking: who's the woman who misunderstood, Pace? Which one of you got awkward? That's the 1 a.m. text. *He told her he has one friend. ONE. With Randi's lipstick ring still on him somewhere.*

And it hurt anyway. That's what's annoying. The one-friend thing is devastating even knowing what I know — maybe more, because the thing he's got with Randi is clearly not the thing that fills the gap, or he wouldn't be counting Sheri alone. "The window opens once. Men don't start at twenty-two." "It isn't tragic." Of course he says it isn't tragic; the good order was the tell, and Vee saw that, which is the second time this chapter she read him right, and I'm proud of her and a little scared of how good she's getting at it, because the better she reads him the worse it's going to land when she reads the rest.

Vee herself — the Daphne pass. She finally looked at it. "The ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue" — she won't say it, the book won't say it, and I've been saying it in my head since the bench, and now she's holding both things stacked, "the best two years he had" and "the wrong shape it actually was," and it hurts her to hold them both. That's the interiority I keep begging books for. She didn't resolve it. She let it be two things. And then she did the Theo math — she's been the one holding the line and never stood on his side of it — and that's a girl growing up in real time over cold tea.

The part where my thumb got restless: the long freshman-year riff. Trays, forty to a floor, "she set Pace down at eighteen beside all of it, and he fit nowhere." I got there two paragraphs before she finished and the book kept walking me through the lecture hall. It's her thinking, it's earned, and I still skimmed the back half of it. The page where it explained the subtext to me was the one page I'd have cut.

She came around the counter and held him the way Sheri held him. The book made sure I'd clocked the Sheri hug last chapter — cheek flat to his chest, whole weight committed — and then let Vee copy it without saying she was copying it, and *that* was the thing that worked, far more than the freshman-year page. He wasn't happy and he wasn't sad. Chin on her head, looking at the window, thinking. And I know what he's thinking about, or who, and she doesn't, and the bread's cooling, and I am not okay.
