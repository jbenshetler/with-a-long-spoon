Two pages. I've never had one before and I turned the page looking for the rest of it and there wasn't any, and I sat there with my thumb on nothing, and then I read it again, which I think is the point. I don't know yet if that's a technique or a shortcut. For this one, tonight, it's a technique, because the book had already done the work in 44 — the flannel, "I need you to wear it for me," him pulling it on over the shirt he already had on, standing in his own kitchen in two shirts because she asked. I cried a little at that one and the book knew I did, and it waited four chapters, and then it handed me the same shirt in the dark and said: *it was warm because she was warm.* That's the sentence. He isn't in it. She's been breathing herself back and calling it him.

The order of it is what got me. Shirt over her shirt — for the hall, for Cassie, a thing she wears, it hides what it is. Then hers off, his straight onto her skin, "the way she wore it when no one could see," and for a while it works, it always works, and I was right there in it with her, the sawdust and the salt. And then the thought arrives and she can't un-have it, and she takes it off and lies there naked in the cold and *stays*. Doesn't put it back on. Leaves it in a heap, not folded, not on the pillow — this from the girl who hung the dress with the seams squared. Ten steps naked in the heels and didn't cover an inch, that was for him. This is for nobody. This is her choosing the cold over a lie that feels nice, and I'm so proud of her I want to throw the book across the room.

And the phone. His name near the top, where it stays. Her thumb over the field. *I miss you. Can I come by early?* — she texted first once and it got her a Friday and a shake and a flinch. Now there's nothing she can type that comes back as anything but small and warm and leaves her exactly here. She knows that about him. She knows it the way you know a floor's going to be cold. "I love how good we are together" — I have been angry at that sentence since the porch and I'm angrier now, because she built that cracker-and-meal thing for *weeks*, and he took two breaths, which she'd never heard him do, and then he handed her a compliment about her body. He heard it. I'd bet the drawer on it. He heard it and he chose the smaller sentence, and now she's in a cold bunk at four in the morning counting the things he does — the rooms, the plate, the hunger built first — and none of it says what she is to him. I wrote *he never says* in my own notes like it was a quirk. It's the whole book.

Cassie breathing below, slow and easy, window at its two inches. Still at the door, even asleep. I love that the book won't let her wake up for this. Nobody gets to come.
