Two pages. So that's what one of these is. I turned it over expecting the next scene and there was nothing there, just a girl in the cold and a shirt in a heap, and I sat with my thumb on the edge of the page for a while before I let it go. I've never had a chapter end that fast and I'm not sure yet what to do with my hands.

Here's what it did to me, and it's not small. Four chapters ago she knelt over her bag and handed him that flannel and said *I need you to wear it for me, the whole weekend*, and I had the hot foolish prick of it right alongside her, and he put it on over the shirt he was already wearing and I let that buy him a lot. And now she's lying under a dorm ceiling and the book takes that same shirt and turns it inside out: *it was warm because she was warm.* He wasn't in it. The smell is all him — sawdust and salt, every single thing he does — and the heat is hers, given back. That's the whole arrangement in one piece of cloth. He gives her the rooms and the plate and the hands, all of it real, and she brings the warmth and calls it his. I've been writing *he never says* in my notes for forty chapters like it was a quirk, a thing to watch, and the book just made it the only thing in the room.

And I know why he can't say it. That's the knife. I was on the couch in chapter 43. I heard him tell Randi *then I'll be careful with you* and *you are extraordinary* — he has the sentences, he spends them, he spends them in the other bed when the other woman says *say something nice to me*. Vee built hers for weeks, the crackers and the meal, said it once into a sunrise, and got *I love how good we are together*, which is a man answering a different question on purpose. So she's lying bare in November because of a conversation she'll never hear, and I'm the only one at the table who's heard it, and the book is making me carry that for her. It's working. I'm furious at him in a way I haven't been since the bench, and the bench is still in his bedroom, and I'm still forgetting to be angry about it until I write it down.

What I respect — what kept me from just being sad — is that she takes it off. Not the phone, she gets that part right too: there's no text that gets the shape of what you are to someone, and I've DNF'd a hundred books that made a whole plot out of the one she didn't send. She knows better than those books. But the shirt. She pulls it off and the cold hits her everywhere and *it was so much worse, and she did not put it back on.* That's the long step at the induction. That's *yes, we find out.* She'd rather be naked in the truth than warm in a smell, and she doesn't fold it, doesn't set it back on the pillow, leaves it where a dropped thing lands. Nobody's thumb behind her knee for that either. He'll never know it happened. I think that's what makes it a turn — it's the first thing she's done against him, and she did it where he can't see, which is where she keeps everything that matters now. The pile. The book calls it *the place she puts things*, I've been calling it the shelf for ten chapters, and we're both watching it fill.

Now the honest part. This is the third chapter running on one note — the porch, the bed after Chili's, and now the bunk — *he won't give me the name*, played a little softer each time. The Chili's chapter had his control going and that bought the note another pass. This one has nothing but the image, and the image is good enough to carry two pages, so I'll give it to the book: a night that won't end should be short, and this is exactly as long as not sleeping. But I noticed the paragraph where she *could have gone through it and named every part* — the hands, the rooms, the plate — and for one line it was the back cover reading itself to me, and I felt my eyes slide. It got rescued by *none of it told her what she was to him*. If the next one is two pages of the same ache I'll know it was a shortcut, not a technique.

And where is Randi. Chapter 43 is the last time I had her mouth, and she had it on Pace's couch saying *God, I want her* into the wood. Since then the book has given her one point on a graph and nothing. Vee's lying there with the silence at the center and I know the shape of the thing that's going to fill it, and it's five foot nothing and smells like bitter orange, and it's got Saturdays and Tuesdays and Thursdays now. The book just stripped her bare and put her in the cold. Something's going to offer her a coat. I'm reading on to see whose.
