REACTION

Okay. So this is what a two-page chapter is.

I came in braced. "She'd been not-sleeping a long time" — and I thought, that's three in a row now, Vee on her back in the dark with the word she can't say, porch quilt, then his bed with his spend going cold on her, now the top bunk. I've read this girl lie awake. I know the shape. I was ready to skim to the window going grey.

And then the shirt did the thing.

It's the shirt from the drawer scene — the one she washed and handed him and said *wear it for me*, the one that made me tear up a little in his kitchen — and she's wearing it *over* her own shirt. For the hall. For Cassie. "It hid what it was." That's such a specific, true, slightly sick little detail, a girl dressing her own need in a layer so her roommate won't have to not-say anything. Then she takes her own off and puts his on skin. Then she takes his off and lies there naked in the cold with it in a heap beside her. Three states in a page and a half, and I felt every one of them go through me. Hidden, honest, bare. Nobody touched her. Heat zero. And it's one of the more naked things the book has done.

The line that got me is "the heat in the shirt was her own, given back." Because that's it, that's the whole relationship in one sentence and she found it herself, in the dark, without anybody's hand on her. He's warm because she's warm. He gives her back exactly what she brings — the drawer, the second shirt pulled on over the first without being asked twice, "I'll be here" — and never one degree more that she can prove is his. She's been calling that luck for forty chapters. Tonight she called it a smell she was holding in the dark, and took it off.

And I'll say this for the book: it put the phone in her hand. Dangled the unsent text, the single most tired engine in my entire Kindle, and then had her look at the field and understand there's no sentence. "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them." Thank you. I have read two hundred books where the plot is that she didn't press send. This one knows that pressing send wouldn't help, and *she* knows it, and that's the difference between a girl with an inner life and a girl with a device.

The thing she can't see that I can: she breathes him off her shoulder and names the parts — clean, salt, *sawdust*. The work. She's lying there inhaling the bench and the table three feet from her pillow and filing it under "the things he makes with his hands," like it's the gentlest thing about him. I've been forgetting to be angry about that table for ten chapters and the book just put it in her nose and she thanked it. That's cruel, and I noticed, and I'm not sure she's supposed to for a while yet.

And Cassie breathing below her, two inches of window, asleep. The one person in the book who'd ask the flat question, and Vee lies naked and freezing a foot above her and doesn't make a sound. That's where I'd text a friend: *she'd rather be cold than wake Cassie.*

What I don't love: this is the third chapter running where the whole move is Vee turning the same not-word over in a dark room, and the dramatic irony is getting heavy. I was on that couch in chapter 43. I know why he can't say it — "she consents every time or I'm done," "for the threesome, when she's ready," "God, I want her." I know what the warm, exact, careful not-answer on the porch *was*. So every time Vee grinds on "how was she even to know," I'm sitting there going: I know, and I'm getting tired of knowing by myself. Randi has been off the page five chapters. Five. The book is saving her again and I've said what I think about being saved for. This is a beautiful two pages, and it's a coda to 48, and I'd like the next thing to have a table and the other woman at it and Vee walking in with *this* on her face.

Is the short chapter a technique or a shortcut? Here — technique. One image, in and out, nothing explained to me. If the next one is also two pages of a girl breathing I'll revise that.
