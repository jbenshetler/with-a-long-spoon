REACTION

Okay. Okay okay okay. I sat down with this expecting to give it one chapter and I'm sitting here at the end of chapter one with my jaw a little loose.

First thing: the bench is in the *first sentence*. Not a meet-cute, not a chapter of her walking to class thinking about her thesis — he lifts the top off the table and there's the thing under it, and I'm in. And then it's slow, but slow the way I actually want slow, where every beat is charged. The apple. God, the apple. He sliced it thin because she won't eat thick slices, cut sides up because she eats them faster that way — and then the line about the thing behind her face that polices food, her mother living on egg whites "a thing she had told him with a knife in it." I know that girl. I've been in a room with that girl a hundred times and no book has ever bothered to look at her. Bite, kiss, bite, feeding him a slice, and she's already rocking on him and pointedly not mentioning it. I was gone before they left the kitchen.

And the undressing. Him on one knee folding her cardigan. Her eyes going *past* him to the bench while he's undoing the second button and her hand closing on nothing — that little detail did more to me than most full sex scenes. Then the bench itself and how she reads it, part by part, "her hands closed on nothing" again, "where everything a surface usually covered would hang free" — I actually had to put the phone down for a second. "That's not a table." "Not only a table." "You said it was a table." "No, *you* said it was a table. I didn't correct you." That is a man I would follow into a room.

The spanking-and-edging is explicit and long and it doesn't cut away and it also doesn't feel like the scene I've read four hundred times, because the whole thing is about the *please*. Not the first please, the waiter please — the one that comes "from below." When he says no to three pleases in a row and she goes still and starts worrying the grips, "a woman reaching for a handle that had always been there and not finding it" — I don't think I've ever seen a book name the pouting script that precisely and then take it away from her. I'll admit "good girl" and "you're doing so well" got a half-second eyebrow out of me, because BookTok has worn those to the nub, but it turned — because she wasn't performing anymore, so it wasn't the line I've heard. And her crying, just leaking, not knowing she's doing it. Him leaving the water bottle out in advance. Her pressing the cap edge into her nail bed. That one scared me a little.

And then the name. "There you are, Randi." I actually said "wait" out loud. The jacket had me braced for Vee and a sorority girl named Randi who's the glamorous friend — and this is *her*, the one who's going to be steering. So I already know something Vee never will: I've seen Randi lock a bathroom door and decide, "quiet as a deadbolt going over," that he can have the face and she's never coming out again. And he's sitting on the bed thinking it was a remarkably good evening. That gap is the thing I'm going to be chewing on for the rest of this book. "The face was lying. The face was good at lying." The mirror scene is exactly the interiority I keep begging for and never get — and it's *after* the hot part, sitting on top of it, not instead of it.

Things I'm suspicious of, in a good way: Pace is very close to too perfect — the bench, the apple, the water, the Daphne backstory arriving to explain why he won't knock. I want to see him get something wrong. But the book already showed me him getting something wrong — "You're beautiful," and she flinched. He doesn't know what that cost. And the "still hard, that's not what this night was for" — what *is* it for, then? He built a bench for a girl he thinks he's just found. She's about to go find someone to hand this to instead of feeling it herself. I can see the shape of Vee coming from here and it's making my stomach do something.
