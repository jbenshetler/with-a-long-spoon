**REACTION**

Okay. Okay okay okay. I did not expect to be *that* awake at the end of chapter one.

First, the thing I'd text you at 1 a.m.: it's Randi. The whole chapter I'm reading this girl — blue eyes, sorority bag, the apple thing — and I'm going, this is Vee, right? This is my girl from the jacket? And it's not. It's Randi. The "glamorous new friend." The one who's going to steer Vee toward him *laughing, as if by accident*. And the last thing she decides, standing in his bathroom with her hair fixed, is that the real one is going back behind the face and *not ever* coming out again. So now I'm doing the math the book wants me to do — he found her and she can't survive being found, so she's going to find him somebody who can. She's going to hand him a girl. That is so much colder and so much sadder than "two people planned the best year of her life" sounded on the back cover, and I'm in.

But the sex. Let's talk about the sex, because that's what I'm here for and the book knew it. I've read — God, I don't know, forty? fifty? — spanking scenes, and most of them are counting. One, two, thank-you-sir. This one is *slow* in a way that made me put my phone down. The two minutes on the bench before he touches her where she's waiting to be touched, and the book telling me her shoulders have climbed a quarter-inch. Him walking around to her face. "Hi." "Hi." Taking her heels off in the middle of it. The thread of wet stretching between his fingers and her skin and breaking. The warmth of his fingers pressed against her spine — "her own heat, returned to her in the wrong place" — I actually made a noise. This is explicit, it's not shy, nothing is behind a curtain, and it's also written by someone who's paying attention. The furnace line. The nail polish on her toes being the one place she steps out of line. He notices she eats the apple faster cut-side up. That's the kind of man I want to read, not the kind who growls "mine."

And he doesn't come. He's been hard since the kitchen and he just — sets it down. *That's not what this night was for.* I had to sit with that. Half of me is turned on by it and the other half is like, oh, this is what it looks like when a man is actually *running* something, and I'm not sure yet whether I trust him. Which I think is the point. The jacket said "courtly, exact," and yeah, but courtly is also a kind of control, and the Daphne paragraph — one paragraph, no speech, no explanation, he went in where he wasn't invited and it cost him — tells me he knows that about himself.

The thing that scared me: the bottle cap. She presses the sharp inner edge under her thumbnail, slow, where it'll hurt, and he's *right there* and doesn't see it, or sees it and takes the bottle. One sentence. Nobody explains it. That's when I believed the book. Also the flinch at "you're beautiful." Also her locking the bathroom door, which she's never done, right after we were told she locks his front door first thing, before anything. The book is building a girl out of locks and it never once says so.

What made me twitch: "Good girl." "You're doing so well. You are doing so well." I have those lines tattooed on the inside of my eyelids from a hundred BookTok dukes and hockey captains, and for about a second I thought, oh no, here we go. But it's surrounded by so much that isn't stock — he's crouched at her face, he's checking her hands and shoulders, she's *exasperated* with him — that it read like what a real man might actually say rather than what the algorithm says. I'll allow it. Barely. Watch it.

What I'm suspicious of: the chapter leans close to telling me what the bench is doing to her — the paragraph about the "wanted position," the script, the pout — and then in the bathroom it says it outright: she's been more alive than ever, it came from being seen. It got away with it because it's *her* thinking it, not the narrator leaning over my shoulder, and because what she does with that knowledge is the opposite of what a romance heroine would do. But if it keeps spelling it out, I'll notice.

I'm also aware I bought this book for Vee and Vee isn't here. Normally I'd be annoyed. Instead I'm thinking: the jacket promised to put me ahead of her from the start, and now I am, and I don't like what I know, and I want to see her walk into this room not knowing it. I want to see Randi look at her over lunch. I want to know if Pace knows what Randi decided tonight. I don't think he does. He's sitting there deciding between "stay" and "dinner" and she's building a wall. That gap is the book.

How I feel about them: I like him more than I trust him, I understand her more than I like her, and I think that's exactly where I'm supposed to be.
