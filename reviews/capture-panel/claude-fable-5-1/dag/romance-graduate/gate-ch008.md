REACTION

Okay. Two things happened to me in this chapter at the same time and I want to hold them apart because they're pulling in opposite directions.

The first thing: I was *in* that restaurant. The bread, the steam coming off the torn halves, "may I choose" and the relief going through her — that's the hottest thing in the chapter and nobody took off a sock. I know exactly what the book is doing with the eating, and I don't care that I know, because it's doing it so well. Randi gets apple slices cut thin and laid cut-side-up so she'll eat them faster; Vee gets a table crowded with little dishes and stops watching herself eat without noticing she's stopped. The book didn't have to put those side by side for me. It just let me have the second one and trusted I'd remember the first. That's the kind of thing I keep begging these books to do and they never do. And the drip off his hand, him licking it without a pause mid-sentence, and she *loses the thread* — I felt that one in my stomach. "Always the right hand, never the other" — I don't know if that's a kink flag or a table-manners thing he picked up from Christine's kitchen and the book isn't telling me, and I like not being told. One more closed door with something behind it.

Then the kiss. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he just *goes*. Starts walking her back. No lean, no wait-and-see. And she's the one who stops, with their hands pulling taut. That's the whole book's thesis in one beat and it worked on me: the choosing is the erotic thing. First kiss small, him easing back a breath, her standing in the wanting "longer than she'd have thought she could bear to" and then taking it. I grinned. I'm not proud.

The second thing, and this is the 1 a.m. text: I am so uneasy I could scream. "Somewhere in there she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong — that low background readiness... the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there." Honey. The bill is coming. The bill has a name and a scrunchie and I watched her drink wine off his tongue and ask *did she eat all of it*. I know where this dinner gets reported. I know Randi is going to want to hear about the bread. And the little stab of it — he took Vee somewhere Randi literally cannot go. Christine's. A place where a woman eats with her hands until she's full. He could never bring Randi there. He knows it. That's not nothing and I don't think the book thinks it's nothing either.

Where it dipped for me: twice the book leaned over and told me the thing. "He wasn't going to take it any further than she had. Whatever came next was hers too." I had that. I'd had it for three chapters. And the boots line — "the boys in this building bought new shoes to look like they'd lived, and he just wore the ones that had" — is a little pleased with itself. Small stuff. But this is also the second quiet chapter in a row on Vee's side, after the pool, and I felt my thumb start to check the page count around the tea. The chapter earned its length but it was aware it had a lot of it.

What I want, right now, is Randi. I want the couch. I want to see what the bread does to her face when he tells it, and whether he tells all of it, and I want to see Vee's phone light up with *love you, girl* the morning after and watch her not know what it costs. The book's left me standing in a lobby waiting for the doors to open.
