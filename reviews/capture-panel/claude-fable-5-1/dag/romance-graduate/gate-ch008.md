REACTION

Okay, so no sex, no Pace, no Randi, and I'm not mad. I want to be clear about that up front because I know what I am and I know I'd usually be tapping my foot by now. This is the third short one in a row after those first long ones, and I still don't know what the book is doing with these little chapters — whether it's rationing me or whether it's just how she breathes — but this one earned its two pages, and the froyo one did too, and I'm choosing to trust it for one more.

What got me was the water. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." I read that line twice and put the Kindle down on my chest for a second. Because that's the whole thing with Vee, isn't it — she's got the body every girl at Chi Latte is trying to buy, and it's the thing she carries down the bleachers like a towel she can't hold up against herself. And then she goes under and the room switches off and her hands remember the scull and nobody up there can see her and nobody down there wants anything. And she *grieves*. The book said grieved. It didn't say "a wave of something," it didn't hedge. That's the interiority I keep saying I want — a heroine whose relationship with her own body is a real thing with history, not a tic the hero gets to fix in chapter twelve by telling her she's beautiful.

And of course that's exactly where my stomach went. Because I've already watched Pace say "you're beautiful" to Randi and watched her flinch like he'd hit her. He's going to want the lights up with Vee. He's going to look at her the way the bleachers do but *correctly*, and it's going to work, and I'm going to be turned on by it, and I already know that's the trap the jacket promised me. This chapter is the book quietly putting the shame on the table so that when he turns it into heat I feel every degree of the conversion. I see you, Helen Rivers. I'm going to fall for it anyway.

Cassie, though. Cassie is killing me a little. "They will never know the love of a woman" — said low, just for Vee, when she has the lungs to pitch it to the whole rec center. And that "Goodbye, Vee" from the lecture hall is still sitting in the back of my head like a splinter, the whole word, flat and finished. Either Cassie is in love with her best friend and can see the yellow café and the pretty stranger for exactly what they are, or Cassie is the only person in this book who isn't running a script on Vee, and both of those hurt. The splash fight was the truest tenderness so far and there wasn't a hand on anyone. That's the two-people-talking scene I always say I'd take over a sex scene, except it was two people not talking, which is even better, and I noticed the book knew that.

The one thing I noticed and can't decide about: Vee doesn't think about Pace once. Not in the water, not on the tile, not in the shriek-laugh. Two chapters ago she had her phone facedown on the table letting herself want it to light up, and now she's under the surface and he doesn't exist. I don't know if that's the book telling me the water is the last place that's hers before he gets in, or if it's just a scene that forgot the plot. I'm betting on the first. It would be very like this book.

I'll also say, because it's 1 a.m. and I'm honest at 1 a.m.: I want the date. I want the room where he looks at her. I've had the apple and the bench and the wine and I know what this man does with a woman who hands him the evening, and Vee doesn't hand anything to anyone yet — she's never been asked. Give me the asking.
