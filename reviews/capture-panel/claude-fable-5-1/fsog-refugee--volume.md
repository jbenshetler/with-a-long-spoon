VERDICT
BOOK TWO: yes — and I resent how much. I'd buy it tonight, in the shop, standing up, because I need to be in the room when she finds out, and because I need to know whether the man with the best hands in this genre has the spine to tell her himself before someone else does.
MAILING LIST: yes. Anyone who can write a first-time scene where the shame is the fuel and not the obstacle, and a spanking that's an accident and a question and a *please* — I want to know when she does it again.
TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so. You know the thing we've been looking for since Fifty Shades — the intensity, the man completely focused on one woman, but with a *no* that actually means something? This is the closest anyone's come. He asks. He asks with his eyes, he asks on his knees, he stops the kiss on the doorstep, he stands in the rain so she can change and doesn't move when she calls him back in — the truck scene alone, I'd hand you the book for. And the sex means something, it's funny, it's two particular people trying to save a dress mid-act. Now the bad: the whole thing is a setup. Two people picked her off a quad as a game, and she doesn't know, and the book tells *you* on page one and then makes you watch her hand over everything freely while her secrets get traded in someone else's bed. There's a chapter where he gives up the thing she asked him to pretend she never said, and I nearly threw it. The book knows exactly what it's doing — it's honest about the cost, it lets the plan eat the people who made it — but it ends Volume One with her still in the dark, and I felt a little conned myself. So: the best consent-on-the-page I've read in years, sitting on top of the biggest consent problem I've read in years, and I'm buying Book Two anyway. Make of that what you will. Read it in two nights. Skip nothing, but keep Cassie close; she's the only one in it who's telling the truth.
