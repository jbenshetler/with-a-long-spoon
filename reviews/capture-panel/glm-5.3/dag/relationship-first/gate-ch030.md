REACTION — Okay. I put this one down and just sat there for a minute with the lamp on.

Three times now this book has given me the same scene — Randi arrives at his house, they talk about Vee, the talking becomes the sex — and this is the first time the repetition felt like a spine instead of a habit. Ch. 23 it was a fight. Ch. 26 it was a seduction. And now ch. 30, and I finally see what the shape has been building toward: Randi has run out of ways to want Vee at one remove. She used to get the *report* — "tell me" — and now she's the one out there generating the material. She knelt on her own closet floor and put her hands up a girl's skirt and pressed her thumb into the back of her knee, and then she drove to the man and told it into his ear because telling it was the only door she had. That's not a woman running a plan anymore. That's a woman converting currency. She wants Vee and she can't hold Vee, so she holds Pace and pours Vee through him. "I had her standing in my room this morning. In her bra." The whole thing was her having Vee by proxy, and both of them in the room knew it.

And here's the line that did me in, the one I had to read twice: "I wanted to keep her." Out of Randi, who told the headboard story like an invitation and couldn't stay the night in the house where the plan lives — she said *keep*. My note from ch. 14 was "I want us, torn out of her, said to the wrong person." She's still saying the true things to the wrong person. But the pot isn't just boiling now; it's boiling over onto Vee's side of the stove.

What I'm suspicious of, because I'd be lying if I weren't: "Give her a day." She said it to Vee as advice — *don't go today, go tomorrow, sure not sorry* — and then she says it to Pace like a dispensation, like the day is hers to hand out, and he *set that down beside the rest*, which is the scariest little clause in the chapter, because what's in the rest? And the two of them lying there in the afterglow planning Vee's entrance like stage parents — "She's going to be so beautiful when she walks in. You wait." That's the third game, live, in real time. Vee thinks she's driving across town tomorrow night on her own nerve to make something right, and the truth is two people who love her — and I do think they both love her, that's what makes it unbearable — are sitting in a warm bed *rehearsing her arrival*. She isn't wrong that it's her choice. But the room she's choosing to walk into is dressed.

Also, and I can't decide if it's tenderness or theft: they both *keep pieces of Vee*. He doesn't tell Randi what Vee said at the bar, or how his face felt when it went — "those were his, and that was Vee's." And Randi doesn't tell him everything either; she keeps the sound Vee made and hoped wasn't heard, and she *gives* him the bra and the thigh and the knee. They're rival collectors, filing her. Two games, and the third game is that each of them is quietly building a private Vee the other doesn't get.

The small things: her clothes on the table against the wall, and "it was not the look you give a table" — the table's surfaced, not the payoff I've been waiting for, more like the gun walked past the mantel. The blanket coming up over her *before she had to reach for it* — he's learned her chill the way she's learned his drawers. And him not texting — "It was not a thing he had decided. It was only what he was" — while Randi had already predicted it to Vee down to the inch. She can read him perfectly, which is either the most intimate thing in the book or the most operational, and I genuinely cannot tell, and I don't think the book wants me to.

The heat worked on me, fully — the wine still in her mouth, the blue on her toes being the only color on the bed. But the thing I'll actually be thinking about tonight is a girl crying in a desk chair at nine in the morning, saying all the parts that made her look bad first, while the woman comforting her files it away as fuel. Randi held her *and* spent her. Both true at once. That's the whole book now, isn't it.

If you'd asked me a month ago whether I'd be desperate for a chapter that's mostly waiting for a doorbell, I'd have laughed. I'm desperate for the doorbell. Tomorrow night, the black dress, the borrowed shoes, the scarf she can now tie herself. I know exactly what I'm doing tomorrow.
