REACTION:

Okay. I set this book down and sat there for a minute.

I've been waiting for this chapter since the truck in the rain, and what I expected was the consummation, and what I got instead was the thesis. The shame that has been chasing her the whole book — *floozie*, "crystal strings," her hand moving to cover herself and stopping — doesn't get defeated here. It gets *fused*. "She could not find the seam between the heat and the shame because there was no seam, they ran on the same wire, and she had stopped looking for one." That's the line I'd underline twice. The book isn't curing her of her mother; it's teaching her to run the current through it. And I honestly can't decide if that's the most honest thing a book about desire has said to me in a while or the most seductive lie — and the fact that I can't decide is exactly why I'm still reading. I'm inside the same trap Vee is. The jacket told me I would be.

"Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — I read that three times. It's the *may I choose* question mutated into a new register, and she says no and "made herself say it so it was true," which is the most Vee sentence in the book: saying the yes into existence. And then "he stopped governing it" — the word *governing* doing all that work, because that's the word I've privately been applying to him since chapter five. I felt almost warned by my own notes. This is what it looks like when the governed man lets go, and it's glorious, and it's also, in my ledger, the moment the lure closes.

The small things wrecked me as usual. The frosted plum toenails hidden inside the ballet flats — wanting, and hiding the wanting, and the hiding being its own wanting. The flannel going thin and her having to *chase* the smell, and then the sheets being the source, the actual thing behind the proxy — that's such a precise piece of physical writing about how women actually live inside a new affair, smuggling artifacts. And "Trade." One word. Her handing him the folded panties with the spot "exactly where she'd put it" — she's learned to present her own evidence as a gift. He taught her that on the sun porch, on her knees, smiling up. I felt the click of it in my chest and also a cold finger somewhere down my spine.

Two hesitations, real ones. The chapter is a little in love with its own pattern — the shame-arrives, shame-doesn't-close-her loop runs three or four times and by the last pass I could feel the machinery. And the scar on his thigh, one clause and gone: that's a loaded gun on the mantel and I noticed it being placed there, which is not nothing. But those are small tolls to pay. This was the night the book promised, and it paid.

Cassie wasn't in it. Randi wasn't in it. Which means next chapter one of them finds out, and I want that more than I wanted this.
