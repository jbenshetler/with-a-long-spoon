REACTION — I put the book down after the truck scene and just sat there for a minute.

The flannel. That's the thing. He hands her his only dry shirt and gets out and stands in the rain with his back to her, and she reads it as a courtesy — privacy — and I read it as a man who needed a second where she couldn't see his face. Everything Pace does runs on that double layer and the book never once points at it, which is why I trust it. "He'd been a long way from the question of whether he was cold." That line. He genuinely didn't know he was shivering. She finds out by accident, reaching for his hand to kiss it, and the next thing she was already going for stops dead and becomes *you're freezing* — and I love her for that, I love that her want and her care arrive in the same gesture and neither cancels the other.

And the undressing. That's the best explicit scene so far and he doesn't touch her — she calls him back to the window with a crooked finger, keeps his eyes the whole way down the buttons, and the mother's voice runs underneath as *fuel* instead of brake and the book finally says that out loud: the heat that knew the voice was right and wanted to be the very thing the voice said. That's the whole engine of this book in one sentence. And then the fourth button — she looks down *on purpose*, lets his eyes go with hers, lets him see what he's seeing. She's learning to stage her own wanting. Under somebody's plan or not, that part is hers.

The small stuff is doing so much: the moleskin and "he didn't make her small to give it to her" — the cherry pit on the wax paper, the shame flash and then she just *has it*, the lesson absorbed without being taught. That's the Pace method and also, queasily, the Randi method, and I notice I can't always tell whose curriculum I'm watching. The russet dress — "you put a thing back on the shelf and keep walking" — and him: "That sounds like a beautiful dress." No weight on it. He is going to buy her that dress. I'd bet the book on it. And Brooke filed something just last chapter; the "dear friend" in public.
