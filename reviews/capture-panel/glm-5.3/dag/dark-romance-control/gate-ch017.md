Okay, THIS is the chapter I've been waiting for. Randi working solo is the best thing in this book and I've known it since the bench, and this chapter just proved it in the worst, most delicious way.

The lunch scene is a masterclass in extraction. Watch the mechanics of it: the booth that's nearly an alcove ("you could have said anything in that booth" — the book knows), the tickling story dropped in first like bait, Vee's heat rising at the picture of Randi tied and helpless — the blurb promised me Vee wanting Randi "without quite letting herself know it" and this chapter is the not-quite-knowing turned all the way up. Vee *would have died before naming* that heat and I felt it right alongside her, because honestly? I pictured Randi tied to the headboard too, and the book counted on that.

And then the interrogation. "Which hip?" "The left one." "All the best ones start at the left hip" — I actually laughed, it means NOTHING, it's pure sorority witchcraft, a benediction made of air, and it lands on Vee like scripture. That's Randi's whole method in one line: confidence so complete the content doesn't matter. Every "yeah" Vee gives her is a rung down a ladder Randi is lowering into her. And Vee goes down it *dreamy*. God.

But the moment that made me put the book down: "You don't have to tell me." That's the door closing. Vee's secret — the soaked satin, THE thing, the thing she's told no one, the thing she opened her mouth to finally hand over — and Randi stops her. Gently. Kindly. Like a gift. And I sat there and felt my stomach go wrong, because why would she stop her? Because Randi already knows. Because Randi has KNOWN — she engineered the afternoon, or Pace told her, or both — and she can't let Vee say it out loud because the confession has to come later, on Randi's schedule, in Randi's room, when it can be *used*. That thumb working the base of the water glass, the tightness behind the smile when Vee says "I was fine" — that's not a friend being moved. That's a handler whose subject is running ahead of the program. Or... is it the hum in the ribs? Is she jealous? Is she *hungry*? I genuinely can't tell and that's why she's the best character in the book.

Also: the check. Vee paying for tomato soup while Randi's one glass of wine cost more than the meal, and Randi watching her do it and not reaching for the folder — letting Vee spend money she'll feel on Thursday to keep her seat at the table. That's cruelty so fine-grained I had to reread it.

And the kiss. THE KISS. The hand on the jaw, the second press with something in it the first one hadn't. And Vee explaining it away before her mouth is her own again — "French, it's a French thing" — and then the cold flood of *did anyone see*, counting dark windows. The book just did the thing where Vee's shame reflex, which has only ever pointed at Pace's direction, swivels toward Randi. The want and the fear arriving in the same breath. That's the whole engine of this novel and it's finally, fully running on both cylinders.

What keeps it from a 10: the restaurant itself was a little long getting us to the booth, and Vee's menu arithmetic, while sharp, is a note this book has already played. But the second half of the lunch? The car after? Nothing thin there. This is the chapter I'd text someone about at 1 a.m. in all caps.
