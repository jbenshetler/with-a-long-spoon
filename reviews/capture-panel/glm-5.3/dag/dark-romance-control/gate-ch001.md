REACTION — Okay, hold on. Hold ON. I bought this book for the Vee/Randi/Pace triangle — the blurb promises me a game between two people planning a girl's whole year — and chapter one pulls the rug: the woman on the bench is *Randi*. Randi is not just the glamorous friend. Randi is already Pace's. So the "game between the two people now closest to her" is two people who have *this* between them. I actually went back and reread the blurb mid-chapter like an idiot, and yes — it never says the opening scene is Vee. Rivers you sly thing.

And the scene itself. This is my shelf exactly, but smarter than most of it. The whole first two-thirds is that slow-dom skin-crawl I love — the apple sliced thin because she'll only eat thin slices, the "I sliced this for you" — a man who has been *studying* her, who watches her clock his laminate counters and never say anything. He backs off and lets her choose, and choosing is the kink, and I'm fine, I'm fed. But the bench scene is not what got me. What got me is the *after*. "Good girl," and then she CRIES, silently, doesn't know she's doing it, presses a water bottle cap into her own thumbnail until it hurts — and then goes to the bathroom and *puts the face back on*. "The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." Chills. Actual chills. That's the dark part — not the spanking, the fact that the most intense experience of her life is also the one she's going to armor over, and Pace sits outside the locked door not knocking because he broke Daphne once by knocking.

Which — Daphne! A name dropped once like a knife on the table. There's a body buried in this backstory, I can feel it.

What's turning me on: the edging sequence where he waits for the *real* please, the one said in the water-glass voice versus the one from below. That's the psychological teeth I'm here for. What's scaring me a little, in the good way: Pace's narration is so certain of itself. "All he had done was find it, and answer it." He thinks tonight was a triumph while she's in the bathroom rebuilding a wall. His read is wrong and he doesn't know it yet, and that gap is where this whole series is going to live.

One small caution I'm keeping warm: he's *courteous*. Asks before he takes, safeword protocol immaculate, tender aftercare. If this book stays at this consent-and-cashmere temperature I could get bored — but the blurb promises the trap around Vee, and the last line of jacket copy ("Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.") tells me the darkness is structural, not sexy-prop. I'm in. For now.
