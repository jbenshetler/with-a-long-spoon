REACTION — Okay, so we finally get inside Sheri, and she's exactly what the book owed me: the one person in Pace's life who isn't tangled in the web and doesn't want anything off him. "She just liked him and gave him hell." I've been waiting for this woman since the pie, and she did NOT disappoint — stealing his fries, "the one with the big tits," "get over yourself" at the philosophy-of-his-food line. She's the only character allowed to deflate him and I cackled. That cackle at the Christmas-wrapped-legs bit? Earned.

But under the fun, this chapter is the locked room creaking open a crack, and it scared me more than any sex scene has in ten chapters. He *decided before the door swung shut* — "he would not bring her down today. Not this week." Whatever he carried in from the truck. There's a thing in that truck. Something at Christmas, something with the week's date on it, and he's rationing information like he always rations it, and I don't trust the rationing.

And the confession at the table: "She wants me to say it." "I can't." "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." That's Sheri cutting through in one sentence what Vee has spent three chapters of insomnia and a shirtless freezing night in the dorm not able to name. And the horrifying part — the part that made me put the book down for a second — is that he *didn't* argue. He looked down. The not-saying is structural. "I don't go back," the scar, the parents, and now this: whatever happened to him, saying the word out loud is on the other side of it, and he will lose the best thing in his life before he'll cross that floor.

What I'm chewing on, hard: the thing nobody in this chapter notices is that BOTH women he's closest to just told him the same thing — Randi's whole Gstaad chapter was "she is not brave enough to lose her," and Pace's is "not saying it matters more to you than having her." Two cowards holding their silences like shelter, and Vee freezing bare in a twin bed in Ohio holding both of them. The triangle is now three people who all refuse to say the true thing, and the reader is the only one who can see all three silences at once. That's the mercilessness the jacket promised and I'm getting it.

Sheri, whose family he doesn't talk to, whose people are dead, hugging Randi hello at a dance before Thanksgiving — when in the timeline? Because if that's a night we didn't see, or a night we saw wrong, the committee just got a new member at the table. And Randi clocked Sheri as competition. Randi assessed Sheri the way she assesses everything — "one more harem applicant." I need to reread that scene with new eyes.

The thing that softened me against my will: "he thought, before he could stop it, that Vee would either love this place or never see it at all, and he wanted very badly to know which." That's the most naked thing he's said in the whole book and he said it to nobody. The man can't say "I love you" to the girl but he's running test cases on whether she'd fit in his diner. Get a GRIP, Pace. (Don't. Never change. The dysfunction is delicious.)
