Oh, this chapter is *cruel* in the way I bought this book for. The whole thing runs on dramatic irony so thick I could spread it on toast. Vee sits on Randi's bed sobbing over a blonde Sheri she invented at 3 a.m. — and I've SEEN Sheri. I sat in that diner last chapter. Sheri is five feet of trouble in a chambray shirt, calls Vee "the one with the big tits," steals Pace's fries, and friend-zones him harder than anyone alive has ever been friend-zoned. Vee is agonizing over the least romantic woman in the tri-county area and I got to watch it happen knowing the whole time. That's the "psychologically merciless" the jacket promised.

And Randi. RANDI. This is the woman who engineered the dress, the induction, the walk — and now she's engineering the reconciliation, and it's *for Vee's own good and also entirely self-serving and also the most in-love thing she's ever done*, all three at once. "You just show up already being the answer" — she's writing Vee's surrender for her again, walking her past the slut-shame ledge a word at a time, and every step of the walk is Randi touching the thing she wants and can't name. The detail that undid me: Randi tells the whole Gstaad fairytale in full performance — the children named, the dress, the dancing — and then goes *silent* for the first time in the entire book. "And then it got very late." Randi, who narrated the blond boy's dance with relish, cannot narrate the night she felt nothing. And the nothing is because of the girl sitting on her bed. "Miss your face most though, gorgeous." Chills. She's so cooked.

The hug past the end of a hug. The breath off Randi's neck — bitter orange, nothing sweet — and Vee "didn't want to stop." Vee still can't let herself know, and now she's about to walk across the cold to Pace's door in Randi's heels. Randi's literal shoes on Vee's feet on Pace's doorstep. If this book doesn't know what it's doing with that image I'll eat my Kindle.

Also — the peace plan itself. A bare-under-the-coat doorbell scheme. It's hot, it's exactly the shame-to-heat pipeline this book does better than anything else on my shelf, and it's *wrong in a way I can't look away from*: she's going back to fix a fight about naming things by offering the one thing that doesn't need a name. The word Vee bled for all break, and Randi's advice is "don't use words, words are the whole war." That's not a reconciliation, that's a relapse. I'm feral for it.
