REACTION — Okay. I read this whole chapter with my heart in a strange place, because here's the thing: this is the warmest, most generous chapter the book has given me since the mushroom morning, maybe warmer — and I read every single page of it knowing what Vee doesn't. He gave her a *key*. "I've never given a key to anyone." And I'm sitting there thinking: you sleep with Randi, you liturgize this girl in another woman's bed, and now you cut her a brass key and put it in a box you made with your hands. Both of those men exist in the same body and the book keeps making me hold them both and I honestly don't know anymore whether the tenderness is the con's finest instrument or the truest thing in it. That's the book doing exactly what it does — making the mark's eyes-open love the complication.

The worship scene. Look, on its own terms it's superb — the blindfold, the naming, "the spill is the part I want most," taking the exact things she's hidden her whole life (the heaviness, the wet, the body that "answers") and not just accepting them but *treasuring* them out loud. That's the fantasy done right, from inside her, with her shame given real anatomy. And then "Go ahead" — the moment he makes her do it herself, blind, in the daylight, and she can't spank herself, the little absurd lurch of laughter — that's the book being honest about what a body can and can't give itself. I was fully in it. And then: "What are you thinking about?" and she says "You," and she comes apart on that one syllable, and I felt the floor tilt a little. Because I'm the reader who knows there's a woman in the middle of everything. Her body said *you* and meant him, and the book has spent sixty-nine chapters teaching me that her body keeps a second file it doesn't get to name. The "you" is true. Two things I'm flagging hard, texting-a-friend-at-1am style: **the mirror.** She describes him — brown hair, cleft chin, birthmark by his ear — and "something crossed his face, a shadow, there and gone." WHAT was that. That is not nothing. The book planted that in the middle of the softest scene it's run in chapters, buried it, and moved on. Is he not who she thinks he is? And **the word, again.** "He said house. Some small exact part of her... waited half a beat for the other one, the one with a hearth in it. The other one didn't come." The book keeps doing this — key, house, "I wanted to please you," everything but the sentence, and Vee keeps getting better at "letting it go," which is the saddest skill she's learned. He's rationing the word while spending her name in someone else's bed. I keep waiting for the detonation and the book keeps handing me grace instead, and the grace keeps being real enough that I can't even be properly mad.

The tin roof of it all — the tikka masala in a thermos, the cider planned to pair with it, "You don't have to understand it to eat it" — I loved the porch. I'd sit through that twice. Everything she confesses to him now, I know Randi gets fed. That "yes, certainly," said easy and barely attended, while she's already sinking onto him — that's another contract signed in the afterglow, and she has no idea what she's underwritten.
