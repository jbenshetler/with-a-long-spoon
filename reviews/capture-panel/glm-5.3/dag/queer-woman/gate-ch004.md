Oh, this is the chapter. This is the one I've been waiting for since the jacket — the moment the girl being hunted gets a head, and it turns out the head was already full of exactly the thing they're hunting her for.

The passage in Chi Latte with the two girls in the sports bras — that's it, that's the whole reason I read this shelf. She clocks them, her mother's word arrives (*floozies*, arriving whole and in the mother's voice, which is so exactly how that works), and then underneath the word, "a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for." And then — *she could wear what they were wearing, she had more than they had* — she lets herself have the look by laundering it through competitiveness, the same way Randi laundered her wanting through the threesome-gift two chapters ago. The book keeps doing this, keeps showing me the mechanism women use to carry a want they can't hold in their hands directly, and it does it without smirking. Vee's desire for women is not new. It's not being installed by anybody. It's been living in her under her mother's voice this whole time, and Randi just walked into the room and stood in front of it at three feet. That matters to me more than I can say cleanly — I've quit so many books at exactly this juncture, where the innocent straight girl gets Queered by the schemer. This book keeps insisting the hunger was theirs first, and the scheme is just the door.

And God, the dramatic irony is doing real work now. "A girl walked into her. Or she walked into the girl; it had genuinely not been clear." No, baby. It was very clear. We watched it choreographed — the drift, the shoulder, the redhead's hand flying to her own mouth. Vee carrying that morning around "like an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter life" while I sit there knowing the brighter life has a committee of two — that ache is the whole engine of the book, and I felt it in my teeth. Same with "chosen, plainly, for reasons she didn't need to understand." The reasons are: a man pointed and his girlfriend went feral. And yet the feral part was *real* — we saw the bare *yes* on the quad, we saw Randi fail to hide the grin. So I can't even file this under "girl gets played," because everyone playing is also falling.

The blank transcript thing wrecked me a little. The hour being total and the words being gone, keeping only the *feeling of being a person who got to sit at this table with this girl*. The whole thing is the math of a crush arriving from a position of scarcity, and the book is inside it, not above it smirking.

Latent variables — "the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" — okay, Helen Rivers, I see you. The entire novel is that lecture. Vee's wanting for women is the latent variable; everybody's inferring it from what they can measure. I actually laughed.

And Cassie. "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word, flat and finished, the word for a longer parting. Something in that girl sees the shape of what's happening to her friend, or at least sees that something's happening, and the book let the wrongness sit there without explaining it. Small chill.
