**REACTION**

I did the shirt thing. I said that in my notes and I'll say it here: I have slept in someone's flannel for a reason, and this chapter reached into that memory and put its hand flat on it. The heat in the shirt being *her own heat, given back* — that's the sentence I'll be carrying around for days. Because that's the true thing about comfort objects, isn't it, the thing nobody puts in the poems: you're breathing your own warmth off somebody's collar at 3 a.m. and calling it closeness. The book knew that. It let the ritual work for chapters — the washing, the Sunday return, "so it smells like you again" — and then it took the whole apparatus apart in one page, and it did it *honestly*, without melodrama. The shirt didn't fail because he failed. The shirt failed because it was always her body keeping it warm.

And she doesn't put it back on. That's the part that got me. She takes it off, the cold comes down on her, *it was so much worse, and she did not put it back on.* She stays in the worse thing on purpose. This book keeps doing this to me — it gives her suffering and then it makes the suffering a *choice*, every single time, and I can't tell if that's mercy or if that's the book teaching her that deprivation is a flavor of authorship. She's been taught all fall that choosing is the erotic thing. Now she's choosing the cold. The curriculum is portable and I'm scared of where she'll carry it.

The phone beat, before the shirt — I felt that one in my teeth. "There was no text that made it come clear." She's finally, *finally* rubbing up against the fact that the medium is wrong, that you can't extract the shape of what you are to a person through a little lit field. And she doesn't send anything. Three chapters in a row now she's assembled something huge — the cracker sentence, the "is this what we are now," and now nothing at all — and each time the mechanism of the not-saying gets more visible to her while staying completely in charge. Chapter 41 she told Cassie "both, probably both" about why she can't say it. Chapter 42 he gave her "I love how good we are together," which I read three times in the laundry-room scene trying to decide if he *knew* — and the book wouldn't let me decide, which means it won't let her decide, which I think is the point. Chapter 43 she drowned the word in her own orgasm *twice*. And now the word is so big she can't even hold a surrogate for it against her skin.

Small thing I noticed and loved: she wears his shirt *over her own shirt* for Cassie's benefit. "It hid what it was." My girl is learning to keep things — MIRH, the photo, the kiss-file — and now she's managing her own tenderness for an audience. She's becoming a keeper of ungivinged moments *about herself*. Where's the dread: he's asleep across town "easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." The book keeps showing us his sleep as the verdict on his interior — he goes down easy, always, and she doesn't, and one of these chapters his easy sleep is going to get re-read in the light of the plan and I'm going to have to decide if a man who planned her could still sleep that honestly. That's the bet this whole book is making with me and I'm still in it. But this chapter was the first time the silence at the center had a *temperature*. She's lying bare in a cold room because the warm thing turned out to be her. If you'd told me at chapter 20 that the shirt would become the loneliest object in the book I wouldn't have believed you.
