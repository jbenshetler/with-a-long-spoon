REACTION

Oh, this one got under my skin, and not in the way the last few did. The book just did something to me I'm not sure I forgive it for: it took the exact scene that made me queasy in chapter 52 — that "ugh, gross, wrong" flinch, with the installed grandmother-voice shutting Vee's own tongue down mid-reach — and it made the *telling* of it the event. Not the sex. The telling.

Vee builds the story as a party trick. She's polished it in the shower, she has a closer, she's practically doing standup about her own reflexive shame — and the whole performance exists so she never has to look at what actually happened in that bed: her tongue went for it first, before her mind arrived, and her body had *no complaint*. The book keeps staging this precise thing — the body voting before the committee — and each time I brace for it to be garnish, and each time it turns the knife one more degree instead. Randi wouldn't take the tidy version. "Don't you dare fade to firelight." And then that flat little landmine: "I don't" think it's gross. "It was powerful. It turned me on." Said in the restaurant-recommendation voice. Randi spending almost nothing to say the one thing that unseats Vee's whole closing bit, and then — this is the part that kills me — *no verdict, no lecture*, just "Mm," and bread, and the subject is dead. Randi does to Vee exactly what Pace does: hands her the room and steps back. The parallels are getting louder and louder and Vee is the only person in the book who can't hear them.

And the sidewalk. The goodbye kiss on the neck — Vee's mouth landing "before any of her had convened on the question," which is verbatim the grammar the book used for her tongue going hunting in Pace's mouth. Vee's desire is arriving the same way at Randi's skin as it did at his mouth, and the book is not being subtle about the rhyme, and Randi's whole body did "a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long" — Randi's mask slipping again, the second time now, and this time we got it from Vee's side. Two women, each holding the true thing about the other, and the chapter ends with Vee carrying two sentences home in the wind: *he's enthusiastic* (said out loud, under oath) and *I don't* (Randi's, lodged and running on loop). That's the shape of Vee's next six months, right there, in two lines of reported speech. God.

Also: Marsh. The boxwood lice comb, older than Giza, hand to hand up the rows, never asked back. The cleaner fish. The grooming lecture. The anthropology class is a mirror held up to the entire plot — reciprocity, trust, who grooms whom, the hau of the gift — and I don't even mind that the book is showing its scaffolding because it's *good* scaffolding. "Somebody in this room, by spring break, is going to tell me what you spend it on now." That's a gun on a mantel aimed at Vee's whole economy with these two people.

Small thing I loved: the coat. The dark bloom flashing the loft light for one second — only Vee saw it, the coat "remembered, and shown her, and she'd shut it away against the wall like a drawer closing on a photograph." The book keeps making objects carry the sex she won't confess — the lining, the cashmere, the blanket she laid out flat that morning like a gift with no card. And Randi took one bite of the special and left it. Randi, who eats nothing at these dinners. She comes for something else entirely and it isn't the braise.

I notice what I didn't get: no Pace on the page, and I didn't miss him — which is itself information. This chapter was two women across a white cloth, one of them narrating sex at the other, the other sitting very still with her fingernail on the rim of her glass. I know exactly what that stillness is now. The reveal is loading. And Vee's "you picked well" correction — she *knows* she didn't pick, she just doesn't know how much she knows. The book keeps letting her half-see the plan and file it. The pile grows.
