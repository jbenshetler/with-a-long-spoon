REACTION — God, this book. It gave me a whole Explicit Weekend — the coat, the doorstep, the truck, the roadhouse, "Vee's boyfriend" said six times like a man spending his whole vocabulary in public — and then it pulls the door shut and gives me *this*: one girl alone in a shut room, one hand, and one breath's width she can't cross. And honestly the alone chapter hit me harder than any of the sex. This is the book knowing the difference between what a woman does for an audience and what she does in the dark, and going to the dark one.

The scene is built so precisely around that failure. Everything is ready — the smell that turns out not to be the stink the boys' jokes promised but a hothouse, hers, *unmistakably hers*; the wanting still lit; Pace's untroubled face as the counter-image; Randi's flat little "it was powerful, it turned me on" as the permission slip. And her tongue stops behind her teeth and she holds there until her lungs hurt and *nothing in the room tells her why.* That's the shame circuit exactly — not a decision, an absence of decision, a hand that won't close a distance the mind has already approved. I've lived adjacent to that stall in other keys and I felt it in my own throat.

And of course Randi is the hinge again. "Had Randi's looked like this?" — Vee can't run the experiment without imagining Randi running it first. I keep flagging that as my tripwire, the desire always routed through Randi's coaching, and the book keeps answering me: the pulse answered *before* Randi was in the room. The clench happened at the wet kiss itself, twice, before anyone explained anything. Randi is the vocabulary, not the cause. But I notice the book also notices — *greedy* sitting there like a splinter, the absolution "harder to believe" now that she's alone with it. The coaching that freed her is also the voice she can't get out from under, and she can't tell yet whose verdict the stall belongs to. That's exact.

Small things that got me: the strands between her fingers, "as though the surface of her had disappeared" — that's the plenty of her made visible again, the oldest shame, and the book knows it. The window. She shuts Cassie's window to make the room hers, then has to shove it open at the end, January pouring in "like water through a broken door," because the room held everything and *evidence* is still her word for her own body. And then — the detail that ached — she finds the damp patch on the sheet, cool now, and settles her hip over it. *Her mess. Hers to lie in.* Not cleaned up, not apologized for. That's a half-step of self-acceptance that looks nothing like triumph, and it's the truest thing in the chapter.

The mouth still empty at the end. She'll get there — the book has taught me it never flinches away from where she's headed, it just makes her pay the real price of the room between — and I suspect the arrival is going to land harder for this stalled reach than a dozen doorsteps would have. Also Cassie's lamp burning over nobody, the book tented mid-sentence, the whole room baked and briefly abandoned — the book does loneliness in objects better than most books do it in monologue.

I did miss dialogue this chapter, I'll admit — Randi's voice, Pace's, anybody's. And there's a part of me that wants to ask whether we need every private act also filed as data for the Randi economy. But this is a chapter about what's hers alone, and it ended with her lying in her own mark on her own terms, and I'm not leaving a book that can do this to me.
