REACTION — A breath of a chapter after the two big ones, and I needed it — but the book never actually lets the breath be clean, does it. Vee took her people to the sun porch and spent Christmas money on them and I loved every minute of her being the one who says *get in my car, I know a place* — that's new, that's the house leaking into her, the woman who ordered off the left side of the menu and did no arithmetic. Watching her be the provider for one morning, of all people, did something warm to me.

But god, the lies are stacking up in the most Vee way imaginable — not evil lies, *load-bearing* ones. The sweater story to Cassie (he shrank it, poor man, taking the blame for a garment that never existed because he did a load of laundry she invented on the spot), and now "a big fight" and "a big apology" — she flashed him on his porch and that's the *apology*? "It was a big fight" / "That's a big apology" and Meg, who I want to adopt, clocks the shape without knowing the size: *make it right in their language.* Except Vee's language was her whole body in January cold, and nobody at that table knows that, and Vee sat there in the middle of the light with the true thing folded up small under the true-sounding one. "We're better than we were" — which *sat on the table looking exactly true.* That's the book's whole hand right there: everything she says is true and none of it is the thing. She's learning Randi's grammar. That's what chilled me, quietly, under all the waffles.

And "I flashed him on his front porch" — she can tell Kayla *that*, the scandal, the crime, the joke, but not the drive, not the cold, not the month of the door. The parts that are funny are currency; the parts that are real are state. She's portioning her life out by audience now — Randi gets nearly everything, Kayla gets the comedy, Cassie gets the sweater, Pace gets the body. Nobody gets all of her. That used to be Randi's architecture. Now it's Vee's.

Wednesday. That's the thing humming under the whole chapter. Pace said "I want to meet your people" and then, into the sink, no weight on it: "Bring Cassie, too" — "And Meg" — "Kayla's the one who can talk the paint off a wall?" He has the shelf. All fall he was filing every half sentence she ran on at his back, and now he took the whole shelf down one-handed. "Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off."
