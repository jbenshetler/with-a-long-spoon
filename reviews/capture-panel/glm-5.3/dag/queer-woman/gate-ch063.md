Vee walks in the door and *pushes him onto the bed.* "You wait." She goes down on him first because he always brings her over first and tonight she's getting there before him — that's not a girl being seduced anymore, that's a woman who knows her own power and is spending it glad. And the scar. She put her mouth on the scar he's always moved her hand off of, once, softly, asked it nothing, and went on. I nearly cried, actually — that's the locked room getting a crack without anybody forcing the door.

The painting itself. I've read a hundred food-and-body scenes and they're almost all wallpaper — whipped cream as garnish, the woman as the plate. This was the opposite. He painted *her* as the feast, cantaloupes and grapes and one enormous cherry over her open sex, and the whole argument of the picture is abundance — the body the world called too much made the whole point. And her response: "Take a picture." She left the drip where it was. The pride came "fierce enough to hurt" and the shame never showed up at all. That's the girl who wiped her mess on the sheet as *penalty* two chapters ago, standing fully in the light now. The book is spending its money where it said it would.

And the part I'll be rereading at 1 a.m.: every color brought his mouth and a taste to her lips. Walnut, parsley, grape, melon, cherry — the feeding ritual, each one a little communion. And then the gesso. The brush loaded with her own wetness, painted across her mons — and *nothing came to her lips.* The absence opened "before the thought did." She lay there wondering what it would feel like on her mouth — slippery, salty, sweet, sour, "something with no word she had" — and the question "had nowhere to go. She did not give it words." Do you see what the book is doing. This is the breath's width from chapter 58, transposed. Her mouth is still empty. Her mouth is *hunting*. Whether it's his mouth or — well. There's another mouth in this book she hasn't let herself want by name yet.

The scar, the one friend, the grapevine growing out of her open palm — the gift carrying the giver. Yeah. I'm not going anywhere.
