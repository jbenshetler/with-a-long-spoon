REACTION — God, this book. I put it down and had to just sit there for a minute.

The dress in the window. I knew the fitting was building to this and I still wasn't ready for the light coming through it — "lit from inside itself" — and what undid me wasn't the dress, it was the *labor of hiding the labor*. "He'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That's a whole philosophy of love in one line, and it's also Pace in miniature: the man whose restraint is indistinguishable from devotion, and I've stopped needing to know which it is. And yes — he was up all night finishing it. The hand that shook in the rain, the man who freezes himself spending all his gladness on her. That's twice now the book has let his body pay for what his stillness won't show. I see what it's doing and it keeps working anyway.

But the chapter's real heart is the mirror, and the book knows it. "Floozy." There's the mother's voice, right on schedule, the word arriving automatic and her face going hot — and she *stays*. She makes herself look until the looking turns. And what it turns into isn't his gaze, isn't anyone's — "a lot of woman who knew exactly how much she was." That's hers. Nobody installed that. This is the book paying its bill again: the shame-to-wanting wire running the whole length and her choosing to stand on the current.

And then the underwear. I actually laughed out loud, alone, at the mechanism of it — the slit opening, the pale edge crossing the line, her first thought being *keep your steps small*, and the second thought coming up hot behind it and *not caring what shows*. She strips herself bare so the line runs clean. That's Vee at her most Vee: the shame of it crossing into wanting "almost before it was shame at all," and her striding down the hall with her leg given and taken back with every step *to show him what he'd made*. She's not wearing the dress for him. She's wearing it for the woman in the new mirror, and bringing the evidence to him. I love her so much it's a problem.

The standing sex could have been pure choreography and instead it was the funniest and somehow the most married-feeling scene in the book. "Ow. Don't point down." "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me." Him *twitching* at it, twice, and her delight, and the laugh so hard he has to stop undressing her and wait. This is the thing porn never gets and this book keeps getting right — the logistics ARE the intimacy. Two people laughing with their hands full of each other and neither willing to let go of one single thing they're holding. And the fingers — "closer," him studying them with the care he'd give a hem, then the slow thorough cleaning with his eyes on hers — that went through *my* knees, I'll admit it at 1 a.m. The care of a man who treats her slickness like fabric he's fitting. It's the same attention as the pedestal, and she knows it, and it ruins her in the good way.

Two things I'm carrying with suspicion, because I can't fully surrender:

The mirror with the bright screw heads. Newly hung. He built the pedestal-mirror setup *for her to see herself* — and that's either the most generous thing in the book or the most designed, and the fact that I genuinely can't tell is the book's whole trick. A dress cut to "the actual shape of her, and no one else's" — I felt the chill under the warmth, and I think I was meant to. Curation is happening and this chapter made the collection beautiful on purpose. The dress going dark in its window at the end, "getting away with something" — that's her line, and it's also the book's, and the something she's getting away with not-knowing is still sitting under all this happiness like a bill accruing interest.

And the daydream. Her imagining the induction — walking in late, on purpose, in this, the room turning, "it just felt like what she was for." That's the happiest sentence in the chapter and it scared me a little, because that's Randi's choreography being dreamed by Vee herself now. The plan doesn't need to push anymore; she's authoring the show. Whether that's the book's dark point or its triumph — "nothing was taken that she did not hand over" — I don't know yet, and the not-knowing is the whole reason I'm still here.

No Randi at all this chapter, and I felt the absence like a held breath. Every chapter of pure happiness makes the collision louder. I keep waiting and I'm not tired of waiting, which is the most honest thing I can say about this book.
