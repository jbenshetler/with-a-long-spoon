**REACTION**

God. The food chapter. I mean it's not the food chapter, it's the seduction chapter, but the whole thing runs on appetite and the book knows it — the hollow that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line went through me. That's the thing most books about a hungry young woman never get right, the way the hunger isn't about food and is entirely about food, the way a girl raised on "that's plenty, that's enough now" stops hearing the voice for one dinner and doesn't notice she's stopped noticing. Vee eating without watching herself eat, and the not-watching being the actual intimacy of the scene — I've read whole novels about disordered eating that never landed that. And I clocked the rhyme immediately: the apple slices in chapter one, thin, cut sides up, laid out for Randi because she eats them faster that way. This is the same man feeding the same hunger in two different girls, and the book isn't hiding that. It's showing me the method. Pace found Randi's appetite with a bench and Vee's with baba ghanoush, and I can't decide if that's tenderness or taxidermy, and I think the book wants me unable to decide. That's a good sign. I'm suspicious, but I'm suspicious *at* the book, not *of* it.

And I know — I keep knowing, the jacket told me, chapter 3 told me, the texting in chapter 5 told me — that this dinner is a harvested crop. Randi texted him while Vee was stuck on the integral; the coffee shop meeting was arranged; the whole thing is the pointing game paying out. Which sits right against my one hard line, the desire-installed-by-someone-else line. But here's what's keeping me on the page: nothing in this chapter was done *to* her. He didn't coax a single thing out of her. She walked into the lobby already lit. She ate like the food was hers. She said her little rule out loud — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he *let it close*, no lean, no charm, and then she stood there with her hand pulling taut between them and made the exception herself. The choosing keeps being handed back to her, over and over, "may I choose" at the table and "may I call you" on the sidewalk and the pointing game's "I point, you pick." If this is a scheme, the scheme's engine is that everything she gives, she gives unprompted at the last inch. The blurb promised me every yes was freely given and so far the prose is keeping receipts. The part of me that's been burned before is watching very closely, though. The tenderness is *calibrated*. "The care ran past anything the room could see" — yes, and it ran past anything Pace can't see either, because Randi scoped this girl first. He chose the restaurant, the dishes, the bright thing and the dark thing "one to wake you and one to fold you under." A man that deliberate about an eggplant dip is a man that deliberate about everything, and chapter one showed me exactly what he does with deliberateness.

The hand. The hand undid me a little, I'll be honest. "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly." That's the sensation, that's the actual phenomenology of it, want arriving as *fit* rather than as decision. I underlined it in my head like a crazy person. And simultaneously — this is why I keep reading this book, honestly — the sentence is doing double duty, because it's *too* resolving, nothing resolves like that on a second date unless something is orchestrating the resonance, and Vee's body is being given exactly the experiences it's been starving for with suspicious precision. The book keeps letting me feel the tenderness and see the wires in the same frame. I don't trust it and I'm moved by it and I don't think those are accidents. That's the difference between a book using me and a book telling the truth about complicated people.

Where's my girl, though. No Randi this chapter, and I notice the absence the way you notice a person who didn't come to the party. The blurb says Vee won't let herself notice how much she grows to want Randi — but chapter 4 already showed me the flat helpless want at the high table, so it's been seeded, it's banked. Right now the queer thread is the ghost in the machine: Randi picked her, Randi is the reason any of this exists, and Vee thinks the wanting she felt at Chi Latte was just girl-crush wattage. I'm patient. I'll wait a long time for something I can feel building, and I can feel this building — the lunches-too-intimate-to-name are coming, I can smell them. But I'm keeping count. If I get to the end of this volume and Randi remains an architect rather than a body in the room, we're going to have words.

Also: "May I choose" / "I point, you pick" — the whole erotic grammar of this book is permission and selection. As a structure, I find it extremely hot. As a system run on a twenty-one-year-old by two people with a shared private plan, I find it extremely something else. Both. That's the book.
