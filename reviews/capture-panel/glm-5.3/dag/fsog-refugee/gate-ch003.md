REACTION

Oh. *Oh.* It took me half the chapter — I was enjoying the walk, enjoying the game, enjoying watching him read her — and then the redhead came out of the dining hall and my stomach dropped. "Her name is Vee." Vee. The Vee. I actually put the book down and stared at the wall for a second, and then I went back and reread the jacket, and then — this is the part that got me — I went back and reread chapter one in my head. The bench. The begging. The crying in the bathroom. That was *Randi*. That was this woman, this polished, managing, terrified creature, four weeks into whatever this is with Pace — and I'd read that whole chapter thinking she was the heroine. She's the *scheme*. Or half of it. "We've got a stats class together" — I got actual chills. The book told me on the jacket that the invitation began as a game between two people, and I still wasn't ready for the moment the game touched the girl it was aimed at.

And here's what's unsettling me in the good way, the way that makes me need to keep reading: chapter one was genuinely one of the most consensual, careful, attentive dominance scenes I've ever read in this genre. He checks, he waits, he lets her come to things. And chapter three is *also* that — "I point. You pick." He asks the question in bed, then asks it again after, when she's gathered, when she's free to say no. He hands her the choosing and delights in her having it. That's the thing I've been hunting for for years. And yet the whole apparatus is pointed at a girl walking out of a dining hall who has no idea she's being hunted. The tenderness is real and the hunt is real and they're the same man. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." I get it now. I *hate* it and I get it.

The moment that almost broke me a little: Randi's bare "Yes" — the word coming out alone, nothing around it — and then watching her reach for the costume. That woman cannot let one true thing stand naked in daylight, and he sees it every time and keeps gently peeling. The collision she engineers is so clean it's scary, and the scary part is I was rooting for her. Lit up. Happy footwork she didn't decide to do. She's starving the same way the blurb says Vee is starving, and neither of them knows the other one is the answer yet.

Also, quietly: "Do you actually want to taste another woman?" — the flinch, the deflection into "a threesome, with you," and him narrating exactly what she'd done and why, and letting her have it anyway. He sees through everyone and still makes room. That's the tenderness-dominance thing I read for, done better than almost anything I've found.

I did side-eye the fact that Pace narrates Randi's inner life with total confidence. Nobody's that right about another person. But I'll allow it — the book seems to know he's a man who thinks his reading is love, and I suspect it's going to charge him for that eventually.
