**REACTION**

Okay. I need to talk about this book.

So the first two chapters I was already in deep — I read the bench chapter with my heart in my throat, because that man *asks*. "Tell me your word." "Tell me anytime." He waits for the real please and he knows the difference between a please and a *Please*, and the whole thing is exactly what I've been hunting for since Fifty Shades, the control that makes room for her instead of swallowing her. And then the after — the blanket she brought herself, the water he left in advance, him NOT opening the bathroom door because he learned that lesson with somebody named Daphne and it cost him — that's the tenderness and the dominance in the same man, no contradiction, and I'm gone.

But here's what I'm actually chewing on at 1 a.m.: the book knows something Pace doesn't. Or knows something and is letting me watch him not know it. Because Randi came apart on that bench — really came apart, cried without knowing she was crying — and then stood at that mirror and *decided to go back behind the face forever*. "Not ever." And he's sitting on the bed thinking it was a remarkably good evening while she rebuilds the mask in his bathroom. And the water bottle cap under her thumbnail. He took the bottle away and I don't think he understood what he was taking away. He reads her body like sheet music and he can't read the part of her that just got seen for the first time in her life and panicked. Or — worse, and the book's good enough that I can't tell — he *does* see it, and he's patient, and he thinks patience is the same thing as care. I don't know yet. I'm watching him.

And then THIS chapter. The question in bed. "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and she comes before she can decide what to want, and the book does the thing I love, which is that her body answers first and *the answer is hers*, it's not something done to her. And then — this is the part I'd underline — he asks her AGAIN. Later. When her breath is down and the heat's out of the room, because he wants her "gathered back into herself, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer." That's it. That's the whole thing I read for, in one paragraph. He heard her body say yes and he still made sure her *self* got to say it too.

And her move — "Only in a threesome. With you." God. I felt that in my chest, because he narrates exactly what she's doing: she can't own the wanting, so she hands it to him and becomes its instrument. A girlfriend offering a gift is "merely sexy"; a woman who hungers is "unsurvivable." Twenty-one years old and she's already built the whole architecture of never being the one who wants. And he lets her have the camouflage. He doesn't make her stand naked in it. That's its own kind of tenderness and I'm not sure it's a healthy one and I don't care, it worked on me.

The walk itself — the pointing game — is the most fun this book has had. "I point. You pick." He keeps handing her the choosing and she keeps lighting up brighter each time she notices. And I LOVED watching her taste be wrong — not the mirrors of herself, not the matched pairs, not the girl who runs the room. The "maybe" on the library steps with the beanie. And then Vee. The redhead laughing with her whole body, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." And Randi stops walking and can't look away and says "Yes" with nothing around it — the bare word, before the costume catches up. That's the freest she's been in three chapters, and it's over a stranger. The one told me everything: Randi can't be seen, and she picked the girl who forgot she could be.

And now the part where I put the book down and stared at the ceiling. "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

Because I read the jacket. I know what this is. That line is the plan being born — the game between Pace and Randi, the secret choice made about Vee, the girl who doesn't know there's an invitation. And the manufactured bump-and-flirt on the quad was charming, but it was a *con*. Vee got picked on a walk, rated like a dish, and she has no idea she's been chosen, and the bench is already waiting in a room she's never seen. The book isn't winking at me — it showed me the whole machinery, cold, which is the only reason I'm still here. But I'm on notice now. The jacket says every yes was freely given, says that was the problem, and I'm going to hold it to that. The second Vee's no stops mattering — the second Pace's beautiful asking turns out to be a technique, a hunting tool he aims at women the way he aimed that question at Randi — I'm out. What happened to Randi's yes being hers has to happen to Vee's too. And I'm already worried, because the person who'll be drawing Vee "further open" is the woman who just decided never to come out from behind her own face. Two girls who can't be seen, and the man who sees. This is either going to be devastating in the best way or it's going to break my heart, and honestly I'm not sure those are different things anymore.

Also: three chapters in and the blurb's "heroine" has existed for one paragraph and has no idea she's in a book. I came here for Vee and somehow I'm *protective of Randi* now. Fine. It worked. I didn't even notice the chapter end.
