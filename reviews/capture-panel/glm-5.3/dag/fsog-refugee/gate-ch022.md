REACTION — That chapter worked me over from two directions at once and I felt every minute of it. On the surface it's the warmest thing the book has given me: Vee sitting in that sun room, saying "I wanted it, and I took it. For once," and meaning it, out loud, to another woman. That's the thing I came to this genre to find — her desire as HERS, named by her, in her own voice, and Randi handing it back to her clean: *You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky.* I actually got a little wet-eyed at "let herself be lucky for a second," because I know how hard-won that is for a girl with her mother's voice in her head. The toenail interrogation was delicious — "you paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands" — because it's true, Vee knows it's true, and being *seen* like that is its own kind of heat. And the curb kiss, met this time, no scan of the windows: the want-Randi thread is tightening and I'm not even pretending to be ahead of it anymore.

But underneath, my God, the dramatic irony is almost unbearable now, because I know what this brunch actually is. "Give me something to live on" — Randi says it as a joke and it's the literal truth; she's starving, she left his bed, she's eating twice alone, and this interview is her only meal. And the questions! "Did you do anything to get ready for him?" — that's not a girlfriend's curiosity, that's Pace's register, that's the same "tell me" channel. "You drove over there sure" — she's walking Vee right up to the edge of owning her own desire, which is genuinely good for Vee AND feeds the plan AND gets Randi her fix, all at once, and the book still won't let me split the kindness from the extraction. That's the cruel craft of it: the most consensual, most empowering conversation in the book so far is also the con operating at full capacity. And the fold — Vee folding the middle of the night back into its tissue "before anyone can pick it up" — she kept something! She kept the best of it from Randi, the way Pace kept the truck. She's learning to have insides. That made me proud of her, and then immediately afraid for her, because I don't know what happens when Randi's mask needs more than she's being given.

The fear flash — "how much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to" — showed before she could catch it. The book keeps letting her feel the danger of being gone on someone and then soothing it. At some point that soothing has to fail, and I think it fails when she learns about the plan.

Also, small thing that rang hard: the second mimosa she did not price at all. That's her whole arc in one drink order.
