REACTION

Well. I had to put the book down after the Brooke part, and then pick it right back up.

First half — I was so relieved I could cry. Vee coming back wrecked, and Randi being *this good* to her — "the fear's a liar, and you haven't slept" — walking her off every ledge, the Sheri-built-out-of-spite girl dismantled in one line ("don't hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face"). And it worked on me too, because I'd been carrying that invented blonde since chapter 52 and I knew she was invented and I still half-believed her. Randi saying "that's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — I don't know if it's true, but I *want* it to be true, and so does Vee, and that's exactly the dangerous thing about Randi: she gives you the version of the truth you need. Which is true here AND is what the con does. The book is doing that on purpose, I'm sure of it.

The coat plan. Oh, honey. And here's the thing — the book played it exactly right for me: Randi didn't push her into it. She stopped at "you've got that good long coat" and let Vee finish it, and when the slut-shame voice came up — her *mother's* voice, that whole lineage — Randi talked her down it one rung at a time and Vee chose it on her own nerve. That's the pattern of every threshold in this series, her own hand on the door. So I'm not side-eyeing the manipulation here the way I side-eye the con. Mostly. There's a whisper of "how convenient for the curriculum that her heartbreak funnels into this" and I filed it.

And then the kiss. THE KISS. Under all those composite faces, four months of Randi leaning in and Vee holding sweetly still — and this time Vee went back in, both hands on her face, *took her time*, until the thing in her chest had somewhere to go. I actually gasped. That's her nerve again, the daring that's become the thing she craves. And the second POV shift — Randi with the back of her hand to her mouth, the matcha she never made, "a sentence with *means* in it, and she left it where it was" — that's the loneliest woman in this book getting the one thing she couldn't run a scenario on. Her heart going like she'd come up three flights. I'm a little in love with how much this hurts her.

And then Brooke. Christ. The warmest knife in the whole book. Not a threat, not a rule — "play all you want, privately, or for the boys" — she put the whole hypocrisy on the table without ever raising her voice, and then: *whether you want to keep your position.* And Randi, hands not hers, mug cold, said she did. So now Vee's new-opened door runs straight into a locked house policy, and Randi chose the position. Right after the kiss. That's the door raised and not yet opened, and I'm going to be sick about it.

Also: Randi telling Vee about John and stopping at "we went up" — Vee read it as discretion, and I read it as the one room Randi couldn't narrate because it was *nothing*. And "everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — she said the whole chapter 51 cold-truth in one joke and Vee laughed and didn't hear it.

Tonight, though. Tonight is Pace, and the coat, and the heels Randi pressed into her hands. If he opens that door and still won't say it — if the book makes the naked coat replace the word instead of forcing the word — I'm going to be furious. That's my line and the book knows it's my line.
