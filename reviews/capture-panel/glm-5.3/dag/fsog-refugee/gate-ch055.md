Okay. I put the book down and just sat there for a minute, and then I picked it back up and read the doorstep scene again, because *she* did that. That's the thing I keep turning over — after fifty-four chapters of him building everything, the meal, the heat, the hours, the whole architecture of care, it was her who drove out through the dark and knocked and opened the coat. She decided. She wound herself up at the mirror and denied herself twice — the discipline of that, walking away from her own orgasm because it wasn't hers to finish, because she wanted it to be *his* — and the cop lights almost killed me, I actually gasped, bare under the coat with her hand between her legs and a siren coming up behind her. And then the cruiser just... going past. I'm not over that. The book scared me exactly as hard as it scared her and then let her keep going.

And him. The formal voice at the door — "Is everything alright?" — my heart dropped, because that was December's voice, the wall voice, and I thought for one horrible second she'd staked everything on a man who'd shut the door in her face. And then "Vee, I—" and the hand coming off the frame, and the look when she opened the coat. "Like a starving man, and like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look." I underlined that with my finger, actually pressed it into the page. That's everything I've been waiting for since he sat in that diner and couldn't say it. He still didn't say *it* — I noticed, I'm not letting him off — but "I missed you so much" with his voice cracking on it, on one knee at her chair with pancakes in front of her, is more than "most important person in my life" ever was. Chapter 53 showed me why he can't (Daphne, the words being the difference, "he had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him" — I had to stop reading there), so I know what it costs him, and the doorway was the word said in everything but letters.

The heat itself — door open, wind pouring in, him on his knees on freezing boards giving her his mouth before anything, the "Those stay on," her coming apart and him losing his patience for once and barely making it three steps before the couch. It's hot, genuinely one of the hottest chapters this series has done, and it's hot *because* it was her offering and his answering. Nobody took anything. She brought it, he received it, and the sobbing into her own hand on his head at the end is the tenderness I read these books for and almost never get.

But I have to be honest with myself about one thing, because I'm the reader who left this genre over exactly this: the plan wasn't hers alone. Randi built it — "you just show up already being the answer," the coat, the shoes, walked her right up to the edge and past the word *slut*. That's not a complaint — that's the book doing its job. Vee's desire is rendered so much from inside (the bra drawer, the shoes first instead of last, the giggle at her own toes) that I believe it's hers. But I'm watching Randi now. "God, I want her" and the hand to her own mouth in the foyer — that woman is not running a con anymore, she's drowning, and Vee kissed her first this time. First time in four months. The book knows what it's doing with that, and I'm going to keep holding it to the standard it set in chapter one: no door Vee doesn't walk through herself. She walked through this one.
