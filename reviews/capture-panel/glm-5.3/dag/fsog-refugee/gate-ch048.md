REACTION — Okay, that one hurt. Two pages and it hit harder than the whole Chili's chapter did, because there's nowhere to hide in it — it's just her, in the dark, with the shirt, and the shirt turning on her. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back.* God. That's the whole relationship problem in one line, and she found it herself, alone, at 2 a.m., smelling a man who is asleep across town "easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." The contrast between her not-sleeping and his easy sleep — the book keeps doing that to me and it keeps working.

That's her refusing the substitute, the boyfriend-shaped smell of him, the thing that almost-works. She left the shirt in a heap, not folded, not on the pillow — the not-folding is a scream. I noticed my own superstition: don't let the book punish her for this honesty.
