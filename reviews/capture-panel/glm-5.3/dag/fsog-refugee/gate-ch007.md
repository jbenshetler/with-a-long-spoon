REACTION — Okay, so nobody gets touched in this chapter and I still liked it more than I expected to, mostly because of Cassie. Cassie is quietly becoming my favorite person in this book. "They will never know the love of a woman," saved just for Vee, pitched low so it's only theirs — that's the tenderness I read for, even when it's not the romantic kind. And the splash, from ten feet off, not moving a step closer, because Cassie knows asking would make the sad thing bigger — God. That's a friend written by someone who has actually had one.

The underwater part got me more than I was ready for. Vee grieving, underwater, for a body that "wasn't a problem" yet — for synchronized swimming at sixteen, before the cardigans and the mother's voice and the looking. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." I had to put the book down for a second at that line. I've read a hundred heroines with body shame and it's always rendered as tragic backstory in a paragraph; this is the first one that made me feel the *before* of it, the place where she was just a body doing a thing it was good at.

What I'm noticing underneath, and it's making me itch a little: the blurb told me Randi and Pace planned this. So when Cassie says "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word — two chapters ago, and here keeps her dry, deadpan watch on everything... is Cassie the one person who sees it coming? The way she watched Randi text and gave her the flat look — "she's not serious." I don't think Cassie knows about the game specifically, but I think Cassie is the only character watching Randi with clear eyes, and that's going to matter. The book put her here for a reason and I don't trust that reason is kind.

Also, the catcall scene: I love that Vee has armor for it — the blink, the eyeroll, the girl who has *meant* to arrive — and that the book knows the armor costs her fifty yards of held breath. The shriek-laugh breaking out of her was the whole chapter's payoff for me.

It's a breather chapter and I know it. No Pace since the froyo walk, no Randi since she pushed Vee at him, and the blurb's promise — the heat aimed at *Vee*, the "may I" man — is what I'm here for. This was lovely but it was the vegetables. I'm ready for him to call her again, or for Randi to start drawing her further in. The one thing I'll say for the pacing: my patience holds when the care is real, and the care in this chapter is real. Just — next one, please, let something happen between people who want each other.
