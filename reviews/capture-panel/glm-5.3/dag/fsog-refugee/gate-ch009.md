REACTION — Okay, this chapter did something to me that I wasn't fully braced for, because the jacket told me from page one that there's a plan, that Pace and Randi chose Vee before she knew — and knowing that, I read this whole dinner with two sets of eyes. And the maddening thing is: it *still worked on me.* That's the trap the blurb promised — "lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does" — and I fell for it exactly as she did, and I'm a little mad about it and a lot hooked.

The dinner itself was almost embarrassingly effective on me. "May I choose?" — three words, and it's the same move as the bench chapter, the same move as "may I call you," the asking that gives her the room to say yes. He doesn't take the menu from her; she hands it over and he *receives* it like it weighs something. And then the one question — "is there anything you wouldn't eat" — checking the edges before he takes the whole territory. That's my whole checklist in one dinner. A man who chooses for her without erasing her. I could live in that.

But the part that actually got me was the eating. Vee, who's been rationed her whole life — apples on the safe side of the line, the sorority built to one scale — sitting in that warm room eating without watching herself eat, and *not noticing she'd stopped watching.* "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." I had to put the book down for a second at that line. And him not seeming to notice, or not thinking there was anything to notice — that's the tenderness. He's feeding her the way he sliced the apple thin, and on some level I know he's doing it *on purpose*, he's a man who thinks about most things, and that's exactly the double edge this book is built on. Care that is real AND calculated. I don't know yet how I feel about holding both of those at once. I know I can't stop reading it.

The kiss at the end — I actually gasped a little, which at my age and my reading history is embarrassing. Her rule coming out of her mouth, him not arguing, not leaning in, not doing the patient-wait thing that aggressive men do when they hear a no-shaped thing, and then *her feet staying where they were.* "I'm making an exception" — the choice swinging all the way back around to her, twice, because he left the last inch hers and she took it. The grin coming up in her mid-kiss and going back for more with it still on her mouth. That's the thing I left the whole genre to find. Heat and consent in the same beat, no contradiction, no wink.

And the hand. "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving." Yes. That's the line of the chapter. That's what I read for.

Underneath all the warmth, though, I'm tracking the other thing, and I can feel the book winding it tighter: Randi across that coffee table with the wine, "I already do." Randi holding a want too big for her face. Every scene of Vee falling for Pace is also a scene of Randi hearing about it and wanting more. And now the two threads are going to have to touch. The jacket says the game between Pace and Randi gets real — truer than they planned. What I'm watching for, chapter by chapter, is whether the book keeps Vee's choices actually hers once she's inside the thing she didn't choose. Tonight it did. The kiss was hers. The exception was hers. Keep that and I'm yours, Helen Rivers.

Also: Cassie. "That's not a boy. That's a man." Cassie is doing the Greek chorus thing, the one clear-eyed person in Vee's life, and I'm clinging to her a little. Somebody has to see Vee clearly who isn't part of the plan.
