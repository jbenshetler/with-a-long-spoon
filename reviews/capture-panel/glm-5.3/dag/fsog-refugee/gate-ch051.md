REACTION — Oh, this one got me. I've been so deep inside Vee's head for fifty chapters that stepping inside Randi's felt like being handed the key to a room I'd been pressing my ear against — and the room is *cold*. That's the whole chapter, isn't it: warm where she touches him, cold everywhere else, and the cold is hers, carried in and out. "She had put nothing on the table and had won exactly that." I had to put the book down for a minute after that line.

And the timing is almost cruel, and I mean that as a compliment. Vee just walked out into the cold refusing hands in place of a word — and here's the woman across the table making the exact same bargain in the exact same direction. Vee won't settle for what's true-but-not-it. Randi settles for it *on purpose*. "Let the thing keep the shape it was already taking. Let Vee arrive at it on her own." She's not running a con here — she's doing what she's always done, arranging the world so she never has to be brave, and she *names* it, and naming it doesn't free her from it. That's the difference between her and Vee, and the book knows it, and I felt it in my chest.

The John sequence was beautifully done even as it chilled me. He's not a villain — that's the trick of him. He's faultless, cotillion-good, "nothing happened to her eyes." And the moment at the door — he never asked where the night was going, he assumed it, and she *liked not being asked* — the book let her like it, honestly, and then showed her the bill. That's the thing I left this genre over, the disregard played as romance, and this book did NOT play it that way. It let her choose it and then made her pay for it alone in the mirror, putting the face back on. "The smile still worked, she checked, and meant nothing." God.

And Pace — "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best. She wished it wasn't true." I'm sitting here mid-fight with him, furious that he won't spend the word, and Randi's whole chapter is the counterweight: he goes straight to the person she keeps hidden and doesn't look away, he makes her say it out loud every time. The man who won't say the word is the same man who won't let anyone else get away with assuming. I'm still angry at him. But I understand the machinery of him a degree better now, and I hate that I do.

The text from Vee — "i'm here if you want to talk. or not" — and Randi giving her the fairytale, every word true, none of it the thing, except the one line she meant. That's the most honest dishonesty I've read in ages, and it hurt, because *Vee did the same thing to her parents*, didn't she? Gave them the true surface. These two women are both fluent in the language of the acceptable world and they use it on each other's behalf, never at each other — except that one unrunnable scenario, Vee's face while she says it.

No Vee-and-Pace alone in a room, obviously — they're mid-fight across a continent — but I didn't feel the gap, because this chapter *was* about them. Both of them, from the outside, through the one person orbiting them both. Quiet chapter, and it cost me nothing; the care on the page was everywhere, just all of it sad.
