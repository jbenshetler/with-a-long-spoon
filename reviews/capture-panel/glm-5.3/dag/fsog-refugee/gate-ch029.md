REACTION — Oh, this chapter. I finished it and just sat there for a minute, because the book did the thing I've been begging this genre to do for years, and it did it in a kitchen, over a sink full of dishes.

First, the apology. The "humble pie" label made at three in the morning — I loved her, I loved the print-shop absurdity of it, and I loved that the book agreed with her that a joke collapses when you're standing in the driveway actually holding your contrition. And then he wouldn't let the clean machine run. "But what happened?" That's him all over — he can't take a closed door any more than he can make one. And her falling apart into the *true* confession — "she handed me a lie and I picked it up because some part of me was just standing there waiting for one" — that's her whole childhood in one sentence, the shame that's always crouched waiting for an excuse to be true. And his answer: "Thank you for telling me what she said." Not thank you for the apology. Thank you for the *truth*. I teared up, actually. And then him lifting the weight out of her hands "like the pie" — the callback, the whole book in one image.

Also: she's taller than him in the heels. That tiny missed-stair wrongness, her whole body's map of him suddenly wrong at the door — and instead of the genre's usual move (make it a humiliation, make him bristle, make her shrink), he says *leave them on* and gives her that gorgeous extravagant line about beauty loaned out by the gods. A man who isn't threatened by an inch. I could weep.

And then the tickle fight. I need to be careful here, because a spank mid-tickle-fight in the wrong book is exactly the spot where I leave — she swats him, he swats back, and the scene turns without anyone agreeing to it. I was watching the door very hard in that moment. But look at what he does with what he sees: she has this huge involuntary reaction, body saying things she didn't sign off on saying, and he goes still. He watches her face. And then — and this is the load-bearing part — when his hand comes to the place, it *settles*. Flat, covering, bearing down. No strike. "This?" — a question. Asked into her hair. And she says "please" from the bottom of her voice, twice, and he doesn't escalate, doesn't perform, doesn't take a single step she didn't take first. Her body discovered something and he held the door open for it without walking through ahead of her. That is the thing. That is the entire thing I read these books hoping to find. Her shame-response lighting up under his hand and the book making it hers — "she left her face where it was and let it sit there, unnamed, hers" — I actually put the book down and texted a friend at 1 a.m.: "the spank was consensual by NOT being repeated, does that make sense, the restraint was the sex."

The Sunday-Monday tail got me too — the folded undershirt placed for her to find, the smell of him head to foot and her going still over it (that stillness is doing double duty, book, I see you), and "You're always beautiful." *Always.* Not you're-beautiful, which she'd have argued. He knows her down to the argument she'd have. The pan soaking down the hall the whole time — I'm not built to resist a chapter that ends on a pan.

If I have a ledger note: the thing he saw is now in the room between them, unspoken, and this book is written by someone who knows that "unspoken" is a debt that accrues. The bench stories. The "all us girls love him" blonde who was telling a kind of truth about a world Vee hasn't been shown. I don't think we're headed anywhere clean. But I trust the hands on the wheel.
