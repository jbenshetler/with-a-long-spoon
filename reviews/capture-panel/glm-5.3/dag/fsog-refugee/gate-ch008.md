The hand-holding broke me a little. "Less like something beginning than something resolving" — that's exactly the feeling I read this whole genre chasing and almost never get handed, and here it is over a *hand*, over nothing, and it lands because everything before it earned it. The whole dinner is the slowest seduction and nobody touches anyone. He feeds her — no, he doesn't even feed her, he *chooses for her when she asks him to*, and the asking is the point. "May I choose" — the same man who says "may I call you," "may I choose," who asked for the pencil with his eyes. The manners aren't a costume, they're the whole architecture of him, and Vee feels it in her body before she can name it, which is precisely where I like feeling it.

And I love that she almost ruins it. The rule coming back at the worst moment — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — I know that reflex, I've watched a hundred heroines get steamrolled right at that beat by a hero who smiles and leans in anyway. He doesn't. He lets it close. And *then she gets to be the one who closes the distance anyway.* Her no mattered and then her yes mattered and the book gave her both, in that order, and the yes is hers. That's the whole thing. That's the thing I've been looking for.

The hunger writing is doing double duty and it's a little merciless — she hasn't eaten since breakfast because she was dressing for him, and the "hollow that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" is the kind of line that makes me trust the author knows exactly what she's doing with the apple slices in chapter one and the water and everything.
