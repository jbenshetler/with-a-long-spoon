**REACTION**

I had this whole thing built in my head from my notes — "walking in late on purpose" — and the book took it away from her and from me, and I love it more for that. She'd pictured the dramatic entrance and the actual room was warm and dull and kind and nobody turns on their own, and you can feel her sitting there going *oh. It's just a room.* And then her name is read and the moment isn't the fantasy, it's a real one happening in real time: the first careful step, the breath, the second step *long*. "She gave the room her leg on purpose." I read that line twice. That's the whole book in one stride — not what's done to her, what she decides, half a second before her head signs off on it. Her body agreeing to a thing she chose. After all the dark romance I slogged through where the heroine's "yes" is a technicality, this walk across a stage — silk open to the hip, nothing under it, face giving away *nothing* — is the most genuinely wanton thing I've read in years, and it's entirely hers. The murmur going through the room. "And the room knew, and not a soul in it could have said so out loud." I put the book down for a second just to sit in that.

And the ending of the walk — "whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing." That's it. That's the line I'd text someone at 1 a.m. She's not performing for anyone. Pace isn't even in the state. Randi isn't in the room. It's just her and the dress doing the thing it was built to do and her letting it, and the fact that the reader knows the dress was built by a man who was *chosen into this* with Randi before Vee existed — that irony sat under the whole chapter for me like the sour note it's supposed to be, and it still didn't sour her walk. Because the walk wasn't the plan. The plan, whatever Randi and Pace arranged, can't have included *that* stride. The con gave her the dress; the leg was all her own.

The photo nearly did me in. "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." She sends him the whole picture, uncropped — the dress doing the thing he built it to do and didn't get to watch — and keeps the heat in it for herself, and I actually had to press my lips together. That's tenderness across three time zones. That's what I'm here for: the intimacy that doesn't need the room. Him being sorry in his quiet no-performance way about missing it, and her not telling him it was only a dress because it wasn't, and both of them knowing it.

Cassie. "You look—" and not finishing it, "which from Cassie was a standing ovation." And the bright eyes she'd deny to the grave. My clean girl, holding the phone, taking the photo that documents her friend's happiness, still not knowing the floor under it. The longer the book holds her clean the worse the fall is going to be, and I keep bracing for it and keep being glad she's still on the page being the only person who never wanted anything from Vee but Vee.

Small ache I'll own: Randi was there in the dressing — knelt to do the ankle strap, sat back to *look* — and then absent from the room. She dressed the girl and missed the walk. Or did she? She'll get the story across a lunch table, drawn out of Vee piece by piece, and I already know how still her face is going to go. The plum nails woke and threw the light when they moved — Randi's color from the boutique, carried all the way onto the stage. Randi is literally on Vee's hands. Nobody at that ceremony knew whose fingerprints were on the honoree.

One flutter of unease, small, honest: "the dress as it was built to be worn was to wear nothing under it, and she had wanted to wear the dress." The book is very careful to make the bareness her want and not the dress's command, and this time I bought it. But I'm watching that line. The moment the made-to-measure starts deciding for her instead of her deciding in it, I'm out. This chapter stayed on the right side by an inch and made the inch feel like a mile of her own choosing.
