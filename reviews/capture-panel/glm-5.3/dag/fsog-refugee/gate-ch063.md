REACTION — This one got under my skin more than the truck chapter, honestly. That's strange to say about a chapter where she's alone in a flannel shirt and nobody touches her, but this is the thing I'm always begging these books for — her wanting rendered from the *inside*, not performed for anyone. The book put me in the room with her and made me hold my breath at the exact moment she held hers.

The whole sequence of that reach — fingers up, breath on them, tongue coming forward — and then *stopping*, one breath's width, lungs hurting, and neither she nor I could name why. That's the truest thing this book has done with the "gross" thread. Because it's not disgust anymore; the flinch happened and she went past it. The smell was fine, better than fine — "warm, damp, alive, a hothouse" — his face came back glad, her tongue went out on its own like it does with the salt. Her body wants it and has wanted it, twice, hard. Something *else* won't close the distance. And the book just leaves it open, no verdict, no Randi there to press her one quarter-inch further. I noticed that, that Randi wasn't in the room this time. If she had been — even secondhand, even as "It was powerful" replaying — I'd have had my guard up about which direction the pressure was flowing. Instead it's just Vee and the wanting and the wall, and the wall is hers, and that's the only way I want her crossing it.

And the "greedy" running underneath. *Not greedy, hungry out loud* — the absolution Randi handed her, and it stops working the second she's alone with how much of her there is, the shine to the knuckles, the strands between her fingers. The old verdict on the *amount* — "the shameful plenty of her made visible" — that hit me somewhere personal, I don't mind saying. That's a girlhood sentence, that one, and the book knows it. And then *hers to lie in* — settling her hip over the damp patch like a claiming instead of a shame. My god. That little shift from mess to territory. That's the whole arc of Vee in four words.

Small things I'm hoarding: Pace's flannel with the smell gone thin by Thursday — she's rationing him, she doesn't say so. The window open and shut, the heat as a sealed room — everything the room holds, nothing goes out, which is also this whole plot right now. Her mouth still empty, last line, and I *felt* it. And the faint ghost-thread of Randi's fingers in the wondering — "a mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers" — the book keeps doing that, keeps sliding her in sideways, and keeps never winking. She's drifting toward it asleep. The dinner Thursday is going to be a live wire.

The heat score is tricky — it's hot, but it's hot in the quiet held-breath way, not the cab way. What raises it is that it's *hers*, solo, nobody managing her, no audience. A heroine's desire that's actually hers, in her own hand, with her own stop on it. This is the chapter I'd point to if anyone asked me why I'm still reading this genre at my age.
