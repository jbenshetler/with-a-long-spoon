**HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM**

Pace — fully, hopelessly attached, ledger still running. The spank scene was the test I've been waiting this whole genre to pass: his hand settled instead of struck, he asked, he waited, her discovery stayed hers. That plus "thank you for telling me what she said" plus "leave them on" — a man who isn't threatened by an inch or thrown by a flinch. And the wound is real now: Peter, the hallway, Daphne named as luck, the scar still sealed behind "not tonight" — which the book honored as *his* no, and that counts double. But the ledger: the mirror was newly hung for her, the dress finished at 3 a.m., the riser built before her yes — all of it inside a plan he's still running with Randi, whose file he keeps as "not finished." The man is the man. The debt is the debt.

Randi — the ache is now unbearable and I've stopped pretending I can split her. "That was kind of hot" — spending Vee's shame as foreplay with her lover — is the ugliest thing she's done, and "I keep waiting to get bored of her and it doesn't come" is the truest, and both came out of the same mouth in the same scene. She coached Vee's apology with the manual to her own partner. She harvested "did you deserve it?" and watched Vee's face take each piece of the bench story. And she also — held her hand, gave her the real laugh, knelt under the skirt, said "dinner in Paris" about her. She's drowning in her own machine and I can't rescue her and I can't hate her. When she finally gets caught in her own net, I think it will hurt more than Vee's fall does.

Vee — attached, proud of her, scared for her. The hand pinned to her own knee is my favorite thing she's done: nobody made her, she governs her own wanting, she doesn't hand the yes over yet. But "yet" is the book's word and I know it. The routing worries me — twice now her repair path runs through Randi, not Cassie, and Cassie is being edited out in real time. The credit card is the first real cost paid in money, and there will be others. She picked up the blonde's lie because she was standing there waiting for one — the shame engine is the con's best accomplice and nobody had to build it. Her daydream of the induction entrance — "it just felt like what she was for" — is the harvest rehearsing itself as her own idea.

Cassie — fierce irrational love, and it's actively being wounded now. "See you in the room," said to the side of her head. She'll come at 3 a.m. in whatever she slept in and Vee won't call. She's the only person who wants nothing from Vee, and the book is slowly moving Vee out of her reach. Still my price for leaving.
