**WHO'S WHO**

- **Pace / Peter** — the word-arc finally has its answer, and it's worse and better than I guessed: he CAN, and he chose not to. Chapter 53 opened his side of the porch: he heard the cracker-and-meal sentence, the true answer rose in him "whole and ready," and he picked the smaller truth on purpose — safe, afraid, "at the moment she asked him to be brave he had been weak." The machinery underneath: Daphne, finally whole. Tutor, first everything, the only woman he ever said it to; she left, and "there had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told." He taught himself the words were the difference between survivable loss and the other kind. Then Vee came and broke the math. Sheri said it to his face at the diner: "not saying it matters more to you than having her... from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." He didn't argue. And then he drank alone for the first time — hunted the occasion — and sobbed bent over cold chicken because an engine on the highway wasn't her car. The man who kept the freezer in columns. That's him at the bottom. And THEN chapter 55: she opened the coat and the wall "didn't come down, it crumbled" — starving man and forgiven man in one look — and at midnight, on one knee at her chair, "I'm sorry. I never meant to hurt you. I missed you so much," voice cracking. He still hasn't spent the word. But I've now been inside the room where he keeps it, and I know it's full of a dead girl and his own terror, and I can't be simply angry at him anymore. Also: he keeps EVERYTHING. Meg's boots from one half-sentence on a trail, the heel slipping out of the right one. Kayla's paint-off-walls line in her own voice. "I want to meet your people" — he volunteered for a party. "Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one."
- **Vee** — my girl came and got her man. The coat was her nerve, her plan (mostly), her body: wound herself at the mirror, denied herself twice on purpose — "it was not hers to finish" — drove the county road bare under wool, the cop lights nearly stopped her heart, and she kept going. Opened the coat in the January dark without one word, because "there was no version of it in which she stood on his step and talked." That's the arc of this whole book in one image — the girl who wouldn't take the second step made long now handing her whole self over on a doorstep. And she's grown in the daylight too: spent her Christmas money on her friends without doing the arithmetic, told Kayla "I flashed him on his front porch" and kept the drive and the lining to herself — private that's HERS now, not shame. The pleasure princess reversal (she counted it off on her fingers — he brings her off first, always, rises in the dark for bread, "some slave") is the thesis of why I'm still in this genre. But the pile grew: she thinks the cream cashmere "appeared at the foot of the bed." She spread it on purpose, soaked it, slept on it. It's Randi's. And the kiss with her own taste on his mouth — she sputtered "gross," he went still and read which no was which and gave her mouth back until she took it — and then her tongue keeps going out hunting the salt, "she didn't send it; it went," and her tidy explanation (he forgot to wipe, this once) is her doing the small dishonest thing with herself again, because he's been managing that for months, hundreds of after-kisses, and she won't look at it straight. And in the restaurant bathroom, at the top, it was Randi's face that arrived behind her eyes. She says "obviously" it was about Pace. The book keeps not agreeing. I noticed. I notice everything now.
- **Randi** — I got her whole childhood in two chapters and I understand her completely, which is dangerous. Then John — faultless, cotillion-good, never asked where the night was going, and she "liked not being asked," and the book let her like it and then made her pay alone at the mirror: "The smile still worked, she checked, and meant nothing." **WHERE THINGS STAND**

**HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM**

Pace — the anger got complicated in the best way. Chapter 53 did the thing I most respect this book for: it made both things true at once. He deliberately chose the smaller truth out of fear AND he sobbed alone over an engine that wasn't her car. I've been in the sealed room now. It doesn't excuse him — the book doesn't offer it as an excuse, he says so himself — but I understand the lock. And everything since the coat has been the man I came back for: reading which no was which, giving her mouth back until she took it, "Those stay on," meeting her people. I'm soft on him and watching him. The word is still the toll.

Vee — fiercely, fiercely proud, and starting to worry me in the way the book wants me worried. Her desire is hers, rendered from inside better than almost anything I've read — the drawer shutting on all of it, the shoes first-and-only, the walk down the hallway where performance and wanting merged. But she's editing again: the sweater lie for Cassie, the tidy "he forgot to wipe," the "obviously it was about Pace" the book keeps declining to co-sign. She has layers of private now and most of them are healthy. The one under all of them isn't, and it's Randi-shaped, and she's drifting toward it with her eyes shut, exactly the way Randi planned and exactly the way Randi is terrified of.

Randi — loved, and held at arm's length, and I can't fully separate those anymore. She's the best friend Vee has ever had and the sentence is true both ways she means it. The Gstaad chapter made her childhood legible — a girl raised by a woman who weaponized knowing her color learns to arrange the world so nothing can be used on her — and then she uses arrangement ON Vee. The coat plan was care and strategy in the same gesture, and I felt the book flag it and I'm holding the flag. And then she read Vee through a restaurant floor and said so out loud, pleasant as a hostess, and I gasped. She's not running the con. She's past the con. She's in the place where the only exit is the one scenario she can't run, and Brooke just lit a fire under it.

Sheri — trust her completely. She's the only person who tells Pace the truth at no discount. I want her at that dinner and I'm afraid she won't be.

Cassie — dread, still, but softer now. The card, the saved boxes, the closet of Christmas. She's the floor under Vee and she doesn't know about the graph yet.

**WHAT'S FADING**

Gstaad is going soft already — what's left is John's hands crossing over his heart on the stairs, "she walks in beauty" unfinished, the cold along the seam, and the fairytale text. The ball's menu is gone except the truffle and the waltz where his lead was firm and Pace's was a listening thing. The Ohio midnight survives as the room that didn't know her, the blonde moving into the fra diavolo memory, and the cropped photo — "she was the one who divided it." The Sheri diner holds better because the lines were load-bearing, but I've lost the Elvis-under-glass details and most of Paige. Daphne's math STILL needs re-running every time — tutor, older, gone when he was young, and I've lost the exact ages again. The coat night is compressing fast, the way the hottest chapters do: what remains is the formal voice, the crumble, "those stay on," the pancakes, and the cracked "I missed you so much" — the siren scare and the stall are already half-gone. The pointing game is down to the wrist-kiss, the "gross," and the taking-it-back; the whole middle geography of his mouth is blurring into "he was thorough and I was ruined," which is honestly the load-bearing 10% anyway. The sun-porch breakfast with Kayla and Meg is mostly Meg's maple bacon speech now. What never fades, the spine under all of it: he chose the smaller truth and I watched him choose it; she opened the coat on her own nerve and the wall came down; and the two of them alone in a warm room is still the thing I'm counting chapters between. They're back in the room together. That gap is closed. It's the OTHER room — the one where Vee and Randi are alone at a table twice a week — that's opening now, and I know it, and she doesn't, and the book has never once winked at me. I'm holding it to that through Wednesday.
