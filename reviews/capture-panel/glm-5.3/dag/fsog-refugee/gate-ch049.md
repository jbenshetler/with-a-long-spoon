REACTION — Oh, this chapter. I had to put the book down twice and then pick it right back up again.

First: Vee getting ready is one of the best things this book has done for her. The whole mirror sequence — her deciding that "the girl in the glass looked braver than Vee felt, and tonight the two would just have to be the same thing" — that's my girl grown all the way up. And her stopping herself at the edge, twice, once in the mirror and once in the car, because she wanted to arrive wound and *give it to him* — that's her desire, hers, from the inside, choosing him as the one who finishes it. I've read a hundred "nothing under the trenchcoat" scenes and they're all a show put on for the man; this one was a decision she made for herself, over her own shaking hands, and the shame turning into heat instead of shrinking her is exactly the difference between a heroine who's acted on and one who acts. Even the cop-car scare — which nearly stopped my heart along with hers — was the book refusing to let the drive be pure fantasy. I laughed out loud at Kayla doing the laundry monologue while Vee stands there bare under the wool. Perfect.

But the doorway. THE DOORWAY. "Vee, I l—" and *she didn't let him finish.* I actually said "no, no, NO" at the page. A month of silence, a whole tearful Ohio Christmas, the cracker sentence, the fight in the cold — and he was finally, FINALLY going to spend the word, and she cut him off with the coat. And I understand her — she'd decided words were the war and she was bringing the one thing he couldn't refuse, and how could she know he was mid-sentence into the thing she'd been starving for? She couldn't. But oh, it hurts. And now the book has shown me the word EXISTS in him, it rose, it was on its way out — after chapter 53 had him admit it to himself in the sober light of morning, that he'd chosen the smaller truth because he was afraid. So the machinery isn't broken; it's just that no one will let the two halves meet at the same moment. That's exquisite, and I'm furious about it, and I want the next chapter immediately.

The heat — yes, fine, a 3, and I don't hand those out. Doorway, on his knees, cold air on wet skin, him carrying her and giving up three steps from the bed — graceless is right and it's right that it's graceless, because it's not a performance, it's two people who spent a month starving. What undid me more than any of it, though, is after: him kneeling by her chair with his voice cracking on "I missed you so much," and her sitting there with her mouth full of midnight pancakes going "there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." That's the tenderness and the dominance in the same man and the food meaning something and the sex meaning something and I am entirely this book's creature.

Small flags I'm keeping honest about: he never actually said it. "I'm sorry, I never meant to hurt you, I missed you so much" is everything except the word, and she declared herself satisfied tonight — I don't fully trust "no room in her for wanting anything," because the wanting has come back before and it will come back again. And Randi coaching her into the coat — "he's not going to open his door and say no" — Randi steering her straight back into Pace's bed the same week Brooke told her to choose. I'm watching Randi. And Cassie's "Hmm." That single syllable is a lit fuse.
