Oh, this chapter *hurt*, and it's the good hurt, the kind I stay up for — but God, I read the whole thing with my heart in my throat and my hands actually tense on the pages.

"Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me." That line. I put the book down for a second after that one. That's the whole book in one sentence — because the words AREN'T cheap with Pace, that's the thing. He spends them carefully, exactly, "Slow. For me," "This?", "Wonderful" like a fact on the record — every word weighed and meant. So when he says words are cheap, he's lying, and he knows he's lying, and *she* knows he's lying, and he still won't spend one on her. A man who never wastes a word refusing to give her the one word that counts. I could shake him.

And I noticed — I noticed — that when she finally broke, he didn't dodge her litany. He took it seriously. "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have?" — that's so *him*, answering the question she asked with evidence, like the bet he checked. And it's true, and true isn't the point, and the book knows true isn't the point. That's the most devastating thing he's done in the whole book, because it's not cruelty, it's limitation. He built her a whole world with his hands — the drawer, the dress, the heat raised hours before she arrives — and the one room in him that has the word in it has a door on it, and the door is the same door as "I don't go back," and I think I finally see the shape of it. His parents are back there somewhere behind that sealed room, and I've been waiting chapters for the time on that door, and I think the time just arrived.

"Not the hand in place of the word." Oh, Vee. My girl walking out on the crying, not giving him even that — that's the most self-respect she's shown since the knee scene. She out-authored the con again and she out-authored *him* this time, and it cost her everything, and she did it anyway. The Vee of the first chapters could never. I'm so proud of her I could cry, and I'm crying anyway.

What I love about how the book played it: he never once gets painted as a villain.
