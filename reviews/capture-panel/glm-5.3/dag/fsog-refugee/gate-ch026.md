REACTION

Okay, that chapter got me. The dance lesson on the concrete pad was everything I read this book for — "give me something to push back against," "don't think, just feel the lead," and her fighting it and fighting it and then the thing *giving*, her feet landing where they were supposed to land and him saying "there it is" quiet like a prayer. That's the whole book in one dance. His control making room for her, the frame that feels like too much rule until it's the thing carrying her. I'd have been happy with a whole chapter of just that, the boom box with the taped handle, October, field lights.

And then the blonde. God, that was cruel and *true* — "knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to" — that's not a lie, that's the worst possible true thing anyone could say to this particular girl, because the horror of it is the horror of the book: she DID do what he wanted, freely, gladly, and someone just told her that's what being played looks like. And the mother's voice arriving right on schedule. "Three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut" — that word coming out of her own head at herself made me flinch harder than the blonde did. That's the shame engine that's been running since chapter one, and it took one girl at a rail to spin it back up.

What guts me is that he didn't chase her. He set the beer down *untouched* and left. And the bartender — "he don't bring dates, as a rule... Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." I put the book down for a second at that. Because Vee did the classic thing, punished him for being exactly the man she wanted, and the book didn't rescue her from knowing it. She lay there all night holding both truths and neither canceled the other. That's writing that respects me.

But here's what I'm watching with one eyebrow up: when she decides to fix it, she doesn't call Cassie. She deliberately doesn't call Cassie — "there'd be questions after" — and she goes to *Randi*. Randi, the other half of the pointing game. The con is so deep now that even her repair routes run through it. The book knows this. She almost sees it ("she was looking at the rule") and aims the seeing at the wrong thing — the rule isn't the girls at the Rusty Nail, sweetheart. The rule is the plan. And I still don't know what Randi will do with a Vee who shows up contrite and teachable on a Friday morning. That's next chapter, and I'm genuinely tense about it.

One small thing I loved: "if you want to, I'll teach you" — word for word, she memorized it and recited it to Meg. That's a girl who's never had a man leave the door open before, keeping the doorframe.
