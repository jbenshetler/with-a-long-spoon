REACTION — God, this chapter. It's sweet on the surface — two girls laughing over shoes — and underneath it's one of the most quietly devastating things this book has done to me, because I spent the whole thing with my stomach slowly filling with ice.

First the good, because there was real good: the shoe-store comedy landed. Randi in the pink rhinestone platforms asking gravely "do these go with burgundy?" — that's the version of her I'd be friends with, and Vee being the funniest woman in Virginia in her own store, on her own ground, that mattered to me. For twenty minutes the class difference between them was just *funny*, which is the only time it ever is. And "He didn't make this dress *for* you, he made it *to* you" — I put the book down. That named the thing Vee cried over and couldn't say, and the fact that Randi could name it instantly is the scariest thing about her. She sees everything. She always has.

And then the boutique. The thumb on the toenail. Vee grabbing her shoulder to stop the room from swinging, and Randi looking up, smiling, saying *nothing*. That's the third escalation of the Vee–Randi thing and it happened with a shoe fitter three feet away and it happened to Vee's *body* before her head could vote. I've been waiting for it and it still ambushed me. My ache for Randi is now officially out of control — the loafers bought mid-afternoon without a glance at the number, "box my old heels, I'll wear these out," money as the water she swims in — and the sheer gulf of that next to Vee's white knuckles. The book keeps making me love her in the exact same gesture by which she's ruining everything.

Because here's the ice: Vee put the *credit card* down. Not the debit — the emergency card, the one with nothing under it, and the book did not flinch and neither did she, and I felt every dollar of it. And I couldn't help noticing that this entire afternoon — the boutique, the "I've got her" at the fitter, "it goes on these" — is Randi steering her toward the expensive yes the way she once steered her toward Pace in a doorway. The blurb's "lightly, laughing, as if by accident" — that's this chapter. Randi even heard "lover" rearrange Vee's chest and *used the word on purpose.* Vee thinks she's found the friend of her life. I think she just took on debt she can't carry to walk into a room Randi wants her to stop. And the worst part is I can't tell how much of Randi's delight is the con and how much is her drowning — the "which is exactly why you're not buying that" had real tenderness in it. The good shoes ARE what the dress is owed. Both things are true. That's this book's whole cruel genius and I'm fully in its grip.

Also — "same feet, baby, same size, same everything. We can share." I caught that. I don't think I was supposed to miss it. Randi said *same everything* and I need a minute.
