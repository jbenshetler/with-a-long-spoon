REACTION — I put the book down after the last chapter and just sat there, and this one let me live inside the afterglow of it through Cassie, which is exactly what I needed. Vee telling the story to her roommate is my favorite kind of chapter — the scene replayed through a woman's own mouth, so you get to hear what she *kept*. And what she kept is the stuff I care about: "his eyes asked, *may I look*." That's the whole book in one line, and she found it herself, without the narrative having to underline it.

But the beat that undid me was the bra question. Cassie doing the detective work — "Did he just say it? Take it off?" — and Vee circling it: he didn't use the word, but he waited. "He left me a door." I actually got a little emotional, because this is the thing I've been hunting for years and mostly finding badly imitated. A man who states the need plainly and then stands back from the door — and a heroine who notices the door and walks through it on purpose. And Cassie, bless her, names it for both of us: "He asked." Vee's "He didn't use the word. But yes." I'd have highlighted that sentence if I had a pen near the bed.

The heat in this chapter is almost all retrospective and it still worked on me. "I locked eyes with him. And then I let him look." The box conversation — "you keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any" — is funny, but it's also the chapter being honest about the wanting, which I appreciate. The whole book is teasing at a slower tempo than anything I usually tolerate and I'm not even mad. The "I was grinding on *him*" reveal had me laughing out loud — her spending her new power, her giddy little wickedness — and Cassie throwing the pillow is the friendship the blurb promised me Randi would pretend to be.

And that's the shadow over all of it, isn't it. The real friend is here, on the bed, being delighted for her with no agenda, and I spent the whole chapter loving Cassie and remembering that the "dear friend" with the cool hand is the con. Vee narrates the heat turned up in the house — hours before, as soon as he knew she was coming — as pure tenderness, and it is, and it's also a man who architects intimacy the way he architects dresses, and I still don't know what the book owes for that. He built the riser before she said yes to the dress. He matched the silk to her instead of the dress she described. Every beautiful thing in this book is also a man reading a woman three steps ahead of her and being right. The blurb says the debt comes due. I'm fully invested in finding out how.

The shirt on the pillow. "She'd put it there without thinking." God, this book does the small objects so well.
