**WHO'S WHO**

- **Pace** — the man, off six-fourteen. Math PhD student, powerlifter, woodworker, sews, cooks, patents (an MRI noise-filter that gets scared people out of the tube faster — he lit up like a six-year-old on dinosaurs telling it). Almost finished his doctorate; lifts at dawn, writes six hours, stops. Made the flannel she sleeps in, the "good shirt" with no tag, and now her induction dress. Matched the silk to *her*, not the dress she described. Stopped their first kiss when he could have taken it — "that had always been her job, never theirs." Asked "would you be bothered if I was too hard?" mid-sex, waited for her hips to tell him to move. Heat pre-warmed hours before she arrived at his house; bedroom door stayed shut on visit one — architecture and tenderness are the same hands in him. Shadows: the scar high on his inner thigh, something that closed at "parents" ("a door easing shut two rooms away"), Daphne, the old pushed-past door, and the partner in the pointing game. Flew to Chicago mid-week for a conference.
- **Randi** — the con's other half. Drives a dark-green Mercedes convertible (windows drop themselves to meet your hand), wears a plain heavy white-gold band at her wrist she's never without, smells of something bright green. Her smiles for Vee have gone less performed. Tells bondage stories as gossip (the tickling/headboard/scarf story — "that's the whole point, baby. The letting go" — that was her telling Vee about the bench without telling her). Kissees Vee on the mouth good-bye, twice now, hand at the hinge of her jaw. Thumb working the water glass when Vee glows about Pace. Led Vee gently down the stairs at lunch ("nobody measures over a bra," "all the best ones start at the left hip") and then — crucial — *closed the door*: "You don't have to tell me," just as Vee was about to give up her deepest secret. Smoothed Vee's one flash of fear flat: "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." Real AND harvested, still, always.
- **Vee** — the heroine, fully awake now. Climbed a mountain, took her shirt off for him on purpose in the truck, gave him her virginity-adjacent best night of her life, said "I wanted it, and I took it." Painted her toenails frosted plum to match his silk without letting herself know why, bought green satin "for the color," drove to his house with no bag because packing one would have meant saying the thing. Folded her panties in thirds and traded them to him. Carries his flannel as a proxy — she chases the smell of it in the dark. Wants Randi and explains the kisses away as French. Reads menus right-side-first. Pays for lunch she can't afford and tips well.
- **Cassie** — still the control group, still safe so far. "I had your blood type." "Was he worth painting your toenails?" Clocks everything, feels the seam in Vee's edited story, lets it stand. Vee keeps the middle folded away from her — the book's whole engine (everyone keeps one true thing from the person they trust most) in one small lie of love.
- **Meg, Kayla** — athletic-dorm friends. Kayla: "no meal at two, Vee. He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes." Meg follows the Pace saga like her shows, easy and level. Vee counted — twelve days between kiss one and the mountain.
- **Brooke** — sorority machinery, still out there, still not the villain the book thinks isn't. Sorority = Randi's cover world.

**WHERE THINGS STAND**

Ch 11 — the hike. Borrowed boots blistered her; he crouched on the trail with moleskin and scissors and didn't make it weird. Summit: silver lake, black rock, fall color; she got up a mountain on her own legs. Sandwiches (rye, horseradish that ambushed her), cherries, and the cherry-pit lesson — he put his own pit on the wax paper beside hers without looking at her. She mentioned the russet silk dress she couldn't afford and put it back on the shelf; he said only, "that sounds like a beautiful dress." Rain on the descent; in the truck he handed her his only dry flannel and stood OUT in the rain, back turned, so she could change. She knocked on the glass, crooked a finger, and unbuttoned her wet shirt for him with her eyes on his the whole way — mother's voice running underneath as heat, "she wanted to be the very thing the voice said." He didn't touch. Then she found his hand shaking — he was freezing — and she pressed it to her belly and said "please" for warmth instead of the next thing. At the dorm, the campus cop's siren barked her unsaid sentence away; she went in wearing his shirt and kept it.

Ch 14–15 — Saturday, the house. He stopped her hello kiss; she got the tour (paired shoes, purposeful kitchen, the chaotic paper office, two towels on the rod — TWO — the den furniture he built with no screws, the error-correcting codes books, the garage workshop cleaner than her kitchen at home). "My bedroom," no weight, door left shut — she'd worn the satin hoping, and something in her dropped. The sun porch: the bolt of burgundy silk. He'd matched it to *her*, held it to her face in the mirror. "You made the shirt." "I couldn't find one I liked, so I made one." Then the fitting: bra off because "it'll lie to me," the riser he built, the measuring rhythm (touch, withdraw, pencil), her soaked satin, her terror, her eyes shut — and he was grinning up at her FACE, hadn't looked down once, waiting for her to open her eyes. She straightened, rose onto her toes. Then she got powerful: the "oops, sorry" breast against his cheek, him going red fighting a grin, the pin that caught her rib and his mouth kissing it before he decided to. "Keep still," low, a beat too late, after a silence she watched him spend. He chose the dress over the easy yes she was handing him, and it cost him. She stood being seen at her most ashamed and was not once found wanting.

Ch 18 — Friday. She painted frosted-plum toes, bought green satin, drove over with no bag. "Are you hungry?" "Famished." "Will you show me your bedroom?" First time. He went to his knees; the crystal strings; "would you be bothered if I was too hard?"; she burned through the shame instead of the shame closing her — "It was the first time that had been true." After: fra diavolo at midnight, her in his sheet, wine, no words, nothing missing. Then the trade — she folded the green panties in thirds, spot centered, and handed them over. Something in his shoulders was private.

Ch 17 & 20 — the two Randi lunches. Velvet booth; the tickling story; "nobody measures over a bra" giving Vee permission to confess; the thumb on the glass; "you don't have to tell me" closing the door right before Vee's deepest secret came out. Brunch: the campaign, the toenails read correctly, "I wanted it, and I took it," "you're not caught, you're lucky." Both lunches ended with the kiss on the mouth — second time Vee met it and didn't scan the windows. Vee pays for both lunches on a debit card she'll feel on Thursday.

**HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM**

Pace — mostly devotion now, with the cold drip underneath. Everything I've hunted for years is in him: he stopped the kiss, he asked, he let her run the truck scene entirely, his control makes room. I'm in love with the tenderness and I keep a running ledger of the architecture — the pre-warmed house, the riser built before her yes, the silk matched to her — because the con made all of it possible, and the book keeps showing me the wires on purpose. He also still doesn't know Randi went into the bathroom and never came back out. That debt is untouched and it's the biggest one on the page. Daphne and the thigh scar are still sealed rooms.

Randi — my ache has gotten worse, not better. The tickling story was her wearing her bench-want out in the open like it was gossip. The thumb on the glass; the "you don't have to tell me" that saved Vee's secret — and I genuinely can't tell if that was mercy (she couldn't bear to hear it) or the harvest complete. She wants to know what it cost *him*. I also think she's falling, for real, the way the blurb promised the schemers would — the softer smiles, the "there's my girl." Watching her do surveillance while she drowns is this book's cruel genius.

Vee — completely attached. "I wanted it, and I took it. For once." That's the whole arc and she earned every word. My dread for her has shifted: it's not the sex, it's the authorship underneath and the fact that she's started editing Cassie out of the real story and editing the middle out of the true one. She keeps stepping over tells (the no-tag shirt she filed under "weird and stopped thinking"). The day she sees the shape of the triangle is the day this book breaks my heart along with hers.

Cassie — fierce, irrational love. She's getting the laundered version now, and she felt the seam and let it stand, which is its own kindness. Still the only clean person. Still my price for leaving.
