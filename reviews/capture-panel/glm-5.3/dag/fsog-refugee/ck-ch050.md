**WHO'S WHO**

The photo chapter: she offered, he caught her wrist not to stop her but to say *you don't have to, I like you exactly like this* — she went and made herself up anyway, and then HE arranged her, a kiss at every place his hands set her, and the picture he handed back was her as his eyes had her, lovelier than anything she'd have caught alone. **HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM**

Pace — I love him and I'm angry at him and the book has finally made me hold both. He's not a villain; he's a good man failing at the exact thing she needs, and the book never once asked me to find his silence romantic — it let it be a real wound the whole way. But "words are cheap" from a man who weighs every word like gold is a lie he knows he's telling, and the door on his sealed room and the door on the word are the same door, and she is standing on the mat outside it asking to be let in. I need him to earn this back with the actual word, out loud, awake. Nothing less.

Vee — proud past sense. The doorway scene is the most self-respect she's shown since "Don't." She asked clean and she left clean. I'm also frightened for her — she retired her best sentence, and that's a door closing inside her, and "she would not have to learn tonight what her life looked like without him in it" is doing a lot of load-bearing.

Randi — my whole read wobbled and re-formed. Not mercy, not harvest: drowning, exactly as I'd half-written. Everything smooth and aimed, and the one true thing she's said came out ragged by accident. Her silence to Vee isn't villainy — it's cowardice with the price tag showing, and the book showed me the price: she'd trade the most important hour of her week for terrified hope. "Every word of it true. None of it the thing." That's her soul. I ached for her in Gstaad more than I've ever ached for anyone in this book except Vee in chapter 48.

Cassie — still clean, still drifting, still the day I cry in public. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to" — she's closer to the truth than anyone but Vee.
