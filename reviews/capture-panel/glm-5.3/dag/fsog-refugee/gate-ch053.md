REACTION — Oh, this one got me. Not the way the hot chapters do — quieter, but it landed harder than either of the Christmas chapters, because for the first time since she walked out the door, we're standing on the other side of it. We're in HIS head. Fifty-two chapters of me wondering "is it can't or won't," and the book finally gives me his side of the night — and it's worse and better than I feared. "He would not bring her down today" — his first thought walking in is protecting her from his mood. That's the man I fell for in this series, still in there, still doing care as his first language. And then Sheri, gloriously, says everything I've been screaming at this book since chapter 47: "She didn't ask you for reasons." YES. Say it plainer, Sheri. "That's a hell of a lot of reasons for a man who's already got the girl" — that's the whole critique of "I love how good we are together" in one sentence, delivered over stolen fries.

But the thing that stopped my breath: "I can't." Out loud. To another human being. He finally said it — not the word, but the confession that the word is sealed. Sheri's "then not saying it matters more to you than having her" is brutal and I don't fully buy it — she doesn't know about the two breaths, the door I think is welded shut to a whole scarred childhood — but she's not wrong either. And him laying bricks — "every brick was true and the true things were supposed to come to something" — that's the most honest sentence about him the book has written. He thinks truth accumulates. It doesn't. She needs the one true thing said plain, and he knows it, and he can't, and he's just told someone.

Also — the reveal that he brings Randi dancing AND brings Sheri to that same hall, and Sheri clocked Randi's "harem applicant" look. This man has a whole quiet ecosystem of women he treats decently, and the book keeps proving he's not a player — "never been a hit-'em-and-quit-'em man." Which is exactly why Vee's midnight jealousy-tantrum hurt me to read last chapter: she's wrong about him, and we know she's wrong, and she doesn't. The dramatic irony is now fully loaded on both barrels — she's building a blonde out of spite while he's sitting in a diner describing "the air crackles" and going red in the face over her legs at Christmastime. He's gone on her. Genuinely, completely gone. He just can't hand her the word.

And Sheri and Paige. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." Two lines and I know why he never pushes her about her people — because he's the same. That's the door. Grief and wordlessness are the same sealed room, in him and in her, and the book just lined them up without saying so.

The gap is still there — Vee and Pace haven't shared a page since chapter 50, and this was a Sheri chapter, not a reunion — but it didn't cost me anything this time, because the care was on every line of it. This is the book telling me he's dying too, and that he told a witness. I'd have read three more chapters of this. Though God help me if "the usual" is all I get for another volume and a half.
