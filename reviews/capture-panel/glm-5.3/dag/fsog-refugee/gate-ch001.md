REACTION — Okay, so I opened this for the blurb's promise — "every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — and chapter one goes straight at exactly the thing I'm here for. The consent is *lovingly* done. He backs off and lets her decide the kiss. She turns his chair, she straddles him, he lets her. "Tell me your word." "Red." "Tell me anytime." And then the actual scene — he's not in a hurry, he *refuses* to be in a hurry, and her frustration with that is the whole erotic engine. The begging sequence wrecked me a little. When her "please" finally changes registers — from the ask-a-waiter voice to the real one — and he hears it and says "There you are, Randi" — I put the book down and had to sit with that. That's the thing I've been hunting for in this genre for years: dominance that's actually *attention*. He's not taking, he's waiting for her to arrive. And it's hot as hell while being genuinely tender.

But here's what's got my stomach in a knot: the last third. She locks the bathroom door — and the book notes she's never done that — and he *lets her*, because of Daphne, because he learned that a closed door is hers. And that's the right thing, and I love him for it, and it's also exactly what lets the lie happen. Because what's behind the door is her putting the face back on. "He could have the face. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." And he's sitting on the bed thinking it was a remarkably good evening while she does it. The flinch at "you're beautiful." The water bottle cap under her thumbnail — she pressed it into her nail bed and didn't know she was doing it, and he took the bottle but did he *see* it? That one line scared me more than anything on the bench.

And then I remembered the jacket, and got chills: "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one — it began as a game between the two people now closest to her." So this is *Randi* — the glamorous friend who's supposedly steering Vee toward Pace — and we've just watched theManipulator (is that too harsh? she doesn't feel like one yet) get cracked open and slam the shell shut. If Randi and Pace have a history, if there's a plan, then everything he did tonight reads differently on a second pass. The apple. The bench built "for you. Tonight." How long has he been reading her? I'm suspicious of him now in a way the chapter itself didn't make me — I'm suspicious of the *book*, and I mean that as a compliment. It's doing the "reader falls for it exactly as she does" thing the blurb promised, and I'm falling for it and watching myself fall.

The writing itself — the Steely Dan, the honeycrisp slices cut side up because she eats them faster that way, the sorority-pink toenails versus the blue — it's precise in a way that feels like the author is also watching her closely, the same way Pace does. Sometimes it tips over into too-precise, a couple of those "the small breath of a laugh" beats repeated one time too many. Don't care. I'm in.
