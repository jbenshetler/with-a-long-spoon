REACTION:

Okay. I bought this book for Vee — the quiet starving girl, the mathematician who asks before he takes — and chapter one gives me none of her. Instead I get *Randi*, the glamorous best friend from the blurb, on a bench in Pace's bedroom, and I spent the first three pages confused and then not confused at all, because the blurb told me exactly what to look for: *it began as a game between the two people now closest to her.* This is the game before the girl. These two already have a whole history — the overnight bag, the cashmere blanket she brought in week two, the deadbolt she turns behind her — and I'm being shown the machinery from inside before the machine gets pointed at anyone. I should be annoyed. I'm not. I'm unsettled in the way I pay money to be unsettled.

Because Pace is — God, Pace is *exactly the thing I left the genre looking for.* The safeword, twice, before anything starts. "You okay?" mid-scene. He crouches at her face and just... stays there, thirty seconds, longer than a transition is supposed to be, stroking her hair while she's facedown and red and nobody's getting anything out of it but her. He takes her heels off like they're something. And the edging — the whole engine of the chapter is that he won't accept the *performed* please. He hears her first please, the one in her waiter-asking-for-water voice, and he says *no*, and I held my breath, because I've read that beat done cruelly a hundred times — denial as flex, the dom enjoying the no. This no was different. He was listening for *her*, the one under the face, and the moment that please came up from below, broken and ugly and real — "Good girl" — I actually put the book down for a second. That's the heat I came for. Not the spanking. The *finding*. "There you are, Randi." I got chills.

And then the book does something the blurb warned me about but I wasn't ready for: it takes it all back. The bathroom. The mirror. Her trying the photo-smile on top of the face that begged and *seeing the disjunction*. Her deciding, quiet as a deadbolt, *not ever.* "He could have the face. The face was very good." That broke my heart a little, and it scared me more than a little, because she was more alive on that bench than she has ever been and she's going to wall it off anyway — and he's sitting on the bed thinking it was a remarkably good evening. The tenderness worked. That's the problem. The blurb's last line is starting to make a horrible kind of sense.

Two things I noticed and can't stop chewing on. The water bottle cap under her thumbnail — she presses the sharp edge into her nail bed and he takes the bottle away without comment, and that's a girl who has a whole private relationship with pain that has nothing to do with his bench. And Pace's quiet "small warm pleasure of how much further she had gone than he had thought she would." Helped her there, his word. *Found* it, his word. There's a collector's patience under all that care, and the Daphne backstory — the door he didn't respect, once, when he was young — is either the reason I trust him or the reason I shouldn't, and I genuinely can't tell yet. A man who learned consent as an expensive lesson can be the safest man in the world or the most dangerous one, depending on what he does with the skill. I'll be watching that.

But — and this is what keeps me honest — everything he actually did in this chapter was clean. The begging was real and it was *hers*, freely built, freely given, tears and all. He asked. She answered. The one line I'll be turning over for days: *Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over.* Blurb promised me that. Chapter one already made it complicated, because she handed it over and then wished she hadn't, and that's not a consent problem, that's a *being-seen* problem, and I've never read that exact thing before.

Also: where is Vee. I was promised Vee. I assume she's coming through Randi, being drawn "further open," and I now know things about Randi's marriage to her own mirror that Vee doesn't, and the dramatic irony is already loaded like a gun. That's the hook. That, and I need to see Pace meet a girl who isn't armored the way Randi is — or worse, armored a completely different way.
