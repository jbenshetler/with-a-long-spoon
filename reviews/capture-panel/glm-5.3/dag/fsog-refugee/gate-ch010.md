REACTION — Okay, this one got under my skin in a way the explicit chapters didn't, and I need a minute to sit with why.

Vee sitting on that high shelf of a chair, telling Randi about the dinner — "May I. Not can I. Who even says that" — and Randi saying "You let him choose for you. And you liked it," slow, holding it up to the light. I actually put the book down there. Because I know what Randi's doing. I've read the jacket. The reader is *supposed* to know: Randi isn't just enjoying the story, she's taking a full inventory of her own product. Checking that the gift landed. And the awful, delicious part is that every word Vee says is true. The night really was that good. The softness was real. The kiss was Vee's — she stopped her own feet and made it happen. Nothing here is a lie, and everything here is being harvested. I keep flip-flopping between *this is the most sinister chapter so far* and *this is the tenderest one*, and the fact that it's both at once is exactly what I signed up for with that blurb.

And then Randi says "Vee's my dear friend," with the cool damp hand on hers in front of Brooke, and I felt that hit Vee the way Pace's "may I" hit her — a key in a lock. "No one had chosen her out loud like that in a long time." God. That's the whole engine of this book, isn't it. Vee is starving and these two people are feeding her, and the feeding is genuine, and the feeding is also a program. The hand on the table costs Randi something socially, and she spends it — and she spends it *knowing exactly what she's buying*. Whereas Vee gets to walk home feeling picked, carrying it the whole way. I'm both heartsick for her and — I hate to say it — a little envious of her. Which is uncomfortable, and I think it's on purpose.

What keeps me on board, and this matters to me more than anything else in a book like this: Vee's yes keeps being hers. When Brooke and the tall one walk in and Vee shrinks — "to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi" — that's the old shame reflex doing its thing, and Randi answers it with the hand and the name. The care is real even inside the con. Pace's asking is real even inside the setup. I can live inside this dramatic irony as long as nobody's *no* gets steamrolled, and so far the book has been almost show-offy careful about that. Chapter 8 was the one I'd hand to anyone who thinks this genre can't do consent: he smiles and lets the rule stand and walks on, and she pulls him back. That's the thing I've been hunting for years, right there.

The detail I can't stop chewing on: Brooke was in that smoothie room in Chapter 2's world — the machine that sized Randi up over Pace's name, the "he's hard to say no to" line Vee hasn't heard. And now Brooke is smiling down at Vee, taking her measure, "top to bottom, and stay warm the whole way." I wanted to crawl out of my skin. The sorority apparatus is going to be watching this whole thing unfold, and Vee has no idea she's on stage in a play two other people wrote the opening scene of. The "we should all get dinner sometime" — which is lovely, and means nothing — was pitched perfectly. These women are scary and I don't think the book thinks they're the villains, which is scarier.

Also, small aching thing: Cassie getting edited out of the smoothie plan, "it isn't really her place," and Vee's comfortable little rationalization while something "turned over, small, and she let it lie." Cassie is the one person in this book with no agenda and functioning eyes, and the book is slowly, gently peeling Vee away from her. "Goodbye, Vee" back in chapter four is going to haunt me the whole read. If something bad happens to Cassie out of this I will genuinely be upset.

I'm in. Fully. I said yes to this book the way Vee said yes to the exception, and I know the tree is coming where I should say my starchy rule, and I'm not going to.
