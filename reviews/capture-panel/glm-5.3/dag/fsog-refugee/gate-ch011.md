**REACTION**

Okay. I put this down and had to just sit for a minute.

The flannel. The whole chapter is the flannel, and I mean the whole engine of it. He hands her his only dry shirt and gets OUT of the truck — out into the rain, back turned — and I initially went "wait, why?" right alongside her, and then the penny dropped exactly when it dropped for her: he left so she could change in private. He gave her the cab and took the weather. And then she *knocks on the window and crooks her finger at him.* She calls him back IN, and then she undresses for him with her eyes on his the whole way, mother's voice running like a soundtrack under it, and — this is the part — the mother's voice doesn't stop her, it becomes part of the heat. "She wanted to be the very thing the voice said." That's the most honest sentence about shame and desire I've read in this genre, maybe ever. I've read a hundred heroines "overcoming" their hangups; I've read almost none who *metabolize* the hangup into the want. That's her desire, from inside, exactly what I'm here for. And he sits there and doesn't touch her. Full attention, no hands, lets her run the whole scene and take it as far as she chooses. He doesn't know what she's called him back for and he waits to find out. God. That's the thing. That's the whole thing I've been hunting.

And then the gut-punch, the thing that made this chapter more than a lovely day: his hand is *shaking.* He's been standing in the rain giving her the truck, freezing, "a long way from the question of whether he was cold," and her reach for the next thing stops mid-air and she presses his hand to her belly and says *please* for warmth instead. She chooses him over the heat. First time she's had the next thing in her hands — she's literally holding his hand on the way to her mouth — and she puts it down. That's consent culture rendered as tenderness and I nearly cried in a coffee shop about it.

The smaller beats are doing so much work. The moleskin — "I didn't think to ask about the boots," he apologizes for something no romance hero ever apologizes for. The cherry pit scene made my chest hurt: she spits, she's mortified, the voice says *common, no one taught you* — and he puts his own pit on the wax paper beside hers without looking at her. He teaches without ever making her small. That's dominance in miniature. That's the man.

And the dress. She puts the russet dress back on the shelf — "you put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking" — and he says only, weightless, "that sounds like a beautiful dress," and lets it stay down. I don't trust that for a SECOND. He's going to buy her that dress, and a man with a plan and a partner who's been picking Vee's clothes-adjacent moves all along buying her the dress she denied herself is going to feel both wonderful and like a chess move, and I'll be chewing on which it is until it happens.

The siren at the end is pure villainy of craft — barking her unsaid sentence right out of her mouth. She was about to put a small thing where a big thing went and the book snatched it. I'm annoyed and impressed.

The dread never fully left me, though. I know this day was authored. Randi picked her, the collision was engineered, and now here's Vee on a mountain giving him a striptease in his flannel, every yes free and every yes *planned for.* The chapter earns every one of her yeses — that's what makes it hurt. He walked at the speed of her not knowing how. So did the con.

Cassie screaming in the hall about the induction letter — still the only clean person in this book. Protect her.
