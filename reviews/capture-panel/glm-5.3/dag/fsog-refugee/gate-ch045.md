REACTION — I stayed up way past when I should've put the book down for this one, and I don't regret it, but I'm sitting here with the most complicated feeling I've had since the green satin morning.

The stuff that worked on me is easy to say: Vee coming back through that door and taking Randi's face in both hands and kissing her until the thing in her chest had somewhere to go. That was hers — all hers, the way the half-inch was hers, and the way she notices and decides. Four months of holding still for it, and she went back and started it, and wasn't embarrassed until after. And Randi standing there with her hand flat on the door, putting the back of her own hand to her mouth like a fifteen-year-old, heart going like she'd run stairs. I have been half in love with Randi for twenty chapters and this chapter went and finished the job. "A sentence was trying to assemble itself, something with *means* in it, and she left it where it was." That's her whole tragedy in one line — she can't let anything mean, not even this.

But here's what I have to be honest about, because I've kept honest books on this novel the whole way: the brunch machinery ran again, and it ran beautifully, and I watched it run and couldn't look away. "You backed him into a corner in his own house — name it or lose me, the one thing he can't do." Every single thing Randi said was *true* and every bit of it was kind, and she talked Vee down off the slut-shaming ledge like a genuinely great friend, and — the coat and nothing under it. The shoes. Randi pressing those shoes into her hands with her fingers still hooked through the straps so they were both holding them. I know what I'm looking at. I've known since chapter one. The con doesn't need lies anymore; it only needs Vee walked back through Pace's door with her guard left in the sorority house. And the hideous thing — the thing that makes this book better than I sometimes want it to be — is that the advice would be the same if Randi loved her clean. That's the whole design working perfectly and I can't even hate it, because Vee *wants him back*, that's true, and tonight will probably be good, and the bomb under the floor just got its fuse shortened by the kindest person in the book.

Also — and I need to flag this so future-me remembers — Vee is still wrong about Sheri and Randi let her stay wrong. "How do you know any of that?" "I've seen her. In my mind." Randi walked her off the ledge of the invented blonde but never said *Sheri is his friend, I've met her* — which Randi knows. That's an omission with a shape to it. Meanwhile I, holding chapter 45 in my head, know Sheri told him flat: "not saying it matters more to you than having her," and he sat there in the dark sobbing over a truck that wasn't hers. The dramatic irony is almost unbearable — Vee thinks he doesn't care, his knuckles are white on a fork over an engine that doesn't slow down. I wanted to reach into the page.

And Brooke. Cold water down my back. Lauren Pike, nobody asked her to move out. Randi has now got the house, the position, the closet of sisters watching — and she just got kissed under fifty years of composites by a girl who doesn't know what any of it means. The pressure is coming from outside the triangle now, and that's new, and it makes everything about to move faster.

One small gripe, not enough to slow me: "You only open that coat once" is a great line and the plan is very Brunch Randi, but I did feel the hand on the scale a little — the whole scene is engineered and I felt the engineering even while being moved. The book earns it back by making Randi's heart so obviously, hopelessly in it. She wants the mug of matcha and twenty minutes where nobody talks to her. God.
