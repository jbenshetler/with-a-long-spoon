REACTION — Oh, the shirt. The *shirt*. I knew it would come back someday — "the borrowed shirt" has been sitting in my notes since Brooke/Meg faded out — and I did not expect it to come back like this, as a scent economy, a whole little liturgy of him she's built to survive Tuesdays through Thursdays. "It stopped smelling like you" — and she *washed it*, and brought it out here for a refill. That's the most naked thing she's done in this book with her clothes on. And he put it on over his other shirt, right there, immediately, two shirts in his own kitchen, because she told him to. That's him. That's the whole man in one gesture — the drawer, the scrunchies, places made instead of promises, and now this: he wears her homesickness for her.

And I noticed what the book is quietly doing with the five nights. She asked for the space — *she* asked — and he said of course, no fight, no tax, no going cold, and the chapter is honest that the grace *hurt* her, that some unreasonable corner wanted him to fight for the nights. I've been that unreasonable corner in a hundred books where the man did fight and it was called romance. Here it isn't. Here the door just stands open and she comes back through it on day five with her body keeping a tally her mouth won't read out. That's the version of being wanted I actually trust — he wants her so much that five nights of not-grasping is the harder proof.

The doorway scene — the hopping out of jeans, the bra he wouldn't pull off, just held, so she had to unwind out of it herself — I laughed and it was hot at the same time, and that combination is this book's signature. And the pizza! The cotton candy versus cut strawberries line. Her mouth waking up is apparently a permanent condition now, and he watched her count the flavors and said nothing, and neither of them could say whether the dough or the woman got the larger share of his attention, and she *didn't mind*. That's a marriage sentence, forty years down the road, hiding in a Tuesday.

One thread pulled tight: she still can't say it. She built him a scent-fetish, colonized a drawer, cried over a flannel — and the word stays in the dark. It's closer now, though. It's in her eyes blinking down tears in his kitchen. And "one more door raised and not yet opened" — that's Randi, and Randi just got caught by a graph, said "God, I want her" into a couch cushion and walked it back in one breath, and told Pace the goodbye kisses are "work" while coming undone describing them. The hum is as loud as it's ever been. But this chapter was all Vee-and-Pace, the two of them alone in a house, which is the thing I track underneath everything — and it delivered.
