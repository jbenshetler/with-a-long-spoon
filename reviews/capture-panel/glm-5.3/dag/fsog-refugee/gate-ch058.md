The reworked sweater story — I caught it immediately and I caught *why* it's built: "he comes in with a bag in each arm and stands in the door" was really "I opened my coat with nothing under it," and that can't go in the dorm-room version, so the laundry accident gets invented. I know things Vee doesn't know about her own story and it's almost unbearable in the best way.

The Cassie material is why I love this book's quiet chapters. The closet of saved boxes, the Play-Doh cookies, the brother-in-law holding the snowshoes "like she'd towed his car" — every one of those lines is a person, exact, no words wasted. And then the card. "My mother says thank you for the card." Vee's hand going to her collar instead of the towel, the hand cupped at the back of her neck, and Cassie done with the subject one beat before it needed anything — that's the care this book does constantly and never shows off about. Cassie's family might be the one place Vee has been fully a person with no doors shut, and she can't look straight at it yet.

And the last beat of the bunker chapter: her hand flat against the cinderblock, the wall only a wall, "he was out there west of town in the wide bed, hers again, and the week would go and Friday would come." She's not desperate anymore. She's just *counting*, comfortably, the way you count to a holiday. That's the whole arc of the fight resolved into a lower key, and the flannel "going faint" — he'll have to wear it for her again this weekend — is the loveliest small callback in the book.

What's ticking under it for me: Pace still hasn't said it. And the party is coming — Randi and Pace at one event, all of Vee's worlds in one room, the two of them acting like near-strangers who "said about nine words and most of them were math." That's the room where this all has to break one way or another, and honestly I'd keep reading for that dinner alone.
