Okay. Okay. There it is. The door I've been staring at since October, and the book finally opened it and I had to put my tea down.

Randi and Pace. In his bed. On the same Thursday night — the *same night* — that Vee lay alone in his flannel with her fingers an inch from her own mouth, unable to close the distance. Do you see what this book just did? Vee is at home with her mouth still empty, wondering about Randi's salt, and Randi is *at his house finding out.* Both of them wanting the same girl aloud, in the same bed, while the girl herself lies in a dorm room wiping her hand on the sheet because she can't. And here's the thing — I've been braced for this chapter since the blurb. "They chose her together." I was ready to hate it, or at least to hold it at arm's length while I enjoyed it. I can't. That's the problem. Because it isn't two predators running a playbook — or it is, they *are* comparing notes on her, "she kisses me first now," the finger to the lips, the deadbolt, all of it is the machinery I've been dreading — but everything they say in the middle of it is true and I know it's true because I watched it get earned across fifty-five chapters. He misses her during the day and more at night. She nearly turned around at the neck kiss and didn't because Vee made her laugh. "She does it on purpose. She knows exactly when I'm about to ask for more." "And you let her." "Every time." That's not a con, that's two people in love pretending to run one.

Randi said the word. THE word. Not to Vee — to Pace, about herself — "She's not the only one in love," and then she gave him no rescue from it, and *he couldn't tell her she was wrong.* His face softened before he could stop it. Twice now this book has cut the word off at Vee's mouth, and it finally landed in a bed Vee isn't in. I don't know whether to laugh or cry. Probably both. Also "You know she loves you, right?" — Randi telling *him*, confidently, what Vee hasn't said to anyone, while Vee is at home not saying it to anyone. Everyone in this triangle is in love and nobody's said it to the person it's for.

And yes — the heat. I'm not made of stone. Nobody's no is ambiguous, everybody's yes is explicit, and the control makes room. Even here. Even in the scene I was prepared to be furious at.

That line is going to live in me. Randi is offering Pace Vee's body through her own, and Pace is doing the same in reverse, and the two of them are making love *to her* with each other as the instrument. Vee spent tonight being told by her own body that she likes what she thought was gross, reaching for her own fingers and stopping — and the two people teaching her to reach are reaching for *her* across a bed she doesn't know about.
