REACTION — Okay. There it is. The door I've known was there since the jacket — "Randi and Pace chose her together" — and I've spent fifty-six chapters with my hand on the knob, and the book just opened it while I wasn't braced, mid-Thursday, no warning, *Randi came against his mouth* like a sentence I'd already read.

And my honest first reaction, the one I'd text at 1 a.m.: **I'm not even shocked and I'm wrecked anyway.** I KNEW. We all knew — the blanket, the brunches, the two of them holding the same shoes. But knowing the con exists and watching it *have sex* are two different animals entirely. I expected to feel slimed. I braced for the genre's old trick — the two of them arranging her, the reader invited to find it hot because the book does. And instead what's on the page is two people who were supposed to be running a game on a girl and got caught in it themselves, telling each other the truth in the one room where they don't have to lie — that they miss her. That she kisses him first now. That Randi nearly turned around when Vee kissed her neck — *the same neck kiss I watched Vee land on Thursday, and Randi went home, called him late, and showed up at his door.* Two chapters happening at once the whole time. The chronology is doing me violence. Thursday night Vee lay alone in the flannel with her fingers at her mouth, one breath from her own answer — and across town this was happening. Same day. The book gave me both halves of the same Thursday and I have to sit here holding them.

What saved it from feeling gross: the consent in this chapter is *airtight in a way that reads like the point*. "I said all right." "I heard you." He stops. She grants. She takes command back — puts his hands flat on the mattress, rides him at her own tempo — and he lets her, that's his version of devotion here too. And the "showing off" / "you noticed" exchange — that's two people who like each other, who are *playmates*, who've been doing this long enough to have shorthand. Even the mess and the cleanup is played for laughter, not degradation. This is what I left the genre to find and here it is, in the one pairing I've been dreading.

But the ache, the real ache: **the sex is a séance.** He asks nothing about Randi; everything said in that bed is about Vee — her neck kisses, her breast in Randi's hand, "she makes me happy" as his entire answer for why he likes hearing his lover want another woman. And Randi: "I want her to know exactly what she does to me." Both of them using each other as the nearest thing to touching her. That's not a con anymore; that's two people conducting current through each other because the third rail is asleep in a dorm in a flannel shirt with her mouth still empty. And then the knife: "You know she loves you, right?" "She's not the only one in love." *He couldn't tell her she was wrong.* The word that's died twice on Vee's doorstep — and Randi *gave him the confirmation of it in bed*, third-hand, which is the most this book could possibly dangle in front of me. And he thickened inside her at "she's not the only one in love" — and the book let Randi *see* his face soften. She knows about him too now. Everybody in that room knows except the girl it's all about, and that's the whole engine, and I'm furious and I'm hooked.

Where it leaves me uneasy, and I won't pretend otherwise: this is the con, consummated. Randi talked Vee back into his bed, coached the coat, took the debrief — and then went and got debriefed herself, in the flesh. Every "Hmm" at Tuesday's dinner now has a mouth behind it. When Vee says "you picked well" is a lie on a technicality, the technicality is that *they picked her*, and I'm holding that for her. But — and this is why I'm not stopping — the jacket promised me "the two who moved her have fallen every bit as far," and this chapter is that sentence made flesh, and falling is the only apology this genre has ever offered that I actually believe. The question the book just asked me out loud is whether Vee finding out will be a violation or a door, and the answer depends on what they do *next*, and I need the next chapter like I need to know how the word finally gets said.
