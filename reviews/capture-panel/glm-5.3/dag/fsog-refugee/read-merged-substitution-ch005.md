Okay, the waiting was worth it. Four chapters of watching these two circle her — the bench, the pointing game, Randi colliding with her on cue like it was choreography (it was) — and now the pickup itself, and it's *lovely*, and that's the book's whole dirty trick, isn't it. I knew going in it was staged. The blurb told me. And I still sat here with my hand on my chest watching him cross the floor.

The math lesson is going straight into my folder of favorite scenes in this genre. "Asked for it with his eyes first, it reached her before anything else." He asks for the *pencil*, he asks before he takes a *seat*, and the thing that undid me was him handing the pencil back at the exact line where she could solve it herself — she sees it, too, with her econ eye, catalogs the tutor's path versus the show-off's path. That's the thing I've been hunting since I quit the mafia books: a man whose power is all in what he declines to do to her. And then "May I call you?" — the *may*. The book heard me underline it and underlined it for me. I would have texted a friend at 1 a.m. just the word *may*.

Vee is winning me fast. The borrowed name-bit — trying on Randi's little swerve, landing it, then wanting her own name back — "Vee," quickly, suddenly wanting her own name back — that's such a precise piece of girlhood, the costume fitted and then discarded. And her body running a half-second ahead of her mind all chapter, the forearms, the watch-glance that makes her want to fall through her seat. She's not being acted on. She's *leaning*. I'm inside her wanting, which is where I need to be to forgive this book anything.

And I do need to forgive it something, because I'm reading every scene twice. Randi steering them to the place on Wilson — the same address she gave Brooke as her alibi, the lie now made true with Vee standing in it — that gave me an actual chill. Randi texting before the bell tinkled. The cup that goes up and comes down with no sip. And "He was cute. You should go out with him" — the manager stepping in to direct. Randi even hands Vee the *interpretation* — the forgotten-coffee read — teaching her how to want him correctly. There was a flicker there where I felt the hand on the scale and bristled. But here's what keeps me in my chair: everything staged about the setup, the *content* was real. He did look at her like the room was empty. He did answer Randi like you'd answer anybody — no performance for the co-conspirator, which is its own kind of honesty. Nothing was taken. She handed over her number, gladly, and I believe the gladness because I felt it from inside her.

Cassie's "he looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" made me laugh out loud, and then made me sad, because Cassie is the one person telling the truth in that coffee shop and Vee smoothed it over. *Goodbye, Vee.* Cassie knows. I trust Cassie with my life and I'm worried about what she's watching happen.

And the title — *Substitution*. Turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do. Euler's formula: two things that won't give on their own, put them at right angles, they become one plain thing. That's the whole book, that's the three of them, and it's also the ugly reading — Vee as the substitution in a game Randi and Pace already knew how to play. I caught it and I'm choosing to stay anyway. The blurb promised every yes was freely given. I'm holding it to that.
