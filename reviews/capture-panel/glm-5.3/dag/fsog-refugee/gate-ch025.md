REACTION — I had to put the book down after the middle of this one, not because it was bad, but because I needed a second to see what I'd just been handed.

So the chapter opens all warm and easy — him in the shop, her coming through the door on a Friday, the deadbolt, the shavings swept off his shoulders — and I'm settled in, thinking, fine, this is the Pace-and-Randi chapter again, I know this shape. And then she starts telling him. About Vee in her bra. About her hands up under the skirt, the thumb behind the knee, "she made a sound, Pace, and hoped I didn't hear it."

And here's the thing: on the page, from Randi's mouth, into Pace's body, it's *hot*. It's explicit and it's charged and the telling is doing real work between two people who actually know each other's rhythms — the stop-me-before-I-come hair pull, the "not yet, lie down," all of it consensual, all of it adult. That part of me was fully in the room.

But I'm also the reader who has been keeping notes. And Vee told Randi all of that — the bra, the shoes, the hand under the skirt — as *confession and repair*. She walked to that house at nine in the morning to fix the worst thing she'd ever done to a man she loves, and she stood in her bra because Randi made it dress-up, made it safe, made it two girls and a problem and a whole morning to solve it in. And it was that. It genuinely was that, for Vee. And it was *also* — I now know for certain — material. Randi sat on Pace's chest, still inside the afterglow of Vee's last visit, and converted all of Vee's undefended morning into this. "I haven't stopped thinking about her," she said, and I believe it, and that's exactly the problem: the con stopped being the con for her chapters ago and now it's just her life, and her life is feeding on a girl who doesn't know the table has three settings.

"I'm glad it was you," he said. "She'll come. Give her a day." They're *staging her*. Randi dressed her, schooled her, sent her home with marching orders pointed at his door — the black dress, the neckline, the scarf, the walk-in-sure. Tomorrow night Vee is going to walk into that house believing she chose every stitch, and she did choose, and it was all arranged. Both things at once, like the book keeps insisting. Simpson's paradox again, wearing heels.

And then Sheri. Okay. I did not have a cycling friend on my bingo card. Two years of Saturdays, a woman who remarks on women "the way other people remark on the weather," who runs the arithmetic out loud: "she knows. She likes her." "And the redhead?" "Not yet." I actually flinched at "not yet" — that's the cleanest statement of the whole architecture anybody has said in twenty-five chapters, and he said it *casually, on a bike, to a third party.* Vee now exists in the mouths of people she's never met. Sheri knows her backside, knows her name, knows the ending before Vee does. That should be horrifying and somehow the way it's written — Sheri laughing off down the road, "I bet it's worth it" — it just lands as *weather*. Which is the trick of this whole book, isn't it. It keeps making complicity feel like a nice morning for a ride.

The small thing that got me most, though, the thing I'd text someone about at 1 a.m.: her hand flat on the table after she folds her clothes, and "it was not the look you give a table." That's the chair by the door again. That's a piece of furniture with a person's shape in it. I don't know what that table is holding for her but I know it's not furniture.

Also — "the tit goddess was generous with that girl" made me laugh out loud, in the middle of everything, because Randi's mouth is a menace and I'm fond of her and I resent how fond I am.

Tomorrow night is the black dress and the door. The book has been rehearsing this walk for chapters — the mirror with the bright screw heads, the slit to the hip, the daydream of coming in late and the room turning. And now I know he's waiting on the other side of it knowing she's coming, or believing she is, and I know Randi dressed her for it, and none of that changes the fact that Vee is going to walk in having chosen to. I keep waiting for the part of this I can't hold both of. It hasn't come yet.
