Oh, this one got me. It's the same forty minutes from both sides — Randi in Gstaad lying in borrowed warmth, Vee in Ohio lying in lavender — and neither text was a lie exactly, but neither was the thing, and they sent them across the dark to each other like two women passing notes through a wall. "Every word of it true. None of it the thing." And then Vee, on the other end, *believes* the fairytale — "a devastatingly handsome billionaire" she invented, when the actual man is asleep having given Randi a good sandwich and nothing to keep. I had to put the book down for a second. They're both envying each other's lives and neither of them wants the one she has. That's so cruel and so true.

The room that doesn't know her. The ribbons, the little gold swimmers, the clarinet — "the room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." That's the saddest single image in a book that's been running a competition for it, and it landed on me hard because it's not about Pace at all. It's about a girl who outgrew a whole house and can't say so to anyone in it. Her mother asking about the young man at a fresh angle every day, and Vee smiling in front of a shut door. The cropped photo — "she was the one who had divided it" — same picture, one house, two selves, and this chapter is the first time I really felt the *cost* of that split rather than the cleverness of it.

And then the spiral, the one I know too well from my own bad midnights: she takes her best memory, the first night, the sheet slipping while he cooked, the warmest thing she owns — and can't keep her own night. The blonde moves in and takes her seat at the table. "She could not keep her own night." I've done that. Every woman who's ever lain awake not knowing where she stands has done exactly that, taken her best memory and let some imagined replacement into it, and the book rendering it from inside her instead of mocking her for it is why I trust this book with her. It flags her dishonesty-with-herself, but it never makes her small.

What I noticed underneath, and I can't stop chewing it: Randi's side of this chapter is essentially the con's afterlife. She decided, explicitly, lying next to John — keep the brunches, let Vee "arrive at it on her own," keep the word under cover. "She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be. Vee was her gorgeous." That's a woman choosing the arrangement over the truth, and the two texts crossing midair — one built to demand nothing, one built to reveal nothing — are the arrangement working exactly as designed. Two people who love each other, texting past each other on purpose. And meanwhile the actual bomb ticked forward: "he had never asked her where the night was going... and she found, tonight, that she liked not being asked." Randi *liking* an unasked-for assumption, after eleven chapters where the asking was the whole point — that scared me a little. Not because I think less of her; it's honest, she's exhausted from Pace's kind of work. But John is the book showing me what the safe life costs, and Pace is what it pays, and Randi knows both and chose the seam of borrowed warmth. That's her door, raised.

And Vee's floor is gone — "they had only ever held her up while she was the one... and now she did not know she was anything." Her self-worth was load-bearing on his attention and the fight knocked the wall out. That's the real cliffhanger for me, more than anything: not *will he come back*, but *who is she standing on when he does*. She ended the chapter holding both of them and setting neither down, and so did I.

No heat, no contact — the two of them are further apart than they've been since October, and I feel the gap the way I'm apparently built to feel it. But the chapter knew it, and made the gap itself the material. I'd text my friend: "the midnight chapter broke me, the blonde in the fra diavolo memory, I'm not okay." One quiet chapter like this costs me nothing when it's this full. Two would start to cost. Three — I'd want somebody in a room together by the end of the next stretch, and I want it to be them.
