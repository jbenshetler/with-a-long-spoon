REACTION: Oh, this book. It put them both in cold rooms on the same midnight and didn't tell either one. Randi lying next to a faultless man feeling nothing, and Vee lying under ribbons from a girl who doesn't exist anymore, and they texted each other exactly the lie the other needed to keep — Randi giving the fairytale, Vee demanding nothing — and neither one knows the other is starving too. That's the cruelest, most gorgeous thing this book has done to me, because I *like* both of them and the friendship is real on both sides and it's built entirely on what they can't say.

The Randi chapter wrecked me more than I expected. "He had never once assumed anything in his life. Pace made her say it, out loud, every time. She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked" — I actually put the book down. Because the book isn't winking at it. It names the cost one page later: nothing left behind it to keep, warm only along the seam, the cold is *hers*, she carried it in. "A good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." That's the whole thesis of why I read this genre and not the other stuff — pleasure with nobody found underneath is a sandwich, and this book knows it. And then "she was not brave enough to lose her. She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be." Randi deciding to let Vee arrive at it on her own, to keep the word under its cover — that's the con's next move and it's made of loneliness, not malice. "Vee was her gorgeous." Ugh.

And Vee's chapter — the divided photograph is the image of the whole book. One house, same picture, she's the one who scissored the leg away, cut off the exact thing that made her feel like herself. Then her own best night getting stolen from her in real time — the blonde sitting where she sat in the wine light — I've done that at midnight, and it was almost too honest to read. "There was no message she could send that came back in the shape of what she needed" — and the line about him leaving her the room, appreciated once and hated now. The book is making me pay for a virtue I was rooting for. That's craft that earns it.

Two chapters and the leads never share a room, and I barely noticed, because everything in both chapters *points at him*. "She'd have Randi back the day the term started... and Pace came with none of that." That's the question the whole volume turns on now. He has to come. Or she does. Someone has to spend the word.
