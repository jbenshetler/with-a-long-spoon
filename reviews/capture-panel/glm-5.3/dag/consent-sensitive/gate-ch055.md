First read: I loved it. Kayla at the locked dining hall doors, "the last morning of my life," the mimosa in a flute repairing her underage feelings completely — this book does comedy better than it gets credit for, and the brunch beat is the whole Randi thesis transplanted into safe soil: good food, chosen company, money spent without arithmetic. And when Vee said "I flashed him on his front porch" I actually cackled. Kayla shrieking over the socks she now realizes she narrated to a woman committing a crime — that's the Kayla payoff the doorway scene promised, and the book cashed it exactly right. Vee never has to say one word about the drive, and the prose winked at me about it: "never had to say one word about the drive." Good. The drive is hers. Some things she doesn't owe the brunch table, and the fact that the book drew that line felt like it protecting her, finally, from the thing I've been dreading — which is that Vee tells Randi everything.

Second read: "We talked all night. It's — we're good." That is the size of it she brought, and the sentence knows it. This is a Vee who has learned, somewhere in the word-war, to hold a private border. She stood in a doorway and answered a question with no words at all — her one real act of authorship in fifty chapters — and here she narrates it to her friends as a story with her name on it, hers to edit. The Vee of the induction would have poured the whole night out. This one kept the drive, kept the coat's dark bloom, kept the shower and the pancakes and the tears. The book is letting her have an inside. That's new, and I'm suspicious of how much I'm relieved by it.

The Meg beat about the maple bacon dozen landed harder than it should have — "you have to make it right in their language." Because that IS what Vee did, and the book let her friend say it innocent, say it as a compliment, with no idea the language Vee chose was her own body because the words failed. And Kayla's "Danny would have rather you borrowed Vee's high heels" — the book is now having her friends circle the thesis on their own, unknowing. This dinner on Wednesday is the collision the whole book has been walking toward: Randi and Pace at one table, the two architects and the girl, plus Cassie one inference away and Kayla who can talk paint off walls, and Pace — who knows about Randi's appetite, who is holding it, who "carried something in from the truck that could keep" — saying *I want to meet your people*. The one thing keeping me from floating: everything at that brunch was true and nothing at that table was the thing. "Better than we were" is the plural sentence again, the sentence with no person in it — Vee quoted it to her friends as a victory and it's the same shape as "I love how good we are together." She heard it as reconciliation. I heard it as the word still not said, the whole war still unfought, and now Wednesday's dinner is going to happen on Randi's terrain with Randi as guest of honor and nobody but Pace and the reader knowing what Randi is. This book keeps handing me warm chapters that are secretly loaded, and I keep falling for it exactly as the blurb promised I would.
