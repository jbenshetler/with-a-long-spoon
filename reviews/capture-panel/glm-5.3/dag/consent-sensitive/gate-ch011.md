Okay. This chapter got me. It got me the way the froyo chapter got me, except this one went further, and I need to be honest about the two things living side by side in my chest right now.

First: the jacket said this book was going to make me fall for it exactly as Vee does, and this chapter is the book cashing that promise in full. The hike. The moleskin. The sandwich with the horseradish that ambushes her, the whole "the hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." — I read that line and actually put the book down, because that's the entire seduction in one sentence. Every gift in this book is engineered hunger-management: the thin apple slices, and now he *manufactures the hunger* so that being handed food feels like rescue. Pace doesn't just answer appetites in this book, he schedules them. And the prose knows it — "that was what it was for" is not a line a naive author writes. It's a wink. It's the book sitting beside me on the couch saying *watch him work.* I'm still in, because of lines like that.

The cherry pits destroyed me a little. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." That's Vee's whole developmental arc in miniature — the education that her mother never gave her, delivered without shame — and delivered by the one man on earth I don't fully trust, which is exactly the book's cruelty and exactly its tenderness.

And the dress. The russet dress on the rock that she "set down" and "he didn't pick it up again." I was waiting for him to buy it. I was braced for it — for the gift that would be too big and would quietly indebt her. And he said "That sounds like a beautiful dress," and took a cherry, and moved on. Either that's real restraint or it's the long game — he's teaching her that his generosity has no visible quid pro quo, which makes the *next* gift land harder. I genuinely can't tell which, and that's the best thing about this book. I'm being managed alongside Vee and I feel it.

Now the truck. The truck, the truck. Him stepping out into the rain so she could change — the book sold it as chivalry and her reading of it as *both his shirt and the truck, both for her* — and my handler-alarm went ping, because a man who thinks that far ahead about how a gesture will land is a man who knows exactly what picture he's painting. But then the chapter turned around and bit HIM. She initiates the whole strip-tease — knuckles on the glass, crooked finger, buttons, all of it, the voice roaring *floozy* and her thighs pressing — and this is the freest and hottest she's ever been, she's RUNNING this — and then his hand is shaking because he's been soaked and freezing the whole time and never once mentioned it. She catches it. She stops mid-reach, mid-seduction, and goes "Pace. You're freezing," and holds his hand against her belly to warm it.

I gasped. That reversal did something to me I didn't expect: it gave HER the power to see him, for the first time. He's been the seer this whole book — the man whose gaze turns her shame to heat — and in that moment the sight ran backwards. She saw him cold and took care of him. That's the first brick of something real that wasn't in the plan, I think, and if the blurb means it — "how real it would get, as true for the two who started it" — this is the book starting to build the thing that will hurt everyone.

Also, lord, the cop. The siren eating her sentence. That's the outside world barging into the sealed warm cab, the first intrusion of anything institutional since Brooke, and it came exactly when she was about to say the big thing. It felt like the book refusing her the moment on purpose. I'm suspicious of that in a good way — Chekhov's sirens.

The mother's voice being *the heat* now — "the voice was the heat and the heat was the voice" — that's the kink thesis of this book stated plain, and I notice the book keeps letting her voice make the choices *inside* consensual frames she builds herself. She crooked the finger. He came when called. He sat still with his hands down and let her show him. Even at my most paranoid I can't find the violation in that cab — he stepped OUT of it, into the rain, cold, and paid for it in shivers she had to discover by accident. The power ran both directions in this chapter more than any chapter so far, and I want to believe that's the arc. I'm watching Randi not being in this chapter at all — first full absence? — and I felt her not being there like a held breath. The handler let the mark off-leash for a day. I don't think that's nothing.
