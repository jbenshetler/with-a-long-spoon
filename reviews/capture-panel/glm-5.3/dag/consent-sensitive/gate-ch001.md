The smile sitting on top of the face that begged. The bottle cap under her thumbnail. The scene itself: hot, genuinely. The slow hand, the edging, the "Good girl," the crying she doesn't know she's doing — all of it landed for me. And the moment that got me most wasn't the orgasm, it was "There you are, Randi." Him naming her, after all that. And then her deciding, silently, to vanish again. That's the chapter's real climax and it's devastation, not sex.

Two things I'm watching warily. One: he told her "not only a table" only when she was already standing naked in front of it. He engineered the reveal's timing for maximum destabilization, and the book hasn't yet marked that as a choice with a cost — Randi hasn't had a thought about it, and I want the book to notice eventually that Pace's courtly asking-before-taking has a hole in it shaped exactly like *surprises*. The bench "doing what it would continue to do, waiting for the next time" — that closing image is the book keeping the door open, and I don't trust him yet the way the book doesn't quite trust him yet. If Rivers loses that edge and lets Pace be simply wise, I'm out. But right now every character in this book is hiding something from every other character, including from themselves, and the prose is holding all of it.

Also, small delighted note: Vee is going to walk into a pre-existing engine, and I already know more than she does. That's the exact trap the jacket promised me — fall for it exactly as she does. I'm in it.
