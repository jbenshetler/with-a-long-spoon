REACTION

Okay, so. On the surface this is exactly the kind of chapter I'd usually wolf down without chewing — a full morning of the good stuff, gorgeously written, and I did feel all of it. The naked-in-the-house opening delighted me. The "pleasure princess" reversal made me actually laugh out loud on the couch — "some slave," the finger-counting of everything he does for her — that's Vee's interiority doing real work, and it's the healthiest, funniest self-talk she's had all book. The toast-burning bit is perfect farce. Him answering "I can put the sweats on if you need to concentrate" with "No" before the sentence finished — chef's kiss, genuinely. The blood orange section might be the best food writing in the book so far, and her newly educated tongue reading the breakfast in layers is the whole Vee arc in one plate.

But here's what I can't stop thinking about, and it's the thing the chapter clearly WANTS me to not stop thinking about: the kiss.

Because she did the math at the end and got the wrong answer. "He must have forgotten to wipe." And I'm sitting there going, honey — no. You counted yourself, it's been *hundreds* of times, every single time, and it never once tasted like that. Either he's been wiping every time for months (which is its own devotion, and its own concealment), or — and this is the one that made my stomach drop — he's done it before and you never noticed until now. Either way, her "tidy and kind" explanation is a cope, the book knows it's a cope, and the minor-key question "settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, waiting" is doing exactly what it says. That little light staying on is the most charged thing in the chapter, more charged than any of the three orgasms, and I mean that as praise — because the heat here isn't just the heat, it's Vee discovering a whole room in her own architecture she didn't know existed. The installed voice from childhood shutting things down "over the tongue's head," and then her tongue sneaking out later, hunting, on its own — she didn't send it; it went. That's her whole shame-to-appetite journey compressed into one lip-lick, and it's the first time I've seen her want something and *refuse to notice she wants it* since Randi entered the story.

And oh — the Randi symmetry. She's lying there rehearsing how to tell Randi "the strange small thing" in a way that doesn't sound like what it sounds like. Randi, who she's already editing herself for. Randi, who loves her and won't say. The book is building these two women toward the same forbidden noticing and I can see the blueprint now and it's going to wreck me.

Also, small accounting note: "a pleasure slave... a gladiator, once. Her beauty had turned him" — the induction night mythology still living in her head. She has no idea there was a game. The dramatic irony is compounding like interest.

If I have a complaint, it's that the doorway chapter already gave me the reunion catharsis and this one is the long luxurious exhale — one quiet-in-quotes chapter where nothing moves the plot an inch except that final seed. But the seed is big enough, and the prose good enough, that I'm not calling it a stall. I'm calling it a fuse.
