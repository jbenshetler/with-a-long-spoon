REACTION

The blurb said "courtly and exact, he asks before he takes" and I thought I knew what that meant. I didn't. "May I choose?" at a dinner table with a menu she can't read — that's the most erotic three words this book has done since the bench, and nothing happened but ordering food.

I need to start with the hunger, because the hunger is the chapter. "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" — that line went through me. The book has been building Vee's appetite since the froyo cup that got away from her, and now it says it plain: the hollow she's filling isn't a stomach. And what wrecked me was "she wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." That's the whole Vee wound from chapter 7 — the body that's a problem, the rationed portions, the mother's voice — and for one dinner it's just gone. She got to eat without supervision. He built her a meal she couldn't read and let her want things she had no names for, and I sat there knowing exactly what I was watching: the bench technique in its fully clothed form. She's falling for the feeling of unrationed appetite, and the man serving it to her is doing it on purpose. Or — and this is what keeps the book honest — maybe he isn't, entirely. That's the trick of him: the kindness is real AND the script is real, and I can't ever fully split them, and neither can she.

The kiss at the end. I want to text someone about the kiss. The reflex fires — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he doesn't argue, doesn't charm, just smiles like she's told him something fine and starts walking. AND SHE STAYS. Her feet stay. Her hand pulls him taut. That's chapter one inverted: on the bench, he withheld until her please was real; here she withholds the yes from HERSELF until her own please is real. She does the Pace method to herself, out in the moonlight, and he just holds the space. When she closes the distance and says "I'm making an exception" — that's Vee's version of the real *please*. He gave up the kiss and she had to come take it. That's the most consensual kiss I've ever read in this genre and it's also, technically, seduction technique, and I'm furious and delighted at the same time.

Small things I loved: Christine knowing him (he has a LIFE, places where he's loved — "I've been coming back to get even. I'm losing"), the nine words of Arabic, seven are food, the right-hand pattern filed as "one more thing he knew that she didn't" (which is the trap in miniature — she's collecting his unknowables as romance), Cassie's "That's not a boy. That's a man," which is the honest voice approving of the thing I'm terrified of. And the borrowed shoes. She borrowed shoes off a girl down the hall. She borrowed a version of herself, the way she borrowed Randi's name-ritual — the copying keeps flowing one direction.

The dread is still there — I know about Randi and the plan and the bench waiting at the end of this — but this chapter did something to me I wasn't braced for: for two hours I forgot I knew. I got seduced. The book made me complicit.
