REACTION — I put the book down after this one and just sat there a second, because that last exchange did something to me. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" and "Both" — that's the whole book in four lines, and Helen Rivers knows it, and she let Cassie be the one to ask. Of course she did. Cassie has been carrying the outside case file since "you don't come home school nights," and now she's asking the question Vee can't even ask herself in the pool at six a.m.

The parents' dinner though. I've read a hundred "meet the parents" phone calls in this genre and they're always farce or they're nothing. This one was arithmetic. Dad pricing the dress through the photograph — a father doing threat assessment on a printed-out picture, getting the age wrong, picturing grey hair, and being *more right than he knows* in his suspicion of too-good. And Vee curating him down to the version that fits: PhD, hiking, boom box. She's doing to her father exactly what the arrangement did to her — handing him a cropped version and keeping the full frame for the people she's actually in it with. She even says it straight: the family's copy "stops at the waist." The girl who sent Pace the uncropped photo of herself sent her mother the cropped one. Same girl, two doors, and she knows exactly which frame goes through which door. That's not shame, that's navigation, and it's the most grown-up thing she's done in the book — and it's also the saddest, because "boyfriend?" with the question mark out loud is the maybe from the pool wearing a dinner dress.

And the sewing. THE SEWING. He made the dress. On a sun porch, on a machine, because she mentioned a magazine once. Forty-five chapters of drawers and scrunchies and folded self, and this lands as the biggest of all of them — and Vee's read on her father is dead-on, that "weird is worse than wrong" because wrong fits in a box and weird doesn't fit anywhere. That's also, I notice, a perfect description of Pace himself in the world: a man the categories don't hold. And my paranoid ledger twitches again: that dress had no room for underwear by design, "the dress as built required it." He built it. I'm not sure what I'm holding yet but I put it on the pile next to Berkeley.

What keeps this from being a filler chapter: it's the first time Vee says the shape out loud to anyone who isn't inside the operation or asleep. Cassie doesn't push, and Vee is "a little, not" grateful for that — she wants to be asked and can't bear to be. That flinch is real, and the book keeps earning it.

Also, small awful detail I loved: Mom taking the picture out to show people who'd already seen it. That's a mother's whole heart in one line.
