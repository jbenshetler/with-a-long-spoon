REACTION — Okay, I need a minute. This chapter did something to me that the last five couldn't, and I think it's because everyone finally stopped performing for exactly one scene.

The Vee-and-Randi bed scene is the best thing this book has done since the drawer. Randi sitting there in a face she hadn't had time to put on — I felt that detail in my teeth, because I've spent forty chapters watching her armor and this is the first time the plot itself yanked it off. And her counsel is *good*. That's what kills me. "You backed him into a corner in his own house — the one thing he can't do." She's right! She's reading Pace with the accuracy of someone who's been on the receiving end of that exact wall, because she has, in bed with him, and Vee doesn't know why the read is that precise. The dramatic irony in this chapter is almost unbearable: the reader knows Randi is half-conducting and half-collapsing, and there is no line where one stops and the other starts anymore. "That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — she could be talking about the morning she left with the green satin in her fist. She IS talking about that. She just can't say so.

The shoes. Oh, the shoes. The almost-sentence in Randi's head — "something with *means* in it" — that she leaves unassembled. God. She's not brave enough to lose her, and now Brooke has walked in and priced the whole thing.

Brooke terrified me. "You've got a position in this house, and you've got her. You need to choose."
