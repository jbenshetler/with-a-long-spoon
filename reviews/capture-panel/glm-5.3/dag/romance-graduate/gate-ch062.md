REACTION — Oh, this chapter *got* me. I read the taproom chapter with a grin on my face and then this one snuck up behind it and hit me in the back of the head with a two-by-four. "Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else." I actually put the Kindle down for a second. One friend isn't a number a person has — and the book knows that, because Vee thinks almost those exact words, and that's when the whole architecture of Pace finally came into view for me. College at fifteen. Off the end of every scale. The window opens once and his never opened. And the detail that wrecked me wasn't even the big one — it was "what are you doing for Christmas, your folks coming up." Every ordinary question with a gap under it. That's the loneliest sentence this book has written and it's sitting in a hangover breakfast chapter between cinnamon raisin bread and soft eggs.

And the Daphne thing — the book finally let me look at it almost head-on. "The ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue and that she kept pressing back down." So Vee and I are holding the same word now and neither of us is allowed to say it. Two truths stacked and unreconciling — best two years of his life AND a wrong shape — and the fact that Vee can't smash them into one clean thing is exactly why I trust this book. A lesser romance would have done the reveal by now, tidy and traumatic. This one keeps the drawer open an inch and lets me hurt in the gap.

The hug at the end — she copies Sheri's hug, cheek flat to his chest, whole weight committed, and *doesn't* apologize because sorry would've asked him to stand still and be comforted. That's the emotional intelligence I'm starving for right there. She understood what the moment needed and it wasn't words. And him — chin on her head, "somewhere else with it," not trying to make either of them feel better. He doesn't perform. He never performs, and I believe his not-performing more than most heroes' grand speeches.

Small delights: the water glass on a folded paper towel so it wouldn't mark the wood. That's the whole man in one image. The echo of "I like her" said drunk and then sober. Sheri's "she can be like that," flat as milk, catching exactly what Vee meant about that look at the shoes — he never pretends not to have caught it.

Is this a quiet chapter? Yes. Second in a row after the taproom? Sort of — the taproom had the blush and Sheri's coming-out story, this one had no heat at all and I don't care. This is the good kind of quiet, the kind that's moving the *inside* of the relationship an entire mile. Though I'll admit my eye is starting to tick toward Saturday's dance and the armed triangle — two people in love with Vee are sharing a bed and I've now had two chapters of Sheri-and-Pace sweetness without any Randi. The bomb's still ticking under all this tenderness and I want to feel the fuse again soon.
