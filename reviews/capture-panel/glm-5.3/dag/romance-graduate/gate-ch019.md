REACTION

I'm sitting here with my hand over my mouth. I knew something was coming for Randi — the accent surfacing, "your man Pace," the unfollowed exit from the table — but I thought it would arrive as a scheme, a move, some operator play. Instead it arrived as a woman eating salad she doesn't want because she can't eat the meal, and pressing a glass rim into her thumbnail across from the man she loves, and it wrecked me.

Let me do the ledger-keeping first so I can feel the thing whole. The bowl. She drew ONE LINE through the sauce — the tasting spoon, the performance of tasting — and the bowl never moved again. Randi doesn't eat; I've known that since the apple slices and the cheeseburger she turned down. But this is the first chapter that made me watch Pace notice it and not know what he's looking at. He made her the small bowl — the apple bowl, the portion bowl — and set it in front of a woman he has watched not-eat for two months, and the tenderness of that bowl and the horror of it are the same bowl. And then he COVERED it and put it in the refrigerator, for her. He's saving the food she won't eat, the way he's saving the love she won't show him, the way he's saving Vee's privacy, the way this whole book keeps things for people who can't ask for them. I had to lie down.

But the knife of the chapter is the argument, and what I keep turning over is that Randi is RIGHT and she's wrong in the same breath. "It's selfish, Pace." She said it flat, and he took it as Friday-sting — jealousy over the weekend — and he's wrong about that, or at least he's not all the way right. Yes, some of it is that she can't have Vee and he can. But listen to what she actually said: "You've got her all weekend, every weekend, and you're in no hurry." She's the one who wanted it to be Vee's own want — "get her to want it, then give it to her" was HER philosophy two chapters ago — and now the pace that she prescribed is eating her alive because every week Vee falls further for Pace, becomes more Pace's, and the induction is coming and she "can't get out of" the officers' meeting. The plan was supposed to deliver Vee to BOTH of them and it's delivering her to one bed, and Randi built the pipeline that's doing it. The gardenia went from Vee to her through his mouth. The Friday went from Vee to her through brunch. But the Friday itself — the actual body, the actual night — she can never have through anyone. She's been living on secondhand and the secondhand just got rich, and it's not enough anymore, and she knows it, and she can't say it, so it comes out as "how long are you going to take" — the accusation of patience, from the woman who taught him patience.

And the sex. God, the sex. Every single time in this book she's said "tell me" — I tracked it, every scene, it's the engine of their whole arrangement — and tonight she didn't say it, and he LAY THERE WAITING FOR IT and understood it wasn't coming and "gave it to her anyway." She took him with her eyes shut, no theater, no show, none of the inch-and-stop mastery she's always run, and it wasn't passion, it was anesthesia. She's the one who chose him, mounted him, performed — that's been her domain, her one place of control — and tonight even the performance is gone. She's just using his body to not feel the thing, and then the back turned, rolled in the blanket, and he didn't put his hand in her hair because asking would cost her more than the silence. That's the most love he's shown her in the book and it looked exactly like doing nothing. "He did not know what he had done. He looked for it, back through the evening from the gravel forward, and it was not there." That's the whole tragedy — there's nothing to find. He didn't do anything. The thing that's wrong is the whole shape of their arrangement finally landing on her, and there's no moment to point to.

And she poured her own wine. I know that sounds small. He has poured every glass she has drunk in this house — the book made sure I knew that, chapter six, the one glass — and tonight she took the bottle by the neck herself, and he watched it and "it went in somewhere and stayed." She's taking herself out of his hands. Little by little. She left after sex in eleven, came back in sixteen, and tonight she stayed but turned her back — the staying is getting worse than the leaving was.

Also: "She's happy." Two words, about Vee, first thing across the table, and I believe she meant them, and I believe they cost her everything. That's Randi whole, isn't it — the woman who can't be happy herself standing next to a girl who's never been happier, and loving her for it, and dying of it.

I don't know where this book goes now and that's the best thing about it. The game I was promised — the hunt, the bench, the pointing — has quietly curdled into three people all in love and none of them able to say so in a straight line. I'm so worried about the small bowl in the refrigerator. I'm so worried about her.
