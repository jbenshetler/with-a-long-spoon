REACTION

The flannel. I need to start with the flannel because I'm going to be thinking about it for days. His only dry shirt, folded under his gym clothes, held out without a word — and then he gets OUT of the truck so she can change without being watched. And the book let me sit in that with her for the exact right number of beats before I understood, along with her, why he was standing in the rain. He gave her the shirt AND the privacy and charged himself the rain for both. That's the "may I choose" man again, and I keep bracing for the technique to curdle into manipulation and it keeps NOT, quite, and that ambiguity is doing more to me than any straight love scene would.

And then she knocks on the glass. Oh, she knocks on the glass. This is the chapter where the book's whole engine turned over for me: the mother's voice came up — *floozie, girls in trucks, three dates* — and for the first time it wasn't a brake, it was FUEL. "Her body answered each one with a low pull of heat under the cold... wanted to be the very thing the voice said." I actually put the book down for a second. The voice that's been rationing her since chapter four just became the thing she gets off against. She's not being unguarded by Pace this time — she's transgressing on her OWN initiative, with her own hands on her own buttons, eyes held on his the whole way. The bench made Randi's real please come out; the truck cab made Vee's real *look at me* come out. And she did the fourth button by choice — she looked down and LET his eyes go with hers. She's authoring the show. I don't know if the author knows what a big deal that is, but I do, and I felt it in my chest.

But the beat that undid me: she reaches for his hand to kiss his knuckles, to start the next thing, and the hand is *shaking*. He's been shivering this whole time — soaked, jaw set, driving her home — while she was inside the theater of her own yes, and neither of them knew. She brings his hand against her belly under the flannel and says "you're freezing," small, and asks him to take her home warm. He'd been "a long way from the question of whether he was cold." That line. He was so far inside looking at her that his own body stopped reporting. For one chapter the smugness I've been carrying a grudge about since "I didn't correct you" was just... gone. He was *undone*. I've read a hundred heroes undone, but never because looking was the whole event.

The cop siren eating her sentence — cheap trick, honestly, the interrupted declaration right before the drop-off, and I'd roll my eyes in any other book. But the book earned it because she didn't get to say the small thing in place of the big thing, and the big thing just has to live in her unspoken, wearing his flannel through the rain. Fine. Take it.

And Cassie. The ending made me tearier than any of it. "Vee," just her name, "like setting something down where it wouldn't break." Cassie is not being edged out this chapter — she got the whole story, held it, and said keep the shirt. My fear about the Cassie split eased for one chapter, and I'm grateful and still worried, because the smoothie sorting happened in her head where nobody can undo it.

The dress on the rock. He didn't pick it up. He said "that sounds like a beautiful dress" and let the russet stay where she set it down. I don't trust that for one second — either he's going to buy it (which is a whole power move dressed as kindness) or he's laying it down like a track for later. Either way it's loaded and the book knows it.

No sex, and the hottest chapter since the bench. The shirt coming open in a cold truck cab beat half the explicit scenes I've read this year.
