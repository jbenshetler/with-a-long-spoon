REACTION — This chapter put a cold hand on the back of my neck, and I mean that as the highest compliment.

Nothing "happens." A girl tells her roommate about a kiss, then tells her new friend about the same kiss, and gets smoothies. And I sat there the whole time with my jaw tight because *I know what Vee doesn't.* Every single thing she hands over — "I stopped watching myself," "I just handed it over," "because of him" — she's saying directly into the ear of the woman who built the trap, and the woman is *feasting.* Did you catch it? "Ask and then ravish," a beat late, at Vee's own volume, low. Randi went home after that smoothie and did something with it, I'd bet the book. And the "little minx" swat — the delight has a second layer under it and the prose never once nudges you. It just lets you sit in the dramatic irony and sweat. That's trust in your reader and I could weep.

Vee telling both stories back-to-back was the structural trick of the chapter and it worked on me differently each time. With Cassie it was pure — the spin, the bean bag flop, "Look at me," Cassie flatly asking how much he'd given her to drink and *believing* the answer. I love Cassie with my whole chest. Which made the little wrinkle hurt: "it isn't really her place." Vee deciding the smoothie shop is "not Cassie's world" and letting her go up the aisle alone — that's the first drip of the sorority logic getting its hooks in, and the book didn't editorialize about it, just let it turn over under the comfortable thought and let me find it. I noticed. I'm keeping the receipt.

The "dear friend" hand-hold in front of Brooke — that's the beat that got me. Randi *spending* something Vee can't price, publicly, and Vee feeling the cost without being able to name it. And Randi's line about the boring rich date who "is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else" — I actually laughed, because that's Randi talking about Pace without saying Pace, a whole review of her own lover delivered as gossip. These two threads braided tight in a chapter where nobody took a stitch of clothing off and I was *leaning*.

The one flicker of impatience: it's the second Vee-interiority chapter in a row without Pace in the room, and Vee's "fine vs. not fine" register — cardigan, wrong size for the room — is territory the pool chapter already covered beautifully. A hair of repetition in the not-belonging beats. But the Brooke scene earned its keep and then some, so I'm not dinging it hard.

Sunday hike. Real shoes. I'm not waiting, the book is still in my hand.
