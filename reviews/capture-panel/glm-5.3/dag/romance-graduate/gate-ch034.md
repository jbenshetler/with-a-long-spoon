REACTION — God. I've been waiting for this chapter for thirty chapters and it still got me, and it got me because it wasn't the chapter I was promised. The daydream was the room turning — she'd built it herself, walking in late, everyone looking. And the book just... quietly took the fantasy away and gave her something better: a dull, kind, beige afternoon ceremony, no dramatic entrance available, nothing for it but a name card taped to a folding chair. And then she did it ANYWAY. That second step. "She took a breath, and made the second step long." I actually put the book down for a second. That's the whole book in one sentence — the dare was never going to arrive, so she manufactured it, on her own nerve, in front of somebody's parents, wearing nothing under the burgundy because the dress as-built *required* it and she wanted to wear the dress.

And the nothing-under-it is doing so much quiet work. She found it out in his mirror, she was careful the whole way there, hand at the hip holding the slit shut — she kept the secret through all the boring preamble — and then chose, mid-walk, on the stage, to spend it. The heat coming up "to meet a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it" — that's the exact machinery of the kitchen, the mirror, the please-twice, now happening in a *hall full of parents* and nobody can name it. "The room knew, and not a soul in it could have said so out loud." She's learned what she is from a man behind a door and a woman across a table and she just wore it into a departmental ceremony like armor.

The photo gutted me. Cassie catching the exact second — "her chin level and her face giving away absolutely nothing" — and then Vee sending it to Pace UNCROPPED, and "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." She's finally got something in this arrangement that's genuinely only hers. Not reported to Randi, not engineered by anybody, not on anyone's blueprint — the thing under the picture belongs to nobody but her. After chapters of watching everyone run version control on this girl, that's the first frame she owns outright. I nearly cheered.

And the Pace absence was the right call and it hurt the way it should: the man who built the dress misses the wearing it was built for, and she "hadn't told him it was only a dress" because they both know it wasn't. Also — paranoid note — he's in Berkeley the *same three days*. The one event she's been daydreaming toward for half the book and he's out of state. I don't think the blonde was a weapon but I do think the universe of this book removes him at charged moments, and I noticed it, and I'm filing it next to my Daphne rhyme whether it belongs there or not.

The ending — "whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing" — is the book finally saying its thesis out loud a little, and normally that's my quit trigger (stop explaining your own subtext), but it earned it here because it's a *question she can't answer about herself*, not the author tapping my shoulder. Barely earned. Don't do it twice, Helen.
