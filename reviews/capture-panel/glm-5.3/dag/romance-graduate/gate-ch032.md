REACTION — The drawer. I have to start with the drawer because I finished it with my hand over my mouth. He didn't say "it doesn't matter" — he folded his entire careful self down into one cramped drawer and gave her the top one, the one your hand goes to first, with *room to spare, room for more of her, laid out like something prepared and waiting.* That's the whole man in one piece of furniture. That's the anti-Daphne move: the boy who learned sex as an IOU is quietly building infrastructure for a person to stay. And no note — "There was never a note" — god, this book knows that the unsaid is only unbearable when it's not being said in drywall and silverware.

But the dinner scene is the one that had my heart going, because I'm the reader and I know what Vee doesn't, and the book used me *ruthlessly*. She's sitting there in his shirt telling him about her new best friend Randi — the funny accent, the boys' stories, "she's so pretty and still can't find what she wants" — and he's standing at the stove with his back to her, and I'm watching him listen to his lover described to him as a stranger by the woman they're both lying to. "On the mouth. And then she comes back and does it again." And he mimes the cheek kisses, playing along — that's him *managing* it, that's the performance, and then the look under the grin that isn't a joke, and "I'll pretend it was that kind of French," which she took as flirtation and I took as him covering a reaction. The whole scene was doing two things at once and Vee only got one of them and I got both and it was almost unbearable in the good way. This is the dramatic irony the jacket promised, actually *delivered*, finally, at the dinner table with salmon.

And the way she said "Peter" to his sleeping ear — she's keeping the name like a secret again, only now with the love confession attached. "She could live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying" — which is lovely and also, knowing this book, a cliff she's standing on. Twice "never been so fortunate, or so happy," and now this. The fall is scheduled and I can feel it from here.

Worry list update: she's losing weight, bras going looser, and the book slipped it in between the good meals like it was nothing. Field-hand appetite at his table versus nothing at brunch — Randi ate the plate at his house; Vee's eating at his table and not elsewhere. I noticed it. I don't think I was supposed to miss it.
