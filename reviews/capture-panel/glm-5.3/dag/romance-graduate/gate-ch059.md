REACTION — Well, there it is. The book took the taproom chapter's warmth and cashed it straight into grief, and I didn't see the switch coming until I was already inside it.

"I'd love to meet your other friends." "Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else." I had to put the Kindle down for a second at that line. Not because it was sad — because it was delivered the way this man delivers everything, flat, pre-decided, *it isn't tragic*, which is exactly how you know it is. The whole monologue that follows is the best thing this book has done: the pack, the losing-to-him, the door on a timer. "You make a friend from the middle of the pack — you lose to him, and lose again, and come out the other side friends. I was never once in the pack." That's not romance-writing, that's just true, and I've never seen it said in a book in this genre, and it explains him more than the Daphne stuff ever did.

The callback is doing so much quiet work — the physical vocabulary of this book is its own language, and she's learning it.

The drunk-laugh echo, "I like her so much" said twice, the second time funnier. She's getting literate in him.
