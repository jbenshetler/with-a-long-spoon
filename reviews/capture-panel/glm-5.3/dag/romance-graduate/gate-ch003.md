**REACTION**

Oh, this is *nasty* and I mean that as the highest compliment. I literally put the book down for a second when the redhead laughed with her head thrown back, because I knew — I knew before the text got there — and still, when Randi came back glowing with "Her name is Vee," my stomach dropped in the good way. The blurb told me from page one that the invitation was a game between two people, and I'd been half-waiting for the machinery to show itself, but I didn't expect it to show itself like *this*. I thought we were still in Randi's aftermath. Instead the book flips the whole first chapter over in my hand.

Because now Chapter 1 reads completely differently, doesn it? The bench scene — "the best of her life" for Randi — and then the bathroom mirror, the face going back up, "he could have the face... not ever." I read that as a retreat. And maybe a page of me believed she was done. But no. Two chapters later she's on a sunny quad playing the pointing game, laughing "I see what you're doing," and then she stops dead at a stranger and says "Yes" with nothing around it — the same bare yes she couldn't give Pace at the bench. The want never went away. She just found a shape it could survive in: *with you*. Girlfriend offering her man a gift. The book literally says it — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable" — and okay, yes, it's telling me the subtext, which usually annoys me, but here it's doing it in Pace's head, as him reading her, and he's a little too pleased with his own accuracy, and I think I'm supposed to notice that.

And that's the thing that's starting to unsettle me about Pace in the best way. Chapter 1 I trusted him completely — the not-respecting-Daphne's-door backstory, the waiting, the "I don't take it for her answer." He's careful, he's ethical on the surface. But this chapter he's running an operation. "Would you like to taste another woman?" asked *inside her, before she's done coming* — he clocked it himself: her body answered before she could make the wanting mean anything safer. And then the whole afternoon is him engineering a situation where Randi chooses without ever having to sit up and name it. Which is tender! It IS gentle! And it's also a man conducting a seduction of two women at once and calling it a gift. The blurb says the invitation began as a game between the two people now closest to her. So this is it. This is the game beginning. And he chose the angle by the dining hall doors "fast and without seeming to." I noticed. Randi didn't. Vee doesn't even exist in it yet except as the most alive person on the quad, laughing like someone who forgot she could be seen — which is everything Randi is not, everything she's starving next to.

The collision scene is my favorite thing in the book so far. Randi drifting into that shoulder-bump with three years of Greek-lifetime training folded into three seconds — and the *redhead* apologizing. I actually laughed. And then "Do you think she'll like the bench?" said low into his chest, in daylight, and my skin did something. Because that's Randi handing him Vee's body to plan around, the same way Vee (per the jacket) is going to hand everything over freely. The predation is so warm and so polite and everyone in it is having a wonderful time. I'm a little in love with how disturbed I am by my own complicity — the book told me on the jacket I'd fall for it exactly as she does, and it's right.

Also: "Let's go feed you." After a whole chapter of her not-eating — the cheeseburger "no," the thin apple slices. He keeps feeding these girls. I see the pattern and I don't trust it and I want more of it.

One small reservation: the middle of the chapter, the walk itself, got a little listy for me — point, verdict, point, verdict — though I think the rhythm is deliberate, the game teaching itself to her in rounds. And Pace's interiority occasionally tips toward admiring his own cleverness ("he liked, even better, the places she surprised him"). Keep that edge. It's what makes him interesting instead of a saint with a flogger.
