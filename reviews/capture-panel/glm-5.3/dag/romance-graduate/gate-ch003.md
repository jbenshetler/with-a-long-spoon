REACTION — Okay, so the floor dropped out from under me about two pages in and I had to put the book down and just sit there. *She has a name.* The woman on the bench — the face in the bathroom mirror, Brooke's Saturday ambush, all of it — that's **Randi**. Not Vee. Vee is the redhead. Vee is the girl who comes out of the dining hall laughing with her whole body, the lush one in the soft drapey clothes, the one who "could have been Randi's sister" — no, wait, that was the first girl, the ponytail one. God. The whole first chapter I thought I was reading about the heroine, and I was reading about the *scheme*. The blurb said "two people have planned the best year of her life" and I thought, fine, vague marketing — no, this book means it literally, and chapter 3 is the plan picking her up off a brick path like she's a dropped scarf.

And here's the thing that's got me a little spooked, in the good way: I *liked* the bench chapter. I was rooting for her. "There you are, Randi" wrecked me a little. And now I watch that same woman — the one who cried without knowing she was crying, who decided in the mirror that the real one was never coming out again — walk across a quad and cold-approach a girl using a fake shoulder-bump so polished it reads as the victim's fault. A decade of dance and three years of sorority, folded down into three seconds. I should be horrified and instead I'm leaning closer. Because you can see the fault line: the game stops being a game for her and she doesn't notice. She stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was *shopping*. And that bare little "Yes" — the one that came out with nothing around it before she could dress it up as a favor to her boyfriend — that's the same voice as the begging on the bench. The voice that comes from below. Pace heard it both times. Pace is counting on it.

The pointing game itself is doing something structurally clever that I'd normally resent if it were less hot — it's literally a tasting menu of Vee, the reader meeting her through Randi's eyes before Vee gets an interior. Every "no" Randi hands down is a "no" to her own mirrors — the ponytail girl, the matched pairs, the chair of things — and then Vee walks out and the sentence changes: nothing curated, nothing apologized for, laughing like someone who forgot she could be seen. Randi, who has never once forgotten she can be seen. Of *course* it's her. The book is a matchmaking algorithm and I'm watching it run.

Pace still fascinates and unsettles me in equal measure. "He did not take it for her answer" — the care is real, and it's also the care of a man designing something, and the chapter knows it: "the live private charge of something he imagined they could make together." He's tender and he's running an experiment and I genuinely can't tell how much he knows the difference, and I don't think *he* can either. That's the best thing about him. Also "You dirty boy" made me laugh out loud — she recovers in the exact register I'd recover in, and the book letting me see the seam between her performance and her actual heat is doing everything I read these books for.

The one sour note, and it's small: "Let's go feed you" as the closer right after she asks "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — because the bench question gave me an actual chill, Randi already imagining Vee facedown on it, and he softens it into the apple thing, the feeding thing, his little ledger of her appetites. It's a lovely line. It's also a man steering the moment away from what she just revealed, and I can't decide if that's tenderness or management. I think I'm supposed to not be able to decide.

Also — the Vee of the jacket is a college junior who's "quietly starving" and has never had a man who was more than fine. She has no idea the two people smiling at each other on that lawn already discussed her. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." I have chills. This is exactly the book the blurb promised and I'm annoyed at myself for doubting the blurb.
