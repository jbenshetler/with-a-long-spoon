REACTION:

Okay, so I've been riding this thing for nine chapters now and I have to admit what's happening to me: the book is seducing me exactly the way Pace seduces Vee, slowly, and I'm not sure I appreciate being worked like one of the characters, but here we are. The first chapter gave me everything — the bench, the begging, the bathroom mirror, "there you are, Randi" — and I thought, fine, this book earns its keep, this is the real thing with the real heat. And then chapters two through eight went quiet. Chapters two and three were still carrying charge because I knew what Randi was doing, I could see the mechanism — the pointing game, her saying "yes" before she could dress it up, "do you think she'll like the bench," which was the filthiest line of dialogue in the whole book and nobody touched anyone. Then the pool chapter. A pool chapter. And now a dinner chapter.

But. But. The dinner chapter got me, and I'm a little annoyed about it. "May I choose?" — three words, and I felt it land in my chest the way it landed in hers, and that's the thing this author can do that none of the interchangeable hockey bros can: they keep making the charge come out of *surrender* instead of contact. The whole meal is foreplay and nobody's hands go anywhere. She stops watching herself eat. He drips the baba ghanoush off his bread and licks his hand mid-sentence and she loses the thread of what he's saying, and I lost it too — that beat worked on me at a table with bread on it, fully clothed, which is the whole trick of this genre when it's actually written well and almost never is.

And the kiss. The rule she blurts — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and him just... smiling and letting it close and walking on, and then her feet not going. That's the best first kiss I've read in a long time precisely because he leaves the last inch to her and the author makes me *wait in the inch* with her. I grinned at the end. Actual grin, at the "exception" and going back for more with the grin still on her mouth.

What's holding me: I know what this dinner is. I know Randi and Pace are running this like a hunt, that the "May I choose" at the table is a lower-stakes echo of everything he does at the bench — the book is being thematically merciless about appetite, hers, his, the food and the sex run on the same current, and the girl eating bread without watching herself is the same girl who's going to end up on the bench. Every bite of this chapter is a promise about a later chapter. If this book doesn't eventually pay that off with full, explicit tenderness, I'll be furious. But so far every door it's opened, it's walked through.

My honest hesitation: this is now three quiet chapters in a row (pool, dinner, and the coffee shop before those was mostly talking too). The heat is all in the frame, not the frame's content. I'm tolerating it because the prose is actually feeding me and because the bench chapter proved the author goes there — but I'm watching the clock. Next chapter should start moving toward the three of them, or toward Vee and Pace in a room with a door. I didn't pick up a volume of a series called *With a Long Spoon* for restaurant reviews.

One small thing that bugged me: the "right hand" pattern note — "always the right, never the other" — I caught it, I filed it, I trust it's going somewhere, but if the book keeps telling me it has secrets instead of cashing them, that's the thing that'll sour me.
