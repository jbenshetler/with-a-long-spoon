REACTION

The thing I keep turning over: Randi told Vee a story about Friday night. A tall, dreamy man, dressed like money, a candlelit French place, a man who talked about himself — and I was in that bedroom on a Friday, sort of. I was in chapter six. The "date" was Pace. She's converting her real evenings with him into single-girl wreckage to feed the friendship, and the detail that "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table won't bother taking care of you anywhere else" is her telling Vee, with a straight face, the exact opposite of the man Vee is actually falling for — the man who *was* listening to Randi on a couch while she rode his hands and demanded the debrief. Randi is dating Pace and describing him as the villain of her Friday to keep the two worlds apart. I sat there with my hand over my mouth. The machinery is so good it recycles its own parts.

But this chapter got me somewhere I wasn't braced to go, and it's the bean bag chair. The whole first section is Vee coming home lit and Cassie catching her, and Cassie is *wonderful* — "How much did he give you to drink?" is exactly the right question from exactly the right friend, and Vee's "I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose" landing on her like a victory is the healthiest beat in nine chapters. And then. Six days later, Vee doesn't even watch Cassie go, because Randi has her by the wrist. And the line that went through me: "And it isn't really her place, the smoothie one. All the." — Vee *uninvited* Cassie, in her head, before anyone asked her to, and then thought a comfortable thought about how Cassie has her own people, and "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the book showing me the split forming. Cassie saw the great-aunt look. Cassie is the only witness who knows Vee's real laugh for free. And Vee just voluntarily sorted her out of the Randi column, for the crime of not wearing leggings. I felt that like a hand on my sternum. That's the thing the blurb is going to break my heart with, I think: not the sex, the losing Cassie.

The Randi material is devastating in a quieter way. "Ask and then ravish" — a beat late, and low, at Vee's own volume, while Vee is half up the trail in her head. That's Randi's actual want leaking out through the wingman patter, exactly like the car scene: she can only be in the fantasy as the one narrating it. And the "dear friend" hand-hold. I've been inside chapter two — I know what Brooke's warmth costs, I know what that sorority measuring feels like from Randi's side — so watching Randi spend social capital on Vee in front of Brooke, cool damp fingers, no hurry to let go, was almost too much to hold. Randi *claimed* her. Publicly, in the enemy's currency. And Brooke's smile "did not change," which terrifies me, because I have Brooke's file-building from chapter two and Vee just got a page in it.

Also the smoothie order. Beet and wheatgrass and egg white, "a drink the color of a bruise," ordered as her usual. Randi doesn't eat — the apple in chapter one, the cheeseburger she can't eat — and Vee ordered the strawberry banana and then said "oat milk" *because Randi was standing there*. Vee is already rationing herself in Randi's direction. The copying flows toward Vee becoming a little Randi, and now it's reaching her food.

No heat, and it's the second low-heat chapter in a row — but the smoothie scene is charged the way I've come to expect from this book: with knowledge, with dread, with Randi's want showing its seam. What I need next is the hike. Sunday, the trail, real shoes. The book has earned the right to put these two people on a mountain.
