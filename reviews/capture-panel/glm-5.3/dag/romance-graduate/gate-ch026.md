REACTION — Oh, this one *hurt*, and I mean that as praise. Three chapters of gold and then the book does this — and it's right, it's exactly right, because Vee was overdue to detonate and the author knew it before she did.

The lesson on the concrete pad might be the best scene in the book so far, and nothing even happens in it. "Close the space, and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours" — I actually put the book down. That's the whole relationship in one line of dance instruction. He's been leading her the entire book; here he just says it out loud with his hands, and she learns to push back instead of going jelly, and the moment it "gives" — "the decision pulled from her somehow" — I got chills. It's the mirror scene and the fitting scene and the kneeling scene all over again, and I don't even mind the repetition because this book keeps finding new physical grammar for the same thing: her learning that structure is what lets you be free.

And then she blows it up. The blonde — God, the blonde is *good*, that venomous little "all us girls love him," and my stomach dropped with Vee's because I felt the count she'd been keeping too. But here's what I love and hate about this book: the bartender. "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." The book set Vee up to be wrong in a way that isn't cheap — the blonde was genuinely being vicious, and the truth was genuinely kind, and Vee still has to sit there all night eating it. She didn't get rescued from her own jealousy. She had to do the whole bar-closing purgatory alone.

"Three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut" — there it is. The mother's voice, fully arrived, weaponized now against her own happiness. Every happy chapter she banks really is a chapter she'll have to re-audit, and this was the first taste of the audit, and I dreaded it and it *delivered*.

And the last page. She can't call Cassie — because she can't put words to what she did — and she goes to RANDI. She goes to the co-conspirator to fix the thing the conspiracy built. I actually laughed out loud, that grim laugh. Of course that's who you go to, baby. That's who was always going to catch you. This is the pointing-game engine turning over with a sound I could hear.

I did notice this is the first time the book's spent real heat elsewhere — chapter 21 was the big explicit one, and since then it's been talking, shopping, talking, dancing. But I don't care. Chapter 25 gave me Daphne and the scar; this gave me the dance floor and a fight. These aren't quiet chapters — they're *load-bearing*. The spring is coiling.
