REACTION — Chapter 67

Okay, so this chapter is the other shoe — I KNEW the kiss couldn't just detonate and be done, and watching Vee spend three whole days trying to put the fire out with her bare hands was almost worse than the party was hot. Almost. Because the party WAS hot and this chapter isn't trying to be — it's the fever chapter, the lie-awake chapter, and honestly? It's the chapter where the book's whole engine becomes visible and I can't look away.

The image that gutted me: her standing in the closet with her arms caught in the plum sweater, unable to tell if she's dressing to be looked at or dressing to be covered up. That's her ENTIRE arc in one sentence. That's the girl from chapter one who wanted to be seen and couldn't afford it, except now the question is aimed at Randi, and it's the same question and it isn't. I put the book down for a second after that one.

And the Lifesaver thing. God. The wintergreen gone soft, the graze on the inside of the wrist, her pulse "leaping up under it to be found" — and she can't even blame Randi for it, because Randi didn't do anything. That's the horror of it and the craft of it: Randi is EXACTLY herself, right down to the uncapped pen, and "nothing about her had changed and all of it landed different." The book keeps making the point that nobody took anything from Vee, and this chapter is the purest version yet — Randi hands her a Lifesaver and Vee nearly drops the roll. The taking is all happening inside her.

But here's what I'm chewing on at 1 a.m., and I texted my friend about this: the neck kiss at the end. "How do you like it?" — because Vee did that to Randi TWICE and Randi flinched both times and called it ticklish. And now Randi does it back, precise, on the exact spot, and watches her come apart. That's not a friend being silly. That's a woman returning serve, and doing it KNOWING what it does — the same woman who's sleeping with Pace, who told him in bed she wanted to put her hand on Vee. Every soft thing Randi does now has that under it, and Vee can't feel it, and I have to. The dramatic irony is at full pressure and my chest hurts from holding it for her. "I've wanted to do that for the longest time" — Randi said that OUT LOUD, about the dance, and it's true in a way Vee can't hear yet.

And then — the brunch. Vee turns her mouth away, wins the small battle, and then her body, straight out of the ringing on her neck, offers a brunch. Her own voice commits her before her mind can vote. That's the book's whole grammar again — the yes spending itself before the fear arrives — except now it's aimed at the wrong person, and she can't charge it to Pace like she did Saturday night. He's not in this chapter at all, and I didn't miss him, which is its own piece of information I'm sitting with uncomfortably.

Cassie. CASSIE. "What was that with you and Randi?" — with the worry in it, and Vee standing there with no answer because there IS no answer yet. Cassie's been the clean hands this whole book and now she's clocking it before Vee can even name it. And the chapter just... ends there. On Vee not having the words. That's a knife of an ending — quiet, but my thumb moved.

I'll say the true thing: this is the third quiet-ish chapter in a row if you count the morning-after and the waxing — no, actually, the painting chapter was a full blaze and the Pace confession chapter was one of the best in the book. This one is the cooldown after two of the biggest chapters, and it's doing exactly what a bridge chapter should: it moved Vee an inch, it armed the brunch, it let Cassie's eyebrow graduate into a question. I don't resent it. But the heat is banked, and the thing pulling me forward isn't the sex anymore — it's the collision. The brunch. Thursday's standards committee ("discipline a bad girl" — Randi said that with a straight face and a whole double life under it). I'm in this for the reckoning now.
