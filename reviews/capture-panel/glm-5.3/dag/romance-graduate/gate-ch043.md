REACTION — Two quiet scenes and a dinner tray, and I'm still sitting here a little stunned by how much got done. This book keeps doing the thing I've begged books to do: take the scene where nothing "happens" and make it the scene. Vee narrating her own Thanksgiving to Cassie, itemized like evidence — the mom's collarbone touch, the nurse face watching the seconds, the dad burning rolls on purpose — I know these parents. I've *met* these parents at my own table. The mother sitting on the end of the bed asking if she's making herself sick was the first moment the book took Vee's weight loss seriously from outside the machine, and I felt my stomach drop right along with hers. Because her mother is RIGHT. "Happier than I've ever been" and dropping pounds she isn't asking to lose — both true at once, and the book letting both stand without adjudicating is why I'm still here at chapter 43.

The irony layered on the dad pricing the dress is delicious in the cruelest way — he's calculating what it cost, panicking that his daughter is in deeper than a man's bank account should warrant, and it cost a magazine clipping and a man's own hands on a borrowed sewing machine. Vee's right and she knows it: "sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's the truest sentence about parents I've read in a romance in years. Wrong is a category dads have. Weird isn't.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" — "Both. Probably both." No push after it. That girl is going to break my heart before this is over, and the fact that Vee is "a little, not" grateful for the not-pushing tells me she wanted to be made to answer. Cassie's the only person asking her the real questions — "Do you love him," now "what is he to you" — and Vee keeps handing her half an answer with the question mark on it out loud, like she did with the boyfriend thing to her parents. She put a question mark on *boyfriend.* Out loud. The mouth is still filing "it's not," and now I've heard it do it in front of both her mother and the only honest friend she has.
