REACTION — I put my tea down and read this one twice, and the second time was the one where it got me.

Six weeks of this book holding the scar and the parents door and Peter's name back, and it pays out all three in one fireside chapter, and not one of them lands where I braced for. "My real name is Peter." Five words, flat as a countertop, and my chest hurt. And the mother taken out of the answer *again* — he answered "what does she call you" with a name that has nothing to do with her, and Vee let him, and the book didn't underline it. That's the kind of quiet I'm here for.

The Daphne thing. I did the arithmetic right along with Vee — nineteen and fifteen, two years — and I sat there with that ugly clean word under my tongue just like she did, and then I watched *him* not have the word. "He had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck." That line is going to live in me a while. Because here's what it does to the whole book: this man who asks before he takes, who leaves room to choose, who is *precise* about consent — that's not just courtliness, that's a boy who was somebody's project at fifteen building the thing he needed and never got, and now aiming it at a girl picked off a quad. It makes him both more forgivable and more unknowable at the same time, and I genuinely don't know which way the book is going to cut when the reveal comes. Is Vee his Daphne inverted — the girl he chooses *for* and not *on* — or is she the same arrangement wearing better manners? The book still won't tell me. It just keeps feeding me evidence for both verdicts and watching me hold them.

And the scar. Her fingers find it, "more of it than she'd known," and he doesn't flinch, doesn't move her hand — he *presses it down onto it and holds it there.* "Not tonight" with no wall in it, a door with a time on it. I actually said oh out loud. That's the most intimate thing this book has done and nobody took a stitch of clothing off past a robe.

Also: "the ball and the leaf and the stars are all doing the one thing" — I've read a hundred heroes explain their manly hobby and this is the first one that made *me* feel it, and then Vee's realization — "It's the lift. It's the same face" — did something to her in front of me and I believed it in my body before she finished the sentence. And her hand going to the scar right after, like the intimacy had a current and it had to go somewhere. That's choreography. That's writing.

Now, my honest ledger: this is a talking chapter, robe-and-quilt, fire-and-tea. There's charge in it — his palm on her breast like it's just where warmth lives, the scar-touch — but it's a simmer at most, and I know what it is: it's the second building chapter in a row after the shoe-shopping. But unlike a stall, this one is the book *spending* everything it saved up. Six weeks of door-holding and this is the payoff: the name, the age, the hallway, Daphne, the deferred scar. I don't resent a single page. What I'd text my friend at 1 a.m.: "the courtly math boy just gave her the groomer backstory and the book did NOT flinch and neither did she and I need to lie down."

The one thing that nags me — "she had not done anything to deserve it," for no reason she'd done anything to deserve. Vee. Baby. There IS a reason. It's on the jacket. The dramatic irony has been a low hum for twenty-six chapters and now it's sitting right next to "I have never been so fortunate," and the book knows exactly what it's doing putting them in the same paragraph. The induction with the scar and the parents door still unopened — the gun count on the mantel is getting genuinely comic. I'm in. Feed me the next one.
