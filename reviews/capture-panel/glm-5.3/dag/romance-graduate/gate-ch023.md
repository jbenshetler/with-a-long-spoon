REACTION — I put this book down and sat there for a minute, and I'm still a little mad, which I think is the point but I'm not letting it off the hook for it.

The dance lesson is the best scene in weeks and I mean that in the way I've said the best scene in a book is two people talking. The whole thing is charged without a single item of clothing coming off — "give me something to push back against," "close the space you're keeping and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours," and her finally getting it when she stops guessing. That's this book's whole thesis in dance steps, and I'd have been happy to live on that concrete pad for ten pages. The Rusty Nail was good too — him being known, loved, easy with all of it, and her starting a count she "hadn't meant to start."

But here's where I'm uneasy, and it's not what the book wants me to be uneasy about. The blonde's speech is *written*. "Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to" — that's a poisoned dart so precisely aimed at Vee's whole situation (a man who leads, a girl who follows and blooms under it) that I read it twice trying to figure out whether the book knows how on-the-nose it is. Is that a real mean girl in a bar or is the author's hand showing? And Vee's spiral after — "spread my legs like a slut," "she was just next" — that word choice is raw and true to her shame programming, the floozy-voice from the mirror chapter turned nasty, and I believe it *from her*, but it's also a lot of self-flagellation to sit inside for that many pages.

What saved the chapter for me, genuinely: the bartender. "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." I actually put my hand over my mouth. And Vee lying awake holding both the shame AND the memory of the lead arriving before her feet knew what to do with it — that's the grown-up interiority I'm here for, the two things being true at once without the book hammering the irony. "She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be" — yes, that landed.

What I'm grinding my teeth about: this is the third or fourth structure where Vee panics about Pace's history and someone outside hands her the corrective (Brooke, the bartender, eventually maybe Randi). And Randi was completely absent. After chapter 23 — Randi confessing in his bed that she can't get bored of Vee, that she's gotten past her — I need the triangle breathing in the same chapter or at least the next one. The setup for "go to Randi's" is promising but also terrifying, because every word Vee tells Randi goes straight to Pace's pillow, and I know that and Vee doesn't, and the dramatic irony is starting to sit on my chest like a cat that won't move.

Also — no heat this chapter, and that's fine, but I notice it. The lesson scene was a 1, maybe brushing a 2, and everything after is cold. If the next chapter is an apology-and-makeup I want it to *cost* something, not slide into bed as a reset button.

Still turning pages. Faster than I want to admit.
