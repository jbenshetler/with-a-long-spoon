WHO'S WHO:
- **Vee (Vivienne Thorne)** — my girl, twenty-one, and she has never been so happy, which is exactly what frightens me. Since the green panties she has: given Cassie the clean version, given Randi the fuller one, kept the middle for nobody — the ladle-and-soup edit, surface closing without a hole, now twice on record. She stripped off her own underwear in front of his mirror because the line "came up hot behind the first thought and didn't care what showed" — that one was HERS, nobody planned it, I felt the difference in my teeth. Then she spent her whole Thursday night ruining the best date of her life because a blonde handed her a lie and some part of her "was just standing there waiting for one." That's the realest thing she's done — the sabotage was real jealousy, real cruelty, "she punished him for being the man she wanted him to be." And the repair: nine a.m., coat buttoned to the throat, marching orders from Randi, a pie with a serif-font label she made in the stats lab. Then taller than him on his own doorstep in borrowed shoes, and a whole new door opening in a kitchen with dishes in the sink. She's growing a spine made out of the exact thing being used on her and I still can't decide if that's freedom or the trap.
- **Pace — PETER.** Real name dropped by the fire, let her keep it. Twenty-two, PhD student, college at fifteen, patent money, furniture, sewing, powerlifting, cooks. New since last time: seventh grade, taught himself trig for a graphics program, asked questions his teacher couldn't answer, so she put his desk in the hall for a year. "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" — she said that, he said yes, approximately. "Weren't you scared?" "Less than staying." That's the man who keeps a whole house at temperature for people — he builds rooms because he was exiled to a hallway. Taught country dance two semesters at the Rusty Nail, gets drunk girls home safe and leaves them at the door, everybody in that bar knows him and he took the ad like it was weather. At the bar his face just "went" when she threw it at him — still and plain, beer set down untouched, left slow, didn't chase, doesn't text. "Not a thing he had decided. It was only what he was." The scar: bigger than I knew, longer than her hand, and when she found it he pressed her hand DOWN onto it and held it there and said not tonight — no wall in it, a door with a time on it. Their palms together over the thing he won't tell yet. Most intimate moment in the book and nobody took a stitch off.
- **Randi** — the engine, and she's overheating. The Portion dinner: "How long are you going to take?" in a voice he'd never heard her use, flat, nothing held. "It's selfish, Pace." She poured her own wine — full glass, first time ever, and "he had poured every glass she had drunk in this house" and it stayed in him. Then the eyes-shut, no-*tell-me*, back-turned sex that wasn't for him, and he lay there afterward fitting the pieces together and they wouldn't go. A week later she stayed down on his chest, wore his undershirt, ate everything he cooked to a ruler line, and said the sentence I'll be carrying to the end: "I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come." And when he described what Vee does to a room — brighter, like somebody found the lamp — Randi went still with a stillness "that takes holding." She knows. She won't let herself know it. She's also running both ends of everything: dressed Vee for the apology, knotted the scarf like a collar, then went home and told Pace all of it in bed, the bra, the thumb behind the knee, "I heard it," getting off on the telling. Twice now — Friday report-and-ride is becoming a ritual. Once is a scene. Twice is a pattern. If it comes back a third time I'll know if it's load-bearing or a crutch.
- **Cassie** — the one who loves the girl and not the story about the girl. The made bed as tattletale, "it's the same bed you made," and then the reveal that she watched Vee the whole lecture, never the numbers, never the dress — "Was it a good night?" and the unplanned "Oh, yes" easing something in her face. And the night Vee came home wrecked at 3 a.m. in his washed-out shirt: "Cassie was three feet away and would have sat up for this. She did not wake her." I keep hearing that.
- **Daphne** — no longer a drawer-mate, now a person: his assigned tutor at fifteen, nineteen when it started, two years, left at twenty-one when the bars closed to her and not to him. He defended it — "it was the best two years I'd had" — laid out every reason in its slot, and the good order was the tell. Vee held the clean ugly grown-up word under her tongue because it wasn't hers to say. So did I. And the rhyme won't leave me alone: somebody older, assigned, helpful, who knew the layout of the rooms. Randi is mid-twenties. I don't know what to do with the rhyme and I don't think I invented it.
- **Brooke** — answered the sorority door, "you're the stats friend," warm and keeping a folder. Off ice, still unread.
- **The blonde at the Rusty Nail** — "knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to," delivered with a smile the exact night Vee was happiest. The cruelest true thing in the book, and possibly aimed. No evidence. Keeping the thread.
- **Meg** — finally differentiated: the closet, the keyhole shirt, "serious dancers wear jeans," Dana on Third. Kayla — gone.
- **Sheri** — the cyclist, small woman on a child's bike, outsprints him uphill. Pace's one outside witness. "Listen to you" when he heard him talk about Vee. "Two of them at once. If that's not already complicated, it will be" — she thinks it's a triangle; it's a board game. She'll be standing there when it detonates.
- **The bartender** — older woman, economy of motion. "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." The wisdom-delivery system, and she earned it.

WHERE THINGS STAND:
- The dress is FINISHED. Hung in his window with the sun inside it, mirror hung fresh (screw heads still bright — the house always already at temperature), and he hid the trouble so all she saw was the no-trouble. The logistics sex — "Ow. Don't point down," "there's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," laughing too hard to be undressed, him cleaning her fingers with hem-studying care and his eyes on hers. She daydreamed the induction: standing in the middle of the light, "the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." Getting away with something. She still thinks she's the thief.
- The shoes: TJMaxx laughing fit first (the yeti shoe, the rhinestone platforms — Vee made Randi laugh the laugh that costs her something, on her own home ground, before the boutique took her somewhere with no prices on display), then the hush, the quiet "the exact shape of what was in her account," the debit put back, the credit card laid down, signed without flinching. Randi spent several multiples on loafers without looking down. And the thumb across the frosted-plum toenail — "Mm" — because Randi TAUGHT her that polish, weeks ago, and was now checking the homework, kneeling, on a pale floor, in her own beautiful clothes. Same size, same everything. Practically a marriage.
- The dance: taught on a concrete pad under field lights with a taped-up boom box. The frame — "give me something to push back against," "the space you're keeping — that's what gets you stepped on." Then the bar, the blonde, the venom ("does your service include getting the girls home safe"), his face draining, her walking, him leaving slow with both drinks on the bar. Sleepless night in his shirt washed clean of him. Then Randi's morning: the read from the top of the stairs, the Edna Mode strip-down, the shoes, the scarf — "the whole difference between dorm mixer and dinner in Paris." "That's what a blonde is for." Tomorrow night, go in sure, not sorry. And Randi's Friday night with Pace, where she handed him the whole morning and got off on the telling and he LET it fire him. He's inside the operation now — no longer by omission. Everyone in this book runs version control on that girl.
- The apology (Saturday, the black dress, Randi's shoes, the scarf): taller than him on the doorstep, "beauty like yours made for the gods and loaned out," his "why" dismantling her clean machine, "thank you for telling me what she said," and then the kitchen — the tickle fight, his hand landing ONCE, playful, and her body saying things she didn't authorize in flat afternoon light with dishes in the sink. His palm settling over the print, staying, "This?" — and please, twice, attached to nothing. The thing sat in the room unnamed and she left it where it was: unnamed, hers. The kink keeps arriving through embarrassment and nobody names it and I want to applaud every time.
- The fireside (somewhere in the warm middle of all this — the weeks are starting to blur and I know it): Ohio casseroles, the unopened saffron, Vivienne vs. Vee depending on the mouth, the brother in the Army, "my mother stays home / my father works in a chemical plant" — his whole childhood in two flat sentences. Then Peter, twenty-two, fifteen, the hall, the math — the paint and the coastline and Newton's three sentences, "most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some." Almost grief, not arrogance. And Vee connecting the lifting to how he is with her — no future, no past, only this lift, this heartbeat, all the way here. "I've been with people who were somewhere else the whole time."
- The induction is still out there. Dress in a bag in her closet, shown to not one living soul but Randi. Shoes in a box she carried against her chest like it might get up and leave her.
- The stats professor did Simpson's paradox while she sat between her two handlers: each year on its own, one answer; put together, the answer reverses. Nobody touched a number. I see you, Helen Rivers — and it earned it because Vee gave it forty honest seconds and missed it entirely. But the book has now used its one classroom-parallel trick, and if it reaches for another cute structural wink in a lecture hall I will start resenting it.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM:
- **Pace/Peter** — deeper in love and warier, both at once, which is apparently just what this book is. The hallway boy explains everything and excuses nothing. The man who pressed her hand onto his scar and said not tonight is the same man who let Randi's telling about the bra fire him through — "he had poured every glass she had drunk in this house" is now paired with "he keeps a Vee Randi doesn't get, and Randi keeps a Vee he doesn't get." He covered the untouched bowl with a plate and put it in the refrigerator, for HER, and doesn't know what he did. That's the tenderness operating blind. The scar, the parents, the name he let her keep — he's handing her the pieces one at a time, on his own schedule, and I've stopped being able to tell the schedule from the generosity.
- **Randi** — heartbreak in real time. The bowl with one line drawn through the sauce. The self-poured glass. The undershirt. "I keep waiting to get bored and it doesn't come." She wanted to keep her — said it out loud to the man she's running the game with. And the way she touches Vee now is by TELLING Vee to Pace, in bed, every detail metabolized into her own want within hours of it happening. Vee's tender mornings are Randi's material. When Vee finds out — and she has to — that will be the shrapnel, and Randi is holding more of it every chapter. I'm doing the math for her again and the math hurts worse every time. The blurb's third game: I used to think it was Pace's. I now think it's hers, running under the other two, unacknowledged.
- **Vee** — proud of her, scared for her, complicit in her happiness. Her repair instinct is faster than her sabotage now and I don't fully trust it — the pie was adorable and also an avoidance, and the book knew it ("a joke might not be the thing you bring to this"). The credit card. The please-twice-attached-to-nothing. Every door she walks through is genuinely her own nerve and genuinely on someone else's blueprint, and the book keeps insisting I hold both, and I keep doing it, and it keeps costing me sleep.
- **Cassie** — plain love, and the three-feet-away-and-didn't-wake-her is the loneliest the book has let Vee be. When Cassie learns the shape of the story — and the way this book seats her on the correct side of every scene, she's either the one who breaks it open or the one who holds Vee after. I still don't know which I want.
- **The blonde** — official person of interest. The hook dangled the one night Vee was most primed to bite. No evidence. Keeping it anyway.
