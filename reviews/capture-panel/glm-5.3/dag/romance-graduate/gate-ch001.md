REACTION — Okay. I need a minute.

First, the thing I have to say out loud: that was **Randi**. The blurb promised me Vee, the starving college girl being slowly reeled in by Randi and Pace — and chapter one is Pace putting *Randi* on a bench and taking her apart. Which means the "best of her life" they're planning for Vee has a history, a proof of concept, a wound in it already. I didn't see that coming and I love being wrong like that. The book starts with the predator's own girl getting destroyed-and-rebuilt, and *flinching when he calls her beautiful*, and locking the bathroom door. That's not a prologue of fantasy power, that's a prologue of damage. Randi isn't just the glamorous friend steering Vee toward Pace — she's someone who got seen once, couldn't survive it, and put the mask back on. And now she's going to curate the same experience for another girl? That's the most interesting thing this book could have done.

And the sex. God. This is what I keep telling people I want and almost never get. The explicitness is *fully there* — the spanking, the edging, the wax, the begging — but it's all doing psychological work. The "please" that's still her waiter-voice please versus the please that comes from below. Him checking her back and shoulders mid-scene and her snapping "*They're fine*, Pace" — that little flash of exasperation inside total surrender is more intimate than ten pages of throbbing anything. And then it goes *dark* after: her pressing the sharp bottle cap into her thumbnail where it would hurt, him noticing, not saying anything. Him still hard and just... setting the fact down. I got chills at "the size of a key turning in a lock."

The Daphne paragraph — one ex-girlfriend, one door he didn't respect, told in four sentences — is doing more character work than most romances do in a whole series. He's not a brooding billionaire with a tragic backstory monologue; he's a man who learned one specific lesson the hard way and now runs his whole tenderness through it.

If I have a complaint it's that the POV is almost all *his* — I was starving for her interior during the scene itself, and I only got it in the bathroom, which, fine, that's where she lives, behind doors.
