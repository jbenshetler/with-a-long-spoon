REACTION — Okay. I sat down to read one chapter before bed and it's now 1 a.m. and I'm texting my friend Sarah a voice memo, which I haven't done about a book in probably two years.

Here's the thing — I almost want to be annoyed. The jacket sold me Vee. Vee is the girl I signed up for, the quiet starving one. And the book opens on some unnamed sorority girl with a monogrammed bag and startlingly blue eyes, and I'm thinking, fine, here's the rival, here's the friend, let's get through this so Vee can show up. And then he says "There you are, Randi" and the name lands like a ice cube down my back. *Randi.* The glamorous friend from the blurb. The one who's going to steer Vee toward this exact man. So the book isn't starting with the love story — it's starting with the *secret.* The two conspirators, already deep in whatever this is, before the girl who doesn't know there's a plan even exists. I had to put the book down for a second and just sit with the shape of that. The jacket told me the twist on purpose and the chapter one still managed to make me feel it.

And the chapter itself. God. The apple. He slices it thin because she'll only eat thin slices, lays them cut-side up because she eats them faster that way — and he's watching her *do the math* about whether food is permitted, and he's sorry about it, and he doesn't say any of that out loud. That's the whole book in a bowl. I've read five hundred heroes who "notice everything" and it's always noticing her ass in a dress. This man notices the fine hairs the razor misses on her calves. I'm not going to pretend that doesn't work on me.

The bench scene itself — I've read a hundred spanking scenes, I could recite them, and this one still got me, because it isn't about the spanking. It's about him refusing her first two *pleases* because they're the please she uses on *waiters.* The please that's a performance, the please that's currency. And the book knows exactly what it's doing with her whole apparatus of charm — "she could not pout her way back" — she's spent her life in the wanted position and the bench just... isn't there. When her real voice finally comes out and it's "almost ugly compared to the bright sorority-house voice" — that's the moment. That's the thing I keep reading these books hoping to find and almost never do.

Then the bathroom. The smile "sitting on top of the face that had begged." Her trying the half-smile and seeing the disjunction and hearing herself make the *there you are* sound. And the ending — she's putting the found girl back behind the face, "not ever," and the deadbolt from the first paragraph comes back, quiet as a key turning in a lock. She locks his front door coming *in* and locks his bathroom door going *out* and if you think I didn't clock that, book, I clocked it, and I'm a little bit in love with you for not pointing at it.

Things I'll be honest about: it's all his POV until it isn't, and he's very nearly the Magical Dom who sees everything and knows everything — left the water because he knew she'd be thirsty, knew she'd get cold, built the bench. That's one draft away from the omniscient boyfriend I'm exhausted by. But the bathroom chapter-section is what saves him from being a fantasy object — because the book shows me what he *can't* see, and shows him choosing not to open the door (and there's a Daphne back there, a whole previous mistake, a man who learned something — an actual history). And the thumbnail. The water bottle cap pressed into her nail bed, one sentence, no comment. That's the detail that made this not a fantasy. That's a girl who hurts herself in tiny managed doses and a book that trusts me to notice without underlining it.

Pace never comes — he's hard the whole night and sets it down, "that's not what this night was for," and weirdly that's the most charged thing in the chapter? The whole thing is heat and almost none of it resolves and I don't care.

Where's Vee. That's my one low hum of doubt. If chapter two is another Randi-and-Pace evening I'll start wondering whose book this is. But one chapter, and the chapter did everything I ask a first chapter to do: something happened, everyone in it has an inner life, the prose feeds me instead of showing off, and the heat is real heat with a brain behind it. I bought Vee's book and got handed the conspiracy first, and I'm going to allow it, because now every scene Randi shares with Vee is going to be unbearable in the best way.
