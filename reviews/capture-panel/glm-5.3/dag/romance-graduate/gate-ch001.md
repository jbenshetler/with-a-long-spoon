I had to read "There you are, Randi" three times because I genuinely thought I'd misread. And suddenly the whole blurb reorganizes in my head: this isn't the heroine on the bench, this is the woman who's going to build the plan. I actually said "oh no" out loud, alone, at whatever time it is.

And here's the thing — the misdirection isn't cheap. It works because Pace isn't narrating "the heroine." He's narrating *a woman whose face is a product*. That bathroom scene is the best thing I've read in this genre in maybe two years. Her doing inventory in the mirror — "the face had begged" — and trying the photo smile on top of the begging face and watching the disjunction crack her own eyes. That's not romance-novel interiority, that's the real thing, and it's *in a book with a custom-built spanking bench in it*, which is exactly the combination I keep telling people I want and never get. Depth AND heat. I kept waiting for the prose to get coy once the bench came out and it just... didn't. Wet, specific, unhurried, and somehow the most explicit thing in the scene is "he was still hard. That's not what this night was for." A dom who doesn't get off and the *abstention* is the hottest sentence in the chapter — I'm furious about how well that worked on me.

The apple made me ache. Thin slices, cut sides up, because she eats them faster that way, and he's *watched her clock the counters three times and said nothing*. The whole book is in that apple. Someone who pays attention as a form of love, and someone who reads everything as a price tag, and what happens when the price tag can't be read.

So the plan in the blurb — the game, Vee as the chosen target — suddenly reads as Randi outsourcing the being-seen. Handing another girl to the man who sees too much. That's a psychological engine, not a plot device. I'm a little sick about it and completely hooked.
