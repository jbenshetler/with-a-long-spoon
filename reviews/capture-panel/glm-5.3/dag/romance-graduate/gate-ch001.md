REACTION — Okay. I bought this for the blurb, which promised me Vee, and chapter one gives me *Randi* — the glamorous friend, the one who's supposed to be doing the steering — strapped to a bench, sobbing, and deciding behind a locked bathroom door that the real girl is going back in the box forever. That's not a bait-and-switch, that's a thesis statement. The blurb said "a game between the two people now closest to her" and the first chapter shows me exactly what the game is built *out of*: a man who is very, very good at finding women's hidden hunger, and a woman who got found and cannot survive it. So when Vee walks in later — the fresh appetite, the one who might actually be able to say yes all the way down — I already know the stakes. Smart. Possibly merciless. I'm into it.

And the craft. God. The apple slices cut thin because she won't eat thick ones, laid cut-side-up because she eats them faster that way — that told me more about both of them in three lines than most books manage in three chapters. The spanking itself I could have written in my sleep; that's not where this lives. Where it lives is the *please*. Him hearing the difference between her waiter-*please* and the real one. Her trying her photograph half-smile on in the mirror and watching it fail to cover what's underneath. And then the bottle cap under her thumbnail — she hurts herself small, automatically, and *he takes the bottle away*, and neither of them names it. That's the chapter that earned the "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" line on the jacket. The heat and the interiority are the same event, not alternating scenes. That's the thing I've been starving for.

What scared me, in the good way and the wary way: Pace's whole POV is a man reading a woman like an instrument he built. He's tender about it, he asks, he waits at the locked door because of some Daphne backstory — but there's something chilling in "He had been keeping his hand away from the front of her on purpose" being described with the same calm as the apple. The book knows he's a little frightening. Randi's bathroom scene confirms it: he gave her the best thing that ever happened to her and she's already planning the lie. I don't trust him fully and I'm not supposed to, and I don't trust *her* — and chapter one has already made both of them real enough to want anyway.

Minor wobble: I hit "the face" paragraph about six times and thought *yes, I get it* around four. And I clocked immediately that I'm being set up to enjoy a seduction that will be done *to* Vee without her knowledge — the jacket told me, so I'm complicit on purpose. Whether that lands as devastating or icky depends entirely on whether Vee gets an interior life like Randi's. She has to, or the whole thing curdles. But chapter one bought a lot of my faith.
