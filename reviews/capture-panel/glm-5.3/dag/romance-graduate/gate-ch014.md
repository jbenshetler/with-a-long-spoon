The house chapter. Every romance has the house chapter — the walkthrough where the heroine reads the hero off his possessions like a clearance rack. I've read a hundred of these and I can recite them: the giant kitchen, the tragic framed photo, the one locked room. This one had me by the throat because every single thing she read off that house was a thing I read off *him*, and the book knows it. The shoes in the closet — "one right pair for each thing and no more." The worn KitchenAid. The fantasy paperbacks with cracked spines low on the shelf where nobody puts things for show, and the middle-schooler flat on his stomach, gone clean out of the room — that little ghost of a boy did something to my chest. And the error-correcting codes. "Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it." Honey, the *metaphor* — a man whose whole life's work is making sure a message survives transmission, seducing a girl who's been receiving nothing but noise her whole life. I clocked it and I'm not even mad, I'm impressed.

But here's what made me put my tea down: **two towels on the rod.** The book put that detail in and walked away from it. Vee didn't clock it. I did. Randi's toothbrush isn't here. Randi's anything isn't here. The woman who sat on that bench, who locked his bathroom mirror behind her own face — where does she live? Not here, apparently, not even a second towel's worth.
