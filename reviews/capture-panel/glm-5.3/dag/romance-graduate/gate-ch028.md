REACTION — Okay. There are two chapters in here and they're not the same quality.

The pad under the field lights is this book at its best. The whole lesson is the whole book in miniature — "Give me something to push back against... being jelly doesn't work," "the space you're keeping, that's what gets you stepped on, close it and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." That's the measuring, the Trade, everything he does with her, said in the grammar of a lead's hand at a shoulder blade, and Vee *feeling* the metaphor arrive in her body before her head gets it ("Don't think. Just feel the lead" — and then the moment it gives, and she's busy, and I'm grinning). I loved "There's no sorry unless you take off a foot." I loved the boom box with electrical tape on the handle. That half of the chapter is a 9.

And then. Look — the blonde is well done, genuinely nasty ("Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to" is a beautiful little poisoned dart, and "he's so good about getting a girl home safe" is even worse). The spiral itself is believable. The bartender scene is good, and "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey" earned its land.

But I have a real problem, and it's not craft, it's arithmetic: *three dates?* She's slept over whole weekends. She stood in his window in a dress he made *to* her. She handed him her folded underwear with a word attached. And this chapter has her thinking "three dates, and I'd spread my legs like a slut" like a girl who hasn't already crossed every one of those bridges on her own nerve. The mother's voice running on the same wire as the heat — that's established, I know the book knows how to do that. But here it doesn't read like the mother's voice coming back; it reads like the book forgot where she was. The Vee of chapter 24 took her own underwear off *on purpose* and let the wanting cross before the shame. This Vee regresses three volumes and the text doesn't seem to know it's a regression — that's the part that worries me. If it's deliberate, if the next chapter has her *noticing* the backslide, fine, I'm in. But right now it smells like a slip.

Also — and this stings, because I said out loud two chapters ago that the best scene in a book can be two people talking — the fight itself is the trope I left the shelf over. One catty woman at a bar and an unsaid sentence and a night ruined? I've read this fight forty times. What saved it from being a DNF-moment is that the book sided against her, clearly, and made her know it by morning — "she'd punished him for the man she wanted him to be" is the one line in the spiral that felt true to her.

I'm staying. The ending — cool embarrassment is just information, going to Randi's to fix it — is exactly her, and Randi's kitchen is where I want to be next. But I'm watching this book now in a way I wasn't at chapter 25.
