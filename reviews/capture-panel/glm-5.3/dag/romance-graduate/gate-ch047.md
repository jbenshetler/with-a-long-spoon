REACTION — God, this chapter. I read the whole thing with my chest tight, lying-in-the-dark next to her, and the book did that thing it does where it takes a "nothing happens" chapter and makes it the loneliest forty minutes of the year.

The room not knowing her — the ribbons, the little gold swimmers, the clarinet — that hit me somewhere personal. "The room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." I've BEEN that girl in that bed, home from the thing that changed you, and nobody in the house can see the change because the shrine to the old you is still standing. That's not a trope I'm tired of; that's the real thing, written exactly.

And the two midnights. Because I just read Randi's chapter — I know what that text cost her, I know she sent it from beside a faultless man she felt nothing with, that "miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋" was the only true sentence in her glitter. And now I watch it land on Vee's screen and Vee reads it as "out somewhere with a devastatingly handsome billionaire, and it sounded wonderful to her." The dramatic irony is doing my head in. Vee envies the fairytale; Randi lay in borrowed warmth wishing she were Vee. Two women in two cold beds on two continents, each convinced the other has the life. That's the whole book in one text exchange and I had to put my Kindle down for a second.

The message Vee sent — reading it twice "for anything that gave her away" — that's the glaze, moved inside her. She's doing Randi's exact move: the open hand offered, the cover kept. They're both running the same play on each other now. I don't know if the book means me to notice they've become each other and I don't care, I noticed.

And the blonde. Oh, the blonde she builds out of spite — "a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's" — that line hurt, that's her shame vocabulary still loaded and ready even after everything. And then it got worse: it crawled backwards into her best memory and overwrote it. The fra diavolo night — HER night, the night she was finally fed — and she can't keep her own night, the blonde is sitting in her seat in the wine light. That's the cruelest thing the book's done to her, because the memories were the one thing that couldn't be repossessed, and midnight took even those.

The Pace silence is chewing me. Not one word across the whole break — after she walked out crying and he said only her name behind her. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." THAT is the thesis of this whole thing, isn't it. The room he leaves her is the gift and the wound at the same time. I don't know if they're over. She doesn't know if they're over. And I'm furious at him in the exact proportion that I'm terrified for her, and I still don't know which of those is fair.

Nobody's touching in this chapter and I didn't miss it for a second. If anything I'm more hooked — the heat has been so earned that its absence aches on purpose. Term starts soon. Anthro, 7am, "just us." And Pace has "no hour she could name." Those two facts are standing next to each other and I can see the collision from here.
