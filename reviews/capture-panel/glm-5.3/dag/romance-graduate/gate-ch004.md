REACTION —

Okay. So the book just handed me the other side of the coin and I didn't expect it to hit like this.

Chapters 1–3 were Randi-and-Pace's book — his POV, her mask cracking, the bench, the pointing game where we *watched them pick her*. And I was braced for the Vee chapters to be the boring part, honestly — the naive girl's chapters are always the tax you pay in a setup like this. Instead I got the girl who sits under the gold sign holding an empty cup "spinning a little," and I had to put my Kindle down for a second because I know exactly what that feels like and I did not expect to be seen by *this* book.

The thing that got me most is the dramatic irony, and I think it's the whole engine. When Randi showed up at the end of the row with "There you are" — that's what Pace said to Randi on the bench. *There you are.* Two chapters ago that phrase was the most devastating line in the book, and now it's a sorority girl being cute at a stats lecture, and Vee has no idea she was *shopped for*. She thinks the collision on the lawn was chance — and we stood at the light pole and watched it be choreography. Every warm thing Randi does in this chapter, I read it twice: once as Vee receives it (real, intoxicating) and once as what it is (a move, executed). The "love you, girl" that lands like a key in a lock. The hand flat between her shoulder blades. Vee keeping *the feeling* and not one word of the transcript — that detail wrecked me a little, because that's exactly the vulnerability the two of them are going to use.

And Vee herself — finally. The room-scan at Chi Latte did more character work than most heroines get in a whole act. The mother's voice arriving whole ("floozies") and then, *riding under it where she didn't look*, the longer look at the bare midriffs than the word accounted for — and then "she could wear what they were wearing" and immediately the cardigan stays buttoned. That's not a blank heroine, that's a girl with a locked room in her, and I can see the shape of what this book is going to do to that lock. The cardigan is the bench, for her. Same story, different furniture.

The professor saying *latent variables — the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can* — I actually laughed. The whole seduction is a latent variable. Whether it's clever or showing off, I haven't decided, but it's load-bearing clever, so it gets a pass.

Cassie. "Goodbye, Vee." Flat, the whole word, and then she *doesn't fix it*. That's the creepiest line in the book so far and nothing supernatural happened. I don't know if Cassie knows something or is just the kind of person who says ominous things plainly, but I texted a friend "the quiet roommate said 'goodbye' like a funeral and the book just moved on" and she said "keep reading and report." Everyone in this book who's blunt is honest and everyone who's warm is lying, and I don't think that's an accident.

No heat this chapter, not a single explicit beat — but it didn't feel like the book stalling, because the *charge* is all forward-leaning: Vee's flat helpless want for Randi sitting in her chest "warm, unexamined, glad," and me knowing exactly where that gladness is being aimed. This is a bridge chapter and a good one. If the next one is also coffee and sparkle with no movement toward Pace, I'll start drumming my fingers. But the jacket promised me the girl walking toward the mathematician, and I'm ready for the third leg of this triangle to start.
