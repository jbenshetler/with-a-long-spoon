REACTION

Oh, this one hurt in a way I wasn't ready for. Three chapters watching the machinery from the inside — the bench, the mirror, the pointing game — and now the book turns the key and gives me Vee's POV, and I know exactly what she doesn't. That's the whole design of the chapter: dramatic irony as a weapon. When she writes the date at the top of the page so her hand has "somewhere to be," when she makes herself wait one full beat before saying yes to coffee — I'm watching a woman guard herself against being too much, too eager, and I know the eagerness was the point. She was chosen *for* it.

The Chi Latte section is the best writing in the book so far, and it's all interiority. The "floozies" in her mother's voice, and then that long beat of looking that the word doesn't account for — the sprinter's stomach, the low waistband — and the thought she has to shut down with her mother's voice, "she had more than they had, and better, and she was the one covered to the collarbone, and she did not look at either fact straight." That's Vee in one paragraph: hunger and cover, hunger AND cover, both real at once. The cardigan as armor she doesn't let the room know is a choice. This is exactly the explicit-with-clothes-on interiority I'm here for.

The transcript-blank paragraph might be the most quietly devastating thing the book has done. "The leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed." That's what a crush on a charismatic person actually feels like — you come away drunk on the feeling with nothing you can repeat. And it's *engineered*: Randi'sKentucky accent hiding under "esses that run a half-beat long" — Vee has invented a Swiss finishing school for a girl from Kentucky. The reader knows the accent is Kentucky because we've been inside it. Vee builds her a better origin story because the truth — that Randi is performing — is unthinkable.

Cassie. I loved Cassie in forty lines. The flat "Cassie," the declined invitation, and "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, flat and finished. That's the book doing something: Cassie is the one person in Vee's life reading the situation correctly and she can't articulate it, so it comes out as a wrongness Vee smooths over. "It's coffee, weirdo." The one honest voice in the chapter, dismissed as weird. I want Cassie around; I want her to be right about whatever she senses.

Also the blurb's lunches are arriving. Randi listening and "drawing her further open" — the transcript being blank isn't a flaw of the prose, it's the trap: Randi fills the space with warmth and Vee keeps nothing checkable. Nothing to tell Pace. Nothing to hold Randi to. Just the feeling.

No heat at all this chapter and I didn't miss it. This is the counterweight chapter and it's the third quiet one by heat count, but it's not quiet in the way I'd skim — it's the first time we're inside the target, and the whole thing is charged with everything I know. My skin was crawling pleasantly the entire read.
