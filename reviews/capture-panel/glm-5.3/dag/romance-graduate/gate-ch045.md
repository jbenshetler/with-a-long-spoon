REACTION — Oh, this chapter. This is the one I've been braced for since the porch and the cracker-meal sentence, and it still hit me harder than I was ready for, because the book did the cruelest truest thing: it let her be *right* and let him be *sincere* at the same time, and those two facts collided in a doorway and she walked out into the cold.

"Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me." I actually put the Kindle down. That line is going to live in my chest for days. It's the whole book in one sentence — because he's not wrong that words are cheap, he's a man whose entire language IS the doing, the heat turned up hours before she arrives, the wine poured before she knocks, the drawer, the lamp, the flannel. He has never once spent a word where a gesture would do. And she's not wrong that the doing has a ceiling: you cannot hold a warmed house up to your father. You cannot introduce a perfectly calibrated thermostat to Cassie. The doing lives inside the door and the door was her whole complaint.

What killed me is that his answer — "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?" — is SO him, and so good, and so completely beside the point, and the book knows it, and Vee knows it: "she hated that he'd made the true thing pull against the point." That's the most psychologically exact line in a chapter full of them. That's every smart man's move, isn't it — answer the question you can win instead of the one she asked. And he does it out of love, which is what makes it unbearable.

And then the ending. She won't take the hand. "Not the hand in place of the word." Three words and I felt my own throat close, because she has taken that hand a hundred times — after the spanking, after the fight at the dance, after every time the wanting pooled faster than the naming. This is the first time she's ever refused the currency he pays in. Whatever else happens in this series, that refusal is the hinge. She walked out into the cold and I sat here with my heart going, proud of her and terrified for her in exactly equal measure, because he was standing in that doorway reaching and she's the one who left — and I still don't know if the wall is a wall or a locked room with Daphne and "I don't go back" behind it. The stillness, the arms folding, "holding a line at cost" — that's not a man who doesn't love her. That's a man who cannot, for some reason I don't have yet, spend the word. And I need to know why more than I've needed anything from this book.

Also, small devastating thing: "her name was not the word she came for." He said "Vee." He had one word available and it wasn't the one she needed. God.
