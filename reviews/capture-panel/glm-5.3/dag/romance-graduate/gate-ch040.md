REACTION — This is the kind of chapter I'd normally clock as a stall and yet I read every line of it. Nothing happens: Vee swims seventy-five minutes, thinks about Cassie's question, gets out, thinks about a sandwich. That's it. And it's the loneliest and maybe the truest chapter in the book so far, because it's the first time Vee's been completely alone with herself since the whole thing began — no Randi running the interview, no Pace's attention making a room of the hours, no Cassie holding the door. Just her body doing the one thing it's known how to do since she was twelve, the thing that predates all of them, and it turns out her body has an opinion about *do you love him* even when it's tired of having opinions: it doesn't. Down here it just works. "Nobody was counting but her and nothing was riding on it" — after thirty-nine chapters of everyone version-controlling this girl, of her clothes and her polish and her confessions all being homework somebody assigned, a lane with a black stripe in it is the only unsupervised room she has. That landed hard.

The four strokes where she can't tell Pace from somebody she built out of wanting him — that's the book being honest with me in a way I didn't expect it to be. Two months. She's losing weight, her bras are riding up, she's moved into his drawer, she said *maybe* to a question the answer to was sitting plainly in her. And she knows the *maybe* was a lie and doesn't know why she told it. I know why. I've been that girl. You say *maybe* because *yes* is a door and every other door this spring someone else has held open for you, but that one you'd have to walk through on nerve alone, and there's no Randi to dress you for it. That's the first genuinely unarranged threshold in the book and her body — the one thing the book has never let anyone else run — flinched. And the flinch happened in the water, alone, where nobody could harvest it for a Friday-night telling.

I noticed the structure, and I'm choosing not to resent it: the hymn line, "the second verse of a hymn — you were just already in it," right after we've spent chapters on induction and ceremony. The pool as the one ritual nobody built for her. Fine. It earned it because the prose carried the repetition the way laps actually feel — *Do you love him. The wall, and the roll* — and because it used the count (sixty-one, sixty-two) instead of explaining what the count was doing. Nobody explained the subtext to me. That's the thing I keep getting thanked for by this book: it trusts me.

But let me be honest about the ledger. Chapter 39 was Cassie in the bean bag — warm, quiet, no heat. Chapter 40 is quieter and no heat, and my fingers were looking for dialogue by the back half. The swimming is beautiful but I came to this book for what happens between these people, and I've now had two chapters in a row where nobody touches anybody. The last charged beat I got was the hallway in the heels, which feels like a month ago even though it isn't. I'm not stopping — the *maybe* is a live wire and I need to see whether she ever says the word to anybody but a sleeping man's ear — but if chapter 41 gives me another solo meditation I'm going to start wondering if Helen Rivers is resting on how much goodwill she's banked.

Also, small thing, the kind I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "her arms feeling like they belonged to somebody generous." That's the sentence of the chapter. That's what Pace's attention has done to her — it's made her own limbs feel like gifts. She's only generous to herself in the water, and only because he made her feel worth spending. I don't know if the book knows it said that. I think it does.
