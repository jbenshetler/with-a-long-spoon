REACTION:

The drawer. The drawer, the drawer. I've read a thousand "he clears space in his closet for her" scenes and every single one of them TELLS you he's committing — he says something, she cries, the narrator underlines it. This book does it with furniture and then shuts up. He folded his whole ordered self down into the bottom drawer — a man who kept one comb in ranks, who squares his razor to the edge — and gave her the top one, the one your hand goes to first, and never said a word. And the beautiful, exact thing is that Vee panics FIRST. The bare counter reads as rejection for one full second — "a guest's things go once the guest had been too much" — because that's where her whole life has trained her to go. The book keeps doing this: giving her the evidence of love and letting her almost not recognize it. That's not a trope. That's a person.

And the first half — "Peter," said into his sleeping ear, like a word you say to keep it — I actually put the Kindle down. She names it in her own chest where no one can take it back, and she decides she can live inside the everything-he-does without the saying. And I'm sitting there going: sweetheart, no, the saying matters, and also — I know this book — the fact that she's settled this comfortably is exactly the beat right before something unscrews it. The jacket said "every yes was freely given — that was the problem," and her happy little treaty with the unsaid I-love-you feels like one more yes she's giving away without a fight.

Also noticed, with my audit-brain fully awake: the eating. She's eating like a field hand, losing weight, burning him off — and the book frames it as devotion, but there's a ledger being kept somewhere, I can feel it. And the counter moment in part ii — his eyes moving down to her sprawl, holding there, doing nothing. Two chapters ago I'd have read that as pure tenderness. After the bathroom at brunch — the glaze on her lip she never touched — I don't trust anything warm in this book to be only one thing anymore. That's why it's got me.
