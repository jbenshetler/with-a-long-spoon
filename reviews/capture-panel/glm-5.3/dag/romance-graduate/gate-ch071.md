REACTION — Okay. I had to put the phone down after the Sunday phone call. Vee on the line, hungover and small, thanking Randi — "I don't know what I'd do without her" — while Randi is literally under the green sheets erasing herself off him, and Pace lying there "in the middle of the two of them and let it happen." That's the most devastating image this book has produced, and it produced the gesso chapter a few chapters ago. The symmetry of the two cleanings — she washes herself off him with her mouth, he runs the Speed Wash on the sheets — both of them scrubbing the night out of existence before Vee reaches the door, both of them doing it *for* her, and the whole thing is the tenderest betrayal I've read in years. Nobody in this book can love anybody cleanly. They all love in the dark and launder it by morning.

And I've been waiting for a Pace POV chapter since the blurb promised me the third game, and it delivered exactly what I was afraid it would: he reads Randi "in the body and the present tense" — that's his whole worldview in one clause, the man who trusts only what's in front of him — and she *played him*. The fake hour. The invented clock. He caught it, named it — "he knew he'd been managed" — and set it down anyway, because she was warm on him and the fear had nowhere to live. Which is exactly what Vee does with every lie she doesn't tell him. These three people are going to destroy each other so carefully.

The middle of this chapter is peak Helen Rivers: the clamps, the chain as a rein, her shuffled on her knees across green sheets Vee chose — that detail, Vee's green under Randi's body, I gasped. And "She's perfect. For your first woman." I actually said oh my god out loud. Because it's what Randi asked for — "tell me she's better than me" — and he gave her the truth wrapped in the cruelty she needed, and it worked, she went over "all at once." He paid her in the exact currency she came to collect and he knew it going in. Hot as hell, and I feel icky about how hot I found it, which I think is the point of this entire series.

What scared me: Randi lying flat-out. "Vee's been delayed. You have an hour." That's not their usual architecture — everything between them before ran on permission and words ("Red," handed over "like a thing she had no intention of needing"). She manufactured stakes with a lie, and the book didn't underline it, it just let me sit there with it while she sobbed on his chest with no performance in it. That sob is the realest she's been since "I want her to know exactly what she does to me." And the grinning "I have secrets of my own" after — she told Vee everything over mimosas, Vee pink to the ears, "she loves you like a house on fire." So VEE TOLD RANDI before she told Pace. The confession happened off-page, at brunch, to the wrong person. And Randi came straight out here with the information and used it. I'm on fire.

The one wobble: I wanted a beat more from Pace when he realized the hour was fake. "He turned it over once and set it down" — I get the character, I do, but the man who collars five-pound plates didn't get one full sentence of reckoning with being lied to? Actually — no, that's exactly right. That's who he is. He read her, he gave her what she came for, and the crying afterward was the payment she didn't know she'd priced. Fine. I retract my wobble. I'm just… wary of him now in a way I wasn't at the start of the chapter, because we're inside his head and he *enjoys* being told, he "found he liked being told," and I don't know yet what that's a door to.

Vee is coming out tonight with a surprise and Don't make plans. Sunday night, clean sheets, no idea. If the book doesn't put her in that bed with some part of her knowing, I'll eat my Kindle.
