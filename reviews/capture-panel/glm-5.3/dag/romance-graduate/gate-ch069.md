Okay. I've been side-eyeing the last stretch for Randi content — I know, I know, the girl needs a breather and Vee needs to pay for Saturday — but I was braced for a cooldown chapter and instead I got one of the most quietly devastating chapters in the book, and it did it with zero plot. Just a gym, a jar of yellow paste, and a man being exactly who he is.

The gym section *worked* on me and I want to be honest about why. It's the same trick this book keeps pulling — the charge isn't the choreography, it's the attention. "He set her under it as though it mattered... That the smallness of what she was doing bought her back none of his care." I felt that line in my sternum. Every heroine I've read for a decade gets tossed a bar with a paternal chuckle; Vee gets collared plates and a full spotting brief like she's loading for a meet. And Vee's inside-my-head experience of it — the private knowing about his shorts, the laughing-while-pressing — that's her interiority doing exactly what I'm paying for.

Then the accident. And this is where the book earns its keep twice over, because what it actually gave me was a study of Pace under three different loads: competence under his (he never lifted what she couldn't save him from — I actually gasped, I missed it the first read, that detail where you "only then" see what he'd done), fury on her behalf delivered at room volume, and then — the one that undid me — "We're done," easy, about the workout and not about her. He knew she was hurt before she did, and he let her find it out on the drive instead of telling her. That's a man who refuses to be right at her. The apology scene, the boys looking to *him* for release and not her — chilling and right, and it says something about him I'm filing next to the Daphne drawer rather than on top of it.

But the balm scene is the chapter. And look — denial is a dime a dozen in my KU library, it's usually authored by a man doing a bit. This wasn't a bit. Her neck took the finish off the table before anyone decided anything, and then she DEMANDED the fire anyway — "Yes," twice, before he'd asked — and he made her say it out loud because his body alone would never be enough evidence for him, and then the burn arrived in her *own grammar of escalation*: warmth, heat, burn, the fire under the fire, mapped in slow motion from throat to collarbone to breast to tip. I read that sequence with my jaw set. She could read her future off her own skin and she kept her hands pressed over his. That's the whole book in miniature — every yes freely given, given with the cost in full view — and the fact that this exact chapter comes right after she *lied* to him ("No. Nothing.") about Randi... I don't think that's an accident. He took her yeses seriously tonight; she's keeping a secret from the man who waits on the word. That knife is going to come for me later and I know it.

The "we" when he said "we need to wait." The kiss withdrawn because turning her head would hurt her. The stillness of his hard-on against her spine — "the stillness he made of his own wanting was worse than any taking would have been." God. And "some furious part of her hated him for the gentleness of it, for knowing to the exact degree how much to leave her" — yes, that's the frustration the chapter means to produce, and it landed, and it's a *good* frustration, the kind you carry to Tuesday.

Also, small alarmed note: she's limping toward Randi-dodge again — lied to Cassie, turned the goodnight kiss away, and then her body overruled her and booked the brunch anyway. The girl cannot stop scheduling her own ambushes. Saturday is armed and I'm terrified of it. The taste thread is still ticking too — I noticed she's still not connecting dots, and I'm still holding the Randi-Pace bed for all three of them.

One knock: the gym-culture detail with the uncollared boys tipping the plate was a touch convenient as an accident-delivery device, and "you want it in the butt" as the smack that causes it was a little broad. But it happened fast enough that I forgave it by the end of the drive.
