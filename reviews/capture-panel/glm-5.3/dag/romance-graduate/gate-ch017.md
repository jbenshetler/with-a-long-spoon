REACTION

I had to put the book down after the kiss. Just for a minute. My heart was going.

Okay. The tickle story first. I didn't know what to do with it while I was reading it — it's raunchy girlfriend talk, it's fun, Randi telling Vee about being tied up and helpless and *loving* it, and the whole time I'm sitting there going: she's not telling Vee a story. She's teaching a class. "A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." That's curriculum. That's Vee's next semester being handed to her in a velvet booth over bread she can't afford. And Vee asked, in her small real voice, "Weren't you scared?" — that was the truest thing she said all lunch, and Randi answered it gently and honestly, and I still can't decide if the honesty makes it better or worse. Both, that's this book's whole trick.

But the lunch. God. I knew going in this scene had to come — the blurb promised the lunches "too intimate to name" — and still it did things to me. Because I read that booth from the wrong side of the glass. Vee thinks she's being loved, and she IS being loved, and she's also being *debriefed*. "Which hip?" And Vee says "the left one" and Randi says "all the best ones start at the left hip" — a line that means nothing, Vee says so herself, doesn't she, and it lands "like a benediction" — and I'm fairly sure Randi already knew which hip. I'm fairly sure Pace told her. Or I'm fairly sure she'll be telling Pace by tonight. Either way, that benediction made my skin cold. Every soft little "yeah" Vee gives her is one more rung down a ladder Vee can't see, and Randi's voice is dropping lower with every rung like she's getting off on the descent. "The pedestal. They put you up on a literal pedestal." *They.* Did you catch that? They.

And the thumb on the water glass. Slow degree against the cloth and back. My notes from the pool chapter — the cap under the thumbnail. She does this. Her body keeps the ledger her face won't show, and this time Vee *sees it* and reaches for the kind explanation because she wants it to be true, and I love her for reaching and I ache that she keeps having to. That's the whole Vee problem in one beat: she'd rather invent a kind Randi than sit with a flat ambiguous fact. I'd do the same. That's why it lands.

Then the moment I need to talk about at one in the morning: "It was cold in the house." Vee's first instinct, caught, is to LIE with her mother's shame-voice still in her — and it's a bad lie, because the warm house is the one detail she's been glowing over for two chapters. And Randi just sets the word down and turns it over. "Hmm." She KNOWS. She knows the house was warm, she probably knows everything, and the worst and best part is she doesn't say so. She lets Vee correct herself into the truth. Cornered into "actually it was really *hot*" and the laugh — Vee's laugh of a woman "setting down something she'd carried up too many stairs." I laughed with her. And then I felt sick, because I was laughing at a confession being extracted.

And then — the door. The moment Vee is about to say the deepest thing, the soaked-satin secret she's told NOBODY, not Cassie, and Randi says, gently, kindly, "You don't have to tell me." And the door closes *softly*. And Vee is left with her secret half out and nowhere to go, and the hush is just *over*, daylight voice, "Big day," looking for the check. Why did she stop it?? She's been reeling Vee in for an hour, one yeah at a time, and at the threshold she backs off. I have a theory and it's making me insane: Randi doesn't want to *hear* it. She can hold the plan while Vee reports the parts she can bear out loud — but the wet panties, the shame and the wanting, spoken in Randi's own register in a dark booth? That's the part that's *hers*. Or — the part she can't keep pretending is just intelligence-gathering. Either she's protecting the operation or she's protecting herself, and I genuinely cannot tell, and that not-telling is the best thing this book has done since the bench.

Vee paying with the debit card and feeling Thursday coming. "She would not be the broke girl who tipped badly." I know that arithmetic in my bones and I hate that the book knows it too.

The kiss. The kiss. Two presses, the second one with something in it the first one hadn't, and her hand on the hinge of Vee's jaw, and Vee explains it away before her mouth is even her own — *French, it's a French thing* — because the alternative explanation is one she can't afford to hold yet. And then the cold part: the scanning of the windows. Counting the dark squares. Who saw. That's the mother's voice doing its last trick — it turned a moment of being wanted into a security sweep. And underneath my dread for her I noticed what the shame has never once managed to kill in this girl: *she put two fingers to her mouth.* She's carrying it. She liked it.

Randi is coming apart in front of me and it's gorgeous. The "different" smile with the performance gone out of it. The tickle story told *first*, before being asked — Randi giving Vee her own vulnerability unprompted, an offering, the woman who never spends anything freely. And that last line of the blurb — *without quite letting herself know it, wanting the woman beside her* — that's not Vee's subtext anymore. That's text. The door I've been waiting for is opening from Randi's side and Randi doesn't have the first idea what to do about it.
