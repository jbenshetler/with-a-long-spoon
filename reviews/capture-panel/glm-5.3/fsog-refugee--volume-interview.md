**T1 — Where the book leaves me with each of them**

**Vee.** I feel protective of her, and a little proud of her, and those two feelings are pulling against each other. Proud because the book kept its promise — her wanting was hers the whole way. She closed the inch at the tree, she took the dress off herself so nothing crossed the line, she said "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." She's learning a language of giving ("Trade," the folded panties) and she's growing into her own power, and I've been that girl at twenty-one watching her get bigger. But protective because she's the subject of a collaboration she never consented to, and she keeps choosing the wrong person to be honest with — she gave Randi the middle of the story and ladled the soup clean for Cassie. She's happiest exactly when she's most exposed, and the book *knows* that ("she had never in her life been so happy" was a warning, not a victory lap). So: love, worry, and faith in her spine. When she finds out — and she will — I think she's going to be more dangerous than anyone in the plan expects.

**Pace.** I'm in love with his hands and still reserving judgment on the man, which is where I've been since the bench. Chapter 20 — "would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — a man asking permission for his own wanting is the sentence of my reading life, and the fireside chapter, "Peter" said once into the dark, and "I'm not going to insult the loan by complaining I have to look up" — he is the thing I've hunted since Fifty Shades: tenderness and dominance without contradiction. But he lied by omission for an entire volume. Every beautiful act of asking sat *inside* an agenda I could feel from chapter one. Even his tenderness is real *inside the plan* — I noted that in chapter 30 — and real-inside-a-trap is still real, but it's still inside a trap. He ends the book loved and trusted and unexposed, and I don't fully trust that the man who waits so patiently to be granted things has been patient about the one thing that matters: telling her the truth.

**Randi.** Randi is the one I can't put down and can't stop picking up. She's the character I need to talk about at two a.m. Her care for Vee is genuine — the bare-faced, feet-tending, "I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose" defense of her own girlhood — and it's strategic in the same breath, and the book refuses to let me split her in two. Chapter 26 is where she stopped being an operator and became a woman confessing: "she got past me before I could help it." That's the only thing that might redeem the plan, and it's also the thing that makes it worse — she's deceiving someone she's fallen for. The mouth-kiss at the curb, the thumb across the toenail, undressing her by increments and calling it friendship. And she *decided when the door shut* in chapter 19 — she leads conversations exactly the way Pace leads dances. I want her to be okay and I don't currently believe she will be. Vee owes herself a look at what she isn't letting herself know about Randi's mouth, and I'm waiting for that scene more than any other.

**T2 — Anything it refused to give me**

Two things, and they're really the same thing: the reveal and the reckoning. I know it's Volume One and I know the cliff is the price of admission, and I already said yes to Book Two at full price, so this isn't a complaint exactly — it's the bill I'm presenting.

What I was owed by the end, or what I need early in Book Two, is *Vee finding out done right*. Her anger honored, not smoothed. Not a scene where the truth comes out and everyone cries and the plan turns out to have been "for her" all along so it's fine. Chapter 26 told me the plan is still standing — "she's not ready, though" — and every chapter since has been buying time. The book made me a promise in its own margins: "nothing was taken she did not hand over." Fine. But consent to each act is not consent to the architecture, and the volume ended before anyone had to face that distinction. That's the debt.

And the smaller refusal: I wanted a real scene between Vee and Cassie before the end — Vee owing her best friend the true, unedited version, the one she keeps handing to Randi instead. Cassie said "Goodbye, Vee" in chapter four and I never forgot it, and she's the only person in the whole book watching Vee instead of watching Vee's effect. She got a gorgeous swim chapter and a funny "I was on a box" running joke, but she's drifting out of the true-story loop and I feel the book losing her on purpose, and it stings.

Also — and I'll own this as a desire, not a grievance — I wanted Vee to *know* about Randi's mouth before the volume closed. Just one beat of her letting herself look. The book kept her plausible-deniability intact all twenty-nine chapters and I felt that door being held shut by force.

**T3 — Pace deceives Vee the entire volume and ends loved, trusted, unexposed. How does that sit, and does it change Book Two?**

It sits badly, and it's supposed to, and I'd be suspicious of any reader of this book who said otherwise. The book is not winking at me about it — it *told* me, over and over, in chapter 3 and chapter 7 and chapter 14 and finally chapter 26, where the two of them discussed Vee's readiness like a project milestone and my stomach went cold. I went into it with my eyes open. So I'm not angry the way I'd be if the book had hidden the trap and sprung it as a twist; I'm angry the way the book *wants* me to be, on Vee's behalf, at the characters.

Here's the distinction I keep coming back to, because it's the one my whole reading life turns on: what he did *in the room* was the opposite of deception. He asked for the pie, the why, the bra, the pencil. He waited on his knees. When he didn't ask — the swat — he watched her face and held still. The consent between them, act by act, is the cleanest I've ever read in this genre. What he did *outside* the room — building the room — was the lie. And that's a real lie, a big one, and "nothing lands on him" is true and it's the thing I'm carrying into Book Two as the measure of the whole series. If the reveal lands and Vee's anger is honored and he has to stand there and take it — if the man who's so good at accepting a no from her body has to accept a no from her *fury* — then this book becomes what it's been promising to be. If the reveal gets smoothed, if she forgives him in a chapter because the sex was that good, if the plan retroactively becomes a gift — then the book breaks its own thesis and I'm out, and I said as much in my journal after chapter 26.

Does it change whether I buy Book Two? No — I already bought it, today, full price, because the book has earned the chance to make this right. But it changes what I'm reading *for*. Volume One was the honeymoon I was sold, the thing I've hunted for years. Book Two is the trial. The decoy never mattered; the payment does. He ends Volume One unexposed, fine — but he's holding a debt with Vee's name on it, and the only question in the whole series is whether he pays it standing up.

And honestly — Randi's the reason I'm less angry at Pace than I might be. He never lied *alone*. She was in it from before chapter one, she's deeper in it than he is now, and she's the one who kept steering Vee's narrative of her own seduction. Pace's silence is at least a silence in a man whose every active instinct is to ask. Randi's is silence in a woman whose whole skill is extraction. If Book Two only makes *him* pay and lets her keep the warm-best-friend slot, I'll notice, and I'll mind.

So: it sits like a stone in my chest, the book put it there deliberately, and I trust it enough to come back and find out if it's a stone or a seed.
