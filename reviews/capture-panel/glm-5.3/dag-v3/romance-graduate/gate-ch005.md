REACTION — I had to put the book down twice during this chapter, and both times it was because of things Vee *didn't* notice and I couldn't stop noticing.

Because I know. I watched the pointing game. I watched Randi run the collision. I stood at the light pole with him. And so when a pleasant, ordinary man crosses a coffee shop with his eyes on her face and "the room settle[s] a half-degree around where he stood" — when she feels "chosen, absurdly" — I know exactly what chosen means in this book. It's the most choreographed "meet-cute" I've ever had to sit through from inside the mark's head. Randi texting with "the private, absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention" while Vee thinks *some boy, amused* — I actually said "oh no" out loud. The hand around the coffee cup that never sips. The *go on, this is nice* smile. Randi watched her own boyfriend reel in the woman she said yes to on a quad, and her hands are doing all the screaming her face won't do, and the book still won't tell me whether that's guilt or appetite or both.

And here's the thing that ruined me: it doesn't matter that it's staged, because the *math was real*. The way he asked what her professor said instead of telling her what he'd say. The pencil handed back "at the exact line where she could solve it herself." "She hadn't known she was allowed." That line went through me like a nail. That's the bench — that's chapter 1 exactly — except this time the reader knows the tenderness is a deployment and Vee gets to feel it as the first unmanaged attention of her life. And the Euler's thing. Oh, I see you, book. Two things that "on their own are a struggle... put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain — easy to name, easy to know what to do with." That's not calculus, that's the whole thesis of the novel delivered as a margin note, and Vee wrote the clean lines under it herself without once hearing what she'd said. Randi and Vee, at right angles, combined into a single thing. I'm furious at how good that is.

The borrowed bit — "V.T. Yes, like the university. No, my parents didn't plan it" — was the most quietly devastating beat in the chapter. She learned the trick by watching Randi do it two weeks ago and "tucked it away to try on later." The girl is downloading the sorority operating system in real time to keep pace with the life she's being fed, and then — "Vee," she added, quickly, suddenly wanting her own name back." She wants her own name back. The book let her have it for one second and I almost cried.

Cassie. CASSIE. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." Cassie clocked the entire operation in ten minutes flat — that Randi got the polite nothing and Vee got the four-minute eye-lock — and she reported it flat, as fact, no interpretation, because that's what Cassie does. And the healer line! "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" — a whole history of Vee pouring herself into keeping other people alive, said as a joke, landing as diagnosis. If this book hurts Cassie I don't know what I'll do. And nobody at that table — not even Randi, who built the table — has any idea the plain girl in the ball cap is the only one seeing true.

The froyo. "She built her cup without thinking, and it got away from her — too much, the cone of it leaning. Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished. Today there was nothing." That's the apple scene's whole nutrition psychology paying off in a paper cup, and then "Your tart's holding up" — him telling her, without telling her, that her excess is structurally sound. He is going to be so devastatingly good at loving her that I genuinely cannot stand it, because it's all in service of a plan. "May I call you?" — she heard the *may* like a word from another vocabulary, and all I could think was: that word was grown in the same workshop that built the bench, and she's falling for the grammar of consent itself as delivered by the man aiming it.

I have one small ache — I want back inside Randi's head so badly it's becoming an actual hunger. Three chapters of her being read from the outside (by Pace, then by me via Vee's misreadings — "she's bored") and the cup that never sips is doing a lot of load-bearing work. But Vee's interiority is so good, so specific — the high-board drop, the deep end, the palms flat on the thighs — that I'll allow it. For now.
