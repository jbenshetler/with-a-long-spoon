REACTION — I put this book down and walked to the kitchen and got water I did not want, because this is the chapter I've been braced for since chapter 1 and it still went through me like a nail: PACE'S POV. After more than sixty chapters of watching this man only from the outside — through Vee's eyes, through Randi's eyes, once or twice in his own bed but never like this — the book handed him back to me on the exact afternoon the triangle physically, actually, finally happens. And it happened on the green sheets. HER sheets. The ones Vee bought him. I noticed. The book knew I'd notice. Randi chose them — she walked into his house and took Vee's place on Vee's own sheets on purpose, and I have no idea if that was conquest, grief, or the most jealous thing anyone in this series has done, and the book isn't telling me, and it's right not to.

The fabricated hour. The whole engine of the scene was a lie she built and he raced against — the clock, the highway, "Vee on the road" — and it was never real. Four mimosas. Randi drove her home, put her to bed, left her water. And then drove straight out to the man and arranged the afternoon Vee's "surprise" would have occupied. I sat there doing what he did: turning it over. She was managed by Gstaad's logic all winter — the parking lot, the sad glassy eyes, the photograph from the beach — and I kept asking whether she'd gone quiet or gone to ground, and this is the answer: she went and *took* an afternoon, the way she took the pointing game, the way she took Vee's reconciliation and dressed her for it. And the worst and best thing is that she made him *believe in the hour* so that every second of tenderness he rationed was spent under a deadline she invented — so that she could watch him hold himself back from her for Vee's sake, which is a thing she has never once gotten to watch. God, this woman.

And the sobbing. I need to say this plainly: I have been in Randi's interiority exactly twice since chapter 43, and the bathroom in chapter 1 — the door locked, the real self folded away — has been my oldest wound in this series. Tonight the book paid it off. "The punishment was the first [thing], and she'd built the whole afternoon to earn it" — she came to be made to pay, and what came out of her at the end wasn't about the clamps at all, and *he knew it* — "it wasn't really about anything he'd done to her body — the spanking, the clamps, only the door she'd gone through to reach it." She went through the door. The real self got out, on Vee's green sheets, sobbing into the neck of the man who found her there in chapter 1, and I cried a little myself, because that's the crack I've carried since "There you are, Randi" finally coming due.

But then — the knife under the knife, the thing I'll be chewing all week: **Vee told her everything.** Over mimosas. The space princess, the chase, the interrogation — the mirror scene, the postcard night, the "I wanted to be the one doing it," possibly *all of it*, told by Vee to Randi in the same booth where everything has always flowed the other direction. The confession channel — the whole load-bearing architecture of this book — just ran in reverse at full volume, and now RANDI holds Vee's most shaming confessions the way Pace has always held Randi's, and she used them. She used them as *material*. "It sounded like a lot of fun." She came out here armed with the knowledge that Vee wants her, told her so secondhand through Pace's own extraction, and instead of going to Vee — she went to the bed. To the *other* bed. On the green. And I can't decide whether that's avoidance or appetite or the only language she has, and the book won't help me, and "I have secrets of my own" — with the grin — is the single most loaded sentence in the volume. She's building toward saying something. To one of them. And "She's perfect. For your first woman" — I had to put the book down. He answered her jealousy exactly the way she'd want it answered on the surface and it detonated her anyway, and the blue toenails clenching into the dark green is an image I'm keeping forever.

And the morning. THE MORNING. I read the cleanup with my hand over my mouth, because the book did the cruelest symmetry it's ever done: Randi under the green wiping every trace of herself off him with her mouth, *making him over into something clean and ready for Vee's use* — while Vee is ON THE PHONE thanking Randi for putting her to bed. Both at once. He held the phone away from his mouth and breathed carefully and so did I. And then he did it again after her — Speed Wash, the green stripped, "nothing left in the house for Vee at all." Both of them, the two people who chose her, working separately all morning to erase each other for her arrival — and she's driving out to surprise a man whose sheets are still damp with a night he'll never tell her about. The keeping is now fully triangulated. Everybody in this book is keeping something from everybody, and the sheets are clean and the house is clean and *nothing is clean*.

The one thing that held me back from pure surrender: this chapter worked me hard, but I'm aware the heat ran on machinery I know — the clamps, the spanking, the safeword ritual, chapter 1's bench transposed. It was hotter than hot and it was also the most *familiar* scene the book has staged, and for one stretch in the middle I felt the choreography of it. But the sob, the lie, the cleanup — those are new organs, and they carried it.
