REACTION

Well. That was the long con from the inside, and it was almost worse — better? — than I was braced for. Because I've *been* Vee in that coffee shop. Not literally, but the whole architecture of her wanting — the joke she'd stood outside of for two years and suddenly gets to walk under, the "best hour she'd had in months" whose transcript is blank because she was keeping the *feeling* and not the words — that's the most exact portrait of being charmed by somebody out of your league I've read in years. And the book knows exactly what it's doing letting me sit there in the warm glow with her while I know, from chapter three, that every bit of it was aimed. The collision that "genuinely was not clear." Randi coming in the back door of the lecture hall so the entrance would land. The "I've decided." I watched the machine work and I *still* felt the pull of it through Vee's eyes, and that's the trick of the whole book so far: the manipulation is real AND the warmth is real, and I can't split them, and neither can she.

The details that got me. The Chi Latte sign — Χ ΛΑΤΕ — resolving only "because she had the sound in her ear now from Randi's mouth." Being let in on the joke *by* the girl is the whole seduction in one image. And the room-measuring — "floozies" arriving whole in her mother's voice, and then the longer look than the word accounted for, and then the cardigan staying buttoned. Oh, Vee. Vee has a mother in her head with opinions about bodies, and she has *more* than the room is selling and covers it to the collarbone, and the book just quietly puts that on the table next to the laughing-with-her-throat-bare thing from last chapter. She's Randi's opposite number: Randi built the face to control what's seen; Vee covers what's seeable and has never once been looked *past*. I'm genuinely afraid of what happens when Pace's particular attention aims at her, because Vee doesn't have Randi's defenses. She doesn't have any defenses. She has a cardigan.

Cassie. I need to talk about Cassie, because "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, flat and finished — and the thing that passed over her face that Vee "would not have been able to name and did not try to" — and then the chapter's title turns out to be Randi's exit line, *see you later*, held up right next to Cassie's *goodbye*. The book is rhyming them on purpose. And I don't think it's as simple as "Cassie senses something." It might be — the flat-declining girl who takes no bit's open door is the one person in the chapter whose warmth isn't for sale — but there was something in the "something passed over her face" that read almost like grief. Like she knows she's losing Vee already. I want more Cassie, and I'm mildly terrified of how the book will use her, because right now she's the only person in Vee's life operating without an angle.

The one craft thing I'll allow myself: Randi's accent. Vee hears esses a half-beat long and arches the a's and files it as "Switzerland, Paris, a mother with a different alphabet." I heard Kentucky in chapters one and two. Randi's daytime voice for a *non-sorority* mark isn't Kentucky and isn't sorority-bright — it's a third register, built, aimed. Vee thinks it means Randi came from further off; I think it means Randi came from exactly here and has a costume for every audience. If the book knows I'll catch that, good. If it doesn't, even better, because it caught me anyway.

Heat: none, and this is the second low-heat chapter out of four, but it's not the stalled kind — it's load-bearing, the whole blurb's promise ("she doesn't know there's a plan") just snapped into focus from the inside. The tension of the coffee shop, watching a girl fall for a person who doesn't exist, is doing the same work a scene does. I'm in. But the next chapter needs a hand on skin or a clock ticking faster; I've now spent two chapters watching Vee be handled at arm's length, and I know what this book can do up close.
