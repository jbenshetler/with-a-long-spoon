REACTION

So *that's* where Vee comes from. Not from a scheme hatched in bed over a calendar — from a girl walking out of a dining hall with her head thrown back laughing, and Randi stopping mid-stride and saying one bare word. The blurb told me "a game Randi and Pace started in bed," and the book went and made the game real instead: it started with a question mid-fuck ("would you like to taste another woman?") and she came so hard it shocked her, and I sat there genuinely unsure for a second whether the second asking — the calm one, in the afterglow — would go the same way. The flinch when the question left the bedroom and became *a question*. He felt it "rather than saw it." This book keeps doing that to me: the body confessing one chapter before the face can file the paperwork.

And then the threesome line. "Only in a threesome. With you." I actually put the book down for a second, because I know that move — I've *made* that move, okay, years ago, and watching Pace clock it in real time ("she had made herself not a woman who hungered for another woman, which he could see was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy") was like having my own old tell read back to me. He sees through it and doesn't humiliate her with the seeing. He says "Okay," easy, and takes her to go get a sandwich. That's the same discipline as not knocking on the bathroom door, and I'm starting to understand it's the spine of the whole thing.

The pointing game itself — the walk — is the hottest thing in the book so far and nobody so much as unzipped anything. Her taste being revealed by elimination: not the mirrors, not the sealed pairs, "God, no" at the girl who runs the room, and *maybe* — the longest look — at the round-faced girl in the beanie taking up room on the library steps. And then Vee. I could hear the orchestra come in. All that hair, the laugh where she forgot she could be seen — of course. Of course the woman Randi can't look away from is the one who does the thing Randi has spent two chapters being unable to do. The book didn't say that once and I heard it the whole way down the walk.

The collision pickup was genuinely fun to watch — he reads the *oh-I'm-so-sorry* in her shoulders, Randi's hand staying a half-beat too long — and then the ending. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Low, into his chest, in the daylight, in public. My stomach dropped, in the good way. Because I've read chapter one. I know what the bench does. I know it showed Randi herself and she shoved it back in the closet, and now she's wondering — half as a joke, maybe, but not really — whether to hand that machine to a girl who laughs with her throat bare to the sky. And my suspicion from chapter 2 is halfway confirmed: this isn't just seduction, it's Randi aiming *being seen* at someone. I don't know yet whether that's a gift or a weapon or she doesn't know either, and that's the best thing about this book.

The one wobble: "he would answer it again" cadence is getting familiar, and Pace being right about everything — right about her taste, right to wait, right to stop walking and kiss her at exactly the "maybe" — edges toward the perfect-boyfriend thing I flagged. But the Vee ending buys him a lot of rope. Whatever he thinks he's doing, Vee isn't the redhead of his fantasy either. Something's going to go sideways of both of them. That's what I'm here for.
