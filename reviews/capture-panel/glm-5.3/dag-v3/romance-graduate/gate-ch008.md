REACTION — Okay, the third Vee-charmed chapter in a row happened, and the threads still haven't collided, and I'm *still* not setting the book down. I'm a little annoyed about that. But let me be honest about why it worked.

"May I choose." Three words, and my stomach dropped before Vee's did, because I've heard those words before — in a bedroom, on a Tuesday, when a man handed a woman a game and called it hers. The jacket's promise — "courtly and exact, he asks before he takes, until choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done" — landed right on schedule, and the book knows I know it's the same methodology. The choosing is erotic *for him too*. He didn't take the menu lightly; he weighed what he was choosing for her. That's the bench. That's the pointing game. He's doing to Vee exactly what he did to Randi, and I watched it happen with my stomach in my shoes while Vee experienced it as the most restful evening of her life. "She let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could." God. That sentence is the whole trap, isn't it. Randi sealed a bathroom because Pace taught her something she couldn't survive learning, and now he's teaching the same lesson to a girl whose only defense is the glow-filing — and she's *eating* it, literally, the hollow that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon," and I nearly had to put the book down at that line because it's her whole architecture in one clause.

The food scene is genuinely one of the best-written dinner scenes I've read in this genre — and I did notice the discipline of it: she didn't know the words, so each dish was "a closed door with something behind it." That's Vee's whole psychology done in menu form, and again, nobody said it out loud. Her hunger going down "into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" — that's the bench hunger, the apple hunger, and it's being fed by a man who knows exactly what he's feeding. My F5.1 fear just put on a nice shirt and held a door.

The kiss. Yes, I gasped, and yes, I liked it, and I liked *how* it was built — the rule coming out of her mouth reflexively, him not arguing, not leaning, not waiting her out, just *letting it close* — and her feet not going. Her making the exception herself. "Whatever came next was hers too." That's the real romance beat of the chapter and it earned the 3. But here's what chilled me while it was happening: *I can't tell anymore which parts of him are love and which are procedure.* The "he'd showered, the recently for this" — genuine. The leaving-the-last-inch — genuine tenderness, OR the exact technique that cracked Randi open, the withholding that makes the wanting do the work. The book still won't tell me which, and I still can't decide if that's its best quality or if I'm being played.

New suspicion I'm now carrying: **the right hand.** "Always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." The book hung that lantern deliberately — a pattern Vee noticed and couldn't read. What's the left hand *for*? I don't know if it means anything and I don't care — I'm now watching his hands for the rest of this series.

And one small dread: no Randi at all in this chapter. Not one breath. Which means she's offscreen with a Friday night and a wine glass and no report coming — and the first long lunch (my W4.5, still overdue!) just got *more* loaded, because now Vee has a first kiss to tell her about and I don't know if she will. The Cassie scene at the top bought my affection back completely — "That's not a boy. That's a man," said without teasing, because Cassie knows the difference too.
