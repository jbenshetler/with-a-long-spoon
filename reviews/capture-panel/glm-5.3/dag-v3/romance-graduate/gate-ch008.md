REACTION — Okay, I need to talk about the bread, because the bread is doing the thing this book does, which is take something you could get in any romance — the "he orders for her" scene, a trope I've read fifty times and mostly hated — and make it *load-bearing*.

Because I know what "May I choose?" is. I've known since chapter 1. It's the bench. It's "Tell me your word." It's "Don't answer tonight." It's his whole operating system: hand her the choice so deliberately, so ceremonially, that the choosing itself becomes the erotic act. And the book has the nerve to have Vee *feel* it as relief — "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would" — without once telling me I should be worried. It's the most seductive thing in the chapter and it's the most dangerous thing in the chapter and it's the *same sentence*. I keep saying this book won't tell me whether it knows, and chapters like this make me think it knows exactly, and it's just sitting there with its hands folded watching me fall for him too.

The hunger. God, the hunger. "It went down into the hollow and the hollow only opened wider... something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the apple from chapter 1 — that's Randi's portioned slices — transposed into Vee's key, and the difference is that Vee doesn't police it, Vee's hunger just *goes*. She ate and didn't watch herself eat "and she didn't notice she'd stopped." After seven chapters of the cardigan and the floozies and the mother's voice in her ear at every meal, that line landed in my chest. And Pace watching her do it without "seeming to think there was anything to notice" — that's the froyo cup again, "your tart's holding up," the man whose entire seduction method is *your excess is structurally sound*. I am so mad at how well this works on me.

And the drift. The whole middle where she stops doing "any of the things she normally do[es]" and loses the thread of his sentences and *lets it go and nothing bad happens* — I got a little emotional, honestly, because I know what that vigilance costs a girl like her, and the book rendered its absence as an actual sensory experience. The mint tea surfacing her back into the room like coming up from the pool. Chapter 7's underwater scene paid off in a teacup. This author is playing a long game with images and I'm keeping pace with a highlighter in my heart.

Small things I'm holding like contraband: the Arabic — I clocked it the second the words came out "soft, rounded, opening at the ends," because I've been sitting on S4.5, the accent with the esses half a beat long, and now I have TWO characters with foreign-schooled vowels, and the book did it so quietly I almost missed the rhyme. The "right hand again, a pattern now" — she's doing his inventory again and I still don't know what the rule is and neither does she and I don't think that's an accident. "Four years he'd been in town" against "finishing a PhD, more or less" — my Daphne drawer is rattling. And Christine saying something at the door that made him duck his head — the man has a whole soft life in this town that predates the plan and the book keeps handing me crumbs of it.

The kiss. The *kiss*. I've read a thousand first kisses and I have never once read one where the hero declines to close the last inch and *waits in the dark smiling* while the girl argues with her own rule and loses. "She let the thread go" — the same thread she'd been catching her whole life — and she went back with the grin still on her mouth and felt him smile *inside the kiss.* I put the book down and pressed my hands over my face. That grin inside the kiss is the most romantic thing this book has done and it's three words long.

And underneath all of it, cold as the river under the bridge: I know about the pointing game. I know about the bench. I watched her walk into that restaurant hungry and let a man choose for her and stop being afraid of the bill coming due — and the *bill is coming*. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." The jacket line has never felt less like a blurb and more like a sentence being carried out. She stopped bracing. That's exactly what he wanted. I'm fanning myself and grieving at the same time and I don't want to be anywhere else.
