REACTION — Okay. I picked this up because the blurb promised "psychologically exact" and I've been burned by that promise maybe a dozen times, so I opened it braced for pretty sentences and closed doors. Instead I got a woman locking a bathroom door so a man can't see her cry, and I put the Kindle down and just sat there for a second.

The whole chapter is one long scene — arrival, apple, bench, breakdown, bathroom — and it never once felt padded because every beat is *about* something. The apple slices cut thin because he noticed she eats them faster that way. That's the kind of detail I'd normally get in a throwaway "he's so attentive" line, except here it's load-bearing: he's feeding a girl who's been rationing her own wanting, and the book knows it and doesn't say it out loud. It never explains itself to me. When she tries the photo-smile in the mirror over the face that just begged, and sees the disjunction — the book just shows me. I did the gasp, not it.

I can already see the shape of the tragedy and we're one chapter in: he thinks tonight was a breakthrough, she thinks it was an exposure to be contained, and the tenderness is what she can't survive. The woman who just decided never to come out from behind the face is going to watch another woman get what she ran from. I have chills. Also — knowing there's a plan makes his "he was deciding whether to ask if she wanted dinner first" tenderness feel slightly like a rehearsal, and I hate that I think that, and I'm supposed to.
