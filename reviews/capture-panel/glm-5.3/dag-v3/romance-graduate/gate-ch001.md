REACTION

Okay. I sat down expecting to clock another brooding alpha with a bondage bench and I got something else entirely, and I'm a little rattled by how much it worked on me.

The apple. The thin slices laid cut-side up because she eats them faster that way — that's the detail that hooked me before anything sexual happened. This isn't a man who *performs* attentiveness ("you're so beautiful" on loop, the way these books do it); he *practices* it, in the grain of the actual scenes. And then the whole seduction is structured around noticing: the fine hairs the razor never caught, the blue toenails as her one step out of line, her voice arriving out of Kentucky when something moves her. He clocked the Kentucky in the middle of teasing her with apples and *kept it off his face*. That's a writer who understands that being seen is the actual kink here, and everything else is instrumentation.

The scene itself is hot — properly hot, the edging built with real patience, the "that's not the right please yet" beat is one of the better pieces of power-play writing I've read, because he's not training her to obey, he's waiting for her to stop *ordering off the menu*. But what got me was the last third. The water bottle cap under her thumbnail. She presses the sharp edge into her own nail bed after the best orgasm of her life, and he just quietly takes the bottle away and pulls her closer, says nothing. And then she locks the bathroom door for the first time ever, and he doesn't knock — because of Daphne, because of some history we get one paragraph of and it lands like a brick.

And her mirror monologue is the thing. "The face was lying." She got seen — actually seen, past the blue eyes and the sorority polish — and it was the most alive she's ever been, and she's deciding *in real time* to shove that self back in the closet. Not ever. He can have the face. God. That's not romance-tension, that's a person's actual psychology on the table, written clean, no explaining. The book trusts me to catch the deadbolt callback ("quiet as a deadbolt going over") and I did, and it hurt.

The jacket promises Randi-and-Pace planning Vee's year, so I know where the plot engine is — but this chapter is a *backwards* cold open, right? It says "Book One" and gives me Randi on the bench and no Vee at all. Which means the blurb's future and this past are going to braid somehow, and I don't know the shape yet, and that not-knowing is exactly what's going to make me pick up the next chapter. The one thing I'm watching: Pace's interiority is rich and hers is rich, but he's the one doing all the reading of her, and the book is largely in his head. If the whole series lives in his POV, the "courtly exact Dom" thing curdles into author's-perfect-boyfriend fantasy no matter how good the prose is. Chapter 1 earns the benefit of the doubt. Barely any heat wasted, no coy fade, prose that feeds instead of performs. This is the thing I've been telling people I'm looking for.
