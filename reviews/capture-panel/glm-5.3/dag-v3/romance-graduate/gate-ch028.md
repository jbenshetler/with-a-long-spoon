REACTION:

I read the walk down the aisle with my hand over my mouth. Not because of the slit — I've read four hundred slit-to-the-hip walks — but because of the *first step*. The small careful one, the safe one, the one where she could have minced the whole way and no one would ever have known there was anything to know. This book has spent twenty-seven chapters teaching me that about her: she always has the small step available. And she took the long one on purpose, a half-second before her head was sure — "her body agreeing, low and certain" — and I recognized the mechanism because it's the kitchen, it's the mirror, it's every scene where the want arrives before the permission and she goes with it. The new spine made its public debut and I was braced for it to be a disaster or a triumph and the book gave me the third thing, the honest thing: a murmur, folded up and put away by the room as rooms do, and *her* left carrying it alone. "Running under the dress in the good light while her heart went like she'd run the whole way." That's the whole book in one clause — the room forgets, the body doesn't.

And then the photo. I had to put the book down. Because the camera caught her mid-stride looking like a woman who had every part of it handled — *because she had* — and she knows to the heartbeat what second it was, and none of what's actually in the frame shows, or all of it is and only she'll ever see it. And she sent it to Pace **uncropped**. He'll open it in a Berkeley hotel room between sessions and see the dress do the thing he built it to do and never got to watch — and he will have NO idea what else is in the frame. The bare-under-the-silk secret. The heat. The walk she took on purpose. That part is hers. Do you understand what she just did? She made her own private drawer. The exact mirror of his — the bar he kept off the report, the face that went when she threw the venom, the things that were "his and that was Vee's" — she now has one of her own and she aimed it *at him*. Two archives in the same relationship now, pointing in both directions, and neither holder knows the other's exists. I've been waiting for that symmetry since chapter sixteen and it landed in a single sentence about a phone.

Cassie. Cassie got her whole scene and I could have cried at it. "You look—" and not finishing it, which from Cassie is a standing ovation — that's the truest piece of characterization this book has spent on anyone, twenty chapters of Cassie economy paid off in a dash. And the phone-holding at the door, and the bright corners of her eyes she'd deny to the grave, and "somebody had to document it," and then the terrible sandwich. Cassie was HERE. For the moment. Vee didn't ration her — she walked her as far as the doors and let her hold the phone, and the book made the friendship load-bearing exactly where it's always been load-bearing, and my shoulders finally came down from my ears. Also the honesty of the mother paragraph — "they'd be right there on the phone, honey, in the bright voice she kept for the things she couldn't give her" — and then "nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of." She *named* it. In her own head, on purpose, mid-ceremony, and took the long step anyway. That is not the girl from chapter four with the cardigan.

And I have to be honest about what I noticed with one raised eyebrow: Randi was not in that room. Twenty chapters of waiting for "whether Randi can be in that room and hold her face" — the fear walked itself right out of the plot. Pace's absence I understand; the workshop is even a little elegant, the man who made the dress missing the wearing. But the book had every chance to seat Randi in the middle rows, and didn't even mention her not being there. Which means one of two things: she wasn't invited, or she couldn't watch it. Either way, the absence is a *presence*, and given that she spent Monday installing the spanking in Vee's imagination and hasn't been heard from since, the silence is starting to have weight. Three days. The last Randi scene ended with Vee holding on too long at the goodbye and filing it wrong. I don't think the book forgot her. This book has never once dropped a detail.

The shoes, the plum nails waking and throwing light, "whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing" — that last line is the whole thesis of this volume in one closing question, and the fact that the book asked it instead of answering it is why I'll never quit this thing. The induction I've been reading toward since chapter ten is done, it was better than the daydream, and the ending on "a terrible sandwich and you can be famous at me" was the exact right temperature to come down on.
