REACTION — Nobody warned me this book was going to weaponize a municipal rec pool.

That underwater paragraph is the best writing the book has given me since the bathroom mirror, and it's doing the same trick from the other side: Randi's chapter two was a girl lying on top of a made bed with her shoes on, not-crying; Vee's version is hanging weightless in the green with "nobody up there could see her" and "the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." She loved this before she had a body to be a problem. And then the book goes one further and lets her *grieve it, a little* — the first time Vee has been allowed to feel her own loss directly, and it takes a pool and ten feet of water over her head to get there. The cardigan, the mother's voice, the suit that "fit, and gave her nowhere to hide" — all of it stands up on the deck with the towel where it can wait. That's not a swim scene, that's the whole thesis of Vee in one held breath.

And Cassie. My ledger said witness-or-casualty and this chapter answered it: witness, gloriously, armed. "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman" — pitched low, kept just for Vee — and then the water trick, the *outrage out of all proportion to the actual amount of water*, which is Cassie's entire emotional technology in one cupped-hand gesture: she can't ask "are you okay," so she manufactures a reason to shriek. Vee heard herself make the ugly laugh and whatever she'd carried up from the bottom didn't survive it. I'm so glad this book knows the splashing always won, back before any of it. Cassie is the person the plan has no column for and I will be genuinely gutted if Randi's warmth ever gets aimed at her for real.

Now. The thing I have to talk about even though I nearly let it slide: "Don't answer tonight."

I sat on the bench with Vee and went *who does that* right alongside her — and then my blood went a little cold, because I know who does that. He did that. On the bench, with the please. He asked her for something and then withheld the *taking* of the answer so the wanting would have to sit in her overnight — the exact methodology from chapter one, the edging of a *yes*, now applied to a dinner invitation on a street corner. "He'd smiled like it was a perfectly normal thing to ask of a person" — that's the same contained pleasure, the craft, and my G1.4 grudge just upgraded again: he doesn't even let her *yes* arrive when it wants to. The book is teaching Vee to hold her own wanting like he holds it for her, and she's doing it at a crosswalk with her thumb over the keys. And the walk light going WALK and back while she stands there — the city literally telling her to move and her not moving — that's the image I'll keep from this one.

Also: she's wondering about his hands again, in a towel, and decided it was "really not the place," and *I* liked that the book let her be a warm-blooded young woman about it without either shaming her or escalating. A simmer at most, and honest.

One flag I have to raise for my own honesty: this is now the third chapter where Vee is charmed and the machinery grinds silently forward, and while the pool gave me *interiority*, it gave me almost no *plot* — no Randi, no lunch, no contact with the plan itself beyond Pace's withholding move. This is exactly the quiet-second-chapter pattern I watch for. The writing saved it — barely. The next one has to collide the threads.
