The room not knowing her. "The room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." That's the whole Vee-arc thesis rendered as housework — the ribbons and the little gold swimmers are for Vivienne, and Vee is a stranger sleeping in Vivienne's bed, and the lavender and the fabric softener are the smell of a girl who doesn't exist anymore. That's the cracker sentence's childhood prequel, and the book said it without saying the word "crackers," which is exactly the restraint I've learned to trust in it. The text exchange, though — that's where I had the book flat on the table in front of me. And then the book did the cruelest thing it's done since the Chili's: it reached for her best night, the fra diavolo, the falling sheet, the safest room she has — and the blonde walked into it and took it over. "She could not keep her own night." That's the discovery chapter loading itself, is what that is. The ending.
