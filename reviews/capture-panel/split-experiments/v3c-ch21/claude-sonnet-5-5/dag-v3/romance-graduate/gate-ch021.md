REACTION

Oh, this is the chapter that makes me hurt for her. Randi lies on Pace's chest and tells him, in the soft after-voice, how much she likes Vee. She says "She got past me before I could help it." Then "God, she's lovely." She says it as news to him, not as the thing she's drowning in. And he reads it as romantic. He pictures the two of them naked and laughing and lets himself look, and he still doesn't see that she's telling him the truth in the only way she can stand to. The "Tell me" returns, and she goes still "the kind that takes holding." That's the ch1 deadbolt again. I felt it.

The bit I loved most is the end. Last chapter she wouldn't touch his stew. Now she asks him to make her something, eats every scrap, and asks for no more. The chicken, the white undershirt from the second drawer, the feet up on the chair. It's a quiet repair, and she gets it because she's said out loud, almost, what she feels. Even the "Pace, darling" glass is a small counterpoint to chapter 18's pouring her own. That's the book trusting me again, and it works.

But the chapter's title is "Gone," and I don't see what's gone. Maybe that's Randi, quietly. Maybe it's a warning: she's slipped from performing to actually falling, and Pace is happy. I'm uneasy that he's asking nothing. He sees Vee "ashamed," says it with certainty, and gets it half right. It's the same blind spot, and it irritates me.

The sex scene is short and warm, not the dead mechanical one from 18. The imagined threesome with the red hair falling into black is hot, a real 2. Then it's gone fast, which is fine. Small gripes: "her hand still at the side of his face" twice in a row is that stacking tic, and a few lines explain her ("something undefended in her face"). But I'm in.
