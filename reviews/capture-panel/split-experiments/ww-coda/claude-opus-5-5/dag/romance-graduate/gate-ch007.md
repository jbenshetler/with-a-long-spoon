Okay, I didn't expect a pool chapter to get me. The underwater part did. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." She stays down past when her lungs ask, and she grieves a little. That's interiority. Up to now Vee has mostly been someone being looked at: Randi's target, Pace's project, a girl reading a room for where she fits. This is the first time I've been alone inside her without anyone watching. And the book doesn't spell it out. It never says "her body has been policed since she was sixteen." It's the old synchro suit that fits too well, her mother's voice in her head again, and then her chest finally relaxing in the one place nobody can see her. I got it without being told.

Cassie is quietly becoming my favorite person in this book. "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman." She could have yelled it at the bleachers and she kept it for Vee. Then there's the splash trick, from ten feet away, so she never has to ask what's wrong. Between this, the "Goodbye, Vee," and watching Randi text in the café, I'm sure Cassie is the one person who's going to see the shape of this, and I'm already afraid she'll get pushed out of it. The friendship felt real and old. I wanted to be in that pool fight.

The catcalls from the bleachers felt stock to me, a scene I've read a hundred times. It's the one spot where my eyes slid.

Then "Don't answer tonight." Ohhh. Rereading it with what I know, it's nasty. In chapter 5 Vee wanted to make him wait a day and Randi talked her out of it. Now Pace has taken the waiting away from her and made it his. He's courtly, he's giving her time to choose, and it's a move. He's doing to her what he did to Randi on the bench: hold back, let the wanting grow, and make her be the one to reach. She stood on the corner with the yes still in her mouth. And she likes it, "odd" is the word she keeps landing on. Meanwhile I know he went home that same night and fed her froyo cup to Randi like foreplay. The dramatic irony is doing a lot of work for me.

The towel bit was a small, honest simmer, his thick knuckles, "which was really not the place." It's girlish and real, not heat yet, and I'm fine with that because Saturday is coming. The ending is a good thumb-mover: her phone, one text from him, the light going to WALK and back. If I'm honest, I want to see Vee alone with him on the date, and I want to see what Pace is like when he isn't narrating her.
