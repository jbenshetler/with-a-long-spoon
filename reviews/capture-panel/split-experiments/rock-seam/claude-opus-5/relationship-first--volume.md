TELL A FRIEND: Okay so it's literary erotica, which I know, I know — but the sex is written by someone who can actually write, and the hottest chapter in it is a man with a tape measure and his shirt on. The setup is that two people pick a girl off a quad like a dessert, and you know it from the jacket, so the whole book you're reading a love story you've been told is a trap, and it keeps being a real love story anyway, which is the mean trick and it works. The girls are the thing: there's a best friend named Cassie who says about nine words a chapter and every one of them is a knife, and the moment the heroine starts editing her stories before handing them over you can feel a friendship quietly bleeding out in the background while everyone's happy. My gripe — and it's a real one — is that she tells the same three nights to four different people, and there's a stretch around the middle where I was reading a summary of a chapter I'd loved forty pages earlier and getting cranky. Then there's a lunch scene, chapter nineteen, where a woman says "you don't have to tell me" at the precise second the girl is about to hand her the secret, and I realized every one of those retellings was a harvest and I'd been reading it wrong. Also: an untouched bowl of stew with one spoon-line drawn through the sauce does more damage than most divorces in fiction. It does not resolve anything and I don't want it to. Read the hike chapter and the fitting chapter even if you read nothing else.
