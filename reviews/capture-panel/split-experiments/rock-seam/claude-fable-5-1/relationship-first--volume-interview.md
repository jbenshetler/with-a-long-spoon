**T1**

Vee is the one the book walks away from, and I noticed. The last two chapters are Randi and Pace under a quilt, and Vee is somewhere offstage with a credit card that has nothing under it. That's where she's left — having done the bravest thing anyone in the book does, twice: the debit card on the folder, and then not looking down the street after the kiss. She's the one who moves her own life, and she's the one who doesn't know the game she's in. How I feel is something close to protective and a little ashamed of it, because I enjoyed watching Randi lead her down one *yeah* at a time as much as anyone. And she's lost Cassie, or Cassie's been let go of quietly, and that's the loss I actually mind. I like Vee. I worry the book likes her the way Pace does — for what she does to a room.

Pace I came around on late and against my judgment. For twenty-six chapters he was the man who decided a body's answer is the true one, and I held that as either the book's subject or its blind spot. Then "Peter," and the tutor who was nineteen, laid out in good order — the good order the tell — and him calling it luck. Where the book leaves him is with his hand on a scar he won't explain, saying "Not tonight." I feel tenderness for him, and I feel wary of the tenderness, because it arrived exactly when the author wanted it to. He covers the bowl for her. He measures without looking down. He also hasn't once looked past Randi's face when she's not on a bench, and the book knows it and hasn't made him pay. He's a careful man who built a philosophy out of something that was done to him, and I'm sorry for him, and I don't trust him, and both are true.

Randi is the one I'm most alive to right now, and she's the one doing the most harm. The book leaves her eating — actually eating, asking if there's anything in the house — and then saying "She got past me before I could help it" into his chest where he can't see her face, and then taking her shirt off to change the subject. That's the whole novel in a paragraph. All book long I trusted her less every time she was warm and more every time her hand did something her face hadn't authorized, and now something's got past the face for good and she's holding it very steady, like a drink at a party. I feel for her the way you feel for someone who's just found out something about herself and has nobody to tell. She's the person I'd want in the kitchen. She also owes Vee the truth and can't pay it, and I haven't forgotten that.

**T2**

I expect the triangle to turn over. Book One was Randi and Pace choosing Vee; Book Two is going to be Randi wanting Vee and Pace not seeing it, because he takes the body's answer and Randi's body has been telling him one thing while she's been telling him another. I expect the kitchen to be where it plays out — the bowl, the glass she pours herself — and I expect the debriefs to continue, and I'm braced. I expect Cassie to come back once, with the goodbye she already said. I expect more of Peter, sideways, the way the scar was given. I'd like to think Vee finds out; I don't need her to.

The one thing that would make me put it down: if she finds out by device. A phone seen over a shoulder, a door opened at the wrong second, the campus cop's siren dropped into the middle of the novel again. This book has earned that somebody has to *say* it — Randi, into a face this time, not into a chest — and if the plot does the telling instead of a person, then the plot is moving the people, and that's the exact moment I leave a book. I'd rather Vee never know than know because a letter fell out of a bag.

Yes. I said I'd buy it tonight and I would. What I'm buying it for is not the reveal. I'm buying it for Randi not eating and then eating; for another forty pages under a quilt where nobody explains anything; for sex written as two people who can't put anything down. I'm buying it for the scar, and I'll take it slant. If the book gave me Vee's discovery in chapter two and spent the rest on it, I'd be disappointed — that's the plot I didn't buy this for.

The hypothetical: I'll take it. I'm not a romance reader and I never learned what a volume owes me at its end, so "Vee still doesn't know" isn't a broken promise to me; I've loved books that ended on a question and I distrust the ones that answer everything. If Book Two ends with her not knowing because the not-knowing has been the subject — because it's cost Randi something I can see, because it's changed what gets said in the kitchen — then yes, I'd buy Book Three on the strength of the nights. But here's the honest part. I notice repetition across nights; it's the thing I notice most. Two full volumes of a girl not knowing something the reader knew in chapter three is a long time to hold a coin in your pocket, and by the tenth held-breath scene I'd know whether it was Randi keeping the secret or the author keeping it for me. A secret that long stops belonging to the characters. So: yes, probably — and I'd trust the book less on the way in, and I'd be reading for the moment it told on itself.
