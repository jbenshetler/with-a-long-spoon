**T1**

Vee — she's the one I'm holding my breath for. The book leaves her with her hand pressed down on a scar, a name said once so she can keep it, and a girl inside her who did the arithmetic and held the word under her tongue instead of saying it. She's more herself than she was in chapter one — she took her own underwear off in front of a mirror because the line deserved it; she said "I wanted it. And I took it. For once," and I believed her — and she still doesn't know there was ever a lawn, a pen, a plan. So what I feel is two things at once: proud of her the way you're proud of a friend who's finally spending something she's been hoarding, and sick underneath it, because I've been ahead of her for twenty-four chapters and I know what's coming down the hall. I'm not scared she'll be hurt by Pace in a room. I'm scared she'll find out how she got into the room.

Pace — I trust him with her body more than I've trusted a man in one of these since I started reading them. The face turned up while she was in her mother's word, waiting for her to come back to her own eyes; getting out of the truck into the rain; "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" That's not a performance of consent, that's a man whose whole attention is a question. And I'm still angry at him. "Her stomach is soft" was hers, given to him alone, and he handed it to Randi with *tell* in his ear and the book's "he did not say how he knew" didn't buy him anything with me. He owes Vee the truth and he knows it now — he pictured the three of them at the table and couldn't say it yet — so I'm holding the debt with him rather than against him. But the thing I feel most, right now, about Pace is a kind of exasperated tenderness: he can see the shame on Vee from across a fitting and he can't see his girlfriend leaving his house shaking. He covers the bowl and puts it in the fridge and looks for what he did wrong and can't find it. I found it. I want to grab him by the collar.

Randi — she's the one I didn't expect to feel for and now feel for most, against my will. The record shows it happening: gate 7 "starting to feel for her," gate 14 "she's bleeding and he can't see it and I can," gate 23 "she's in love with the girl and he's cooking." She's the only one of the three who knows what the plan is doing to her, and she's stopped eating in his kitchen, and she kissed Vee at a curb without asking because she couldn't hold it anymore — and I wrote that one down as the closest this book came to the thing I left. Where the book leaves her is in his undershirt with her chin on her knees, saying "She got past me before I could help it," which is the truest thing she's said since the mirror in chapter one. I'm not scared *of* her anymore. I'm scared *for* her. And I don't fully forgive her — she harvested the fitting, she shut a door on Vee mid-secret — but she's the one it's costing, visibly, every chapter, and that's why I could keep reading.

**T2**

I expect Book Two to pay the two debts on the page. The reveal — Vee finding out about the lawn, the pen, the high table, the schedule — that has to happen and it has to cost. Not gently. Someone has to lose something real; my guess is it's Randi first, because she's already breaking, and the book has spent twenty-seven chapters teaching me that she's the one who bleeds. And the scar — "Not tonight" with a time on the door means the door opens in this one. Daphne, the mother taken out of the sentence, fifteen, the stepfather or whoever built that scar — Peter's story has to come out and Vee has to be the one he tells it to, in a room, alone, the way he's earned every other room with her.

I also expect the geometry to shift. He's already picturing three at a table. Randi's the one wanting Vee out loud now; the toenail moment in the shoe wreckage was the most honest thing between the two women and it was a touch, not a taking. So I think Book Two stops being a campaign and becomes an actual question about whether three particular people can want each other honestly — and I think the book knows the only way to get there is through the wreck of the reveal, not around it.

The one thing that would make me put it down: if Vee finds out and the book has everyone be so *reasonable* about it that it doesn't cost anyone. If she's talked into being okay. If Pace's asking — which has been the whole point, the thing I came back for — turns out to only apply in bedrooms, and the biggest question of the book gets answered *for* her by two people who've already decided. I wrote "if Vee gets handled instead of asked, I'm gone" at gate 3 and I meant it. Everything since has been the book earning my staying; one chapter of her being managed through the truth and I'd feel the old genre come back in through the side door, and I know exactly what that feels like, and I'd close it.

Smaller version of the same thing: one more mouth arriving before a question, played as sexy, with no cost on the page. The book got away with it once because it made me feel the break. It doesn't get to do that twice.

**T3**

Yes. I said I'd buy it tonight and I still would. I'm buying it for three things, in order: the moment Vee knows, and what it does to all three of their faces; the scar, and whether he lets her hand stay on it; and — honestly — the next time he asks her a question on his knees. I want the reveal to hurt and I want the rooms after it, and I trust this author with both because she's never once winked at me. She let me watch a girl stop eating and made me feel it.

The hypothetical — I'll answer it, I don't need to refuse. If Book Two ended and Vee *still* didn't know: no. I wouldn't buy Book Three.

Not because I'd be bored. Because it would mean the book had been lying to me about what it was. The only reason I got through the debrief chapters, the wineglass, "her stomach is soft," the plan laid bare across a kitchen table, was that the book kept the debt on the page — it kept saying *they owe her this*, and it made Pace picture telling her and fail. That's a promise. Two full volumes of a girl who doesn't know how she got into the room, with the two people who put her there getting to keep her body and her secrets and her yes without ever handing over the truth — that's not a slow burn, that's the thing I left. It's a heroine being acted upon with extraordinarily good manners. I'd have spent two books being asked to find their disregard romantic, just dressed up in *may I*, and no. I forgave a slow beat because the care was real. I wouldn't forgive that. I'd close it, and I'd be sad about it, and I'd know exactly where the truck scene was on my shelf.
