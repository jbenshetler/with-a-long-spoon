REACTION — The kiss at the end did something to my chest, and I'm annoyed about how much. "May I choose?" — the title, and the whole chapter is that question. This man ordering for her, tearing the bread, letting her lose the thread of his sentences and stepping into the pauses or not, either fine — I could feel what the book is doing with my own body, the way Vee keeps describing hers: the hollow opening wider, "down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." Hunger as the whole engine of this book. Her appetite being *fed* by the same man who's choosing her for a cage.

And the knife under all of it: the right hand. "Always the right, never the other" — she's already filing his patterns the way she filed Randi's, and she has no idea she's cataloguing her handlers. The dramatic irony is almost unbearable now. We watched him and Randi pick her out on the quad like a dish. He literally engineered the coffee shop meet. And she's sitting there falling for "the most restful thing she has felt in as long as she could remember" — the thing that's restful about him is that there's no manipulation, except there's nothing but. That's the cruelty and I don't think the book knows yet whether it's cruelty. That's what I can't stop reading for.

The hand-holding paragraph — "it felt less like something beginning than something resolving... her hand had been wrong all its life for want of his exactly" — that's a sentence I'd underline in a book I owned. And then *her* line, "I'm making an exception," closing the distance *he* gave up — him leaving the last inch and her taking it. For a reader who's bored of alphas taking, a hero who steps back and lets her close the distance is the whole thing. The chapter is essentially chaste and it's the hottest one so far.

Also, the "don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" being her rule and her breaking it herself — her reflex hand on the thread, and then she lets the thread go. The book keeps doing that: watching her surveillance systems tick and then get lulled into silence around him. Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" is starting to make sense, and I don't love that either.

Two quiet-ish chapters in a row, technically (dinner, then this), but this one *is* the heat, clothed. If the next chapter goes back to Randi and Pace I'm in; if we drift a third scene without one of the three of them in a room with tension, I'll wobble. For now — that kiss under the tree. Yes.
