**REACTION**

Oh, Peter.

I spent four chapters angry at a man who was sitting in a dark recliner with two fingers of whiskey and a horn record, scrolling through four pictures of her. Four, because he only ever took the ones she was in on purpose. I had to set the book down at that line. Then the bleach. The mop and bucket outside the bathroom door. He was sick on the floor of his own house on New Year's, alone, the same night she was in her twin bed building a blonde out of spite. Neither of them knew. Every chapter now is two people lying in the cold, each sure the other one has the warmth.

The truck finished me. His head lifts before he knows he's moved, the fork stops halfway, and his whole body leans toward the gravel cut. It's the wrong engine. He knows it's wrong "as he'd have known a voice that was not hers," and he holds on to it anyway. Then he's bent over the table sobbing over frozen chicken and broccoli he called prison food, and he eats it after, because he told himself to. I've wanted this man to put a word on a feeling for fifty chapters, and the book gave it to me with no word at all. It gave me a man crying at a passing truck. That's believe-what-I-do turned back on him, and it worked on me completely.

Now I know why. Daphne. "There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told." He taught himself that the word is what decides whether a loss is survivable. And then he says it plainly to himself: *he had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him.* He knew the true answer on the porch. He felt the words come up, and he chose the smaller truth. I was right to be hurt for her, and I was wrong about why. It was never contempt, and it wasn't really Randi either. It's an old wound in a careful man. That makes me softer on him, but it doesn't let him off.

Sheri doesn't let him off either, and I love her for it. "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." She says it straight to his face and then steals his fries. She's the friend he deserves and the one Vee needs. She also has her own grief: Paige, seven at the table, "call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas." "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." The two of them don't go home, and they eat the usual together.

What I'm holding against him, still: "the choosing was hers; he would not take it from her." He's using that rule for Randi and for Vee, and it's become a hiding place. Vee didn't ask for a silent week. She's waiting on a field with nothing in it, and he's waiting on gravel. Somebody has to drive. Then, two glasses in, there's the kitchen picture he's been "adding to for weeks": Vee in his sheet, Randi with her feet up, nobody hurrying. The plan is still alive in him even now, and he still gets hard remembering the sidewalk kiss told to him "while he was inside her." I flinched.

And the things the chapter shook loose:

- Randi hasn't been out to his house since the green panties, six weeks back. So the five nights Vee stayed away were him alone. I owe him that one.
- Sheri: bleached blonde, too much makeup worn like a dare. "I got maybe four seconds of her at the bar before she started throwing things." Hold on. Was Sheri the blonde at the Rusty Nail? "All us girls love him"?
- He took Randi dancing before Thanksgiving, and Vee's been to the hall once.
