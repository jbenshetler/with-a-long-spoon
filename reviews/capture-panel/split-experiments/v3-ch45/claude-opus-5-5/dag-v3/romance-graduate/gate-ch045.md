**REACTION**

Oh, *Pace*. I asked for him and the book gave me him at the bottom of a whiskey bottle, and I wasn't ready.

It's the same night as the last two chapters. December 31 again. Randi in a hotel bed with a man she feels nothing for, Vee in her twin bed under the swim trophies, and now Pace in his recliner with the lamp off, scrolling the same few photos "like reading." Three rooms, three people alone on the one night you aren't supposed to be, and none of them knows the other two are lying awake too. At chapter 44 I thought the book was stalling. Now I see it was building three panels of one picture, and this one is the last panel. I forgive some of my counting.

Sheri first, because I love her now. "The philosophy of your food. Lord almighty. Get over yourself." The fries stolen by the fistful, the shake pulled over "like something owed," the kid putting the single in front of her. She's the only person in this book who wants nothing from him, and she's the one who says the sentence nobody else would: "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. From this seat, that feels pretty shitty." I clapped. And then Paige, and the "call your mama, Sheri" sing-song, and "Ironic. Isn't it." So Sheri doesn't talk to her family either, two people who don't go back, eating burgers at a table on New Year's Eve. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." That line went right through me, and it's the first time Pace has said anything about his parents that wasn't a closed door.

Then she drops the bomb in passing: "That brunette you brought dancing before Thanksgiving." He took RANDI to the Rusty Nail? The man who "doesn't bring dates, as a rule," whose taking Vee there was the whole fight? And Randi looked at Sheri like "one more of your harem applicants." I need a minute with that. So the rule that bartender swore by isn't a rule anymore, and Vee is the only one who doesn't know it.

The recliner section was gorgeous and it scared me. "It was not missing her… This was underneath it." The girl with brown hair and glasses, laughing with her hand over her mouth. Then the kitchen fantasy: the three of them after, Vee in his sheet with too much honey, Randi with her feet up, "nobody hurrying anywhere." He's drunk, alone, and heartsick for Vee, and he's *still* building the threesome in his head. Adding to it for weeks. "Whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her." That's tender and it's terrifying, and it's the most honest he's ever been with me about the plan. He loves her and he's still arranging her.

Randi hasn't come since the panties. Six weeks. "She had always been the one who came, and the choosing was hers." So he's sitting there waiting on Randi to choose, and waiting on Vee to choose, and choosing nothing himself. That's his whole flaw in one posture, and I don't think he sees it.

The morning after nearly made me cry: the bleach smell, the mop with foam crusted on it, the freezer in its columns, chicken here and broccoli there, "enough to go on like this a long time." Prison food. The man who feeds everyone eats a bowl that holds "everything a body could need and nothing his mouth wanted." Then the engine on the highway, his head lifting before he knows it, the fork stopping, and the truck going by. And he breaks and sobs over the bowl. Then picks up the fork. "He was not hungry. He ate anyway." That's the best ending this book has had in ten chapters.

Now my gripe, and it's a real one. The Daphne part goes into flat, short, declarative sentences: "Then she left." "He never told any of them he loved them." "He had taught himself that the words were the difference." And then: "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." That's the whole subtext of five chapters, read aloud to me like a case summary. I'd half worked it out from "I don't go back" and the scar, and I wanted to find it, not be handed it. "Then he had chosen the smaller truth" was enough. The book didn't trust it. It also made Daphne simpler than I'd built her. My theory was that she groomed him and he calls it luck. Instead she's just the lost love, the reason. Maybe that's still coming, but for now the darker version got flattened into a sad backstory.

Still, he *knows*. "He knew he loved her." He has it whole and ready, and he's sitting in his house hearing tires go past. Somebody get in a car. Either of you. Please.

Text to a friend at 1 a.m.: *he got blackout drunk alone on NYE looking at her pictures, threw up, mopped it with bleach, then SOBBED when a truck that wasn't her drove by. also he took Randi dancing at the bar he takes nobody to. I'm on the floor.*
