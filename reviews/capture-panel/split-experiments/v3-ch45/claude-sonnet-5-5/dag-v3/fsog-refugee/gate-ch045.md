REACTION

Oh. I didn't expect to get Pace, and I didn't expect to get *this* Pace. He's been a closed door for so long that watching him sob over a fork when a strange engine goes past on the road made me want to climb into the page. The ear straining for her tires, the "wrong sound," the stoop where he used to wait for her to find him: that is the tenderness I read for, and for once it's on the page and not just in his hands.

Sheri is a gift again. "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. From this seat, that feels pretty shitty." Thank God somebody said it. He couldn't answer. "I can't." And then the whiskey, and Daphne. So that's the reason. He said "I love you" once, and losing her took over his life, so he taught himself the words were the danger. It's a real wound, and it explains the porch. It doesn't excuse it. He says it himself: "he had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." He knows he's been weak. And he knows he loves her, which is the thing I've wanted confirmed for twenty chapters. It's also exactly the thing Vee lay awake in Ohio not knowing. The two of them are in two beds, each aching, and the one thing that would fix it is a text. I wanted to throw something.

The part that disturbed me: Daphne is still "the only woman he told," and she was nineteen to his fifteen, and he is still telling it as romance, a first love who "knew how things worked." The book hasn't flinched on that, and neither has he. I still want somebody to say it to him. The scar is still unexplained, and the girl "with brown hair and glasses" under the whiskey is another thing buried.

Randi is almost the saddest bit. He hasn't seen her in six weeks, he lets it be "hormones" in his own head, and he thinks of her glad and warm, her blanket kept on the chair. He misses her. He doesn't know she's in a bed in Gstaad with a ski bro, grieving the sandwich. And his three-in-a-kitchen picture is still sitting there, so gentle and so wrong. And not a word of the other woman Vee is imagining. Thank God he isn't with anyone. I wanted to tell her.

The bleach, the mop, the chicken and broccoli and the shaking hands: grim and exact. He's eating prison food to survive. I don't need him punished, but I needed to see this.

I'm a lot more on his side than I was. But if he now knows and still sits on it, I will lose patience fast.
