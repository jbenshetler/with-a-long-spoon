## REACTION

The first half mostly lost me. I watched the tree kiss happen one chapter ago, and now Vee tells it to Cassie, and then six days later she tells it to Randi. I get why. Each retelling bends the story toward whoever's listening, and the Randi version is clearly the one that counts. Still, I was skimming the Cassie scene. The spin into the bean bag chair is cute, and "what do I tell the state troopers" is a good line. But the only thing in it that matters is "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." Vee just gave her best friend the vaguest address possible, she's about to see the bench house, and Cassie is the only person who'd come looking. I clocked it.

Then the smoothie scene woke me up, mostly because of Randi.

"Paul?" She snapped her fingers trying to remember the name of the man she sleeps with, the man she aimed at this girl. That gave me a little chill.

Then the "catastrophe" date with the lacrosse guy who wouldn't listen: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She's making up a bad man so the good one looks better. She's selling Pace to Vee without ever saying his name properly. And she keeps handing Vee the lines and getting her to say them back. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Vee had the dignified version ready and traded it for Randi's. That's grooming, done in the voice of a fun friend, and it's creepier than anything Pace has done.

But the cracks are what I'll be thinking about. "Ask and then ravish," a beat late and low, "the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That one line carries more heat than the whole chapter. Randi is picturing it too, picturing Pace against a tree with Vee, and it slips out of her.

Then Brooke shows up. Brooke is the president who already grilled Randi about Pace in chapter 2, and Randi takes Vee's hand on the table and says "dear friend" right in front of her. Vee thinks it cost Randi something, and Vee's right, she just can't know how much. Randi is spending her Standards-board standing on a girl in a pilled cardigan, in front of the one woman already sniffing around. Is that strategy, or did the want get away from her? I honestly can't tell yet, and that's why I'm still reading.

Gripes. A lot of this chapter is a replay of the Chi Latte chapter: the high table, the cardigan "over the better shirt," the hand flat between the shoulder blades, "Love you, girl." Maybe that's deliberate, like a ritual repeating itself. Reading it, though, it just felt like the same chapter again. And the narration keeps explaining things to me. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." "Only this time not to get out in front of her own humiliation, but to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi." I saw Cassie get dropped. I saw the look on her face folded away. You don't need to explain it.

Where I am: this is the third chapter without real heat (the pool, the dinner, now this), and the last explicit scene was back in chapter 3. Vee's inner life is strong enough that I'm not bored, and the Randi undercurrent is sharp. But the hike has to deliver something, and so does that house out past the gravel. If I get another chapter of soft walking and listening, I'll start doubting the "explicit" on the jacket.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the girlfriend held the new girl's hand in front of the sorority president and said 'dear friend' and I think it was the realest thing she's done in the whole book and also maybe a con. I can't tell. help."
