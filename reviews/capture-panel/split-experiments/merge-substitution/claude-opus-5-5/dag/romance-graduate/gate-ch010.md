Okay. The truck.

I've read a hundred "she's soaked and he gives her his shirt" scenes. They always go the same way: he turns around like a gentleman, she peeks at his back, and then they bang against the tailgate. This one went somewhere I didn't expect. She calls him back in. She kneels on the bench and holds his eyes and unbuttons her own shirt while her mother's voice is going *girls who do this, three dates, your mother would*. The voice is what turns her on. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." That's the interiority I've been begging this genre for. The shame doesn't stop the desire. It's part of the desire, and she knows it, and she does it anyway. Then she looks down at the fourth button and his eyes follow hers. That got me. She's directing his looking. He doesn't lift a hand. And she still reads the old wiring in herself as "floozie, in her mother's exact word," and her thighs press together. God.

Then she reaches for his hand to start the next thing and it's shaking. He's been standing in the rain so she could change, and he's freezing. She brings his hand under the flannel to her belly. That's the moment the bond turned for me. She stops performing and takes care of him. I liked her more right then than at any point so far.

But I don't trust any of it, and the not-trusting is the fun. This whole chapter is Vee's POV, so I can't see inside Pace, and I've read chapters 1, 3, and 6. I know he goes home and *tells* Randi. I know "Ask and then ravish" came out of Randi's mouth a week ago and planted this exact picture in Vee's head. So the moleskin, the right-handed eating from the dinner, the "we take nothing and leave nothing," the way he lets her catch him looking: is it care or method? The book's answer seems to be both, and that's the most unsettling thing about it. The cherry pit made me squirm. It was gentle, he didn't shame her, she learned something. But it's also a man quietly teaching a girl his rules, and the chapter is called Leave No Trace while the three of them leave traces all over each other. And "That sounds like a beautiful dress"? He's buying the russet dress. I'd bet my Kindle on it. Whether that's lovely or awful depends on whether it gets reported back to Randi on the couch with a glass of wine.

The foot thing also got me. Chapter 1, the bench: "He liked her feet. He had not yet told her that." Here he has Vee's bare foot in his hand on a boulder in the cold. Nobody says anything, and I noticed it anyway, which is exactly how I want a book to treat me.

The prose works. The short choppy sentences on the climb ("Push and lift. Push and lift.") had me breathing like I was on that trail. The summit paragraph is one long run-on and it earns it. The sandwich, the horseradish in her sinuses, food as hunger answered: this book keeps feeding its women, and I'm keeping count.

The cop. Ugh. A siren squawk as the interruption device is the one cheap move in the chapter. It felt like a hand reaching in from outside the book to stop the scene before anyone had to say the big thing. I'll forgive it a little, because he parked in a fire lane, which is his fault and in character, and because she's left on the curb in his flannel with the moleskin still on her heel. That's a gorgeous last image. Ten chapters in and Vee still hasn't had sex on the page. Randi's chapters have carried the heat so far, so it's fine, but the book is spending its credit on me and I'm keeping track.
