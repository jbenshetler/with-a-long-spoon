REACTION

Okay so I opened this expecting Vee — the blurb put her front and center, twenty-one and quietly starving, and here I am two chapters deep and she hasn't walked onstage once. And I'm not mad about it. I'm actually sitting up. Because what chapter one did with that last-line reveal — *there you are, Randi* — this chapter cashes in. The book spent twenty pages teaching me exactly how the inside of that woman works, and then it put her in a common room at ten in the morning in last night's clothes and let me watch the machine run.

And god, the machine. Brooke not standing up. I had to put the book down for a second. *The not-standing was the move.* A whole power structure in one withheld gesture, and Randi reading it from the inside because she's on the board too, she knows the tooling, she just has less of it than Brooke does. "How's the paper coming." "Where did you end up working?" And then the seamless pivot — *and, I just want to make sure I have this* — like she's checking a spreadsheet cell. "Engineering, isn't it." Demoting him a notch without appearing to reach for it. "I kept thinking you'd said Chase." That one's filthy, honestly, in a way the spanking wasn't. And the *are you in his section* feint, the mock-relieved *I was about to be scandalized* — she's not scandalized, she's establishing that there's something here worth being scandalized about and letting the room hear her decline to be. Four minutes. Mostly smiling. I have been in that conversation. Not with a sorority, but I have been in that conversation and my hands got cold reading it.

The pitch-matching killed me. Kinsey asks if he's short-cute, and Randi's laugh comes out *at the same pitch as Kinsey's*, too fast, before she could choose it. That's the tell the whole chapter is built around. She didn't choose it because the thing that chooses isn't home right now. And then she agrees. She says *he's cute, yeah* about the man who built a bench for her with his hands and took her shoes off one buckle at a time, and lets the word "cute" stand, and walks up fifteen stairs with perfect posture, and doesn't look back because she doesn't need to, because nothing stopped.

Then upstairs, which is where it really got me. The closet — bone to camel to oatmeal with the one disciplined oxblood at the end, which is the toenail polish again, the one permitted deviation, and I noticed it without being told and I want to kiss this author for not telling me. Her hand half-lifted toward the row and she can't remember what she came for. Two notifications she won't read. Phone facedown. Shoes on. And the duvet: *It was light and synthetic and adequate. It was not — it was a duvet.* It was not the cashmere blanket she bought for his house. She doesn't finish the sentence. She can't finish the sentence. That's the most romantic thing in the chapter and it happens in a sentence that breaks off.

And then: *He's hard to say no to*, the line that landed perfectly in the kitchen, coming back in a different key. In a key she can't sing in. And she stops the thought where the words stop because past the words there's a quiet she has no way to cross. Cold. Beautiful. And the last paragraph — the not-crying that isn't a decision, that isn't a door being held shut, that's *there being no door*. The held breath that stopped feeling held. That's twenty-one years described in two clauses.

What I'm sitting with: I came in believing the blurb, that Randi's the architect and Vee's the mark. Two chapters in and Randi scares me *for* herself. The water-bottle cap under the thumbnail last night, that little flare of self-harm she did while being held — this chapter is what that gesture looks like with a whole house around it. And the plan the blurb promises, the thing she and Pace started in bed? A woman who just decided at a bathroom mirror that she is never coming out again is going to need something to do with her hands. She's going to need someone else to be the one who gets opened. I think I just watched the motive get installed, in a chapter where nothing happened except three girls and a bowl of cereal.

Also Pace isn't in this chapter at all and I missed him, which is its own kind of trick. He thinks he had a remarkably good evening. He thinks he found her hunger and answered it. He has no idea the door closed. I'm going to be sick about it for a while.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the heroine isn't even IN book one yet and I don't care, the other woman just got dissected by her sorority president in four minutes of small talk and I have chills*
