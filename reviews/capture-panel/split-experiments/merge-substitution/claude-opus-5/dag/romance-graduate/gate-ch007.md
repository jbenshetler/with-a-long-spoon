REACTION

Okay, so this is the chapter where I figured out what the book is actually doing to me, and I'm a little annoyed about how well it worked.

No Pace. No Randi. No heat. Two girls at a rec pool. On paper I should've thumbed past it. Instead I got to the part where she crosses the painted stripe on the bottom and goes under and the room switches off, and I just — stopped. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." I actually put my phone down. That's the whole character in one line and it doesn't explain itself, doesn't follow it up with three sentences telling me what it meant. It just drops it and lets her hang there sculling in the green and grieving a little, and moves on. That's the thing I've been starving for. Nobody in a KU alpha book has an interior life that exists *when no one is looking at her*. Vee's entire personality so far has been being perceived — by Randi, by Pace, by the room at Chi Latte — and this is the first time we get her with the perceiving switched off, and it turns out the only place she's ever been free is six feet underwater where nobody can see her. Cool. Great. I'm fine.

The bleachers. God. "You won't need floaties with *those*" and she gives it *a long blink, half her mouth pursing, less than a grimace* — that's so exact it hurt. Not devastation, not a feminist speech, just the micro-expression of a girl who has absorbed this eight hundred times and has a calibrated facial response filed for it. And she *walks it like an entrance* because the bleachers are the only route to the water. That's what a body being a toll booth feels like and the book didn't once stop to explain it to me.

And Cassie. Cassie, Cassie, Cassie. "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman," said low, *just for the two of them* — and then the line that gutted me, that she could have pitched it up into the bleachers, she had the lungs, and she kept it for Vee. Followed by the splash: she doesn't ask, because asking makes the sad version bigger, she just shoots a sheet of water at her best friend's head to break the mood. That's love. That's the most loved Vee has been in seven chapters, and it came from the girl in the ugly navy suit who said *Goodbye, Vee* in a lecture hall three chapters ago and didn't fix it.

Which is exactly why I'm suspicious as hell. You don't write a chapter this warm about a friendship unless you're planning to feed it into something. Randi is being built up as the brighter life and Cassie is the one who goes "I've got a thing" and declines the yellow coffee shop and watches Randi thumb her phone with a flat patient look — and I noticed in chapter 5 that Cassie is the only person at that table who correctly read what was happening, out loud, in one sentence, while Vee's own narration kept trailing off mid-thought. Cassie sees it. Cassie said *goodbye.* And now the book gives me a whole chapter of her being the best person in it, with no agenda, no plot, nothing but a splash fight — and I know what that's for. That's a receipt I'm going to be handed later.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the book spent a whole chapter at a swimming pool and I would now personally fight a bleacher full of college boys for a side character named Cassie." Also: Randi has never once in this book done a single thing that wasn't for herself, and I'm waiting for Vee to notice what the quiet one is worth before it's gone. She won't. That's the knife.

Downside, honestly: I came for heat and this chapter has zero, and it's the first time the book's run on something other than charge. I don't resent it — it earned its place and it's short — but I'm aware my thumb is now pointed hard at that unknown number on Vee's phone. One more quiet one and we'll talk.
