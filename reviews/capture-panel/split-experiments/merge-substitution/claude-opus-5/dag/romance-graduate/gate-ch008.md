REACTION

Okay. So I was braced for this to be the chapter where the book coasted — the obligatory nice-date chapter, the one where the author rests on the reader's goodwill. It isn't. It's the hungriest chapter in the book and almost nothing happens in it.

The food. God, the food. I have read approximately nine hundred restaurant dates and they are always about the man ordering for her as a flex, and the heroine either finding it hot or being spunky about it. This did something I've genuinely never seen: she *hands* him the menu because she can't read it, and the handing-over is the erotic event. "May I choose?" — three words, and I felt the floor drop the same way she did. And then the sounds of the words she doesn't know: "each soft sound a closed door with something behind it, and she sat there with her mouth watering at promises she couldn't have read aloud, wanting things she had no names for, content for once not to know." That's the whole book in one sentence and it's dressed as a girl looking at a menu. It's about *her*, not about him being impressive. And the hollow that had been opening since noon that turns out to be older than noon — that landed in my stomach. I was hungry reading it.

And the right hand. The right hand every single time, a rule he keeps without thinking, "one more thing he knew that she didn't." I noticed it two paragraphs before she did and then she noticed it and I made a noise. Because I know what the other hand is for, and *she doesn't*, and he's not going to tell her, and that's a whole different kind of intimacy than the one she thinks she's in. That detail is doing so much work for so little money.

What I'm genuinely moved by: she stops performing. The thread of conversation goes and she lets it go and nothing bad happens. She isn't watching herself eat. "She let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could." I have never had a book name that specific exhaustion so cleanly — the constant background labor of keeping up your end — and then just let a woman set it down for ninety minutes. That's not a romance beat, that's a *relief* beat, and it's hotter than most sex scenes I've read this year.

The kiss. Oh, the kiss. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he doesn't argue, doesn't lean, doesn't charm, just smiles like she's told him something good about herself and *starts walking*. And her feet don't move, and the hand goes taut between them. That's the best physical beat in the book so far and nobody took their clothes off. The exception being hers. The second kiss being hers. He leaves the last inch where it is, twice. The grin coming up inside her mouth and him feeling it and smiling back inside the kiss — I put the book down and looked at the ceiling.

And here's the thing that makes it unbearable in the good way: I am sitting over here *holding the whole file*. I know Randi rode him on a couch two nights ago and drank wine and said *tell me*. I know he said "she's fun, you'll like her." I know Randi asked whether Vee would like the bench. So every tender thing in this chapter is double-exposed — the plainness of the text with no reply demanded of her, which genuinely moved me, is also someone who has done this before, with someone, deliberately. He left the quiet in it without being asked. Did he? Or does he know exactly what it does? And the worst part is I can't tell, and I don't think he can either, and I think that's the actual engine of this book. He's not a predator with a mask on. He's a man who does this *well* and is doing it inside a game he and his girlfriend started in bed, and the better he is at it the worse it's going to be.

Cassie saying "That's not a boy. That's a man" — and Cassie said *Goodbye, Vee* four chapters ago, flat, the whole word. I have not let go of that. Cassie is the only person in this book who can see, and she keeps declining to come along.
