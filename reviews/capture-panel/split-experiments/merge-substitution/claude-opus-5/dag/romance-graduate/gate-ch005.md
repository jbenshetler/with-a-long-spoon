REACTION

Okay so I was braced for this chapter to be the bad kind of inevitable — the blurb already told me Randi steers her toward Pace "as if by accident," so I figured I'd be watching a setup I'd already been spoiled on and feeling smug and bored. Instead the thing I did not expect: the coffee shop scene is *the best meet* I've read in probably two years, and I knew the whole time it was a trap, and it worked on me anyway. That's the trick. I was Vee. I knew better and I was Vee.

The math. I want to say something about the math. I have read approximately nine hundred scenes where a man demonstrates competence at the heroine and it is coded as hot, and all nine hundred of them are him being impressive. This one is him *not* being impressive on purpose — reading the problem like he's never seen it, asking what her professor said instead of what he'd say, making a margin mark instead of a rewrite, and then putting the pencil back in her hand at the exact line where she can finish it herself. "She hadn't known she was allowed." I made a noise. And then the actual content of the help — two things that won't give on their own, put them at right angles and they become one plain thing, easy to name — like, I see you, Helen Rivers. That's the whole book sitting in the margin of a calculus problem and she doesn't once lean over and tell me it's a metaphor. Thank you. Thank you for not explaining it.

And then the gut-punch I didn't see coming is Randi's hands. The cup. The cup goes up to her mouth twice and no sip happens. Her hand goes to the cup the second Pace's eyes land on Vee, and again when he answers Randi's stats question *correctly and briefly and then turns back to Vee.* Randi — who gave him her name as one bare word, no bit, no adjective routine, because she couldn't perform in front of him right then — is sitting at that table watching the plan work and *not liking it*, and she doesn't know she doesn't like it, and Vee explains it to herself as "she's bored." That's the most erotic thing in the chapter and nobody took their clothes off. The woman who ran this whole hunt is already losing, three days in, and the loss is arriving through her hands. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the jealousy is in the COFFEE CUP.*

Cassie is doing something to me. "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter sat in my chest like a splinter and I didn't love that I noticed it — and now Cassie's in this chapter doing the one thing nobody else does, which is looking straight at the thing and saying it out loud. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." That's a *demolition*. And the healer line — a whole semester up until two keeping a business major alive in a video game — tells me more about who Vee has been to men than three pages of backstory would. Cassie is the only person in this book with no angle and I'm scared the book knows that and is going to do something to her.

The froyo. Please. I have been so bored of dates. But — she builds the cup too big and for once her mother's voice isn't in her head saying *that's plenty, that's enough now*, and she bites her lip knowing it'll drip and decides she doesn't care. That's the whole arc of a starving girl in one cup of frozen yogurt. And then she says the humiliating true thing — *this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time, and it's froyo* — and he doesn't pretend and doesn't make it large, he just lets it have its space and then says *your tart's holding up.* I'm sitting here with my chest hurting about soft serve structural engineering.

What's turning me off, mildly: the "the thought did not finish" move three separate times. First time: lovely. Third time: I can see the author's hand on the lever. And there's one paragraph — the long one where Vee works out exactly what Pace did with the pencil — where the book starts walking me through its own cleverness, "the path of a person who wasn't performing — not showing off, not hiding it either." I had it. I had it from the pencil. I don't need the annotation. That's the only place my eyes slid.

Heat-wise this is the quietest chapter yet and I don't resent it, because the charge is all displaced into objects — forearms, a pencil changing hands, a cup that doesn't get sipped. But I notice I'm three chapters out from the bench now, and the bench was a 3, and if chapter six is another lovely daylight conversation I'll start to feel the pattern. What I actually want is for Vee to be in a room with both of them at once. The thing I'm most afraid of, which is also the thing I'm most reading for, is the moment Vee finds out there was a plan. And the thing I didn't expect to be afraid of: Randi finding out she's not the one running it.
