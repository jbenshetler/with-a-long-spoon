WHO'S WHO

**Randi** — the one I thought was Vee in chapter one. Twenty-one, sorority, Standards board (so she rates a single room), black hair, startlingly blue eyes she's built her whole adult life around being looked at for. Pace's girlfriend, four or five weeks in. Mother lives on egg whites and tofu; Randi eats thin apple slices and counts them. Has an accent that only showed up when Vee was listening — long esses, arched a's — and I still don't know if she's putting it on or if Vee invented it. She is funny, she is warm, she is genuinely fun, and she is drowning.

**Pace** — math PhD, finishing, younger than he should be for that. Small house, cheap laminate counters, furniture he built himself with his own hands, house kept too warm, Steely Dan low in the front rooms, old white pickup. Courtly to the bone — *may I*, *may I choose*, asks before he takes. Sliced the apple thin because she'd eat thin slices. Built the bench. Keeps a gym bag behind the truck seat. There was a Daphne once, long ago, whose door he didn't respect, and it cost him more than he knew anything could cost, and he learned. He is the only person in this book not lying, and that's exactly what makes him dangerous.

**Vee / Vivienne Thorne** — the actual heroine, and she didn't show up till chapter four. Econ major, stats class, dark red hair that sparks copper, freckles, snub nose, built on a lavish scale and has nowhere to hide in a swimsuit. Synchronized swimmer at sixteen. Gets to class early, has the right pen. From somewhere that doesn't get its name read out at the front of rooms. Her mother lives in her head and says *floozie* and *that's plenty, that's enough now*. Honor society induction coming — weeks off, there's a russet silk dress in a lobby magazine she can't afford and a black one in the back of her closet that'll do.

**Cassie** — Vee's roommate, four words a day, ball cap, ugly navy swimsuit, lab at noon, rec season lungs. The only person in this book with no angle. Says the true thing out loud in one flat sentence while everyone else trails off mid-thought.

**Brooke** — chapter president. Does not stand up. Terrifying.

**Sloane, Kinsey, Meg** — set dressing, mostly. Meg's boots were a half size too big.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Chapter one: Randi on a bench Pace built for her, spanked and edged until she begged in a voice that wasn't hers — and then cried, and then locked his bathroom door and decided at the mirror that he could have the face and the one he'd found was never coming out again. *Not ever.* He heard the lock and honored it, because of Daphne. He thinks he had a remarkably good evening.

Chapter two: she goes home in last night's clothes and Brooke dismantles her in four minutes of smiling small talk. Then lies on her bed in her shoes under a synthetic duvet that is *not* the cashmere blanket, and doesn't cry.

Chapter three: in bed, he asks would she like to taste another woman and she comes on the question, before she can decide anything. Then, dressed back up in herself, she repackages it: *only in a threesome. With you.* He lets her keep the costume. Then the walk on the quad — *I point, you pick* — and the taxonomy of nos, and then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bare and Randi's stride drops out from under her and she says *Yes* with nothing around it. She engineers the collision. Comes back glowing with a name and a class schedule. *Do you think she'll like the bench?*

Four: Randi finds Vee's row in the lecture hall, takes her to Chi Latte, calls her *dear*— no, calls her *love you, girl* and *see you later*, and Vee cannot afterward recall one word that was said. Cassie declines to come and says *Goodbye, Vee.* The whole word.

Five: the meet, staged at the place on Wilson. Pace crosses the room, offers to help with calculus, reads the problem like he's never seen it, hands back the pencil at the exact line where she can finish it herself. Euler's at right angles. He asks what's drawing her to econ and then *listens.* Takes her number, leaves without his coffee, texts in twenty minutes. Froyo that afternoon — the cup she builds too big and decides not to care about. Randi's coffee cup goes to her mouth twice and no sip happens.

Six: Randi comes to his house that night, locks the deadbolt first thing, straddles him on the couch, feeds him red wine out of her own mouth, and makes him *tell.* He tells her about the cup. He does not tell her the true thing Vee said on the walk. He never drinks a drop of the wine.

Seven: the rec pool. Bleachers yell at Vee's body on the only route to the water. She goes under and the world switches off. Cassie splashes her instead of asking.

Eight: dinner at Christine's little place. *May I choose?* She hands him the menu. She eats without watching herself eat, loses the thread of what he's saying and lets it go. Mint tea in brass cages. Under a tree on the way home she says she doesn't kiss a man she's known a couple of days, and he smiles and starts walking her on, and she doesn't go — the hand goes taut — and she kisses him. Twice. The second one hers.

Nine: she tells it to Cassie (confession) and then to Randi (deposit), and Randi turns it a quarter inch until *I made an exception* comes out as *because of him.* Randi pretends she can't remember his name — *Paul?* — and snaps her fingers. Brooke turns up at the Smoothie Bird, prices Vee top to bottom with a warm smile, mentions a Thursday Vee knows nothing about. And Randi takes Vee's hand on the white tabletop in front of her and says *Vee's my dear friend.*

Ten: the hike. Meg's boots rub; he takes her bare foot in his hand on a boulder, moleskin, relaces it, apologizes for not asking about the boots. The summit — lake, burning ridges, silver sky, and he watches her face instead of the valley. Horseradish sandwiches. Cherries. She spits a pit; *we take nothing and leave nothing*; he puts his own pit beside hers and doesn't look. She tells him about the russet dress and puts it back on the shelf; he says *that sounds like a beautiful dress* and does not pick it up again. Then rain, hard and cold. In the truck he gives her his only dry shirt and gets back out into the weather with his back to her. She knocks on the glass, crooks a finger, and unbuttons her wet shirt for him with her mother's voice in her ears — and looks down at the fourth button on purpose so she can watch him see. He doesn't move a hand. Then she reaches for his hand to go further and it's shaking, and she stops, puts it on her bare belly under the flannel, and says *we should go.* A campus cop eats whatever she was about to say at the dorm. She goes in wearing his flannel.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace: I keep bracing to catch him and I keep not catching him. I don't think he's running a cruelty. I think he's a man who is unusually good at reading what a person needs, inside a game he and his girlfriend started in bed, and the better he is the worse this ends. The two things I can't forgive him for yet are small: he kept Vee's true sentence from Randi and called it respect (first wall in that house, built facing his girlfriend), and he's pocketed that russet dress. He is also the one most likely to get wrecked, because he's the only one who believes what he's seeing.

Randi: I came in with her as the villain and I'm nowhere near that now. She scares me *for* herself. The water-bottle cap under the thumbnail. The duvet sentence that breaks off. *He's hard to say no to* in a key she can't sing in. She wants Vee and she can't be a woman who wants a woman, so she'll be a girlfriend being generous instead — and underneath that, I think she wants a girl who doesn't ration. She also wants Pace's attention back, and doesn't know that's what the cup-with-no-sip was. *Vee's my dear friend* is the first real thing she's spent, and she spent it in front of Brooke, and I believe it because her hand was damp. I don't know whether she's lying and I don't think she does.

Vee: I love her and I'm frightened for her and the book has made me complicit in watching. Everything good happening to her is real and was also arranged. She's owed nothing and has been given everything, and she doesn't know there's an invoice.

Cassie: the only clean love in the book. She could have shouted at the bleachers and she kept it for Vee. She saw Pace's look in one sentence and she said *goodbye* in a lecture hall and didn't fix it. She's going to get subtracted by omission — nobody will be cruel, Vee will just stop being available — and that will hurt worse than cruelty.

Brooke: worse than the bench. A woman who stops people without standing up.

WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

Randi's head. I haven't been inside it since the wine and I'm nervous about what's growing in that silence — it's been four chapters of Vee's warmth and I want to know what the architect is doing while her plan goes beautifully. The russet dress: he's going to produce it, at the induction, and it's going to land on her like a gift and cost something she can't see. The three of them in one room — that's the chapter this book is walking toward and I dread it correctly. The moment Vee learns there was a plan. The moment Randi learns she isn't the one running it. What Cassie does when she finds out, and whether anyone notices her leaving. Whether Randi ever lets herself be on that bench again, or whether sourcing Vee is precisely how she avoids it forever. And the sentence the siren ate.

Also, practically: heat. The bench was the last real heat and it's nine chapters back. The truck was charged as hell with one shirt coming off, and the displaced stuff — pencils, menus, cups — is feeding me better than most on-page sex. But I'm counting. I want these people in a bed together before this volume closes, and I want the book not to go coy when it gets there. It hasn't yet.
