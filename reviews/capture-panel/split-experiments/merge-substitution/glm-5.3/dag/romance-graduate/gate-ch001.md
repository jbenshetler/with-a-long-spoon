REACTION — Okay. Okay okay okay. I sat down expecting to have to give this the one-chapter audition and instead it went and did the thing — the actual thing, where explicit and psychologically exact aren't trading off, they're the same event. The blurb promised "psychologically exact" and I thought, sure, everyone says that. Nobody says that and then writes a woman sitting on a man's lap eating thin apple slices he cut *face-up because she eats them faster that way*. That apple detail did more to me than most full sex scenes have in months. He's studied her. She doesn't clock how studied she is. I clocked it and it made the back of my neck hot.

The bench reveal — "Where I've been laying my underwear?" — I laughed out loud. That's a real woman's deflection, not a romance heroine's quip. And the spanking itself: I've read a hundred of these. The craft is usually in the strike. Here the craft is in him crouching by her upside-down face and saying "hi" — twice, and the second time mid-scene — and taking her heels off like it was liturgy. The "good girl" earned its landing because he made her beg her way *past her own please*, the one she uses on waiters, and I felt that distinction in my sternum. That's writing that knows what performance is for.

And then the bathroom. Oh, the bathroom. The POV flip into Randi's head is where I stopped being horny and started being *afraid* — the good afraid, book-friend afraid. The smile settling over the face that had begged. "The face was lying." And her deciding — the deadbolt line, which is perfect because she locks his front door coming in, always, first thing, and now she locks the bathroom door going out from herself — that he can have the face, not ever the one he found. And he's sitting on the bed thinking it was a beautiful night. He's wrong and he doesn't know it yet and I do, and that gap is where the whole book lives now. This is exactly the woman the jacket said Vee can't see past, and I already ache for Vee being handed to this person who has just decided to go back behind glass. Permanently. "Not ever." Chills.

Small wrong note: he's still hard and does nothing about it and the narration *tells* me "that's not what this night was for" — the book brushing its own sleeve admiringly. Once, fine. Don't make a habit of it, Rivers.

The one thing I'm watching: Pace is being written as nearly flawless — patient, exact, respectful of the locked door for reasons involving someone named Daphne, thank god, a past, give me that door. If he stays this frictionless I'll sour. But Randi's bathroom decision just armed the book. I'd text my friend at 1 a.m.: "the dom cut the apple thin and the mean girl cried alone in the bathroom and put her face back on. starting book one is going to hurt. bring wine."
