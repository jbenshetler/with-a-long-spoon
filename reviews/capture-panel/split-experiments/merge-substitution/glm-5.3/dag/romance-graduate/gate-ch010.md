REACTION — Okay, the truck scene is the hottest thing in this book so far and nobody even touched anybody. Her unbuttoning her wet shirt in front of him while the mother-voice runs in her head — "the voice was the heat and the heat was the voice" — that line did something to me, because that's the actual psychology of shame and desire and most books are too cowardly to write it that plainly. Almost.

But the moment that got me wasn't the strip. It was his hand shaking. She reaches for his knuckles to kiss them, all momentum toward "the next thing," and his hand is shaking because he stood in the rain so she could change in private — the man who wanted her so badly he couldn't move gave her the cab and took the downpour, and never said a word about it. And she just stops, and puts his cold hand against her belly. That's the tenderness I keep reading for. That beat alone was worth the chapter.

Also, unrelated: she still has his flannel. He drove away in a soaked blue shirt with no dry one. That is going to matter to me until she gives it back.
