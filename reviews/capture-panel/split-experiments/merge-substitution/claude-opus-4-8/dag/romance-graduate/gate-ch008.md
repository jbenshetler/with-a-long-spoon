REACTION

Okay so I see exactly what this chapter is doing and I don't entirely resent it, which is new for me with a structure this obvious. We've had the bench chapter (Randi, devastating), Randi debriefing Pace on the couch tasting the wine she wants to be the girl he's describing, and now we loop back and get the froyo-to-dinner sequence from *Vee's* side, and the whole architecture of the con is clicking into place — Randi steered her, Pace "happened" to be in line, the pointing game chose her. I know all of that. Vee doesn't. And the awful clever thing is that knowing it doesn't blunt the chapter, it sharpens it, because I'm watching a genuinely tender seduction land on a girl who has no idea she's the third point of a triangle two people drew in bed. Dramatic irony as an erotic device. I'm into it and slightly ashamed of being into it.

The restaurant. God. The *food* as the whole language of it — her being hungry for once and not watching herself eat, the hollow that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon," him choosing for her with "May I choose," the right hand every time (I clocked it, and so did she, a beat late, which is this book's entire signature move — the body knowing before the mind gets there). This is the same man who sliced the apple thin for Randi because she'd eat thin slices. He *feeds* starving girls. That's his thing. That's literally in the blurb — "quietly starving" — and I half expected the book to explain that to me and it didn't, it just let me watch him do it twice now, to two different women, and let me feel the chill of the pattern underneath the warmth of the scene. That restraint is why I'm still here.

The kiss under the tree worked on me, which I did not expect to admit. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" / she doesn't go / "But — I'm making an exception." He gives up the inch and she takes it back. The whole choreography of consent-as-the-erotic-engine that the jacket promised — "choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done" — and it's actually true on the page, not just a line. He leaves the last inch where it is and makes *her* close it. Twice. I felt that.

What's keeping me up, though, is the dread. Because I just read the bench chapter. I know what being *seen* by this man did to Randi — it cracked her open and she fled back behind the face and decided *not ever*. And now here's Vee, who has no face to hide behind the way Randi does, no armor, just a soft hungry girl who's never had anyone wait out the end of her sentences, walking straight into the exact machine that broke the woman who built it. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Randi asked. I have not forgotten that. This froyo-and-mint-tea tenderness is the bait, and I know it, and Vee is going to get *fed* and it is going to cost her everything, and the book is making me complicit in how nice it looks.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the con artist love-bombs the mark over hummus and it's the most romantic thing I've read in a year and I want to throw the book across the room." Also: this is three Vee-POV-ish warm chapters in a loose row now (Chi Latte, the pool, this dinner) and I can feel the book's rhythm — sweet, sweet, sweet — and I'm starting to want the blade back. I want Randi and Vee in a room together. I want the triangle to actually touch. The seduction is gorgeous but I've now watched Pace do the courtly-attention thing enough times to know the moves, and a fourth helping of "he listened like it mattered" without the other shoe will start to go slack on me.
