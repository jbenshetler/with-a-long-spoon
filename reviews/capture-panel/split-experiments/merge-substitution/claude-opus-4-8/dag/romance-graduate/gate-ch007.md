REACTION

Okay so this is the first time the book has let me just *be* with Vee without a predator in the frame, and I didn't expect to feel protective of her this fast. The pool chapter is doing a thing I actually love — it took her out of the froyo-and-flattery glow and showed me the machinery underneath. That green-and-white one-piece from sixteen, "when there'd been less of her to fill it." The rolled towel carried *down at her side, not up against her.* The entrance she makes down the bleachers because the only route is past the audience. I've read a hundred heroines described as insecure; I have almost never watched one *choreograph* her own body through a hostile room the way a dancer blocks a stage. That's real interiority, and it's the same muscle Randi and Pace have been reading and using on her. Which is the part that chilled me, honestly — I'm sitting here going, *oh, this is the raw material. This is exactly the hunger they clocked on the quad.* The book doesn't say it. It just lets me carry it.

And then the underwater passage. "The room switched off." "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." That got me. The one place nobody's looking, nobody wants anything, the one thing that was never taken from her — and she *grieves, a little,* in the quiet. God. That's the whole wound in one paragraph, and it's set right next to the two men planning the "best year of her life" like a knife on a counter.

Cassie. Cassie is the only person in this entire book so far who wants nothing from Vee, and the splash — the tiny lobbed sheet of water with "outrage out of all proportion to the actual amount," a grief-interrupt disguised as a prank — that's the most genuinely *loving* beat in seven chapters. "They will never know the love of a woman." I'd text a friend about Cassie at 1 a.m. I trust Cassie. Which means the book has now given me exactly one person to lose, and I'm watching that word "Goodbye, Vee" from chapter 4 sit in my stomach like a stone. Cassie *sees* it. Cassie keeps flatly refusing the game — the single-word name, declining the coffee, naming the way Pace looked at Randi "like somebody's great-aunt." She's the smoke detector. And the book keeps gently turning her volume down inside Vee's attention.

Do I miss the heat? A little. This is my second non-Pace-bench chapter in a row if I count the froyo, and there's zero charge here — it's clothed, it's a best-friend scene, it's a bridge. But it's a bridge that's *building the thing the heat will cost someone*, so I don't resent it the way I resent filler. It's the opposite of filler. Two pages wouldn't have done it; this one earned its length because the underwater beat needed the room.

What I notice I'm doing: I'm no longer reading this as hot. I'm reading it as a slow-motion crime. That's a weird and good place for a spicy book to have put me by chapter 7.
