REACTION

Okay so here's what this book keeps doing to me, and this chapter is the cleanest example yet: it lets me sit inside Vee's whole radiant good day — the brass-cage tea, the spin that nearly takes out the closet door, "look at me," *because of him* — and the entire time I'm two floors above her watching the rigging. I know Randi engineered the collision. I know Pace is a hunter who built a bench. And the dramatic irony isn't a gimmick here, it's *grief.* The girl is happiest when she's most owned and she doesn't know it. That's the engine and it hasn't dulled.

What got me this chapter specifically — Cassie. God, Cassie. "Goodbye, Vee" back in chapter 4 was a splinter I'd half-forgotten and now it's throbbing again, because this is the chapter where Vee quietly *trades* her. "It isn't really her place, the smoothie one." The little hand-wave at the leggings and the matched hair. Vee's narrating her own abandonment of the one person who shoots water at her sadness from ten feet away without asking her to explain it, and the book lets her think it's *fine, it's healthy, people have their own people* — and then "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the knife. Not that she's cruel. That she's being drawn open exactly the way the jacket promised and the first casualty is the real friend. I wanted to reach into the page.

And the Randi mechanics in the smoothie place are so precise I got a little sick with admiration. The fake French-date catastrophe — which I know is a lie, I *know* she was on the bench Friday, I read it — deployed to make Vee feel chosen by comparison. "Ask and then ravish," said a beat late and low, at Vee's own volume, Randi climbing into the fantasy beside her. The accent Vee keeps mis-explaining as Switzerland/Paris. And then the actual knife-that-looks-like-a-gift: "Vee's my dear friend," the cool damp hand on the white table in front of Brooke, costing Randi something real in front of her own queen. The horror is that it probably *is* costing her something real. That's the line the jacket drew — how real it gets for each of them. Randi is grooming this girl and also, visibly, starting to actually want her, and those two things are the same gesture now.

What I love is that nobody explains any of it to me. The book trusts that I clocked the Thursday Randi "missed" was the bench night. It trusts me with the brow money, the oat milk Vee orders because Randi's standing there, the duvet that was "a duvet" back in chapter 2. It never once leans down to tell me what the subtext is. After the hundred books that narrate their own cleverness, this feels like being treated as a grown-up.

Heat-wise this was a zero and I did not care, which is the thing — chapter 6 and chapter 8 banked enough charge that I'll ride a dry chapter happily as long as the dread keeps tightening. And it did. Vee saying "he'd ask, though. Before he ravished me," running her hands down her thighs — that's the only warm current, and it's warm precisely because I know she's right, he *will* ask, that's his whole weapon.
