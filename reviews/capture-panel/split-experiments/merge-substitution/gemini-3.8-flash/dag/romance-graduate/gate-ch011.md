REACTION

Oh, my chest.

That was barely two pages, and it completely leveled me.

After that awful, chilling "Goodbye, Vee" from Cassie outside the sorority house—where it felt like Cassie was washing her hands of the whole shiny trainwreck—I was dreading how Vee coming home would go. I expected Cassie to be sarcastic, or cold, or to drop another heavy, warning stone into the water that Vee wouldn't understand.

Instead, this was pure, unvarnished love. Real female friendship, the kind that doesn't cost you anything, the kind that isn't engineered over thirty-dollar smoothies or wrapped in an audition for a threesome. Cassie sat there in her sweats with Vee's blood type ready, listened to the breathless, tumbling girl-spill about sandwiches and a tight blue shirt, and then she *clocked* the shift. When Vee got to the part about knocking on the glass—about calling him back into the rain-streaked cab to strip in front of him on purpose—Cassie didn't lecture. She didn't scold. She didn't call her an idiot. She just went still and held space for it.

*"Vee," Cassie said finally. Soft. Not* Oh, Vee. *Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break.*

I almost cried. Because Cassie knows. Cassie knows Vee is in freefall. She knows Vee just crossed an invisible, irreversible line into something profound and terrifying, and instead of snatching the flannel away or telling her he's a creep, Cassie just says, *Borrow real boots next time. Keep the shirt.* It’s the gentlest permission to be human, to be reckless, to be happy, even while you can feel the tragic irony humming under the floorboards because *we* know what Pace and Randi are doing.

Vee saying *"I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain"* is the thesis statement of her entire existence. She feels capable for the first time. She feels powerful. And that’s what makes what Pace is doing so breathtakingly seductive and so monstrously perilous. He’s genuinely feeding her. He’s giving her the tools to feel her own strength, which only binds her closer to him.

Short chapters usually make me nervous—they often feel like lazy scene-breaks or authorial throat-clearing. But this wasn't filler. This was the exhalation after the storm in the truck. A perfect, quiet landing gear.
