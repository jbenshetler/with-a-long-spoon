**REACTION**

He sobbed at a truck that didn't turn in. I had to put the book down and walk around the kitchen for a minute.

For two chapters I've been lying in cold rooms with Vee and Randi, and I've held a grudge against this man for a week of silence over Christmas. Now I see where he's been. On New Year's Eve he was in a double-wide diner eating "the usual" with Sheri. Then he drank alone in the dark for the first time in his life, going through four photographs "slowly and in order, like reading." The next morning there's bleach on the bathroom floor and boiled chicken out of a freezer bag. He's the man who cooked fra diavolo at midnight, and now his own food is something "his mouth didn't want," eaten "because he told it to." Vee was picturing him at the stove feeding some blonde, and he can't even feed himself. That one got me.

Then the porch, from his side. "The true answer had been there, whole and ready. He had felt the words rise. Then he had chosen the smaller truth." And: "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." That's the answer I've been begging for since the crackers. It wasn't a lie of omission about Randi, which is what I'd half talked myself into. It was Daphne. She's the only woman he ever said it to, and there was no one to tell she was gone, because she was the person he would have told. He decided the words were what made a loss unsurvivable. I believe it completely, and it takes the coldness out of every non-answer he's given her. It doesn't excuse them, though. Sheri put it better than I could: "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." She didn't soften it, and he looked down. I love her for that.

Sheri, my God. "Her name is Vee." / "Yeah. The one with the big tits." I winced, and I laughed anyway, because she's crude and fond and wants nothing from him, a Cassie in cycling boots. Then Paige, the girl with the big laugh, and "call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas." So Vee's little flicker of jealousy at Thanksgiving was aimed at a woman who doesn't date men and has nowhere else to go. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." He gave that to the right person.

Things the book settled for me: Randi hasn't been to the house since the morning with the green satin. Six weeks. So my suspicion about who was there during Vee's five nights away was wrong, and the place was empty. It also means Randi's "fairytale" came out of a long silence on both sides. He's waiting for her to come, "the choosing was hers," and he's waiting for Vee the same way. He has turned his whole consent ethic into never reaching out first. I admire it, and right now it's destroying him. Two women are lying awake waiting on him, and he's sitting on the stoop waiting on both of them.

What still sits wrong with me: even drunk and grieving, he's still adding to the picture of the three of them in his kitchen. Vee in his sheet with too much honey in her tea, Randi with her feet up. And "Randi had told him so while he was inside her, and it still worked on him now." Honey. You're crying over her taillights and you're still building the staircase she can't see. "Whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her." Asking whether she can take it isn't the same as asking her.

The prose went short and clipped after the hangover, sentence after sentence, and for a page it felt like a different hand. It worked, though. It reads like a man holding his head very still.
