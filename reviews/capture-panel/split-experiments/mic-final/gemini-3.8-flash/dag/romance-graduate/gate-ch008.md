REACTION:

Oh, this is devilish. It’s genuinely diabolical what this book is doing to me, because on the surface, this is the sweetest, most tender, most exquisitely written first date I’ve read all year. The torn steaming bread, the way he orders for her—*“May I choose?”*—the mint tea in the little brass cages, the way he just absorbs her chatter and makes her feel completely safe and fed. I was melting. I swear to god, if I didn't know what was actually happening, my toes would be curling purely from the sheer, delicious romance of it.

*“The other ones always made you answer. Right then, on the phone... So they'd know where they stood... He just left it with me. Like it could keep.”* God. That hit me right in the chest. That is such a real, profoundly accurate observation about the difference between an impatient boy and a deliberate man.

AND YET.

Because we have the jacket, and because we watched him work Randi over on that bench in Chapter 1 and pick Vee like a target on the quad in Chapter 3, every single crumb of gentleness is laced with absolute arsenic. Pace is a craftsman. He’s taking her entirely apart by being *so good to her*, because he knows she’s starving for it. He’s not rushing her, he’s not pushing for the kiss—hell, when she pulls the "I don't kiss a man after two days" line, he just accepts it and turns to walk, knowing *damn well* that leaving the space is the exact thing that will make her step into it! He asks before he takes, until choosing becomes the trap! The jacket warned me: *"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem."*

Watching her break her own rule under that tree, watching her lean in and taste the mint in his mouth while she thinks she’s making an exception for a nice, quiet grad student who powerlifts... I had my hand over my mouth. It's so hot, and it hurts, and it's thrilling. She is so completely doomed.
