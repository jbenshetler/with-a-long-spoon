REACTION

Okay, this is the first chapter in a while where I felt the trap closing and still wanted to walk into it.

It's a date chapter with no heat on the page. The only physical contact is a hand and a kiss. I normally resent that. But I read it knowing the setup. Randi pointed Vee at him. Pace is running a game, and I just watched him run the opening on a girl who thinks she's choosing. "May I choose?" is the sexiest thing in the chapter, and it's also a little chilling. Handing over the menu is the first small surrender. He cast it as courtesy, and she took it as relief. He eats with his right hand and she copies him. She lost the thread of the conversation and nothing bad happened. I know what he's building, and she doesn't. That gap is where the charge comes from.

The food writing is real. The soft names she can't read, the bright dish and the dark dish, the hollow that turns out to be older than noon. That's hunger doing the work the sex scenes would normally do, and it's pointed straight at the Vee who flinched at the pool and at her mother's voice. The line "Fine has never once been enough" is basically dramatized here.

The "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" beat, and then the exception, is nicely done. It's her choice, and she makes it twice. The jacket promised "choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done," and this is the first on-page delivery. I did get the tug of the second kiss.

What nags at me is that the book sometimes explains itself ("the old reflex… the part of her that always caught the thread"). Vee also has been a bit too grateful and soft for three chapters running. The tenderness is doing the job of the spice, and I'm on borrowed credit from Chapter 1, which was explicit. I want that credit repaid soon.

Also, Randi isn't in this one at all. I missed her. I know she's across town with a locked bathroom door and a smile she's wearing, and that's the pull of this book for me.
