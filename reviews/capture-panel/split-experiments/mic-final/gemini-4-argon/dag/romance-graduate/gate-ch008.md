REACTION

Oh, I am in *so* much trouble with this book, and so is Vee.

First of all, Cassie continues to be the secret MVP of this entire novel: *"That's not a boy. That's a man."* Exactly. Thank you, Cassie. The way he asked her out on Thursday night and told her not to answer until Friday so she wouldn't have to perform an answer on the spot? That would have ruined me for every twenty-one-year-old boy on campus immediately.

And then the dinner. God, the way this author writes food and eating in this book is doing something to my brain. In Chapter 1 we had Randi and the thin apple slices, policing every bite because of her mother and the sorority scale. And here we have Vee, who also has her mother's voice in her head usually, just completely sinking into warm bread and baba ghanoush and tabbouleh because Pace takes the menu and says *"May I choose?"* and then feeds her without watching her eat. And she *goes under*. Like, she literally goes into subspace at a Lebanese restaurant over pita bread and mint tea! The way the room blurs out and she loses the thread of what he's saying when he licks the dip off the side of his hand, and she realizes she doesn't have to do the exhausting work of "keeping up her end" because he's carrying the whole evening—that is the most accurate, non-corny description of why competence and quiet dominance are erotic before anyone's clothes even come off.

Also, we got the answer to the forearms! Powerlifter. ("Badly. Best I've ever done is third.") I love that he isn't some naturally ripped alpha god who never works out; he's a short-ish math PhD who lifts heavy in the mornings and builds furniture and eats with his right hand only because he knows the cultural etiquette and doesn't make a show of knowing it.

And the kiss at the end. I caught the callback immediately—it's the exact move he did with Randi at the door in Chapter 1 ("he backed off and let her decide"). When Vee panics and throws up her rule (*"I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"*), he doesn't push, doesn't pout, doesn't even do the fake-patient wait that's actually pressure. He just accepts it warmly and starts walking again. Which is the exact thing that makes her stop her feet and choose it herself. And then when she gives him a small kiss, he gives her a small kiss back and leaves the last inch for her to cross again. The blurb said *"he asks before he takes, until choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done,"* and watching that happen in real time without the book underlining it in red ink is *chef's kiss*.

What's giving me this delicious, awful stomach-drop, though, is that this felt completely, 100% like a real first date where two people are falling into something deep—and I know Randi is probably waiting at his house right now with a glass of wine wanting the report. Or Vee is going to tell Randi at lunch tomorrow. Pace is so genuinely tender and attentive with Vee here that I cannot reconcile it with the game, which is the whole point. He's not faking how he looks at her or how he treats her; he's really giving her this. Which makes the setup ten times more dangerous.
