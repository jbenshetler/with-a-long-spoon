REACTION

Oh, this one's quiet and it still got me, which is the test these days and this book keeps passing it.

Section i is the first time Vee has let the word live in her own chest — *I love him* — and Rivers was smart enough not to make her say it out loud. She breathes "Peter" to the sleeping shape of him instead, and that's the whole thing, isn't it. The real name, the one he gave her in the quilt chapter, said to him when he can't hear it. She's practicing the biggest word on the one that's safe to try. And the detail that undid me a little was the *bras* — her clothes going looser, the band riding higher, her body "quietly burning down whole weekends of him and she'd never asked it to." That's such a true, bodily way to say *I am changed by this man in ways I'm not tracking*. Not a montage, not a mirror monologue about self-worth. A bra band. I believe it.

And here's the thing that keeps this book off my DNF pile: she lands on *I can live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying.* And I know — I KNOW, because I'm a step ahead of her from page one like the jacket promised — that this is exactly the settling-in that's going to cost her everything. She's decided the silence is enough. She's made peace with the one open wound the whole book is built around. That's not relief for me, that's dread wearing relief's coat.

The drawer. God, the drawer. I was braced — when section iii opened with the scrunchies gone from the counter, my stomach actually did the thing, because I thought *here it is, the first withdrawal, he minded, she was too much.* That's the old trained flinch, the manufactured-conflict reflex a thousand bad books put in me. And instead she opens the drawer and he's folded his whole ordered careful self down into the bottom one — pressed in too tight, built to hold half of it — and given her the top. The easy one. The one your hand goes to first. No note. *He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead.* I had to put the phone down for a second. That is the most married thing a person can do and he will still not give her the word for it. That's the cruelty sitting right inside the tenderness — he'll build her a drawer, he'll build her a dress, he'll build her a *place*, and he will not say the one thing. And Daphne is right under all of it: a man who was taken in at fifteen and calls it luck, now making a place for someone the way a place was once made for him.

What I'm suspicious of, still, at 1 a.m.: there was no Randi in this chapter. None. Three sections of just the two of them, the realest and most domestic it's been, and the pipe didn't run once — unless it did and we just didn't see it. After last chapter's *sticky fingers* and the sugar on her lip she never tasted, a chapter this intimate with Randi completely absent reads less like a break and more like the eye of something. The drawer is the deepest Vee and Pace have gone alone, off the grid, unwitnessed — and that's precisely the thing that can't be brought to the Saturday table either. The book is quietly building the second room. His, hers, and not Randi's.

What I'd text a friend: *he gave her the TOP DRAWER and crammed all his own stuff into the bottom one and still won't say I love you and I think I'm going to be sick about it in a good way.*
