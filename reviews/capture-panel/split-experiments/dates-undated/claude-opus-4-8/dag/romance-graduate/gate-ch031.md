REACTION

Oh, she didn't walk in late and make them turn. That's the thing that got me — she'd built that whole movie in her head, the room turning on her entrance, and Helen Rivers just quietly took it away from her. The actual room is "warm and dull and kind," nobody watches the navy girls any longer than the walking takes, and I felt that deflation in my own chest because I've sat in that room. And then she gets the better version anyway, the real one, and she *chooses* it: the first step small and safe, and she could have minced the whole way and nobody would ever know — and she takes a breath and makes the second step long. That's the walk. That slit falling open off her bare thigh and her *giving the room her leg on purpose,* chin level, face giving away nothing. I had to put the book down for a second. That's the most alive she's ever been on the page.

And it's hers. That's what undid me. Her mother's not there — and that line, "nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of" — God. The one time she's free of that voice, and she walks into a room of strangers with nothing under the silk and nobody to flinch for. The shame was the whole architecture of her and for ninety seconds it just isn't in the building. I've been dreading the induction for a hundred pages as the detonator and instead it's the one chapter where she's unsupervised and she uses it to *own herself.* That's a hell of a reversal.

The photo. The photo is the knife. She sends Pace the uncropped frame and he'll see the dress do the thing he built it to do and have "no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." The pipe — the thing everything travels down — and she just kept something back from it, on purpose, without even clocking it as a betrayal of the grid. She's got a private room now too. Her and Pace both hoarding. That's new and it's the first real crack.

What I keep turning over at 1 a.m.: Pace wasn't there. Berkeley. The three of them in one room STILL hasn't happened and now the induction is spent and they weren't even in the building — the book keeps loading that gun and not firing it and I'm starting to trust that the not-firing is the point. And Randi did the ankle strap this morning, two fingers, sat back to look — she's still in the frame even when she's not. I'm a little suspicious of how *good* this felt, how clean the win was, because this book doesn't give clean wins. The last line won't let me have it either: she can't tell if it's pride or just the heat still running, "or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing." Her self-possession and her arousal fused into one thing she can't separate. That's gorgeous and it's also exactly how he built the dress — pride and heat sewn together so she can't tell which she's wearing.

No Randi debrief yet. I want to see what happens when this one hits the Saturday table — or whether it doesn't.
