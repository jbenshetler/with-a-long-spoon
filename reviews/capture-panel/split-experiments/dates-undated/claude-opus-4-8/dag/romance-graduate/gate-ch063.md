REACTION

Okay so this is the one I'd have been bracing against on paper — morning after, hangover, no skin, a man leaning on his own counter talking for four pages — and it gutted me anyway, and I'm a little annoyed at how completely it worked.

Because here's the thing. I came out of 61 knowing. The jacket told me and chapter 61 showed me: Randi and Pace, together, him going down on her and her saying *I want her to know exactly what she does to me* and him answering *she makes me happy* — the two of them running Vee between them in bed like a current, in love with her and with the running of her, and I read that whole chapter with my stomach in a fist. And then 62 took my blonde away — my beautiful invented rival detonated into a five-foot gay woman with crooked makeup who looked Vee up and down and went *oh* — and that was a gift, the best table in the book, because it was warm and it was also quietly another brick in the cage (now Pace has *witnesses*, now there's a Valentine's dance *settled*). So I came into 63 going, all right, show me you're not just going to let me float in bread smell again.

And instead the bread was the bait. He baked a cinnamon raisin loaf while she slept off three ciders and left ice water on a folded paper towel so it wouldn't ring the wood, and I thought *here we go, the warm-weekend choreography, I've logged this four times* — and then he said *Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else,* and the whole chapter turned over in my hands.

One friend. Not a number a person has — she's right, that landed exactly where it was supposed to. And then the *why* of it, which is the best sustained piece of Pace interiority the book's given me: the window that opens once, men making friends from the middle of the pack by losing to each other, and him never once in the pack. Fifteen in a building of grown men. Eighteen when his own age finally showed up and by then he had the patent and the house and *money of his own, which is worse than having none.* I believed every word. And then the flat one — *weren't safe or weren't welcoming* — no name, and he doesn't give a name, and she does the arithmetic she wasn't asked to do and lands on the black-window line from before, *less than staying,* and I did it right alongside her.

What actually got me, though, the thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: Vee setting him down at eighteen next to her own freshman year — the trays, the dorm carpet she can still smell, the forty raw kids to a floor all starting at once — and him fitting *nowhere* in the picture. A seminar of eight, youngest by years, teaching himself to cook because there was no one to eat with. That's the book doing the thing I keep begging books to do: making me feel the shape of an absence through a person who actually has the opposite. She has Meg's closet and Kayla's fry tax and Cassie's *oh, Vee* and Randi. He had Daphne. One, doing the work of four, and the only reason he had even that was the wrong thing she still won't name — and the book let her hold both at once, *stacked and unreconciling,* and didn't resolve it for me, didn't explain its own subtext. Thank God.

But — and this is the knife, and I don't think Vee heard it — *women misunderstand the friendship. It gets awkward.* His one surviving friend is a woman who can never want him. The only kind the world lets him keep. And Vee hears that as *poor Pace, he's so lovable it ruins him,* and reaches across and holds him — and she does not for one second apply it to herself, or to Randi, or to the fact that she is sitting in his robe having been handled unconscious and tucked in, inside a thing that is categorically not a friendship and never was. He told her the whole mechanism of how people fall into him and she patted it like a sad fact about the past. The gap's still wide open and now it's got a diagram.

The hug at the end — her crossing the counter, both arms, Sheri's hug repeated, *with her whole weight committed,* and his arms coming *not fast,* and him looking past her at the middle of the morning, not happy, not sad, somewhere else. That's a real turn in the bond and I felt it in my chest even knowing what I know. The problem for *him* is she's falling further in while the thing under the whole book goes unlooked-at, and the problem for *me* is this is a man I was supposed to keep resenting and I just want to feed him.

Zero heat and I didn't miss it, which almost never happens for me. But I'll be honest about the count I'm keeping: this is now the quiet domestic kitchen with the rolled sleeves and the window again, and the depth bailed it out *this* time, hard. It can't bail out the next identical morning. I need Randi now. I need the Tuesday where Vee lays Sheri on the table and hands Randi the win — because I know what Randi's face is going to do, and I know Vee's going to call it being right, and I want to be in the room for the thing Vee won't see.
