REACTION

Oh, this one got me. This is the chapter I've been waiting for without knowing I was waiting for it — the two of them just *talking*, under a quilt, tea gone cold, and I didn't skim once. I kept telling myself this book was about the machine, the con, Randi's crawl, and then it put me on that couch and made me forget to watch the door.

The Peter thing. God. *My real name is Peter.* He handed her the name and took his mother back out of the sentence in the same breath, and she *let him* — "He had taken his mother out of the answer. Vee let him." That's the whole relationship in one move: he shows her exactly as much as he's built to show, and she's learned to read the size of what he's not saying. And then her saying it back at the end, soft, "to keep it" — not trying it on, not asking for anything. That undid me a little.

But here's where I went cold, and it's the good kind of cold, the kind that means the book is smarter than me: Daphne. Nineteen and *fifteen*. Two years. And him laying it out in that flat good-order voice, every reason slotted, calling it *luck*, calling it the best two years he'd had, defending it without hearing himself defend it. And Vee holding the ugly grown-up word under her tongue because it wasn't hers to say. I was screaming it in my head. The book knows. It let her know, and it let him not know, and that gap is the scariest thing in the chapter — because THIS is the shape of what he's doing to Vee right now. A brilliant, lonely person taken in early by someone older who "sees" them, who makes it feel like the best thing that ever happened, who names the power imbalance as fortune. He's reenacting Daphne on Vee and he cannot see it. "He had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck." That's the key to the entire book and they slid it under the door so quietly.

And the hallway. Seventh grade, taught himself trig because he loved it, and they moved his desk into the hall and gave him long division for a *year*. "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment." That's Vee's mother and *floozie* — they took her body and made it the thing to be ashamed of. These two broke in exactly matching places and that's the terrible tenderness of it. The scar he still won't tell her about — "Not tonight," a door with a time on it, not a wall. I've been owed that scar for pages and I'm glad they held it. Delay it right and I'll follow.

The math talk should have bored me and didn't, because it wasn't math, it was him finally lit up, the one place he wasn't in the hall. The paint-the-shape-you-can-fill-but-never-cover, the coastline, the three sentences for the ball and the leaf and the stars — "most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it." And then the last line, her feeling it all came to her *one piece*, in six weeks, luck she did nothing to deserve — she's using his exact word now. *Luck.* The same word Randi used — "you're not caught, you're lucky." The word is metastasizing. Everybody's calling the trap luck.

What scares me most: there was almost no heat here and I didn't want any. I wanted them to keep talking. That's new for this book and it's exactly why I'm not putting it down.
