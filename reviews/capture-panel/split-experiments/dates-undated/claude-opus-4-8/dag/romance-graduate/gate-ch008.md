REACTION

Oh, this one surprised me. After three chapters of the machine getting built — the bench, the pointing game, the froyo, the wine Randi drinks off his mouth — I braced for either another seduction beat or a stall. Instead I got Vee and Cassie at the pool, and it's the first time in the whole book I've been let *all the way inside* Vee with nobody engineering her. No Randi curating the warmth, no Pace reading her like a problem set. Just a girl in an old synchro suit that used to fit, walking the bleachers because it's the only lane to the water, taking the catcalls on the chin with a game face she's clearly worn her whole life.

And then she goes under, and the room switches off, and God — *"She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem."* That gutted me a little. That's the whole book's thesis about her said once, clean, and then left alone. The bench chapter told me Vee portions herself; this chapter *shows* me the one place she was ever free, and it's the one place nobody can look at her. She grieves a little down there. I felt that in my sternum.

But what I actually want to text a friend about is Cassie. Cassie is the first person in this book who loves someone without a plan. The splash — ten feet off, barely gets her, "outrage out of all proportion," doesn't move a step closer, never asks because asking makes the sad version bigger — that is the single most tender thing anyone has done in eight chapters, and it's the only tenderness that isn't *for* anything. Everyone else's warmth is a tool. Pace's froyo is a tool. Randi's "love you, girl" is a tool. Cassie shooting a sheet of water over her best friend's head to break her out of a drowning-dry moment is just love. And "Goodbye, Vee" two chapters back with its wrong flat finished weight — Cassie *knows something's coming.* She sees it. The book keeps letting Cassie name the look that slides off her every day. She's the Cassandra and nobody in the story is listening to her, including Vee.

So I'm not turned on by this chapter and I don't think it wanted me to be. It's doing something I didn't expect from a book that opens on a spanking bench: it's making me care whether this girl survives what I already know is being done to her. The jacket told me the plan. This chapter made the target a person. That's the move that keeps me from skimming.

Is it a two-page shortcut? No. It earns its quiet. It's the first breath of pure Vee-interiority I've gotten, placed exactly where I needed to feel the stakes before the trap closes. My only flicker of wariness: I can feel the architecture — "the body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck, where it could wait" is the book explaining its own metaphor to me, one beat too neat. But I'll forgive it because the chlorine and the scull and Cassie's deadpan all landed real first.
