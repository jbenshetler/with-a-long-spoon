Okay so this is the hangover chapter, and I mean that about Vee, not the margaritas. After the dancefloor and that absolutely wrecking midnight drive to Pace where she *bought her own orgasm back inch by inch with the truth* — after all that, this is the book making her live inside what she's done in daylight, and God, it works on me even though almost nothing *happens*. That's the thing I keep clocking: three times this stretch now the book has gone quiet and I keep not minding, because the quiet is load-bearing. It's not idling. She's not oblivious anymore. That's the whole shift. For sixty-some chapters she curated and edited and handed her friends the shiny two lines, and now she's lying in a top bunk with her hand flat on her stomach like a lid on a pot, *knowing exactly where it'll go the second she stops minding it*, and that is a completely different woman than the one who called the composite kisses "Randi being Randi."

The hand on the stomach killed me. "A lid held down on a pot." Because as long as she doesn't give it the thing that would make it true, it can stay only wanting. She's bargaining with her own body using the exact logic she used on Pace about cheating — it's not the doing, it's the... no, wait, she's inverting it now. She told him the first lie of the whole book last chapter — "No. Nothing." — cold, sitting under everything, and now she's policing her own hand so the lie doesn't *retroactively become more of a lie*. That's so precise and so sad.

And the class. Oh, the class. The wintergreen Lifesaver. I want to text someone at 1 a.m. about the Lifesaver, because it's the smallest possible thing — a soft, gone-stale candy passed between two people who've done it a few times — and Randi's fingertip finds the inside of Vee's wrist on the way and Vee's *pulse leaps up to be found* and she nearly drops the roll and tells herself it's static. The skin's thin there, everyone's is. STATIC. She's doing the thing Marsh has been lecturing at her for the whole book — explaining the subtext to herself in order to not hear it — except now I can feel her knowing it's not static while she says it's static. The gap between what she says and what she knows has finally, finally closed to nothing, and she's standing in the nothing.

The reciprocity lecture running under this one — the three obligations, give, take, pay back, a gift as a debt laid on you whether you want it or not — I see you, book. Randi has started saying the quiet part.
