REACTION

Okay so now there's a Sheri. Another woman who knew him before Vee, another one on a handlebar instead of a barstool, and the book keeps doing this thing where everyone in his orbit is a woman who's already taken his measure and finds him worth keeping. I clocked it: Cassie went rapt, the bartender vouched for him, and now Sheri — faster than him on the climbs, two years of remarking on women's backsides across the bars — gets handed the thesis line to say out loud. "You want me to not like her?" / "She couldn't take your heat." That's him telling me Sheri is a flamethrower who's never once turned it on him. The whole chapter is character references for Pace, stacked one more deep.

But here's the part I actually leaned toward: I liked watching him *spill*. "He was past the name before he'd decided to be." He told her about the thumb on the walnut joint, the math book Vee pulled off the shelf and didn't reshelve like it burned her — and he heard himself going and didn't stop. That's the tell I've been waiting for. He's the man who withholds, who says it twice so the noise can't kill it, who covers the bowl and puts it in the fridge — and here he's running his mouth about a girl to a bike buddy because he can't help it. Sheri caught it: "Listen to you." He didn't even know what there was to listen to. *That's* him in trouble, and it landed cleaner than any of the bed scenes have lately.

And then the cold line, the one I'll carry: "And the redhead?" / "Not yet." Everyone's got the folder. Sheri, Randi, Brooke, the bartender, Cassie's starting to. The only one who doesn't know she's the last to know is Vee. The jacket told me I'd be a step ahead of her and the book keeps widening that gap on purpose — now even the woman on the bike is ahead of her. "Two of them at once. How do you sleep? That must be better than your dreams." It's the first time someone's said the arrangement out loud as a *thing a man is pulling off*, flat and ambient, like it's enviable, and that chilled me more than any of Randi's scheming because Sheri doesn't have a stake. She's just reading it off him like weather.

What I'm texting a friend at 1 a.m.: "new chapter, it's TWO PAGES and it's just him telling a cute cyclist about the girl he can't shut up about, and I think that's the point? he's falling and the whole county can see it but her." Did it move me? A little. Did it *do* anything? It confirmed. It's a breather after three big chapters, and I can feel it being a breather — his POV, no Vee, a new face to introduce one more witness. I don't resent a bridge. But this is the quietest the book's been and I noticed I was reading for the two lines that mattered and skating the cold-fingers-breath-over-shoulder stuff. I want the reconciliation at his door. Vee's walking in tomorrow night in the borrowed shoes and I want to be *there*, not out on a bike watching Sheri not-get-winded.
