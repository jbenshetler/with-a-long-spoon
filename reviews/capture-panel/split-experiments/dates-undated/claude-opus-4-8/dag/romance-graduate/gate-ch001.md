REACTION

Okay. Okay. I need to text somebody.

So I bought this for the premise on the jacket — the plan, the game, the triangle — and I came in braced for the usual: a billionaire-adjacent "dominant" man and a doe-eyed girl and a bench that's really just set dressing for a spanking scene I've read four hundred times. And the scene *is* a spanking-and-edging scene, the mechanics are nothing I haven't seen. But my God, what this did with them.

Two things cracked me open. First: the whole chapter I think I'm reading about **Vee**. The blurb told me about a shy twenty-one-year-old named Vee, and here's a twenty-one-year-old, apple slices, sorority, chapter dinner, startling blue eyes — and I sat there the entire time building the wrong woman in my head, feeling tender toward her, rooting for her to let herself have this. And then: *"There you are, Randi."* I actually put the book down. Because the jacket told me Randi is the one running the game, Randi is the predator, "the first woman Randi has ever let herself want" — and this chapter just showed me Randi *on the bench*, begging in a voice that came from below, crying without knowing it, and then locking a bathroom door she's never locked and deciding — *not ever* — to go back behind the face forever. The architect of the game got taken apart tonight and nobody in that room except the reader knows it. That reframing is vicious and it's earned and I did not see it coming the way it came.

Second: the man. Pace should annoy me. The courtly-dom-who-asks-first is a type that's gone so stale it curdles. But he's not written as a fantasy of control, he's written as a specific person with a specific damage — the *Daphne* door, the "he had thought that loving someone was a kind of permission," the fact that he respects the lock *because he once didn't*. And the gut-punch is that he's *wrong* about tonight. He's sitting there thinking he fed a starving girl and found her hunger and he'll answer it as often as she brings it — warm, happy, generous, completely misreading that he didn't open her up, he *scared her shut*. He found her and she's going to spend the rest of the book making sure he never does again. The dramatic irony is the whole engine and the book trusts me to feel it without a single line explaining it to me. No one circled it in red. Thank you.

And the cap detail — the water bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail where it would hurt, *slow and steady* — while he's being tender. That's the tell she doesn't know she's giving and he doesn't catch. That's a writer who knows exactly what she's doing.

The heat worked on me, which is the part I don't take for granted anymore. The apple game on his lap, bite-kiss-bite, the hands rising and turning back before they arrive — the *withholding* is the erotic engine, not the acts, and the prose knows that. It's genuinely written. It's explicit without being a parts-list. It fed me instead of performing for me.

What I'm suspicious of / watching: the "quietly starving girl" interiority here is phenomenal but it's Randi's, and the blurb promises me Vee as the center with Randi as the one doing the steering. So this gorgeous chapter is, I think, a *prologue to the trap* — it's showing me the trap's teeth before the mouse arrives. I want to know if Vee gets an inside this rich. If she does, this book is going to wreck me. If Vee turns out to be a flat ingénue and only the schemers get interiority, I'll feel the floor tilt. But on the evidence of one chapter: this is the first thing in months that gave me depth AND heat in the same breath and didn't make me choose. I'm in. I'm so in it's a little alarming.
