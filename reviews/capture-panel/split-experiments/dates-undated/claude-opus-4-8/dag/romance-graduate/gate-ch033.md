REACTION

Oh, this one. This one got me and it didn't do a single loud thing to do it.

First — thank God for a chapter where nobody's being handled. After the induction and the "we find out" chapter, I half-expected the machinery to grind back in: Randi on the phone, the pipe, somebody scripting Vee's next move. Instead I got the two of them alone in a house with the trick-or-treaters not coming, and it was the most relief I've felt reading this book in ten chapters. The air cleared. And the thing is — it's *her* chapter. Start to finish, she drives it. She's the one who sets him in the recliner with a flat hand and makes him stay. She's the one who invented the game. Three weeks ago she was being buckled into shoes by Randi's two fingers and now she's running a private little theater and he's the audience she's commanding. "He stayed put. She'd put him there." I felt that in my teeth.

The two-costume structure is genuinely clever and I didn't see it land until it landed: costume one is the dress — the thing he *made*, the thing the whole room got on the induction stage, given back to him alone since he missed it. Costume two is nothing at all but the heels. The world got the dress; he gets the body the dress was hiding. "The other is for the world. This is just for me." That's the whole blurb, isn't it — *in his house in the woods, the body she has always hidden becomes the thing he treasures, and every step further is hers.* And here it's literally hers: she picks, she walks, she stops him from rising. The naked walk down the hall in four-inch heels, the house's warmth touching everything she isn't wearing, and him not moving a muscle, letting the looking be the touch — that's a 3 on heat with zero contact. Clothed, or unclothed and untouched, doesn't matter. The charge was in the *withholding of the touch*, which this book keeps turning out to be better at than most books are at the act itself.

And the restraint is characterization now, not coyness. His hand coming partway up and being put away, "the putting-away cost him something she could see" — that's him doing to himself what she's always done to herself. The wall that picks her up and sets her down is sitting on his hands because she told him to. After last chapter where she watched the wall *flinch* for the first time, this is the sequel to that discovery: she has power here, and she's testing the shape of it, and it's intoxicating to watch her find out she can hold a man that still.

Now — the dread. It's quieter in this chapter but it's not gone, and the fact that it's quieter is almost worse. Because I noticed what I *didn't* see. No Randi. No Saturday table. And last chapter's whole private thing — the spanking, the "we find out," the thing she can't bring to brunch — that's sitting underneath this sweetness like a floor that might not hold. This is the second thing now that's *theirs alone*, off the grid, and the book is letting them build a real room while I know the whole house is rigged. The dress in the cold dark car, fifty feet from the man who made it — she brought the dress and no overnight bag, "nothing for a night she wasn't letting herself plan on." She's lying to herself about staying and the dress tells the truth. God, she's so far gone and it's so lovely and I'm so scared.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she set him in the recliner and he STAYED and I have never wanted to be either of them more and I have never been more sure one of them is going to be destroyed.* And then: *why is the sweetest chapter the one that scares me most.* Because it's real now. The blurb promised me it would become real for all three and that's the cost. This is the realness accruing. Every tender beat is interest on a debt.

Only thing that kept it from being a clean 10 for me: it's a duet, and I love the duet, but some small part of me is starting to lean toward the door marked *where is the third person.* Not out of boredom — out of terror. I want the collision. Two chapters of just them, gorgeous as they are, and I can feel myself bracing for Randi to walk back in and turn all this intimacy into evidence.
