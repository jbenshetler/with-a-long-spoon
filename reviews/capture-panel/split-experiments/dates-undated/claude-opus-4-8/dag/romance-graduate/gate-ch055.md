Okay so this is two quiet ones back to back — 54 planning the dinner, 55 coming down off the weekend — and normally that's exactly where my thumb starts getting itchy. But I didn't resent it, and I want to be honest about why, because it's not just "the writing's pretty." It's that this chapter is doing something underneath the cozy that I couldn't look away from.

She's lying. Not a big dramatic lie — she's sitting in the bean bag with her hair wet telling Cassie the sweater-shrank-in-the-wash story, the whole laundry-accident cover for why she spent two days naked in a man's house, and "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." That line landed like a stone in my stomach. Because I was *in* chapter 53. I know there was no cranberry sweater. I know she walked out her dorm bare under a coat on Randi's instruction with Randi's stripper heels on, and now she's got a tidy domestic fib built around it that she's telling her safest person. She's getting good at the clean cover story. That's new, and it's not cute, and the book knows it's not cute — it just lets it sit there and doesn't underline it, thank God. That's the restraint I keep staying for.

And then — the thing I was actually bracing for didn't come. The taste on his mouth from 53, the "why does he like it so much," the little minor-key question that settled in and wouldn't go all the way. End of 53 she promised herself she'd tell Randi, well, *nearly* everything. I thought maybe 55 would be the Cassie version of that confession. It wasn't. She didn't bring it to anyone. She buried it under a bad-wool joke and climbed the ladder. So now she's carrying TWO secrets she won't look at — Randi's father's name from the stats project, and this small wrong thing about Pace's mouth — plus a lie. The girl's whole interior is becoming a storage closet she keeps shutting the door on, and I find that genuinely more suspenseful than any "will they get back together," because they already did, on page.

Cassie. God, Cassie. "Pace. Shrank your sweater." / "To a doll size." — the flat repetitions, the snowshoes-in-the-house brother-in-law, and then the gut-punch at the end: "My mother says thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again." And Vee's hand stopping halfway to the towel, going to her collar instead, "like a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck." That's the realest love in this book and it's not even romantic. Cassie's mother remembers her. Her OWN mother clocked her weight loss with a nurse's face across a silent table, and here's a woman two states over saving a card. Vee can't even answer it at full size — "spring break, sometime, tell her I said hi" — because the real size of it would mean feeling how little she gets at home. Cassie heard the size and let it go exactly one beat before it needed anything. That girl is the whole heart of the thing and she's being wasted as the safe door nobody walks through, and I keep saying that and keep being right.

The cinderblock wall a hand's width off the bunk, after two nights of all that acreage and his heat at her back — nice. Small, earned, not milked.

What this chapter didn't do: it didn't move the triangle one inch. No Randi, no Pace, no dinner yet, no Sheri, no stats fallout. It's a held breath. And a held breath is fine once. If Friday's dinner chapter is ALSO a warm nothing, I'm going to start skimming for who finally collides. But right now I'm not tired — I'm leaned in, because the lie just got easy for her and the book let me watch it get easy.
