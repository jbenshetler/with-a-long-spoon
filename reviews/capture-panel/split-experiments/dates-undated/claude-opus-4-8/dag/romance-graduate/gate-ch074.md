Oh, this is the chapter where she stops being able to lie to herself and starts being able to lie to *him* on purpose, and the book knows exactly what it just did and makes her lie down inside it.

The easy week got me first. The drawer that didn't fill. The coffee in the thermos for a girl who isn't awake — "because of her." That's the warmest Pace has ever been and I wanted to live in it, and the book let me want that for about a page before it put the phone in her hand. Smart. It made the good thing real so the betrayal had something to cost.

And then the texts. *this drink has a FLOWER in it. a whole flower Vee. what am I supposed to do with the flower.* I was grinning with foam on *my* chin. That's the thing about Randi — the con and the charm are the same muscle, and even knowing what I know from 61, she lands every single time. The missed Tuesday, the seven-o'clock drop in her stomach — that's the tell the book's been building. The class was never about class. It was the sanctioned hour of Randi, and without it she's reaching for the phone before she's awake.

The photo. The *tan lines*. God, that detail. Not the nudity — "the hard bright edge of it against the brown, the fact of a boundary. Somebody had drawn a line on Randi with the sun and Vee could see exactly where it was." That's the most erotic sentence in the chapter and nobody's touching anybody. The hand arriving before the vote. "arrived without a vote." She's completely out of the driver's seat of her own body now and the book keeps saying it in these little clean phrases.

But here's what actually made me sit up in the dark with her: *wish you were here.* Randi is not oblivious. Randi sent that. Randi, who chose her with Pace before she was anything, who is drowning in her own feelings, is on an island sending her best friend a topless photo captioned *wish you were here* at the exact hour she knows Vee will be in bed with Pace. That's not a dog in a raincoat. That's a move. And the horror/thrill is that Vee reads it as *just Randi being Randi* even as her hips move.

Then the clean empty place where the toll should be — "how free the whole thing was, how *fine*" — and she won't look at it because looking means standing still. That's the whole book in one beat. The machine runs on her not standing still.

And then "It wasn't you." Out loud. Out of a mouth on top of him. I actually inhaled. Because she's handed him the one true thing by accident and then she *can't say the word* — "I could not even say the word to deny it; saying it would build the picture." So she turns the phone around instead of speaking. And he pulses inside her, and her flood of relief — *oh thank God, he's just a simple man who likes two girls* — is the saddest, most dramatically ironic thing in the chapter, because I know he isn't simple, I know he chose this girl *with Randi*, and that pulse is not what she thinks it is. It might be the most loaded reaction in the book and she reads it as boring. That gap is exquisite and awful.

The interrogation-by-inches. He's doing the 68 thing again — rationing her own pleasure for the truth, holding her an inch off him until she gives up the tidy version, letting her down an inch for every real word. "The picture." *Nothing.* "Her chest." Down an inch. Every time she smooths, he goes still, and the stillness turns her around to look at what she skated past. And the thing he's dragging out of her is the exact upgrade from January: not *I wanted her mouth* (done to me, passive, already paid for) but *I wanted to be the one doing it. I wanted her in my mouth.* The want has verbs now. She's the subject. That's the whole arc of her body crossing over, and he's the one who makes her conjugate it.

What's got its hooks in me deepest, though, is the end. The quiet after. The January memory coming back *unprompted* — nobody holding her, nothing withheld — "the same hand, the same name, the same man lying under her waiting to be told." And she doesn't say it. And "this was different from the last time, because the last time she had not had to decide." That's the line. January was a reflex lie. This is a chosen one. She's a liar now with full knowledge, in the one safe bed, and she feels it "take up a small permanent amount of room." I believe that room. I've watched this book build it brick by brick.

And the cursor blinking in the reply box. She looks at it. She turns the phone face-down. "Tuesday was ten days off, and she did not know yet what her face was going to do." I'd text a friend: *she can't even write back and she can't stop looking and she does not know what her face will do when she sees the woman. this book is going to make someone bleed and I think it's Pace.*

What I'm suspicious of, deliciously: that pulse. Pace being "not angry, not jealous, not even surprised." The book framed it as Vee's relief, but I read it as Pace getting *exactly what he and Randi engineered* delivered to him warm in his own bed. He's not simple. He's winning. And Vee calling it boring is the third game running right across the surface of the sheets while she looks straight at it and sees a pool.

Three quiet-ish domestic chapters would've worried me. This wasn't quiet. This was the lie turning load-bearing.
