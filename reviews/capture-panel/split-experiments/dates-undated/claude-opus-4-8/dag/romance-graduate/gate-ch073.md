REACTION

Okay so the whole first half of this I was just — warm. Genuinely, uncomplicatedly warm, which the book almost never lets me be, and I knew that was the setup and I let it work anyway. The cabin he maybe *built*. The tikka masala carried up a mountain in a thermos so it'd still be hot on a freezing porch. The dry cider cutting the cream — "those should not be friends" — and him saying *I wanted to please you*, flat, like a fact. Days ago in his kitchen with her nowhere in the room, planning which drink goes with dinner. That's the thing that gets me about him every single time and it's exactly the thing I can't trust, because I know he plans like that about *everything*, I've seen the ledger, I know the care and the running are the same muscle. And she clocks it too — she reaches for the December word, the big one, and it doesn't come, and she *lets it go*, she's good at letting it go now. God. That little bruise she's learned to press down on her own.

The key. The box he made with his hands, the oyster-pale silk inside it — and she *half-remembers that silk against her skin and can't place it* and it slides off her, and I went cold, because I can place it, that's the paint-photo, that's something, the book put that there on purpose and she's the only one in the room who doesn't get to know. "I've never given a key to anyone." A key cut to his door. And I'm sitting here going: a key is a thing that locks, and the last thing-that-doesn't-come-off in this book is on Randi's wrist, and who gives whom the hardware. He gives *house*, never *home*, and she waits half a beat for the hearth-word and it doesn't arrive and she lets *that* go too.

And then the mirror. The blindfold made of his flannel, his smell on it, so she can only feel. The inventory. And look — somewhere in the middle of the body-part catalog, the little toe, the eyelid, I felt the prose start to flex at me, I felt it *reaching*, and that's my hair-trigger, the sentences trying to impress instead of feed. But it pulled back before I bailed, because the whole thing isn't actually about her body, it's about a woman who has spent her entire life getting these things *covered and unremarked* being held in full daylight and gone over inch by inch and not once turned away from. "Heavier than it looks… and the spill is the part I want most." The exact thing she hides, named and wanted in one breath. That's not decoration, that's the wound, and he's pouring warm water directly into it. The speech at the end — *a beautiful body is just a fact, the world's full of them, cold as the marble, it's whose this one is* — that undid me and I hated that it undid me, because it's the most devastatingly effective thing a man has said in three books and it is also *precisely* the thing a man would say who has studied exactly what this particular starving girl needs to hear.

Then "show me." The hands that don't come. Her waiting inside the held breath certain it'll give, and it doesn't give, and she has to do it herself, blind, in the light, while he watches. That's a genuinely new thing — I have not read this, the self-touch as a thing he *withholds his hands to make her do* — and the detail that you can no more spank yourself than tickle yourself, that some of what he gives her she cannot give herself, it needed him. Hot. Legitimately hot, all the way to 3, and smart about it.

But here's the text I'd send a friend at 1 a.m., the thing that made my stomach drop through the floor: at the very end, soft, post-everything, hand moving on her breast — *"Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?"* And she says yes "easy and breathy and barely attended at all, the way you say yes to something small." IT IS NOT SMALL. He just installed a reporting line straight into the one room of her where the truth lives — and we all know her fantasies don't actually stay on *him*, we know whose mouth comes up out of the dark, we know the lipstick. He's asked her to narrate her private wanting to him going forward, and she's agreed without reading the contract, the same way she lied to him about Randi without deciding to. He is either about to find out about Randi through the front door she just unlocked, or — worse, more likely — he already knows and this is him making her hand it to him herself, on her own nerve, like everything else. The courtly gift that's a debt. Marsh, out loud: a gift is a debt laid on you whether you want it. He gave her a key and then asked for one back.

I trust him less than I did an hour ago and I want him more than I did an hour ago and I am furious that the book can do both to me at once with a thermos of curry.
