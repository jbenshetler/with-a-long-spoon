REACTION

Okay so this is the chapter where I finally stopped being able to pretend the Randi thing was subtext. The hands. The *hands*. She's kneeling on the closet floor in her robe with no makeup on and she's doing the shoes up under the long skirt where Vee can't see any of it — and the thumb pressing into the soft hollow behind the knee on the way down, the hand bracing high on the back of the thigh every time Vee lifts a foot, the breath Vee "hoped wasn't out loud." That's the jacket promise cashing out exactly: *lets the reader, a step ahead of Vee, feel every degree of the heat come up.* I felt it come up. And the sick genius of it is that Vee's narration keeps filing it under dress-up, under *the oldest game there is, two girls and a problem*, while my stomach is doing something completely different. The skirt hiding the work so Vee only gets the *feel* and never the sight — that's the exact same move Pace pulled at the fitting, the trouble hidden so only the tending's left. They really did choose her together. They run the same play.

And underneath the heat, the thing that actually scared me: Randi *coaching her back to Pace.* "He won't text you — don't read it as a door closing. He doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." She knows precisely how he behaves after a fight because she's the one he goes home to. She's standing there repairing Vee's aim at the man they share and calling it girlfriend advice, and it's airtight, and Vee drinks every drop of it because Randi *said so* — that line, "the thing Vee had been holding clenched let go of her, all at once, because Randi had said so." That's the whole con in one sentence. Randi is Vee's nervous system now.

The reframe of the jealousy was so smooth it gave me chills in a bad way — "you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about, that's the only thing that matters and you handed it to him for free." Taking the worst minute of Vee's life and turning it over to show her the back of it. That's exactly what the jacket said she does: *hands Vee's greed back to her as a gift.* I keep watching her do the kind thing that is also the kill.

The kiss at the end. Vee catching up a half-beat late to the fact that she *didn't brace.* Her mouth just met Randi's. "It was only what they do." That undid me a little, honestly — because that's the real conversion, not the dress, not the shoes. The body learning Randi the way it learned Pace, learning not to flinch. The goodbye kisses lasting a little longer each time, like the blurb promised, and now the recipient doesn't even tense. That's a turn.

Two small things poked me. The Edna Mode bit — "I have to see what I'm working with" — made me laugh but also made me nervous, the book getting cute, almost winking at its own fun; it got away with it because the laughter is doing character work (the two of them genuinely delighted by each other, which is the trap). And the shopping-makeover shape is one I've read a hundred times — except this one has a predator inside it and the accent leaking long on *gorrrgeous*, so it earns the real estate.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she tied the scarf with her hands over Vee's hands and I had to put the book down. this woman is going to break my heart and she's being so nice about it.*
