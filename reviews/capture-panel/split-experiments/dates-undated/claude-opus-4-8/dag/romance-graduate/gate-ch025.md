REACTION

Oh, *Vee.* No, no, no.

Okay — so first: this is the chapter that breaks the pattern, and I didn't realize until I was in it how much I needed it to. We've been on the bed-and-brunch loop for a stretch, and here Rivers finally takes the thing outside, into a public room full of strangers, and lets it detonate. The dance lesson on the concrete pad is the best clean beat of romance in the whole book since the fitting — the lead arriving before her feet knew what to do with it, "there it is," quiet. I felt the thing he was teaching her in my own chest: *stop guessing, give me your weight, close the space you're keeping because that's what gets you stepped on.* That's not a dance instruction. That's the entire relationship, and — God bless this book — it does NOT stop and explain that to me. It just lets it sit there and trusts I'll feel it. That's the thing I've been starving for.

And then it uses the lesson against her. The exact skill he gave her — close the distance, stop bracing, feel the lead — is the thing she can't do with the blonde's poison in her ear. The second she starts counting, she's behind. The second she starts protecting the inches, she gets stepped on. She braces, and she steps on *him.* "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" — I actually put my hand over my mouth. I heard the glass go past the point where you can catch it the same beat she did. The venom coming out of the shame coming out of her mother's voice — *floozie, you spread your legs like a slut* — it's all one machine and it just ran right over the kindest man she knows.

Here's what wrecked me, and what makes this more than a manufactured-misunderstanding chapter (which, let me be clear, I came in BRACED to hate — the jealous-blonde-plants-a-lie beat is the oldest, cheapest trick on my whole Kindle). Rivers knows I know this trope. So she doesn't let the lie be a lie. The bartender — older, done being asked for things, no stake in anything — confirms every good thing. He leaves them at the door. Doesn't take advantage. *Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey.* So the conflict isn't a misunderstanding Vee needs cleared up. It's Vee's shame reaching out and smashing the one true thing because she can't believe she gets to be the exception. That's not conflict-from-one-unsent-text. That's character. She punished him for being exactly the man she wanted. "She'd done it to a good man." I believe every inch of it.

And the dread — oh, the dread is doing overtime now, because *I know what she doesn't.* I know the blonde is nothing, but I also know the thing in Vee's ear that she CAN'T hear is right about something bigger and worse than country-dance groupies. She's resolving to go to Randi's to be told it's nothing, girl, nothing at all — and Randi is the fire. The one safe harbor she's running toward is the trap. That's the back-cover promise coming due and I felt it close like a hand.

The one thing that pulls me back out a little: Pace leaving. He set the beer down untouched and walked. I get it, I even think it's *in* character — the withdrawal that leaves her at the closed door, that's his whole move — but a small, tired part of me went "here's the night-apart chapter break, here's where I'm supposed to ache for the next one." And I do ache. But I clocked the machinery for a second.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she went to the place he taught her to stop bracing and she braced so hard she cold-cocked him, and now she's going to cry on the shoulder of the woman who's actually doing the thing she's scared of. I am not okay.*
