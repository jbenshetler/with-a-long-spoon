Oh, she drank from the plate she never touched. That last line undid me — "a thread of sweetness, sugar and butter and something warm under it, the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." Randi took one neat bite, set it down, said *delicious*, and left it. And it ends up on Vee's mouth from the kiss. That's the whole book in one lick. I actually put the phone down for a second.

But let me back up, because this chapter did a thing I've been dreading and wanting in equal measure and I'm shaking a little. The whole brunch is Randi *edging her across a tablecloth.* That's what it is. She's not extracting anymore — well, she is, the pipe's still running, everything's going to Pace eventually — but the direction has fully flipped the way the practice room promised. Randi runs the vanilla needle in ("I had him filed under spicy… and this is all very. Vanilla.") and Vee spends herself trying to prove otherwise, laying each piece of the spanking on the table, and every confession is a hand on her own body. The book's been building this grammar for chapters — telling it *is* the having of it — and here it finally boils over and Vee has to go to the bathroom. Over brunch. At eleven in the morning. I was so warm and so scared at once.

And then Randi's Radford story. The young Robert Redford, the dark corner, "he is just starting to find *my* dark corners—" and the phone rings and he leaves and she takes care of herself in a stranger's bed. Named, known, *seen* — and then instantly handed the pastry plate so Vee can't tell if it was a door or a trap.
