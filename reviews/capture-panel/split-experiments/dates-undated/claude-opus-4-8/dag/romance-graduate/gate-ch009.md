REACTION

Okay, so I went in braced. After the froyo chapter and then the water-wings interlude I'd started to worry the book was going to coast on how *nice* Pace is, and a whole chapter of a man being kind at a restaurant is exactly the kind of thing that puts me to sleep — I've read the sensitive-tutor date a hundred times, the "he actually listened to her," the slow hand-hold under the tree. On paper this is the most trope-shaped chapter in the book so far. And it did not bore me, and I'm a little annoyed at how much it didn't.

Because the thing this book keeps doing is it doesn't let the niceness stay cute. The whole dinner is Vee getting *fed* — and I have Chapter 1 and Chapter 3 sitting in me like a lead weight, I know what feeding means to Pace, I know "let's go feed you" is the line he said to *Randi* after they pointed at Vee across a quad like she was something on a menu. So I'm watching Vee melt into the softest, safest evening of her life — her feet leaving the floor in the pool was the same move, the same giving-over — and the whole time I know the restaurant table is the baby version of the bench. "May I choose?" Yes, please. The relief of surrender going through her "more than she'd have guessed." She has *no idea* the exact shape of the thing she's sinking into, and Randi was on his couch two chapters ago drinking wine out of his mouth asking *tell me, tell me* about this girl eating too much froyo. That's the horror running under the sweetness and it is doing its job. I am not relaxed. I am the opposite of relaxed.

What I actually loved: the losing-the-thread of his conversation and *nothing bad happening.* That's the real seduction and the book knows it — not the forearms (though, fine, the forearms, the rolled cuffs, the always-the-right-hand thing that I clocked and have no explanation for and want one). It's that she stops doing the constant maintenance work of being Vee, the keeping-up, the watching-herself-eat, and she gets to just be somewhere. For a girl who portions herself down to thin apple slices, being allowed to build a leaning tower of froyo and not hear her mother — that's the eroticism. The book understands that about her and it's not explaining it to me, it's just letting it happen.

The kiss. God, the kiss. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he just *lets it close.* Doesn't lean, doesn't charm, doesn't wait her out. And then her feet don't move and she pulls the thread herself and makes the exception and *takes it,* and he leaves the last inch for her twice. That's the same choreography as the bench — he backs off and makes her choose, and the choosing is the charge. It's consistent all the way down, which is why it's hot instead of just sweet. The mint still in both their mouths, the grin coming up inside the kiss. Fine. I felt that.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "he's doing the SAME THING to her he did to Randi and she thinks it's a love story and I want to scream" and also "why is the right hand a rule, what does the right hand mean, I need book two to pay this off." I'm a little in love with Pace and I fully do not trust what loving him costs. That's the exact knot this series is selling and I'm caught in it.

Two quiet-ish chapters back to back now (the pool, then this) — but this one isn't a bridge, it's a trap being baited, and I can feel the spring under it.
