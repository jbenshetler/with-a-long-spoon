WHO'S WHO

Vee — Vivienne Thorne, twenty-one, econ, the redhead I now know is the mark. Lush, copper hair, freckles, laughs with her whole throat like she's forgotten she can be seen — which is the exact thing she can't afford and doesn't know she does. Portions herself down to thin apple slices, mother's voice living in her mouth, cardigan over the better shirt "on purpose." Loves water because it's the one place her body was never a problem. She's being fed and she thinks it's a love story.

Randi — black hair, startling blue eyes, sorority royalty, sits on Standards. The one I thought was Vee in chapter one until "There you are, Randi" took the floor out from under me. She's the hunter — walked into Vee at the dining hall on purpose, runs the warmth like a tool. But she got taken apart on that bench first, begged in a voice from below, locked a bathroom door and decided *not ever* to be seen again. Fake lifted accent she puts on. Can't ask for anything plainly — seduces her way to it even with Pace. The want's always too big for her face and she keeps the face on over it.

Pace — the mathematician. Grad student, finishing a PhD, small house, cheap counters, builds his own furniture (the bench). Courtly, exact, "may I choose," always the right hand (a rule I still can't explain and want paid off). Asks before he takes, makes the choosing the charge. Has a dead-and-gone wound named Daphne — went through a door she didn't open for him once and it cost him. Respects locks now. Genuinely kind, which is the most frightening thing in the book because the jacket already told me what every freely-given yes is for.

Cassie — Vee's real friend. Ball cap, flat "Cassie," four words a day. The only honest instrument in the book, the only love here that isn't a tool. Clocked Pace in two minutes, said "Goodbye, Vee" like watching someone off a pier. The splash at the pool — ten feet off, barely gets her, never asks because asking makes it bigger. She's the Cassandra and she's being quietly walked away from, chapter by chapter.

Brooke — chapter president, warmth as weaponized status, measures you kindly head to toe and stays warm the whole way. Sized Vee up at the smoothie place.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Chapter one: Randi on the bench, taken apart, decides to go back behind the face forever — Pace reads it as a triumph, a starving girl finally fed. He's wrong. Then Randi and Pace, the lovers, cruise campus and point at women until Randi's body says *yes* at the redhead — Vee. Randi runs a clean shoulder-collision con so Vee apologizes to *her*. "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

Then the courtship, all from inside Vee now: Randi befriends her (stats class, Chi Latte, high table, "love you girl," "dear friend" in front of Brooke). Pace "happens" into the coffee shop, solves her calculus by handing the pencil back, takes her number. Froyo date, then a proper dinner — Middle Eastern, "may I choose," she loses the thread and nothing bad happens, she kisses him first under a tree. Randi debriefs Pace on his couch, drinking the one glass of wine out of his mouth — he withholds Vee's realest moment from her. Vee tells Cassie, tells Randi; hiking date coming Sunday. The machine is fully assembled and Vee's in the net, happy.

Randi — the crawl, in the good way.
