REACTION

Oh, this is the one I'd text a friend about at 1 a.m. — the switch itself is the gut-punch. Three chapters I've been sitting inside the people running the con, and now suddenly I'm *her*, the mark, and she's so happy. That's the whole horror of it and the book just... lets it be happy. Vee counting beats of not-looking-at-the-door, making the yes come out smaller than it actually is so she won't seem too eager — I *know* that girl. I've been that girl at twenty-one, wanting so hard to be chosen by the shiny person that you ration your own delight. And I know exactly what Randi's "*There* you are" is doing because I watched Pace say the same words to Randi on the bench. That echo gutted me. The book didn't underline it either — it just let me carry it in. That's the trust I've been waiting for from a book.

Cassie. *Cassie.* The whole chapter the author plants a quiet person who won't take the bit's open door, who says her name flat and picks her pencil back up, who clocks the predator in two minutes flat and gets out — and then "*Goodbye, Vee.*" The whole word. Not see-you, not bye. A longer parting than a Wednesday. My stomach dropped. Cassie *sees* it. Cassie is the only one who sees it, and she said goodbye like she's watching someone walk off a pier, and Vee smoothed it over because smoothing-over is her whole nervous system. I want to climb into the book and grab Cassie by the sleeve: *no, say more, make it land.* But she won't, because that's not who she is, and the restraint is exactly why it's terrifying.

And the detail work — the apple-portioning from chapter one has become Vee's whole relationship to the room: the floozies she half-wants to be, the cardigan over the better shirt "on purpose," her mother's voice living in her mouth. The transcript-is-blank thing — that she kept the *feeling* of being chosen and not a single actual word Randi said — that's so true to how predation-by-charm actually works that it made me cold. Randi's fake accent, the esses running long, and Vee inventing a Swiss school to explain it: the mark builds the glamour herself. God.

What turns me on here is nothing and everything — this is a zero-heat chapter and I didn't want heat, I wanted *this*, the slow closing of the trap, and the dread is its own charge. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is still ringing from last chapter and now I've met the she, and she's lovely, and she has no idea. I'm not bored. I'm sick to my stomach in the best way. This is the thing I came for: grown-up intelligence AND the engine still running hot underneath.
