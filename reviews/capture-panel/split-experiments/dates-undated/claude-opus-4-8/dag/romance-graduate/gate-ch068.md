REACTION

Okay. Okay. I need to just sit here a second because this is the chapter. This is the one the whole book has been walking toward and it did not blink and I did not blink and I think I forgot to breathe somewhere around "the real story, and I'll give you what you want."

First: the drive. She's still wearing the thong that soaked through, she takes off the skirt, she starts to be a good girl about it — *I have a boyfriend* — and then she steps *back into Randi's skirt* to drive to Pace. That detail wrecked me before anything even happened. She's going to her boyfriend wearing the other woman's clothes with the other woman's name in her mouth and telling herself she's doing the faithful thing. "I don't want anything in the car with me that might start asking questions." The book knows exactly what it's doing and it's not explaining it to me, thank God, it just lets her drive.

And then the sex. I have read a thousand confession-fucks and they are always either punishment or titillation, and this was neither, it was *surgery*. He goes hard off her shame and she's glad — "that part still worked, then" — and the book lets that be true and ugly and real at the same time. The inch-by-inch thing, him rationing her pleasure for truth, should have been a cheap dom bit and instead it's the most honest interrogation in the book, because she can't lie with her body held open around just the head of him. Every time she tidies the story — "she moved me around," "danced bigger" — he just *stops*, and the stopping turns her back around to look at the thing she smoothed over. That's not a kink mechanic, that's the whole engine of the book made literal: the con has been letting her curate, and here's a man who won't let her, who physically will not accept the shiny two-line version. The irony that it's *Pace* doing it, Pace who still doesn't know where her confessions go, Pace who is this gentle with the truth — God.

And he knew. "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" — and he doesn't say Randi, he steps around the name "like a gentleman stepping around a puddle," and the whole room knows the name is the only thing in the room. "You keep saying her name. Not me." He is so far ahead of her. He is so far ahead of her and he's being *kind* about it and that is the scariest thing — because I know what I know from the carry-forward, I know Randi reports to Pace, I know they chose Vee together, and this whole tender inch-by-inch confession is possibly also *intelligence gathering*. The jacket's third game. She knows she's in two. Watching him draw the dancefloor out of her stroke by stroke, I could not stop thinking: who is this really for. Is he comforting her or is he debriefing her. And the horror is it might genuinely be both and he might not be able to separate them either.

Then the lie. "Anything else you want to tell me? About Randi." And she runs her whole clean self-image — it's the *keeping* that makes you a cheat, and I don't keep things — and then the goodbye kisses surface, dozens of them, never the cheek, not even the first, "she had taken that tenderness entirely wrong," and her own hand in the dark with the name in her head. And she says "No. Nothing." The first lie she has ever told him. "It went down into her and sat there, cold." I actually said *oh, Vee* out loud, in Cassie's voice, because that's the voice this book has trained me to grieve her in. She just became a person who keeps things. She crossed the line she defined herself against to keep from looking at the thing underneath, and she did it in the one safe bed in her life.

And the ending. Lying in his arms, sore in the bad way, "raw inside, down low, the kind you got from not stopping when you should have" — and her mind goes straight down the county road and back to a cluttered classroom and a girl in it, and she can't make it come back, and "What is wrong with me?" No answer. The book gives her no answer and gives me none and that's correct, that's the only honest place to end it.

What I noticed: the book finally stopped idling. I wrote in my own notes two chapters ago that one more identical Tuesday and I'd check the exits — and instead it fired every gun at once. The mixer kiss, the confession, the first lie, Tuesday looming. And it did it without once explaining its subtext to me, which is the thing that usually makes me throw literary romance across the room. It trusts me with "he stepped around the name like a gentleman stepping around a puddle" and lets me do the math on what that means about *him*.

Heat and dread fused into one thing here, which almost never happens for me and is exactly what I came to this book starving for. I was turned on and sick to my stomach simultaneously and I did not want either feeling to stop.

What I'd text a friend at 1am: *she LIED to him. she drove there in the other girl's skirt and confessed everything and then he asked the one real question and she said nothing and it's the first lie she's ever told him and I think I'm going to be sick, send the next book.*

I'm not setting this down. Tuesday is circled in the dark and so am I.
