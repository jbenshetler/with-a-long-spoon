WHO'S WHO

Vee — twenty-one, and this is the stretch where the floor she's been dancing on finally showed her it was a trapdoor. She got waxed bare because Pace asked (said yes before her mind caught up, like always), went to Randi's sorority mixer in Randi's skirt as Randi's *date*, and kissed Randi on a dancefloor in front of a room — and it stopped being a bit somewhere in the middle and she knew it and kissed her back. Then drove the county road to Pace at 1 a.m. *still in Randi's skirt* and confessed it, and when he asked "anything else about Randi," she said no, nothing. The first lie she's ever told him. It went down cold and stayed. She's not the curator anymore — the curator could always hand herself the shiny two lines; this Vee lies in a top bunk with her hand flat on her stomach like a lid on a pot because she knows exactly where it goes if she stops minding it, and the name that comes with it. She still won't say what she wants. Cassie asked her straight — *is this what you want* — and she went and looked and came up against the edge of the thing and stepped off before she could see its size. "What is wrong with me?" No answer. That's where she is.

Pace — everything turned over in 61. He's not the good man with the confession-gap I spent sixty chapters worrying about. He *knows* where her confessions go. He's the other half of the machine. The reveal was the two of them in bed, him going down on her, her riding him, and the whole time they're trading sightings of Vee — "she kisses me first now," "she looks at you, too" — in love with the same girl and in love with the running of her. And yet. "She makes me happy." I believe it and I can't tell if it's a man in love or a man pleased with his acquisition, and I think the book can't-tell on purpose. Then 62/63 cut the other way again and made me want to feed him: the blush at Sheri's table, the one-friend speech, the father he hasn't gone home to since sixteen, *weren't safe or weren't welcoming,* the window that opens once. And 68 — he drew the whole dancefloor out of Vee inch by inch, rationing her own pleasure for the truth, won't accept her tidy version, stops cold every time she smooths something over. Tender interrogation. Possibly both comfort and debriefing and he may not be able to separate them either. "You keep saying her name. Not me." He's so far ahead of her and he's being kind about it and that's the scariest thing in the book.

Randi — she's the one I ache for now, because she's the one who said the unsurvivable thing out loud to the co-owner and got no smile back: "She's not the only one in love." She's cracking all over this stretch. The thumb that stopped dead on the back of Vee's hand when Vee said Sheri *enjoyed* her — jealous, not of a woman who wants Pace, but of a woman who looked at Vee the way Randi won't let herself. "And you liked it," flat, the music gone out of it. At the dance she came back from the kiss a *moment behind,* drew a breath, blinked, shook her head, and only then reached for the grin — she lost her own plan for half a second. "Having you at the dance was so great. I've wanted to do that for the longest time." *Having.* She's leaking the quiet part now. And she still runs the machine while she bleeds: handed Vee the skirt off her own rail, steered her to Sondra for the wax (and left out the worst half — the roll-over — the way she and Pace left out the whole truth of choosing her). The con and the love are the same muscle. She's got the most to lose and she knows it. Brooke already named her price.

Sheri — the invented blonde collapsed into her and I loved it. Five foot nothing in boots, two-tone perm, makeup a hair off the line, a voice built for a barn, and *gay,* out, loud, watching the ivy-sleeved bartender like a parade. She looked Vee up and down in the taproom and *enjoyed* her — the soundless appreciative *oh* at the shoes — and a month of Vee's armor fell off in one laugh. She's Pace's one friend, his real family, the proof of who he is off-duty. Came out last March, lost the whole tablecloth in a month, cried in a two-step, and Pace drove her home and was on her porch with donuts and fixed the lock. She's the one who handed me *he hasn't been home since sixteen.* Not in any room with Randi yet. I want them colliding.

Cassie — still the realest love in the book, still not romantic, and this stretch she stopped being the door nobody walks through and *opened it.* "Is this what you want?" — plain, worried, the one question nobody else will ask in words. Vee swung the old weapon at her (you've never liked her) and Cassie stepped over it without looking down. Then dropped the whole thing and held out the Cocoa Pebbles — gave Vee a want the size of a bowl because she could see Vee was standing on no floor. That's the mother's-card tenderness again. She's seen something, the night of the skirt, the eyebrow that went up and stayed.

Brooke — still the furnace-warm reader who knows what every room is worth. Ran the mixer, spiked the punch with Chambord nobody could taste ("spend a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime"), carried Vee's whole little history by heart, and *chose not to* read out the wrecked-Friday. Kind, Vee thinks. I think she just filed it. Her ultimatum to Randi from last stretch is still live underneath all of this.

The Love bracelet — Cartier, no clasp, "taking it off requires tools," "a real commitment," on Randi's wrist the whole book and I only just clocked it. Who put it there. It doesn't come off. The book handed me that one for later and I'm holding it.

WHERE THINGS STAND

The jacket's third game is no longer a mystery to me, only to Vee: Randi and Pace are lovers and co-conspirators, in love with Vee, running her between them, and they chose her together before she was anything to either. 61 showed me the ledger and it reframed every oblivious Tuesday back to the start — none of it was idling, it was the other side of this scene.

Then the guns fired in a row. Vee met Sheri (the dreaded rival dissolved). Learned Pace has one friend and why. Got waxed bare for him. Got painted head to foot in fruit and gesso-ed with her own wetness and handed back a photo he kept no copy of. Went to the mixer as Randi's date and kissed her for real. Drove to Pace in Randi's skirt, confessed, and lied for the first time. Sat through a Tuesday class beside Randi with the wall between what-she-says and what-she-knows gone to nothing — the Lifesaver, the wrist, the pulse leaping to be found, "static." Bolted the dinner to protect herself and booked Saturday brunch out of the ringing in her neck. And Cassie asked the question and got no answer.

Valentine's is loaded and dated: Brooks & Dunn cover band, the hall out on 460, settled since December, "leave them shoes home." Saturday brunch with Randi is circled. Tuesday class with Randi is a standing fixture she now dreads.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Vee — I love her and I grieve her now in Cassie's voice, *oh, Vee.* She crossed the line she defined herself against (it's the keeping that makes you a cheat) to keep from looking at the thing underneath, and she did it in the one safe bed in her life. I want her to be able to answer Cassie's question and I'm terrified of what happens when she can.

Pace — I don't know, and it's the good kind of not-knowing, the first genuinely new thing in a long run. I can't keep clean resentment and I can't keep clean trust. He's load-bearing good to Sheri and devastatingly tender to Vee and he is *also* the man steering a girl who doesn't know there are three. Both true at once, unreconciling, the way the book makes Vee hold Daphne. I want to feed him and I don't trust what his kindness is for.

Randi — I can't hate her and can't trust her and now I mostly hurt for her, because she's the one who said it out loud and got nothing back, and she keeps running the machine on the girl she's in love with because it's the only way to keep her. She owes Vee the truth and will die first. Brooke's got a price on her.

Cassie — the one I'd keep. The clean love. She built Vee a floor the size of a cereal bowl when Vee had none. Everyone keeps telling me she's just the roommate and she is the truest person in the book.

Sheri — I trust her all the way, which is almost funny in a book where I trust no one. She's the one warm world of Pace's that isn't the con.

Whether the lie she told Pace stays buried or the book makes her pay it, and whether he already knows it's a lie (he watched a goodbye kiss in the roadhouse lot with his own eyes and said okay). WHAT'S FADING

The exact order of the fruit in the paint scene is already fusing — I keep the gesso, the armpit, the breast-smear deliberately left on his face, and the ending, and the middle fruits are going into one brush-leaves-when-you-move rhythm. The wax choreography is blurring into the wrong-hand idea and "roll over for me, hon," the rest of the salon gone. Sheri's horse story and the filthy radio songs are going except that both songs are ruined for Vee for life. Marsh's reciprocity lecture is already down to the one idea — a gift is a debt laid on you whether you want it — the rest of the EDA detail dissolving the way her lectures always do. The mixer's early middle (the boy who couldn't work out which of them the other belonged to, the margaritas arriving unordered) is softening to the kiss and the bracelet. And the whole shape of what I used to think this book was — the con running one-way on an oblivious girl — is gone for good; 61 overwrote it, and I can barely get back into the head I read the first sixty chapters with.
