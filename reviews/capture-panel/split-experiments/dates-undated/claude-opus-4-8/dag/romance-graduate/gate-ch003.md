REACTION

Okay so I had it wrong and I love that I had it wrong. Chapter 1 I read as *Vee* on that bench — the sorority girl, the apple slices, the begging — and the jacket half-let me think that. But that last line, "There you are, Randi," I'd clocked without quite metabolizing, and now it lands: the woman who got taken apart on that bench, who begged in the voice she hadn't used since she was a child, who then locked herself in a bathroom and decided *not ever* — that's the predator. That's the one who, two days later, is cruising campus picking out their girl. God. That's the whole architecture of this thing and I didn't see the shape of it until it closed.

And the pointing game itself — I was braced for this to be gross, two people shopping for a human being off a retaining wall, and it *is* gross, it's supposed to be, but it's written so I couldn't look away from the exact mechanism of Randi's wanting. The threesome dodge — "Only in a threesome. With you." — and Pace seeing right through it, naming it to himself as her making herself "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which he could see was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift." That's the Chapter 1 bathroom logic *in daylight*. She came off the bench having been seen and decided she'd never be seen again, and here's the next move: she'll do the wanting, but only dressed as a present for him. That's not manufactured conflict. That's a person I understand too well.

What's doing it for me is that Pace is reading her the entire time and letting the camouflage stand because he's enjoying her — and I can't tell yet if that's tenderness or management. He's so *controlled.* He pointed at the blazer girl who ran the room because he wanted her himself, and Randi's "God, no" was the realest no of the afternoon, and he filed it. He keeps guessing her taste and being delighted when she surprises him. It reads like love and it reads like a man running an experiment on someone, and the book isn't letting me settle which, which is exactly why I'm still here.

The heat is in the *looking*, not the bed — the bed stuff up top was fine, the three orgasms, the taste-of-yourself thing, solid but I've read it. It's the "yes" that arrives before she can make it mean anything safer that got me. The shoulder-collision con on Vee — a decade of dance folded into three seconds so clean the redhead apologizes to *her* — I actually said out loud, oh, she's good. She's dangerous and she's good and she's doing it to a girl who laughs with her whole throat like she's forgotten she can be seen. Which is precisely the thing Randi can't do anymore. Of course that's who she'd pick. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — that's the knife. That's the chapter telling me Vee is already on the table and doesn't know it.

Three chapters, two of them stationary-feeling, but this one *moved* and reframed everything behind it. I'd text a friend: it's a horror novel wearing a romance's clothes and I can't put it down.
