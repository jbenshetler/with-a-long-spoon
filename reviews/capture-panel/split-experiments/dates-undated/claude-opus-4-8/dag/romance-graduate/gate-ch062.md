REACTION

Oh, that was NOT the chapter I was braced for, and thank God. I had Sheri filed under "uncollided gun" for sixty chapters — the tall gold model Vee built on the nights she felt stupid — and the book just walked her into a taproom and she's five foot nothing, two-tone perm, crooked makeup, boots included, and the whole blonde Vee invented "didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand." That line undid me. Because that's the whole architecture of Vee's jealousy exposed in one beat — she builds rivals out of her own self-contempt, and the real woman is nothing like the fear, and the fear never needed the real woman to exist. "It had never needed the blonde, it turned out." The book knows exactly what it's been doing to her for a month.

And then it does the thing I keep begging it to do — it doesn't explain it. The look. Sheri's eyes coming down Vee's body with no math in them, no pricing, no placing, the appreciative soundless *oh* sticking at the shoes — and Vee, fluent in the women-looking-at-women language since high school, braced for a verdict and getting *enjoyed* instead. That reframe landed in my actual stomach. She dressed in armor against a rival and got looked at like a gift, and the laugh that came out of her "before she'd voted on it," a month of held breath leaving the ribs — I felt that let-go physically.

Can I just say: a QUEER woman. A gay woman. The book has been so dense about Vee's locked door — the Randi thing she can't look at — and here comes Sheri, out, loud, watching the ivy-sleeved bartender with open appreciation and no hurry, and the book sets her down right in front of Vee like a mirror Vee can't read yet. "You got the legs for it." "Bet those legs feel like Christmas." It's flirting at her, warmly, harmlessly, and Vee is lit up and has no idea what's being switched on. The machine is still running and I'm still the only one in the room who can hear it.

But the thing that actually got me, the thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m., is Pace. Two things. First: he blushed. I have watched this man be unflappable for sixty-two chapters — grave, exact, easy in the dark where nobody stays dignified — and Sheri says "you get to motorboat them things" and the color comes UP, past his ears, past anything deniable, and he looks at the table and rubs his neck. "The single most disarming thing she'd watched him do." I believe that. After all the control, the crack in it is what finally makes him human-sized.

And second — Sheri's whole story. The coming out last March, the Marine Corps father who doesn't retire, the whole tablecloth coming off in a month and nobody catching a dish. Crying in the middle of a two-step at the one place left with her name on it. And Pace driving her home with no questions, on her porch next morning with coffee and donuts, fixing the lock the landlord wouldn't, stopping the faucet, the bike, the bacon cheeseburger. "He never handed me one false word." And the drop: "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen." There it is. The thing the book has kept behind his door as tight as Vee keeps hers. He understood Sheri's exile because he's living his own, and he never once said how he knew. God. The man who gives gifts with no card has a reason he can't sign them, and it's sixteen years old.

So now I have to sit with the whole shape of him again. He didn't bring Vee to meet a friend. He brought her to meet the proof — the standing evidence of who he is when nobody's performing, the one person who gets his real license. And he did it the week after he threw the roadhouse dinner. He's showing her his actual family. And the thing that makes my chest hurt: Randi's not in this room. This is the one warm world of his that isn't the con. Does Randi even know Sheri? Does Sheri know Randi? Two uncollided worlds and I suddenly want them in a room together very badly.

The Valentine's dance just got real too — "that got settled back in December, he knows it's settled," bring your opinions, leave the shoes home. The gun I half-forgot is loaded and dated. And he stood his headlights on her door until the porch light came on. "Like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling." I'm carrying that line out of the chapter.

This is the chapter that broke the Tuesday pattern I was threatening to check the exits over. New room, new person, a reframe that detonated a month of setup, Pace cracked open two different ways, and a date on the calendar. I'm wide awake.
