REACTION

Okay so this is a bridge chapter and I know it on sight — class, a dinner, a goodbye kiss, same shape as 54, 57, 58. And a week ago that would've made me antsy, the third quiet table in a row. But it isn't quiet. The book did something sneaky here: it turned the *telling* into the scene. The sex already happened — I read it in 53 — and now I'm watching her hand it across a table, and watching what she can and can't make her mouth say, and that gap is the whole chapter. That's the thing I'm actually starving for and almost never get: a heroine with enough interiority that watching her narrate is hotter and scarier than the act was.

The Mauss stuff. The hau. A gift carries the spirit of the giver and *wants to go home.* You can't regift the sweater your grandmother knitted. I had to put the book down for a second because this book has spent fifty-nine chapters on a man who gives gifts with no card, no thank-you moment — the drawer, the shirt, the cashmere blanket that just *appeared* — and now a professor is standing at a lectern explaining, to me, out loud, the exact machine I've been watching run. And then the comb. The grooming. One-fifth of the waking day, the baboon with its hands in the fur, *the fleas were never the point.* And the whole book is two people who groom her — Pace with the food, Randi with the leaning-in — and one of them is a con and I still can't tell which lies are which. The lectures scare me because they're the author handing me the key while the characters stay locked out of the room. Vee is sitting *in* the lecture about gift-debt and cannot hear it about her own life.

But here's what actually got me, the part I'd text at 1 a.m. — the court scene, the retelling. She's built the swallow-in-the-truck down to "it was a lot." She's built tasting herself down to a *party trick*, a thirty-second closer with a Victorian hand over the mouth, *ugh, gross, that's wrong,* performed for laughs. And twice the bit falls out of her hands — the thing arrives present-tense in her own mouth and there's "nothing funny in her face at all." She keeps trying to fold the real thing down to a size she can sit behind, and Randi — *don't you dare fade to firelight* — keeps refusing to take the small version. Randi wants the harder one every single time. And I realized halfway through: Randi isn't doing this as a friend. She's doing it the way she got the store credit for the sweater. She's drawing out the confession and the one beneath it. The jacket told me — *leaning in, drawing out the next confession and the one beneath it.* This is the con, running live, and it looks exactly like the best friendship of Vee's life. It IS the best friendship of Vee's life. That's the horror of it.

And then the hinge. "You think it's gross too, don't you." / "I don't." Flat. Level. Not cold. "It was powerful. It turned me on." And Vee goes *too loud* — "But it's gross" — too loud for how little Randi spent saying the opposite. And Randi's only question: *how does he act?* And Vee, before any part of her vetted it: "he's enthusiastic." That's the whole thing, isn't it. That's 53 coming back to collect — *why does he like it so much*, the question that sat down on the edge of the bed. She's being walked, gently, toward a door, and I don't even know if Randi's walking her toward pleasure or toward something that serves Randi, and I don't think Randi knows either, and THAT is why I can't stop.

The coat. God, the coat. She swings it open on the hook and the stain's right there under the loft light — the body telling on her before she said a word — and she shuts it to the wall "like a drawer closing on a photograph." She kept the coat. She never tried to clean it. That detail is doing more than the braise she ate without tasting, which — she swam a mile, ate nothing, and cleaned the plate without noticing, while Randi took one exact bite and left it. One of these women eats. One performs eating. I keep clocking it.

And the neck kiss again. Second time. Randi laid the hollow of her neck open — "tipped her head to settle the hug deeper" — and Vee kissed it and Randi did the catch-and-rise that went on a beat too long and called it ticklish. Twice now Randi has engineered the spot and then flinched from what she engineered. She can run every scenario except Vee's face. She wants the one real thing and is a coward about it. I believe that completely and it still makes the back of my neck cold.

What I love is that nobody explains it. Randi says "how does he act," gets "enthusiastic," says "Mm," eats the bread, and the subject is closed forever. The book trusts me. And the last image — the sentence that walks home with her, *he likes it. actually — he's enthusiastic,* and under it, taking up almost no room, *I don't* — that's a seed going in. Something's going to grow out of "how does he act" and I want to be there.

The only thing keeping this off a perfect pull is that it IS the fourth table-and-goodbye-kiss in a row, and I can feel the pattern's walls. I'm not tired of it yet — the Mauss lecture and the "I don't" earned the repetition — but one more identical Tuesday-table with no collision and I'll start checking the exits. The Valentine's dance is still out there uncollided. I'm holding the book to my chest and also tapping my foot.
