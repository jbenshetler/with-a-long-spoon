REACTION

Oh, this is the same night from the other end, and putting them back to back is the whole trick of the book laid bare — Randi in Gstaad in the pale blue dress feeling nothing in a faultless room, Vee in a twin bed under the little gold swimmers feeling everything about a man who won't name her. Same midnight, same two women facedown over a phone, and the texts that pass between them are both completely true and both total lies. "it's honestly a fairytale out here" — and I was *in* that room, I watched her scrub her mouth off a sleeping stranger. "thinking about you 💛 i'm here if you want to talk. or not." — and Vee's carrying the glass-room secret she'll never say. They're each handing the other the shiny version and keeping the rot. God. The two of them comforting each other with the exact performance the other one most needs to see through.

What got me — the room that loves a girl who doesn't live in her anymore. The best-in-show ribbons, the synchronized-swimming trophies with the little gold girls frozen mid-lift, the clarinet in the closet, the faces on the corkboard who haven't texted in a year. "It kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." That's the realest thing in the chapter. She's outgrown the bed her legs outgrew in middle school, and she's outgrown this whole safe life, and there's no going back to the girl who thought Chili's nachos were a fine night out. Pace ruined that for her — she said it two chapters ago and it's still true. He fed her a meal and now crackers taste like crackers.

And then the thing I was dreading and craving: her night turning on her while she's inside it. She reaches for the fra diavolo night, the sheet slipping, to get warm — and the hair goes gold and the leg goes tan and the blonde she built out of spite is sitting where she sat in the wine light. She can't keep her own memory. That's the cruelest image in the chapter and it's doing exactly what the pool did way back — the thing that used to hold her up going straight through her hands. The bartender's "Pace don't bring dates," Randi's "he leaves you the room" — all of it only ever worked while she *knew she was the one,* and now she doesn't know she's anything. "The floor was gone." Yeah.

What I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: she said "and what about Pace?" and *nothing came after it.* She has a date circled for Randi — term starts, the class, the face that lights up — and nothing at all for him. Not an hour, not a room he'll surely be in. After the "words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me" fight and a whole week of silence, she genuinely doesn't know if it's over, if he thinks it's over, or if he thinks nothing happened. And neither do I, honestly. That's the knife — she can't even tell which of the three it is.

Sheri at Thanksgiving coming back up, and now the unknown Christmas week, and the invented blonde — the jealousy machine running on an empty tank of information. I keep thinking: she's the one person in this triangle who doesn't know there's a triangle, and she's lying here building a rival out of thin air while the real rival texts her 💋 from a billionaire's hotel bed having just proven to herself that Vee is the one thing that room couldn't counterfeit.

It's quiet. It's the third interior-ache chapter stacked up now — 45, 47 the fight, 48 Randi's, 49 Vee's — and I can feel the book holding its breath before term starts and the three of them are finally in reach of one room. But this one earned its quiet. The two New Year's chapters as a diptych did something a single one couldn't. I'm not skimming. I want the term to start as badly as Vee does.
