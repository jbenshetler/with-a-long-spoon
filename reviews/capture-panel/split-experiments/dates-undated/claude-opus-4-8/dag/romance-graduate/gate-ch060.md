REACTION

Okay so this is the fourth time I've watched this exact build — nipples first, the long road down, "found herself slick enough that her fingers moved without a catch," gather the wet, circle, heel dragging the sheet. The mirror, the car, the restroom stall, now the bunk. I could recite the choreography in my sleep at this point, and for the first third of this chapter my thumb was genuinely twitching to skim, because *I have been in this exact room before* and the book knows I have and kept walking me through it step by step anyway.

And then it stopped being that chapter.

The reach. God, the reach. She brings her own fingers up to her mouth and holds them one breath away and *cannot close the distance.* "A breath's width remained. She held there until her lungs began to hurt." That's the whole book in a gesture. Because this was never about tasting herself — that's the alibi, that's the thing she can cross-examine in the safe light of "is this gross, Pace likes it, Randi doesn't mind." The real thing is sitting right underneath it saying its own name quietly over and over and she will not look at it: *A mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers.* It comes up THREE times. "Had Randi's looked like this. Sticky too. Shining. Had she meant a mouth carrying the wet back to her? Or her own fingers, lifted to her mouth?" She's lying in a cold bunk running Randi's hands through a scene she thinks is about her boyfriend, and the one millimeter her tongue won't cross is the same millimeter she won't cross toward the thing she's actually hungry for. The taboo gesture and the taboo person are the identical locked door. She can't swallow because she can't say it.

And after fifty chapters of me white-knuckling about whether this book over-explains its own subtext — here it just *trusts* me. It never once says Randi. It lets the whole freight ride in an unfinished reach and a word — *greedy* — that she keeps flinching off of. That's the version of this book I'd stay for forever.

The window undid me. She closes it at the top — chooses the heat, seals herself into her own hothouse, "every small heat stayed where it was put." And at the end she wrenches it open and stands in the January water in nothing but his shirt, airing the room out before Cassie gets home. She's venting the evidence. And then — THEN — she gets back in and goes looking across the sheet for the cold damp patch she made and settles her hip down onto it on purpose. "Greedy. Her mess. Hers to lie in." That's as close as she's come to claiming any of it, and it's still routed through shame, still a thing she does instead of a thing she says.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she opens the WINDOW to let the smell out so her roommate won't catch her having wanted something, and the thing she wanted isn't even in the room, and it's not Pace, and she STILL cannot see it, I'm going to lie face down on the floor.*

So: the heat bored me and the chapter floored me, in that order, which is a weird thing to hold. The build is a pattern I've aged out of. The reach is the realest two inches in the book.
