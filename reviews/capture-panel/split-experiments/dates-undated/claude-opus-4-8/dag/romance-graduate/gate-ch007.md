REACTION

Okay so this is the one that flips the whole thing over, and it flips it fast. Four chapters I've been inside Vee's dizzy little borrowed-life glow — the right pen, the high table, *love you girl* — and now I'm back on the couch with Randi straddling Pace and I finally remember what the jacket already told me and what Chapter 1's last line did to me. *There you are, Randi.* The girl who walked into Vee at the dining hall, the warm one who "decided" there would be a later — that was the hunt. And this chapter is the two of them debriefing the kill, except the book is too smart to let it be a smug villain-planning scene.

What got me — actually got me, in the body — is the structure of it. She can't just ask him how the date went. She has to climb onto him, feed him the wine mouth-to-mouth, put his hands on her breasts, and press the word *tell* into his ear like it's filthy, because that's the only register she has for wanting something this much. She cannot ask plainly. She's running the exact apparatus on Pace that she ran on Vee, that the whole sorority runs — seduce your way to the thing instead of saying the thing — and I've read her well enough now (that bathroom mirror in Chapter 1, the water-bottle cap under her thumbnail) to know that the performance IS the tell. The wanting's too big for her face so she keeps the face on over it. "A want too big for her face, and keeping the face over it anyway." God.

And the wine. The *wine.* He pours one glass, doesn't pour himself one, and takes every sip of it out of her mouth. "He had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else." That's the whole dynamic of these two in one image — he feeds, she's fed, but here he's letting her hand it to him, letting her run the evening, and he's getting drunk on her secondhand. That's hot in the quiet way I keep hoping books will be and they almost never are. Nobody's naked, barely anyone's undressed, and I was more turned on than by most of the bench chapter honestly, because this is two people who know each other reading each other under the words.

But here's what's sitting cold in my chest: they are talking about Vee. Sweet Vee, who can't remember a word Randi says because she's too busy being chosen, who built the froyo cup too big because for once nobody told her to stop. "She built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop." And Randi lights up *hearing that.* The thing I find myself suspicious of — genuinely, not idly — is that Pace is withholding. "She said the true thing she asked him to pretend she hadn't — and he did not hand it over. It was hers." He's keeping Vee's real moment back from Randi. Which tells me he's already a little bit Vee's, or wants to be, and Randi felt it — "something she was holding level," her thighs setting instead of tightening. The jealousy's already live and none of them will name it. *What none of them sees coming is how real it will get.* Yeah. I see it coming and I still want to watch it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "ok the predator chapter is the most romantic one so far and I hate that it works. also I think the man is going to fall for the mark and the girlfriend who planned the whole thing already knows." Two pages of a couple drinking one glass of wine between them and I'm more unsettled than I've been by whole books. This writer trusts me. That's the thing. Nobody has stopped to explain the triangle to me. I'm just being handed the pieces at the exact angle to feel them land.

It's a short chapter and I clocked that, but it's not a bridge — it's a hinge. It recontextualizes four chapters of Vee's happiness into something I now have to grieve a little while I read. I'm not setting this down.
