REACTION

Oh, he's the camera now. First time I've been inside Pace's head and the book handed it to me on *this* chapter, of all of them — the one where it's not Vee at the door. That's a choice and I felt it land. Everything I've spent seventy chapters not being able to read about this man, and now I'm behind his eyes and he's stripping the bed of the white sheets — *Randi's* sheets, the citrus, "a bed two people had slept in and not aired" — and making it up in Vee's green before Vee comes. That one line did more to me than the whole paint scene. He launders one girl out of the bed to lay the other one down in it, and it's just *Tuesday* to him. Housekeeping.

And then it's not Vee. It's Randi on the green, and I knew it a half-second before he did and my stomach went with his. God, she's good. "Vee's been delayed. You have an hour. With me." The whole thing staged — the pose, the misbehaving-on-purpose, "she hadn't come to be serviced, she'd come to be made to pay for it." The book finally said out loud the thing I've been circling: Randi is bleeding out and the only language she has for it is to build an elaborate scene where someone *does something to her* so she doesn't have to say *I can't stand it, she's in love with someone and it isn't only him, and I set this whole thing up and now I'm drowning in it.* The clamps, the spanking, the crying at the end that "wasn't really about anything he'd done to her body — the spanking, the clamps, only the door she'd gone through to reach it." Yeah. I've been waiting for someone to name Randi's pain and it got named by the man running the clamps.

The heat is real and it's hot and I'm also a little sick, in the way the book wants me a little sick. "She's perfect. For your first woman." He hands her exactly the knife she drove out there to be stabbed with. He reads her like a meter — "in the body and the present tense" — and gives her the cruelty she's begging for dressed as an answer. That's the scariest version of him yet. Not cold. *Accurate.*

But here's what actually kept me up. Randi got Vee drunk. Four mimosas, drove her home, put her to bed, *invented an hour* that was never real, and fed Pace a clock that didn't exist. And the morning phone call — Vee, hungover, *mortified,* thanking Randi, "I don't know what I'd do without her," while Randi goes down on Pace under the sheet to lick her own night off him, wiping herself out of him so he's "clean and ready for Vee's use." Three of them in that one phone call and only one of them doesn't know she's in the room. I had the jacket's line in my teeth the whole time — *she doesn't know there are three.* This is the three. And it's worse than I thought, because it's not just that they chose her together — it's that they're actively *managing her body,* drugging her weekend away, so the two of them can — what? Burn each other down over her? Because that scene wasn't about Vee being away. It was Randi and Pace, alone, and the thing between *them* is its own furnace and it's got Vee's name all through it and Vee's green sheets under it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *he STRIPS VEE'S COLOR OFF THE BED to lie Randi down and then strips it AGAIN in the morning and the symmetry is going to kill me. also Randi is in so much pain she had to get spanked bloody to let it out and I can't hate her, I just want to hold her and also shake her.*

Did it pull me? All the way under. Did it make me want to touch Pace? Less than ever, and that's the book working — I'm with Randi now, the one getting wrecked on the floor, not the man reading her like a dial. What a thing to do with a sex scene: make it hot and make me grieve inside it at the same time.
