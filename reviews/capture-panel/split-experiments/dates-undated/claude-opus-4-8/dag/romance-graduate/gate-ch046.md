REACTION

Okay. So this is the two-page chapter I've never known what to do with, and now I know what it's for, because it just gutted me in less space than a brunch would've taken to order coffee.

She's got both shirts on — hers under his — and that's the whole book in one image before anything even happens. The shirt held *off her skin* by the other one. A buffer. She's wearing the feeling at a remove because the full dose isn't safe in the hallway where Cassie might see "a girl too far gone in a boy." And then she takes the buffer off. That progression — phone first, then the shirt over the shirt, then his shirt straight on bare skin, then *nothing* — is a little stripping-down staircase and every step is her getting closer to the truth and the truth getting colder.

The phone part killed me quietly. "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them." She's lying there knowing there's no text that fixes it, and she *knows* it, and she puts the phone facedown anyway. That's the most awake she's been. All fall she's been building sentences — the cracker-and-meal one, the whole architecture around the words she can't say — and here she finally admits the architecture doesn't reach him. There's no clever construction left to try.

But the thing that actually took the floor out: she breathes him in off the shirt and can name every part of him — sawdust, salt, the clean warm — and realizes *the warmth is her own, given back.* "He wasn't in it." God. That's the con from the inside without her ever seeing the con. She's figured out the exact mechanism — that the comfort is a loop closing on herself, that she's holding a smell in the dark while he sleeps easy across town "with nothing grinding in him" — and she's one half-inch from the whole thing and she turns it into *I have no name for this* instead of *they have done this to me*. She can't get to Randi and Pace from here because she doesn't know they're there. She just knows the heat is hers and not his and it isn't enough.

And then she lies there naked in the cold and *stays in it*. Doesn't put the shirt back on. Leaves it in a heap where it fell, not folded, not on the pillow — after a whole book of her treating his things like relics, the drawer, the washing-it-so-it-smells-like-him. That dropped shirt is a tiny funeral. She's not comforting herself tonight. She's letting herself be cold on purpose, which is the closest she's come to being honest with her own body instead of feeding it.

The thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she finally stopped being able to make the shirt work and she's just lying there freezing and I can't breathe.* After chapter 45 I was worried the not-knowing was going to loop forever — same wound, re-opened every chapter, the architecture repeating. This didn't repeat it. It stripped it. Two pages and it moved: the shirt stopped working. That's new. The grifter's whole comfort apparatus — the drawer, the flannel, the smell — just failed in her hands, and she noticed, and she didn't fix it.

Two pages was exactly right. A long chapter would've explained this to me. This one trusted me to feel the cold come down with her. I'm not setting it down.
