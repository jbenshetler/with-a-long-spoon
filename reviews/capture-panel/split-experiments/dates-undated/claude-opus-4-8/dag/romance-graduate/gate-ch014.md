REACTION

Okay so this is the third time I've heard about the shirt. I read it happen in the truck, I heard Vee tell Cassie, and now Kayla and Meg get the whole saga over the cheese fries. And I'll be honest, when Meg said "you keep getting to it and going soft and skipping the middle," part of me went *I know the middle, I was there, I watched him stand in the rain.* That's the risk of a chapter like this — I've already banked this scene in its real register, so hearing it refracted through giddy dining-hall retelling is a step down in temperature no matter how well it's done.

And yet. It's doing something, and I clocked it about halfway through. This is the chapter where the trap gets *witnessed and approved by a jury.* The whole table falls for it. "He's different. I know how that sounds." And then Meg, easy in her own life, lands the quietest knife in the book so far: "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think." And then — *this* is the line that actually got me — "Sometimes they even are." Because that's the hook the whole series is hanging on, isn't it? He might even be. The kindness is real all the way down. That's what's so sick about it. Meg's throwaway is the thesis and she doesn't know she's saying it and Vee lets it "go by, it didn't catch on anything." Of course it didn't. It's not supposed to catch. That's the design.

The texture I liked: Meg is the one whose boots gave Vee the blister. That little closed loop pleased me more than it should've — the girl lending the wrong-sized boots is sitting right there following the romance "in installments like her shows." And Theo, the nice boy who waved — "They're all nice until you don't want them and then they're wonderful." Kayla's a bright, dumb, fun instrument and I don't mind her, but she and Meg are basically a warmer, chattier replacement chorus for Cassie, and I notice that Cassie — my one clean thing — wasn't at this table. The jacket told me she's being walked away from, and here's a whole new friend-group in a *better dining hall* and Cassie's not in it. That bothered me in the good way.

But the real thing, the thing that made my thumb want the next page: the text. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." Kayla's right and it chilled me a little that a dumb-fun character said the smart thing — *there's no meal at two. He wants you for the afternoon.* I've never been through his door either. Three dates, one kiss, "twelve days" she didn't have to count — all that engineered restraint, and now the first invitation into his actual territory with no stated reason. This is what "ask and then ravish" was loaded for. And I don't forget that last chapter ended with him alone in his doorway, gut-punched that Randi didn't stay. So he goes and summons the redhead. Is this him reaching for the realer thing? Does Randi know he sent it? Is Saturday the day the plan pays out — or the day it starts costing *him?* I'm leaning in.

So: a froth chapter, a touch too much recap, but it ends on the exact door I've been standing outside of since the hike got mentioned. I'm not tired. I want Saturday.
