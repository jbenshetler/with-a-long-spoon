REACTION

Okay, first thing I want to text a friend at 1 a.m.: *the professor is the book.* Dr. Marsh walks in with her canvas bag and her silver streak and says a gift carries the spirit of the giver and the gift wants to go home, and I actually sat up the way Vee did. Because that's it. That's the whole engine the jacket's been billing me for — "what it has begun to cost." The hau wants to go home. Pace sews her a shirt and builds her a drawer and the gift pulls back toward him; Randi hands her a *play* like a queen lending a sword and the gift is inhabited, and it's crawling back toward Randi right now, in real time, in a bathroom stall. The lecture is the thesis spoken aloud. Normally that's one of my hair triggers — a book explaining its own subtext as if I might miss it — and I keep waiting to resent it and I don't, quite, because it's not the narrator leaning in to tell me, it's a character delivering it to a lecture hall while the two people it's about sit there not hearing it. The dramatic irony does the work the exposition would've killed. I'll allow it. Barely. If Marsh comes back next week and explains it *again* I'll be annoyed.

The thing that actually chilled me: this is a near-shot-for-shot restaging. Vee tells her weekend and it's warm and proud and silly — pleasure princess, the shimmy, "I got mushrooms." And underneath, Randi is doing the *exact* thing the carry-forward flagged: she listens as no one ever has, leans in, draws out the next confession and the one beneath it. "Don't start at his door. That's not where a night like that starts." "How much could he see?" "Could he see you were ready?" She's not a friend hearing a story. She's *conducting* one. And she files it all back — I know she does, I watched her do it in bed in the earlier books. Vee thinks she's giving Randi a gift. She's giving Pace a report through Randi and doesn't know there's a third person at the table.

And then the drive to the bathroom. I knew where that was going the second Randi said "could he see you were ready," and I was right, and the part that got me wasn't the sex — it was the face. At the top of it, *Randi's* face arrives behind her eyes, the tonight face, "there's no way he couldn't," and *that's* what shoves her over. Not Pace. She stood in the mirror telling herself afterward "that had been about Pace, obviously. All of it. About Pace" — and the word *obviously* is the whole book. She's doing the thing she does with the blonde and the washed shirt: feeling it and routing it somewhere safe and sealing the lid. "The third restroom she'd touched herself in." The book is keeping a count on her even when she won't.

The underwear story. God, Helen Rivers. Randi tells it as a cocktail anecdote — the lawn boy, one knee in the grass, the teddy bear — and it's charming, and then it turns and it's a *wound*, the same organ as everything in her. The mother who had them laundered and folded and waited *days*, who set robin's-egg blue down next to her plate — "They're not mine. They're your color." "She knew my color." That's the cruelest small sentence in the chapter and Randi delivered it looking at a dining room that wasn't there. And the boy who went down on one knee ate nothing and quit that week and she never saw him again. That's Randi learning young that love caught and named gets confiscated and the other person disappears. It rhymes with Pace's Daphne thing so hard it hums. Two people who each decided, at nineteen-ish, that the saying of it is what destroys it — and they found each other and they chose Vee together. Of course they did. They're the same wound facing two directions.

And the goodbye. The kiss "outlasts the goodbye" — the jacket line, enacted. The second press "with its little more in it." And then Vee bends and kisses the spot on Randi's neck where she'd cried, no thought at all, and *Randi flinches.* "Tickles." Covers it instantly. But Randi — who leans in, who's never not in control of the lean — got touched unasked and her body jumped before her face could manage it. That flinch is the first crack I've seen in her con. The leaner, startled. I gasped a little.

What I'm turned off by, honestly, nothing — but I'm aware this is now my *fourth* chapter in a row of Vee happy and oblivious, the retelling-to-a-confidante structure running twice (Cassie, then Kayla/Meg, now Randi), and the weekend getting narrated to me a third time. I lived the weekend in 52 and 53. I did not fully need it recapped to Randi beat by beat. What saved it from feeling like a lap already run is that the *telling* is the plot here — the confession machine is the point, not the content. But the book is spending a lot of pages idling in Vee's happiness while I stand at the window waiting for the three of them in one room, which is STILL not here, and is now literally tomorrow. The relief at the end — "the closing had gotten her out of it," the little unweighable stone — the neck-kiss story she'll save for Thursday — that's the fuse laid for next time. Fine. But the dinner is the thing, and I want the dinner.

Heat: real but it's the solo-in-the-stall kind, and it's deliberately contaminated — she can't come without Randi's face, and the book knows, and that's more interesting than hot. The weekend heat I've already banked.

What I'm suspicious of: that the dinner tomorrow is where someone collides. Pace meets Randi "properly." Pace, who's done zero homework on the trap. Randi, who wants "to see him again" with that tonight-wickedness. And Vee in the middle glowing. The jacket said three games and she knows two. Tomorrow two of her players sit at one table and I don't believe this book lets that pass politely.
