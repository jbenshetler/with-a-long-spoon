REACTION

Oh, this one did something cold to me, and it did it on purpose.

For six chapters I've been riding so high with Vee — the dress in the window, the shoe shopping, the whole giddy Edna-Mode morning in Randi's room — that I'd half forgotten the thing the jacket promised me on page one: I'm a step ahead of her, and the step is a trapdoor. And this chapter is the book reaching up through the floor and reminding me. Because here's what it is: I just watched the entire morning I loved — Vee crying in the desk chair, telling it straight, the parts that made her look bad first, *I wanted to keep her* — get metabolized into pillow talk. Randi takes the most tender thing Vee did all week and feeds it to Pace mid-ride like a treat. "I had her standing in my room this morning. In her bra." And he gets *hard* off it. The thumb in the back of the knee, the sound Vee made and hoped nobody heard — Randi heard it, and now it's currency, and now it's in his mouth too. Everything Vee confides travels farther than she knows. The jacket told me. I still wasn't ready to watch the pipe actually run.

And here's the thing that's twisting me: it's hot. God help me, it's hot. The construction of it — Randi giving him a thing and taking a thing at the same time, the telling turning her on so plainly it fires *him*, her feet planted on the sheet and the blue lacquer the only color on the bed — it's beautifully built and it's genuinely charged and I'm turned on and sick at the same time, which is exactly the degree-by-degree the back cover promised and I resent how well it's working.

But I'm watching Pace more than I'm watching the sex. Because look at what he keeps back. He doesn't tell Randi what Vee said at the bar, or how his face felt when it went — "those were his, and that was Vee's." He's doing the thing from my carry-forward notes: he keeps Vee's realest things out of Randi's hands. The withholding has flipped meaning on me. In the early chapters it read like his tenderness; now it reads like a man quietly building a second room in the house that Randi isn't allowed into. He's falling, and the falling is now a secret he's keeping from his co-conspirator while she lies on his chest. "It warmed him that the two of them had that between them without him" — he doesn't even feel the irony. The three-way betrayal is going every direction at once and nobody in that bed is clean.

And "give her a day," twice, "as if the day were his to give" — Randi scheduling Vee's return, promising her to him like a thing she can deliver. She's running Vee *to* him now. She's the procurer who's also the jealous one, and I can see both cords pulling and I don't know which snaps first. "She's going to be so beautiful when she walks in. You wait." That line chilled me clean through. She's dressing the mark for the man she loves and calling it a gift and she can't feel the knife in her own hand.

What I'm sitting with at 1 a.m.: the shame didn't rise in me *during* the chapter, it rose *after*. Inside it I was leaning in. And that gap is the whole book doing its job on me, the thing it told me it would do.
