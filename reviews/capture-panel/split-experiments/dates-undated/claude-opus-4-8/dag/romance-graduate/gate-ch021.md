REACTION

Okay. I did not expect to be undone by a *dress fitting* for the second time in this book, and yet here I am.

What got me — and it's the same move that got me on the pedestal in chapter 16 — is that he hid the labor. "He'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That's the whole man in one sentence and it's also the whole seduction, and the book *knows* it's both and doesn't stop to tell me so, which is the only reason I let it in. He stayed up all night. She slept across town not knowing. And the proof of love is that it looks like it cost nothing. God. That's Randi's method too, isn't it — the door shut soft at the peak, the ease that's actually enormous effort — except Pace's version makes a thing *for* her instead of extracting a thing *from* her. Same hand, opposite direction.

And then it got *funny*, and I wasn't ready for that at all. The whole relay of trying to have sex without ruining the dress — "there's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," him twitching at the words, her laughing so hard her leg slides off his hip — I have read a thousand sex scenes and I cannot remember the last one that was allowed to be *clumsy and delighted* in the middle without the book panicking that it was losing its heat. It didn't lose the heat. The two fingers in his mouth, cleaning her off her own hand with his eyes on hers — that came right out of the laughing and it was hotter *because* of the laughing. This is the first time the sex in this book felt like *play* and not like ceremony, and I think that's deliberate, because look who's in the room. It's not Vee. It's Randi.

Because — yes. "She" the whole chapter, the dark long-sleeved dress, drove across town, one Friday night already behind them, the best she'd had "by a distance." This is Randi's afternoon. Pace made the induction dress — Vee's dress, the burgundy, the detonator I've been waiting twenty chapters to go off at Vee's induction — and he put it on *Randi's* body in the sun porch window. And Randi put it on bare, took her own underwear off to clean the line, strode down the hall in it, and then — the thing that actually chilled me under all the warmth — lay in his bed afterward and *daydreamed walking into the induction in it.* Walking in late, on purpose, letting the room turn. That's not Randi's induction. That's VEE's. Randi tried the dress on and tried Vee's *future* on with it, and it fit her, and she liked how it felt.

So the peekaboo isn't the slit. It's this: the reader gets the one piece none of them has. Vee thinks that dress is being made for her name read at the front of a room. Randi just wore it first, barefoot, and came in it, and imagined it was her moment. "Getting away with something." She said it about hanging the dress back up without a stain. She has no idea how much bigger the thing she's getting away with is.

And here's what rearranged me: I came in braced to catch Randi being cold, the hunter, and instead the chapter is almost unbearably *tender* and she cried — the dumb happy kind that beat her to it — and I believed it. That's the trap the whole book has been building. The crack in her is real. Her wanting him is real. And it's going to be the thing that detonates in Vee's face, and the dress is the fuse, and nobody's holding it but me.

I texted nobody. I just sat with my hand over my mouth like she did.
