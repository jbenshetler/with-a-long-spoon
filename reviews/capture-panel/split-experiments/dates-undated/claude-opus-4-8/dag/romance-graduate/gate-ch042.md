REACTION

Oh, that MIRA point. I'm still back in chapter 41 honestly — I read 42 with the ground already tilted. Because that was the best twist this book has pulled, and it did it in the one place I'd stopped looking: a statistics homework assignment. The professor setting down *surprising* without decorating it, the outlier "off on its own in the white, easy to see if you knew to look for it, invisible if you didn't" — the book handed me the whole shape of the con in a metaphor about exploratory data analysis and I didn't resent it, which is rare, because it didn't explain itself. It just let MIRANDA sit over the worst point in the county and trusted me to feel the floor go. Randi's whole glossy accent-and-Mercedes mythology, the daughter of a man who did something — and the "cheating bastards," ragged, aimed too near to be meant for strangers. God. And Cassie, who can't see the screen, taking it as agreement, with her laid-off dad set flat on the table like a fact with no handle. Three women, one truth on one laptop, and only Vee saw it, and what Vee did with it was *give Randi somewhere to put her hands.* That's Vee's whole tragedy in one gesture — her kindness is a professional-grade cover-up and she doesn't know she's doing the con's work for it.

So now chapter 42, the new ordinary, and I can feel exactly what it's doing: it's the exhale after 41. And normally the exhale after a gut-punch is where I start tapping my thumb. This is the — what, fifth? sixth? — warm-house-and-cooking chapter, and the pizza went on a beat long; I clocked the water buffalo mozzarella and the Nebbiolo and I thought, I have been fed in this kitchen before. The "cotton candy versus cut strawberries" thing, the tasting-every-flavor-separately — I see the machine. It's the same note the book keeps playing: she's coming alive to the world one sense at a time because he woke her up. I believe it. I'm also a little full of it.

But. Two things saved this chapter for me and both are good.

The undressing. That was genuinely hot — not because of any act, but because of the *comedy* of it, the jeans cold and tight and winning, the one-legged hopping, the socks, him not helping and so plainly enjoying her losing that she starts laughing and has to stop to finish. "You're not helping." "I'm helping." That's a real couple. That's the thing I came to this book starving for and can't find in the hockey-player books — desire with a sense of humor, a woman undignified and burning the house down for him and narrating her own indignity. And then "her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private" — five nights coming out of her too loud too soon — that's the whole chapter's thesis done in the body instead of the mouth. She called the break for school and her throat ratted her out. Love it.

And the shirt. Okay, the shirt got me. I was braced for cozy and instead she handed him the washed flannel and his face started to *hurt* because he thought she was giving it back — and then "I had to wash it. It stopped smelling like you." A grown woman confessing she'd slept every night away from him inside a dying shirt, breathing it down to nothing, and needing him to re-fill it. That's not a trope. That's interiority with its chest cracked open. And him pulling it on over the shirt he already had on, standing in his kitchen in two shirts because she told him to — "Smelling like you." / "Smelling like me." I'd text a friend about that at 1 a.m.

Here's my unease, and it's the book's, not a flaw: every one of these tender beats is the trap tightening. The drawer, the shirt, the fed mouth — I'm one chapter off MIRANDA, and I know what Vee doesn't, that the man filling her shirt is running her with another woman who is herself a mark who is herself being run. The sweetness is the interest on the bill. So the chapter lands softer than 41 by design and I'm fine with it being a bridge — but it's a bridge after I've just been shown the drop, and that makes me impatient for the water, not the railing. I want the three of them in a room. I have wanted it for ten chapters. 41 put the collision *inside Vee's head* without the others knowing, which is almost crueler, and now I need it to go somewhere.
