REACTION

Oh, this one's small and it's a scalpel. I came into it still vibrating off 67 and 68 — the kiss, the lie in the dark, the "no, nothing" — braced for the book to keep rocketing, and instead it stops dead in a doorway with a cereal box, and I did not resent it for one second. That's the tell that this book knows what it's doing. She's still in her *coat.* Cassie's still in *hers.* Nobody's sat down. The whole thing is staged as two women who haven't finished coming inside, and the question hangs in the heated room "like a third person nobody had offered a chair." Yes. Exactly that.

What got me is that Cassie asks the one question nobody — not Randi, not Pace, not Vee herself — has been willing to put on the table in plain words: *Is this what you want?* Not "what happened," not "what is she to you," not a scold. The clean door I've been saying for sixty chapters nobody walks through, and tonight *Cassie* opens it, gently, from her side. And the horror of it is that Vee actually *goes and looks* — "she reached for it. There was a shape there, close and large, and she came up against the edge of it and stepped back off before she could make out its size." That's the closest she's come to seeing the thing, and she still flinches off it. The book isn't going to let her see it a moment before she's ready, and it keeps being honest about that.

And then she does the ugliest, truest thing: she reaches for the oldest weapon she's got and swings it at Cassie. "You don't like her. You've never liked any of it." The plank laid down to walk out on — and Cassie "stepped over it without once looking down." God. I love Cassie so much and I'm so tired of watching Vee do this to the one person who's safe. It's the same move she pulled on Pace in bed, swinging the whole weight off herself and onto someone else. She does it when the ground gives.

But here's where the chapter actually took me apart, and it's the last beat: Cassie doesn't fight. She just goes, flat, "You didn't have dinner," and holds out the Cocoa Pebbles. She drops the whole thing to the floor and hands Vee a want she can *answer.* And Vee grabs it — "Yeah. Yeah. Thanks." — the first whole sentence she's managed since she walked in, "because here was a want she could find — a want the size of a bowl." That broke me a little. That's love. That's Cassie reading that Vee is standing on no floor at all and building her a small one she can stand on without having to answer the big question. It's the mother's-card thing again — the realest tenderness in this whole book, and it's not romantic, and it's from the woman everyone keeps telling me is just the roommate.

So: did I need this chapter? I'd have said a short quiet beat after two blazing ones is a risk. But it's doing the one thing I begged for in my notes — it's not idling in her happiness, it's pressing on the wound. The question's been asked out loud now. Somebody named it. The book can't un-ask it. I'm *more* keyed up for Tuesday— sorry, for Saturday brunch, than I was before, because now there's a witness.

The one thing I'll say against it: it's very short, and it's almost entirely setup-paying-off rather than new motion. If the next one is also a holding pattern I'll feel the stall. But this didn't feel like a shortcut. It felt like a breath held on purpose.
