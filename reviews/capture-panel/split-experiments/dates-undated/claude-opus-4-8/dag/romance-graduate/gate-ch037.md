REACTION

Oh, this one surprised me. I braced for a bridge chapter — girl swims, girl thinks — and I've skimmed a hundred of those where the heroine does laps and conveniently narrates her entire emotional arc in complete sentences while doing the backstroke. This wasn't that. This did the opposite of what those chapters do, and that's why it got me.

The whole point is that the water takes the *words* off. She goes in with "do you love him" and "did he mind" as these two clean sentences riding her, and the book doesn't let her solve them. It grinds them down. By the fourth piece she can't hold the words on the strokes anymore, by the sixth it's just *him, and the ache, and the count,* and then — *Then the count.* Just the count. That little one-line paragraph did something to me. That's exactly how it works. That's a writer who has actually been inside a body doing a repetitive thing and knows thought doesn't survive it, it dissolves into rhythm. I believe this woman.

And then the knife, the four strokes I keep going back to: *whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting.* There it is. That's the dread I've been carrying the whole book handed back to me *in her own voice* for the first time, not through me-above-her, not through Randi's leaked pipe — her, underwater, getting four strokes of clear sight before the wall comes and rolls it away. She almost saw it. The book gave her the exact thought I've been screaming and then let the pool take it before she could keep it. That's crueler and better than if she'd never thought it at all.

The *maybe*. She lay in the dark with the true answer sitting right there, easy to reach, and it came out of her mouth as *maybe* and she has no idea why. I do. Her body said yes to Pace months ago without telling her; now her mouth is doing the reverse, hedging the one thing that's true. The two of them — mouth and body — have never once been on the same page in this entire book and I love that the book won't let her integrate. She's still two women.

What I clocked: no Randi, no heat, no Pace on the page, and I didn't miss any of it, which tells me the book earned this. It's the first chapter that's *only* Vee, alone, nobody looking at her, and she's still fully a person in it — more, maybe, because the pool is the one place her body "has no opinions" and "only works," which is the single most poignant line in here given that her body is the thing everyone in this book is using to reach her. One room where she isn't being watched. One. And she had to drive to a Tuesday pool to find it.

I'm a little in awe that the burn worry — *did he mind the counter* — got threaded through here too, because it's the small domestic version of the big question. He gave her a drawer. He said nothing. She can't read the silence. The silence where his word should be, says the jacket, and now the silence is spreading from "what are we" to "did I leave too much of myself on your counter" — she's auditing her own footprint in his house, which is what you do right before someone tells you you've overstayed, or right before you move in. She can't tell which. Neither can I, and that's the good kind of not-knowing.

This is the second quiet chapter in a row (36 was Cassie, this is the pool), and I noticed — that's my tell, I always notice the second one. But I'm not reaching for dialogue to rescue it, because this one isn't quiet the way a stall is quiet. It moved the interior further than the last two spicy chapters did. The induction detonator still hasn't gone off, the three of them still haven't been in a room, Randi's aim is still unreadable, and now there's a new fuse: Vee is one wall-turn away from seeing the whole con, and the book has shown me she can almost get there alone. That's not a stall. That's the elastic pulling back.
