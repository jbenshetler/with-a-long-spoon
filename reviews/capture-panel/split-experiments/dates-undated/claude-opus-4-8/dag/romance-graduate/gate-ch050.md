REACTION

Oh, this is the one I was afraid she wasn't going to give me, and she gave it to me sideways — through Sheri, the one person in the whole book who doesn't want a single thing off him. That diner. The amber Elvis under the tabletop glass, the kid who sets the double in front of the big man and the single in front of five feet of Sheri by the grave confidence of arithmetic — I laughed out loud at that, actually laughed, which I needed because I've spent three chapters with a girl crying into a flannel shirt. Sheri is a gift. She's the only character who can say *get over yourself* to him and have it be love. "The philosophy of your food. Lord almighty." She's the reader. She's me across the booth kicking him under the table.

And she does the thing nobody in this book does: she calls it. "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." Clean. No con in her, no angle, just a woman who's lost her whole family to grief and knows exactly what it costs to withhold yourself on purpose. The Paige story gutted me — *call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas* — and she lays the irony right next to his withholding and he's decent enough not to touch it. Two people who won't say the thing, for opposite reasons, eating cheeseburgers.

But here's what the chapter actually did to me. It gave me the Daphne wound whole, finally, and I don't fully know what to do with how it landed. The theory he built: *the words are the difference.* He told Daphne, she left, it ate his life. He never said it again, and loss stayed survivable. So he's not withholding the word out of cruelty or even really out of the con — he genuinely believes the word is load-bearing, that saying it is what makes a person able to destroy you. "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." And I believe him. That's the problem. I believe him and it's still monstrous, because Vee is drowning in a cold dorm room over a riddle he could solve in two syllables, and his reason is a superstition he built at nineteen.

The drunk chapter — him alone, the whiskey he doesn't drink, the bleach smell, the mop with white foam crusted above the waterline that he just *steps around and shuts the door on* — he got sick, he cleaned it up in the night, and the book doesn't narrate it, it just leaves the evidence in the hall. That undid me the way the air guitar undid me. And the freezer: the chicken in one column, the broccoli in the next, enough to go on like this a long time. He eats the prison-food bowl not hungry, because he told it to. *He ate anyway.* That's the whole man right there — discipline where a feeling should be.

And then the engine on the highway. His head lifting before he knows he's moved, his whole body gathering toward the cut in the hill, and it's the wrong sound, he knows it whole and immediate, and he *doesn't let go of it anyway.* It goes past. He sobs over the table until he can't hear the truck. That's the first time I've seen him break and there's no one to perform it for and no gauge to check. I came into this chapter braced to keep hating him on Vee's behalf and I come out of it wrecked for both of them, which is exactly the trap this book keeps springing on me and I keep walking into it with my eyes open.

But — and this is the thing I'd text a friend at 1 a.m. — he STILL frames the three of them in his kitchen, the fantasy further along than last time, "because he had been adding to it for weeks," Vee in his sheet with too much honey in her tea and Randi with her feet up on the lacquered seat, and *he never once thinks the word con, or the word trap, or the fact that Randi is reporting Vee's confessions back to him in bed.* He wonders only "whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her." Even gutted, even sobbing, even having learned the exact shape of his own cowardice, the plan is still running underneath. He's done the self-knowledge homework on the word and done zero on the thing he and Randi are actually doing to her. That gap is the scariest thing in the chapter and the book doesn't underline it, which is why it works.

Four quiet chapters in a row now — Cropped, Seconds, the porch, Bare, Believe Me, Fairytale, Old Acquaintances, and now this. I clocked it around Fairytale and I'm clocking it hard here. But it isn't stalling, because the content keeps advancing even as nobody's in the same room — I got the breakup, I got Randi's whole interior and her cold, I got Daphne. The machine is disassembled and I'm being walked through each part alone. What I'm starving for now is the reassembly. The three of them in one room has STILL not happened and I have been promised it so long it's load-bearing. And now there's a new clock: Valentine's weekend, the Brooks and Dunn cover band, Sheri saying *you're coming and I don't care what the redhead's got to say about it* — a public dance floor, the exact thing Vee begged for and got Chili's. Someone's going to collide at that hall. I can feel the author loading it.
