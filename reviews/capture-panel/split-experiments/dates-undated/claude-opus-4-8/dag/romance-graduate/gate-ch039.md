Okay so this is the third Saturday-brunch chapter in the machine now and I came into it braced for the pattern — open the door, extract, close it soft at the peak, the leaked accent — and the book knew I was braced, because it did the one thing that actually scared me: it took the *story* away from Vee. For thirty-eight chapters the pipe ran because Vee had *content* to carry. Now she's got nothing but the texture of being happy, and she carries *that* instead, and it's worse, it's so much worse, because the happiness is the real intelligence they're harvesting now. "He woke you up." "Like I'd been eating with the sound off." She's not reporting acts anymore. She's handing over the inside of her own transformation and calling it girl-talk.

"It doesn't," she says, low and not quite even — meaning *it doesn't sound unhinged* — but what the book is showing me under the line is that it landed on Randi as a wound. Because Randi chose her as a body first. Randi and Pace picked a mark. Vee reads it as *pride* — "a Saturday of hers could do that to a woman like Randi." I read it as the con collapsing inward on the con artist. That's the whole chapter in one beat and nobody said it, thank God, they finally trusted me not to need it explained.

The photo. God.
