REACTION

God, that hurt. That was a small, sharp knife right between the ribs.

We’ve watched her collect his smells, collect his drawers, collect every little crumb of domestic perfection he drops on the table, convincing herself it equals devotion. And here, in a tiny dorm room with the cold coming through the cracked window, she finally hits the bottom of it. A piece of flannel can’t love you back. The heat in the shirt is just her own skin heating the threads.

I’ve had this night. I think every woman who has ever loved a man who refused to name her has had this exact, miserable 3 a.m. staring at a phone screen. You want to text, you want to demand, but you know the minute you type it out, it shrinks. He’ll reply with something calm, something warm, something utterly reasonable that leaves you feeling like the crazy one for wanting more than "what we have right now." It’s an invisible wall, and she’s finally feeling the bricks under her hands.

Stripping the shirt off and choosing to just lie there shivering in the bare cold rather than take comfort in a half-truth? That gutted me. She didn't neatly fold it. She dropped it like a dead weight. The spell is slipping. Her body has had enough of the anesthesia.
