REACTION

Oh, Pace. You absolute, miserable, self-sabotaging fool.

I’ve spent chapters wanting to shake this man until his teeth rattled for what he’s doing behind Vee’s back, but watching him sit across from Sheri getting read for filth was the exact medicine this book needed. Sheri calling him out on his high-minded bullshit—telling him straight to his face that not saying it matters more to him than having her, and that from where she sits, it feels pretty shitty—felt like the author reached into my brain and handed the microphone to the only person Pace couldn't deflect with a proof or a skillet.

And then the descent into his perspective. The messy, hungover, pathetic reality of him drinking himself sick on the floor, clinging to his rigid meal-prep chicken and frozen broccoli while his house echoes with jazz and ghost memories. I loved finally seeing the root of the trauma with Daphne—the classic, deeply human flaw where a smart man builds an entire architectural defense system around one past heartbreak, convincing himself that withholding three words will magically protect him from destruction. It’s so cowardly, but it makes him breathe as a real person instead of just a calculated villain.

Except the villainy is still ticking right underneath. Even in his grief, the rot is there: he’s sitting in the dark flipping through photos, literally fantasizing about the three-way domestic fantasy—Vee in a sheet, Randi with her feet on the chair, him at the stove. He admits he loves Vee, but he still hasn't dismantled the trap he and Randi set for her. The sheer cognitive dissonance of him sobbing over an engine that isn't her car, while still quietly calculating how she'll handle being brought into bed with his other lover, makes my chest ache. The drama is coiled so tight right now. When this dam breaks, the devastation is going to be monumental, and I am entirely buckled in.
