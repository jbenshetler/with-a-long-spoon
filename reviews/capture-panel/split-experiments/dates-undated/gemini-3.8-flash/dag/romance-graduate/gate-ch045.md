REACTION

Oh, Vee. My stomach hurts for her.

This chapter made me so uncomfortable in the best, most sickening romance-reader way. She is starting to claw at the edges of the cage, but she doesn't even know it’s a cage yet—she just thinks the air is getting thin. The sudden, snappy outburst in the dark? God, I felt that in my bones. Every woman who has ever let an undefined situationship eat her alive from the inside out has had that exact moment: you try to keep it cool, you try to play by the unspoken rules, and then something slip-slides out of your mouth at full volume and sounds desperate and mean because you've been swallowing it for months.

And then Chili’s. The absolute genius of making him take her to Chili’s. It was pathetic and heartbreaking. She wanted so badly to drag this curated, artisan wood-and-cream fantasy out into the fluorescent, sticky-table reality of the world to see if it would survive. And it didn't. He couldn't handle the noise and the TV screens, the food tasted like chemical paste because he’s literally retrained her palate to reject the life she used to have, and she convinced herself he was embarrassed of her.

His explanation when they got back—about sensory overload and not being able to hear her—is so diabolically tender. Because it’s probably completely true! He’s a solitary math nerd who builds furniture; of course a crowded Chili's fries his brain. But it’s also the perfect trap. It gives her just enough emotional logic to fold her weapons away, tell herself she’s "forgiving" him, and crawl right back into his bed to let him fuck the questions out of her head.

The sex was gorgeous, urgent, and heavy with that dreadful post-nut clarity where you realize great orgasms don't actually solve the problem. She got what she wanted—she broke his control, she made him unspool—and the second he fell asleep, the silence filled right back up with the absence of a word. She’s completely his, marked up with him, lying in his bed, and she has literally nothing she could hold up to the light of day. The rot is setting in, and I am leaning all the way forward waiting for the floor to drop out.
