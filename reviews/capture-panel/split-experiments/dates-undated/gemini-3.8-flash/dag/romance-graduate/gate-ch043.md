REACTION — God, Cassie is such a necessary cold shower in this book. Every time Vee spends a weekend drowning in the fantasy cabin, getting fed handmade pizza and wrapped in flannel, Cassie is sitting right there in the fluorescent light of the dining hall, forcing her to look at the edges of the picture she’s actively trimming down.

The detail about cropping the photo before sending it home to her family hit so hard. It encapsulates everything Vee is doing right now: cutting out the parts of herself that are becoming too raw, too exposed, or too strange for the people who actually love her, just so she can keep pretending she’s living a normal, tidy collegiate romance. Her mother noticing the collarbone, the lost weight, the suspicion that something is wrong—mothers always know when a daughter is burning through herself, even if they misdiagnose the fire.

She’s keeping the reality quiet not just to protect the illusion for her parents, but to protect it from herself.
