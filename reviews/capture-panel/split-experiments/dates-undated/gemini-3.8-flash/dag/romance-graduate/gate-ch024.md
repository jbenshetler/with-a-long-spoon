REACTION:

Oh, God. My stomach is entirely sick.

This chapter is utterly devastating, and there isn’t a single raised voice or scandalous betrayal in it. It is just two people under a quilt in front of a fire, and it broke my heart into pieces. Helen Rivers is ruthless for this.

First of all: *Peter*. Finding out his real name is Peter, that he’s only twenty-two, that he was exiled into the school hallway at twelve for being too brilliant, and that at fifteen he was a lonely, isolated boy being tutored and groomed by a nineteen-year-old college girl—it completely recontextualizes him. Pace isn’t just this calculating, hyper-competent sex god; he is an extraordinarily guarded, slightly battered boy who built an impenetrable armor out of heavy squats, courtly manners, and mathematics because the world was too careless with him. And the Daphne detail! The way Vee clocks the arithmetic instantly—*“the clean ugly grown-up word, and she held it down under her tongue”*—and recognizes that he defends it because admitting what it actually was would destroy him. That is so astonishingly perceptive. Vee isn’t a dumb college girl; she has massive emotional intelligence. She sees right into the soft, wounded center of him, and she protects him from it.

And then he explains math to her—the Gabriel’s Horn paint paradox, the coastlines, Newton’s three laws—and it is more intensely erotic than half the explicit scenes on my Kindle. The prose here is breathtaking. *“Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some.”* When Vee tells him that the way he lifts is the way he is with her—*“You’re right here. It’s the lift. It’s the same face”*—I had to put my hands over my mouth. That is intimacy so deep it makes your teeth ache. And her hand resting on the scar on his inner thigh, and him not pulling away, just covering her hand and saying *“Not tonight.”* A door with a time on it. He is giving himself to her. Actually giving himself to her, Peter to Vivienne, stripping off the “Pace” persona piece by piece.

Which makes the ending an absolute gut punch.

Watching Vee lie there in the dark, warm and glowing and soft, marveling at how “fortunate” she is, tracing the timeline—bumping into Randi outside the dining hall, Pace crossing the coffee shop—and believing with every ounce of her pure, generous heart that the universe just decided to bless her with two miraculous people who love her... *“One piece, he’d said... It had come to her all of a piece.”*

It *is* all of a piece, Vee. But it wasn’t fate. It was a trap.

The tragic irony is suffocating. She thinks she was handed grace; she was handed an ambush. But the catastrophic twist is that Peter has accidentally fallen completely in love with her in the middle of the con. The equation has broken entirely. When the truth comes out—and it has to, the induction is coming—it is going to destroy all three of them. I am genuinely terrified.
