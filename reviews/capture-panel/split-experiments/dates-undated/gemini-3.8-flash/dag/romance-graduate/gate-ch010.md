REACTION

Oh, god. My stomach is in knots.

The dramatic irony in this book is becoming an absolute vice grip. Vee is floating, she is practically glowing, spinning around her dorm room like a girl who just found out the world has color in it—and every single detail she’s so proud of is a wire Pace and Randi strung up for her to trip over. "I made *him* stop. And I kissed him. Me." Sweetheart, no. He orchestrated that entire hesitation. He left the quiet open *for* you. It’s so devastating because Vee’s joy is completely, heartbreakingly real. She is so starved for genuine attention, for someone to treat her like a person rather than an ornament or an obstacle, that Pace’s calculated gentleness feels like salvation.

And then Cassie. God, Cassie is breaking my heart. She’s the only real anchor Vee has, standing there with her sock feet and her dry humor, clocking every single red flag that Vee is too dazzled to see. "Off six-fourteen... That's the whole address." Cassie knows. She doesn't know the specifics of the horror show being prepped in that workshop, but she knows the *shape* of a girl getting peeled away from her life by people who have too much polish.

Then the smoothie shop. Randi is terrifying. The way she draws Vee out, pumping her for information about Pace under the guise of giggly girlfriend talk—"Ask and then ravish"—while secretly feeding her own voyeuristic, master-plan fantasy? It’s sick, and it’s brilliant, and I couldn't look away. But what made my pulse actually spike was the interruption. Brooke walking in.

Because we saw Chapter 2. We know the sheer, ruthless, sorority-board surveillance Brooke exerts. We know how Randi crawled back into that house humiliated, nursing the sting of Pace's bench, being cross-examined over the kitchen table. And here comes Brooke again, running her CEO-wife gaze over Vee, taking the measure of this girl with the pilled cardigan cuffs and the oversized body. And Randi—Randi *takes Vee’s hand*. On the table. In front of Brooke.

Randi calls her "my dear friend."

Vee thinks it was a gift. Vee thinks Randi spent social capital to protect her, to elevate her. And maybe she did, but why? Is Randi staking a claim to prove to Brooke that she doesn't care? Or is Randi already so possessive of the toy she and Pace are grooming that she can't bear to let Brooke diminish it? The tension in that handclasp was thicker than any sex scene. It felt dangerous. It felt like Randi crossing a line she didn't mean to cross, claiming Vee in daylight, in front of the very tribunal she fears most.

There wasn't a stitch of skin in this chapter, but the psychological rot underneath all this sunshine is so decadent. Vee is walking into the woods on Sunday. She's borrowing "real shoes." I am terrified for her, and I am entirely hooked on how this trap is snapping shut.
