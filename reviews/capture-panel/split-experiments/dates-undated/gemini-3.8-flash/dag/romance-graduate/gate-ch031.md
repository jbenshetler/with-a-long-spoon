REACTION

Oh, *damn*.

This is the exact kind of high-wire, internal, exhibitionist heat that I live for, and it didn't need a single touch from a man to set the page on fire. That second step across the stage—*“She took a breath, and made the second step long.”* My stomach literally did a flip. That wasn't just a girl showing some skin; that was Vee stepping out of the safe, invisible, department-store-navy version of herself that her mother raised her to be, and claiming the creature Pace and Randi are carving her into.

And the psychological layering here is breathtakingly sharp. The ache about her parents not being able to afford the flight, the dull ache of her mother’s “bright voice” for the things she can't give her, layered instantly over the absolute illicit thrill of knowing she’s standing there completely commando in a bespoke couture silk gown and four-inch heels bought by people whose world she barely understands. *“Nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of.”* What a line. It’s intoxicating and deeply tragic all at once. She thinks this is her power. She thinks the secret belongs to *her*.

When she sends that picture to Pace in Berkeley—uncropped, mid-stride, leg bare to the hip—she thinks she’s gifting him a view of his masterpiece. She has no clue that Randi probably picked the shoes to orchestrate this exact visual, or that Pace is sitting in a conference carrying the weight of Daphne and Randi and every calculation he hasn’t confessed to. Vee feels like the author of her own sexuality for the first time in her life, and the irony is so sharp it cuts: she’s holding the reins of a runaway horse and thinking she’s the one driving.

It was short, but it hit like pure espresso. The absolute confidence of this book to give us a clothed (well, commando-under-silk) academic induction ceremony and make it feel dirtier, bolder, and more erotic than three chapters of standard KU thrusting is why I am utterly obsessed with this story.
