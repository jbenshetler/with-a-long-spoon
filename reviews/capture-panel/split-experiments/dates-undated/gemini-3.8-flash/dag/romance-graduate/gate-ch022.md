REACTION:

Oh, god. My stomach is in actual knots. This chapter was breathtakingly good and completely sickening at the same time.

That opening in the lecture hall—Simpson’s paradox. Rivers is not being subtle with the metaphor and I don't even care because it lands like a physical blow: *Each year on its own, the first man is the better hitter. Put the two years together and the second man is. Same numbers.* Pace alone looks like the dream lover. Randi alone looks like the dream, incandescent best friend. Put them together and Vee is walking straight into an absolute abattoir, completely blind. And Cassie—god bless Cassie sitting there in her two-year-old fleece, ignoring the professor entirely just to watch Vee's face to see if she was safe. Cassie is the only clean soul in this entire book.

And then Randi. Randi on her knees on that boutique floor. The echo of Pace kneeling before Vee with the measuring tape was so loud it made my ears ring. But where Pace touched her with reverence and restraint, Randi running that thumb over Vee’s plum-polished big toe—the exact polish Vee put on for *Pace*, that Randi knows damn well was for Pace—was an act of pure, predatory ownership. She literally tipped Vee off balance while Vee was sitting down. And calling him Vee's *lover* right to her face! The sociopathy of it, the absolute thrill Randi is getting out of puppeteering this entire fantasy, it’s chilling.

But what actually broke my heart was Vee pulling out the emergency credit card. That is the darkest turn yet. Vee is an econ major. She knows the TJMaxx math. She knows there is nothing under that card. But she has been so thoroughly seduced by the aesthetic, by the idea of becoming this un-apologetic, expensive woman that Pace and Randi have manufactured for her, that she bankrupts her real life to live up to their stage play. And beside her, Randi casually drops three times that amount on loafers without looking, completely oblivious (or totally indifferent) to the fact that she’s financially drowning this girl.

The dress hanging between them on the sidewalk at the end. Vee thinking she's never had a friend like this. It is dramatic irony written at an absolute masterclass level. I am terrified for the induction ceremony.
