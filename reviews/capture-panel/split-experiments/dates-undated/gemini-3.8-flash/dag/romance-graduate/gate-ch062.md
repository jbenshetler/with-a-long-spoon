REACTION

*Oh.*

My God, my chest hurts. Coming off chapter 61—which practically melted my screen, watching Pace and Randi drown in each other and use their bodies to process how deeply they are both in love with Vee—to walk straight into *this*? It is a masterclass in emotional whiplash, and I felt every single mile of it.

First of all, Vee arming herself in Meg’s keyhole top, a borrowed micro-skirt, and Randi’s five-inch induction heels to go face down a fictional, golden-skinned Amazon in a low-rent taproom is so painfully, perfectly twenty-one. We have all been that girl. We have all strapped on an absurd, shivering armor of shoes and liner because we built a monster in our heads out of three offhand words. And then the door opens, and Sheri isn't an ex or a model; she’s five-foot-nothing of bleached roots, loud-mouthed butch swagger, and total, immediate warmth.

The moment Sheri’s eyes tracked down Vee’s body—completely bypassing the competitive high-school ranking math Vee was bracing for, just openly, genuinely *appreciating* her, appreciating what Pace gets to come home to—and Vee just broke out laughing? I felt that breath leave my own lungs. What an exquisite release. And Pace blushing! *Pace.* The man who just spent chapter 61 holding Randi open and taking her down in increments without breaking a sweat, turning bright red up to the ears because a loud girl in a taproom asked if he gets to motorboat Vee’s chest. It stripped all the calculating architect out of him and left just a twenty-two-year-old boy who loves his girl.

And then Sheri gives her the keys to him.

*“Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen.”*

God. God. Two sentences from a girl peeling an empty longneck label, and the entire architecture of Peter Pace falls into focus. Why he makes pancakes in the middle of the night. Why he fixes door locks and dripping faucets without a word. Why he dances with women without demanding a down payment, why he hoards people at tables, why he can't say three simple words out loud but will stand his truck headlights on a dark porch until the light clicks on inside. He is a boy who lost his entire home in one violent break, and he has spent the six years since quietly, obsessively building an unshakeable shelter out of food, wood, and steady hands for the people he refuses to lose.

It makes what he and Randi are doing so much more poignant—and so much more terrifying. Because Vee is falling deeper and deeper into real, grown-up, marrow-deep love with the actual man behind the fortress. Sitting in the middle of that bench seat, watching his hands at ten-and-two carrying her home like a full cup he refuses to spill... it's breathtakingly sweet. But I'm sitting here clutching my Kindle knowing the foundation this whole house is built on has a fault line running right down the center.
