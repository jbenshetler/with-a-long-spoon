REACTION

Oh, Vee. *Vee.* The dramatic irony in this book is becoming an actual physical ache in my chest.

Watching her sit in that sunlit athletic dining hall, giggling with Meg and Kayla, glowing like a girl who just won the lottery because this gorgeous, respectful older PhD student is treating her like porcelain—while *I* just watched Randi ride him bare-assed on his bed while he fed her the scent of Vee’s skin like an appetizer—is almost unbearable. It’s so brilliantly, agonizingly sharp. Vee thinks she’s in a slow-burn indie romance. She thinks he’s so courtly, so restrained, climbing the walls just asking her what she wants for dinner. She tells her friends he was “shivering too hard to kiss” and edits out the fact that she stripped bare for him in the front seat, keeping that sacred little secret for herself. Meanwhile, he went home, showered her scent off, turned on Blondie, and gave Randi a play-by-play while Randi clenched around him.

And Randi leaving right after! That’s still ringing in my head from chapter 13, and it casts this massive shadow over chapter 14. Randi couldn't stay. Randi got off on Vee’s scent and the thought of her soft stomach, and then she bolted into the night because she couldn't handle how big and terrifying the want was. And now Pace texts Vee: *Saturday. Two o'clock.*

Kayla is 100% right: "Two in the afternoon is not dinner. There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat."

My stomach dropped when that text came through. We know what is in that house. We know about the walnut bench. We know about the restraint, the surgical precision, the way he dismantled Randi in chapter one. Vee is worrying about whether to wear a sweater that doesn't look like she's trying too hard, totally unaware that she is stepping directly into the lion's den.

This was a classic bridge chapter, the college-girl debrief, but it worked on me completely because it grounded Vee back in her normal, sunny world right before the floor drops out from under her. Meg and Kayla felt like real college girlfriends—the fries, the debate over "nice" guys, the immediate mobilization over hair. It makes Vee feel so young, so tender, and so devastatingly vulnerable. Saturday at two. God help her.
