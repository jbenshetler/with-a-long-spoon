**REACTION**

This is the breath after the sprint.

The previous sequence had been ratcheting the heat up until it was almost claustrophobic—the bet in the bedroom, the naked parade down the hallway, the utterly humiliating, suffocatingly intense brunch with Randi where Vee basically had to flee to a restaurant stall to keep from melting through the floor. It was getting loud. And to drop straight from that into the cold, chemical echo of a college pool on a Tuesday afternoon is brilliant pacing.

I know bridge chapters when I see them. Usually, in romance, an author treats physical activity as filler—a way to make the heroine feel "relatable" or show she has hobbies. But this felt completely authentic to an athlete's brain. That specific mental mechanism where heavy, repetitive exertion grinds down complicated emotions until they’re just rhythm and muscle ache? That’s real interiority. She isn't just swimming; she’s actively trying to outrun the realization that her life is slipping out of her hands.

Cassie’s question was the tripwire: *Do you love him?* And the four strokes where Vee wonders if she’s falling for the actual man or just the terrifyingly addictive experience of being worshipped—*that* is the core question of the whole book. Because we know what he is. We know about Randi, we know about Daphne, we know the architecture of the trap she’s in. Vee doesn't know, but her subconscious is finally trying to tap her on the shoulder.

It’s completely sexless, totally quiet, and yet I didn't skim a single sentence. It felt earned. That said, it’s a bridge, and a bridge only works if it leads somewhere solid on the other side. She’s cleared her head; now let’s see what she walks into next.
