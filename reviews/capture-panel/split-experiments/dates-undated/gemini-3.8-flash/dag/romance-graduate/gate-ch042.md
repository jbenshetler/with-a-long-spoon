REACTION

This chapter almost made me sick with how beautiful it was. That is the genius and the cruelty of this book: if you ripped this chapter out of context, it would be the absolute peak of high-tier, slow-burn contemporary romance. The kind of writing that makes you hold your breath.

The frantic, clumsy urgency at the front door was so real—the jeans sticking to her hips, hopping on one foot in the hallway, laughing while trying to strip off cotton socks, the utter lack of movie-set grace that actually makes intimacy feel alive. And then the contrast of the kitchen afterward. The way he cooks with that quiet, monastic absorption, making artisan sauce from scratch and sliding pizza onto a stone while she sips wine in his oversized robe. It caters directly to every single fantasy of being cared for by a competent, grounded man who doesn't perform his devotion, but just lives it.

And then the flannel shirt. God. That cracked my chest open. Walking in with the shirt she’d washed because his scent had faded, bracing herself for the humiliation of admitting how pathetic she felt sleeping with it every night—and his only reaction is that rare, expensive smile before pulling it right on over what he’s already wearing. It is such an achingly tender, emotionally intelligent beat.

Which is precisely why it feels like watching a lamb happily climb onto an altar. Every single gesture of true, domestic gentleness from Peter twists the knife deeper, because I know what happened on that couch just days ago. I know he picked her out like game on the quad with Randi. Vee is handing him her entire heart, building a life in his top drawer, begging to carry his scent home like a lifeline, completely oblivious to the fact that her sanctuary is built inside a cage. It’s sickening, it’s gorgeous, and I couldn't tear my eyes away if I tried.
