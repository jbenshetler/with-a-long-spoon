REACTION — God, the flannel. I've read a hundred "he gives her his hoodie" scenes and this one undid me anyway, because it's backwards: she's *returning* it, and he thinks she's giving it back and decides not to ask, and that half-second of hurt on his face before she gets the words out — that's the whole man, isn't it. "He was good at not asking things." Devastating little line, because it's the same skill that keeps the word from her. The book keeps doing this to me, taking his flaw and his tenderness and making them the same muscle.

The shirt ritual is the most honest thing Vee's done in forty chapters. She stood in his kitchen and admitted, out loud, to breathing a dying shirt for five nights. She's stopped managing herself with him. And he pulled it on *over* his other shirt, immediately, two shirts in his own kitchen because she told him to — that's Pace saying I love you in the only language he's got, objects and compliance, and I'm choosing to find it romantic rather than broken, though the book is smart enough that it's both.

The doorway to the bed — okay, yes, the hopping out of the jeans with the denim inside out at her ankle, the bra he *held* so she had to unspool out of it, "You're not helping" / "I'm helping" — that's the playful-possessive register done right, and the shamefully fast first one, her body "publishing the number" — I laughed and then went a little hot. That's the voice this book has: it'll make a joke and a confession out of the same beat. The second one, the slow roll into his neck, gave me the romance more than the first gave me the heat.

And the pizza. I want to be clear that a man making water buffalo mozzarella pizza for a possibly empty house while his girlfriend watches in his robe is doing more for me than any billionaire penthouse scene I've read this year. "The care was only the shape his attention took when it came down on a thing" — I underlined that with my thumb. That's the thesis of him. Also "she took him without question now... nothing left in her that wanted to argue the point" — small chill under the warmth, which I suspect is on purpose.

What I'm chewing on after: five nights was *her* calling it, and she wanted him to fight for the nights and he didn't, and the book names that corner of her as unreasonable — but it isn't, is it? She wants to be *wanted out loud*. That's the drawer, the word, the room, all of it. She keeps asking for his want in objects and proxies because he won't hand it to her in words, and the flannel is just the newest proxy. Meanwhile chapter 41 is sitting behind this one like a loaded gun — MIRA, Randi's father, Vee watching her take the hit and deciding kindness means silence. Two chapters in a row now of Vee holding knowledge back from someone she loves. She's learning their habits. That's the shadow I can't shake while the pizza was still warm.
