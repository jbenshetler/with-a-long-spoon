REACTION — Oh, this one got me. It's the mirror chapter, and the book clearly knows it: Randi lying awake in a gold room feeling nothing, Vee lying awake in a lavender room feeling everything, both of them sending the *exact same text* to each other. Two women, two beds, one New Year's, and neither of them is where she wants to be. That symmetry could've been cheap if it were pointed at — but it's not, you have to catch it yourself, and catching it yourself is the whole pleasure. That's the difference between a book explaining its subtext and a book trusting you with it, and this one keeps trusting me.

The line that broke me a little: "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone." This whole chapter is about the room — the ribbons, the little gold swimmers, the clarinet in the closet. Her childhood bedroom loved Vivienne and doesn't know who Vee is, and she *knows* it doesn't know her, and she can't go back and can't see forward. That's the loneliest kind of alone, where even your own bed is a stranger's hospitality.

And the spite-girlfriend invention — I've DONE that. 1 a.m., building the blonde your man is obviously with out of pure self-torture, handing her the exact opposite of your own body ("a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's" — that's a knife of a line because it's Vee's insecurity in Vee's own cruel phrasing, not the book's). And then worse, the memory-corruption — going back to the best night she has to warm herself and *watching it turn into the blonde in the falling sheet.* "She could not keep her own night." That genuinely upset me, in the good way, the way a book earns.

Also noticed, and I'm proud of myself for it: the Sheri thread. "He had cooked for Sheri at Thanksgiving. *She's alone,* he'd said" — and now a whole Christmas week she can't see into, and "now he was doing it for someone, and she didn't get to know who." The book is loading Sheri and I can see it loading and Vee can half-see it loading, which is worse. And the two of them — the two texts crossing, each writing warm careful non-revealing messages, Randi giving the fairytale and Vee giving the open hand. Neither telling the other the true thing. The dramatic irony from the jacket is doing exactly what it promised: I'm a step ahead of both of them and it *hurts.*

No heat at all this chapter, and I didn't miss it — this is the kind of quiet chapter I'd defend to anyone. But it's the second one in a row with no contact between the three leads (Randi's was last chapter, this is Vee's, and I'm guessing Pace gets one or we cut back to campus). The pattern is deliberate — parallel solitude before the term restarts — but the book has spent its budget of quiet for now. If the next chapter isn't back at school, back in a room with two of these people in it, I'll start tapping my foot.
