REACTION — Okay, the last four chapters together have quietly become my favorite stretch of the book, and this one landed hardest of all, which is funny because nothing "happens" — a girl gets a wax. But that's the whole thesis of this series, isn't it: nothing is ever just the thing. This chapter is interiority with the heat fully intact, and I honestly didn't know a salon scene could do this to me.

The middle passage is the one that got its hooks in me — the wrong hand. "It held her the exact way she wanted to be held and it did not want her at all." That's the thesis of Vee's whole arc in one image: she's learned a new grammar of the body, Pace's grammar, hold-still and be kept, and now she can't unknow it, and a bored professional's palm on her thigh opens "a hole in her a mile across where the right hand should be." I read that paragraph twice. It's doing the thing I keep begging these books to do — explicitness as psychology, not choreography.

The mother's register sliding in — *the kind of girl who* — that voice shutting her eyes and it staying anyway. I felt that in my teeth. And the small detail that she doesn't look at Sondra's face to see if the body told on her — the book keeps that thread from chapter 60 alive (the body telling on her before she'd said a word) without ever naming the thread. It never explains itself to me. I have to bring the echo myself, and I get to feel clever doing it.

The structure of the yes continues to be the real engine: she answered before her mind caught up, the fear arrives after, too late to be any use — "that was nearly the point of it." That's the dare-becomes-the-craving thing from the jacket actually being paid off in the prose, not just promised on the jacket.

And Randi's omission — "the part Randi hadn't said" about rolling over. I don't think that was an accident, do you? Randi has been curating Vee's experiences from the boutique floor on. Coaching her into deeper water, each yes a door with another door behind it. The jacket says Vee doesn't know there are three games and I am side-eyeing Randi SO hard right now — is she scatterbroning this or is this Pace-and-Randi still moving her, one unveiled step at a time? Either way, the fact that the book let the omission sit there unjudged — Vee rolls over and doesn't quite blame her and I, the reader, DO — that's trust in me.

Also, small applause: "a girl who said sorry to furniture" swearing at the first rip, and the girls in the waiting room — one there to be looked at, one there to be touched, and Vee knowing which one she is. That's a whole character sketch in three sentences.

What am I hungry for now is the payoff — his hand finding what she did for him. The book has been training the weekend like a loaded gun since Sunday. If it skips that scene or closes the door on it, I will throw my Kindle at a wall. It won't. This book has never once taken a door away from me.
