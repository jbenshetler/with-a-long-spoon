REACTION — The drawer. I've read hundreds of grand gestures — heroes buying penthouses, heroes standing in the rain — and this man folds his razor and comb down into one cramped drawer so her scrunchies get the top one, the easy one, the one your hand goes to first, and I had to put my coffee down. "Room for more of her, laid out like something prepared and waiting." He didn't tell her it didn't matter. He *made her a place.* That's the whole book in one gesture, and the fact that there's no note, that there's never a note, is exactly why it landed like it did. He says everything in objects and never in words, and Vee lying awake in section i doing the inventory — the door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the coming back — is her learning his language. And then "Peter," barely a breath, to his sleeping shape. My chest.

But I'm me, so I can't just take it. Because what is that drawer from *his* side? "Room for more of her" — prepared and waiting. He bought dozens of scrunchies before she ever slept over twice. He has a schedule under "not yet." This man does nothing unplanned, and Vee reads the drawer as love (and it is), and I read it as also architecture. Both can be true and that's why I can't quit this book.

Section ii actually scared me more than anything in chapters — her stuff colonizing his counter, his eyes holding there a second, and Vee waiting to find out if she'd been too much. That's the oldest fear in her, the good-enough-black-dress fear, and the book let it sit unanswered for a week of her time. And the answer was the drawer. Set-up and payoff inside one chapter, earned, quiet. Also: she's losing weight, eating like a field hand, her body "quietly burning down whole weekends of him" — a sentence I'll be thinking about, half romantic and half warning, and I think the book knows it's both.

The one flicker of impatience: section i is a lot of retrospective telling — a week missed, the door spin, the salmon, all summarized rather than lived. It's beautiful summary, and I get that we're compressing to get to the drawer, but a page of it I was reading faster than I read the rest. The chapter rights itself the second she opens that drawer.

Also "she could live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying" — no, sweetheart, you can't, and the book and I both know that sentence is a lie you're telling at your happiest so the crash has somewhere to fall from. "He gives her everything but a word for what they are" — jacket promise, still unfired, and now she's armed it herself.
