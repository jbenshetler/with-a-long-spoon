REACTION — Okay. This is the chapter I'd have told you this book couldn't write, back in my hundred-hockey-players era: a woman alone in a flannel shirt, trying to taste her own fingers and failing, and it's the most charged thing I've read all week. The whole thing is one held breath. I was literally leaning forward over my Kindle with my tea going cold.

What gets me is that the failure is the scene. Any other book would have her power through — brave heroine, little triumphant lick, character development, check. Here she stops a breath's width away and *holds there until her lungs hurt*, and the book doesn't rescue her or shame her. It just lets the gap stay open. "Her mouth was still empty." God. That last line. And her settling her hip over the damp patch — *her mess, hers to lie in* — that's not defeat, it's a truce with herself, and it's hotter for being unresolved.

And the thing I keep turning over: the smell. The way she braces for the stink the boys' jokes promised and finds a hothouse instead. I remember being twenty-one and someone, some boy, acting like it was a favor he was doing me, and how long I carried somebody else's verdict about my own body. So watching Vee hold her fingers up in the lamplight, seeing the sticky strands stretch, naming it *excess* in her mother's voice and then — almost — not believing it? That landed somewhere personal. The amount of her. "More of her than she would have admitted even alone." That's the whole book in one sentence, honestly.

The Randi thread running through it is doing sneaky, devastating work. She's building Randi's *It was powerful, it turned me on* into her fantasy like load-bearing architecture, imagining Randi's fingers lifted to her mouth, and she doesn't clock what she's doing any more than she clocked the bathroom climax being narrated in Randi's face. I clock it. I clock all of it and I'm biting my knuckle. And *Careful* — that single italic word that interrupts the fantasy, right when she's picturing Pace's carefulness — whose word is that? Pace's or hers? I genuinely don't know and I don't think she does either.

Small thing I loved: her shutting Cassie's window at the top, mothering the whole room, and then having to shove it back open at the end to air herself out. That little comedy of heat management — the room as her body, closed and opened. This writer does things with houses and rooms and coats that I usually only see in literary fiction, except nobody in literary fiction is ever this wet.

I did feel the structure a hair — third solo session across recent chapters (the mirror, the bathroom stall, now this), and each one is doing different work so I'm not bored, but the pattern is visible now. One more self-directed interlude and I'll start wanting the plot's other shoe. Because there are so many shoes: MIRA still live, Brooke's ultimatum to Randi hanging unfired, "the two who moved her have fallen every bit as far," and Vee's mouth is *empty and reaching*. The next time she's under Pace and he comes up wet — I need that scene. I need it like air.

This is the book I'd have said I was starving for, if I'd known it could exist: the explicitness with the actual inside of a woman's head in it, shame and wanting braided together so I can't always tell them apart either.
