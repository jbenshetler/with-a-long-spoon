REACTION — Oh, this chapter. This sneaky little piano-closet chapter.

I knew going in that everything Vee tells Randi travels — the jacket promised me that, and I've watched it happen for thirty chapters — but knowing it doesn't inoculate me against the *feel* of it, which is the whole point of this book. Vee sitting in that soundproof cell telling Randi about the wet hand and the sting, and me knowing exactly where it's headed: straight into Randi's mouth on Friday night, straight into Pace's ear, straight into the machine. And the book doesn't wince or wink. It just lets her talk, half-feral and flushed, while I sit there doing the arithmetic she can't.

The scene where Randi draws it out of her is the best interrogation-by-affection I've read in ages. "Did you deserve it?" — and Vee says *probably, yeah* and only hears what she's agreed to a beat too late. That's the book's whole method in one exchange: Randi never pushes, she just sets questions down on the desk between them and lets Vee pick them up herself. Vee thinks she's confessing; she's being *auditioned*. And Randi's own story — over his knee, counting out loud, his hand slipping — told in that low lazy register with her eyes on Vee's face the whole time — that was hot, genuinely, and it was also a *tool*, and I felt both of those things at once and couldn't separate them, which I think is exactly the intended experience. Randi got what she came for, and so did I, and I'm a little disturbed by how much the overlap is.

The bathroom beat is what tipped it from good to great for me. Vee standing there soaked through, catching her own hand, *I will not say yes to it* — that's her whole character in one gesture. She's still doing the not-saying, still running her talent for almost-knowing, but the ground is visibly moving under her. And Randi's story about "the mouse that roars" — I don't buy it as biography, not entirely. "Mine. You'd never have picked him out of a room." Slight, soft-spoken, apologized to furniture — that's not any boyfriend, that's a *portrait*, and I half-think she's describing Pace sideways, or describing what she wants done to her by someone who never has, or just building the room she wants Vee to live in. The not-knowing is doing work.

And the ending — the goodbye kiss Vee's arms are slow to leave. She held on. She noticed it *after*. That's the book moving its pieces with the tiniest increments, and I'm watching every one.

Small complaints: the professor's outlier lecture is doing the book's symbol-work with a slightly heavier hand than usual — first kind leaves no trace, second kind carries forward into everything after — yes, I see you, book, you can stop pointing at it now. It's a good metaphor for Pace-in-Vee's-life but I caught it being a metaphor, and this book is usually better than that. And Cassie got granola-bar crumbs this chapter, which I noticed, because my dread-compound-interest on Cassie needs feeding.

But the heat was real, the psychological pressure was real, and the last line — *she put it down to that* — is the most Vee sentence in the book. She's putting things down to that more and more often, and one day there won't be a *that* big enough.
