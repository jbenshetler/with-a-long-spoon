REACTION — Okay. Okay okay okay. I finished this chapter and just sat there with my Kindle on my chest like a Victorian lady myself.

This is the chapter I've been reading fifty-eight chapters for and didn't know it. The taste conversation — I've read the "he likes going down, did you know" conversation in maybe thirty books, and it has NEVER done this. Because in those books it's a pep talk. Randi isn't giving a pep talk. Randi says "I don't" with the exact flatness she'd use for a wine pairing, and then — "It was powerful. It turned me on" — in her restaurant-recommendation voice, and Vee's whole body answers before her mind gets a vote, and the book doesn't underline any of it. The sentence just walks home with her. "I don't." That's the whole chapter, that's the thesis, and Rivers trusted me to carry it.

And Randi's still Randi, which is what makes it hurt: "Did you swallow all of it?" "Didn't spill a drop?" — she's prosecuting the case with visible relish, she's getting as much out of this debrief as Vee is, and then at exactly the wrong (right) moment she goes still and stops playing and just *listens*. "She'd set the court down and gone still." That's the woman who loves her. That's the Gstaad chapter paying off in a restaurant booth.

The neck. THE NECK. Vee kisses that hollow on pure muscle memory from Tuesday, "landed before any of her had convened on the question" — this book keeps doing that to her, her mouth acting ahead of her committee, and Randi's whole body does "a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long" and calls it *ticklish* and I actually said "oh, honey" out loud. Two women standing in the cold, both of them lying beautifully, both of them knowing the other one is doing it. That's hotter than half the explicit scenes I've read this year, and this is a fully clothed goodbye on a public sidewalk.

Small things I want on record: the Lifesaver tapped against Randi's wrist, the palm closing without either of them looking away from the front — Marsh's grooming lecture doing double duty the whole hour and never once winking at the camera. The coat swinging open on the dark bloom before Vee catches it, "the body telling on her before she'd said a word" — that coat is doing so much quiet work as the book's id. "He parked" about the wrist. Randi leaving her entire meal after one bite because the food was never the point. And "You picked well" — with Vee privately noting she didn't pick him, he was already crossing the floor, the whole thing decided before she knew — the blurb's shadow falling all the way across a nice compliment. Chills.

The Marsh chapters shouldn't work. A lecture as chapter furniture is the laziest trick in campus fiction, and this woman has me leaning into lice combs and baboon budgets because every single thing she says is secretly about the three of them and she never says so. "Watch who grooms whom for one week and you can draw the whole politics of it." Miss Ma'am. I see you.

One tiny friction: the middle does spend a while re-telling the weekend I just lived through in full. I didn't mind — Vee's retellings are performances and Randi's reactions are the point, and the way Vee keeps trying to fold the stories down "to a size she could sit behind" and Randi keeps making her unfold them is its own little arc — but I felt the length once or twice.

The last lines, though. The sentence keeping pace with her in the wind, "arriving again at its own intervals, like a song that gets in." That's the waking-up the blurb promised, and it's happening in her *body* and her own voice, not in anyone's mouth but hers. She's not being told. She's noticing. That's the grown-up version of this story and I've been starving for it.

Tuesday. I need Tuesday like I need air.
