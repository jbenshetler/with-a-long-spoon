REACTION — Oh, this chapter wrecked me. Quietly, completely wrecked me. I've read eight hundred versions of "he can't say it back" and I saw the deflection coming the second she opened her mouth — but seeing it didn't protect me at all, because the book did the cruelest thing: it made me feel her hope *first*. The sentence she built, "I had been eating crackers my whole life" — I got chills. That's the best love confession I've read in years and it isn't even a confession, it's a workaround, and it's exactly what a smart woman with no word for her relationship would engineer. She checked it for heat the way you check a package for wires. And then his hand on her shin "began two different responses and put both down before either had finished" — that detail is going to live in me. He heard it. We watched him hear it. Two breaths he'd never taken before. And what he assembled was *I love how good we are together*, which is the cruelest sentence in the English language wearing a kind face, and the book knows it and SHE half-knows it and files it in the pile.

The laundry room section is where it got me — "maybe he understood and chose to say it like that. Maybe it had gone past him entirely. She could not test it." That's the whole tragedy of this book in three sentences. She's so honest with herself up to the exact edge of what honesty would cost her. And "she had been received warmly" — I gasped. The jacket line, the title, dropped into her own mouth as the lie she tells herself. That's the author showing her hand on purpose and I'm furious and grateful at once.

Also — and I need to say this to somebody — the context makes it so much worse. She's standing on the porch with the man who *chose her with another woman*, who discusses her body with that woman on a calendar, and she's spending weeks building the bravest sentence of her life to push across a table where she's the only one who doesn't know the table exists. "He had not said the sentence back." I nearly texted my friend at the concept of a chapter that's two people under a quilt and it still hit harder than any hockey locker room scene I've read this year. This is the "two people talking" chapter I said could be the best in a book, except nobody's talking, that's the point.

No heat at all and I don't care. This is the chapter I'd reread.
