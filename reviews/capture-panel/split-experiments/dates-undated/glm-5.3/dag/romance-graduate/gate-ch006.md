**REACTION**

Two pages, and I'm sitting here a little stunned. That last line — "Your tart's holding up" — actually made me put the book down for a second. Because I know what he did there. She said *this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time, and it's froyo*, which is one of the rawest things a woman says out loud, and every man I've ever read in this genre would have turned it into a Moment — would have stopped walking, taken her hand, said something about how she *deserves* to be treated, would have explained the subtext back to her. He just walked beside her and let the true thing sit there, and then gave her back her own cup, standing. *Your tart's holding up.* Which is the same thing, said sideways, about her. God.

And the froyo detail — her hand building the cone too big and reaching for her mother's voice (*that's plenty, that's enough now*) and it not coming. I caught my breath at that, actually. This writer knows that restriction is a hundred tiny voices, and that freedom is the voice not arriving. And it's the apple slices from chapter one again — he fed Randi before he ever touched her. There's a whole theology of appetite running through this book and nobody's said it out loud once. I love a book that trusts me this much.

Also, unglamorous confession: the froyo assembly logic ("chocolate on tart is an argument the whole way down") is the exact register of my actual last crush. Specific competence about something small is apparently my entire sexuality now.

I noticed the chapter is *short*. My first two-page chapter ever, and I'm surprised by what it does — it doesn't feel thin, it feels held. But I'm on watch. A technique twice is a rhythm; three times is a habit; after that it's a book stalling on vibes. One is fine. Two better carry the same weight.

And I'm carrying dread I'm supposed to carry: Randi saying "he wants to see you naked" while grinning, Randi *took the phone out of her hand to read the text*. Everything warm in her has an operator's hands under it. Every yes freely given — the jacket told me where this goes and I still fell for Vee falling. That's the trick of it.
