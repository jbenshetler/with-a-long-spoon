REACTION — Oh, this chapter. I've been waiting for this since the drawer, and the book gave it to me with the door-kiss withheld and her hands in her pockets and I actually put the Kindle down for a second before she said "What are we doing." I've read a hundred "define the relationship" scenes and they always go one of two ways — he capitulates sweetly or he's revealed as a cad. This did neither. "Believe what I do" — the jacket literally told me that was his line and it still landed like a slap, because *he thinks it's a real answer.* That's the tragedy of him: he's not withholding to be cruel, he's withholding because in his operating system the word IS cheap, and he cannot see that what she's asking for isn't information, it's a handhold in the world. And her comeback — "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me" — that's the best line this book has written. I'd quote it to the group chat at 1 a.m.

The thing that wrecked me is the double vision I have that she doesn't. She's standing there asking "what am I to you OUT THERE" and I know — I've been in the kitchen, I've been in the room — that the true answer involves Randi and a calendar and "when she's ready," and his silence isn't just evasiveness, it's a load-bearing wall of the whole scheme. If he names what they are, the question becomes *who else knows we are it.* That's why he can't. She walked out and I was proud of her and terrified, because this is the woman who apologized with pie. The woman who manufactured "forgiven" like a warm coat. She has never once left. This is the first time she chose the cold over the warm, and the book made the cold *worse*, twice, on purpose — the shirt scene taught me what "worse" means now.

Also noticed: the two poured glasses of wine on the counter, standing there through the whole fight, going warm. That detail is doing so much work and it never gets mentioned again. And her catalog of him — "like my body comes with me attached" — that's her repeating Randi's brunch phrase back, I caught that, the book trusts me to catch that, doesn't underline it. That's the kind of writing I'm here for.
