REACTION — God, this one got me. I read the whole dance lesson with my heart climbing, because — I know this move. The whole chapter is the book's whole argument done in boots on a concrete pad: give me something to push back against. Being jelly doesn't work. Don't think, feel the lead. *That's him with her in bed, that's the entire relationship,* and the book just says it out loud through a three-step and I sat there feeling very smart and very seen. That's the good kind of subtext — it doesn't explain itself, it just puts the metaphor in your feet and lets you arrive.

And then that blonde. I hissed out loud. "Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to" — that's a scalpel, that's a woman who wanted him and didn't get him (the bartender TOLD us: wasn't him they were jealous of) going for the one soft thing Vee has, which is that she still can't quite believe she's the exception. And the horror is it works for exactly the reason Vee's mother installed: *a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them.* Her mother arriving "flat as ever" on the tail of a hug count — that landed like a bruise. This is the book I've been reading all along, the shame running on the same wire as the want, and the wire got yanked.

What killed me is that Vee's venom was real and ugly and *right for the character* — "spread her legs like a slut" is her own voice doing to herself what the blonde couldn't. And she threw the "getting girls home safe" in his face — she punished him for his decency, and the book doesn't soften the landing of that realization at the end. Three feet from Cassie, not waking her. That's the loneliest sentence in the book so far.

But — and this is where I'm almost angry at how good it is — the two thoughts she keeps BOTH. The shame AND the hand at her shoulder blade. "The lead arriving before her feet knew what to do with it." She's homesick for surrender, in the middle of being furious about it, and the book holds those together without resolving them. That's grown-up writing. That's what I'm here for.

And the last line — she's going to RANDI'S. Randi. The woman who's in love with her and sleeping with Pace. That's a bomb with a slow fuse and I know it and Vee doesn't and I actually said "no no no" out loud, which means yes yes yes.
