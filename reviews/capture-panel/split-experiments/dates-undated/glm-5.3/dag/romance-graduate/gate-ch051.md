REACTION — Oh, this one got me. The plane window, the frost, the drive where her hands know the road before her head does — and then that gut-punch of "a town that had him in it and not one inch of him for her." I actually put the book down at that line. And Randi's room: sweeping the Gstaad dresses off the bed without a glance because the wrecked girl in the doorway rates more. God. This book keeps doing the thing where an act of tenderness is also an act of concealment, and I can't catch my breath between the two.

The Sheri thing is delicious agony. Vee built the blonde out of spite in Ohio, and now she's reciting her to Randi like intelligence — and Randi, who KNOWS the woman, knows she's five feet of diner and zero threat, has to sit there and talk Vee down off a rival she could name in one syllable. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face" — that line did something to me, because Randi's protecting the scheme, sure, but she's also just... right. And the interrogation! "Do you actually believe he was using you, or is that the fear talking?" — Randi is so good at this, warm and surgical at once, and the horror of it is that all that skill is in service of the lie. She's the best friend Vee has ever had AND the reason she's in the maze.

The shoes. THE SHOES. Randi pressing those stripper heels into her hands while her fingers are still hooked in the straps — that image is going to live in my head. She's dressing Vee for Pace's door, choosing what her lover will see, getting to decide the version, and Vee reads it as pure gift. "Randi always knew the shoes." I almost couldn't. And Vee's shame flare — "it's such a slutty thing to do," the mother's voice coming up her neck — and Randi walking her down off it word by word: that was the best-written thing in the chapter, the shame and the want lighting each other, which is this book's whole thesis in miniature.

Four months of Randi being the one who leans in, and Vee took her face in both hands like she was something worth holding still — under fifty years of photographed sisters, all of them looking slightly to the left, which is such a creepy-perfect detail. And Randi afterward with the back of her hand to her own mouth, the matcha she never poured, her heart going like she'd climbed three flights. That POV switch wrecked me. And then Brooke. Cold water. "The day he thinks it's true—" and Lauren Pike, and "you need to choose." A new wall, a chapter president with ninety girls and a soft knife. Randi's been brave exactly once today and the house is already pricing it. I'm furious at Brooke and terrified she's right and I need to know what Randi does with that mug she never drank.
