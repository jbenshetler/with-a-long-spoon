**REACTION**

FINALLY. Okay. Okay okay okay.

I've waited sixty-seven chapters for this kiss and it was worth it. It wasn't worth it because it happened. It was worth it because of the held inch before it. Randi's hand in her hair and then *nothing*. Vee's eyes going over her face "as they had wanted to all autumn." Then the perfume file she'd been "quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping." That line got me, because I've had that file. Everyone has had that file. Then the press, still, "and let Vee feel it, and feel herself not pull away." Randi is *making her notice*. She's never done that before. Every goodbye kiss until now was deniable on purpose, and this one was built so it couldn't be denied, then denied anyway.

Then "I almost believed it." I put the Kindle face down on my chest. I read chapter 61, so I know what Randi said to Pace with him inside her: "I want her to know exactly what she does to me." And here she is, finally doing it, then handing it back to Vee "neat and folded" as a bit. That isn't a cover. That's terror. "She didn't speak — only looked at Vee, and drew a breath, and shook her head, blinking." She came back a beat behind Vee. She got lost in it too, and then she picked up her composure like dropped change, which is exactly what Vee did with the mushrooms. They're the same animal. I ache for both of them, and for once I'm not angry at Randi. I'm just scared for her.

And Brooke. "I chose." "I can see." In public, with an arm around Vee's waist that hadn't been there a second before. That's the answer to "You need to choose." Randi answered the ultimatum to the president's face, and Vee heard a girl being asked about her plus-one. Then the rest came "a beat late": "As my date." God. Brooke's little speech was a threat delivered as a memory ("our Randi"), and Vee thought she was being *kind*. That dramatic irony was delicious and it hurt. Also, the Chambord through the trash-can punch is the best character note Brooke has gotten. She spends a fortune so you'll think she didn't spend a dime. That's the whole house.

The Love bracelet, "taking it off requires tools." A little on the nose, sure. But I've been carrying "the bracelet's cold line under Vee's hand" since forever, and now I know what it's called. Randi locked into something. Into whom? I have suspicions.

The heat worked for me in a way the painting chapter only half did. The skirt climbing, Randi holding her wrists up so she *can't* fix the hem, "found out twice over: once to the room, and once, worse, to herself." That's the book's real kink and it's Vee's: being made to show, with someone else's hands taking the choice away. It's the same grammar as the rail and Sondra's palm. Here it's Randi's hands, and that's the turn. I love that Vee's mind "decided not to check," though the book did lean in and explain that to me a couple of times. The shiver at the ear, "before she could rule on whether she'd allowed it." Yes.

The quibbles, quickly. The foyer inventory ran long: the braid "inlaid," "machined," the jewelry "agreed with her." I get that it's Vee's gaze lingering, and that's the point, but I skimmed it. Two near-identical tall boys with beers was one too many. The first one did the "she's here with me" beat and Harrison did the show beat, and they could have been one boy. And "it was exactly the mouth a boy wanted to kiss" is lovely as Vee's self-deception, but I felt the author nudging me.

1 a.m. text to my friend: "THEY KISSED. ON THE DANCE FLOOR. AND RANDI SAID 'I ALMOST BELIEVED IT' AND I WANT TO DIE. also she told the sorority president 'I chose' and vee thinks it was about a plus-one."

Vee ends wanting it again "so badly her hands were not quite steady," with Pace tomorrow. Is she going to taste Randi on his mouth? Is she going to *tell* Randi any of this? She's keeping the painting photo from her already. The secrets are now running in every direction and I'm here for all of it.
