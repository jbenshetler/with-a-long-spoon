**REACTION**

I thought I'd been building the blonde for a month along with Vee, and I hadn't. I was building her too, and I'd stocked her with a whole Valentine's-weekend war. Then a five-foot woman with crooked makeup and a bad perm got off a stool and said "You're late" loud enough for the whole room to hear, and the blonde "failed to find anywhere to stand." I laughed out loud right when Vee did. That's rare for me. Usually I see a jealousy fakeout coming a chapter off. This one I saw coming and it still got me, because the book didn't treat the reveal as the point. The point was that "the heat in Vee's chest stayed exactly where it was. It had never needed the blonde." Then the hug, which "wasn't the kind you gave a lab partner." The jealousy didn't go away when its object turned out to be wrong. It had nowhere left to go. That's real.

Then the look. Sheri's slow trip down from the keyhole, the leg, stopping at the shoes with an "oh" she was saving for later, and Vee realizing it was *enjoying* her. Coming straight after ch61, where Randi said "I want her to know exactly what she does to me" with her hand on her own breast, this felt like the book giving Vee a rehearsal. A woman looked at her openly, wanted her a little in public, and nothing terrible happened. She laughed and a month went out of her ribs. And the shoes she was standing in were Randi's induction heels. Of course they were. Those heels keep pulling home.

"And you get to motorboat them things?" Pace blushing up into his ears is my favorite thing he's done in the whole series. I needed him to be embarrassable. I've been grumbling that the book is in love with him, and here it actually let him be a person someone else has a license with.

The coming-out story hit hard. "Whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish." "I'm not crying about it now, so don't you start either." Sheri has a voice. She's somebody, not a device. But I'm noting that it's another testimonial. Every new character arrives with a speech about how Pace is the one good man in the hall. "Hands stay where the dance puts them. Every time." It's lovely, and it's also the fourth or fifth witness to his sainthood. The new part, the part I'll carry, is "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen." That finally gives his rooms full of precise care a history. It's the first crack of interiority he's had that isn't Vee's reading of him.

Now the suspicious part. "We cycle." "She dances." He picked those facts, and he handed over the one with the blade in it "like a dried spoon." Did he know? A man who remembers which boot her heel slipped out of in October doesn't hand over fragments by accident. I can't tell whether he's teasing out her jealousy on purpose, the way Randi teases out her confessions, or whether he's really just that oblivious at a sink. After ch61, I believe the two of them run games. And "Bet those legs feel like Christmas," back in December, means Sheri has known about Vee and been told things for a while. Everybody knows more than Vee does. Again.

Small gripes. Ch61 left me on a heavy beat, "She's not the only one in love," with Pace unable to deny it, and this chapter didn't go anywhere near it. It's back in Vee's head as if that night never happened. I understand the structure, two POVs and dramatic irony, but I wanted at least one flicker of it in Pace's face. And "It read as affection. It worked as ballast. With him… those had never been two different things" is the book explaining itself a little. A few metaphors are trying too hard ("the heels met it like a rumor").

But this is exactly what I asked for in my notes: a new event, three people in a room, no retelling, no Vee alone in a vehicle. The headlights held on Sheri's door until the porch light came on. "Bacon cheeseburgers," to the window. That made me soft.
