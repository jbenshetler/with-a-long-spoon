**REACTION**

Two pages and I was done. My thumb went to turn and there was nothing. So that's what a chapter this short does: it hands you one thing and stops. I can't decide yet whether that's confident or lazy. Here I'll call it confident, mostly because of what the one thing was.

The thing is "Listen to you." He tells Sheri about Vee running her thumb down the grain of the walnut chair, then the math book she pulled off the shelf and didn't put back, and he can't stop. Sheri has to point out that he's gushing, and "He didn't know what there was to listen to." That's the man I've been watching for twenty-seven chapters. He'll sit up all night building a dress to fit her body, and he still can't hear himself falling. He told Randi Vee "makes the room brighter" and thought he was just reporting. He still thinks so. The book doesn't explain it to me, it just lets someone else catch him at it. I liked that a lot.

I liked Sheri instantly. She's tiny, she beats him up every hill, and she comments on women like the weather. "Hell of a backside on her, though." She's also the first person outside the three of them who gets the whole picture from his side, and she gets it from him, casually, at the top of a hill. "She know about the redhead?" "She knows. She likes her." Likes her, Pace? I've watched that woman go after a glass rim with her thumbnail at three different tables. Then "And the redhead?" "Not yet." Those two words gave me a chill. *Not yet*, like a plan with a date on it. Of everything in this chapter, that's the part I'd text someone at 1 a.m.

The useful news is that Sheri watched Vee all night. Vee danced with nobody, nursed one drink, and left alone. Now he knows she was loyal even while she was furious. That's a small kindness to slip him before tomorrow night.

My gripe: "Two of them at once. How do you sleep? … If that's not already complicated, it will be." That's the jacket copy coming out of a supporting character's mouth. I already know. I've known since page one. It's not a full lecture on the subtext, but it leans that way, and I felt the book nudging me.

Mostly, though, this is the book making me wait. Randi said tomorrow night. The cognac shoes, the scarf Vee has to tie herself, the black dress with the neckline down. And here's a Saturday morning bike ride, then a cut. I don't resent it, because it's short, and short is the only acceptable way to stall me. If chapter 29 isn't Vee at that door, I'll get cranky.
