**REACTION**

I almost skimmed the first half, and I want to be honest about that. Cassie's recap is the third time the book has handed me a scene I already read, after Cassie in "Rye" and the girls in "What to Wear." When Vee said "And the house was warm," I felt my thumb twitch. I KNOW about the thermostat. I watched him think of it. Then I got it again at lunch. The book is starting to retell itself the way Vee does, and that's a pattern I'm going to start counting.

The Cassie part still paid me two things I didn't have. The first is "Like I was supposed to be there." That isn't a recap. That's Vee finding out who she is out loud, and Cassie's hand on her knee, once, taken back, got me. The second is "*I* was grinding on *him*." I laughed, because chapter 16 played that bend so deniably that I'd half-missed how bold she was being. The "are you sure he's not gay" bit, though, is a joke I've read in forty books. Cassie deserves better lines than that one.

Then Randi got in the car and the book woke up.

That lunch is the best scene since the bench. On the surface it's two girls in a velvet booth. Underneath, it's an interrogation dressed as a girlfriend gossip session, and I watched Randi run it like a pro. First she feeds Vee the bondage story, wrists tied, helpless, "the letting go," to see what Vee's face does. Then "Nobody measures over a bra," normalizing it, taking the shame off so the next thing can come out. Then "Which hip?" "The left one." "All the best ones start at the left hip." I actually said "oh my GOD" out loud, because that's a woman walking a girl down a staircase one step at a time. And Vee catching herself with "It was cold in the house," two minutes after telling Randi he'd turned the heat up, was delicious. Randi's "Cold. Hmm." Caught.

But the moment that made me sit up was Randi's thumbnail under the rim of the glass, bearing down while Vee talks about being looked at. Vee reads it as "she's moved, she's happy for me," and I wanted to shake her. Then at the exact moment Vee is about to give up the wet-satin secret, the deepest thing she owns, Randi says "You don't have to tell me." She engineered the whole confession and then couldn't stand to hear the end of it. She shut that door because hearing it from Vee, about Pace, would break something in her. And then she asks for the check. That's Randi's crack showing, and the book didn't explain it to me. It trusted me. Thank you.

Now I'm suspicious of the tickling story. Saturday? Pace was with Vee Saturday afternoon. So either the "beautiful idiot" is invented bait, like "Paul?" and the lacrosse date, or there really was a guy and Randi is spending herself somewhere because Pace's attention is pointed at a redhead. Either way she's testing whether Vee likes the idea of being helpless. With a Hermès scarf. And the bench is in that bedroom behind the one closed door.

Then the kiss. Hand at the hinge of the jaw, the first press, the pause, then back with *more*. That second press is what got me, and it had more charge for me than half the explicit scenes I've read this year. Vee explaining it away as "French," then scanning the dorm windows in cold panic: yes. Brooke. The tall one. Someone saw that, I'd bet money on it. And "See you soon, gorgeous" after Randi spent the whole lunch steering Vee toward Pace's knees. She's not doing this for Pace anymore. I don't think she's done anything for Pace since the smoothie place.

Also: I loved Vee reading the right side of the menu first. The soup, the debit card, Thursday's balance, tipping well so she's not the broke girl. That's interiority I'd never get from a hockey book.

The 1 a.m. text would be: *the friend just kissed her on the mouth in a fire lane after pumping her for details about the boyfriend's knees and I'm not okay.*
