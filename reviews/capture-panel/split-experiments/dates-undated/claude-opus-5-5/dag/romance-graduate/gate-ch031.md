**REACTION**

I've been waiting for this walk since chapter 21, and the book made me earn it. I liked that.

The opening is quiet in a good way. Cassie says "Go make your mother cry again" and takes the phone. Then Vee admits she's a little relieved her mom isn't there, and Cassie's kind answer isn't the true one. Vee goes looking for the real reason, can't put it into words, and leaves it. A few paragraphs later it shows up by itself: *nothing at all under the silk, and nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of.* That line got me. This chapter isn't about the dress. It's about who's watching, and for once it's nobody from Ohio, no Pace, and no Randi. That last one hit me late. Randi found these shoes and knelt to buckle them, and she isn't in the room. Nobody's directing this. For the first time in the whole book, Vee's heat isn't being placed by anyone. It's hers.

The walk itself is so good. First a small, safe step, then "She took a breath, and made the second step long." My stomach dropped with her. It's a full exhibitionist scene with nobody touching her, and it's hotter than half the explicit scenes on my Kindle: the room waking up, her face held still, the heat "agreeing" half a second before her head does. The fantasy she had in ch21 was the late entrance with the room turning. The book took that away from her (the room was "warm and dull and kind") and made her *choose* it on the stage instead. That's a better version of her fantasy, and she's the one who wrote it.

Cassie is the one who's there, of course. She takes the photo, tears up and would deny it to her grave, and offers a terrible sandwich. My clean heart. I wish Vee had seen her a little more clearly. She got the frame from Cassie and sent it straight to Pace.

The photo is where my suspicion kicks in. "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." Oh honey. Nothing in this book stays yours. Saturday is coming and you'll tell Randi, and Randi will tell him. And Pace is in Berkeley for THREE DAYS. Randi has Vee all to herself, nobody at the cabin, nobody to report to in person. That's what makes me want to turn the page.

Small gripes. The last sentence, "whether that was pride or only the same heat... the two came to the same thing," is the book saying its theme out loud. The walk had already shown me that, so I didn't need it said. Also, a murmur going through an honor society's crowd is a little too wish-fulfillment. I let it go because she earned the indulgence.
