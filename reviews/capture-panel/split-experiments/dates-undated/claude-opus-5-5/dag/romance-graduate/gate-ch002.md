**REACTION**

Okay, so I'm still a little rattled by chapter one. The jacket told me this was Vee's book, so I spent that whole bench scene assuming the girl was Vee. Then he says "There you are, Randi" and I actually said "oh" out loud. So the girl on the bench, the sorority girl with the apple, is the glamorous friend, the one who "steers" Vee later. That changes how I read this chapter. I'm not watching a side character. I'm watching the woman who's going to run the game, the morning after she got caught off guard.

And the morning after is nasty in a good way. The common room scene is the best part. Brooke doesn't stand up, and the not-standing is the power move. "Engineering, isn't it," pushing him down a notch, then "Math. PhD," and the smile takes it in without giving the notch back. "Short-cute." Sloane's "I love that you don't care about cars," which means *we've clocked the pickup.* I know these women. I've been interrogated by a Brooke over brunch. It's four minutes of smiling and it's more frightening than half the "villains" on my Kindle. The detail that got me most is Randi's laugh matching Kinsey's pitch "very fast," before she'd had time to choose it. That's a heroine with an inner life, and the inner life is a machine running on autopilot.

Now I'm suspicious. "Not his section." She hesitated longer there than anywhere else. Is that a lie? Is Pace TAing her math class? If so, that's a real problem, not a one-unsent-text problem, and I want to know.

What wore on me: the empty-room stretch. She walks to the vanity, doesn't sit; walks to the bed, doesn't sit; opens the closet, closes it. I get it, she's in shock. But "The duvet was light and synthetic and adequate. It was not — it was a duvet" is the book nudging me to compare it to his cashmere blanket, when I'd already made that connection myself. Same with "fraction of a second" and "smile arranged" coming back so often they turn into a tic. The ending, though: "the not-going did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." That's good. Put it next to Pace in chapter one, sitting outside a closed door he swore he'd never open, and it hurts. He respects her doors, and she's built herself so there's no door to knock on.

"He's hard to say no to," heard "in a different key." Yeah. That one landed and I'm not sure where.

No heat at all this chapter, and that's fine. It's the bridge after a scene that earned one. But it's quiet chapter number one, I still haven't met Vee, and I'll be counting.
