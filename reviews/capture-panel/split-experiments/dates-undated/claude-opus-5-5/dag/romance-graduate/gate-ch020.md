Oh, this one hurt. Mostly in the good way.

The small bowl got me first: "the one he sliced her apples into." Twenty chapters later and the apple from the bench comes back as a detail he doesn't think twice about. He made her a cream stew. A cream stew, for the woman with the egg-white mother who polices every bite. He meant it as love, and it's maybe the cruelest thing he could have put in front of her. She drew one line through the top of the sauce and never touched it again. Then she ate the whole salad, down to the leaves soaked in dressing, with a second glass she poured herself. I knew exactly what I was watching, and the book never once told me. After all my eye-rolling about it underlining its own subtext, it let the bowl do the work. Thank you.

The "Tell me" thing is what wrecked me. Every night she's said it at his ear, and tonight it doesn't come, and he lies there waiting for it. In chapter 13 he was feeding her Vee's gardenia and chlorine. Tonight she doesn't want any of it from him, because she got it straight from Vee this morning over mimosas. Then the thumbnail pressing into the rim of the glass, the same gesture as at the lunch when Vee said "he looked at me and I was fine." Same tell, two different tables. I clocked it and felt smug and sick at the same time.

Pace. God. "How do I get her from where she is to us, on your schedule?" Hearing him say "to us" out loud, after he spent a whole chapter on his knees at the riser, made my stomach drop. He's so decent and he's so in on it. "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything." "It was the want, with an edge on it tonight." No, baby. Then he covers her bowl with a plate and puts it in the fridge for her. That small gesture nearly made me cry. He's lost and he's tender, and he's going to keep cooking for a woman who can't eat it.
