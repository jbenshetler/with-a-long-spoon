**REACTION**

Oh, Vee. She walked out.

I'll admit I'd started bracing coming into this one. Chapters 44, 45 and 46 were all the same ache in three costumes: the cracker sentence that got "I love how good we are together" back, the Chili's fight, the shirt in the cold dorm. "Not the big one and not the small one" showed up twice in two chapters, and I was starting to hear the book circling. The first paragraph here nearly confirmed it, with "holding a smell in the dark that told her nothing." Yes, I was there, I read it yesterday. My thumb twitched. Then she kept her coat buttoned to the throat and her hands in her pockets, and she didn't lift her mouth for the door kiss. She let him walk up to that and watched him not get it. That got me sitting up. Four chapters of her swallowing the word, and in the first page of this one she does something with her body instead.

The speech is long, and I didn't mind, because it's the most adult thing she's said in the whole book. She doesn't accuse him of anything. She lists everything he gives her, honestly and almost gratefully: the heat turned up before she arrives, the poured wine, the food, "Like my body comes with me attached." Then she points at the door. "Out there." Then the line that made me put my hand over my mouth: "What do I tell my mother, that he feeds me and we have a lot of sex?" With no name on it, the best thing in her life comes out of her mouth sounding cheap. That's the whole wound, said plainly by a 21-year-old.

And then "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me." I'd text my friend that line at 1 a.m. with no context and nothing else.

What scares me is Pace. "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?" That isn't an answer, it's a proof. He's arguing the conclusion and skipping her question, and it's so very him. "You're the most important person in my life" sounds lovely until you remember Monday night, Randi in dark-red silk, and "extraordinary." Is that sentence true? Has he said it to Randi too? Then the arms fold and he holds "a line at cost," and I know what the line is, even though Vee doesn't. He can't call her his girlfriend because he has one. Or there's a third thing: the noise at Chili's, the fork squaring, "I don't go back." The book has made that ambiguous on purpose, and I can't tell how much is Randi and how much is just how he's made. I like not knowing. It means he's still a person and not a plot device.

I got a little tired of the shape of a couple of lines. "Not the hand in place of the word" leaned toward explaining the subtext to me. I'd already felt it from his hand lifting. But it was short, and the exit was clean: "Vee," once, "and her name was not the word she'd come for." She doesn't go back in.

What I really want to know now is Saturday. She'll take this to Randi, all of it, the crying and the door, and Randi will carry it straight to his couch. In chapter 41 Vee saw MIRANDA on that screen and protected Randi without a word. She's the one person in this triangle who keeps a friend's secret out of pure kindness, and she's the one being kept from. I haven't seen Randi properly in six chapters, I miss her, and I'm terrified of that brunch.

No sex, no heat beyond her own list of what she wants, and I didn't need any. This was the fight the jacket promised, and it landed.
