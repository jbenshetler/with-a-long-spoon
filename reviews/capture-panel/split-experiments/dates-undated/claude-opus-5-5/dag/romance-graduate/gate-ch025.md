**REACTION**

Okay. The concrete pad under the field lights was so good. Pace in creased jeans and a short-sleeved shirt in October because he dressed for the dancing and not the weather, and a boom box with electrical tape on the handle. Then "Give me something to push back against… Being jelly doesn't work." I sat up at that. It's the first time he's asked her to *push* instead of just giving her the choice. Every step being hers has been the whole song so far, and here he's telling her that leading needs her resistance, her weight in the frame. Then "There's no sorry unless you take off a foot," and the moment it gives and she stops guessing. "She said nothing. She was busy." The heat was all clothed, his hand at her shoulder blade, and it worked on me more than I expected.

"The space you're keeping, that's what gets you stepped on" was a little bit the book handing me its thesis. I noticed it. It got a pass because it's also true about dancing.

Then the bar, and I braced hard. A jealous blonde with too much makeup drops one poison line, the heroine snaps, and the hero walks out. That's the exact manufactured misunderstanding I've DNF'd forty books over. The wise older bartender explaining it all at closing time ("Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey") is a device I've seen used *so* many times. It's tidy. Too tidy.

What saved it: Vee watches herself do it. "She heard it the way you hear a glass go past the point where you can still catch it." "She knew what she was doing, and she did it anyway." Her mother's voice is right there before anyone says a word: *a man like that has girls*. She was primed, so the blonde wasn't the cause. And "three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut" hurt to read because it's the floozy voice, and this time it's turned against her instead of feeding her want. That's grown-up. It isn't a contrived plot glitch. It's her shame.

I'm a little mad at Pace, though. This is a man who drives drunk strangers home, and he leaves his own date at a roadhouse at midnight, puts the beer down, and walks out? I get it. She said "I'll handle that myself" and he respects every step being hers, even this one. That's his rule turning cold on him, and it might be the point. But no text all night? She paid for a disgusting cab with money she doesn't have, on top of the credit card from the shoes. The money keeps piling up, and I like that the book keeps counting it.

The ending, though. *Go to Randi's.* Oh no. Oh, YES. She's going to take this fight, her whole shame and her "he chose me," to the one person who's sleeping with him and who's already in love with her. Randi gets to comfort her. Randi gets to decide what to give back. That's what I'm turning the page for. And she didn't wake Cassie again. Another sentence of Cassie let go.

The washed flannel, "fabric softener and no one." Ugh. That one landed.
