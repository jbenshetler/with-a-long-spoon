**REACTION**

Okay. I came in braced. Chapter 20 left me holding Randi rolled up in a blanket with her back to him, that one line drawn across the cream sauce, and I wanted the book to stay in that room. Instead it handed me the happiest chapter so far. My first reaction was a little sour: wait, where is she? Is she just *off* now? By the second page I'd stopped minding. I'd been taken, which is a different thing from being distracted.

The opening paragraph wobbled me. "There'd been one night before this... One Friday night wasn't enough to have him figured." I *know*. I was there. For a paragraph it read like the book had forgotten I'd been in the room for the fra diavolo. Then the hello-after landed: neither of them knows how to do it yet, the careful peck that's a question, the hug that swings her off the step. That's a real thing nobody writes. The awkward second date after the first night. I forgave the paragraph.

The dress reveal got me. It wasn't the silk-and-light description, though that was lovely and didn't show off. It was the line about him taking the trouble and then taking the further trouble to hide the trouble. That's the most romantic idea in this book, and it's the most *Pace* idea: the outlined tools, the moleskin, the error-correcting codes. And "Yes," he said, and didn't make it smaller. I'd marry that sentence. The mirror with the screw heads still bright. The man got up and hung a mirror. Same with the lining "set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away."

Then the slit and the underwear. She takes them off herself, on purpose, to be looked at, and the floozy voice goes off and she *lets it come*. That's the arc of her through this whole book in one gesture, and the book did not explain it to me. Thank god. Also it's a callback to "Trade" that I felt in my stomach. Last time she gave them to him. This time she just leaves them on the chair.

The sex is the best in the book, and I mean that as someone with a Kindle full of standing sex that's physically impossible. This one admits it's nearly impossible, and the comedy is the *point*. "Ow. Don't point down." "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me." Him twitching in her hands and her saying it again slower to make him do it again. I laughed out loud. Drying her hands down his flanks while his arms are full of silk. "Are they clean enough?" and then the fingers in his mouth, which went through MY knees too. Funny and filthy in the same breath is the rarest thing in the genre. Then the feet on his chest holding him off, and him stopping where she put him. Every time this book does consent, it does it as heat and not paperwork. "You're enjoying this." "Very much." Yes, sir.

What I'm suspicious of is the ending. "Getting away with something." The dress going dark in its window. I'm reading her daydreaming about the induction, the room turning, "it just felt like what she was for," and all I can think is: Randi. Randi in the room. Randi seeing the dress Pace made, on the girl. And the shoes. The book made a whole beat about her not owning the shoes. Someone is going to buy her those shoes, and I'd bet my Kindle it's Randi, and that it'll be a weapon or a love letter or both. Also, does Pace tell Randi about today? Does he "keep" this one the way he thinks he kept the truck?
