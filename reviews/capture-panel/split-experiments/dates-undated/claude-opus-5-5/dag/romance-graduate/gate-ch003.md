**REACTION**

Okay. The book has been holding the woman on the cover out of reach for three chapters, and she just walked out of a dining hall laughing with her head thrown back. And I met her from the outside, through *them*, pointed at like a menu item. I knew from the jacket that this is how it starts. Seeing it happen still made my stomach drop. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Oh no. Oh, *yes*. I'm texting someone.

The opening did exactly what I'd been afraid the book might not do after chapter 2 left Randi in last night's clothes on her bed. It went straight back into his bed, and it's hot, actually explicit and not coy. Then the question lands and her body comes before her mind can catch up. That is such a smart way to write a want she can't admit. And then: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." I read that line twice. It's the whole book's problem in one sentence, and it's also the reason I trust Pace more than I trust most romance heroes. Still, "He had decided this beforehand" is sitting in me like a splinter. He's courtly, he asks, he waits. He also set the whole moment up so she'd be at her most open when he asked. Both things are true, and the book knows they're both true. That's what I keep coming back for.

Randi's deflection, "Only in a threesome. With you.", is devastating, because I watched her decide in that bathroom mirror that nobody gets her again. So now her wanting has to come dressed as a gift for her boyfriend. Pace sees through it and lets it stand. Then the cheeseburger: "I can't eat that." And the last line is "Let's go feed you." That pairing got me right in the chest. It's tender and it's ominous at the same time.

The pointing game itself made me squirm, and I liked squirming. It's gross in the way real desire can be: two pretty people grading strangers on the quad, and she's literally the Standards chair. That's her day job. Watching her move from offended manners to "a very good sport" to actually shopping was great. So was the bare "Yes," with nothing around it, before the kitten voice came back to cover it. The bump-into-her move was a little chilling. The redhead apologizes for a collision Randi engineered. That's the jacket's "as if by accident," and now I've watched it happen and I'm complicit.

Where it sagged for me: the campus establishing paragraph. The colonnade "composed two centuries ago by men who wanted the eye to travel exactly this way" is the book admiring itself for a second. Then there's a parade of girl descriptions. By the sage-and-lavender pair I was skimming the athleisure inventory. The "follow before the lead" dance metaphor showed up twice, and once was enough. And the threesome line got explained to me ("unsurvivable… merely sexy"). It was sharp enough that I forgave it, but I noticed.

What I'm suspicious of: whether Randi wants Vee for herself or as the safe costume for a want she won't let herself have. And Pace, who keeps getting to stand at the light pole and watch.
