**REACTION**

Okay, I'm smiling. I didn't expect this book to give me a chapter that's just *happy*, and I'm a little suspicious of how much I liked it.

What got me is that this is the induction walk with an audience of one, and it means the opposite thing. On stage she was giving her leg to strangers who "could not have said so out loud." Here the one person who *can* say so has to sit in a recliner because she put her hand out flat, and he obeys. "She watched him make himself do it." That's the power flipped from Friday. Friday he set the terms, the bet, "Slow. For me," the bed made ready like an altar. Tonight she sets every rule: no guessing, no helping, wait in the chair, pick one. She learned it from him and handed it straight back. That's real emotional intelligence, and the book doesn't stop to explain it to me.

His hand coming partway up and being put away, the "putting-away cost him something," was hotter than half the sex scenes on my Kindle. And "knowing that, and going anyway, was the entire costume" is the line I'd text at 1 a.m. That's Vee. The blush running down past where the mirror ends, and she doesn't cover one inch.

His boots, too big, the clomp across the gravel, the bag carried "like something sleeping." The lamp clicked one notch brighter without a word. I love him in these small moments. "Take your time," then nothing, no footsteps. He's a man who knows how to leave a woman alone with a mirror.

Things that rubbed me wrong:
- "Sunrise and sunset" is a greeting card. Vee gave him an eyebrow and so did I.
- Then he lands it anyway: "The other is for the world. This is just for me." That's sweet, but I'm reading it against the jacket. He gives her everything but a word, and this is another *beautiful* sentence that isn't the word. "Just for me" isn't "you're mine to the world." I noticed.
- It's three chapters in a row of Vee showing her body: the stage, the flash in the den, now the walk. I love the arc from hiding to giving, but if chapter 34 is a fourth display I'll start feeling the pattern.
- Ending on the looking, before any touching, is fine. That's restraint, not a fade. But it had better not skip what comes next the way the apology Saturday got skipped.

What has me itching: Randi has been absent for three chapters, and her fingerprints are all over this one. "The dear black straps Randi had knelt to buckle." If this is Friday the 31st, tomorrow is Saturday, and Saturday is brunch. Vee's going to sit across that table with a bottom that still remembers his hand, and Randi will want "every detail, slowly." Friday night the bed had "no Randi to be told." Tomorrow there will be. That's the chapter I want.
