REACTION

Oh my god, this chapter gutted me in twelve different directions at once.

First of all, the quiet domestic tenderness of that opening—waking up hungover to the glass of ice water on a folded square of paper towel, realizing he held a glass of water for her in the bathroom, put his soft worn white undershirt on her, and kissed her forehead in the dark after he thought she was out. And then he baked cinnamon raisin bread from scratch while she slept off three ciders! That alone would have melted me.

Wait until we talk about the dramatic irony that made me physically wince over my coffee: *"Sheri's my friend... My one friend. There isn't anyone else."* AND THEN: *"Women misunderstand it. The friendship. It gets awkward."* And Vee concluding in her head that his one friend on this earth is a five-foot queer woman who could never want him—*"the only kind, she was sure now, the world had ever quite let him keep"*—while WE literally just read Chapter 61 where he and Randi were fucking each other's brains out and talking about how much they both love Vee!! Excuse me?! Is Randi not his friend, or did he just erase her from the board to keep the lie intact, or does he not count Randi as a "friend" because of what they actually are—collaborators, lovers, coconspirators? When he stood there at the counter looking out over Vee's head at the end, *"somewhere else with it, thinking, the way he got,"* while Vee held him with her whole weight because she was grieving how lonely he was after Daphne left (*"No Randi to bring it to"*—GIRL, STOP, YOU ARE KILLING ME), I wanted to scream. You know what he's thinking about over the top of her head! He's thinking about the massive, terrifying lie sitting right in the middle of that kitchen, or about Randi!

And yet—here is why this book has me by the throat—everything he told her about why he doesn't have male friends was *also* completely, devastatingly true. The way he broke down the math of being fifteen in a building full of grown men (*"off the end of every scale they measured on... And the ones already there, when I was small among them, either weren't safe or weren't welcoming"*), and then being eighteen with a patent and a house when the freshmen finally showed up, and how friendships are built on ordinary doorway questions (*"What are you doing for Christmas? Your folks coming up?"*) that all have a hole underneath them for him. When Vee put together that the reason he graduated four years early was because he never went home for the summers after sixteen, and pictured him alone on an empty campus at Thanksgiving teaching himself to cook because there was no dining hall... my chest physically ached. And her trying to hold the reality of Daphne—*"the ugly grown-up word that kept forming under her tongue and that she kept pressing back down"*—alongside how he holds it as the best two years of his life, realizing that when Daphne left, he had nobody at all.

Also, Vee realizing she can't wait until Tuesday to tell Randi about Sheri—and saving for last the way Sheri looked down her body in the taproom (*"She liked me... I liked it, honestly. Best thing that's happened to my legs in a Virginia January"*) and how Pace clocked it immediately without a flicker (*"That way too. She can be like that"*). Oh, Tuesday night with Randi is going to be electric.
