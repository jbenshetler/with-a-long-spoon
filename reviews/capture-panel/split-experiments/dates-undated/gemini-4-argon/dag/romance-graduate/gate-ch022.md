REACTION

Oh, my god. I have chills all the way down my arms and a pit in my stomach the size of a crater. This chapter didn't have a single sex scene in it and I was holding my breath like I was watching someone walk a tightrope over a canyon.

First of all, the contrast between Cassie and Randi in that stats lecture hall is just surgical. Cassie doesn't give a shit about the dress, wears the same fleece for two winters, and spends forty seconds just looking at Vee's face to make sure her friend is actually okay ("Was it a good night?"). And meanwhile Randi has her pen capped and "nothing at all on her face" before she snaps the mask back on and calls her a lucky slut. Even the stats lecture itself—Simpson's paradox! Each one on its own looks like one thing, *put the two together and the whole truth flips without touching a single number*. I see what you're doing, book, and it wasn't even heavy-handed because I was too busy watching the two girls flanking Vee.

And then the dorm room. Watching Randi walk into Vee's messy, normal, milk-crate-and-Stevie-Nicks college life and then watching Randi touch that silk on Vee's body... I practically stopped breathing when Vee said, *"He was up all night finishing it. He fell asleep after. I watched him sleep."* Because we know what happened the last time Randi was at Pace's house! She refused to eat his stew, rode him in the dark with her eyes shut, and walked out into the night, and now here is Vee glowing in a hand-sewn silk gown talking about watching him sleep while Randi's hand is frozen on her bare shoulder. And Vee—sweet, generous, blind Vee—clocks that Randi goes quiet behind her face, clocks that it's jealousy, and thinks Randi is jealous *because Randi doesn't have a man like Pace who stays up all night for her!* I wanted to scream.

Then we get to the shoe shopping, which is so much fun in the rack store (the yeti snow-tire shoe, the pink rhinestone stripper heels, Randi actually folding over the bench laughing for real—which makes it worse, because Randi *does* genuinely adore her!), right up until Randi steers her out of her tax bracket and into the boutique.

Let's talk about two things in that boutique that nearly ended me:
1. Randi dropping to one knee on the floor in her expensive clothes, taking Vee's bare foot in both hands, looking at the frosted plum toenails *she already knows about from brunch*, murmuring *"Mm,"* and stroking her thumb once across Vee's big toe until the whole room tips and Vee has to grab her shoulder so she doesn't fall off the bench. That single thumb-stroke on a painted toenail had more erotic charge than entire chapters of most KU books I've read this year.
2. The credit card. Oh, this made me sick with anxiety in the best, most awful way. Randi is casually reading Vee's secret daydream back to her (*"It wants to walk in late and stop the room cold"*), calling Pace her *lover*, getting Vee drunk on this fantasy of who she gets to be, and Vee is too proud and too seduced by Randi's world to say "I can't afford this." So she puts away her debit card and puts a pair of luxury boutique heels on her emergency gas credit card with *nothing* behind it to pay the bill next month—while Randi doesn't even offer to buy them or loan her a pair (they're the exact same size seven! Randi just said "we can share, it's practically a marriage" ten minutes ago!), and instead drops *multiples* of that price on random everyday loafers right next to her without even looking at the receipt so Vee won't feel like a charity case, or maybe because rich people genuinely don't feel the ground drop out!

That closing line—*"And Vee thought, as she had been thinking lately and could not stop, that she had never once in her life had a friend like this"*—while Vee is clutching an unaffordable shoebox to her chest like a little kid and Randi is carrying the dress bag on her arm... it's a masterpiece of dramatic irony. I am terrified for Vee's bank account, her heart, and her sanity, and I need to see this induction dinner immediately.
