REACTION

Oh, Vee, *no*. I was reading the bar scene with my hand physically over my mouth, wanting to reach into the page and stop her before she opened her mouth, even though every single beat of why she did it made devastating, gut-punch sense.

Look at the setup: first we get that gorgeous, patient dance lesson on the concrete pad under the field lights with the taped-up boom box. The way Pace teaches her to dance—*“You have a job here. Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead... Being jelly doesn't work”* and *“That's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it — come all the way in”*—is so hot and so completely *him*. And when it clicks for her, when she stops guessing and lets the decision be pulled from her through his frame, you can feel why she’s falling so hard it terrifies her. She even catches herself thinking on the dance floor: *how much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join.*

She’s already waiting for the bill to come due because she thinks this is too good to be true. And then her mother’s voice slips in (*a man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them*), and that venomous little bar-rail blonde drops poison right into the exact open wound Vee has been carrying her whole life. *“All us girls love him. He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night.”*

In a normal romance novel, a misunderstanding like this annoys the hell out of me because it feels fake—like the author just needed a third-act breakup. Here, it didn't feel fake for a second. It felt awful and earned because we've lived inside Vee's head and heard her mother's voice (*three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut; Floozies*). And notice what Pace does! He doesn't chase her across the bar and make a big alpha scene. He answers her straight, and when she tells him directly, *“I don't need you to get me home safely. I'll handle that myself,”* he takes her at her word and leaves. Because *of course* he does! His whole religion is never pushing past a woman's no!

And the bartender setting her straight at closing time—*“Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey”*—followed by that horrible, expensive cab ride and putting on his washed flannel shirt that doesn't even smell like him anymore... my heart was in my throat. She realizes what she did before the sun even comes up. She doesn't let it fester for three chapters. *“The embarrassment was still there but it had changed temperature overnight, and cool embarrassment was just information. She'd been wrong. She was going to fix it.”* God, I love a heroine with a spine.

Except—WAIT A SECOND. Look at that last paragraph: *When it was light she knew what she was going to do... which was: go to Randi's.*

She didn't wake Cassie three feet away. She's going straight to *Randi's* at dawn on a Friday morning to figure out how to fix things with Pace?! My pulse just jumped into my throat. Randi is the one who has been aching to get her hands on this schedule, and now a heartsick, humbled, sleep-deprived Vee is about to walk right up to her door asking for help with Pace. Turn the page right now.
