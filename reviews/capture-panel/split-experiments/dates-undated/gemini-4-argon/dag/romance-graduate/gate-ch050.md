REACTION

Oh my god. Oh my *god*. I have tears in my eyes.

For forty-nine chapters, Pace has been this steady, courtly, unshakable monolith of a man—the guy who turns the heat up hours before she gets there, who makes pizza from scratch for an empty house, who never loses his temper and never misses a step. And now we finally get inside his head when Vee isn't there, and he is getting blackout, puking-in-the-bathroom drunk on a dusty bottle of whiskey in the dark, sleeping under *Randi's* cashmere blanket, boiling unseasoned bags of frozen chicken and broccoli like "prison food" (so he does NOT make gourmet meals for an empty house—he only cooks like that for *her*!!), and sobbing over his kitchen table because a truck drove past on the highway without turning into his drive.

I am completely undone by this chapter. Everything that made me want to shake him in Chapters 44 and 47 just cracked wide open. When Vee was lying in her twin bed in Ohio imagining him suavely cooking shrimp fra diavolo for some tan, blonde model—or cooking for Sheri!—he was actually sitting in a converted double-wide trailer diner eating a plain burger with Sheri, who is five feet tall, broke, estranged from her own family too (*"People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice"*), and very clearly queer (dating a design student named Paige!). Vee's whole midnight spiral about Sheri and the blonde was a phantom, and Sheri is actually the one real, unvarnished friend Pace has in the world who doesn't want anything from him and isn't afraid to look him dead in the eye and say: *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* YES, SHERI. Crown her immediately alongside Cassie! And I had to laugh through my ache when Sheri called him out on *"the philosophy of your food"* right after shamelessly admiring Vee's backside and long legs from the four seconds she saw her at the Rusty Nail!

And then the truth about the porch morning in Chapter 44, and about Daphne. *He knew.* He didn't miss Vee's cracker-and-meal confession at all. He heard every word of it, he knew he loved her, the real answer rose up in his throat, and he chickened out in real time because when he was a fifteen-year-old kid at university who didn't even know how a meal plan worked, nineteen-year-old Daphne was his whole world—and when she left him, there was no one to tell that Daphne was gone because *Daphne was the person he would have told*. That line gutted me. A mathematician'smagical thinking: he convinced himself that saying *"I love you"* was the variable that made losing someone unsurvivable, so if he kept the words locked inside, he'd survive if Vee left. *"He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him."*

And yet—even in the middle of all this raw, bleeding heartbreak that makes me ache for him—his compartmentalized delusion is STILL sitting right there in the kitchen! When he gets drunk and pictures the future he wants, what does he see? *"The three of them in his kitchen in the middle of a morning, after. Him at the stove with something going. Vee at the table in his sheet with her hair loose and too much honey in her tea. Randi across from her with her coffee, feet up on the lacquered seat. The two of them laughing at something he had missed, and nobody hurrying anywhere."* He really, truly believes he is building a warm, domestic morning-after for the three of them! He sees how Vee lights up for her Saturday brunches with Randi, he knows Vee chased Randi's mouth on the sidewalk, and his only worry is *"whether the room would ask more of her than she had in her"*—not the fact that he and Randi have been lying to her since September!

Watching him bend over his cold bowl of boiled chicken and sob until the highway is quiet, then pick up his fork and eat anyway... this book is operating on a whole different level. I need them back on campus for the start of term *right now*.
