REACTION

God, that common room scene made my skin crawl in the best possible way. I have read *so* many college/sorority romances where the mean girls are cartoon villains shrieking over frat boys, and this was not that. This was surgical. Brooke not even standing up from her laptop? Asking about the paper she *knows* Randi wasn't writing, and then smoothly pivoting to "Wait, is his name *Pace*?" right after? And Sloane with the "I love that you don't care about cars" about his pickup truck, and Kinsey jumping in with "short-cute"—they just completely filleted him, and by extension her, in four minutes flat without anyone ever dropping their smile.

What really got me, though, is how this chapter reframes everything that happened on the bench last night. In Chapter 1, we see Randi's armor from the inside as this psychological defense mechanism against intimacy. Here, we see the ecosystem that built it. She's the Standards chair! She is literally an enforcer in this hyper-policed, terrifyingly polite panopticon, and she just lost massive social ground because she stayed out all night with a math PhD who drives a white pickup. No wonder being cracked open and begging on that bench terrified her so badly. If you aren't holding the frame in her world, you get eaten alive before 10 a.m. over a bowl of cereal.

The back half of the chapter in her bedroom is so quiet and devastating. "The palette running bone to camel to oatmeal with the one disciplined oxblood at the end"—I know this girl in my bones. And the contrast between Pace wrapping her in that warm cashmere blanket last night while he held her, versus her lying flat on her back in a cold room with her shoes still on, pulling a "light and synthetic and adequate" duvet over her wrinkled cardigan because she can't even bring herself to finish the sentence comparing it to his bed.

I'm completely hooked on the psychology here. We don't even have Vee yet (from the blurb), we're still entirely in the prologue of how Randi and Pace become the couple who plots the rest of the book, and I am *fascinated* to see how a girl this locked down pivots from "he saw me and I can never let that happen again" to bringing another woman into their bed. It feels like her brain is about to invent a very dangerous game just to keep Pace from looking directly at her again.
