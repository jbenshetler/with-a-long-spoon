REACTION

Oh, my chest physically hurts. I am sitting here staring at the page with my stomach on the floor.

*"I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal. I didn't know what a meal was until you fed me one. And now I know."*

She spent *weeks* building that sentence! Because she knows—she bumped into the invisible rule from the inside—that she isn't allowed to say *I love you* first, so she carefully built this gorgeous, trembling little bridge out of food and hunger, the exact language he taught her, and pushed it out into the cold morning air while looking at the moon so she wouldn't have to look at his face. And what does he do?!

First of all, that physical tell on his shin—his hand stopping, pressing once, stopping again *"as if he had begun two different responses with his hand and put both down before either had finished,"* and taking *two breaths* before he speaks. He knew! Don't tell me he didn't know what she was saying! A man who is that exact, who reads pulses and notices everything, heard her offer him her whole heart in code, took two breaths to steady himself, and intentionally stepped sideways into: *"I love how good we are together."* And then reduced her entire soul-deep metaphor about being starved her whole life to sex! *"What you do — what your body does — I've never."*

I wanted to reach into the book and shake him by the collar of his flannel shirt. Using the word *"love"* in *"I love how good we are together"* when a woman has just handed you her heart on a platter is the oldest, most cowardly dodge in the book, except with Pace it's worse because he follows it up by pulling her in, kissing her hair for an extra beat, and resting his cheek on her crown so her body gets flooded with warmth and can't even process the rejection in real time!

And Look at the title drop right there in the middle of her trying to convince herself: *"She had been received warmly, she would remind herself, in the small private way you remind yourself of evidence whose weight you are not sure of."* A WARM RECEPTION! I literally gasped out loud when I read that line. The blurb warned us from day one—*They welcomed her warmly. A warm reception is also what you call walking into fire*—and here it is, not even about the threesome trap yet, just the quiet, devastating way he receives her love with a warm quilt and a kiss on the head while refusing to meet her where she is.

Watching her sit on that dorm laundry table two days later while the dryer spins, running her sentence and his sentence side by side until she realizes *"either end of it hurt"*—because either he understood her and deliberately deflected it into a compliment about her body, or he completely missed the most important thing she's ever tried to tell him—broke my heart into pieces. *"She had said her best sentence. He had not said the sentence back."* And now there's a growing pile inside her of "small flickers" where she has to do repair work to keep her idea of him functioning, and she just folds her laundry and puts her best sentence away forever. I am so mad at him right now, and so completely, hopelessly protective of her.
