REACTION

Oh, Randi. This is exactly why you are dangerous.

The shoe fitting got me more than half the explicit scenes in other books do. Her going down on one knee, taking Vee’s foot in both hands, noticing the plum polish, that one thumb over her toenail—and then looking up and saying nothing. That was not friendly. That was not ambiguous. Randi knew exactly what she sent through Vee’s body, and Vee knew it too, even if she still refuses to give it the right name. I was holding my breath over a foot in a shoe store. This book understands charge.

And then it made the same scene hurt. Vee putting shoes she cannot afford on an emergency credit card because she has translated desire into obligation—“what the dress was owed”—made my stomach drop. No, baby. The dress is not owed your debt. Pace’s beautiful gift does not require you to buy your way into being the woman worthy of it. Randi saw Vee freeze in that store, recognized the money problem instantly, and steered around it with glamour instead of asking the one kind question. Then she casually spent several times as much beside her. I don’t think Randi meant to humiliate her, but intention is becoming a very thin defense with this woman. She makes every threshold feel like Vee’s own idea while quietly deciding which direction the room slopes.

The worst part is that the afternoon is genuinely lovely. They are funny together. That ridiculous shoe-store laughter felt easy and young and real, and Vee getting to feel socially fluent beside Randi—not merely admitted into her world, but delightful inside it—made me ache for her. “Same feet, baby” and “practically a marriage” absolutely landed. Randi is not playacting this attachment. She wants a shared category with Vee so badly she will build one out of shoe size.

Cassie asking only, “Was it a good night?” nearly undid me. She does not need the dress, the performance, or the details; she wants to know whether Vee is all right. And Vee recognizes that. Thank God. But then she spends the lecture “happy in the middle” while the professor explains that the same numbers produce opposite truths depending on how they’re grouped. Yes, book, I see you. Vee has two loving friends; Vee is also sitting between the only safe person in the arrangement and the woman secretly arranging her. Both readings use the same facts. The metaphor is a little neon, but unfortunately it works.

Randi seeing the dress was almost unbearable. “He made it *to* you” is perfect because she understands Pace’s attention from the inside. She can name what he did because she knows how he loves—and Vee reads Randi’s silence as longing for some imaginary man who might love her that way. That misunderstanding is now actively cruel. Vee is feeling tenderness toward Randi for a loneliness Pace has already spent years answering, while missing that Randi is jealous because Pace got to make something *to Vee*. I need somebody to tell this girl the truth before every beautiful memory of this week becomes contaminated.

And still: the dress, the shoes, the induction coming. I want to see her walk into that room so badly. I want the room to stop. I also want Cassie to look at the shoes once and know exactly what they cost Vee—not just in dollars—and ask the question Randi chose not to.
