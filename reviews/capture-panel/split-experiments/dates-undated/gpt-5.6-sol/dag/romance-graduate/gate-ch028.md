REACTION

Oh, Pace. “Not yet” is doing a horrifying amount of moral work for you.

I was almost lulled by him spilling over about Vee—the chair joint, the math book, the fact that he hears himself talking and cannot stop. That is exactly the evidence I want: she has gotten into the private rooms of his mind, not merely his bed. Sheri’s “Listen to you” made me soften because yes, listen to him. He is gone. He just doesn’t know what being gone sounds like.

And then Sheri asks whether Randi knows about Vee. “She knows.” Whether Vee knows about Randi. “Not yet.” Just clean, factual answers, as if those two facts are remotely equivalent. As if Randi knowing everything and Vee knowing nothing is merely an unfinished sequence. I wanted to reach into the book and grab his handlebars. He can perceive the tiniest change in Vee’s face, build a dress to the exact dimensions of her body, wait through every sexual hesitation—and still reduce the foundational deception to a piece of information she has not received *yet*. That is his entire moral failure in two words.

Worse, Sheri runs the obvious arithmetic and reacts as though he has landed the fantasy: two women, lucky man, complications probably worth it. Pace lets that framing stand. He doesn’t say Vee hasn’t agreed to “two of them at once.” He doesn’t say she believes Randi is simply her friend. He doesn’t seem to feel the difference between a complication and a trapdoor. His silence here frightened me more than a defensive speech would have.

I’m also intensely suspicious of Sheri now. Is she the blonde from the bar? The chapter never says so, but “after you left Thursday” means she was there, she watches women, and she volunteers Vee’s backside before I’ve even settled into the ride. Whether or not she planted the jealousy, she is another woman in Pace’s life who gets more of the truth than Vee does. Everyone is casually standing one step ahead of Vee while she thinks she has finally become the woman in the middle of the light.

And Vee is preparing to walk into his house dressed by Randi, in Randi’s shoes and Randi’s scarf, to apologize for distrusting him—while Pace spent Friday night having sex with Randi as Randi described touching her. Now he spends Saturday morning telling a friend, almost cheerfully, that Vee doesn’t know yet. I feel sick for her. The apology is going to be sincere, and he does deserve an apology for the bar, but she is about to abase herself before a man withholding something vastly larger. The asymmetry is unbearable.

This chapter is tiny, but it is not a shortcut. It is a blade. It took Pace’s beautiful precision and showed me how precision becomes evasion when he wants it to. I’m already turning the page because I need to see whether Vee walks through that door before he finds the courage to tell her what room she is actually entering.
