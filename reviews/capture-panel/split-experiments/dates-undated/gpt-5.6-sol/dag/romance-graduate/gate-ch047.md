REACTION

Oh, thank God she asked him. Properly, unmistakably, with nowhere for him to hide—and he hid anyway.

“Do any of your friends have a relationship you’d rather have than this one?” made me furious. It is such a clever answer because he knows the honest answer is no. He offers her the quality of what they have as if quality and definition are interchangeable, and for half a second even she gets caught in it. This is what he does: gives her something beautifully true at an angle to the truth she asked for. He can’t claim not to understand anymore. She said *out there* over and over. Cassie. Her father. Her mother. Relationship. Who am I to you. There is no ambiguity left for his precision to shelter inside.

And “Believe what I do” is almost obscene now. What he does includes Randi. What he does includes passing Vee’s confidences between them, choosing her together, preparing her without her knowledge, making promises narrow enough to remain technically unbroken. He is asking her to trust evidence while deliberately withholding half the evidence. I wanted to reach into the book and tell her that her doubt is not insecurity. Her body has finally caught the lie before her mind knows its contents.

“Words are cheap, and you still won’t spend them on me” absolutely gutted me. That is the sentence. She saw the contradiction cleanly and did not let his warm house, his food, his care, or the fact that no friend has anything better talk her out of it. I am so proud of her for arriving without the bag. She knew before she opened the door that she might not stay. That detail hurt almost as much as the confrontation.

And then he folded his arms. That’s the moment that changed him for me. Until now I could keep imagining some wound, some terror, some inability to say love. But that was not helplessness. That was a decision. He understood the price of withholding the word, watched her standing there paying it, and held the line. Only when she cried did he reach for the mechanism that has always worked: his hands, his care, her body settling under his attention. No. I was almost cheering when she refused to let touch substitute for an answer.

Her name is not the word. God.

I desperately want him, still. That is part of why this hurts so well. The warm house got me too; the wine already poured got me; the chicken, the piano, the porch under a clear sky—all of it still feels like love. But Vee is right: if all that devotion cannot survive being named outside his front door, then it is also a cage, however beautifully heated.

Now I need to know what happens after she drives away. I need her to go to Cassie, not Randi. Please, not Randi—not tonight, not when Randi already knows the answer and has helped keep it from her. And I need Pace to understand that the disaster is not that he failed to say “girlfriend.” The disaster is that the real answer to “What are we doing?” is something Vee has never been permitted to hear.
