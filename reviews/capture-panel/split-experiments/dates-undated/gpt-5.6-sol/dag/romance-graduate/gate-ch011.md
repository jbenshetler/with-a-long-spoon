REACTION

Oh, that truck scene. That is exactly what I keep looking for and almost never get: explicit charge built out of character, shame, choice, attention, and actual tenderness instead of somebody reaching for the standard choreography.

Vee calling him back in because she wants to be seen—then making him hold her eyes until she deliberately lets his gaze follow hers—got me completely. The mother’s voice becoming part of the heat rather than a little psychological obstacle conveniently cured by a good man felt painfully true. She isn’t suddenly free of it. She chooses while it is still speaking. And Pace’s response being want and gladness, without grabbing or congratulating himself for his restraint, was almost unbearably hot.

Then she reaches for his hand because she wants more, discovers it is shaking, and changes course. That is the turn. Until then he has been the competent one: setting the pace, fixing the boot, teaching her the trail, feeding her, quietly correcting the cherry pit, giving her privacy. But she finally sees his body and takes care of him. Bringing his freezing hand under the flannel against her belly instead of bringing it to her mouth did more to me than the kiss would have. It made them feel like two people in the cab, not an experienced man conducting a beautifully managed awakening.

And God, the food again. This book understands that Vee’s hunger is not decorative. “The plain animal rightness of needing something and being handed it” is basically the whole seduction, and it scares me because Pace is so exquisitely suited to what she lacks. He never shames her ignorance. He gives her the rule and lets her keep her dignity. I understand exactly why she is falling.

Which means the original deception is hurting more, not less. Nothing in this chapter felt false. Her desire was hers. His tenderness was real. That is precisely why I’m furious that she still doesn’t know the room was arranged before she entered it. Randi’s “ravish” is inside Vee’s fantasy while Randi herself is absent, which is delicious and ominous: Vee is already taking both of them into her body without knowing they put each other there first.

Also, that interrupted goodbye was cruel in the best way. The siren didn’t create some silly misunderstanding; it stole language from a moment that was already too large for Vee’s language. Now she has his shirt, he has seen her, and neither of them got to reduce it to an ordinary date-ending sentence. I would already have turned the page.
