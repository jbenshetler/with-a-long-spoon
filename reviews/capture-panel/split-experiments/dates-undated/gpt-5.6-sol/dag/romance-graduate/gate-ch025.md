Oh, Vee. Oh, honey, no.

The lesson itself had me absolutely gone. “Give me something to push back against.” “Close it—come all the way in.” The information traveling through their bodies, Pace refusing to drag a limp partner around and teaching her that moving together requires her pressure too—that is exactly the kind of dominance I want. Not mind reading, not obedience dressed up as chemistry: communication so physical it reaches her before thought does. And when she finally stopped anticipating and felt him? “There it is.” I felt that everywhere.

Then the bar made me nervous almost immediately. Not because women wanted Pace—of course they do—but because Vee has so little experience being publicly chosen that every woman who knows him becomes evidence against her. Her mother’s voice was already loaded and waiting. The blonde only had to pull the trigger.

Still, the cruelty of what Vee said shocked me. She took the exact thing that makes Pace good—the fact that women trust him, that he gets vulnerable women safely home without exploiting them—and made it sound predatory and cheap. His “Yes. Sometimes. When it’s required” hurt because he understood the accusation perfectly and would not scramble to sell himself back to her. And then he left. Good. I didn’t want him chasing her through the room, absorbing whatever else she needed to throw, and calling that devotion.

I’m furious with her and aching for her at the same time. “Three dates, and she’d spread her legs like a slut” is the real attacker here. The blonde supplied the story, but Vee’s mother supplied the weapon, and Vee turned it on both herself and Pace. That does not excuse what she did. It does make the whole thing feel horribly human instead of like one more romance misunderstanding that could be cured by a five-second text.

The bartender’s correction was almost painful in its simplicity. He doesn’t cycle through those women. He takes care of them. He doesn’t bring dates. They were jealous of *her*. Vee had correctly understood that she was being watched and then interpreted every fact backward because she still cannot believe being chosen is more plausible than being used. That is going to destroy me when the larger deception comes out, because this time she was wrong about Pace—but she is not wrong that there are things he has kept from her.

And I hate, hate, hate that she did not wake Cassie. Cassie was three feet away. Cassie would have come. Instead Vee lay there alone, understood exactly what she had done, and decided to go to Randi—the person who secretly helped choose her, who knows the whole truth, who benefits from every fracture between Vee and Pace. I am already reaching for the next chapter with dread. Randi could help her repair this beautifully. She could also use Vee’s shame to pull her closer, and both things could happen in the same conversation.

The nastiest part is that Vee is going to Randi because she thinks Randi is outside the situation. She is walking directly into the fire to ask it what heat means.
