Oh, the shirt. The shirt absolutely got me.

Vee confessing that she spent five nights sleeping in his smell until it faded—and then asking him to wear the flannel all weekend so she can take him back with her—is so nakedly needy that it feels more intimate than the sex. And Pace understands immediately. He doesn’t tease her, doesn’t make her translate it into something less embarrassing, just puts the ridiculous second shirt on and promises to return it smelling like him. That is exactly the kind of care that keeps making me fall for him against my better judgment. It is also exactly how Vee can persuade herself she doesn’t need words. Who needs a declaration when a man will stand in his kitchen wearing two shirts because you asked? Except she does need one. She needs several, beginning with the truth.

I loved that she took the five nights for school. I loved even more that he simply let her take them, because her life should still belong to her. But I understood the awful little sting when he didn’t fight for the nights. She wants proof that her absence costs him something. He keeps answering emotional questions through flawless behavior while leaving her to invent the language herself, and I am increasingly convinced that his “good at not asking things” is not merely courtliness. Sometimes it is avoidance. He thought she was returning the shirt, felt hurt, and was prepared to accept it without asking what it meant. That is not always generosity. Sometimes love requires risking the question.

The reunion was hot because it was undignified. Jeans stuck at one thigh, white socks, laughing against the wall, her bra caught so that she has to unwind herself out of it—yes. This is what I want instead of another polished billionaire sex routine. Her body blurting out how badly she missed him before she could preserve any pride was wonderful. And then that green thong landing on the table he built with her in mind: excuse me, book, I saw that. You do not get to put it there and expect me to forget the table exists.

The long pizza interlude should probably have cooled me off, but it didn’t. The erotic current stayed in the fact that Pace’s attention has a recognizable texture whether it lands on her body or dough. Vee isn’t jealous of the cooking because she is learning that care is not scarce with him. And her tasting every ingredient separately feels like the emotional problem in miniature: she can distinguish tomato, yeast, onion, mushroom, wine—but she still accepts her relationship as one unnamed whole because taking it apart might tell her what is missing.

I’m soft, turned on, and furious. This chapter gave me such a convincing new ordinary that I could feel why Vee would protect it from any question capable of breaking it. The drawer, the sex, the food, the shirt-refresh arrangement: they are building rituals that look and feel like love. But Pace and Randi still know the actual shape of Vee’s life while she doesn’t, and every gorgeous domestic detail raises the eventual price. I would text a friend: “He wore two shirts for her and I’m crying, but he is STILL LYING TO HER.”
