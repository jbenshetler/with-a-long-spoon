REACTION

Oh, the drawer got me. Damn him. That is exactly why I’m so far gone over this man: he doesn’t reassure her that her mess is welcome; he reorganizes his own life until there is an easy place for it. He packs himself into the inconvenient drawer and gives her the one a hand reaches for without thinking. That is love in a form Vee can actually believe because it exists whether she understands it or not.

And of course that is also the trap. She has finally named that she loves him, then immediately decides she can live without hearing him name anything back. No, sweetheart. You can live inside gestures for a while, especially gestures this exquisitely specific, but the jacket has already told me where that bargain goes. The chapter kept making his silence feel romantic and then turning it a fraction so I could see the blade: *He never said.* *He hadn’t said.* *There was never a note.* Vee keeps treating absence of language as proof that action is purer. Meanwhile the one fact she desperately needs—the actual shape of the relationship she’s in—is precisely the thing his actions cannot tell her.

Her whispering “Peter” to him while he sleeps hurt almost more than an “I love you” would have. She uses the name he entrusted to her as her private declaration and asks nothing back because she has already decided asking would be hunger. She’s learning to be hungry out loud sexually, yes, but emotionally she is still making herself very, very small.

I loved the physical ease between them—the deranged reunion at the door, not making it to the kitchen, sex again after dinner because desire can have an after instead of being a single scripted event. That feels adult and lived-in and genuinely hot even in recollection. And her appetite coming back at his table should have delighted me, except the unasked-for weight loss put a little cold finger down my spine. Maybe it is only the book noticing that her old clothes never fit her real body properly. But paired with Randi’s complicated relationship to food, Vee “burning down whole weekends of him,” and the bras keeping the secret, it feels like something I’m meant to clock.

Mostly, though: he made room for her. Literally. Quietly. Lovingly. While still withholding the truth that would let her know whether she should be moving in at all. I want to crawl into that bathroom drawer and scream.
