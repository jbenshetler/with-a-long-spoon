REACTION

Oh, that hurt. That hurt in exactly the place all his beautiful gestures have been buying him access to.

She gave him “I love you” in the most Vee language possible: appetite, awakening, being fed into a larger life. It was not coy. It was not actually ambiguous. Anyone who knows her—anyone who has listened to her describe what food has become in his house—knows what she handed him. And Pace knows her better than anyone. So I do not believe it went past him. I believe he heard it, needed two breaths, and chose a sentence containing the word *love* that carefully did not attach love to her.

“I love how good we are together.” God. That is a response engineered to provide warmth without exposure. Then “what your body does,” as if what she offered him was principally sexual compatibility. I wanted to crawl under the quilt and shake him. She told him he had changed the scale of her life, and he answered that they fuck exceptionally well.

And the awful part is that he does love her, or behaves with such sustained tenderness that the distinction has become almost perverse. The waiting coffee, the quilt, the hand on her shin, the kiss held against her hair—none of it feels counterfeit. That is why this is worse than some chilly commitment-phobe cliché. Pace is not emotionally absent. He is emotionally exact. He can feel the weight of her sentence enter the room, and then construct an answer that keeps her close while leaving himself technically uncommitted. It is the photograph promise all over again: answer the narrowest survivable version of what she asked and let tenderness persuade her she received the whole thing.

But this time her body caught him.

That little *wait* behind her breastbone made me almost sick with relief. She did not instantly translate disappointment into proof of his goodness. She let the warmth soothe her, yes, but it did not erase the mismatch. Two days later she laid the sentences beside each other and saw they were not the same shape. That is enormous. This is the same woman who has been turning drawers and dinners and careful hands into all the words he refuses to say. Now she knows, at least privately, that the evidence requires “repair work.” She has a pile. She knows there is a pile.

And “the words went through him first” broke my heart. That rule is not romance; it is fear wearing etiquette. She cannot ask the only person who could answer because she has somehow decided his willingness must authorize her honesty. So she makes one exquisitely vulnerable attempt, gets a beautiful evasion, and retires the sentence forever. I want better for her than having to bury her best language inside her body because the man who treasures that body will not risk one plain word.

Also, the title. Seconds: another helping, the second breath, the second sentence, the second thoughts arriving after the warmth. I am absolutely not putting this down now. The deception is still the larger betrayal, but this may be the first crack Vee cannot completely love him out of.
