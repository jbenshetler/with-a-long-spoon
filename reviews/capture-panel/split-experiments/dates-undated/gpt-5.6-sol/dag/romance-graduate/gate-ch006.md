REACTION

Oh, this got me. The stupid froyo got me.

Her cup quietly becoming too much because her mother’s voice doesn’t show up in time—and then her simply not caring—is exactly the kind of tiny freedom I want this book to notice. Pace paying attention without turning his attention into a performance is catnip to me, especially after so many romance heroes whose idea of “seeing” a woman is announcing her eye color and diagnosing her trauma. He lets her embarrassing truth sit there without rescuing her from it or making her reassure him. Then: “Your tart’s holding up.” I would absolutely text a friend at one in the morning that this man made frozen yogurt feel intimate.

Except I know what Vee doesn’t: this wasn’t chance. Randi and Pace arranged her. So every sweet beat has a blade tucked inside it. I believe Pace is genuinely interested in her, which somehow makes it worse and better at once. He isn’t faking the listening, but he entered her life under false pretenses and already knows exactly how starved she is for it. Watching her experience ordinary consideration as extraordinary kindness made my chest hurt.

This is a very short chapter, but it doesn’t feel empty. It feels like one held breath—the soft, dangerous beginning of Vee trusting him. And after all the erotic machinery of the opening chapters, I’m impressed that “your tart’s holding up” has nearly as much charge for me as the bench did.
