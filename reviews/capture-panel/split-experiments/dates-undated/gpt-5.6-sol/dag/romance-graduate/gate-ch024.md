Oh, this got me. This is the grown-up intimacy I keep looking for and almost never get: naked under a quilt, his hand resting on her breast, and the truly exposing thing is that they’re telling each other who they were before they met. I did not need them to have sex again. His palm simply holding her, her feet under his thigh, the cold glass and the fire behind them—it was charged because neither of them was performing. They were just there.

And Vee is so good with him. “They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment” went straight through me. She sees the injury without making him display it for her, then has the sense not to force comfort on him. The scar moment did the same thing: “Not tonight,” and she understands that it means later, not never. Turning her hand beneath his so they’re palm to palm over the thing he cannot tell her yet may be one of the most intimate gestures in the book.

Peter. God. Pace is the name with momentum, the man he constructed; Peter is the boy who got put in the hall. Her saying it once at the end, only to keep it, absolutely undid me.

Daphne is worse than I expected. Vee’s horror was mine, especially when she understood that he was defending Daphne from a word she hadn’t even spoken. He has built this extraordinarily exact ethics of asking and waiting, and now I can see more clearly what may be underneath it: he was the brilliant child everyone treated as prematurely adult, and he still cannot fully name where an adult failed him. I feel protective of him in a way that is almost painful.

The math speech should not have worked on me as well as it did, but it did. Not because I suddenly care about coastlines, exactly. Because this reserved man lit up and let Vee see the world through the part of him everyone else had punished or misunderstood. “Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some” is devastatingly beautiful—and then she recognizes that his total presence under a squat bar is the same total presence he gives her in bed. This whole chapter let her believe she was finally learning the hidden language of the world while she remains the only person who cannot read the design of her own life.

I would text a friend at one in the morning: “His real name is Peter, his college girlfriend was nineteen when he was fifteen, and Vee is cuddled around him thinking fate brought both of them to her. I am NOT OKAY.”
