REACTION

Two pages. Two pages and I'm sitting here with my hand over my mouth.

Cassie asked it. She finally asked it, and she didn't ask the question I'd have asked, which is *what is going on with you two* — she asked **"Is this what you want?"** And that is the question. Twenty chapters of me yelling *push* at that girl and she doesn't push, she just sets the one door down in front of Vee that nobody in this book has ever opened, because everybody else in this book deals in *offerings*. Pace asks for things and leaves the reason blank for her to fill in. Randi asks questions she already knows the answers to. Cassie asked a question with no agenda under it at all and it nearly took Vee's legs out.

And Vee *looked*. That's what got me. She went and looked, "because Cassie had asked it so simply it seemed like a thing a person would just know about herself" — and she came up against the edge of a shape that was *close and large* and stepped back off before she could make out its size. Same half-inch. Same breath's width as the hot sealed room, same *nothing in the room told her why.* She can get her hand to it and she cannot close it, and the mechanism is so smooth now that she's got a vault for a question she asked *herself*.

Then she tried to swing it onto Cassie. The old sorority fight, laid down "like a plank to walk out on" — and Cassie **stepped over it without once looking down.** I made a noise. That's the third time this stretch somebody's been handed the ordinary exit and declined to use it (Pace holding her off him until she stopped tidying, Randi asking *how does he act*), except this one costs Cassie something and buys her nothing, and she does it anyway, and then she keeps whatever's in her face. She always keeps it. She kept it the night of the skirt and she kept it tonight.

And then. *"You didn't have dinner."* **"Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles?"**

I want to lie down. She asked the unanswerable question, watched the girl fail it, and then handed her a want she could actually find — *a want the size of a bowl* — and let her have the only yes she had in her all night. That's not changing the subject, it's triage. It's the same move as *she wants to know when she gets to see you again*: Cassie hands the thing over and then immediately makes it small enough to carry.

And — God — tonight's lecture was *reciprocity.* The three obligations. Give, take, pay back. The gift as a debt you lay on somebody whether they want it or not. Vee sat through that with her knee against Randi's and took in none of it, and then came home and was handed the **only** gift in three books with nothing underneath it. No shoes. No blanket. No skirt off the rail. No *you're going to love it, barely stings.* A box of cereal out of the gap beside the bunk. The hau wants to go home and Cocoa Pebbles doesn't want anything, and the book didn't point at it once, and I'm the one sitting here doing the math at 1 a.m. again.

What I'd text: *the roommate just asked her the only real question in 70 chapters and she couldn't answer it and then got offered cereal and that's the kindest thing that's happened to anybody in this entire book*

I was braced for two pages to feel like a shortcut. It isn't. It's the only length this scene could be — anything longer and Vee would have talked, and the whole point is that she ran out, and *she never runs out.*

Also noting, quietly, that she thinks the postmark shows. That Cassie might have passed close enough to see the mouth at the neck and hear the sound. We don't know what Cassie saw. The book is holding that, and Cassie is holding it, and I have to go to Thursday — no, Saturday, *brunch,* which Vee asked for out of the ringing in her neck with no part of her thinking — carrying it.
