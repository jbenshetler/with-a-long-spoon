Oh, he *moved.*

I have been screaming for fifty chapters that somebody pick up a phone, and what I actually got was better and sneakier than a phone call: a man at his sink with his sleeves rolled, saying "I want to meet your people" in the voice you'd use for a grocery list. Five weeks ago she stood on his mat with her coat buttoned to the throat and said *all of that is in here, inside this house, with the door shut* — and *out there, what are we? How do I say this to Cassie?* He had nothing for her that night. He folded his arms. He said her name at her back. And now, without being asked again, without a single reference to that night, he opens the door himself. He's answering December. Late, and in his own dialect, and he's answering the exact question she asked, which is more than the word would have done.

But the boots. The *boots.* That's the thing that got me in the chest. One half sentence in October, on a trail, thighs burning, *these are Meg's and they're a half size big* — the kind of filler you say so the silence doesn't get weird — and he put it on a shelf with her friends' names on it and took it down one-handed without turning around. *Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off.* And then *Kayla's the one who can talk the paint off a wall,* which is her own line, handed back. All those nights she talked at his back thinking his back was just a polite place to aim it. He kept all of it. I spent two volumes watching this man prove himself in heat and food and thermostats, which are all things he's good at, and this is the first time he's spent the one currency that actually costs him nothing and proves everything, and it turns out he's been quietly stockpiling it since autumn. *Words are cheap, believe what I do* — okay, Peter. Fine. This one lands. This is the receipt.

And *"Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one."* The man who paints the outline of every tool on the shop wall just solved a seating problem like a knot in the wood, and the solution is: no table. Open the room. Put everybody in it. He was *less* than the room required at Chili's and he's being more than it requires here, and he did it off a one-word problem she hadn't even finished stating.

Now. The part my stomach did while my heart was doing all that.

**"You could meet Randi," she said. "Properly, I mean."** Properly. Honey. He's met her properly. He's met her in his kitchen with her back against his chest asking him to say something nice to her, he keeps the cashmere blanket on the chair because she wants it about a minute after every time, she arrives unannounced and pleased about it — and Vee just offered to introduce them over real napkins, and he said *bring Cassie too,* and *and Meg,* and *bring Theo,* and built himself a crowd. And then she handed him the venue and he leaned back on the counter with a face she'd have called happy if he'd let it out any further and said **"Let me handle it."**

That's the coldest warm sentence in the book. He's been building that kitchen tableau for weeks — *further along than last time, because he had been adding to it for weeks* — and the one thing a man in his position needs is a room where Randi can stand next to him in Vee's life without it being an event. A dinner for three has ends. A party has none. *What he wondered was how she would carry it.* He's going to find out with twelve people for cover and Vee thinking she arranged it. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: HE SAID LET ME HANDLE IT. HE ALREADY KNOWS HER. SHE'S PLANNING THE GUEST LIST FOR HER OWN AMBUSH AND IT'S THE SWEETEST SCENE IN THE BOOK.

Two smaller things I'm turning over. One: *"Bring Theo,"* no question asked, and her feeling something settle warm side down. I know what she felt. I'm not sure that's what it was. A man with no jealousy in him is either the most secure person alive or a man with no claim staked, and this particular man has gone on record as having staked nothing in words. Second: **"She'd watched somebody be fine about it before. She wasn't going to look at when."** That's the fourth vault. MIRA, the shirt in the heap, her own leg cropped out of the photograph, and now this — and this one she doesn't even open the door on. One sentence, turned away from. Cassie at some table, or Sheri over the pie, or whoever it was being a good sport while Vee let it happen; she won't say and she won't look. I keep thinking her not-looking is going to come due, and the book keeps showing me the mechanism getting smoother.

And nobody said the word. Still. Five weeks of silence, a coat opened in a doorway, and a man inviting her whole life into a room, and the word is still unspent, and she didn't ask, and she *won't* now — because she's been handed a shape she can rest inside, and that is her oldest move. Only this time he paid real coin for it, so I can't even be mad.

Tuesday. Randi across a little table wanting every detail, and Vee's going to lead with *Pace wants to meet you properly.* I would pay money to watch that woman's face do nothing.
