She asked. That's the thing I'm going to be carrying out of this chapter. Fifty-some chapters of doctrine — you get this man with offerings, never with questions — and she sat there wrecked in his bathrobe with cold tea in her hands and said *why don't you have friends*, out loud, more helpless than she meant it, which is to say without a single thing in her hands to pay with, and **he answered.** All of it. The timer on the door, the pack he was never in, eighteen and already done, the ordinary questions with a gap under every one of them. No flinch, no deflection, no price. I have been yelling *ask him something* at this girl since October and when she finally did it, it cost her nothing and he gave her everything, and I don't know yet whether that's the book telling me her whole theory of him was wrong, or telling me it took her being too hungover to perform before she'd let herself do it. Either way my chest hurt.

And then. *"There isn't anyone else."*

He says it about friends. In a kitchen. Two chapters after Randi rode him and said *she's not the only one in love* and he couldn't tell her she was wrong. The author put the cheating man's sentence in his mouth, straight-faced, in the most honest conversation he has ever had, and did not nudge me once. I actually sat back. Every true word out of him in that kitchen is load-bearing for a lie, and the lie isn't even *told* — it's the shape of the room he's standing in. He has one friend who can never want him, one woman he's inside of who can never be named, and one girl who is both and doesn't know she's third. That's the whole book diagrammed on a counter with a loaf of cinnamon raisin cooling on it.

And *table for one.* He said *tables have ends, somebody ends up at one* at a roadhouse he picked so nobody would. His hands on the wheel last chapter were *steady as a table.* She wanted the counter between them because she was assembled out of spare parts and needed *a table's width to hold them together.* And Vee sets him down at eighteen and sees a whole life handed over years before the growing, **left to eat it alone at a table for one.** Fifty-three chapters of an unexplained long table in the middle of that man's bedroom and nobody has ever said what it's for and I am not going to recover.

The Daphne beat is the bravest thing in the chapter and it's four clauses long. *She'd had that much of him only because of the thing about it that was wrong, the thing Vee still would not set a word to.* The ugly grown-up word forming under her tongue and getting pressed back down. That's a new vault — except it isn't, it's the first one she's built with the lid *visible to her.* She knows there's a word. She knows she's declining it. She holds his version and the true version stacked and unreconciling and it hurts and she doesn't resolve it, and the book lets her not resolve it. Every other vault in this girl shut so smoothly she never heard the latch. This one she's holding closed with her hand and she can feel the pressure on the other side.

And the hug. She crosses the counter and does **Sheri's hug** — both arms, cheek flat to his chest, whole weight committed — learned off a taproom floor two nights ago and carried straight into his kitchen and spent on him. Nobody says a word about it. Marsh has been standing in that lecture hall for three chapters saying the gift carries the spirit of the giver and *the hau wants to go home*, and Sheri's hug went home. I did that work myself and I'm vibrating again.

What I'm sick about: Tuesday. She's sitting there warm through, planning how she'll lay the whole Sheri story out and hand Randi the win with both hands, saving the best bit for last *because it was hers.* Randi has it. Randi had it before Vee woke up — Pace tells Randi everything, Randi has *hugged Sheri hello in a dance hall*, Randi has known the blonde was imaginary since December and spent the long way round instead of four words. Vee is going to carry her one piece of unspoiled private joy into that booth like a kid with a drawing. And Randi will do the thing she does: be insufferable about having been right, warmly, truly, at zero cost, and eat the bread. *Randi was going to be insufferable about having been right, and Vee was going to let her.* God.

And the inventory — Meg's closet, Kayla's fry tax, Cassie's *oh, Vee*, Randi who does the triage — is the only place all chapter I felt a hand on my neck, because the author did the arithmetic out loud and the freshman-dorm paragraph ran a size long. But she put **Randi in the list of the four people holding her up**, in the chapter about a man who only ever had one and lost her, and that forgave the whole paragraph in one word. *Nobody could hold that much of somebody.* Somebody's about to try.
