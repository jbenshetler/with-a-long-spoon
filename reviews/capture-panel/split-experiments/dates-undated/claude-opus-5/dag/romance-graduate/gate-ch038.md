REACTION

He said *photograph.*

I had to put the book down on my chest. She asks him for one thing — one condition, the only condition she has ever set on anything in this entire book — *only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone* — and he says **"Never. I'd never share your photograph."** She asked about *me*. He answered about *the file*. And he's not even lying. That's what's going to kill me. He will never send that image to another living soul, he'll probably keep it in a folder with a sensible name, and meanwhile every single thing she says over a brunch table walks straight into his bedroom and gets ridden on. He has been sharing her for two books. He narrowed the promise to the one thing he can keep clean and handed it over with a straight face and a warm one, and she *heard a yes.* And then — "she sent it before the nerve could leave her," and "believing him was the brave part." Helen Rivers did not underline one word of that. Did not nudge me once. Just set the noun down and walked away. I'm sick.

And the chapter had already gutted me before that, from the other direction, because the posing is the loveliest thing he's done since the lining. He turns *all the way across* her and puts his head on the lower slope of her breast with his feet off the far edge of the bed — that detail, the feet, a big man arranged sideways like a cat — and she decides it's not sexual, and then she goes tight under his mouth and the corners of his mouth pull up because he *felt it happen,* and she gets to be delighted at being wrong. That's the best kind of wrong in a book. And then he moves her: elbow, hair, hand to the hip, knees, one carried a little ahead of the other, *and kisses every place he sets her* — shoulder, hip, knee. Tilts the lampshade so the glare slides off her skin. *"Hm. Yeah. That's the one."* And then looks up over the phone at **her** and grins like a boy who got away with something.

Except. He also just composed her. Again. He built the dress to her, and now he's built the picture of her, and she handed him the camera and asked *how do you want me*, and what she loved about the photo was that it was *her as his eyes have her.* She went and put her face back on first — the poppy mouth, "the version of herself she wanted him to keep" — and he said *I like you exactly like this* to the wrecked sated one and let her go do it anyway. He didn't fight her for the real one. He never fights her for anything. He just takes delivery.

*He's all mine.* Three words. In a book where I have been a step ahead of her since page one. I actually said "oh honey" out loud at a hardcover.

Now the thing that has me sitting up at 1 a.m.: **Thanksgiving.** She's flying to Ohio. He's not going anywhere, because *"This is my home"* — easy, warm, almost nothing — and then she pushes one inch and his face goes *level and exact* and the words come out set down clean and apart from each other: *"I don't go back."* No door. And she takes her hand off it, which is the most grown-up thing in the chapter and I loved her for it. Fifteen, out the window, and the hall, and the long division, and *I don't go back.* Four words and I know the whole shape of that house.

And then — and *then* — "Sheri's coming over. She'll bring a pie and pretend she made it." And Vee's little flicker goes up, *another woman at his table on a day meant for family*, and she puts it out herself on *"She's alone."*

She flickered at the wrong woman. She flickered at the cyclist with the bleached ponytail who kept an eye on her in a bar, and she will never once flicker at the one who drove out there on a Friday nobody asked her to. Meanwhile I'm sitting here doing the only arithmetic that matters, which is: Vee is going to be nine hundred miles away for four days, and Randi has a family and a Mercedes and nothing stopping her, and there is now a photograph of Vee laid out like a calendar girl living on that man's phone. *I'd never share your photograph.* Sure. Does he describe it? Does Randi ask? Does she say *show me*? He has never withheld one thing from that bed except Vee's worst minute at the bar. She gave him the one picture of herself she's ever authorized and the promise she got back has a loophole you could drive the Mercedes through, and the bill comes due over a holiday weekend with a stranger's pie on the table.

Food, though. Third chapter running with a pan aria, and I'm starting to feel it. The lemon in a fine bright rain, the pan's talk changing, the butter swirled off the heat and the sauce going from bright to silk — it's *beautiful*, I can smell it, and I also skimmed four lines of it, which I haven't done in this book yet. I don't need the stock-then-lemon order. I need the hand held on the counter while it cooks down and nobody talking. That's what the cooking is actually for and the book knows it, so trim the mise en place.

One more: *I could take the thing apart on my tongue and tell each piece from the next. I had used to just eat. Now I tasted, and there was no going back.* That's the whole novel, isn't it. He woke her palate up on purpose and she can't un-taste anything now — not food, not his hand, not Randi's thumb behind her knee. No going back is a promise *and* a sentence.
