REACTION

I put the book down after "I almost believed it" and said *you absolute coward* out loud to an empty room, and I meant it as the highest praise I have.

Let me do the thing I caught first, because I'm still shaking about it. Pace asked her Sunday to be bare **by the weekend**. Randi handed her Sondra, Draper Street, say my name in the first sentence. Friday was Pace. Saturday was Randi — and Randi went down the rail of her closet *without hunting, she knew right where it was,* and came back with a weightless iridescent scrap she'd already clocked would ride on a girl with a foot of leg and an ass she hasn't got. Then on the floor she put her hands on Vee's hips and made the dancing *bigger* — "No. Like **this**" — and the skirt got braver in exact measure, and then she took Vee's wrists and **lifted them and held them in the air** and kept them there for the whole arms-up part of the song, and let it ride, and let it ride, and *then* brought her mouth to Vee's ear and said *your panties are showing.* A whole song late. And when Vee went white: *they already looked — and believe me, nobody minded.*

Randi knows what's under that skirt. Randi is the reason what's under that skirt is what it is. I don't think she engineered the wax — Pace asked for that — but she knew the date it came due and she dressed the girl anyway and then ran her body around a lit floor on the end of her own grip. That is the single most predatory thing in three books and it is also, I am aware, the best night of Vee's life, and the book will not let me have one without the other and I have stopped expecting it to.

Now the four lines that actually broke me, which Vee walked straight past:

**"I chose."** — **"I can see."**

In front of her. Over her head. Brooke in gold lamé with her almost-blue teeth, who two weeks ago said *you've got a position in this house, and you've got her, you need to choose,* and said **Lauren Pike,** and *nobody asked her to move out* — Brooke got her answer at her own party, in two words, delivered by a girl in an aqua strapless with her plus-one's waist in her arm. And then Vee asked *what did you choose?* and Randi said **"You."** and then the save came **a beat late** — *as my date.* A beat late. The book put the beat in. I counted it.

And the kindness Vee felt — *Brooke hadn't touched it, Brooke is kind after all* — Brooke didn't bring up the Friday-morning wreck because Brooke doesn't need to say a thing out loud to anybody. She catalogued it in front of them both: stats all fall, the smoothie shop, the cute little cardigan, *I never forget a thing,* and *you needed our Randi like the world was ending.* That's not warmth, that's a file being read into the record with a smile on it. *Any friend of Randi's.* God.

The Chambord in the galvanized tub. *She'll spend a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime.* Randi said that about Brooke while wearing a cardigan that "wore its money on the inside, soft and unbranded, nothing on it you could say out loud." She described herself to the girl who cannot read money in a room and thinks it took Randi to tell her what she was holding. It did, and it always will, and that's the arrangement.

**The bracelet is called Love.** I need to lie down. Three books of that plain heavy band, the one thing that never comes off, and it finally gets a name and the name is *Love*, and it's screwed shut, and **"Taking it off requires tools."** Said *lightly. Fond.* Already looking ahead to the gold windows. And Vee laughed and filed it with the thousand glamorous things Randi knows. A locked love you need a screwdriver to get out of, on the wrist of a woman whose mother laundered another girl's underwear and set it next to her plate. Nobody in this book will ever point at that and I will be thinking about it in six months.

And then the kiss. Which is the best-written kiss I have read in a decade and I want to be precise about why: it's the *held inch.* Randi closes nothing for a full long event — hand fisted in the hair, the other hand with a slow theatrical handful of Vee's ass for Harrison's benefit, Vee the taller one so she has to *lean, has to give herself to the angle* — and in that suspended inch Vee's eyes go over her face the way they've wanted to all autumn and never let themselves, and the smell arrives *from everywhere*, the perfume she's been cataloguing for months "without admitting she was keeping the file," and **it turned in her like a key.** Then the press. Just the press. *And let Vee feel it, and feel herself not pull away.* That's the whole three-book thesis executed in one line of blocking. Randi didn't take anything. She held still and let the girl's own body file the confession.

And it starts as the goodbye kiss — the one Vee's been calling *only Randi being Randi* since the jacket copy — **and then it doesn't stop where goodbye stops.** The slow forming of the lips. The sound coming up out of Vee *lost in the music*, which is the only reason she's allowed to make it. The hips pressing once, *asking.* The tongue that is explicitly **not a demand, a *please***. Salt and lime and under it *the specific her of her.* Her own hand going up into Randi's hair without asking her and **not taking it back.** And the wanting running so far past the size of its excuse that it frightens her even as she sinks — *she wanted Randi's mouth lower, Randi somewhere they could never be in this room.*

A fully clothed kiss on a crowded dance floor and it's hotter than the entire paint chapter. I'm not even insulted; chapter 66 was a gorgeous bravura hour and I loved the cherry and the brush dipped in her and *fresh canvas has to be prepared with gesso* — but it was a man's composition and she lay in it with her hands on a rail. This was Vee's own hand going somewhere it wasn't sent.

Then: **Randi came back a moment behind.** She didn't speak. She drew a breath and *shook her head, blinking.* She was gone too. She was all the way gone. And then the grin snapped on and out came *oh, that was **perfect**, did you see him, he's going to be thinking about that for a week,* and the mouth to the ear — the same ear, the same warm place — and **"You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it."** And a swat on the arm. Fond.

That's *ticklish, girl, don't you dare weaponize that* with the stakes raised a thousandfold. That's the drowning move at full power, and it is the exact same instrument she used to save Vee in chapter 51 — tell a true thing that is not the thing — turned around and used to wall off the one true thing in the room. She handed the night back *neat and folded*, and Vee took it **because it was the only thing held out to her,** and heard her own voice say *god, his face* in a register that knows how to do this even now. Both of these women have now lied the same lie in the same breath with the same body language and neither one of them can see that she's looking in a mirror.

And the last paragraph is perfect and merciless: *her body had not been told.* Still ringing. Still sure there was more coming. Nowhere to put it. *She knew she wanted to do it again so badly her hands were not quite steady, and that there was no version of the night in which she could ask.*

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: THE BRACELET IS CALLED LOVE AND IT DOESN'T COME OFF. And: she chose. She said it to Brooke's face. In front of the girl. And the girl asked her what she'd chosen and she got six seconds of the truth before the cover story caught up.

What I'm scared of now, in order: Brooke heard *I chose* and Brooke is the only person in this book who has ever sent a bill. Lauren Pike is a name with a vacancy attached to it. Randi has one week before the Valentine's hall, where Sheri — who has hugged her hello, who *does not care what the redhead has to say*, who is out and loud and the one person on earth with a license to say the plain thing at Pace's own table — will be standing in the same room as all three of them. Sheri is going to look at Randi looking at Vee for about four seconds and then there is going to be a sentence. *Bring your opinions.*

And the half-inch. Vee couldn't close it alone in a sealed room in chapter 60 with her own fingers in front of her mouth and *nothing in the room told her why.* Tonight Randi held an inch open in front of her mouth and Vee's lips **moved toward it on their own.** She can close it when Randi's the one holding it. That's the whole answer and she still doesn't have the question.

Also, quietly, from 63, which I'm still carrying: *Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else.* And Vee crossed the kitchen and held him the way Sheri had, whole weight committed, and didn't say sorry. I did not expect this book to put a hug on me and make it the biggest thing in the chapter.

One friend. Three people who have each, separately, decided what the others can handle. Nobody has ever been in a room together with the lights on.
