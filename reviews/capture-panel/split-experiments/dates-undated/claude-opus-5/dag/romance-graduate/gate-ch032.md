REACTION

He plays air guitar.

That's the thing I'm going to be insufferable about. Two books of the most composed man I have ever read — the hand tools in painted outlines, the two towels, the glass held level while he explains that nobody did anything wrong — and the chapter opens with her looking in his own kitchen window like a thief and catching him with no shape to it anywhere, both arms doing something nobody should do sober. He's twenty-two. He has never once been twenty-two on the page before. She put her hand over her mouth and kept it there until she had herself back, and so did I. And then he answers the door and *it costs him to stand there not put together* — that's the line. He'd rather be caught naked than caught unarranged.

And then she hit him and he flinched. "She had just watched it flinch." And she found out she was faster than he was — quick and light while all that mass had to gather and come around like a ship. God, that's good. Two books of being picked up and set down where he wants her, and she gets an afternoon of open floor and discovers she can make his hands close on air. She flashed him to make him *run*. She has never in her life done such a thing and some small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it — that's the mirror again, the burgundy again, the woman she keeps authoring on purpose and then watching land.

Now. The thing I have been grinding my teeth about since the practice room. Whose this was going to be.

It's hers. The book gave it to her and I want to lie down.

Because Randi's story *did* come — it rose up in her like something she'd swallowed, bent over the edge of the bed where she couldn't see him: would she have to count out loud, was that the theatre of it, the hand coming down hard and then harder. Randi put that in her as dread, and dread is what it did. And then "what came was nothing like the story." He was quiet. He gentled her first until the skin started, on its own, to want the thing it was afraid of. The first one was a tap, *a question*. He soothed after every one and kissed her after some of them. No counting, no audience, no humiliation, nothing of the mouse who roars. Randi handed her a script and he didn't use it, because he never had it, because *he kept the kitchen to himself.* He kept it. That's the race I said I was watching and he won it by doing nothing at all — by being a man who doesn't hand over the one thing that isn't his.

And the bet. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." I actually said *oh* out loud. And then the hand going up slow with his eyes on hers so she had nowhere to put her own, and "Don't" — and the hand is *gone*, not a pause, gone, palm turned up and away like a man showing empty hands. That's the whole engine of this series in four words of stage direction. Because then: what came back in was not relief. She'd shooed it away and she wanted it back. "Yes." "Yes?" "We find out." Nobody talked her into anything. The removal is what made the yes possible, and the book knows it and doesn't say it, which is why I'm still here after thirty-two chapters.

"Slow. For me." And the thing that turned over in her was that *he had asked* — it was hers now, give or keep. Every step further is hers, says the jacket, and I'd stopped noticing that line and now I can't stop seeing it.

The finger. The whole length of it coated, gleaming, "more of her than she would ever have admitted she had in her" — and the shame doubled, the wet and then the shameful plenty of the wet, the thing she's spent her life keeping secret from herself. And he looked at it the way he looked at her under the tree when she said she didn't kiss men she'd known a few days. *Like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good.* That's the book. That is the entire book in one gesture and it took them thirty-two chapters to earn it and it earned it. And her tongue touching her lip before she knew it meant to, and then "It's not," she said. "That isn't." She doesn't even have the rest of the sentence. He raises his eyebrows and says *nothing at all* and does not move the finger from where it is between them. I have read hundreds of these men. Not one of them has ever known when to shut up.

"She could not have said which one of them she was lying to." Thank you. Thank you for not explaining that.

And then — juice. He hadn't planned any of what came next *and that was the thing that undid her*. Sweet and cold and faintly ridiculous, held to her mouth before he did anything else, and then her head in his lap and her hair off her face over and over and *wonderful, wonderful*, quieter each time, said like a plain fact he wanted on the record, until the word and his hand were the same thing. Treasured. The jacket's word, and she got there, and she's never been there before in her life.

Then the scrunchies. No note. A whole form of them, deep green with white dots, the kind you find online and never in any store in town, and *more of them than a person could go through quickly, far more, and the plenty said the thing no note would have.* Monday she cried over her hair in his bathroom. He got on a computer. He said nothing. He lined the dress and never told her and she found it with her hands, and now he has bought her a quantity of time and left it on the counter unsigned. He gives her everything but a word — and then finds ways to say the word in objects. I'm wrecked and I'm also suspicious of exactly how comfortable that arrangement is for him.

Two small things kept me from pure bliss. The mirror soliloquy went on a touch long and "My Victorian LARPing" landed oddly in her mouth — it's funnier than she is, and the register doesn't match the girl from the church basements. And the "I don't, I don't enjoy this" denial ran one beat past where it was still working. Both survivable; the first one actually made me blink and look up.

What's screaming at me is the ending. He makes her a curry — toasts the spices, a dozen vegetables, two Chinese beers — and it looks like every gravy-covered dish of her childhood and tastes like none of them, which is the whole thesis of this relationship served in a bowl. And then the chapter just stops. No Randi. Not a thought of her. Which means it's Friday, and tomorrow is Saturday, and Saturday is a brunch table where nothing is too intimate to tell, and the woman across it planted this with a date on it and has been waiting for it to come back to her. Vee has the one thing that is entirely hers, uncorrupted, given and received in a room with no witness — and she is going to carry it into that restaurant like the shoe box held up under her chin with both arms. I don't think she can keep it. She has never kept anything.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: HE PLAYS AIR GUITAR. and then: he didn't use Randi's script. he didn't use it. and then, twenty minutes later: she's going to tell her everything tomorrow isn't she.
