Okay so first: no Randi. Not once. Not a flicker, not a comparison, not a *what would Randi make of this* — and I noticed it on about page two and then couldn't stop noticing it. Six chapters of that woman running the inside of Vee's head like weather, and then a gravel road and a quiet meadow and she's just *gone.* I spent the first third of this chapter braced for her to show up in Vee's mouth or on the back of her eyelids and she never did, and I can't decide whether the book gave me a holiday or set a trap. Both, probably. This book doesn't give holidays.

Because here's the thing I'm going to be chewing on all night, and it's four sentences at the very end, after the sex, after the fire's gone down, said into her hair while his hand's on her breast like he can't get over it: *Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?* And she says yes. And the book tells me exactly how she said it — *easy and breathy and barely attended at all, the way you say yes to something small* — with her hand already around him, her mind already on the next thing. **That is the wax all over again.** *I'd like you waxed. By the weekend. Bare.* — *Will you?* — yes, out ahead of her, before she's gotten back down the sentence to *why.* He asks for the thing itself, clean, leaves the reason blank, and she fills the hole with yes and does the arithmetic later alone. Except this time what she just signed is a **reporting obligation on her own fantasy life**, and I have read what's in there. A week ago she locked herself in a restaurant bathroom with her best friend holding her phone and came standing up against a sink with *what would Randi taste like* in her head. She has now promised to tell him. And she promised it on the one night it was honestly, wholly, *you, you, you* — which is precisely what made the promise feel like nothing to give.

I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: HE JUST PUT A TAP ON HER DAYDREAMS AND SHE SAID YES WHILE SHE WAS PUTTING HIM INSIDE HER. He didn't need the story in 68 either; he had it from Randi across a pillow. He needed it in Vee's own voice. This is the same man, one deposition later, moving from *tell me what happened* to *tell me what you want, as it occurs, standing order.* And I still can't tell you whether it's the most intimate thing in three books or the next floorboard, and I am not even slightly interested in the book telling me.

The blindfold scene. Long. Too long in the middle — there's a stretch where the author actually writes *It went on like that, and somewhere in it she stopped being able to keep track,* which is the book admitting it's going to summarize its own ceremony, and that's where my thumb got restless, because this is now the third elaborate ritual administered to a held-still Vee in eight chapters: the brush, the balm, the mirror. The grammar's the same every time — he arranges, he withholds, he never says why, she surrenders and learns something about herself she didn't ask to learn. It works. It keeps working. But I can feel the shape of it now, and feeling the shape is the beginning of the end for me with any book.

And then it earned itself back about four separate times. *"It overfills my hand and spills past—"* and her thinking *here it comes* — the one word she's waited her whole life to be hit with — *"—and the spill is the part I want most."* The exact thing she hides, named and wanted in one breath, before the guard knew to shut.
