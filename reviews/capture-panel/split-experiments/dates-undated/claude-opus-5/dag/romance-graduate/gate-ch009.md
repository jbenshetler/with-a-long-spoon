REACTION

Okay so I need to talk about the apple.

Because I've been carrying it since chapter one and this chapter just picked it up and handed it to me. He sliced an apple thin for Randi because she'll only eat thin slices, and laid the cut sides up because she eats faster that way, and she sat on his lap and did bite-kiss-bite and never once addressed the fact that she was starving. And now here's Vee, and he says *may I choose*, and he orders a table's worth of food in words she can't read, and she eats — genuinely eats, without the little supervisor in her head taking notes — and the hollow just keeps opening. Same man. Same technique. Two completely different hungers and he's matched both of them exactly. I don't know whether to find that gorgeous or to feel sick, and the fact that it's both at once is the most alive a romance has made me feel in about two years.

And then: *Let's go feed you.* He said that to Randi at the end of the quad chapter, walking away from the girl he'd just pointed her at. Now he's feeding the girl. I would absolutely text a friend at 1 a.m. with just those four words and a skull emoji.

The "May I choose" landed on me physically, which is embarrassing to admit about a menu. But that's the thing the blurb promised — *choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done* — and this book keeps proving it from the inside instead of announcing it. He asks. She says yes and please. Three words and a page-long page of foreign nouns she can't pronounce, and she sits there with her mouth watering at promises she can't read. *Wanting things I have no names for.* That's not a sex scene and it did more to me than most sex scenes do. Then the tea in the little brass cage, made so you can hold it when it's too hot to hold — I know, I know, it's a metaphor with a handle on it, but it didn't nudge me in the ribs about it, it just set it down and let me find it. Same with the right hand, never the left, and her clocking it as one more thing he knows that she doesn't. The book trusts me. God, that's rare.

The kiss. The kiss is the best-constructed thing in the chapter and I want to be annoyed about how well it worked. She says the rule out loud — *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days* — and he doesn't argue, doesn't lean, doesn't do the thing every alpha on my Kindle would do, which is wait exactly two beats and then kiss her anyway to prove his desire. He smiles like she's told him something fine about herself and starts walking. And it's her feet that don't move. The hand going taut between them. *I'm making an exception.* And then — this is the part — she kisses him small, he answers exactly as much as she brought and no more, and eases back and leaves the last inch where it is. She has to come get it twice. He will not take an inch she hasn't handed him. I have read hundreds of first kisses and I cannot remember one where the man's restraint was the hottest thing on the page.

Which is why the dread is so good. Because he's doing all of this *honestly*. That's what's killing me. He's not faking the attention, he's not faking the care, he's not faking liking her — the not-handing-Randi-the-true-thing-she-said-on-the-froyo-walk, "it was hers," that was a real line and it cost him something. He's being completely, courteously, exactly himself, and it's still a plan. And Randi came to his house to drink wine out of his mouth and make him *tell* her about the girl eating too much froyo, and her thighs went tight before her face caught up. Nobody in this is lying and it's going to be a catastrophe.

Cassie, meanwhile. "That's not a boy, that's a man" — fine, lovely, but I haven't forgotten that she said *Goodbye, Vee*, the whole flat word, for a two-hour coffee. I'm keeping that. Cassie is the only person in this book who sees straight and the book keeps putting her slightly out of frame, and I don't think that's an accident.

Only thing that made me blink: this is the second time Vee has had a transcendent hour and come out of it unable to reconstruct a word of it. It worked beautifully at Chi Latte — *the leaning-in was the whole content* — and here it's "she couldn't, later, have laid out what they talked about." Once is a character trait. Twice starts to feel like a way of not writing the conversation. I want, at some point, to actually hear what these two say to each other for twenty minutes.

But that's a quibble I'm raising because I'm invested, not because I'm leaving. I want the phone call. I want the next dinner. I want to know what Randi does with the fact that this girl is going to be real.
