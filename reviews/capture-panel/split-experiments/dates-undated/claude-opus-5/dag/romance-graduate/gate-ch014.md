REACTION

Okay, so: she told Cassie, and now she's telling Meg and Kayla, and I was there for the hike. That's the third pass over the same forty-eight hours and the first time this book has made me feel the shape of its own machinery. Meg says *you keep getting to it and going soft and skipping the middle* and my eye went flat, because that's the author leaning over and telling me we're going to do the shirt again. I didn't need the shirt again.

Except. She doesn't tell them the shirt. She tells them *about* to kiss him, she tells them hypothermia, she tells them the cop — she skips the part where she came up on her knees on that bench and undid six buttons with his eyes on hers. She gave that to Cassie and only to Cassie. Nobody in the chapter notices the edit and the book doesn't point at it, and that one withholding is what kept me in the room. She has a version for the gold dining hall and a version for the girl in sweats with the window cracked two inches. I felt that sort out her whole social world in one stroke.

And Randi is not at this table. Randi is nowhere in this chapter. Vee has two friends who aren't in anybody's plan, who feed her fries and ask normal questions, and Meg — level, dark-haired, eating like she has all night — says *everybody's different at the start, I said it about Danny, word for word.* That's the only adult in this book besides Cassie and she got one line and Vee let it slide right off her. *It didn't catch on anything.* That's the scariest sentence in the chapter.

New things I ate up: he lifts first thing, he writes six hours and stops because his writing's no good after that, he's an actual powerlifter, and Vee delivering his flat voice to the table — *my writing's no good after that* — with the pride not even hidden. That's how I like getting a man, secondhand, in the voice of a girl showing off. And *twelve.* She didn't have to count. Twelve days and one kiss and she's climbing the walls over being asked what she wants for dinner, and she can hear it sounding like nothing, and she says so. That's the interiority I came for — a girl who can audit the ridiculousness of her own condition and is in it anyway.

Then: *would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock.* Nothing else. And Kayla, who is an idiot and exactly right, says there's no meal at two, he wants your afternoon, bring a change of clothes. Here's what the table doesn't know and I do, and it put a cold hand on the back of my neck: that house has a bench in it. He built it. It hides under a tabletop. I have read what happens on it. She's getting her hair done Saturday.

What I'm watching: whose idea was Saturday. He invited her the day after Randi came over, took what she wanted, said *I want us*, and then left for the first time ever and drove off down the gravel — and the next thing that happens is a door and a time and no reason given. Nobody in this chapter knows which of them that invitation came from. I don't either. That withholding I don't mind at all.
