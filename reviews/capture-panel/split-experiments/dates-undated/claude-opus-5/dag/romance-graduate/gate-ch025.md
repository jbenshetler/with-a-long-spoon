Okay so first: twenty-four chapters of this man being unimprovable and the book finally cracked something, and it cracked it from the *inside* of her, not from outside. I was braced. The second that blonde came off the rail with her hand on his forearm I thought *oh no, here it is, here's the shelf* — the Other Woman With A Line, the misunderstanding you could clear up with eleven words, the thing I've DNF'd forty books over. And for about a page I was reaching for the book's throat.

But that's not what it was. The blonde didn't plant anything. She lit a fuse that's been lying in that girl's chest since before she had breasts. *A man like that has girls, and you'd be one of them* — that's the mother, arriving one beat behind the good feeling, exactly the way my notes say she always does, and this is the first time in the whole book the voice *won*. Every other time Vee walked through it: the stoop came undone, the shame crossed into wanting, she aimed him at herself. This time it closed her and she used it as a weapon. *Three dates, and she'd spread her legs like a slut.* She turned it on herself and then fired it at him. The book didn't manufacture a conflict. It let one mature.

And God, *Does your service include getting the girls home safe from here, too* — the venom in the italicized word, and her hearing it "the way you hear a glass go past the point where you can still catch it." I have been that woman. I have heard that exact sound come out of my own mouth.

His answer. *Yes. Sometimes. When it's required.* He doesn't defend himself. He doesn't explain. The good mood drains off him, he gets told he isn't needed, somebody puts a hand on his shoulder, he shakes his head, and he sets a beer he never drank on the bar and walks out into the cold. That's the first time Vee has hurt him on the page and he didn't manage her out of it, and he didn't earn a single point off her for it either. First time this book has let him just be *hit*.

The dance is the whole novel in a body and the book had the nerve not to tell me so. *Give me something to push back against, otherwise you cannot feel my lead.* *You're holding yourself out — that's what gets you stepped on. The space you're keeping. Close it and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours.* *Don't think. Just feel the lead.* That's the consent architecture and the grooming architecture written in one hand, same words, and it is HOT on that concrete pad under cold field lights, and it's also the most frightening thing in the chapter. And then she gets it — *There it is,* quiet — and the thing that got me is she said nothing back because *she was busy.* I don't know when I last read a woman too absorbed in her own competence to answer a man.

And then she brushed the real question. *How much of this was Pace, and how much was the Pace she'd been building out of wanting him, wish laid over man so neatly she couldn't find the join.* Closest anyone's come. And he turned her and the floor ate it. Of course it did.

Two things have me sick. One: the shirt. She rationed the smell of him until it went stale and wrong and then she washed it, hating it, and it came back smelling of *fabric softener and no one*, and she put it on anyway and lay down on top of the covers. That's not a detail, that's a whole grief.

Two: Cassie three feet away, breathing easy, who would have sat up for this in whatever she was sleeping in — and Vee standing outside with the phone in her hand not calling, because *there'd be questions after.* That's the subtraction I've been dreading since chapter twenty. And then the last line of the chapter is she's going to *Randi's.* Of every door in her life she picked the one that opens into the house. Because Randi is free to tell and Cassie costs something. And Randi is going to be magnificent at this — is going to fix it, warmly, completely, and hand Vee back to Pace repaired, and the repair will be the thing that owns her. *Cool embarrassment is just information.* She's getting more adult by the chapter and using every inch of it to walk further in.

Also: Randi already knows the whole dance crowd scene is coming at her from both ends and she's going to hear Pace's side in that bed. Whichever version he gives her.
