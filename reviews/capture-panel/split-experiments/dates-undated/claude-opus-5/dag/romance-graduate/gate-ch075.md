REACTION

Okay. I had to put the Kindle down on my chest and stare at the ceiling for a second, and not because of the clamps.

Here's what I actually can't get over: Vee had a *whole thing planned.* Fancy clothes. A surprise. And Randi poured four mimosas into her best friend, drove her home, **took her out of the fancy clothes**, tucked her in, left the water on the nightstand like a saint — and then drove out past the pavement and got into Vee's place in Vee's bed on Vee's green sheets with nothing on and *grinning.* She didn't just take the afternoon. She took the surprise Vee had built. She stole the gift. Seventy-five chapters of this book being about what gifts cost, and Randi reached over and lifted one right out of the girl's hands while the girl was unconscious and grateful.

And then — *and then* — the phone call. Vee on the line small and hungover and mortified, saying *Randi was so good to me, I don't know what I'd do without her,* while Randi is under the sheet taking the night off him with her mouth. I have read four hundred of these. I have never been handed anything that cruel with that much tenderness in the staging. Because it *is* tender — she isn't marking him, she's **erasing herself.** Going over every inch to wipe her own night off him, cleaning herself away trace by trace, handing him back washed, putting herself *under* the other girl. That's not a woman winning. That's a woman who knows exactly what she is in that house and is making the bed for someone else while she's still in it. And she's turned on by doing it. God.

The laundry bookend broke me, honestly. He strips the white — her citrus still in it — and pushes it *down out of sight* and closes the lid, and only then goes back for the green. And at the end he strips the green and puts it on Speed Wash so nothing of her is in the house when Vee walks in. That's the whole man in two loads of washing. He is not a liar. He is a **launderer.** The book didn't say one word about it either time and I nearly yelled.

And I finally got inside his head for this, which is what I've been asking for since 61 — and what's in there is the thing I was afraid of. There is no guilt in that room. None. He thinks about the clock. He thinks about the road. He thinks about whether Vee is past the girls yet. He *works the thought down* and goes back to the woman in front of him. He never once thinks *this is wrong,* not as a thought he argues with and dismisses, but as a thought that simply doesn't occur. The chair where the conscience should be is empty and swept. And he's not cold — he holds her through the sobbing, he gives her the warmth, he means the kiss. That's the horror. Affection and ballast have never been two different things for him, and apparently neither have affection and *this.*

**"She's perfect. For your first woman."**

That's the sentence. That's the floorboard. She asked *is she better than me* — which is a bottomless, miserable question, the kind you ask because you want to be hurt in the right place — and he didn't answer the question. He answered the *want underneath it,* out loud, in the future tense, and handed her the whole thing: permission, prophecy, blessing, plan. She came apart on it so hard her blue toenails clenched into Vee's green. He has now said it. Nobody has said anything in this book for seventy-five chapters and he said *that.*

And the crying. Not pretty, not performed, no say in it, face pushed into his neck, hands finally still — and his read that it wasn't the spanking or the clamps, those were only *the door she went through to reach it.* The trapdoor's under her and she's standing on it sobbing and she doesn't know it's a trapdoor, she thinks it's the floor of being in love with a girl who she believes is playing the same game back. *I have secrets of my own.* Oh, sweetheart.

Also: she's bare. Waxed to nothing. She sent Vee to Sondra and then went herself. They **match** now, under the clothes, and only one of them knows it.

And the thing I keep circling: Randi handed Pace a fake clock. She built an hour that didn't exist and made him race it and gave him *her word* inside a lie. That's his own move. That's *by the weekend, bare,* that's *barely stings,* that's the false floor, run back at him by a woman who got in under his guard. He turned it over once and set it down because she was warm on him. He got managed and he let it go. First time anybody has gotten anything past that man and it was a woman with no clothes on in the wrong bed.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she undressed her best friend and put her to bed and then drove out and got in the bed she'd just been put out of* — and then I'd add, *and he did the laundry twice.*

The one place the book leaned over and explained itself to me was the read-out — *she hadn't come to be serviced, she'd come to be made to pay for it, the punishment was first, she'd built the whole afternoon to earn it.* I had that from the pose, the pitch, the too-bright voice, the handing over of **Red** like a thing she had no intention of needing. I didn't need the paragraph. It's the only slack in the chapter and I'm logging it, because this book almost never does that and when it does I feel the hand on my shoulder.

And "Red" is now loaded in a drawer somewhere for later, and I have put it in my pocket.
