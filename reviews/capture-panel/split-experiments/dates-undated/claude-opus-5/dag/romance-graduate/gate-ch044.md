REACTION

So it finally happened, and it happened exactly the way I was afraid of, and I'm sitting here kind of wrecked.

She *built* that sentence. Weeks of it. Assembled it in pieces, tested it in her mouth, checked it like a load-bearing beam — *is this I-love-you in a shape he can take it for? no? okay then* — and the thing she built was a food metaphor. Of course it was. Food is the whole language he's been loving her in since the dining hall. The curry, the lemon in a fine bright rain, the pizza two chapters ago coming apart on her tongue into countable pieces. *I had been eating crackers my whole life, and you fed me a meal.* That is the most intelligent thing that girl has ever done. She spoke to him in his own dialect, she said the whole thing without saying the forbidden words, and it was *good* — I'd have cried if somebody said that to me.

And he said **"I love how good we are together."**

He took the word *love* and spent it on the arrangement. And then — God — *"What you do — what your body does — I've never."* She handed him her whole interior life, framed as a meal he cooked, and he answered about her body. The one thing she's been quietly, desperately clear about this whole book is *he wants me, with the body attached, not the other way around.* She told Randi that. It was the thing that made Randi's eyes go bright and unfixed and made her say *it doesn't.* And in the one moment it mattered he answered the body.

But here's what's keeping me up: **"He took a breath. He took a second breath. She had not heard him do that before."** And before that, his hand starting two different responses and putting both down before either finished. He heard it. He heard it *perfectly.* A man who can read Vee's pulse off a hand going slack on a public lawn did not miss *you fed me a meal.* He stood at the edge of it, took two breaths nobody has ever heard him take, and then chose the warmest possible thing that wasn't it — and then pulled her in and kissed her hair *longer than usual*, which is the apology he can't say either. The extra beat on that kiss is him knowing. He bought her off with heat and arms because he has nothing else in stock, and it worked, because it always works, because he is so good at this that the consolation prize is nicer than most men's whole offering.

And the *wait* behind her breastbone, and the quilt being warm, and the small wait going "into the body where small waits go." Third time now: *she sat down, she did not find out.* *Had he minded? He never said.* And now the laundry room. Except this one's worse, because she didn't just decline to ask — she tried first, with the best thing she's ever made, and then **retired it.** *The cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech.* She is taking language away from herself. Everything else about this girl has been getting bigger — the long step, the hand out flat, the uncropped photo, the heels down the hall — and this is the first thing she's given back. That's the scariest line in forty-four chapters and it isn't even about sex.

Also, the title. *Seconds.* Second cup already poured and cool enough. Second breath. And her mother watching her take seconds at the table like they might be a trick. Second-best answer. That's tight, and I noticed it without being told, which is what I want from this book.

What I *don't* want from this book: the laundry room. Hers had been about — and his had been about. *The two things had not been the same shape.* I had that already. I had it the instant he said it; the whole porch scene landed like a dropped glass. Then I got three paragraphs walking me back through it, plus *"She had said her best sentence. He had not said the sentence back,"* in italics, plus a tour of the pile where she puts things, *complete with a label for the pile.* This is the book that gave me a form of green scrunchies with no note and let me do the entire arithmetic myself. It gave me sugar on her lip and never once said the word *kiss* about it. And now it's explaining the drawer to me. I don't need the dryer to be a metaphor and I *really* don't need to be told it didn't resolve. Trust me. You've had me for forty chapters; I'm not going to miss this one.

Half a point back for it being in her voice-ish, and for *I do not know what just happened* being honestly the truest thing in the chapter. But that stretch is the first time this book has treated me like the problem.

One more thing I'm carrying out of these three chapters and nobody has touched: MIRA. Vee is now holding the fact that Randi's father's loans are the outlier, that Randi said *cheating bastards* ragged and unpracticed and aimed too close, that Cassie heard it as agreement and will never know — and Vee's solution was to hand her friend somewhere to put her hands and never mention it again. She is now keeping a secret *for* Randi while Randi keeps one *about* her. And her mother touched her collarbone and asked if she was making herself sick, and Vee lied and said *dancing*, and the real answer is a man and a drawer and four nights a week she can't account for. Everybody in this book is being handled kindly by someone who won't tell them the thing.

And still, three chapters after Thanksgiving: **nobody has said Randi's name about that weekend.** The silence is now louder than a scene would be.
