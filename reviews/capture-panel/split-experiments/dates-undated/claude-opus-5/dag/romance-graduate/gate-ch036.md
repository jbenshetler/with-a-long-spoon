REACTION

Oh, Cassie. *Cassie.*

She keeps the window cracked two inches against the building's heat, and the girl who walks in through that cold is a girl who lives in a house where the heat is cranked so high she forgets what she's got on — and the book doesn't say one word about those two facts sitting next to each other. It just puts them in the same doorway. I had to stop and sit in that for a second.

And then: she *told* her. That's what got me. In my head I'd already written the chapter where Cassie finds out by subtraction, where it lands as an empty bed and a closed window and no conversation at all, and instead Vee spun the desk chair around backward and sat in it like a sophomore and talked. That's the posture of a girl who is still seventeen with this woman. The bean bag, the book turned facedown, the dry look held one beat and then *given over, glad* — I would die for her. She's the only one in this book who doesn't want anything from Vee.

Which is exactly why the accounting hurt. Look at what Vee actually handed over: he cooks with intent. He watches me eat. The house is warm. A tomato burned me. That's it. That's the whole ledger. Four days ago she sat in full sun across a tablecloth and gave Randi the bed, the covers, the heels, the stripping, *he checked* — and she gives Cassie baked tomatoes. And Cassie, who is not slow, got there anyway: *there's only one way that burn happens. Not enough on.* Said wry, dropped, never picked back up. She knows her roommate is eating dinner half dressed in a man's house twenty minutes past the pavement and she said one sentence about it and let Vee run right past. That restraint is a kind of love I have no idea how to pay back and neither does Vee, who didn't even notice it happen. Her hand went to the side of her breast *while she was telling it* and she didn't know her hand had moved. He's rewritten her body's manners and she can't see it from inside.

Then the two flat questions. *You don't come home school nights.* Not a question, and Vee opened her mouth to fight it and had to go count a week she'd never counted and found her own bed empty more nights than not. That's the first honest arithmetic anybody's done on this girl's life in six chapters and it came from a roommate in a bean bag, not from the woman who wants every detail slowly.

And *do you love him.* After last chapter, where she lay in the dark and set it down plainly — *she loved him, there, she'd said it where she couldn't take it back* — and breathed *Peter* into a sleeping man's ear. And out loud, to Cassie, it comes out **"Maybe. I think so."** She downgraded it. The truest thing she owns and she filed the edges off before she handed it to the one person who'd hold it straight. I don't even blame her; saying it full size to Cassie would mean hearing it out loud, and out loud it has no word attached to it from his side, and she knows that. But God, it tells you everything about where this girl keeps her real things now. Not with Cassie. Not with Randi either, actually — with *nobody*. Unnamed, hers. Same as the hand print.

*The ones before him were fine.* And then she reached for the word after *fine* and didn't have one and laughed at herself reaching, and put both hands up at all of it. Cassie watching her run clean out of words, "which Vee never did." That's the best sentence in the chapter and it's not about Pace at all, it's about how long Cassie has known her.

And then — *she didn't say a thing against it, and she didn't say I know.* She's entitled to both. She's got the eyebrow in reserve and the *your bed's made, it's the same bed you made* already on the record from weeks ago, and she spends neither. She just says go to sleep, streetlight, some of us have class, and goes back to her book. Cassie is going to be standing there holding the bag when this comes down and she is going to have earned absolutely none of it.

Two short quiet ones back to back and I clocked it — the middle stretch here, the roll-up of how he cooks and how he notices things before she does, I'd already been given all of that and my eye slid. But this one isn't a stall, it's a *receipt*. Somebody finally asked the question. It was the wrong person in the sense that she's the only right one and she's the only one who gets lied to about the answer.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *the roommate asked do you love him and she said maybe and the night before she'd said it flat out loud to the dark. she's hiding the good one now too.*
