WHO'S WHO

**Randi.** Chapter one's "she," and I didn't know it until the last line. Black hair, the startling blue eyes she built her whole adult life around being looked at for, flat oval face with that permanent faint surprise. Twenty-one-ish, sorority, sits on the Standards board (judicial — the book never once elbows me about the name, bless it). Single room as a perk of the seat. Closet of bone/camel/oatmeal with one disciplined oxblood at the end; blue toenail polish that flatters her eyes instead of sorority pink. Eats thin apple slices only. Mother lives on egg whites and tofu, said with a knife in it. Has an accent Vee can't place — long esses, arched a's — which Vee files under Switzerland/Paris/a mother with another alphabet, and which I'm fairly sure she *built*, same as the smile. Has a rehearsed bit ("yes, like the adjective, my parents had a sense of humor") that she deploys on strangers and that Vee has already borrowed off her.

**Pace.** Grad student, math PhD, "more or less" finishing. Small house west of town off 614 past where it goes to gravel, cheap white laminate counters, keeps the heat too high, Steely Dan low in the front rooms, white pickup parked behind Hahn. Builds furniture, including the bench. Not a big man, nothing in his face anyone would catch in a crowd, and the room settles half a degree where he stands. Soft henley, washed work shirts too good for a man who fixes things, boots kept not bought. Says *may I.* Always his right hand with food. Someone named Daphne, long ago — he went in through a door she'd built, didn't respect it, and it cost him more than he knew anything could cost. That's the whole backstory I have and it's enough.

**Vee — Vivienne Thorne, V.T., like the university.** The girl from the blurb. Twenty-one, econ, lives in a dorm with Cassie, dark red hair that sparks copper in wind, snub nose, cinnamon freckles, built on a lavish scale and dressed to cover it. Green-and-white one-piece from when she was sixteen and there was less of her. Did synchronized swimming. Gets to class early, has the right pen, likes applied math — turning a real situation into a structure you already know. Notices clothes, always has. Hears her mother's voice before her own appetite.

**Cassie.** Vee's roommate/best friend. Ball cap, shorts, yesterday's shirt, four words a day, a lab at noon, rec season lungs, boxy frame and no feelings about it. Wades to her ribs and plants. The only person in this book not playing.

**Brooke.** Chapter president. Warm voice like a perfume that stays in the room. Doesn't stand up to stop you. Filed Pace's name.

Sloane, Kinsey — chorus. Meg from Vee's floor, Christine at the restaurant.

WHERE THINGS STAND

Pace put Randi on a bench he built and hid under a tabletop, spanked her, edged her past the end of her own script, and waited until the *please* stopped being the voice she uses on waiters. She came like she never has, cried without knowing it, and then locked his bathroom door — first time ever — looked in the mirror and decided: he can have the face, the one he found is never coming out again. Walked back out smiling and lying with her whole body. Also pressed the sharp edge of a water bottle cap under her thumbnail during aftercare while he thought the night had gone beautifully.

Next morning: four minutes of Brooke's friendly interrogation in the common room, every question landing on last night's bruise. Randi lied about a paper and a coffee shop on Wilson, upgraded him from engineering to math-PhD on Brooke's scale, matched Kinsey's laugh pitch before she could choose it. Went upstairs, walked the room not knowing what she'd come to do, lay down in last night's clothes with her shoes on under a synthetic duvet and did not cry.

Then in his bed he asked, while inside her, whether she'd like to taste another woman — and she came from the question. He refused to count her body's answer. Asked again afterward, clothed-adjacent, calm; she converted it into "only in a threesome, with you," a girlfriend's gift instead of a hunger, and he let the costume stand. Then: a walk on the quad. *I point. You pick.* He pointed at five or six women, she graded them like a sample sale, and then Vee came out of the dining hall laughing with her head thrown all the way back and Randi's stride dropped out from under her. Bare *yes.* He sent her to go get her. She executed a shoulder-check so clean the *redhead* apologized, came back glowing with a name and a class schedule, and asked: *do you think she'll like the bench?*

Randi then: sat next to Vee in stats, charmed her, took her to Chi Latte, hugged her, *love you, girl*, *see you later.* Cassie declined to come and said *Goodbye, Vee* — the whole flat finished word — and didn't fix it.

Study session on Wilson: Pace walks in, crosses the room looking only at Vee, solves nothing for her — asks what her professor said the trick was, writes the ugly formula in the margin and tells her not to memorize it, makes two crossed strokes (Euler's, at right angles: two things that won't give alone become one plain nameable thing), and hands her back the pencil at the exact line where she can do it herself. Asks *what's drawing you to it,* listens to five minutes of econ without touching his phone, gets her number with *may I call you,* leaves without the coffee he queued for. Randi, watching, lifts her cup twice without sipping. Cassie, to Randi's face: he looked at you the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt.

Twenty minutes later he texts about frozen yogurt. Randi overrides the wait-a-day rule. Vee builds a cup that leans, and her mother's voice doesn't show up. *This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo.* He doesn't pretend she didn't say it: *your tart's holding up.*

That night Randi comes to his house with no hello in her mouth, straddles him on the couch, pours his tannin into him with her tongue — one glass, poured an hour early, he never drinks a drop — and makes him *tell.* He gives her the froyo cup line. Her thighs go tight before her face catches up. Then he says *she's easy to listen to* and keeps back the true thing Vee said on the walk, because it was hers, and Randi *sets*: smile a beat late. He reads it as wanting held. She drains the glass and holds out her hand.

Pool with Cassie: catcalls down fifty yards of bleachers, Vee takes them as an entrance, then goes under and the room switches off, hands finding the old scull on their own — she loved this before she had a body to be a problem — and grieves, a little. Cassie, from ten feet off, doesn't ask; shoots a sheet of water at her head instead.

Dinner Saturday. He texts without demanding an answer that night. Shows up showered, shaved, pressed, in boots he already owned. Walks her down a block she's never turned onto. One-page menu she can't read; she says so, funny about it; *may I choose?* She hands it over and he weighs it before he orders. She eats without watching herself eat. Loses his thread and nothing bad happens. Tea in little brass cages built to be held when they're too hot to hold. He takes her hand on the way back, first time, and it lands like resolving, not beginning. Under a tree she says the rule out loud — I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days — he smiles like she's told him something fine and starts walking. Her feet don't move. *I'm making an exception.* She kisses him small; he answers exactly as much as she brought and leaves the last inch there. She has to come and take it twice. He asks her hiking — real shoes.

She tells Cassie everything that night, spins in the bean bag. Cassie: *how much did he give you to drink?* — nothing, nobody did anything to me, I did it on purpose. Cassie: *that's not a boy, that's a man.* Then six days later she tells Randi, at a smoothie bar, up on a high chair in a cardigan in a room full of bare arms. Randi snaps her fingers reaching for his name — *Paul?* Tells a fake catastrophe date story about a lacrosse boy who talked all night, handing Vee the exact measuring stick Pace is built to pass. Gets Vee to say *because of him* instead of *I made an exception.* Says ask and then ravish, low, at Vee's own volume, a beat late. Brooke appears; Randi takes Vee's hand on the white table in front of her and says *Vee's my dear friend.* Vee walks home feeling picked. Cassie, meanwhile, got explained out of the room — "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one" — and Vee felt settled about it.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Pace: I came in cynical and he's dismantled me, which is the problem. He is the first man in two years of my Kindle whose heat is made of *attention* instead of acrobatics — the hands going up her sides three times and turning back before they arrive, the two *pleases*, the pencil handed back, the last inch left where it is. He's hard from the kitchen chair to the end of chapter one and takes nothing, and a lesser book would cash that in for credit. And he is *working* a twenty-one-year-old who doesn't know there's a plan, and he's doing it honestly, with real care, which is worse. He closed chapter one believing the hunger was in Randi all along and he only answered it, and he was wrong by one room. He's the most competent man in this genre and he does not know he just lost her. Also: he's started keeping things from Randi for Vee's sake, one chapter after meeting her. I don't think he's noticed.

Randi: I'm frightened for her and frightened of her and I can't choose. What Randi does the first time Vee says something about Pace that Randi didn't plant — and what her face does when it becomes undeniable that Pace likes the girl honestly, not operationally.

Whether Randi ever touches Vee herself, and whether it's for "them" or for her. I don't think she can let it be for her. I think that's the tragedy.

WHAT'S FADING

The chapter-one bench anatomy — face cradle, handholds, the open middle, the pelvis support — I've lost the engineering and kept only *head down, everything open.* Which is probably all I was supposed to keep.

The pointing-game women are blurring: the mirror-of-Randi ponytail, the sage-and-lavender pair, the beanie girl on the library steps who got a *maybe*, the blazer blonde who got a hard fast *no*. I have the verdicts and the shape of the escalation, not the faces.

The actual calculus is gone except the two crossed strokes and "turn it into something you already know how to do." *Latent variables*, underlined once, going nowhere — kept, because it winked.

The dish names. Baba ghanoush, tabbouleh, the bright green one and the smoke-dark one. I kept the hands tearing bread and the hollow opening wider, not the menu.

Which day was which. Froyo, the dinner, the pool, the smoothie bar, six days later — the sequence is soft. The order of feelings is intact; the calendar isn't.
