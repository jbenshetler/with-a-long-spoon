REACTION

Oh, that's the apple again. He sliced the apple thin for Randi because he'd clocked exactly how much she'd permit herself, and now Vee builds a froyo cup that leans and doesn't care, and the sentence I actually said *out loud* to my empty room was the one about her mother's voice not showing up: *Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, that's plenty, that's enough now. Today there was nothing.* That is the same man doing the same thing to a second woman and the book didn't underline it once. It just let the two scenes rhyme and trusted me to be holding both. After four chapters of being told this is a book about being *seen*, this is the first time I felt the cost of it — because being seen that precisely, by someone with a plan, is being handled.

And I'm so compromised, because it's also just lovely? "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo." I made a noise. The wince after, the *see, pretend* — and he doesn't pretend, doesn't inflate it, doesn't give her a speech, just walks beside her a beat and says *your tart's holding up.* That's the whole chapter in four words: I heard you, I'm not going to make you pay for having said it, the thing you built is standing. I have read four hundred men say "you're so different from other girls" and not one of them did what that line did. The withholding is the romance. He's the most romantic character I've read in two years and he is *working her*, and those are not in tension, which is the thing that's making this book sing for me.

Small things I loved: him paying before she found her wallet and holding the door with his back; "I've thought about most things," said flat, no apology; Cassie's healer line, which is the single most real piece of dialogue in the book so far and tells me exactly what kind of boyfriend Vee has survived. Randi putting her hand out and taking the phone to read it herself — Randi can't not touch the thing — and then *he wants to see you naked*, which is Randi running her own errand through Vee's mouth. The wait-a-day rule exchange is the one place I twitched; it's the oldest beat in the genre and I felt the book reach for a dating-rules bit. But Randi's "the rule's for boys you're not sure about" is a steering wheel disguised as girl-talk, so even that earned its keep.

It's two pages and nothing happens and my thumb didn't hesitate once. I don't know yet if these short ones are a technique or a trick, but after Chapter 5 built the whole table, this one being small and gold-lit and over fast felt exactly right — like the chapter was as short as the walk was.
