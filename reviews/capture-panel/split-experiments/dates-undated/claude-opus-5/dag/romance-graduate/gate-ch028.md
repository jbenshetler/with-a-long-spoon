REACTION

Two pages. Two pages and a bike, and I'm sitting here with my thumb already past the edge of the screen.

Okay. First: *somebody finally said it out loud.* Twenty-seven chapters of me being the only person in the room who knows, and here's a woman on a small frame bike with a bleached ponytail saying *two of them at once, how do you sleep,* and *if that's not already complicated, it will be.* Not a warning from an elder, not a plot device — just a friend running the arithmetic anybody could run, out loud, on a cold road, and then dropping it because it's his business. I didn't know how much pressure had built up in me until it got let off by a stranger. She isn't going to tell anyone. She isn't going to blow anything up. She's just the first outside weather report, and the forecast is *it will be.*

But that's not the part that got me. The part that got me is: **"And the redhead?" "Not yet."**

*Not yet.* Two words and the whole architecture shifts. Because *not yet* isn't *she doesn't know* — *not yet* has a clock in it. He thinks there's a telling coming. He's got it filed as a thing that happens later, as naturally as he said *not tonight* to Vee's hand on his scar. Which means either he's going to tell her, or he's *decided* he's going to tell her someday and is living inside the deferral, which is the most Pace thing imaginable and also exactly how men like him walk off cliffs. And he said it flat, to a friend, in the middle of a ride, without appearing to notice that he'd just set a timer in front of a witness.

And then the thing that actually undid me, the small one: he started talking about Vee and *couldn't stop.* The redhead stopping at the walnut chair and running her thumb down the grain where the arm meets the leg — the joint he spent a weekend on — and *asking him how he got it to do that.* I never got that moment on the page. I got the house tour and the knives and the two towels and I did not get this, and here it is coming out of him sideways to a cyclist because he can't hold it in. *He heard himself going and didn't stop.* And Sheri looks over and says **"Listen to you."** That's it. That's the whole chapter. The book has spent twenty-seven chapters showing me a man who is precise about everything, and the first time we hear him *babble*, it's about a girl touching a joinery detail, and someone else has to point it out to him. He doesn't know what there is to listen to. God.

Also — and I want to flag this because I've been waiting for it — Sheri asked him if he wanted her to not like Vee. *Offered to go to war for him, standing.* And he said *she couldn't take your heat,* which is both a compliment and a dodge, and Sheri was pleased and let it go. That's an adult friendship in four lines. Two years across a handlebar, she remarks on women like the weather, she's faster than him on every climb and has never once let it go unmentioned. I know her. In two pages. I'd read a book about her.

What I'm uneasy about: the ease of it. He got to say the whole situation out loud, to a woman who likes him, and received *I bet it's worth it* and a laugh that went off down the road. That's the only absolution he's going to get and it was free and he took it and they rode on with the cold going out of the morning and *that was the whole of it.* The book does this — puts the knife on the table and walks away. "We take nothing and leave nothing." Now: "that was the whole of it." It is not the whole of it. It is nowhere near the whole of it. Vee is three blocks away putting on a black dress and borrowed cognac heels and tying a scarf she was taught to tie by the other woman in this sentence, and she's coming to his door *tonight.*

The one beat I didn't love: *hell of a backside on her, though,* and *that must be better than your dreams.* I know it's Sheri's register, I know it's established in a line, and the "backside" one actually did work — it's how she confirms she was watching Vee all night, which is also the only outside view I've gotten of Vee alone on that barstool nursing one drink, glaring at the floor, danced with nobody, *left alone.* That hurt in a useful place. But "better than your dreams" is the one line in the chapter that sounded like a line, and from anyone else it'd be the moment I rolled my eyes. From her, over a handlebar, I let it go.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *HE SAID "NOT YET."* And then, thirty seconds later: *he told a woman on a bicycle about a chair joint for five minutes and didn't notice he was doing it.*
