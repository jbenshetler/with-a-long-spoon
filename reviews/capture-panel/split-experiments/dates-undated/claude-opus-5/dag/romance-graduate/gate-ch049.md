REACTION

Okay so the thing I actually want to text somebody at 1 a.m. is: I read Randi's New Year's Eve *first*, and then I read Vee's, and the author knew exactly what she was doing to me. I watched that text get composed — a woman getting out of a stranger's bed, repainting her mouth because she'd left the color of it on his chest, deciding in a hotel mirror that she was not brave enough to lose Vee — and then I got to lie in a twin bed in Ohio under a shelf of little gold synchronized-swimming girls and watch it arrive on the ceiling and be received as *the fairytale*. Every word of it true. None of it the thing. And Vee read it twice for anything that gave her away and sent hers and turned the phone back over to make sure it had gone. Both of them editing. Both of them sending the warmest honest lie they had. God.

And then she does the time math. Six hours ahead, Randi's still up, Randi's off with *a man out of the stories*, something so wild Vee wouldn't recognize half of it — and she's **right**, she's accidentally dead right, and it buys her nothing because she doesn't know the woman in that bed is in love with her and spent the drive down the mountain deciding not to say so. That's the whole engine of this book working at full tilt in one paragraph, and the book didn't explain one inch of it to me. It let me do it. That's the respect I've been starving for.

The thing that actually hurt, though — the two pictures. One photograph, one house, and *she was the one who had divided it.* She scissored her own leg off before she handed it to her mother and gave Pace the whole thing. That's the induction's long step undone by her own hand four chapters later, and nobody made her do it, and she sees it, and she says it flat: *same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it.* And her mother touching her own collarbone. Her mother's hand going to Vee's arm at the door before hello to say she'd gotten thin. That family loves her out loud and has never once fed her and I don't think they know there's a difference.

Synchronized swimming. Of *course*. Lane three on Tuesdays wasn't the first time her body was allowed to be a thing that worked — it was the second. The first time she was in a pool she was in it to be **looked at**, pointed toes and all, in a sport that is nothing but being seen in formation, and the trophies are still up on the shelf over the bed while she lies under them not knowing if the man who looks at her properly still wants to. The room knows Vivienne. The ceiling came down close. *Breathing bigger air for months.* I had to put the book down for a second on that one.

And the fra diavolo. She reaches back for the best night she has — the sheet slipping and him seeing and her not fixing it — to go in there and be warm, and **it turns while she's in it.** The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, and some invented blonde is sitting in Vee's wine light with the sheet coming off her. She can't keep her own night. That's the cruelest sentence in the chapter and it's the truest thing about where she is: the memories were collateral, and the collateral just got repossessed. And the bartender, and *he doesn't sulk, he leaves you the room* — she reaches for all her receipts and they go straight through her hands because every one of them only worked while she knew she was the one. *The floor was gone.* Yes. That's exactly what that feels like and I've never seen it put that way.

Now. Pace. Not a word across the whole break. Nothing. And I keep turning it over and I can't make it come out kind. I know he leaves the room. I know *I don't go back* and *believe what I do* and the whole architecture of a man who refuses to spend a word. But she walked out of his house crying and he said her name once and then let an entire holiday go by in silence, and the one thing he's told her about his Christmases is that there's a woman who comes over because she's alone. He handed her that in November when she was warm and safe and it has now gone off in her hands like a grenade, and he doesn't even know he armed it. *About Christmas he'd said nothing at all.* The silence is the gift and the silence is the weapon and it is the same silence. I don't think he's unfaithful. I think he's about to lose her to a vacuum he built on purpose and will be genuinely bewildered.

Meanwhile — and this is the part that put a cold finger on my neck — the chapter ends with an *inventory*. Randi: a date she can circle, a class, a face that will light up and call her gorgeous. Pace: no hour she can name, no room he'll surely be in. She isn't choosing. She's doing arithmetic, and the arithmetic chose. *And what about Pace?* and **nothing came after it.** That's the whole trap closing and it closed with a shrug, in the dark, in a bed her legs outgrew. Four nights a week with Randi starting the day term opens, and a man she may not currently be anything to. Randi didn't even have to do anything. She just had to be *scheduled*.

Also noting, because I promised myself I'd notice: she knows about MIRA. She's carried it since the glass room and never said it and never will, and tonight she lay there and envied the mountains *anyway*, knowing whose money bought them. She's got a vault now. She's got a vault, Pace has a vault, Randi has a vault, and the three of them are going to put a class schedule on top of it.

Fourth chapter running with no real heat in it, and I clocked that — 46 was two pages of a shirt, 47 was the fight, 48 was Randi's whole cold perfect night, now this. If the next one is another person alone in a bed with their phone I'm going to start skimming for dialogue. But these four have been *doing* something, and the twinned New Year's Eves are the best structural move the book has made. Give me the first day of term. Give me that lecture hall.
