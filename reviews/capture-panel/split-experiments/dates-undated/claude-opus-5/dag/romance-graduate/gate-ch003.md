REACTION

Okay so I have been reading this thing thinking I knew what the jacket meant by "a game Randi and Pace started in bed," and now I've watched it start, and it is so much worse and so much better than I'd built in my head. He asked her a question *while he was inside her* and she came from the question. Not from the thrust, from the sentence. And then — this is the part that made me put the Kindle down on my chest for a second — he *clocked* that her body answered and refused to count it: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." Three chapters in and this man's entire erotic technique is refusing to accept the easy version of a yes. I have read maybe four hundred alphas who would have taken that clench as consent and ridden it to a threesome by chapter five. He waits for the heat to leave the room. He wants her *gathered back into herself, free to weigh it*. And he's still the most dangerous person in the book, because he knows exactly how to get a true answer out of a woman who has spent twenty-one years never giving one, and he's going to use it to go shopping.

And Randi. God. "Only in a threesome. With you." I actually said *oh honey* out loud. She converted the unsurvivable thing — I want a woman — into the survivable thing — I'm a cool girlfriend giving my man a present — in about one second flat, with a hand on his jaw, and he let her, and he saw the whole architecture of it, and the book trusted me enough to not… okay, it did narrate the conversion for me ("not a woman who hungered for another woman, which he could see was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy") and I don't love being handed the key when I already had the lock open. That's my one gripe. But it's *his* read of her, not the author tapping me on the shoulder, so I let it go.

The walk is the best sustained clothed-and-filthy sequence I've read in ages. "I point. You pick." The rules arriving mid-game and her face doing something when she realizes the choosing is hers. Her verdicts — *too expected*, *those two go everywhere together, you'd get both or neither* — she's talking like a woman at a sample sale and she thinks she's being a good sport, and he knows she stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. *She was shopping.* The dance metaphor landed on me physically, the follow leaning into a turn that hasn't been called. And she's doing it while telling him she's being a very good sport. That gap is the entire book and nobody has to say so.

And then Vee walks out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bare, having genuinely forgotten she can be seen, and Randi's stride just *drops out from under her*, and the word comes out alone: "Yes." No costume on it. The one unmanaged thing she's done in daylight in three chapters. I'm sick about it. I'm sick about it in the way I came here for. Because I know from the jacket that this girl is the one I'm supposed to love, and I just watched two people point at her across a lawn, and the awful part is I understood why — the laugh, the copper in the wind, the clothes that were chosen to cover and failed. They're not wrong about her. That's what's going to wreck everybody.

The shoulder-check. The shoulder-check! Half a head of weight so the *redhead* is the one apologizing, hand flying to her own mouth, and Randi's hand staying on her arm a half-beat past the apology. Executed so clean it looked like nothing. Pace leaning on a light pole, hearing none of it, "which suited him." That's a hunting blind. And the little happy footwork on the brick when she comes back, and the squeaked *yes* and then the low bed-voice *yes* in daylight, and then — "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I would text a friend at 1 a.m. in all caps: SHE ASKED IF SHE'LL LIKE THE BENCH. And he doesn't answer. He says *let's go feed you.* Which, after "I can't eat that" about the cheeseburger, after the thin apple slices with the cut sides up, is the closest thing to a love line in this book and it's also the sound of a man steering.

Where I drifted: the big composed-two-centuries-ago campus paragraph — "the whole thing composed two centuries ago by men who wanted the eye to travel exactly this way" — I skimmed the colonnade and the frisbee and the speaker a half-second behind. It's doing thematic work, fine, I clocked it, but it's the one stretch where I felt the prose admiring itself.

Three chapters, three different kinds of pressure, zero wasted scenes, and I still have not been inside Vee's head once. I want that now. I want to know what she thought about the pretty girl who bumped into her. I'm scared of how nice she's going to be about it.
