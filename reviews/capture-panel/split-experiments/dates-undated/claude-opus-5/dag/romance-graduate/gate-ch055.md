Oh, she *lied*.

She lied to Cassie's face for a solid minute and a half and she built the lie with *craft* — the cranberry sweater, the dye bleeding into the good pants, wool, maybe the water was too hot, which is why Sunday morning she was in his t-shirt, which is why he burned the toast. She even gave the lie a *causal chain*. And then Cassie laughed — the real one, the one she never spends on her own jokes — put her head back and laughed at a Pace who does not exist, a Pace who ruins laundry, and Vee laughed with her with both hands over her face, and I sat there with my stomach dropping out because *I know what she was wearing when she drove out there.* Nothing. She was wearing nothing. And the one line in the chapter that isn't dialogue or socks is **"the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody."** That is the book putting its hand on my arm. That's vault number four and it's the first one she built from scratch rather than just declined to open.

And underneath the sweater there's a bigger lie and she doesn't even know she's telling it: **"we *talked*."** They did not talk. I was *there.* She opened a coat on a freezing porch and he got on his knees and then he made her pancakes at midnight and said *I'm sorry, I never meant to hurt you, I missed you so much* — which is lovely, which cracked his voice, and which is still **not the word.** She drove out in December and asked what she was to him and got folded arms. She drove out in January and asked nothing at all, and the month "came down like a tent with the pole pulled," and she's calling that *we talked.* The pole is still out. The tent's just lying flat on the ground now and she's walking around on top of the canvas calling it a floor. That's the exact same move as *she decided she had forgiven him,* except this time she's selling it retail to her roommate.

But God, the bits of this that are real. **She missed the draft.** Cassie's defended two inches of January and she stood in it on purpose, clean and boiled pink, because it was hers. The shower that was *less* — less pressure, less private, less hers, three kinds of conditioner on the little ledge and a suitemate's towel on the hook — and she was happy in it the whole time. That's the thing I was afraid the book would do wrong. A lesser version of this book would have had her come back from that house and find the dorm unbearable, Cinderella-at-midnight, nothing's enough now. Instead she comes back and the ordinary is *good*, the socks and the tea rings and the ticking radiator, a room switched back on at the wall.

And then: **"My mother says thank you for the card."** In the same register as the snowshoes, not looking over. Vee sent Cassie's mother a card. Nobody told me. It happened in the gaps. And her hand stops on the way to the towel and goes to her collar instead, and something goes through her middle *warm and specific, like a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck,* and she will not look at it tonight. *She wants to know when she gets to see you again.* That's a family offering to have her, delivered by a girl who doesn't carry things into rooms she means to walk out of, and Vee says *spring break, sometime, tell her I said hi,* which is not the size of it at all — and Cassie, who can hear the size of things, caps the highlighter and lets it go **the exact moment before it would have needed anything.** That's the second time Cassie has opened a door and politely let go of it.
