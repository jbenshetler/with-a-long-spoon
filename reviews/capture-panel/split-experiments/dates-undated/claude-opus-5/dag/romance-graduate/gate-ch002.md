REACTION

Okay so the thing I did not expect was to be *this* tense over four minutes of sorority girls asking about a boyfriend. I came off chapter one wrung out and vaguely suspicious that the book had spent its whole budget in one room, and instead it hands me a common room at 9:50 a.m. and makes it a knife fight where nobody raises their voice. Brooke not standing up. God. "Brooke was the chapter president and did not need to stand to stop someone in her own common room" — I have *been* stopped by a woman who didn't stand up. I felt that in my spine.

And every single beat of that interrogation is a hit landing on the exact bruise from last night. "How's the paper coming" — she lied about the paper to get out the door. "Where did you end up working" — she was facedown on a bench. "Engineering, isn't it," lightly, docking him a notch without appearing to reach, and Randi correcting her to *math, PhD* like it matters, like she needs him to score. That's the tell, isn't it. She's defending him on Brooke's scale instead of telling Brooke the scale is stupid. And then Kinsey — *short-cute* — and Randi laughing at the exact pitch Kinsey laughed at, matching it before she could choose. "The matching was very fast." That's the whole character in nine words. The man took her apart on a bench twelve hours ago and she can't even own the pitch of her own laugh in her own house.

Also: she's on *Standards*. She sits on the judicial board, and the chapter is called Standards, and the book does not nudge me once about it. Thank you. I was braced for a sentence explaining the joke and it never came.

The thing I'm genuinely suspicious of is Brooke. "Pace," said Brooke, like she was filing it. She's filing it. And the "you're not in one of his sections" question — that got floated and dropped a little too neatly, which to me means it's coming back. Either Brooke already knows something or she's building something. A man with a white pickup parked behind Hahn whose last name nobody knows is a liability to a chapter president with a spreadsheet.

Then the room upstairs, and this is where I got quieter. The walking — vanity, bed, closet, nothing — a woman going to do a thing and arriving with no idea what the thing was. The two notifications she doesn't read and sets facedown. The closet with the one disciplined oxblood at the end of a row of bone and camel, which is the same single allowance as the blue toenails, and I noticed the book didn't point at that either. And then her lying down in last night's clothes with her *shoes on*, which from this woman is practically a scream, under a duvet she can't let herself call inadequate out loud — because the cashmere one is at his house, because she brought it there, because she gets cold after, and she thought about that for two sentences and then went "it was a duvet." That's the best small heartbreak in the chapter.

"*He's hard to say no to,*" in a different key. Yeah. Okay. And the deadbolt she turned on herself at the end of chapter one is holding — she decided *not ever*, and here she is executing it, and the book is honest that it doesn't feel like holding a door shut, it feels like there being no door. I believe that. I've met women who are that good at it.

Where it lost a little of me: I already got the full mirror aria at the end of chapter one — the face, the hum, the key turning — and the second half of this chapter re-sings a decent amount of it. Not crying, breath held, aliveness unnamed. I didn't need the reprise this soon; the shoes and the duvet would have carried it alone. If chapter three is also Randi alone having a feeling she can't name, I'm going to get restless.

And — two chapters in and there is no Vee. The jacket sold me a girl named Vee and a plan she doesn't know about, and I'm now fully gripped by the woman who's supposed to be the architect of it, which is either very smart or a book that's going to make me wait too long. I'll be honest: right now I want Vee *because* I've seen Randi locked in a bathroom. Whatever Randi is going to go do to that girl, I now know exactly what's driving it, and that's terrifying in a way the blurb's "game they started in bed" never was. The planners are not in control of this. One of them already isn't.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: "she went home in last night's clothes and lay down with her SHOES ON and the president of her sorority filed his name and I am not okay"
