REACTION

Two pages. I've never read a two-page chapter and I was braced for it to be a cheat — a mood tile, a breath between the real ones. It is not. It's the bill arriving for chapter 42, and it's itemized.

She *gave him that shirt to re-scent.* She stood in his kitchen and said the most naked thing she's said in this book — *I wore it every night I wasn't here and it went to nothing* — and he put it on over his own shirt and gave it back full of him. That was the warmest beat in fifty chapters. And now here's what it's actually worth at two in the morning: she's got it on over her pajama shirt, held off her skin, *"it hid what it was"* — she's wearing the proof of him in a way that makes it deniable to her own roommate. Then she can't stand the barrier, strips both off, puts his straight onto her skin. And it works. For a while. And then she works out why it's warm.

*It was warm because she was warm.* That's the sentence. That's the whole thesis of this book in six words and the book did not stop to explain it to me, bless it. Everything he's given her — the drawer, the scrunchies, the door opening before she reaches it, the tomatoes, the dress he *sewed* — all of it is in that cloth, she literally inventories it, and none of it reports back. It's her own heat coming off her own shoulder and she's been calling it him. Fifty chapters of being warmed and it turns out she's been standing next to a mirror the whole time. And *"gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him"* — she's finally doing the arithmetic I've been doing since the drawer. He sleeps. She doesn't. The cost is not distributed.

And the phone. God, the phone. Every book I've read for ten years would have had her send the text, and the next ninety pages would be the fallout of a badly-worded text. This one puts her thumb over the empty field and has her discover *there is no text*: nothing she can type comes back as the thing she needs, every version gets answered "small and warm," which is *exactly* how he answers — small and warm and never the word. She's not failing to be brave. She's correctly identifying that the format can't carry it. That's a grown woman's reasoning at 2 a.m. and it devastated me more than any shouted confession would have.

Then she takes it off, and the cold comes down, and *it's worse* — and she stays in it. She doesn't put it back on. She has spent this entire book being warmed by a man and letting the warmth stand in for the answer, and tonight she chooses the cold on purpose, bare, with no one looking. Shame wants a witness, he taught her. Turns out so does being treasured. Nobody's looking and it's just a cold room.

And the shirt on the floor. *"not folded, not set back on the pillow, down in a heap where a dropped thing lands."* That's the first thing she's ever withheld from him and he will never know it happened. She's learned the private room from both of them and this is her first real one.

Cassie three feet below her, asleep, breathing easy, window cracked two inches. Asked her flat out two chapters ago, *is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to,* and got *both*, and didn't push, and Vee was grateful and *also, a little, not.* That girl is RIGHT THERE and Vee lies naked in the dark rather than say one word down the rail. I'm not even mad at her. I just want to shake the bunk bed.

What I'd text at 1 a.m.: *she figured out the shirt was warm because SHE was warm and I had to put the book face down.*

What I'll say against it: this is the third chapter out of four orbiting the same unspoken word, and the cracker-and-meal chapter already told me she'd filed it. If 47 is Vee turning the name over alone one more time I'm going to start skimming for dialogue. I need somebody else in the room — Randi with her MIRA problem and *God, I want her*, or Pace waking up easy one morning and finding out there's a ledger. The ache is load-bearing but it's been carrying for a while now, and I can feel the book deciding whether this is a slow burn or a holding pattern.
