TELL A FRIEND: Okay, so — you know how I've been complaining for years that nobody can write the heat and the real consent at the same time, that the second a man gets intense he starts overriding her and the book starts calling it passion? Somebody finally did it. There's a man in this who asks before he takes and *waits for the answer*, who steps back after every kiss to let her decide whether to come forward, who gets out of his own truck into freezing rain so she can change in private — and he's the most dominant man I've read in a decade, and none of it contradicts. There's a chapter where he measures her for a dress, she's standing on a box in nothing but her underwear dying of shame because her body has given her away, and she squeezes her eyes shut waiting for him to laugh at her, and she opens them and he's craned his neck all the way up to be looking at her *face* when she does. I had to put the book down. The sex is filthy and it's also funny — there's a scene with a silk dress where neither of them can let go of anything and they're both laughing — and it's all from inside her, her wanting, hers. Now the part you're going to yell at me about: there's a setup. Two people picked her. It's on the back cover, you know before she does, and it means you spend the whole book falling for it exactly as hard as she is while also being the only one in the room who can see the trapdoor, and it's *awful*, in the way I think it's meant to be awful. The book knows. It keeps putting the man's perfect careful consent right next to the one enormous thing she hasn't been told, and never once winks at you about it. If it had winked I'd have thrown it. It also does something I didn't expect, which is make the woman who started the game the most heartbreaking person in it — there's a scene in a bathroom mirror in the first chapter that I've thought about every day since. Complaints: the middle goes on a run of debrief-over-brunch chapters and I started counting how long it had been since the two of them were alone in a room, and I could feel it in my hands before I could have told you the number. Also there's a girl called Cassie who is the best friend in the book and gets about nine pages and I want a whole spinoff. Volume One ends with nothing detonated, so go in knowing you're signing up for more. Read the fitting chapter. If that chapter doesn't get you, we'll finally have proven we don't read the same books.
