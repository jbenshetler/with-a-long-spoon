It's called A Polite Invitation, it's literary erotica, and before you roll your eyes at "literary" — nobody closes a door in this book, the heat is real and it's *wired into* the psychology instead of bolted on next to it. The premise is nasty: two people pick a girl out on a college quad and run a seduction on her, and you know about the plan from page one and she doesn't, and the book makes you complicit — you fall for Pace's "may I choose" courtliness exactly like she does while watching the machinery work. That's the gamble of the whole thing and it pays. Fair warning: the first chapter is a full dungeon scene that turns out to be the *other* woman, and the middle has a run of tell-your-friends recap chapters that had me side-eyeing the pacing around chapter fifteen. Push through — the fitting chapter (seventeen, the dress, the pins, the soaked satin — you'll know it when you hit it) is the hottest thing I've read in years and almost nobody touches. And chapter twenty-seven, which is just two people talking by a fire, made me cry over a boy's desk being moved into a hallway. My one worry going forward: the book has to let Vee be *furious* about the con eventually, or the whole thing curdles. If Book Two flinches from that, I'm out. But this one didn't flinch from anything yet. Buy it. Read it slow. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem."
