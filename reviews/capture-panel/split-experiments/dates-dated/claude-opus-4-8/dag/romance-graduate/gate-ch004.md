REACTION

Oh, this is the one I was waiting for — the whole book flipping to show me the inside of the girl who's being hunted, and the writer did not blink. We've spent three chapters with Randi and Pace being so *competent*, so in-control, and now I'm sitting inside Vee's ribs while she does the third minute of not-looking-at-the-door and I want to put my hand over her face and say *don't, honey, don't, you have no idea.* That's the trick of this chapter and it worked on me completely: I know the con, Vee doesn't, and the dramatic irony isn't cheap because Vee's interiority is *good enough to carry it.* She's not a dumb mark. She reads the room at Chi Latte with a sharpness that's almost painful — the floozies, the fabric math, the "I have more than they have and more fabric than any of them, both at once, and did not look at either fact straight." That's a real person. That's the same portioned-appetite thing Pace clocked in Randi's apple slices, except now I'm watching it from *inside* a girl who doesn't know she's doing it. The mother's voice living in her mouth. God.

And the thing I keep turning over: the blank transcript. Randi *really* talked and Vee can't reconstruct a word of it, only the feeling of leaning in. That's the most honest rendering of being love-bombed I've read in ages — she kept the sensation and lost the content, because there *was* no content, it was pure targeted warmth, and the book trusts me to understand that the emptiness of the transcript is the tell. It didn't explain it to me. It just gave me "the leaning-in was the whole content" and let me feel the floor drop.

Cassie. *Cassie.* That flat "Goodbye, Vee." — the whole word, the word for a longer parting — is the best-placed cold spot in the chapter, and I knew exactly what it was and so did my stomach. The one person in the room who clocks Randi and declines to play, who won't take the bit's open door, who says goodbye like she's watching someone get into a car with a stranger. Vee smooths it over because smoothing is her whole operating system. I wanted to scream through the page. That's a *chaperone* the book is removing from the board, calmly, and Vee helped.

The accent detail — the arched a's, the long esses, Vee inventing Switzerland and Paris to explain it — that's a seed. Randi's got a constructed self too and Vee's filling in a flattering origin story because that's what you do for the person you want to be chosen by. There's something real being hidden there and I'm filing it.

What I love is that this chapter is almost entirely *quiet* — no bench, no sex, two girls and a coffee — and it's the most emotionally charged thing yet, because the charge is dread. The want in Vee is rendered as romantic longing and I feel it as a trap closing. Both at once. That's the grown-up thing I came here starving for.

If I have a flicker of worry it's only that Vee's longing is *so* uniformly upward-gazing — queen, chosen, high table, let-in — and I'd like one more edge in her, something that isn't just hunger-to-belong, before the plot asks me to grieve her. But that's me asking for the next course, not complaining about this one.
