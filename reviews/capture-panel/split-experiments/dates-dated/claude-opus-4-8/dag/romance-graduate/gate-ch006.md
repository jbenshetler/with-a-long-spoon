REACTION

Okay so this is the first properly *quiet* chapter and I was braced to be annoyed by it and I wasn't. That's the thing. On paper it's a froyo date — nothing happens, they walk the long way, her cup doesn't tip over. If you'd handed me this scene cold out of some other book I'd have skimmed it for the next sex scene. But I'm not reading it cold. I'm reading it knowing exactly what the froyo is *for*, and that knowledge turned a sweet little date into something I could barely sit still through.

Because every single thing he does that melts her, I've now watched get built. "He asked her things the way her grandfather used to ask things, as though the answer mattered and there was no wrong one, and then he waited through all of it." She thinks she's being *seen*. And I'm sitting here with chapter 5 still in my teeth where I watched him choose the tutor's path on purpose, give back the pencil at exactly the right line, keep himself out of it — technique, all of it, calibrated. And now the yogurt system, "tart on the bottom holds up." He's thought about most things. He has. He thought about *her* for a whole afternoon on a quad with his girlfriend picking her out like produce. The froyo flavors are a bit, a charming little bit, and I know it's a bit, and she doesn't, and that gap is doing ALL the work. This book figured out that the most unbearable thing you can give a reader is a girl being genuinely, gratefully happy inside a trap.

The detail that actually got me, that I'd text a friend about: the cup. "It got away from her — too much, the cone of it leaning. Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." After the apple slices in chapter one, after Vee's whole starved-girl thing in the coffee shop — she overfills the froyo and the mother-voice doesn't come. That's the same machinery he ran on Randi with the honeycrisp. He *feeds* them. He finds the place where a woman has been portioning herself and he removes the portioning and she feels it as freedom and it is the most intimate possible hook. And the chapter closes on "your tart's holding up" and the stack still standing and I wanted to put the book down and walk around the room. He gave her permission to overfill and then confirmed she didn't ruin it. That's not froyo. That's the whole seduction in one paper cup.

What I'm turning over: there's genuine tenderness here and I can't fully tell yet how much of it is real on *his* side. In chapter one he wasn't cruel, he was careful, he respected the locked door. He's not a cartoon predator and the book keeps refusing to let me file him as one. The froyo walk felt real to *him* too, maybe — "he looked pleased in a contained way, as if he'd set something down exactly where he meant it to go." That "set it down where he meant it to go" is so chilling and so gentle at once. The blurb told me how real it's going to get for each of them. I believe it. That's what's keeping me from resenting the quiet.

Only ding: this was light. After the bench and the pointing game I could feel the book downshift, and I let it, because it's a bridge and I know a bridge on sight and this one earned its place. But it's spending its credit from the earlier chapters. I need the triangle to start *touching* again soon — I want Randi and Vee in a room, I want the long lunches the blurb promised, I want to watch Vee start telling Randi everything. One more pure-sweetness chapter and I'll feel the stall. This one, though — no. This one I'd turn the page on right now.
