WHO'S WHO

That's the rot, dead center, wearing a conscience. Daphne holds and gets worse. But — and this is the thing the book won't let me put down — he did air guitar. The worst air guitar, no shape to his arms, in an empty kitchen with "Thunderstruck" beating the house, when nobody was due for an hour. He flinched when Vee hit his thigh — the wall flinched, first thing she ever landed on him. He gave her the top drawer and folded his whole ordered self into one below it. Tender and cold in the same hand, always, and I've given up trying to separate them because the book keeps proving I can't.

Randi — she's the one breaking now, and that's the turn I didn't fully see coming. The machine is confessing and un-confessing in the same breath, on her knees on his couch. Still running the harvest flawlessly — the pastry, the Redford story, the "you only gave him half?" that pried the uncropped photo loose — but the method and the real wanting have fused and I don't think even she can tell them apart now.

Vee — she's crossed over, and the clearest sign is ch.39: she drove to brunch with *no scene to tell.* No dress, no swat, no afternoon that turned — just her whole shapeless total life, because there's nowhere else to put it. The long step, the leg given to a dull warm room on purpose, the uncropped photo she sent Pace keeping what the walk really was for herself. For once she had a locked room of her own. She's becoming free and becoming him and becoming eaten, all in the same motion.

HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM

Randi — my pity has tipped past fear into something almost like heartbreak. She's the one I now think breaks first. WHAT I'M WAITING FOR

The three of them in a room. "When she's ready" has been dangled for forty chapters and I can feel it finally loading. Thanksgiving is the nearest fuse: Sheri's coming, Vee flew home, and nobody has said the word *Randi* over that table — whose table is it, really.

Who breaks first. I used to brace for Vee getting hurt; now I think it's Randi who can't hold it. She's the one grieving, the one who can't finish a sentence, the one who said the true thing out loud.

Whether Vee ever learns Randi and Pace are lovers — and that she was *chosen*, harvested, not just shared. Whether anyone ever says the word for what they are. Whether the scar / "I don't go back" ever opens.

Whether Cassie gets shown the truth before the cut-out is permanent.

Mostly: the wreck. Still reading toward it, bracing for all three now, not against any one.
