REACTION

Oh, this is a *sideways* one, and I didn't see it coming from this angle. I braced for Thanksgiving, for the three-in-a-room, and instead the book slides a stats project under the door and lets the whole floor tilt on four gray letters. MIRA. MIRANDA. And it's not the triangle at all — it's Randi's *father*, the loans, the money that went up while the jobs went down, Randi's own bright counterexample turning around and biting her in the glass room in front of the two people she least wants to see it.

What got me — what actually got me in the chest — is that for once Vee is the one with the information and Vee is the one who *watches the person and not the page.* The whole book she's been the one read, cast, harvested, looked at. Here she does the looking. "She did it before she chose to." She turns from the screen to Randi's face and reads the color going out of it, the breath with work in it, the jaw. And "Cheating bastards," low, with no *these* or *those*, no clause to fold the plural down into strangers — aimed too near to be meant for anyone but blood. Vee hears it land wrong. God, the book trusts me. It doesn't explain the plural. It just sets it loose in the room and lets me feel Vee feel it.

And then Vee *covers.* She slides the laptop toward herself and gives Randi somewhere to put her hands. She doesn't say the name, then or ever. She decides the kindest thing a friend can do is not make her carry it in front of anyone. And here's the thing I can't stop turning over — that's exactly what *Randi* does to Vee. The managing. The giving-her-somewhere-to-put-her-hands. The protective withholding that is also a withholding. Vee just did the Randi thing, out of love, for Randi, and doesn't know it's the shape of the whole trap she's inside. The student performing the master's move without the knife in it.

The title. *The Outlier.* The professor says it flat at the front of the cold hall — the point that sits off by itself, the one you can set aside if you can account for it, invisible if you don't know to look. And Randi IS the outlier. In the data and in Vee's life. The biggest point, off on its own in the white, easy to see if you know, invisible if you don't. The book handed me its own thesis and didn't wink — it let the professor say it to a tired class about tire pressure and PPP.

What I'm suspicious of now, newly: Randi is not clean *underneath* the triangle either. I'd been holding her as the one who breaks first, the grieving architect, the heartbreak. Now there's this — inherited rot, a father who took the money and cut the people, Randi who grew up *inside* the thing and came into the room *certain* she'd complicate Cassie's sad clean story, certain it would exonerate home. She aimed the whole picture at her own county to prove the money did what it was for. And it didn't. She's a girl who believes the story the money was *supposed* to be, the way Pace believes the consent of a woman without the facts is consent. That rhymes. That *really* rhymes. The two of them both running on a lie about what a thing was for.

And Cassie. Cassie, flat, "my dad got laid off in 2020," no handle left out to pick it up by — sitting two feet from the daughter of the loans and reading "cheating bastards" as *agreement.* "That's just the one that's easy to see." She's right and she has no idea how right. She's always the honest instrument pointed at the exact truth with the label filed off. It's unbearable and I love her for it.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: VEE CAUGHT RANDI. not the big thing, a different thing, and she COVERED, and she doesn't know she just learned to do the exact thing that's being done to her. also Randi's dad is a PPP crook and Cassie's dad got laid off and they're all three graphing it TOGETHER in a glass room. this book.

This was quiet and I was never once bored. That's the tell. A bridge chapter that's a bridge over a drop.
