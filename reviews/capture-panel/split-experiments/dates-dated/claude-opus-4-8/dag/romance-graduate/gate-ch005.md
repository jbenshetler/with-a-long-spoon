Oh, this is the one I've been waiting for since the pointing game. Four chapters of setup and now I get to watch the trap close with the mouse narrating. And God, it *works* on me even though I know — I know — exactly what's happening, because I was in the bedroom, I was on the lawn, I heard Randi ask "do you think she'll like the bench?" And Vee thinks a cute stranger wandered over to help with calculus. That gap is the whole chapter and it's delicious and it made my skin crawl in the good way and the bad way at once.

What got me: the tells. Randi's hand on the coffee cup. "Twice the cup came up to her mouth and went back down, and no sip happened." The woman is vibrating. She's watching the thing she *built* happen and she can't drink her coffee. And Vee's brain, so smart about econ and so blind about this, files it as *she's bored, I'm talking too long.* That's the knife — Vee reading every room perfectly except the one she's standing in. I kept wanting to reach into the page.

The math itself — fine, I don't care about Euler's formula, but I care enormously about *how* he did it. Asking what the professor said instead of telling her. Giving the pencil back at the exact line where she could finish it herself. "He had given her the question and given her back the pencil." That's the seduction, and the book knows it's the seduction, and it mostly lets me see it without underlining it — though that long paragraph where Vee analyzes "the path he picked was the path of a tutor and not a mathematician" came *right* up to the line of explaining its own subtext to me. I can see what you're doing, book. You didn't quite over-explain it because you kept it inside Vee's econ-brain, the part of her that reads people's incentives for a living — so it reads as character, not as the author tapping my shoulder. But it was close.

"May I call you?" — yeah. I felt that land the way Vee felt it land. The courtly thing is the hook the jacket promised and it actually works on the page instead of just being asserted.

And then the ending twist of the knife: Cassie. Cassie is the only honest instrument in this entire book. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." Cassie, who gets slid-off-of every day, naming the look with no flinch. And last chapter her flat "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, the word for a longer parting. This book is planting Cassie as the one who sees, and I am now genuinely worried for Vee in a way that has stakes. The horror underneath the sweetness is that Randi and Pace *closed the deal* and then sat there coaching Vee into wanting the phone to light up. "You should go out with him." The predators tucking the prey back in.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *this book is so mean and I can't stop. the girl thinks she got picked by a cute math boy and actually she got ordered off a menu on a lawn two days ago. and the worst part is HE'S the best listener any of them have ever had, that's the trap, the attention is REAL even though it's a con.* That's the thing keeping me — it's not a cartoon. Pace's attention isn't faked, that's what makes it sick.

Heat's low here, deliberately — this is a daylight chapter, clothed, a con in a coffee shop. But the charge is all in the dramatic irony and it's humming. I want the next one. I want the phone to light up and I want to see what Randi tells Pace afterward in bed.
