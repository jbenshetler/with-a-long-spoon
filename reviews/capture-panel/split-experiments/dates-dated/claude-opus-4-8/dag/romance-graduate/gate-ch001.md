REACTION

Okay. Okay. I opened this expecting the hundredth variation on a dominant-man-breaks-in-a-nervous-girl scene and instead I got — the twist is that *she's* the con. The jacket told me Randi was one of the planners, that she steers Vee toward Pace, that Randi is "the first woman Randi has ever let herself want." So I walked into this chapter thinking the woman on the bench was Vee, the mark. And it's not. It's Randi. The architect. The one who's supposed to be running the game is the one coming apart facedown on a bench her boyfriend built in secret and hid under a tabletop. That reframe in the last fifth of the chapter — "There you are, Randi" — I actually sat up. I'd been reading the whole scene as the seduction of an innocent and it was the unmaking of the operator. That's the smartest thing a Book One cold open has done to me in a long time.

And the *writing*. God. The apple. The thin slices cut-side-up because she eats them faster that way, apples on the safe side of the line she and her mother drew with a knife in it — that's three sentences and I know exactly what her whole relationship to appetite is, without anyone explaining it to me. The water bottle he left in advance because she always drinks more than she expects. The cap edge pressed under her thumbnail where it would hurt — he doesn't see it, *I* see it, and the book trusts me to hold that detail and not footnote it. The flinch at "you're beautiful." The lock turning over in the bathroom door, a thing she'd never done before, and him choosing not to knock because of Daphne, whom I now want to know everything about.

The heat is real and it's doing character work the entire time — the edging isn't choreography, it's the mechanism by which her performance apparatus gets stripped away until the *please* comes from below, from the child asking her mother for something she won't get. The distinction between the waiter-please and the real please is the whole scene and it's erotic *and* devastating at once. This is the thing I'm always starving for and almost never find: the explicitness IS the interiority, not a break from it.

What I'm suspicious of / scared of in the good way: that final turn. She comes out from behind the face, it's the most alive she's ever been, and she decides — "not ever" — to go back behind it and never come out again. And he's sitting there thinking it was a remarkably good evening, that he found her hunger and he'll answer it as often as she brings it. He has no idea she just bolted the door from the inside. That's the engine of the whole series, isn't it. He thinks he opened her. She's already sealing. And they're supposedly going to do this *to a third girl*. The dramatic irony is enormous and it's load-bearing.

What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the dom isn't the one in control, the woman on the bench is the mastermind and she just had the realest orgasm of her life and decided it can never happen again because being SEEN is the one thing she can't survive — I'm wrecked, read this." Also: a male POV that is this attentive without being smug, that keeps his own hardness as a set-down fact because "that's not what this night was for" — I didn't know I'd buy it and I bought it completely.

I am genuinely a little thrown that the opening of Book One is apparently Randi-and-Pace *before* Vee, which means the jacket's "best year of Vivienne Thorne's life" hasn't even started. I don't resent that. I want to know who these two are to each other before they go hunting. Give me the next chapter immediately.
