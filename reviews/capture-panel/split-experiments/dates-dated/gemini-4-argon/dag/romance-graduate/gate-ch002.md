REACTION

God, the precision of that sorority room. Every single line was a stiletto slid between the ribs while smiling. "I kept thinking you'd said *Chase*." "I love that you don't care about cars." "Short-cute." And the way Brooke catches her in the lie about the paper ("Where did you end up working?") and then smoothly transitions to Pace, letting Randi know without ever saying it out loud that she knows *exactly* where Randi spent Friday night, and with whom, and what that costs Randi on the house's social ledger. And "The release was the cleanest hit" — dismissing her before Randi can get her footing back. I felt my own stomach drop reading it.

What kills me is how completely this recontextualizes Chapter 1, and how fast it shows us why the jacket premise is going to happen. Last night, Pace broke through the armor and saw *her*, and she was terrified, and now she walks back into the panopticon where she lives and gets subtly dismantled by Brooke because of him. He's a liability to her armor inside his house, and he's a liability to her status outside of it. And then upstairs, when she pulls that thin, synthetic duvet over herself with her shoes still on, and the narrative goes: *It was not — it was a duvet.* She won't even let her own brain finish the thought *it was not the cashmere blanket he wrapped me in, in his warm house*.

She's completely frozen out of her own feelings. So of course she's going to need a buffer. Of course she's going to find a way to stay near him and near that heat without ever having to be the one on the bench being *seen* again, or the one getting demoted in the common room. We haven't even met Vee yet, and I can already feel the trap Randi is about to build to save herself. This writing is razor-sharp.
