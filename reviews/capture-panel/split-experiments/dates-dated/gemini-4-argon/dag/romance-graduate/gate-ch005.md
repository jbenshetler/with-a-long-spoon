REACTION

Oh, this is *delicious*. And so, so dangerous.

First of all, seeing Pace from the outside through Vee's eyes after spending the first three chapters inside his head is such a trip. He's not some six-foot-four alpha hole with a jawline that could cut glass—he has a "pleasant, ordinary face," he's not a big man, and yet he walks across a room with such complete, unhurried authority that the whole air settles around him. And the forearms! And the calloused knuckles from woodworking! And the *May I call you?* Ugh. The competence kink in this chapter is off the charts. The way he doesn't just solve the integral to show off, but gives her the exact nudge she needs and *hands the pencil back* so the triumph is hers? If a man did that to me in a coffee shop I would fold immediately. No wonder Vee is dizzy.

And I love that Vee is actually smart. She isn't failing basic math so a big strong man can rescue her; she's doing the optional challenge problem for fun because she loves the structure of it. The little dramatic irony of her telling him that what she loves about economics is "the gap between what they say they're doing and what they're doing" while sitting at a table with two people running a whole choreography on her—chef's kiss. The book didn't wink at me when she said it, either; it just let her say it.

Because underneath all of Vee's fluttering, my stomach was in knots watching Randi. That whole setup: "the place on Wilson" (the exact spot Randi lied to Brooke about studying at in Chapter 2!), Randi texting under the table while Cassie watches her, the bell tinkling, and Pace already in line waiting for his cue. They engineered this down to the minute.

Except... did it go according to plan for Randi? Because what I was watching at that table was Randi slowly losing her footing. Look at her hands on that coffee cup. Look at her giving her name as just one flat word—"Randi"—without the cute adjective bit, especially right after Vee *stole her bit* to flirt with Pace! (Which was such a great, slightly awkward, human detail—Vee trying on Randi's poise like a borrowed jacket.) And then Randi interrupting to ask a basic stats question just to pull his attention back for ten seconds, and Pace giving her a textbook, clinical three-sentence answer while giving Vee twenty minutes of Socratic tenderness and deep listening. And Randi lifting the cup to her mouth twice without drinking. And then Cassie—God bless Cassie, who sees everything—dropping that bomb at the end: *"He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt."*

Randi is a girl who built her entire identity on being the most looked-at woman in any room. She started this game inChapter 3 so she wouldn't have to be raw and seen by Pace alone on that bench again, and so she didn't have to admit she wanted a woman on her own terms. She thought she was bringing Pace a toy *for them*. She did not calculate what it would feel like to sit two feet away while the man who broke her open looks right past her face as if she's wallpaper, and turns all that quiet, devastating attention onto someone else. Even if it's an act for the setup—was it an act? Pace genuinely liked Vee's mind here. You can't fake the way he listened to her talk about her research.

Randi covered brilliantly at the end with the observation about him forgetting his coffee—she's still playing her part, pushing Vee right into his lap—but something shifted in that chair. This is the second quiet chapter in a row after the heat of the first three, and I didn't skim a single word because the psychological tension at that table was tighter than a drum. Now I need to see what happens when Pace calls Vee—and what happens when Randi and Pace are alone again after this.
