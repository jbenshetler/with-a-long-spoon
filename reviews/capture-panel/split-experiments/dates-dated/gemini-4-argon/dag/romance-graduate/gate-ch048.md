Oh, Randi. My chest actually hurts.

First of all, jumping straight from Vee walking out into the freezing dark on December 11 (*"Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me"*) to *New Year's Eve* in Gstaad with Randi?! Nearly three weeks just vanished into thin air, and I didn't even get a second to panic about what happened between Vee and Pace after she walked out because this chapter grabbed me by the throat from the very first paragraph. Look at Randi hugging her father at breakfast and *"eating her revulsion"* after finding out in the library what his PPP loans actually were! Look at her hiding her locked white-gold submissive bracelet out of the light while her mother's diamond tennis bracelet flashes across the table, ordering smoked salmon with lemon and capers on a single slice of dark rye while her mother eats egg whites. Every single detail of her family is so cold and sharp you could cut yourself on it.

And for the first two-thirds of this chapter, I was holding my breath watching her try to escape into the normal, rich-girl fairytale. Because John isn't even a jerk! That's what makes this so devastating. In a lesser romance, the "acceptable" rich guy she meets on the slopes would be a smug asshole or bad in bed so she has an easy excuse to run back to the brooding hero. Instead, John is charming, funny, a legitimately great skier who doesn't need to show off, buys her champagne, takes her to a Michelin-starred white-tie ball, dances like a dream, and conjures a literal horse-drawn sleigh in the snow. When she was comparing him to Pace—how Pace's lead on the dance floor takes *work* and listening, how Pace makes her say what she wants out loud every single time, whereas John just assumes and steers and makes it easy—I felt that temptation in my bones. She's exhausted. She wants the easy life where she doesn't have to beg a man in the dark to say something nice to her, or watch him fall in love with the girl they picked together.

And then the transition happens: *"His room was high in the hotel with the mountains black in the window, and he drew her in, and she went. / Afterward she stayed against him."*

Normally a skipped sex scene is my ultimate DNF trigger, full stop. If you'd told me a chapter was going to skip right over the New Year's Eve sex, I'd have thrown my Kindle across the room. *Except here the skip is the entire point.* Because she wasn't even there for it! Of course it had. Nothing had happened to her eyes."*

WHAT A LINE. *Nothing had happened to her eyes.* I had to stop and reread that three times.

And then the truth comes out in the bathroom mirror, and it's not even really about Pace—it's about *Vee*. *"Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best... And Vee. Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of, and her whole body knew it, and she knew exactly what it was."* She isn't even talking about confessing the Pace secret to Vee over eggs; she's talking about confessing that she is *in love with Vee*. *"Vee was her gorgeous."* She's too terrified of losing the Saturday brunches to say it plain, so she's waiting for Vee to arrive at it on her own while hiding inside the double meaning of her words.

And then Vee texts her at midnight from Ohio—*"i'm here if you want to talk. or not"* (Wait: does Vee know Randi is hurting because of the PPP loan thing from Chapter 41?! YES, that's why Vee added *i'm here if you want to talk. or not*! Vee is still quietly taking care of Randi over her dad's fraud without ever making her say it out loud!)—and Randi texts back the fake rich-girl fairytale with the one real line tucked at the bottom: *miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋*, and crawls back into bed with "the perfect nothing" to borrow warmth she can't make herself.

This book is operating on a completely different level. I am gutted for Randi, I am dying to know what happened with Vee and Pace between December 11 and December 31, and I cannot turn the page fast enough.
