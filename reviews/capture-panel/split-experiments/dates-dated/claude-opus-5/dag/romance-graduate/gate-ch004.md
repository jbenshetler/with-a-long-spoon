REACTION

Oh, *fuck* this chapter. I mean that admiringly and also I mean I feel sick.

Three chapters of being inside Pace's head and Randi's locked room, watching them point at a girl across a quad like she's an entrée — and now the door opens and here's the girl, and she's *lovely*. She's lovely in the specific way that guts you: she got there early, she had the right pen, she wrote the date at the top of a clean page so her hand would have somewhere to be. That line. I've been that girl in that room. And she spent three minutes deciding not to look at the door, which is the most accurate rendering of twenty-one I've read in a long time.

The thing that actually did it to me, though, is that she can't remember a single word Randi said. Not one. She kept the *feeling* and the transcript is blank. That's not a cute device, that's the whole mechanism — Randi isn't talking to her, Randi is dosing her. And Vee walked home the long way, warm the whole way, with nothing in her hands but *love you, girl* and a hand flat between her shoulder blades. I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she doesn't remember what was said and she's already rearranging her life around it.*

And the accent. Hold on. Nobody in three chapters said Randi had an accent. Not when she was begging on a bench, not with her sorority spreadsheet president. Now suddenly the esses run long and the a's come up arched and Vee is building her a Switzerland, a Paris, a mother with a different alphabet. Either Randi is *putting that on* for her — pulling out an exotic for a girl she's hunting, which, given chapter three, absolutely — or Vee is so far gone she's inventing a glamour and handing it over for free. Both readings are horrible. I want to know which and I don't trust the book to tell me yet, which I respect.

Cassie. *Cassie.* Four words a day, declines the coffee without a sulk, and then — "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word. Flat and finished. Something passed over her face that Vee wouldn't have been able to name and didn't try to. I felt the floor tilt. I don't know if Cassie clocked Randi in four seconds flat or if the book is just dangling a thread, but I'm going to be mad if she's only a device. She's the one person in this book so far who didn't perform for anybody and I'd read a whole chapter of her.

The Chi Latte room read is where I decided this writer actually knows what she's doing. Vee scanning for her own place in it — the matched sage quarter-zips, the two bare ones, *floozies* arriving whole and in her mother's voice before she'd even finished looking. And then underneath the word, a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for. The sprinter's cut of the stomach. How low the waistband sat. She could wear what they were wearing. And then mom's voice closes the picture and the cardigan stays where it is — the cardigan she put on over the better shirt on purpose. *The room did not know it was a choice, and she let it not know.* That's the starving the blurb promised and it's doing it without a single hand on anybody. I'm not getting that from the shelf I usually shop.

What I'm uneasy about: the book was *this close* to nudging me in the ribs. "Next time we get into the interesting part — latent variables." Come on. I can see the subtext, you don't have to hand me a laser pointer. And "the queen having decided there would be a later" — I'd already felt that, I didn't need it named. When this book trusts me it's ferocious; when it gets nervous it labels.

Heat was basically nothing here and I don't care, because the dread is doing the work heat usually does. But I'll say the honest thing: I want Pace in a room with her now. Not because I want it to happen — I don't, I'm actively frightened for her — but because that's where this is going and the book has made me complicit in wanting to watch. Which is, I assume, the point, and I'm not thrilled about how well it's working.
