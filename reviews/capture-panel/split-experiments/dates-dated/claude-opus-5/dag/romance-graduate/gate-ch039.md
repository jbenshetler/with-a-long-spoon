So the blurb's line finally arrived in the book, and I want to be annoyed about it and I'm not. *You're just finally hungry out loud* — I've had that sentence sitting in my head since the back cover, and the thing I didn't expect was that when it landed it wouldn't feel like a payoff. It felt like a woman handing a girl exactly the right absolution at exactly the moment the girl would take anything, and being completely sincere while doing it, and it still being the most useful thing Randi has ever said for her own purposes. That's the trick this book keeps pulling and I keep letting it.

But the thing I actually sat up for — the photograph.

He said *never.* She asked him to promise he'd never share her, and he said *I'd never share your photograph*, and she believed him and sent it, and the believing was the brave part. That was four days ago in book-time. And then she hands it across a tablecloth to the one person on earth it must not go to, and she does it because Randi said *babe, these are two weeks old* and *you don't have to show me* and slid the phone back, which is the single most expertly placed hand in the whole volume. Then *you only gave him half?* And Vee — who will not be the girl who flinched, same muscle that signed for the shoes, same muscle that made the second step long on that stage — pulls up the uncropped one and puts it in her hand and says *I gave him all of it.* She broke his promise for him. He kept it. She didn't. And she walked out of there *absurdly happy,* thinking *there was no one else I could have shown.*

I had to put the thing down for a second. Not because it's cruel — because it's *right.* That's exactly what you do at twenty-one with the first friend who takes all of you without flinching. I did that. Everyone did that. The difference is the picture is now in the pocket of the woman who's sleeping with the man in it, and he doesn't know, and I do.

And *showing him your toes, eh?* — oh, come on. That woman went down on one knee on a boutique floor and put her thumb across that toenail and said *mm*, and now she's got the picture, and she made a joke about the one detail she's been carrying since October. The two of them gone pink and delighted together over it. That's the hand on the neck and it's also genuinely two friends giggling and I cannot get a blade between those two things anymore. The book will not let me.

The catch. *Like he wants me. With the body attached. Not the other way around.* And Randi's eyes go bright and unfixed and her breath catches and she can't smooth it over, and she says *it doesn't* — *it doesn't sound unhinged* — low and not quite even. And Vee reads it as *of course it reached her* and goes warm with *pride* that a Saturday of hers could do that to a woman like Randi. God. That's the cruelest reading-over-the-shoulder the book has done since *she had stumbled into her.*

Vee just built a second weekly door and put Randi on the other side of it and told herself it was about a humanities requirement.
