REACTION

Okay so the book just did the thing I did not think it had the nerve to do: it wrote an entire Hallmark movie on purpose, dead straight, no wink, and then stood over it at 1 a.m. and said *see? nothing.* The ski meet-cute with the fake marriage bit in the lift line. The black run. The champagne hütte with the smoke going straight up. The dress bought that afternoon in the second shop. The white-tie ball, the orchestra, the truffle, the countdown, the kiss, THE SLEIGH WITH TWO GREY HORSES. I have read that sleigh forty times. I have DNF'd books for that sleigh. And I sat there going *Helen, what are you doing, I came here for the couch and the mirror* — and then she pays it off with *it had meant no more than a good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon: the pleasure real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared,* and I had to put the thing down for a second. That's the whole genre I'm bored of, served to me as a plate, and then taken away with the explanation of why I'm bored. She didn't lecture me about it either. She just let the perfect man fall asleep.

The bracelet. THE BRACELET. Plain white gold, *locked* on her wrist, kept out of the light at her mother's table, and then it catches on the heel of her hand when John turns her out on the marble. She wore it dancing with him. She wore it to bed with him. Nobody says a word about what it is and nobody has to. Two books in and that's the first hard object anybody in this arrangement wears on their body, and it's on the one who runs the room. I have questions about whether she's off the leash tonight or just out of range, and the fact that the chapter never once has her think about whether Pace would mind is the single most interesting silence in it.

And her mother. Black coffee and an egg-white omelette, all edges and points against her in the hug, exquisite, labeled at the waist — and Randi orders smoked salmon on ONE slice of dark rye, no butter, tea, ice water. There it is. There's the whole *one neat bite, Delicious, black coffee* I've been watching for forty chapters, and it was never a power move, it was inherited, it's her mother's plate with her mother's portion on it. She learned to not-eat at that table the same way she learned the smile she "checked" in the mirror afterward. And the father — rare steak, the business, *how many hearts have you broken,* *keep an eye out for an eligible bachelor* — and she sits there and INVENTS boys for him, because the invented ones were always safer at that table. She's been running a fake life for those two since she was a kid and she's so good at it she can do it over breakfast while eating her revulsion. And I know what's under his steak now. I read chapter 41. MIRANDA, off on its own, up and to the right, and he's complaining about his knee.

What broke me is that she caught herself doing it again with John and didn't notice. The ski camp children with the made-up names in the lift line. The dress for *the story she'd tell later, to a daughter she didn't have yet.* She built a whole fake family at a chairlift with a stranger and then went and bought a dress for it. That's not a woman falling in love, that's a woman with a script she can run on anybody, and she's so fluent in it she can't tell it from wanting something until she's lying in the dark afterward warm only along the one seam where she touches him. *The cold did not belong to the room. It was hers. She had brought it in with her and she would carry it back out.* God.

The dancing comparison is the nastiest, cleverest thing in the chapter and I want to fight about it. John leads like a man taught at twelve, firm, unmistakable, never a half-beat to guess — and she thinks *easier was better, I'm done with the work.* Pace's lead is the lightest pressure, a thing she has to be listening for and good enough to catch, *and it left the rest of the way to her.* THAT'S THE WHOLE MAN. That's *your face said yes before the rest of you got there, that part was all you.* That's handing people the authorship, rendered as a hand at the small of a back. And she's standing there on marble deciding she'd rather be steered, and then three pages later: *Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best.* And *Pace went straight to her. That was the whole terror of him.* Same with *Pace had never assumed anything in his life. Pace made her say it, out loud, every time* — and she *liked* not being asked, for one night, and I believe her, and it's the most human thing she's said. The consent he keeps checking isn't only care. It's work. It's a bill she pays every time. No other book I've read this year would admit that.

But here's what I'm actually sitting with, furious and delighted: she gets to the end of the whole reckoning, names it completely — *something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of, and her whole body knew it, and she knew exactly what it was* — and then decides, in cold blood, in a hotel bed, **not to say it.** *Let the thing keep the shape it was already taking. Let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were.* *As long as the word kept its cover.* Three chapters ago Vee stood on a mat with her coat buttoned to the throat and begged a man for one noun and got *words are cheap.* And now Randi — who has been dying over this girl since October, who can't eat, who can only say it into furniture — chooses the exact same silence, for the exact same reason: because saying it means she might lose the thing. Pace's *believe what I do* and Randi's *let her arrive at it on her own* are the same sentence. Two people with a word in their mouth, and the only one in the book who tried to spend hers walked out into the cold on December 11th and I STILL DON'T KNOW WHAT HAPPENED TO HER.

Which is my one real complaint. Twenty days vanished. Vee walked out that door mid-sentence and the next thing I get is a text from Ohio with a yellow heart and *i'm here if you want to talk. or not* — which is Vee, in whatever state she's in, offering to be the listener. Is she still with him? Did she go back the next morning? Did Pace call Randi that night? Does Randi know her girl is in pieces, and sent back *honestly a fairytale out here* on top of it? *Every word of it true. None of it the thing.* Those two women are now both running a curated feed at each other and one of them doesn't know she's doing it.

And I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: *she put her lipstick back on in a stranger's bathroom at one in the morning and checked that the smile still worked, and the eyeliner had held, "of course it had, nothing had happened to her eyes."*
