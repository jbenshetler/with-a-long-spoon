REACTION

Okay so the floor didn't open, but something else did, and I'm not even mad.

Here's the thing I keep turning over: he got out of the truck. He handed her the only dry thing he owned and then removed himself, in the rain, back to her, hands in his pockets, taking the weather full-on for no reason she could see — and that's the Daphne rule again, isn't it. He closes the door on himself before she has to. And she looked down at the flannel and did the math — *his shirt, and then the truck. Both* — and then she got up on her knees and knocked on the glass. That knuckle rap. Three quick. God. That's the whole book in one gesture: he builds the room she can't help but walk into, and the walking in is genuinely, entirely hers. Every yes freely given. The blurb told me and I still felt it as hers.

And the undressing — I have read a thousand truck-cab scenes and I have never read one where the heat came from the *mother's voice*. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." That's the line. She's not overcoming the shame, she's riding it, she wants to be the exact thing the word *floozie* means, and she wants him watching her be it. That's the first time this book has done something to me physically rather than just making me nervous. And then she keeps her eyes on his for the buttons she doesn't need to look down for — and then *chooses* to look down, so he can look down, so she can see what he sees. That's not a girl being undressed. That's direction. She directed him.

And he didn't move. Didn't lean, didn't lift a hand, and the gladness was the whole answer. Which is correct and also the thing that frightens me most about him, because the restraint is the technique, and the technique is also real, and I can no longer get a knife between those two facts.

Then she reaches for his hand to put it in her mouth and it's *shaking*. That broke me a little. She aborts her own next thing — her body already going at it — because he's cold. Pulls his hand under the flannel onto her belly. "You're freezing." That is the first time in eleven chapters anybody has taken care of Pace, and he had no idea he needed it, he "was a long way from the question of whether he was cold." If this book is going where I think it's going, I'm going to want that moment back later with interest.

Now. The dress. The russet silk, the brick red, *the one*, said out loud on a rock to a man who reports to Randi over wine with her tongue in his mouth going *tell. Tell.* She set it down and he said "that sounds like a beautiful dress" and didn't pick it up again, which is the most dangerous sentence in the chapter. That dress is going to arrive. I would bet money. And the induction dinner, with her mother coming down, is a room with Vee's name read out at the front of it and nobody in it knowing anything — that's a set piece being loaded in advance and I can feel the pin.

Two other things I'm chewing: the gym bag that lives behind the seat with a razor and folded briefs in it, which is a bag for a man who sleeps somewhere that isn't his house, and Vee is now walking around campus wearing a shirt out of it. Randi has absolutely seen that flannel. And — he read the sky and said *it's going to come down.* He knew. He had dry clothes. I don't think he manufactured the rain, obviously, but he knew what the descent was going to do and he walked her into it anyway, and then performed the sacrifice she'd have to invite him out of. I can't prove it's calculated. I can't prove it isn't. That's exactly where this book keeps parking me and it's why I'm still here.

The cherry pit thing came the closest to annoying me — I braced hard for Pygmalion, for the man correcting the poor girl's manners — and then the save was good: he put his own pit down beside hers and didn't watch her learn. Fine. I'll allow it. But I clocked the pattern: boot, horseradish, cherry pits, the sky. Four times in one chapter he knew a thing she didn't and gave it to her gently. One more chapter of Competent Man Teaches Girl The World and I'm going to start counting.
