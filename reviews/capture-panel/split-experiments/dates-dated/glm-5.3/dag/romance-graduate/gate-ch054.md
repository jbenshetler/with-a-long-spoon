Oh, this man. This whole chapter is him doing the thing — the exact thing the fight was about — in his own language, and I had to put the book down for a second at "I want to meet your people" because I didn't see it coming and then it was the only thing he could have said.

He wants her *world*. Not just her body on the county road at midnight, not just the naked princess in his kitchen — the roster. And the boots. THE BOOTS. She said Meg's name maybe once, on a trail, thighs burning, a half-sentence of filler — and he kept it, and shelved it, and handed it back one-handed without turning around. "Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off." I got actual chills. That's the answer to "what am I to you" and he doesn't even know he's giving it. He's been listening all fall with everything he has — the listening she mistook for withholding in December. The words were never in the drawer, they're in the *shelf with her friends' names on it*.

And "tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one." He solved the Cassie problem before Vee finished feeling it. He watched her rule out every restaurant in town because somebody would have to be a good sport — he clocked that she'd "watched somebody be fine about it before" — and he just quietly refused to build a dinner where anyone sits at the short end. That's not a man collecting her. That's a man making room for the whole of her life. And Randi in the middle of it, which — after Brooke's little threat in the foyer, after Randi standing with her hand flat on the door not letting herself know what Vee's kiss meant — Randi being invited INTO Pace's orbit in public, at a table he picks... the blurb said three games. I can feel the book setting the board.

The Theo beat was the one that got my guard up, in the good way. She over-explains the never-even, braces for the question, and he says "Bring Theo" and reaches for the next pan. No jealousy, no pressing. The old me would've called that too-good-to-be-true; the me who's read fifty chapters of this man knows it's the same patience that left her mouth for her to take back. But it's also the Daphne shape, isn't it — the man who never asks for things — and I'm watching that now. I'm watching everything now.

The nachos picture almost wrecked me, honestly. Her own mind serving her the Chili's fork squaring to the table edge, not as an accusation, just as a fact she has to see. And then she hands him the venue — "will you pick the place" — which is her asking him to solve the room where she can't hear, and he says "Let me handle it" and there is happiness in his face he's barely letting out. He's being handed her people. This is the man who "I don't go back," who has no world of his own, volunteering for a loud one. Sheri's Valentine's dance, and now this. He's coming out. He's doing it in his own order and at his own speed and I could shake him and I love him.

Yes, it's a bridge chapter — nothing "happens," no heat to speak of, she's just naked at the table talking at his back — but it's the kind of quiet chapter I'd cite as proof this book is better than my whole backlist. Two people in a kitchen, and the whole shape of the story turned over. That said: this is the soft afterglow stretch. 52 and 53 were the storm, 54 is the blanket. If 55 is another "they're happy and planning" chapter, I'll start clock-watching. The party has to actually arrive — with Kayla's mouth, and Cassie's eyes on the man her friend vanished into, and Randi meeting Pace *properly* while running the con, and possibly Theo in the room — that's a powder keg of a dinner. Give it to me.
