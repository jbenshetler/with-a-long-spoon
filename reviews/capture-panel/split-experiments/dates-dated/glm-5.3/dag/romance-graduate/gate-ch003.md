REACTION — Okay, this book. This book is doing the thing I keep telling people I want and never get. Chapter one wrecked me a little — the bathroom scene, "the face was lying," her putting the real one back behind it like a door slamming, and him sitting out there *waiting* because of a woman named Daphne from long ago, and the book didn't explain that, it just left it on the floor for me. That's what I mean by trust. It trusts me to catch it.

And then chapter two — four minutes of smiling in a sorority common room, Brooke doing that thing presidents do, and Randi climbing the stairs knowing exactly what happened in the room without looking back. That chapter was nobody talking about anything and it was one of the most tense things I've read all year. "He's hard to say no to," in a different key. God.

Now this one. The question in bed — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and her body answering before she does, coming so fast it shocks her. And Pace clocking it and NOT taking her body's answer as her answer. Do you know how rare that is? Most books in my library would play that beat as permission. He makes it a choice. And then her recovery — "only in a threesome, with you" — and the narration just quietly names what she did: made herself a girlfriend offering a gift instead of a woman who hungers, because the first is unsurvivable. That's the exact psychological precision I'm starving for, and it doesn't slow the heat down to deliver it.

The walk itself — the pointing game — I expected to find it icky, a man shopping women in public, and instead the whole point is that the choosing is hers. "I point. You pick." She goes from being a good sport to *shopping* and he can feel it through her hand, the dance-frame metaphor, follow breaking frame before the lead calls the step. I felt that in my sternum. And then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her whole body — the girl the grammar can't grade, nothing pinned, nothing apologizing — and Randi's "yes" comes out bare, alone, already true before she said it. That's the blurb's girl. That's the woman they picked in a game started in bed. And I know from the jacket how this goes — "every yes was freely given, that was the problem" — and I'm already braced and already in.

The staging of the collision made my mouth go dry — the redhead apologizing to *her*, the hand staying a half-beat long, Randi coming back lit up like a struck match with a name and a shared stats class. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" said low against his chest, in the register she only uses in bed, now used in daylight. That line. That line is the whole book moving one notch darker and I am fully, willingly on the hook. The only flicker of unease is that I know Vee doesn't know there's a plan, and the tenderness of chapters one and two is going to be aimed at her like a weapon — but that unease is *wanted*, it's the story earning the jacket's last line. Also: "let's go feed you," after she said "I can't eat that" — this man is still working on her hunger with apples and now cheeseburgers. I have a full-body dread-and-warmth situation about where that thread ends.

Two heat scenes and a fully clothed public scene and the public scene was the hottest of the three. That's craft.
