## REACTION

The first half made me groan a little: ANOTHER retelling. Chapter 12 was Vee telling the hike to an audience, and now I sit through the fitting I just lived through, told to Cassie. To be fair, there's real stuff in it. "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look.*" "Like I was supposed to be there." Cassie's "Oh, Vee" and her hand on the knee, once, taken back. And "*I* was grinding on *him*" made me laugh, because the last chapter only gave me "bent into it more than the dress asked," and here's the real version, told to her best friend with both hands over her mouth. But "Are you sure he's not gay?" is a sitcom beat, the "bodybuilder/powerlifter" correction is too, and both made me feel like I was on the wrong shelf for a minute. I was skimming for something new.

Then Randi pulls up in the Mercedes and the book picks me up by the collar.

This lunch is chapter 6 turned around. On the couch Randi said *Tell* to Pace. Now she says it straight to Vee, chin on her hand, in a velvet booth where "you could have said anything." She leads her down one tiny "yeah" at a time. "Nobody measures over a bra" is pure social authority: it's just how it's done, the salad fork goes on the outside. I've watched women do that in real life. And then the part that made me sit up: "*The pedestal.*" "I bet he started at your hip." "Which hip?" Vee NEVER mentioned a riser. She said "he had me take my top off." Randi knows about the platform. Either Pace told her Saturday night, or she's stood on that box herself. Probably both. "All the best ones start at the left hip" lands on Vee like a blessing, and it lands on me like a confession.

And the thumb. She sets the rim of the water glass under her nail and bears down, and that's the bottle cap from chapter 1, word for word in spirit. Vee sees it and decides Randi is *moved*, glad for her, and I almost put the Kindle down. Same move as the Kentucky-as-Switzerland misreading. Vee keeps taking Randi's pain for polish. I noticed the wrist band too, the one she's "never once seen her without," and her fingers going to it during the headboard story. I don't like what I'm thinking about that band.

The tickle story. "Saturday." A "beautiful idiot" who's bad with knots. Saturday afternoon Pace was on his knees pinning burgundy silk, so either Randi went straight from his bed into a stranger's, or this is "Paul?" again: Pace, disguised as his own opposite, told so she can say "A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." That's the bench, sold to Vee over tomato soup. Grooming, in the gentlest voice alive. It also worked on me, and on Vee. The picture of Randi tied up and cracked open put heat in Vee "she had no shelf for," and it put some in me.

Then "You don't have to tell me." Oh my god. It's chapter 11 exactly. "How was it?" and she takes the answer off his tongue. She runs the whole interrogation to bring Vee's deepest shame right up to her mouth, the thing she wouldn't even give Cassie, and then she won't take it. "A door closing softly." Vee is left half-open with nowhere to put it, and Randi tucks her hair back and asks for the check. That's the cruelest kind thing anyone has done in this book. I don't think it was cruelty. I think Randi got right up to hearing the wet satin from Vee's own mouth and couldn't stand to.

And then she kisses her on the mouth in the fire lane. "*French,*" Vee thinks. Honey, it's Kentucky. Then the windows: Vee counts the dark squares where someone could be watching, and I'm counting them for Brooke.

The money threads were great: reading the right side of the menu first, one glass of Rombauer costing more than her whole lunch, a balance she'll "feel on Thursday," refusing to let Randi buy her seat at the table. That's grown-up and real. The car paragraph goes on too long, though ("lit somehow in the dim room the way money is lit"), and the second time Vee reads Randi's tightness, the book walks me through both readings when I'd already caught it.
