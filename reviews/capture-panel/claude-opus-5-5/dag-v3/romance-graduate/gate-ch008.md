**REACTION**

She ate everything. That was my first thought, and I hated thinking it, because Randi's voice was right there in my head: *Did she eat all of it?* This is the most tender chapter in the book so far, and I couldn't read any of it straight. Pace is going home tonight. The deadbolt is going to turn over and someone is going to say *Tell*.

On its own, though, the chapter got me. Vee forgets to eat all day because she's busy with the dress, then the other dress, then the borrowed shoes. Then the smell from that doorway "reached into it and pulled," and the hollow turns out to be "empty a lot longer than since noon." She stops watching herself eat and doesn't notice she's stopped. Her mother's voice, which shut down every pretty picture in Chi Latte, never shows up. It's lovely, and it's the apple slices all over again. He fed Randi on a kitchen chair, and now he's feeding Vee in a little room that smells of garlic and char. Same move, two women. Is it a man who loves to feed people, or a method? I honestly can't tell, and I think the book wants it that way.

"May I choose?" Right, there's the jacket: *choosing becomes the most erotic thing she has ever done*. What I didn't expect is that it starts with her handing the choice away, and the relief of it going through her. Then the kiss is built the same way. Her prissy little rule comes out, he doesn't push at all, she stops him with her own hand on the path, and then he leaves the last inch. "He wasn't going to take it any further than she had." From inside Vee it's the most respectful kiss she's ever had. From where I'm sitting, I watched him do this exact thing in chapter three and then go girl-shopping with it. Both of those are true, and it made the kiss hotter and sicker at once. The second kiss, with the mint still in both their mouths and her grin before she can stop it, honestly made me smile at my phone like an idiot.

And "Don't answer tonight" worked exactly as he designed it. "He just left it with me. Like it could keep." I called it. Then Cassie, my Cassie, the one person who sees everything, says "That's not a boy. That's a man." That stung. If Cassie's sold, nobody's going to catch this.

Small things I'm keeping an eye on. The right-hand-only eating: he knows the etiquette, Christine knows him, and Christine said something at the door that made him duck his head. Has he brought someone here before? Daphne? I'm filing it. "Powerlifter, best I've ever done is third." That's a cute bit of humility, plus those forearms, so okay.

Gripes. It's long and soft-focus in the middle. The baba ghanoush passage, "each soft sound a closed door with something behind it," is the prose showing off instead of feeding me, which is ironic in a chapter about being fed. And the hand-holding "less like something beginning than something resolving… all its life maybe" is a romance-novel line, and this book is better than that. "She'd done it an hour ago over warm bread and the world hadn't ended" is the explaining habit again, connecting the dots for me. So that's two chapters in a row with no bodies, and I noticed. But a first kiss with this much loaded irony is charged enough that I'm not sagging. Barely.

The 1 a.m. text: *he said "may I choose" and ordered for her and she ate EVERYTHING without hearing her mom once, and then she kissed him under a tree and he left the last inch for her. it's the sweetest date i've read in years and he is 100% going home to tell randi every bite.*
