**REACTION**

I recognized the text the second it lit her ceiling. *you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here.* I'd already read Randi writing it, naked in a hotel room with a sleeping stranger and her lipstick on his chest, picking every true word so none of them would be the thing. Now I watched it land in a twin bed in Ohio, and Vee took it at face value: "a devastatingly handsome billionaire, a weekend so wild Vee wouldn't recognize half of what they did." The awful part is she's basically right. There was a sleigh and a white-tie ball and a towering law student. She got every fact right and the whole truth wrong, because the girl inside all that gold felt like a sandwich. Both of them spent that midnight lying in a cold room, each sure the other was living. Then there's "Randi, the year's gift she had literally stumbled into." *Literally.* Oh, honey. I said "stumbled" out loud in that tone you use on a friend who has no idea.

The best thing in the chapter is the fra diavolo memory going bad. She goes back to her warmest night, the sheet slipping at his table, to get warm, and while she's inside it the girl at the table stops being her. The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, and Pace keeps smiling at the stove. "She could not keep her own night." That scared me more than anything has in a while, because it's exactly how jealousy works at midnight. I've done that to my own memories. And the irony cuts deep: she's building a blonde out of spite while the real other woman is the brunette she just texted a yellow heart to. Jealous of the wrong woman again, like the Rusty Nail. That's a pattern now, and a good one.

I loved the photo line too: "Same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it." The cropped one is on her mother's phone downstairs and the whole one is on Pace's, and she's the one who cut it. And "a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had" is grown-up envy, honest and a little ugly. She knows where the money came from and wants the mountains anyway. I believe her.

Now the part that wore me out. This is Vee lying awake in a cold room hearing the silence where the word should be, again. I've had the dorm bunk, his bed, the laundry room, and now her childhood bed. Her thumb hovers over the empty field and she even remembers doing it "in another bed, in another cold room." The book knows it's repeating itself and does it anyway. Then I got the bartender's lines played back, Randi's "he leaves you the room" played back, the dance shirt, and the induction photo one more time. Half this chapter is the book reading me my own notes. The opening is a beige page of Wheel of Fortune and casseroles. I get that the beige is the point, but I was skimming it.

So now I know what happened for three weeks: nothing. He didn't come after her and she didn't text. Three weeks of silence, built out of the fact that neither of them can send a message. That's very close to the one-unsent-text conflict I quit KU to escape. The book has earned it better than most, since he's a man who "leaves you the room" and she's figured out no text can carry what she needs. Still, I felt the trope breathing on my neck. I'm also relieved and a little disappointed that she never took the fight to Randi. The fear I've been carrying about that Friday night was for nothing.

What I want now isn't another night of ache. It's the first day of term: a class, Randi lighting up, and whatever room Pace turns out to be in.
