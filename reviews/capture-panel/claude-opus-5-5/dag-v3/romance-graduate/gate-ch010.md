**REACTION**

I finally get a body, and it's Vee's, and she's the one who decides to show it. I'm a little stunned.

I went into the truck scene braced for the usual. Rain, a wet shirt, the flannel he hands her, him standing out in the downpour like a gentleman. That's the setup for the hero to get a peek, and I've read it a hundred times. Then she knocks on the glass. She crooks a finger at him, holds his eyes, and undoes the buttons herself. What made it hot for me, really hot, is her mother's voice. In the Chi Latte chapter, *floozies* was the word that shut her down. Here the same voice says *girls who do this, three dates, your mother would* and every line of it lands as heat. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." Her thighs press on *floozie*. That's shame as fuel. It's a grown-up idea, I've almost never seen a romance put it on the page, and it's what I keep asking for. Then he doesn't touch her. He just looks, still and glad. Then she reaches for his hand to start the next thing and it's *shaking*. He'd been standing in the rain the whole time and never noticed he was freezing. "He'd been a long way from the question of whether he was cold." That's the first time in ten chapters I've seen Pace not running anything. He's simply undone. She tucks his hand under the flannel against her belly, and that's the moment I fell for both of them a little.

The moleskin scene gave me chills, and not all good ones. She's on a boulder with her feet off the ground, he kneels, takes off her boot and sock, and holds her bare foot in his hands. Chapter one: *He liked her feet. He had not yet told her that.* So now he has two women's feet. Then he feeds her: rye, horseradish, cherries. That's three chapters in a row of him feeding Vee. "The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it." Okay, book, I see it, you didn't need to tell me. The cherry pit got me though. He corrects her and she feels ashamed, and then he puts his own pit right next to hers without looking at her. She learns the rule without being made small. It's lovely, and it's also exactly how he trains, the pointing game with a softer touch.

THE DRESS. Russet, "the red of a brick." Randi's closet runs bone to camel to oatmeal, "with the one disciplined oxblood at the end." I'm sure that dress is going to turn up. Either Pace buys it, since "That sounds like a beautiful dress" was much too light to be nothing, or Randi hands Vee her oxblood. Either way somebody is going to dress her.

Gripes. The campus cop. A siren cuts off her sentence right as she's about to say the big thing? That's a manufactured interruption, the kind I quit hockey books over, and this book doesn't need it. Then Cassie gets the whole hike retold. It's the third chapter in a row where I hear the date twice. The retelling has good lines, like "the way other girls said *and then we kissed*," and Cassie's "Vee," set down like something that might break, but I was in the truck, babe. And no Randi at all, and no house. I thought the hike meant the bench. It didn't, and the bench is still waiting.

The 1 a.m. text: *she TOOK OFF HER SHIRT in his truck because her mom's voice calling her a floozie was turning her on. he just looked at her and he was shaking from the cold and she put his hand under her shirt to warm it. also there's a red dress and I know exactly whose closet it's in.*
