**REACTION**

Randi. Finally. Seven chapters since she left Vee's drawer standing an inch open, and the book brings her back in Gstaad with the best line of the season.

I'll start with the end, because it got me. "In the warmth she could borrow and could not make, and stayed." Two chapters ago Vee figured out that the heat in the flannel was her own body heat given back. She took it off, lay there naked in the cold, and didn't put it on again. Here's Randi in the same position, warm "only where she touched him," cold everywhere else, a cold she "had brought in with her." She picks the borrowed heat. She lies back down against a stranger and stays. Same discovery, opposite choice, three weeks apart, and the book never points at the parallel. I caught it, and I went quiet.

Then there's the text. *it's honestly a fairytale out here.* "Every word of it true. None of it the thing." Oh, honey. That's Pace. It's "I'd never share your photograph," it's "sometimes I take care of it myself," his whole lawyer-true way of talking, and Randi does it too, to the girl she loves, from a bed in Switzerland. Vee's message is the kindest thing in the chapter. "i'm here if you want to talk. or not." She's offering Randi exactly the door Randi always offers her. Randi answers with a fairytale and "miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋," the one true line packed inside the cover.

And she says it, in her own head: she loves Vee, and not as currency. "Vee was her gorgeous." I've known it since the velvet booth, and it still landed, because what follows is a woman running a scenario all the way to the end and hitting the one she can't finish, which is Vee's face. "She had never had to be brave; she had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be." So she won't tell. "As long as the word kept its cover." That's the jacket's missing word coming from the other side of the triangle. Pace won't say it to Vee. Randi won't say it to Vee. Vee is in Ohio surrounded by two people who love her and won't say so.

What I loved most, though, is the John section. It's my entire Kindle. A towering tanned law student from New Haven, a fake marriage in the lift line, a black run where he lets her lead, champagne at a hütte, a white-tie ball, a dress bought that afternoon, a starred chef, a waltz, midnight, silver falling, a SLEIGH. I was rolling my eyes by the hütte and thinking this was the billionaire book I DNF in a weekend. Then the sex is "good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon," and the book takes the whole fantasy apart from the inside. "This was the best the acceptable world could do for her." It's the trope I'm bored of, staged on purpose so a woman can lie in the middle of it and feel nothing. That's smart. I felt seen, which is weird to say about a ski chalet.

The dance comparison was a knife too. John's lead is firm, with no guessing, and Pace's "was the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for." That's the concrete pad, the lesson Vee got. "She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked." She tells herself easier is better, and the book lets her believe it for about four pages.

The breakfast. "Eating her revulsion" at her father. Central Florida, "and did not mention it." So the accent is home, the thing she polishes off. And she knows about the PPP money now, or at least something curdled after that MIRA moment. Her mother in a label belt and a tennis bracelet, the daughter keeping her plain white gold out of the light. Smoked salmon on one slice of rye, no butter. Still starving, and on vacation. And she's making up boys for her father at the table, "invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been." So that's where the fake men came from. She's done it since she was a kid.

What bugged me. "His room was high in the hotel… he drew her in, and she went. Afterward." That's a fade, and my hand twitched. I'll forgive it, mostly, because the emptiness is the point, and the lipstick ring and "felt her own mouth do it from a long way off" do more after the fact than a scene would have. But I noticed. The opening paragraph is clunky, a list of who sits where and what everyone ordered, plus "delicately clomped." And the mirror stretch spells things out again: "He was safe. She had put nothing on the table and had won exactly that." "The flaw was hers." I'd already felt all of it.

The big grudge: the book jumped three weeks. I left Vee crying in her car on December 11 in front of his door, and now it's New Year's Eve, she's in Ohio sending sweet texts, and I have no idea what happened in between. Did Pace text? Did she go to Randi that night? Does Randi even know they fought? Randi's head barely turns to Pace, and it never once turns to the drawer or the panties. She thinks about telling Vee she loves her. She doesn't think about the much bigger thing she's hiding. That's either the book being sly or Randi's denial being total, and I'm betting on denial.
