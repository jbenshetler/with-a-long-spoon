**REACTION**

Okay. That first page is what I keep wanting from this genre and almost never get. She comes because he *asked her a question*. His body didn't do it; the idea did, and it landed before she could decide what it meant. That's hot and it's smart at the same time, and it's the kind of beat that makes me sit up in bed. And then "Only in a threesome. With you." That's such a real move. I have *watched* women do it at parties: take a hunger that belongs to you and rebrand it as a gift for him so it's survivable. Randi is turning out to be the most interesting person in the book. She's two different people, a gorgeous face and a locked bathroom door.

But the book did what I hate a couple of times. It showed me the flinch, the tongue touching her lip, the instant recovery, and that was perfect. Then it told me anyway: "Her body wanted this; her mind had not yet caught up to it." I KNOW. I was there. Same with "The same act. A different owner." It's a great line, but it's the narrator nudging me. Trust me a little.

The pointing game is delicious and it made me feel slightly sick, which I think is the point. I spent the whole walk with the jacket open in my head: *a secret choice made about her*. I'm watching the choice get made. I'm complicit, shopping for a girl along with them, grading strangers' sneakers and scrunchies. The catalogue of women went on a hair long, and the campus-postcard paragraph (pediments, two-centuries-ago men) had me drumming my fingers. But Randi's verdicts kept me in it. "Too expected." "God, no," with real feeling in it at the blowout girl who runs rooms, because that's *her*. Then the round girl with glasses gets a "maybe." She's not picking a partner, she's picking the opposite of herself.

Then Vee. FINALLY, the heroine on the jacket, and we meet her as prey seen across a lawn. Her head is thrown back laughing, she's forgotten she can be seen, and of course that's exactly what Randi can't do and can't stop staring at. The bare "Yes" with nothing around it gave me goosebumps. The fake collision was chilling in the best way: a decade of dance turned into a social weapon, and Vee ends up apologizing for being walked into. And Randi's little happy footwork on the brick? She's not doing this for Pace at all, whatever she's telling herself.

Pace is getting more complicated and I'm here for it. He's scrupulous about consent ("he did not take it for her answer"), and he's also kissing her with her own taste on his mouth in broad daylight to crack her open. Courtly and calculating in the same gesture. I can't tell yet if he's the villain, and I like that I can't.

Then the last two lines. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Oh no. And "Let's go feed you," an hour after "I can't eat that." Texting my friend: *the boyfriend and girlfriend just picked out the heroine like a menu item and I'm horrified and I need chapter 4.*
