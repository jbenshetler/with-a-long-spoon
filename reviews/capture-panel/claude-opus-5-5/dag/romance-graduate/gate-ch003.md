**REACTION**

Okay. *Okay.* So that's the game. The jacket told me "a secret choice made about her," and I filed it as blurb language. Then I spent a whole chapter walking beside the people making the choice while they shopped for her across a quad, and Vee walked out of the dining hall laughing with her head thrown back. She's the one person on that lawn who'd forgotten she could be seen, and she's exactly who they picked. I put the Kindle down on my chest for a second. That's the "ahead of her from the start" thing, and it doesn't feel like a gimmick. It feels like I'm complicit, and the book wants me to feel that.

The opening scene is the hottest thing in the book so far for me, hotter than the bench, and it's because of the question. *Would you like to taste another woman?* lands, and her body comes before her brain can get there. That's what I read for: the sex and the psychology are the same event. Then she pulls herself back together with "Only in a threesome. With you." The silk voice, the kiss to his jaw, handing her appetite to him so it's his kink and she's just the generous girlfriend. That's real interiority, even though we're in his head watching her do it. Randi has more inner life in her deflections than most heroines have in their whole first-person narration.

The pointing game itself is so wrong and so delicious. It's pointing at women in daylight, she's offended for one beat, and then she's *shopping*. It's a great image. I loved her verdicts: "Too expected." "You'd get both or neither." The "God, no" at the blowout girl was the most honest thing she said all afternoon, and she didn't even know it. Then she stops dead in the middle of a step, says "Yes," just the bare word, and scrambles to put the costume back on. The fake collision, where Vee ends up apologizing to *her*, is chilling and gorgeous. A decade of dance turned into a hunting move.

The last two lines did me in. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Oh, honey. Then "Let's go feed you," right after "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger. I saw that echo and I didn't need it pointed out, and the book didn't point it out. Good.

Gripes: Pace admires Pace a lot. He's good at reading her, he guessed right, he planned this, he enjoys the maple. I'm starting to side-eye him. I think the book wants me to, but I want the story to own it rather than let him off. A few lines explain the subtext right after it's already landed, like "The same act. A different owner." and "which he could see was unsurvivable." Those were half a beat too neat. And I skimmed the campus scenery paragraph, the colonnade built by dead men so the eye would travel a certain way. Fine, it's thematic, I get it.

Bigger worry, kept for later: we're three chapters into a book whose jacket heroine is Vee, and she's had two minutes, seen from a light pole. The next chapter had better be inside her head. What I'd text a friend at 1 a.m.: "the heroine just got picked out of a crowd like a dessert and I'm SO turned on and SO uneasy, send help."
