**REACTION**

I nearly lost patience in the first three pages. There's a whole paragraph on board-and-batten siding and seams going to pewter, and then he lists every spice in the tikka masala. I know what it's doing: he's a man who considers things, and here's the evidence. But I already had that evidence from the bread, the paper towel, the collared five-pound plate. I skimmed. The cider pairing was cute. "I wanted to please you" landed. Then the Friday-night sex is waved past in one paragraph, "warm and unhurried and sure," and Saturday morning gets the same summary treatment. Twice in one chapter. In any other book that's the moment I check how many pages are left.

Then the mirror, and I stopped skimming.

The blindfold scene is the best and worst thing he's done, and I can't separate the two. First, the good part. He names the things she has spent her life hiding: the spill of the breast, the wet, the way her body answers out loud. Then he tells her the body is "just a fact" and that what he wants is her laughing with her whole chest and walking into things that scare her. I'm a grown woman and my throat went tight at the little toe. And "Go ahead," her hands hunting blind for him, the absurd lurch of "you can no more spank yourself than tickle yourself." That's the explicit-and-inside-her-head combination I came to this series for. Her hand at the breast "closing harder than he ever had… what she was when no one watched" is real interiority, and it was hotter than anything he did to her.

Then the bad part. "What are you thinking about?" Mid-climb. My stomach dropped. Two chapters ago she came in a restaurant bathroom to Randi's mouth. Then he asks, "Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?" and she says yes "the way you say yes to something small." Honey, it is not small. He's built a confession booth into her own hand. Every time she touches herself now, she either tells him or lies to him, and the person in her head lately isn't always him. I honestly think "you" was true tonight, and that's the trap. He got one true answer and turned it into a standing order. Who hears the reports? I've stopped thinking Pace keeps anything to himself.

And the key in the handmade box, on a pillow of oyster silk she has "felt against her skin somewhere before." I put the book down for a second at that line. Whose silk is it? Randi's? A slip, a blouse, something from the loft? Same question about the plum. He bought plum lingerie the week after the plum sweater. "I've never given a key to anyone." Maybe that's literally true, because Randi doesn't need one. And the shadow across his face when she describes him to the mirror and gives herself only one line: was that hurt for her, or something about how he looks? I can't tell, and I like not being able to tell.

Where is Valentine's? The hall, Sheri, the Brooks and Dunn band, "bring your opinions." It just evaporated between 71 and 72. I'd been holding onto that for two weeks of book time. So had Sheri. Randi isn't in this chapter at all, and I felt her absence on every page, which I suppose is the point.

Pattern note to myself: 71, 72, 73 are three hot chapters running, after the two quiet ones. 72 and 73 are the same shape: Pace controls her pleasure by tending it, holding it back, then directing it. It's working, but the next one needs a different shape. I need Randi in a room, or Vee alone with what she just promised.
