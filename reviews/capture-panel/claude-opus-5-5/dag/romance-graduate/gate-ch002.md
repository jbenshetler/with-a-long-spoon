## Reaction

That was a different book for a while, and I mean that as a compliment. Chapter one was all heat. This one was four minutes of being slowly cut open by a woman who never got out of her chair. I've been in that room. Maybe not a sorority, but I know the friendly voice, and the question that sounds like a question and is really an audit. "Engineering, isn't it." Then "Math. PhD." Then the tiny recalibration where Brooke takes it in without giving back the notch she just docked him. I physically winced at "short-cute." And Sloane's "I love that you don't care about cars" is the meanest compliment I've read in months. Nobody raised a voice and Randi came out of it bleeding.

What really got me was Randi lying about the paper. Last night she got the whole Wilson-street story out smooth. This morning she's a fraction of a second late on every answer, and the book counts those fractions for me: late, longer, wider. That's the bench still in her body. The woman who couldn't perform anymore last night can only half-perform this morning, and Brooke catches it, files it ("Pace," like she was filing it), and lets her go. Randi doesn't get to say any of the three lines she'd lined up. Brutal. Nobody in that common room raises a hand to her and they still beat her.

Then the room. Shoes on in bed, the duvet that's "adequate." It isn't the cashmere blanket she brought to his house because she gets cold after sex. The book doesn't connect those two for me, I did it myself, and it hurt in exactly the right place. Same with "he's hard to say no to," in a different key. That line has turned around on her: she said it as a joke, and it's true now in a way she can't stand. And I'm watching the deadbolt from chapter one. She decided in his bathroom she was never coming out again, and this chapter is what that decision costs her in the daylight.

The suspicious part, the thing I'd text someone at 1 a.m.: the jacket says Randi steers Vee into Pace's path "as if by accident," and that it began as "a game between the two people now closest to her." So the game is Randi's way out, isn't it? She got seen on that bench, it nearly took her apart, and she can't go back. So she finds a softer, hungrier girl to stand where she stood, and goes on getting it secondhand over lunch. If that's where this is going, it's dark and sad and I'm completely in. Also, where is Vee? Two chapters and the heroine hasn't shown up. I'm patient with that for now because Randi has more inner life than most heroines I've read this year. But I'm counting.

One thing slightly off for me. The last paragraph explains the not-crying a little more than it needs to: "it felt like there being no door," "the way a held breath stops feeling held." That's lovely, but I already knew. The repeated "She lay under the duvet" was deliberate, and it worked until about the fourth time.

This is my first quiet chapter, and it was a good one. A second quiet one in a row and I'll start checking the page count.
