**REACTION**

She ATE. That was the thing I couldn't get past. Vee sits down hollow from skipping lunch over a dress and then the other dress, and somewhere in the meal she stops watching herself eat, and "she didn't notice she'd stopped." Two chapters ago Randi got wet listening to how Vee built a froyo cup. Now Pace is feeding her on purpose. He picks the bright dish and the dark dish, the warm bread, the mint tea. I can't decide if that's a gift or a technique, and the book clearly doesn't want me to decide. Either way he has his hand on the feeding thread, and I'd bet anything Randi gets every bite of this in a debrief.

"May I choose?" did it. It mirrors "May I call you," and she answers "Yes. Please," the same two words Randi gave him at the bench. Vee feels relief at handing the choice over, but he asks for it, so the choice is still hers. That's the jacket's line, "choosing becomes the most erotic thing," and here it happens over a menu. Then comes the kiss. He doesn't argue her rule, he just walks on. She's the one who plants her feet and pulls the hand taut. He leaves the last inch where it is, she closes it, he gives back exactly as much as she brought, and then waits again. That's "not yet" from the bench, played gently. It worked on me completely, even though I don't trust him at all. Cassie's "That's not a boy. That's a man" made me grin. Cassie doesn't tease her about this one, and I loved that. It also eases my fear that Cassie is on her way out. She's on Vee's side, not against Pace.

I believed Vee more in this chapter than in any before it. She forgets to get off the elevator. She says Carole King before she can think of anything cooler. She lets go of the thread of the conversation and nothing bad happens. "Following him took nothing out of her." That woman has been holding up her end of every conversation since she was twelve, and I felt it. Pace is fun here too: nine words of Arabic, seven of them food, and a powerlifter who places third, badly. I like him more and trust him less, which seems to be this book's whole method.

What bothered me is that Pace stays a closed box. "He'd been somewhere in himself" in the lobby. Where, sir? Thinking about Randi? Is he falling for her? We don't get his head this time, and I noticed. Then there's the heat count. Chapters 7 and 8 are both quiet. That's two in a row, and four of the last five have been a simmer or less. A kiss is a kiss. It's a beautiful one, but this is the eighth chapter of a book shelved as explicit literary erotica, and I keep remembering Randi's held-out hand that went nowhere. The over-explaining is still here too. "It was the old reflex, the part of her that always caught the thread… The same part that had gone quiet at the table." I watched it go quiet. You don't need to remind me.
