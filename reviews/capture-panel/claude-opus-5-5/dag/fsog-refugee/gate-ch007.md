This one hurt a little, and I mean that kindly. It's short and there's no man in it, and I didn't mind for a second, because I spent the whole chapter scared for Cassie.

Ever since she said "Goodbye, Vee" in the lecture hall, I've been reading Cassie as the friend who's about to get left behind. This chapter shows me exactly who's being left. Cassie keeps "tossers" low so only Vee hears it. She has the lungs to shout it at the bleachers and doesn't. She stands in the water "like a person" and never asks what's wrong, because asking would make the sad version bigger. Then she shoots a sheet of water across the gap instead. That's love, and it's a better kind of seeing than what Pace does in the coffee shop. Pace sees Vee as a project. Cassie sees her and leaves her alone. "They will never know the love of a woman" made me laugh out loud, and then it stung: Cassie may be the one woman in this book who loves Vee with no plan behind it.

The underwater part got me. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem." Vee in her sixteen-year-old synchro suit, doing her entrance walk past those idiots. The cardigan from Chi Latte, her mother's voice. I know that girl. I'm glad the book let me be inside her for a while, not just watch her. Whatever her desire turns out to be, I want it to be hers, and here she's real enough that it could be.

Here's my 1 a.m. text, though: I can't read this chapter on its own anymore. After the Tannin chapter, with Randi on Pace's lap drinking in "she built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop," every soft moment with Vee reads like a portrait of what they're going to take. I'm also counting. Pace and Vee haven't been alone since the froyo walk, two chapters ago. I don't feel the gap yet, but I'll start feeling it soon.
