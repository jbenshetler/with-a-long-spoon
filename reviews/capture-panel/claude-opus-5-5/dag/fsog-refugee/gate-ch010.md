**REACTION**

Okay. The truck.

I didn't see it coming, and I love that. The whole chapter is so quiet. Moleskin, wax paper, a man saying "I'll set a pace, tell me if it's wrong" and then walking at the speed of her not knowing how. I'd settled in for a sweet first-hike chapter. Then she's on her knees on that bench seat, knocking on the glass and crooking a finger at him. That's her. Nobody steered her there. He went and stood in the rain on purpose so she could change without being looked at. What she wanted was to be looked at, so she called him back in and undid every button herself, with her eyes on his. I've waited my whole reading life for that scene to belong to the woman. And he just sits there. He doesn't lean in or lift a hand, just sits there being glad. "The gladness in him was the whole of the answer." I had to put my phone down for a second.

Then she reaches for his hand to start the next thing, and it's shaking. He's been freezing out there the whole time, and he was so caught up in her he'd forgotten. She stops, pulls his hand under the flannel against her stomach and says "You're freezing." That's what got me. It isn't heat, it's two people looking after each other at the same moment. The care goes both ways, and that's the thing I keep hoping a man in this genre will allow.

The mother's voice made me squirm a bit, and I think that's on purpose. *Floozie*, and her thighs press together on their own. Her heat is tied to the shame, and the shame is the fuel. I believed it, because that's how a girl raised like Vee would feel it. But it made me nervous for her. It's the same wiring Randi was sitting on with the apple slices, and we saw where that went. Pace knows how to find a starving woman's hunger. Ch 8 dinner, here the sandwich, *the hike had made her hungry first, that was the trick of it.* It's lovely. It's also exactly the play he ran on Randi.

And now the part I'd text my friend at 1 a.m.: **he's going to tell Randi all of it.** I keep picturing the wine glass and Randi saying "*Tell.*" I keep picturing the wine glass and Randi saying "*Tell.*" Vee's sitting in his flannel telling Cassie "I kept the shirt, he didn't ask for it back." Meanwhile I'm reading this chapter without his POV and wondering whether his stillness was reverence or him memorizing her for later. Probably both, and I think that's the point of the book. Plus Randi going "Paul?" at Vee last chapter, faking that she couldn't remember his name. That one was ice cold.

The russet dress. Oh no. He said "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and nothing else, and I'd bet anything that dress turns up. Then the question is whose money it is and whose idea, and whether it's a gift or a costume.

The campus cop squawk was a bit convenient. The book clearly needed to cut that goodbye short, and I felt the author's hand. But I didn't mind much. She's left holding the big thing with nowhere to put it, which is where these books like to leave you. And Cassie saying just "Vee," like setting something down where it won't break. Cassie is the only person in this book who isn't playing anyone. I'd die for her.
