**REACTION**

I opened this one still sore from forty-three. I read that chapter with my jaw tight. Everything I'd been afraid of was said out loud, on his couch, in her voice: *for the threesome, somebody has to get her used to my mouth, it's work*. Then he came straight back with "She consents, every time, or I'm done" and "You don't have to do it for me," and that was enough to keep me going. So I came into this chapter wanting the two of them alone in a room, or at least wanting to see how Vee carries that kiss into the week now that I know what's behind it.

Instead I got a cold lecture hall and a PPP scatterplot. For about a page I thought, oh no, a homework chapter. Then MIRA showed up, and I stopped breathing along with Randi.

What got me is that Randi is the one being watched this time, and she doesn't know it. For forty chapters she's read Vee's face like a menu. Now Vee watches the person instead of the page, and she catches the plural in "cheating bastards." She hears that it's ragged, said for the first time, aimed too close to be about strangers. "Randi had no idea she'd been looked at." I had to put the book down at that line. It's the practice room turned around, and Vee does the opposite of what Randi did there. She doesn't harvest anything. She doesn't push for a yes. She gives Randi somewhere to put her hands. She hands her logistics, the busywork, things she can answer. That's care with no angle in it, and Randi has never once given Vee anything that clean. I'm proud of this girl in a way that hurts.

It also scares me, because now Vee has a shelf of her own. "She didn't tell Randi, then or after, what she'd seen." She's keeping a secret for Randi out of love, while Randi keeps a much bigger one from her out of something I still can't name. The book set those side by side on purpose and I felt it.

And Cassie. "My dad got laid off in 2020," flat, with nothing for anyone to pick it up by. She took the two words as agreement and went back to her screen, and she has no idea the money behind the Mercedes and the brunches is sitting on her father's side of the graph. Vee pays her own check and tips too much because she's scared of being bought. Now I'm wondering what those brunches were bought with. Somebody's going to find this out, and it won't be in a glass room with a door that latches.

The other thing I keep turning over is that Randi didn't know. "Grew up inside the thing," certain, going to show Cassie how it really worked. She went to bat for a man and the data took it away from her in four letters. Then she rebuilt herself in an hour, and by packing-up time she was brighter than she'd been all afternoon. That's the machine. Pace told her on the couch that she doesn't have to do the work, and here she is doing it alone, in public, perfectly. I want to know if she drives this out to the gravel tonight, and whether it's the first true thing she's ever brought him that isn't about Vee.

The outlier line gets paid off without being cute about it. Two kinds: the one you set aside if you can account for it, and the one that carries forward. Vee just decided which kind this is, alone, and kindly. I don't think she gets to decide.

What I'm missing is him. He's been out of a room with her since the lemon chicken, two chapters back. I don't mind it yet, because the care in this one is real, just pointed sideways. But I felt the absence. Text to a friend at 1 a.m.: *the girl is protecting the woman who's grooming her and she's doing it beautifully and I want to scream.*
