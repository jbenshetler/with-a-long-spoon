## Reaction

I came out of that chapter with my shoulders up around my ears.

First, the thing I didn't expect to feel: I got protective of *him*. For five chapters Pace has been everything I left a whole genre hoping to find. He took the pie out of her hands. His hand was gone the instant she said "Don't," palm turned up like a man showing he's empty. He said "Slow. For me," and it made the giving hers. And now Vee is sitting on a glass porch at eleven in the morning handing it all to Randi, one piece at a time. "He checked." That was the most private thing in the book, the finger, the *much*-of-her, his face looking at it like it was fine. She said it out loud over mimosas. In my notes she was the ladle, the girl who kept the bed from both of them and let the surface close with no hole. Now there's a hole, and Randi dug it.

And it was dug. Randi worked her like a deposition. "Walk me." "What was the bet." "And how was it settled?" Each question was a little lower, a little quieter, warm and relentless, and every answer cost Vee another notch of her body in a public room. The book rendered that from inside Vee, the thighs pressed together under the cloth, the "slow soft give," and yes, I felt it. That's what makes it so uncomfortable. It's hot, and it's hot *because* someone is pulling it out of her for her own use. I don't think the book is winking, though. "She could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." That's a book that knows exactly what Randi is doing, and it wants me sitting where Vee can't afford to sit.

I don't believe the Robert Redford boy for a second. He's the old boyfriend "in a hurry" again, and the mouse that roars again. An emergency phone call, gone. "Forty minutes from home. A dark room." And then she lays that picture in Vee's lap, knees wide, a private dance, and watches what it does. Randi built that story to land exactly where it landed.

The bathroom broke my heart a little. Vee stopped because Randi's *face* came into her head, the looking, not the thinking. She ended up "frustrated and cowed in the same breath." Obedient to someone who wasn't even in the room. Then: "Sticky fingers?" I gasped. I still don't know if Randi meant it, and neither does Vee, and I think that's the point. Randi keeps her guessing so Randi stays the one who knows.

Then the ending. One bite of the pastry, "Delicious," never touched again. It's the line drawn across the top of the stew, all over again. Then "my best friend in the whole world," which went straight into the softest place Vee has, careful, unassuming Vee who got picked. Then the kiss that lasts a beat too long, comes back with more in it, and leaves the glaze on Vee's lip. Randi took one bite of the pastry so she could put the taste on Vee's mouth. That's the whole woman. I put the book down for a minute after that line.

Things I'm carrying out of it:
- I'm braced for Randi to drive up the gravel tonight and narrate "he checked" into Pace's ear. If she does, the question from my notes comes straight back: does he set it down this time?
- Cassie was nowhere in this chapter. Again.
- Vee got her *best friend* from the person who's lying to her the most.

The part of me that read Fifty Shades in two nights is fully lit. The part of me that left the genre is standing guard at the door. Both of them want the next chapter.
