## REACTION

Okay. I did not see "There you are, Randi" coming, and I had to put the book face down on my chest for a second.

I'd been reading the whole chapter as Vee. The jacket told me she's the heroine. Here's a twenty-one-year-old who's "quietly starving," an apple sliced thin because she won't eat it thick, a mother on egg whites. I'd decided this was the two of them a few months in and I was falling for them. Then he says the name and the woman on the bench turns out to be the glamorous friend. These are the two people who are going to *choose* Vee. The book has done exactly what the jacket said it would: put me ahead of Vee, and made me fall for it before I knew what it was.

And I did fall. The kitchen got me before anything else did. He kisses her hello and then backs off so she can decide. She circles a finger and he turns the chair for her. He slices the apple cut-side-up because he's noticed she eats it faster that way. That's the man I left the mafia books looking for. Then he asks for her word, twice, and tells her she can say it anytime and he won't be upset. "How's your back? Hands and shoulders? Tell me." She snaps "They're *fine*, Pace" and he smiles at her upside-down face. He crouches at her head and says "Hi" in the middle of a spanking. He takes her heels off by hand. I was so turned on, and safe enough to be. It's the control I wanted, the kind that makes room for her.

Then it tipped. He tells her "No. Not yet," and that's fine, it's edging, she has her word. But then he describes her back to herself: "Facedown on a bench in a man's house. Ass in the air... Begging." He's waiting for a different *please*, one she can't perform. He gets it, and it's beautiful, and it also put a cold finger on the back of my neck. He's not hurting her. He's taking her *apart*, on purpose, and enjoying "how much further she had gone than he had thought she would." The jacket said "taking it." I'm watching for that now.

Then the bathroom just gutted me. The cap pressed into her nail bed, and he took the bottle without seeming to see why. The lock, which she's never turned in his house. Her deciding, "quiet as a deadbolt going over," that she will never come out again. He gets the face from now on. Meanwhile he's on the other side of the door feeling noble about not knocking, thinking it was a "remarkably good evening," and thinking about Daphne. He's so careful and so blind at the same time. I don't think Randi is a villain. I think she's terrified. If she's the one who goes after Vee, I already understand why, and that frightens me more than a villain would.

The 1 a.m. text: *the aftercare in this is better than most books' sex scenes and then the heroine LOCKS HERSELF IN THE BATHROOM and decides to never be known again. also she's not even the heroine.*
