**REACTION**

Okay. I need to say this first: I was a good three-quarters of the way in, completely gone, and then he says *"There you are, Randi,"* and I actually sat up. I'd assumed the whole time this was Vee. The jacket put Vee in my head, the girl who's "quietly starving," and then there's a girl with a food-policing voice in her head and a mother who lives on egg whites, eating thin apple slices off this man's lap. I filled in the name myself. The book let me do it and I'm pretty sure it meant to. So this is the other woman, the glamorous friend, and these are the two who are going to "plan" Vee. I feel slightly played, and I also respect it a lot.

The scene itself is what I came back to this genre looking for and mostly stopped finding. He asks for her word, twice. He tells her she can say it anytime and he won't be upset. He crouches down to her upside-down face in the middle of a spanking just to say "Hi," and she laughs. He asks about her back, her hands, her shoulders, she gets annoyed at him for checking, and he checks anyway. The control is real. He's holding the whole night and stopping her right at the edge again and again, but all of it is aimed at her. He takes her hands out of his hair and she puts them behind her own back. That small moment did more to me than any of the strikes. The kitchen opening was lovely too. She's the one directing him, turning his chair and climbing on, and he likes being told what to do. That's the man I want: dominant and happy to be directed by her.

There was one stretch where I tensed up. It's the "it was not the right *please* yet" part, where he's waiting for a voice that's *his* idea of the real her. He narrates her back to herself: "Facedown... Ass in the air... Begging." For a second it could have turned into breaking her down, with the book admiring the cruelty. It didn't, and I think that's because his palm stays on her cheek through all of it, and because afterward the book doesn't treat it as a win. The bathroom section is where I really got hooked. She fixes her hair. She presses the edge of the bottle cap into her nail bed while he's being sweet to her, and that hurt to read. Then she decides, "quiet as a deadbolt going over," that she will never come out from behind the face again. Meanwhile he's on the bed feeling warm and proud and thinking about apples. He thinks he fed her. She's busy locking herself back up. The way those two don't line up is the most interesting thing in the chapter.

I'm suspicious of both of them now, and I like it that way. Pace is careful about consent and very good at it, and that might be exactly what makes him dangerous to Vee. The jacket says "every yes was freely given, that was the problem," and now I see what it means. Randi just got seen against her will, in a sense, and her answer is to hide. I'm betting she'll aim Vee at him so someone else takes that gaze instead of her. The Daphne aside, where he once went through a door he hadn't been invited through, tells me he knows his own pull.
