**WHO'S WHO**

**Pace.** A math PhD who's "finishing, more or less." He's a powerlifter, and he says his best finish was third. He built his own furniture, including the bench that sits under a tabletop in his bedroom and passes for a table. His house is too warm, Steely Dan is always playing low, and he drives a beat-up white pickup. He lives out off 614, past where the road turns to gravel. The man asks. He says "May I call you." He says "May I choose." He backs off after the hello kiss so she gets to decide. He told Randi "Tell me your word" twice. He doesn't open a locked door, because of some old thing with a woman named Daphne, where he went in uninvited and it cost him. He also picked Vee off a lawn for his girlfriend. Both things are true and I keep holding them side by side.

**Randi.** She's who I thought was the heroine for the whole first chapter, until "There you are, Randi." Black hair, those blue eyes, a face she built her life around. She's on the sorority board, Standards of all things, and gets her own room for it. Her mother lives on egg whites and she eats apples in thin slices. She always locks the deadbolt first thing. She talks with some faint accent from somewhere abroad. On the bench she begged in her real voice for the first time, and in his bathroom mirror she decided she would never come out from behind her face again. Then on the quad she said one bare "Yes" about a laughing redhead and was lit up all afternoon. She calls Vee "dear friend" and "Love you, girl." I can't tell yet what part of her is real.

**Vee (Vivienne Thorne).** She's the jacket girl, and finally I'm inside her. Red hair that goes copper in the wind, cinnamon freckles, a big lush body she hides under a cardigan. She studies econ and is good at it, and she's getting inducted into an honor society. She swam synchro at sixteen, and the water is the one part of her body that was never taken from her. Her mother's voice lives in her mouth and says "floozie." She's starving and doesn't know it. She has no idea she was chosen.

**Cassie.** Vee's roommate. Four words a day, a ball cap, rec sports, a lab. She keeps the window cracked two inches. She's the only person in the book who isn't playing anyone. She said "Goodbye, Vee" in the lecture hall like it was a long parting. She told Vee he looked at Randi "like somebody's great-aunt." Of Pace she said "That's not a boy. That's a man."

**Brooke.** Chapter president. She stays seated while you're the one who has to stop. Warm voice, quiet interrogation, filed his name away. She turned up again at the smoothie place with a tall silent friend and saw Randi holding Vee's hand.

**The rest.** Sloane and Kinsey (the white pickup, "short-cute"). Meg, whose boots didn't fit. Christine, who runs the Middle Eastern place and loves Pace.

**WHERE THINGS STAND**

The bench night was the first weekend of September. The Brooke grilling was the next morning, and Randi lay on her bed in her shoes and didn't cry. A few days later, in bed, he asked if she'd like to taste another woman. Her body said yes before she did, and she dressed it up as "only in a threesome, with you." Then came the pointing game on campus: "I point. You pick." Randi picked Vee, engineered a bump outside the dining hall, and found out they share a stats class.

Randi courted her: Chi Latte, the high table, love-bombing. A week later Pace "happened" into the study café, helped with the integral (Euler, the little right-angle cross), asked for her number, and took her for froyo. That same night Randi climbed onto his lap with red wine and said "Tell," and he gave her Vee's leaning froyo cup but kept back the true thing Vee had asked him to pretend she hadn't said.

He asked Vee to dinner and told her "Don't answer tonight." At dinner he said "May I choose?" and she ate like she hadn't in years. Under a tree she cited her rule about not kissing a man she'd known two days, he let it go, and she pulled him back and kissed him, twice, on her own terms.

At the smoothie place Randi pretended not to remember his name ("Paul?") and pulled the whole story out of her. Vee said "He'd ask, though. Before he ravished me," and Randi answered low, "Ask and then ravish."

Then the hike. Moleskin on her blister. A summit over a lake with the ridges on fire with color. Rye bread and horseradish, cherries, "we take nothing and leave nothing." She mentioned a russet silk dress from a magazine that she can't afford, and he said only "That sounds like a beautiful dress." It rained. He gave her his only dry flannel and stood outside in the rain so she could change. She knocked on the glass, called him back in, and unbuttoned her wet shirt with her eyes on his. He just looked, glad, and didn't touch her. He was shaking with cold, and she put his hand under the flannel against her belly. A campus cop's siren cut off whatever she was about to say. She kept the shirt.

**HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM**

**Pace.** I'm half in love and fully suspicious. Every move he makes is the one I left the genre looking for: he asks, he waits, he makes room, and his restraint is the hottest thing on the page. But he dated her because his girlfriend picked her, and he carries the details home as foreplay. I haven't been in his head since the couch, and I've noticed. Is the stillness in the truck reverence, or is he memorizing her for Randi? Probably both. He also misreads Randi badly. He thought the bench "fed" her, when she went and locked herself in. He thought her going quiet about Vee was held wanting, and I think it was jealousy. So careful and still so blind. He owes Vee the truth, and right now he doesn't know he owes it.

**Randi.** She's the one I watch hardest. Heat, pity and a chill all at once. I ache for the woman leaking tears on the leather and pressing a bottle cap into her nail bed. I go cold at "Paul?" and "It was a very cute thing, watching you not understand what was happening." She says the true thing as a joke every time. The "dear friend" hand in front of Brooke cost her something real, so some of it is real. And she wants Vee, maybe more than she can survive wanting her.

**Vee.** Protective, like she's my little sister. She's the heroine I wanted: her desire comes from inside her, and in the truck it was entirely hers. Her heat is wired to her mother's shame, though, and that worries me. Same wiring Randi has, different fuse.

**Cassie.** I'd die for her. I'm scared the book will quietly write her out, and Vee is helping it happen ("it's fine to have your own people").

**Brooke.** I don't trust her an inch. She's a threat to Randi, and maybe she's the one who exposes all this.
