**REACTION**

This is a sweet little chapter, and I couldn't fully enjoy it. Vee spinning and nearly taking out the closet door, landing in the bean bag laughing, *"Look at me."* That's real joy. I remember being twenty-one and coming home from the first good date of my life to a roommate who'd sit up for it. Cassie asking the small questions that keep it coming is exactly what a best friend does, and I love her more every chapter. "Borrow good ones" is a hug she'd never call a hug.

But the whole time I was reading, I was hearing the deadbolt from chapter one.

"Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." I know that house. I know what's under the long table in the bedroom. Vee counts it off "like a dossier, like plenty," *see, I know where he lives and everything*, and I wanted to put my hand over her mouth. Then Cassie half-jokes about state troopers and a man she met at a coffee shop. The book is having fun with my dread there, and it mostly works on me. Cassie is the one person in this story who sees anything, and she keeps getting written as just dry and funny. She said "Goodbye, Vee" in chapter four. I haven't forgotten.

The line that stopped me was Vee's: *"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose."* It's true, and that's what's unsettling. I believe her. The kiss was hers, and chapter nine earned it beautifully. He stepped back and left her the last inch. But I've read the coffee-shop scene and the pointing game, and so has the book. When it puts the jacket's thesis straight into her mouth, I can feel the author tapping the glass at me. I don't know yet whether that's a wink at her expense or a real question the book means to take seriously. That's the thing I came into this genre for and then left over. So far I trust this author more than most, because she's shown me Randi's interior and Pace's care is real on the page. But I'm watching.

Also, *rich people hike, poor people walk*. Vee doesn't know Pace built the furniture. Randi clocked the cheap laminate counters. I love that Vee's pile of "things she'd get to find out" is "the best pile there was." It's a lovely line, and it's going to hurt.

Nobody's been alone in a room with anybody this chapter. That's fine. I'd call it a breather, and it ends by setting up the hike, which is two of them alone on a trail. That's the next chapter I want.
