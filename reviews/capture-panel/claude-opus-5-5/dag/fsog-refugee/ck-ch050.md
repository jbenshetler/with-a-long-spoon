**WHO'S WHO**

**Pace (Peter).** He's 22, the mathematician who cooks everything from nothing and blows on each taste before it reaches her. Loud rooms come apart on him, and he squares his fork to the table edge. "I don't go back" is about his parents. He sewed the burgundy dress himself. He picked Vee in September, walking with Randi.
- **Daphne, finally.** She was his tutor his first year at the university, though academics were the one thing he didn't need help with. Then she was his best friend, first girlfriend, first lover, first love. **She's the only woman he ever told he loved**, and she said it back. Then she left, and there was nobody to tell, because she was the person he'd have told. So he made himself a rule: losing someone stays survivable as long as you never say the word. Other women since, a few, and he never said it to any of them. A girl with brown hair and glasses who laughed with her hand over her mouth. I think that's her.
- **He knows he loves Vee.** He admitted it to himself drunk, on New Year's Eve: "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him." He chose the smaller truth on the porch out of fear and he knows it.
- Ate freezer chicken and broccoli hungover, then sobbed at the table because a truck on the highway didn't turn in.
- At the door on Jan 15 he started **"Vee, I l—"** and she cut him off with a button.

**Vee.** She's 21, red hair, swims Tuesdays at four. The frosted plum toes have been kept up since the fitting. At midnight she called herself "a stripper's body," and it still stings. She cut her own photo at the waist for her mother's phone and sent Pace the whole one. **HOW I FEEL ABOUT EACH OF THEM**

**Pace.** I believe him more than I ever have, and I'm angrier with him than I've been. He finally owned it: fear, not decency. Daphne is a real wound, and he doesn't use her as an excuse. "Okay," and leaving her mouth to come back to her when she was ready, is exactly the consent I left a whole genre to find. So are "Those stay on" and the pancakes.

But the silence over the break was cruelty by omission, whatever he calls it. And drunk, he decided Vee "wanted it, a man would have to work at it to miss her wanting it," based on a kiss he heard about *while inside Randi*. That's him deciding her yes for her. Then there's the wiping. "Let me handle it" could be a man stepping into her life, or a man building the room. I think it's both. He's on a clock. The word was literally in his mouth, and the next time he has to say it without being cut off.
