**REACTION**

Okay. This chapter took me apart, and the whole time I knew exactly what I was being shown.

If I didn't know about the quad, the pointing, the red wine, *Tell*, this would be the courtship I've been looking for since I left the mafia books. He texts her and doesn't need an answer that night. He says "May I choose?" and she says "Yes. Please." in the same small, grateful voice Randi used on the bench, and he treats the choosing as something to weigh, not a trick. Then there's the kiss. She invokes a rule, and he doesn't push, doesn't charm her, doesn't do the patient little wait that's really pressure. He just lets it close and starts walking. She's the one who stops. She's the one who closes the distance, and even then he leaves the last inch where it is. He meets her exactly as far as she came, then backs off a breath so the second kiss has to be hers too. That's the thing I came to this genre for and almost never got: a man whose restraint is the hottest thing in the scene. I felt that second kiss in my chest. The grin she can't stop and him smiling into it made me make a noise out loud.

And I can't enjoy it cleanly, because I read chapter 7. Two nights ago he sat on that couch with Randi on his lap and fed her Vee's frozen yogurt cup like it was foreplay. So every lovely thing in this chapter does double duty. The food is the worst of it, or the best. A girl who's been "portioning herself" (that was his phrase for Randi) hasn't eaten since breakfast, and he orders for her and watches her stop watching herself eat. "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the apple again, and the cheeseburger Randi "can't eat," and this time it works. He's feeding the hungry one. I want to know if he clocked it. We never get his head this chapter, not once, and I noticed. "He'd been somewhere in himself" in the lobby. Where, Pace? On the phone to Randi? Then Christine says something at the door that makes him duck his head, and my brain went straight to: who else has he brought here? Daphne? Randi?

What's strange is that I'm not angry at him. Nothing he does here is a lie on the surface. Every yes really is hers. The jacket said that was the problem, and this is the first chapter where I actually felt it as a problem. The consent is perfect, and the choice was made about her on a lawn before she knew she'd been chosen. I'm swooning and feeling a little implicated, and I think that's the book's whole point. It isn't winking at me. It's making me hold both at once, and it's working.

The hand bit: "less like something beginning than something resolving." God. I'd text a friend: *he hasn't touched her below the collarbone and I'm more wrecked than I was by the bench.* And Cassie saying "That's not a boy. That's a man." Cassie is my person in this book, and Cassie said goodbye to her in chapter 4, and I haven't forgotten.

On how long they've been apart: they were alone at froyo, alone tonight, so I'm not starved for the pair of them. I'm aware the real heat has been offscreen since the pointing-game afternoon, but this chapter ended the way a good volume-one chapter should. Now I want to see Randi's face when he tells her.
