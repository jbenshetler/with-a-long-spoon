**REACTION**

Oh, Peter.

I'd been bracing for Randi and Pace in a room for twenty chapters, and I thought I knew what it would cost me. I didn't. It isn't that they sleep together. The jacket told me about "chose her together," and I'm a big girl. It's that it's *Monday*. Vee left that house that morning with her hair wet down her back and his coffee in her steel cup, and the deadbolt went over again that afternoon for someone else. Two chapters ago she lay under his head and thought *He's all mine.* I read that line with my whole chest. Now there's a scratch coming up on his shoulder and I don't know whose nails made it, and I hate that I'm counting.

Then the photograph. I went back to it. "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." "Never. I'd never share your photograph." I heard poetry the first time, same as she did. Now it's the loan all over again: a precise, true sentence that answers a smaller question than the one she asked. The man thanks you for information and keeps his vows in the fine print. I'm cold about it.

What wrecks me, and what keeps me from throwing the book, is that he's the one asking the right questions. "Is she happy?" "That isn't the same answer." "Does she want it?" "That isn't what I asked." "You don't have to do it for me." He keeps putting his finger on consent, Vee's and Randi's both, and Randi keeps sliding off it. He says the kind thing straight into her neck, and it undoes her. So the book isn't winking. It hands the question to the man in the room and makes him ask it twice. But "she *took* it" is the line that took the slow out of him. What he got hottest on was Vee's own wanting, told in Randi's mouth. That's either the most honest thing about him or the most dangerous, and I can't tell which.

The quad. He'd already chosen. He'd pictured her red hair and Randi's black on his pillows before he ever pointed. Then he told Randi "your face said yes before the rest of you got there," and that's the practice room. That's "did you deserve it?" with the smile brightening. He did to Randi on a lawn exactly what she did to Vee in a closet, and he did it first. Randi learned that move from him. That's the shelf, or one board of it, and it's worse than I feared and more interesting.

"Somebody has to get her used to my mouth." I nearly put it down there. That's the grooming sentence, said straight, and I've left whole subgenres over less. What kept me was the very next breath: "It's not a treat, it's *work*," and then "God, I want her," into the wood with nothing around it. Then "For us. That's all I meant." She's lying to him and to herself in the same voice. Randi isn't running a game on Vee. She's drowning in one he built and calling it her job. "I'm doing it for *you*" is the saddest line in the book so far.

And she told him to stop checking the gauge. "I know exactly what I'm doing. I have always known what I'm doing." Nobody who knows says it twice.

"Then I'll be careful with you." "You are extraordinary." That was tender, actually tender. He's careful with both of them, in ways neither one can see. And I still want him, God help me. I want him and I want to shake him and I want someone to tell Vee, and I don't want it to be today.
