REACTION

Oh, that lunch. That's the chapter, and I'm angry at it in the best way.

The Cassie scene is warm and funny. "I was grinding on him" made me whoop, and "Are you sure he's not gay?" is exactly Cassie's dry bit. But look at what Cassie is doing. She puts her hand on Vee's knee once and takes it back, and she listens to her best friend describe the happiest afternoon of her life. I love her and it hurts. Vee also tells her about the dress and the heat. She doesn't tell her about the wet satin. That's the secret she keeps for someone else.

Then Randi, and the whole book turns. The new smile is the first thing I noticed, gentler and unperformed, and it comes from someone who's been doing nothing but being near Vee. The car tucking itself in around her. The wine that costs more than the whole of Vee's order. Vee paying anyway, because she won't let Randi buy her seat. That's the class gap shown as arithmetic, and I felt it.

The interrogation is the best scene-craft in the book so far. "Nobody measures over a bra" is Randi doing what she did to Vee in chapter 9, handing her a rule so the shame can dissolve, and it works. Then the ladder: which hip, the left one, "all the best ones start at the left hip," down and down until Vee is on the edge of confessing the wetness. And Randi shuts the door. "You don't have to tell me." She bites her knuckle. She can't take it. She's the one who built this, and the thumbnail digging into the glass is the bottle cap from chapter 1. Vee reads the tightness as being moved, or as contempt, and picks the kinder reading. Dramatic irony again, and I hate how well it works.

The kiss at the curb is what I've been waiting for since the smoothie. It's soft, lips on lips, her hand at the hinge of Vee's jaw. And Vee explains it away instantly ("French"), then goes cold wondering who saw. I'm sure Brooke has eyes on that dorm. I also noticed Randi's story about the scarf and the tickling is likely invented, and the "letting go" speech is nakedly about herself.

Complaints: the car description and the restaurant ran long, the checklist habit again. And the lunch narration explains Vee's reasoning ("the kinder thing because you want it to be true") a little too much. Heat is a clothed simmer, but the booth interrogation is genuinely hot.

I want Randi's side of that drive home. Does she tell Pace? Does she know what she's done?
