I like this chapter more than I expected to, and I'm also a little impatient with it.

The pool is the first time Vee has felt like a body and not a prize. The bleachers with their "floaties" and "legs for days," her game face going on like a coat, then Cassie's "They will never know the love of a woman." I laughed. Cassie spent the whole walk ready to throw that at the bleachers and saved it for Vee, and that's the most loving thing anyone has done in this book. I'm also now wondering about Cassie, and I mean that warmly. The chapter does it quietly.

Then the underwater passage, which is the best writing here. Nobody can see her, nobody wants anything, and she grieves a little. The water is "the one part of it that had never been taken." That line makes me wonder what was taken, and I want to know. The old synchronized-swimming suit, the "more of her now," the body that "was such a problem": that's the Chi Latte cardigan and her mother's voice again. And Cassie's water trick is the opposite of asking. She doesn't make Vee say it, she just starts a fight, and the splashing wins. Cassie is the one person in this book who loves Vee without a plan.

That makes the shower scene sit oddly. Vee mooning over Pace in a towel is warm and a little hot ("what he did with his hands"). But "Don't answer tonight" is the line that snagged me. It's so Pace. It's courtly, and it's also a man controlling the tempo, and Vee reads it as odd and charming. I think it's both. He's giving her room, and he's also keeping the lead. Vee can't see that, and I can. My dread has more weight now than my crush does. I notice she never mentions Randi in her wanting-list, and I notice that I noticed.

Complaints: it's a quiet chapter, and the second one with no real heat after Tannin. I'll allow it, but I can feel the book asking me to be patient. The opening is a little muddled about whose head we're in. It starts with Cassie's suit and drifts to Vee without a seam. A few lines explain themselves too much ("it gave her nowhere to hide"). The thumb hovering over the phone at the crosswalk is a fine end, but I wanted a harder one.

I'm still reading, and I want Saturday's dinner. If the next chapter is also quiet, I'll start skimming.
