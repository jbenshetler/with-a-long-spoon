REACTION

Okay. The heat is real, and I'll give it that. The apple-slicing, the cut sides up so she eats faster, the hands that rise and turn back before they arrive. That's what I came for. The bench scene is explicit and unhurried, and it earns its length because it's building toward something other than an orgasm. When he withholds until the *please* changes, and the voice goes "lower, broken, almost ugly," I felt that. The edging works because it's about her, not about choreography.

Then the bathroom. This is where it turned from good to something I want to keep reading. The lock turning, the first time she's ever done that. Her thumbnail pressed under the bottle cap. Her whole interior monologue about the face versus the one he found, and her decision, quiet as a deadbolt, to put it back behind the face and never let him see her again. That is exactly the grown-up interiority I never get. And Pace, in the next room, is so tender and so pleased with himself, thinking "all he had done was find it, and answer it," when she's just sealed the door on him. The dramatic irony is cruel and I loved it.

But I'm confused, and I'm reading the jacket against it. The jacket says Vee, twenty-one, Randi and Pace as lovers, and Randi steering Vee toward Pace. This chapter has Randi, twenty-one, sorority, as Pace's girlfriend-ish, with no Vee anywhere. So is this a prior-timeline prologue, the "game" before it started? Randi as the one being unmade, and then a deadbolt in her ribs. It reframes the whole jacket: the woman who procures Vee is a woman who just decided never to be seen again. That's deliciously ominous. I'm suspicious that Pace's "I'm going to answer it" is how a nice man does harm.

Small gripes: the prose over-narrates. "The face was lying." "That's not what this night was for." I didn't need the book to tell me she's hiding. The "the face" refrain got heavy. And the repeated "he waited, he let her" rhythm gets a bit mechanical. Some filler like "the smell of something expensive and clean." Still, nothing near enough to put it down.

Also, the continuity of "she's been on his lap, she's been naked" etc. is fine. Consent is handled with care that's actually part of the tension: every yes is freely given, and still.

I want chapter 2 badly. I want to know who Vee is and what Randi does with that deadbolt.
