REACTION

Oh, this one is a different animal, and I noticed it right away. The chapter is a stack of three vignettes: the Friday night afterglow, the Monday talk with Cassie, and the Tuesday swim. Real heat is only in the summary of the Friday night. After a long run of explicit chapters, this one is a bridge, and I know a bridge when I see one. I don't resent it.

The dinner scene is the dramatic irony at its cruelest. Vee tells Pace all about Randi, "the funny goodbye kiss," "It's French, I guess," and Pace just mimes the cheek kisses. I stopped breathing. The reader knows Pace sleeps with Randi. And he's told her stories about the boys Randi goes out with, which are the stories Randi told Vee. Then his grin leaves and "the look from his bed" comes, and he doesn't say a word. Is he turned on by Vee describing Randi's mouth? Almost certainly. And she kisses him and says "I'll pretend it was that kind of French," and I could have screamed. She's handing him the thing and doesn't know it. The "Peter" whispered to a sleeping man is the tender hit. She loves him, and she's decided to live without the saying.

The counter scene is the sharpest small thing. He looks at her mess, says nothing, smiles. "He never said." She's reading silence as grace again, and Cassie sees it and says "You don't come home school nights." The "maybe" in answer to "Do you love him," when she just decided yes in the dark, is quietly devastating. The pool is gorgeous and wordless, and the "somebody she had built out of wanting" four strokes is exactly the doubt I've been waiting for her to have. It disappears, of course.

Gripes: no Randi on the page, a fifth chapter now without her head. Also, Vee deciding Pace's silence is love is the book repeating itself. The swim is lovely but long, and I felt the second quiet stretch.
