REACTION

Well, this is a left turn, and I'm not sure I like how it's built. I did like most of the chapter. Pace taking her to the gym is a real gift. It's the first time he's let her into his own morning, and it matches "I want to meet your people." The collar on the five-pound plate is a lovely detail. So is the way he sets up the whole workout so she can never be responsible for a weight she can't hold. That is his love again, expressed in doing rather than saying, and I felt it.

The injury is handled well. The plate rolling into her cage, the flinch, the wire in her neck, and then Pace going cold and quiet at the boys. "Apologize to the woman you hurt." That is the best he's been on the page in a while. Then the long careful tending: ibuprofen, the heat turned up before she's out of the shower, the towel, the arm across her chest.

Then the balm. It's explicit, and it's inventive. The burn climbing from her throat down toward the tips, and the fear and wanting being the same wire, is exactly what I read this book for. But it ran long, and I started skimming in the middle. The narration kept telling me what the burn meant ("she had chosen it, and she went on choosing it"), and I didn't need that. I also winced at "Demanded. Twice." She's got a torn neck and she's begging for fire, and he is steering her, and the book keeps telling me he is merciful. The "We. Not tonight" denial is a good beat. It ends on her carrying home the places he hadn't finished touching, which is a nice last line.

What I missed was the triangle. There's no Randi at all, only the "sorority thing Saturday" line. After the kiss and the glassy-eyed photo, I wanted fallout. Two weeks passed in the date stamp and nothing is said about what happened with her. That silence reads as a held breath, so I'm not angry. But I want Randi back now.
