REACTION

This is the first time the book has made me put my hand over my mouth for a reason that has nothing to do with heat.

The surface is gorgeous. Ohio casseroles, the saffron nobody opened, "Vivienne" as the name for trouble. "The name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other" is exactly the kind of line Pace would say, laid down plainly and not reaching. The "they took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" beat made me tear up. So did the desk in the hall. Pace on math is the most alive he's been. The paint-that-can't-cover-the-surface bit and "the language the world is written in" are him showing his actual self, with no technique in it. I believed every word.

Then Daphne, and the floor goes. She was nineteen. He was fifteen. Two years. And he calls it "the best two years I'd had" and "nothing happened, nobody did anything wrong." Vee does the arithmetic and swallows the word under her tongue. I did too, because that is the word. It reframes everything. Ch1's line, "Daphne had built a door, once, and he had not respected it," now reads like him having been the one on the wrong side of a different door as a child. His whole courtly asking-first creed may be built over that. It makes him more tender and more broken, and it makes his blind spots so much worse. He names a hurt "luck." He's defending it while telling it. Vee can see that. She keeps her mouth shut out of kindness, and I'm furious at how kind she is, because there's nobody in this book who says the true thing aloud.

"Peter" is a small devastating gift. The scar gets "not tonight," with his hand pressed over hers on it. I like that he didn't say "never." But I'm now guessing the scar and Daphne are wired together.

The ending is the cruelest part. Vee lying there so happy, thinking of Randi as the lucky accident, her glamorous friend she stumbled into. "She had never been so fortunate." I know it was all staged, down to the stuck integral, and she's calling it fate. Six weeks, nobody to tell her. Also, Randi is absent, and last night Randi was telling this man she's falling for the same girl. Reading the two chapters back to back made my skin crawl.

Heat is low, a simmer: the palm on her breast, bare under the robe. It's a quiet chapter after a quiet-ish one, but it earned the quiet.
