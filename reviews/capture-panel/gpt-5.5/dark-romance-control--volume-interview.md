T1

Vee leaves me protective and hungry for her to get sharper. I like her most when she stops being only wounded and starts using the wound: bare under the dress, bare under the coat, touching herself on the drive, choosing exposure before anyone can take it from her. That’s where she became interesting to me beyond “insecure girl being healed.” I don’t want her healed cleanly. I want her awake. Right now I feel tender toward her, but also a little impatient, because she keeps mistaking being cherished for being safe.

Pace leaves me frustrated in the good way and the not-good way. I believe his devotion. I believe the care, the competence, the erotic control, the kneeling in the cold. He absolutely has the “I built the room before you knew you wanted to enter it” thing that works on me. But he is also too careful, too courtly, too armored by restraint, and by the end that missing confession is not sexy restraint anymore, it is failure. I still want him, fictionally. I still bought him as a romantic threat. But I’m watching him now with narrowed eyes.

Randi is the one I’m most addicted to. She is the real danger in the book for me. Pace has the body and the room and the control, but Randi has the access. She gets Vee’s shame served to her at lunch, in dressing rooms, in confession, and she handles it like silk while steering it exactly where she wants. I do not trust her for a second. I love reading her. She is glamorous poison, and Vee thinking “friend” while Randi is practically licking the blade is the whole reason I need the next volume.

T2

Yes. I wanted the secret to land harder in Volume One, or at least cut somebody before the curtain dropped.

The book gave me a lot: heat, shame, control, Randi’s manipulation, Pace’s refusal to say the thing, Vee becoming dangerous to herself in a way I liked. But it withheld the detonation. Randi and Pace owe Vee the original truth. Not just emotionally, structurally. The whole volume keeps tightening around that fact, and by the end it is still sitting there unexploded.

I don’t necessarily feel cheated, because the final chapter paid me in the currency I came for: reckless wrongness, exposure, devotion, cold air, knees, need. But yes, the book still owes me consequence. It owes me Vee’s face when she understands she was chosen before she thought she was choosing. It owes me Randi having to stand in the blast radius. It owes me Pace losing the luxury of being the careful man.

T3

It sits badly, but productively badly.

If this were sold to me as a clean romance, I’d be furious. But I came in wanting morally gray, manipulation, obsession, people arranging each other and calling it love. So Pace deceiving Vee all volume is not automatically a dealbreaker for me. That is part of the charge. My issue is not that he lied. My issue is whether the book knows he lied and is going to make that matter.

By the end of Volume One, he is loved, trusted, and unexposed, yes. Nothing has landed on him yet. But I don’t read that as absolution. I read it as unpaid debt. The record I kept says that over and over: Pace owes Vee the truth, Randi owes Vee everything, the secret needs to create actual damage. So my Book Two purchase depends on believing the book is holding the knife, not forgetting it exists.

And I do believe that, barely but enough. The last verdict is still yes because the secret hasn’t detonated and I need to be there when it does. If Book Two lets him glide through that unscathed, then no, I’m out. But right now? I buy it. Not because Pace deserves the trust he has, but because I want to watch what happens when he loses it.
