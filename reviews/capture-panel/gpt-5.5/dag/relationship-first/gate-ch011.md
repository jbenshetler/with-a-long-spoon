REACTION —

Oh, this one got me in exactly the place I was worried about and exactly the place I wanted. Not much “happens,” except everything happens: Cassie leaves, Randi takes the hand, Vee chooses the warmer room, or thinks she does. I felt that little hinge in the first paragraph, Cassie looking past Vee to Randi and folding whatever it was away. That hurt more because Vee doesn’t watch her go. I wanted to reach into the page and say, look at your friend. Just once. But of course she can’t, because Randi has her by the wrist.

The smoothie place was almost physically awful to me. Not badly written awful, I mean I could feel my own shoulders coming up. The tall chair, the feet not touching, the cardigan suddenly an apology. That is such a precise humiliation: being made aware of your body as wrong before anyone has even said anything. And then Vee deciding to be happy because she doesn’t know what else to do with not liking it. God. I know that girl. I have been that girl in a room where everyone appears to have gotten the memo and I am wearing evidence that I did not.

Randi is so dangerous here because she is also wonderful. I hate how much I understood Vee giving the story to her. She asks exactly the right questions. She doesn’t overreach when Vee admits she liked letting Pace choose; she lets the admission sit there and become dearer because it wasn’t grabbed. And the “ask and then ravish” moment — that was hot, but more than hot it was a three-person current without Pace even in the room. Randi’s voice coming down to Vee’s volume. Vee feeling her there beside her in the fantasy. That’s the thing I’m reading for: the woman beside her, the wanting she can’t name because it can still pretend to be friendship.

And then Brooke. I wanted to hiss. That warm social cruelty where nothing said is technically unkind and somehow the table has still been weighed and priced. “Who’s your new friend?” is a whole little blade. Vee making herself smaller not for herself but “to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi” is devastating. That’s the most captured I’ve felt by Vee’s hunger for belonging since Chi Latte, maybe more, because now the belonging has a witness and a cost.

And Randi calling her “my dear friend” in front of them. I’m not immune. I’m annoyed that I’m not immune, but I’m not. It did something to me because it clearly did cost Randi something, or Vee believes it did, and either way it becomes real inside Vee. That’s the wickedness of this book when it’s working: the manipulation and the gift are not separate. Randi is recruiting her, yes. Randi is also choosing her, publicly, against some social current, and Vee receives it like water. I can’t take that away from her without becoming as cold as the scheme.

I am worried about Cassie. The book is doing something quietly cruel with how easy it is for Vee to narrate her away as “own people” and “healthy.” That sounded like an adult thought borrowed from a magazine to make abandonment feel tidy. And because Cassie has been the one real unglamorous anchor, watching Vee drift toward rooms where she is more seen and less safe is making my stomach knot.

Still: I wanted this chapter. I wanted the women talking without Pace present, and the book gave me that, except of course Pace is everywhere in the conversation. But the real scene was Vee and Randi. The hand on the table. The word dear. The terrible sweetness of being picked out loud.
