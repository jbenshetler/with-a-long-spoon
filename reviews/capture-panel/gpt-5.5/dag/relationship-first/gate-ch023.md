REACTION

Oh, this one hurt in a different register. After all that bright brunch oxygen, all that Vee-happiness expanding in the room, we come into Pace’s house and it is suddenly small, hot, portioned. The stew, the little bowl, the apples bowl, the glass poured before she arrives. I felt the care in it, and then almost immediately felt the trap of it, not because he means it as one exactly, but because this book keeps making care and control share a wall.

Randi not eating the stew was unbearable to me. That one line through the sauce. The bowl he chose for her. The bread he tore and put on her plate. Her saying “It’s wonderful” like a person trying on gentleness from too far away. I kept staring at the food like it was the chapter’s whole emotional body. She can eat salad. She can pour her own wine. She cannot take what he made for her tonight. I don’t know if that’s punishment, refusal, jealousy, self-protection, or some awful little knot of all of it, but I believed it completely.

And Pace. God, Pace. I’m still moved by him, and I’m still suspicious of him, but here I mostly felt how limited his goodness is when he cannot see outside the system he has made. “She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn’t anything.” That sentence is morally right and also not enough. It’s the best thing about him and the worst place for him to hide. Because Vee is choosing, yes, beautifully, hungrily, truly. But she is choosing inside a room where two people know the map and she doesn’t. Randi sees that, or partly sees it, but Randi’s answer is worse: hurry up, bring her to us, I am starving too.

The sex with Randi was bleak-hot, if that makes sense. Not romantic, not even really pleasurable to read in the ordinary way, but charged because it was all fracture. The absence of “tell me” was louder than the words could have been. She doesn’t want Vee through his mouth tonight. She wants to take something back from him, or prove he doesn’t get to be the careful one while she is left outside herself. Eyes shut, no pause, no performance of permission. It frightened me a little, because it made their old ritual look suddenly like a thing that had been keeping them civilized.

I’m also irritated with both of them, in the good way where I want to keep reading because the irritation has teeth. Randi calling him selfish is not wrong, but she is not clean. Pace saying every step has to be Vee’s is not wrong, but he is not clean. Nobody can stand in the true sentence long enough to let it indict them too. And poor Vee, happy in the wake of Friday, telling Randi the painting of herself in the sheet, has no idea that her joy has become an object on this table between them, untouched and cooling.

The last gesture killed me: he covers the bowl and puts it away “for her.” That is tenderness. That is denial. That is a man saving the portion she refused because he still thinks the problem is something he can hold warm for later.
