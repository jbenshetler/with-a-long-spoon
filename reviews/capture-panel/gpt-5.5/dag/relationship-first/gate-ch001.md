REACTION —

Oh. Well. That was not shy about showing me exactly what kind of book it is going to be.

I’m a little shaken by how quickly I was inside Pace’s attention. Not even the spanking, really, though yes, that is where the chapter is pointed and it absolutely knows it. It was the apple. The thin slices. The cut sides up because he knows she’ll eat them faster that way. That did something to me before the bench ever appeared, because it told me he is not just looking at her body, he is looking at the little humiliating systems she has built to survive herself. And that is much more intimate than undressing her.

The heat worked on me. I mean, obviously. The bench is theatrical, almost absurdly so, but the chapter makes it domestic first: hidden under a table, in a small house, after chapter dinner, with Steely Dan playing low and apples in a bowl. That contrast is nasty in the good way. It made the object feel less like a prop and more like a secret that had been sitting in the room all along, waiting for her understanding to catch up.

But what I really noticed is that I trusted Pace almost until I didn’t, and that “almost” is the book’s whole engine, I think. He is careful. He checks in. He waits. He does not rush her. He seems, in every immediate scene-choice, attentive and ethical. And yet the chapter keeps putting me in his head long enough to feel the pleasure he takes in “finding” her, and I started to feel that old alarm: the man who thinks he knows where the real woman is hiding. Sometimes he’s right. Sometimes that is the most dangerous thing about him.

Randi in the bathroom got me. That was the turn. Until then I was in the heat and the precision of it, but the bathroom made the whole chapter suddenly sadder and more frightening. Not because she regretted the scene exactly. She doesn’t. That’s the awful, alive part. She has been changed by something she wanted, and the change feels like exposure, and she decides instantly to become unavailable to it. I believed that completely. The face going back on. The smile settling. Horrible. Perfect.

I’m also immediately aware of the jacket now in a different way. I was expecting Vee, Randi, Pace. Instead I get Randi and Pace already in this deep private structure, with Daphne somewhere behind him like a locked room of his own. So now the “plan” has a history before Vee even enters. That makes me more interested, not less, because Randi is not just glamorous friend / accomplice. She is already compromised, already hungry, already lying to herself.

I did feel a few tiny repetitive pressure points: “he waited,” “he let,” “he watched,” “there you are.” But in this chapter the repetition mostly felt ritualistic rather than lazy. It started to become the rhythm of his control. I’m watching whether the book knows that rhythm can seduce the reader too, because I can feel it doing exactly that.

I would absolutely read the next chapter. I’m wary, but I’m not bored. I want to know what Randi does with the lie she just put back on her face.
