Oh, Randi.

Not just hot, not just plotted, not just “Pace and Randi made a game and Vee is walking into it,” but Randi getting injured by exactly the thing she wanted. Something happened in the truck that he is keeping from Randi, and I believe it is partly protective and partly possessive and partly because he himself doesn’t know what to do with it yet. That is exactly the kind of moral tangle that keeps me in a book.

Randi leaving wrecked me more than the sex did. She has always been able to put the face back on, but this was different: she got what she came for and fled the bed like staying would mean admitting something she cannot admit. “I want us” came out of her as if she meant all three and could only say it while being split open by him. Then afterward she can’t stay for soup or eggs or whatever he was going to make. She can’t let the want become domestic. She can use Vee’s body as a flame in Pace’s bed, but she cannot yet sit in the warm kitchen and be a person who wants what she wants.

He thinks it’s just the want, because he is also narrating his way around feelings he doesn’t want to have. But he’s lonely in that doorway. For the first time, I felt him not as the conductor of the arrangement but as someone who can be hurt by it.

I am very keyed up now. Also wary. Vee is radiant in his flannel somewhere else, telling Cassie the version of the story that belongs to her, while here Randi and Pace are feeding on the version that belongs to them. The book is letting all three versions exist, which is why I’m still trusting it.
