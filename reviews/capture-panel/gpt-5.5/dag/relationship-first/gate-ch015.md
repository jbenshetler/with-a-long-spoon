REACTION —

Oh, I loved this one. Not because anything enormous happens, exactly, but because Vee is back among women and the book lets her be witnessed by people who are not Randi, not Pace, not Cassie. I needed that. Kayla and Meg are not “lifeline” friends the way Cassie is, maybe, but they know how to sit around a dining hall table and make a girl’s story bigger and funnier and more bearable by passing it back and forth. This is one of the things I read for: the social digestion of romance. The “tell the shirt” of it. The ritual of turning a private, shaking thing into a story with fries.

And Vee is so happy here. Loud, easy, beloved in a room, stealing fries, waving at Theo, doing Pace’s voice. I felt this huge relief that she has other people. Not just Cassie-as-alarm-system, not just Randi-as-doorway-to-the-world, but girls who know enough to tease her and enough to listen. It made her life feel wider again. Which makes the danger better, honestly. The narrower a book makes a woman’s world around a man, the more I start resisting it; here, I can feel all the doors she still has, even as this one particular door is glowing at the end of the chapter.

The way she tells the shirt story also did something sly to me. I know what happened in that truck. I know the middle she leaves out, and the chapter knows I know. So watching her say “he got out so I could change” and then skip to “he was shivering” is delicious and painful. She is preserving something without quite lying. It belongs to her. I liked that so much. She doesn’t hand this part to Kayla and Meg, maybe because it is too hot, maybe because it is too intimate, maybe because saying it would change it from a thing she did into a story other people could manage. Good for her. Keep one match in your own pocket, Vee.

And then the text. God, the way a short message can rearrange a whole table. “Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o’clock.” That is absolutely a ransom note, Kayla is correct, and I laughed because it is also exactly Pace: formal, spare, almost maddeningly under-explained. But because I have the jacket and I have the bench/table in my head, “his house” is not innocent. It is the room prepared before she knows there is a room. It is the thing I have been waiting for and dreading.

What I’m sitting with is that Vee is moving toward it with such ordinary, girlish, funny anticipation: what do I wear, what does two o’clock mean, is it dinner, is it sex, is it an afternoon. That ordinary texture makes the larger arrangement feel worse and hotter at the same time. The book is still honoring her yes. It’s letting her want the door. But I am reading with my hand half over my mouth now, because Saturday is not just a date. Saturday is architecture.
