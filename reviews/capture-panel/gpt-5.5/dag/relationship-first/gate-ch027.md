I loved the stillness of it. Not in a “nothing happened” way, because so much happened, but because the book finally let Vee and Pace be two people under a quilt with cold on the glass, talking long enough for the erotic charge to become almost indistinguishable from trust. His hand on her breast while they talk about casseroles and names and math should not be as intimate as it is, and yet it may be more intimate than half the sex in the book. The fact that she doesn’t mind the robe falling open because he is there looking softly, not taking, just warming himself at her — that undid me a little.

The food conversation was exactly my kind of thing. Casseroles, Cool Whip containers, unopened saffron old enough to vote — yes. And then Pace finding the affection inside the beige food instead of just letting her mock it. That is why she keeps opening with him. He hears the hidden tenderness before the joke has finished landing.

And Peter. Oh, Peter. I felt Vee doing the right thing over and over here: not rushing him, not plastering pity over him, not saying “that’s sad” when he doesn’t need that from her. Daphne, though. There she is. And it is worse and more tender than I expected. I’m very glad Vee clocked the arithmetic. Fifteen and nineteen, then seventeen and twenty-one, and him defending it as luck because it was also the best two years he’d had. I don’t think the book is asking me to tidy it into villain/victim in one sentence, but it absolutely let the ugly word form and stay unsaid. That felt merciless in the right way.

The scar still being withheld worked for me. “Not tonight” could have been maddening, but because he keeps her hand there, because it’s a door and not a wall, I believed it. I also noticed the repetition: not tonight, not yet, she’s not ready. This book is full of doors with time written on them.

The reader knows there was a reason, or at least a plan. The happiness is real. The kindness is real. But she is lying in the middle of a coincidence that was not coincidence, and the book knows exactly how cruel that is.

I want the next chapter badly, but not in a frantic plot way. I want to stay in the ache. That triangle is no longer just erotic. It is becoming an archive of who gets which truth.
