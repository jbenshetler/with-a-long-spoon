Oh, this one got under my skin in a quieter, more dangerous way than the dress chapter did.

Randi eating. Randi asking for food. Randi taking his shirt from the drawer like she belongs there, like some domestic animal part of her has finally come inside after circling the porch for twenty chapters. That felt enormous to me. Not because “woman eats meal” is plot, but because with her it is. Last chapter she left the bowl untouched with that single line through the sauce, like a little autopsy of her refusal, and now she sits in his undershirt with her feet tucked up and eats almost everything he gives her. I felt Pace notice it and not say it, and I loved him for not saying it. Some things would die under being named too early.

Vee in his sheet, Randi in his shirt, Pace at the stove, all of them already smiling about each other. That image is exactly the invitation of the whole book, and it scared me because it was so beautiful. This is the trap, isn’t it? Not the wickedness. The table. The warm kitchen. The being fed and seen and talked about with such tenderness that the secrecy starts to feel like only a technical problem, when it is not technical at all.
