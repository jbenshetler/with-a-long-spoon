REACTION —

Oh, I loved this one in that complicated way where I am half warmed through and half sitting up straighter because Randi is so good at this it scares me.

The morning after the bad night felt exactly right to me: not melodrama, not collapse, but that awful over-bright competence after shame. The cold water, the bare legs in October, the apple, the “I’m going to fix this” putting length into her stride. That is Vee at her best to me: not graceful, not magically healed, but suddenly practical because she has decided she will not keep rotting in herself. I believed every inch of that walk.

And then the sorority house. God. Brooke at the door with the smile and the folder already open on Vee: “the stats friend.” That made my skin prickle. The house knowing her before she knows the house feels like a smaller version of the whole larger danger of the book. Vee thinks she’s arriving somewhere; people have already placed her.

Randi without makeup got me. The freckles. The robe. The room smelling like bitter orange and sleep. I am weak for women being real in the morning, and the chapter knows it. I also think the book knows that this is exactly why Randi is most dangerous here. She is not glittering across a restaurant table; she is up five minutes after sleep, letting Vee into her room, holding her hand while Vee tells the worst thing she did. That kind of intimacy is almost impossible to refuse.

And she is so, so good with Vee. Too good. She gives Vee the emotional shape she needs: you were jealous because you care, he chose you, you can fix this. It’s not false, which is what makes it powerful. I think she’s right that Pace leaving room is not sulking. I think she’s right that Vee shouldn’t show up in panic. But every right answer from Randi comes with a hand on the steering wheel. Vee leaves not with her own plan sharpened, but with “marching orders.” That phrase at the end landed hard for me. It knows.

The clothing scene absolutely worked on me. It was funny first, blessedly funny — Edna Mode, the two of them cracking up, the release of it. I needed that after chapter 28. And then it slid, very naturally and therefore very effectively, into charge. Randi circling her in the bra; Randi saying, “He doesn’t stand a chance”; the shoes under the long skirt, all touch and no sight. That was hot because it was half fitting room, half ritual, half something neither of them is naming. Yes, I know that’s three halves. So does this book.

The shoes under the skirt were the strongest erotic beat for me. Not because it’s explicit, but because the chapter has taught me by now how Vee responds to being held into shape: Pace’s dance frame, the dress, the good shoe, the lead arriving before she thinks. The language of structure keeps coming back. Frame, tension, architecture, held. I am noticing the repetition, and I’m not tired of it yet because it keeps changing hands. With Pace, being held lets her move. With Randi, being styled lets her become visible. But the question under both is: when does being held become being arranged?

And the kiss at the end. Oh, Vee. “It was only what they did.” That sentence is a little trapdoor. She is acclimating. The thing that would have sent her checking windows is now part of the weather. I wanted to shake her gently by the shoulders and say: darling, that is not nothing just because it has become familiar. But also I understand why she doesn’t want it to be something yet, because if it is something, everything gets more complicated than she can survive before tomorrow night.

I’m still very much in. More than in. The chapter did what I wanted after the dance disaster: it didn’t make Vee only Pace’s wounded girlfriend. It gave her a woman’s room, a friend’s hands, jokes, clothes, money/class shadows, a whole emotional apparatus around apology. But it also made the Randi problem brighter. Vee is going to Pace tomorrow dressed by Randi, steadied by Randi, carrying Randi’s shoes and scarf, and that means Randi will be in the room even if she isn’t there. Which is delicious and worrying and exactly the kind of mess I want from this book.
