REACTION —

Oh. Well. That is a first chapter with its hand already on the back of my neck.

I went into this braced because the jacket is basically waving the red flag itself: two people have planned her year, she doesn't know, every yes freely given, etc. And then the chapter gives me Randi, not Vee, and I immediately had that little “wait, interesting” feeling. Because if this is the glamorous friend from the jacket, the one who is going to be steering Vee, then opening on *her* being cracked open like this is not nothing. It complicates her before she can become the dangerous queer woman with a plan, which I very much needed.

The bench scene got to me. It did. I’m not going to pretend I was sitting there clinically taking notes. The slowness, the care, the apple, the lights, the way he keeps finding the places where she has made herself untouchable by being perfectly touchable — yes. That worked. The apple slices especially, honestly. There is something devastating about him noticing the thickness she’ll allow herself to eat. It’s erotic because it’s attention, and it’s frightening because attention that precise can feed you or undo you.

I liked Pace more than I expected to, and I don’t fully trust that liking yet. He is careful. He checks in. He notices. He gives her time. He does not make her orgasm about his own immediate gratification, which is hot partly because it is so rare in this kind of scene and partly because the book knows the power in restraint. But also: he is very convinced he understands what happened. Maybe more convinced than he should be. The moment he thinks “there you are” and then, catastrophically, says “There you are, Randi,” I felt my stomach drop. Because he’s not wrong, exactly, but he also doesn’t get to name that for her. And the chapter knows that. Thank god the chapter knows that.

Randi in the bathroom is what made me sit up. That whole face/person split, the face as the thing she has sent out into the world so nothing reaches her, is exactly the kind of psychological erotica I show up for. It also scared me, because now we have a woman who has had a true experience and immediately decides never again, while the man in the other room thinks they had a breakthrough. That is delicious and awful. The lock on the bathroom door hit harder than the spanking. The lock was the real event.

Queer-wise, I’m watching this book like a hawk. Opening with Randi being the one who is secretly more vulnerable than her eventual role might suggest buys a lot of patience from me. It does not erase the warning signs in the premise, but it tells me the book may know the difference between a manipulator and a person who manipulates because she is also trapped inside something. If Randi later becomes the woman who draws Vee open, I badly want that desire to belong to Randi too, not just as a tactic, not just as garnish. This chapter makes me think the book might actually pay that bill.

Also, “the size of a key turning in a lock” is exactly the kind of ominous little erotic click I hate being susceptible to, and yet here we are. I am susceptible.
