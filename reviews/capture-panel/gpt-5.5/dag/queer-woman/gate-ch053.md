REACTION —

Oh, I am tense in about six directions.

The first part got me immediately, because the relief of leaving home felt so true: not “I hate my family,” not melodrama, just that awful small grief of realizing the room that loves you is loving an archived version. Vee on the plane, exhausted and almost okay because campus exists, because Cassie’s highlighter exists, because Randi at brunch exists, because the pool exists. And then the road to Pace’s house re-entering her body before her mind can stop it. Yes. That is how a place undoes you.

And then Randi. God. This chapter is doing the exact thing I’m most wary of and also the exact thing that keeps me reading, which is irritatingly effective. Randi is so good with Vee here. Too good. She gets her out of the public room, holds her, lets her cry, asks the right questions, separates fear from evidence, gives her back the parts of Pace that are real. I felt grateful to her in the same breath I was watching her steer. Because she is steering. She reframes the whole fight as Vee cornering him, which is not false, but it is also not the whole truth. Pace was cornered because he has built a life around not saying the thing. Vee did not invent that wound by asking to be named.

And then the coat. My whole body went “oh no” and “yes, obviously” at once. It is hot because Vee wants it. It’s hot because the shame-word comes up in her own mouth and Randi walks her through it with such merciless tenderness. But it also scared me because Randi is so practiced at turning Vee’s panic into a beautiful assignment. “Words are the whole war” is such a dangerous sentence here. Vee’s problem was not that she used words. Vee’s problem is that Pace won’t meet her in them. So sending her back with her body as the answer feels both gorgeously genre-satisfying and emotionally rigged. I can feel how it might heal them for a night and leave the central wound intact.

The shoes nearly knocked me flat, though. I know Vee tells herself the response is about Pace and tonight, and maybe it partly is, but come on. Randi hands her these impossible black shoes, and Vee’s body answers before she has language. The book knows what it is doing there. It has to. That is not garnish. That is Vee being erotically authored by Randi, yes, but also Vee’s own body lighting up in the space between them. I’m watching very closely for whether the book lets that wanting belong to Vee, not just to Randi’s design.

And then Vee asks for Randi’s real New Year, and Randi edits herself so painfully. The fairytale is sellable. The sex is not, because the truth of it would expose the whole lack underneath. Vee reads the silence wrong, of course, because she still thinks Randi is the girl who has everything. That hurt. Randi calling him perfect except the bedroom made me laugh, but it also felt like a little locked drawer sliding shut.

Then the kiss under the composites. Oh. That got me. Vee comes back because gratitude is too small, because something in her chest needs somewhere to go, and she takes Randi’s face in both hands. That detail is everything. She is not passively receiving the friend-kiss. She turns around. She goes back. She holds Randi still. She kisses her until the feeling has somewhere to go. I am not pretending that is nothing. Randi certainly isn’t.

And Brooke. Brooke just walked in with the social reality of the house in her hands and set it down like a knife on a table. I appreciated that so much more than if the chapter had let the kiss float in a private erotic bubble. The house sees. The house knows. The house permits performance and punishes truth. “Play” is allowed. Reality is dangerous. That is exactly the pressure that makes Randi’s whole life make sense, and it also makes her more dangerous, because she has just been told to choose, and I do not trust what Randi does when she is cornered.

I’m very pulled, but uneasy. Not in a bad way. In the “this could become the book I’m hoping it is, or it could break my trust spectacularly” way. Vee is going to Pace tonight with Randi’s shoes and Randi’s script and Randi’s kiss still warm somewhere in the chapter. That is not a peace offering. That is a fuse.
