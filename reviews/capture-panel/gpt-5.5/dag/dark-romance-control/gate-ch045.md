REACTION —

Oh, this one finally made me sit up.

This is the first time in a while that the tenderness turned sharp enough for me. Not because Pace suddenly became cruel, exactly, but because he *would not give her the word*. He has built this whole heated chapel around her life, stocked it with food and wine and warmth and sex and attention, and then when she asks him to name the thing outside the house, he gives her philosophy. “Words are cheap.” Sir. SIR. Cheap and yet apparently locked in a vault.

And Vee, finally. Finally she doesn’t fold herself prettily around his evasions. She comes in without the bag. That hit me immediately. She is not arriving as the soft weekend girl with the drawer and the shirt and the hunger. She is arriving as someone who might leave. I loved that she stayed on the mat. I loved that she wouldn’t lift her mouth for the kiss. It felt like her body, for once, refusing the ritual before the ritual could drug her.

The house almost got her anyway. Of course it did. The heat, the wine already poured, the chicken, the piano, the smell of him. That’s the whole trap and the whole pleasure of this book: everything he does is loving, and also it makes escape feel like self-harm. She hates that the warmth moves through her because she knows how trained she is now. That is the dark-romance nerve for me. Not chains. A door unlocked and a girl who can barely bear to walk back through it.

Pace’s “You’re the most important person in my life” should have been enough in some softer book, and here it absolutely is not. Because she is not asking for intensity. She has intensity. She is drowning in intensity. She is asking for public reality. For a handle she can carry into daylight. And his answer about whether any of her friends have a relationship she’d rather have? Gorgeous and infuriating. It is true, and it dodges her. He keeps turning her toward gratitude when she is asking for claim.

And I am suspicious now in the good way. Why can’t he say it? Is it just his wound, his family, Daphne, whatever trained him to believe actions are safer than words? Or is the secret plan underneath this, the original game, making the word impossible because some part of him knows it would be theft to say it before she knows everything? That would make me feral. If he can feed her, undress her, photograph her, make space for her in his house, let her fall all the way in, but cannot call her his because the foundation is dirty? Yes. That’s the rot under the silk I came for.

“Words are cheap, and you still won’t spend them on me” is the kind of line I would text someone at 1 a.m. with fourteen exclamation points. It’s clean and mean and completely fair. And then he folds his arms. Oh, Pace. That was the moment he became dangerous again for me, because his self-control stopped looking purely generous and started looking like withholding. Like a line he will maintain even while she breaks in front of him.

The ending got me. His hand lifting toward her, to fix it with touch because touch is the language he trusts, and her refusing the hand in place of the word. Deliciously painful. She walks into the cold, and the cold is worse, and she still doesn’t go back in. That is the first real leverage she has had in chapters. I don’t know if she can hold it, but I want to see him feel the absence. I want him rattled. I want Randi to catch the fallout and either comfort her too well or use it too well. Preferably both.
