Oh. Oh, Randi.

This chapter got me in the exact place I like being got: not because John is dangerous, but because he is the whole polished cage made flesh. He is literally perfect on paper: tall, rich, white tie, law school, skis better than her without rubbing it in, conjures champagne and sleighs and hotel rooms like the universe is on retainer. And I felt myself almost fall for it with her. The mountain, the invented wife game, the ball, the blue dress, the Byron line dying in his mouth — yes, fine, I am not immune to a man who can produce a sleigh at midnight in the snow. I am only human.

But the whole time there was this horrible clean hollowness under it. John assumes. Pace asks. And this chapter made that difference filthy in both directions. Because with Vee, Pace’s asking has started to feel like a locked room: she has to say everything, expose everything, and he still withholds the name. But with Randi, John’s not-asking is briefly intoxicating and then completely dead. He takes her up because of course he does, and she likes not being asked until she realizes he never actually found her. That is such a nasty mirror. The acceptable man can take the beautiful body and leave the hidden self untouched. Pace, who does not belong anywhere near her approved life, gets under the skin from across the room.

And Vee. God. The second Vee enters the chapter from far away, the temperature changes. Randi can sleep with the fairy-tale man in the fairy-tale room after the fairy-tale ball and still what undoes her is Vee texting from Ohio with that little yellow heart. “I’m here if you want to talk. or not.” That is obscene tenderness, and Randi lying back with the “perfect nothing” after sending the performative version of the night made me ache. She is doing exactly what Pace is doing to Vee, in a different key: giving true things with the center removed. Every word true. None of it the thing.

And yes, I am thrilled that Randi knows. Not vague brunch-flirtation knows. Not “best friend” with a little heat around the edges. She knows. She wants Vee, loves Vee, is choosing concealment because keeping access matters more than being clean. That is the knife. “Let Vee arrive at it on her own” is such a beautiful little moral crime. She is not brave enough to risk losing her, so she will make the wanting deniable and let Vee walk herself closer. Delicious. Terrible. Exactly.

Also, her parents are poison in crystal glasses. The father making jokes about hearts broken, the mother all designer edges, the bracelet locked on her wrist — Randi learned performance at a table where being real would get eaten. So when she performs the fairytale to Vee, I hate it, but I believe every inch of it.

This chapter pulled me hard because it makes Randi not just a corrupter but a captive of her own cowardice and hunger. I don’t want her redeemed. I want her cornered. I want Vee to find out she has been managed by two people who both genuinely wanted her and still chose the arrangement over the truth. That reveal is starting to feel explosive now.
