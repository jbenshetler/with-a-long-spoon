REACTION — Oh, this chapter knew exactly where to put its fingers.

I’m almost annoyed by how effectively it got me, because on paper this is “man helps girl with homework in coffee shop,” which should not be this charged. But it is charged because it’s all attention. The pencil. The cuff rolling. The way he doesn’t perform intelligence at her, he uses it to give her back her own competence. I felt Vee’s little internal door open when he handed the pencil back. That is absolutely the kind of thing that would wreck me at twenty-one and, apparently, still works on me now.

And Randi. God. Randi sitting there while the thing she helped arrange starts happening in front of her. Her coffee cup coming up and not being sipped. Her giving the “go on, this is nice” smile while clearly feeling something more complicated under it. I love that the book lets me be ahead of Vee without making Vee stupid. Vee reads the little hand business as boredom because why wouldn’t she? She doesn’t have the blurb in her lap. I do. Delicious.

Pace is dangerous in the exact way the jacket promised: not because he’s pushy, but because he’s precise. “May I call you?” after all that quiet competence is such a tiny formal thing and it landed hotter than half the dirty talk in other books. He doesn’t need to swagger. He makes the room settle around him, and then he asks permission in a way that makes permission feel like a gift both ways. That is my catnip, unfortunately for me.

Cassie keeps snagging my attention too. She’s not fooled, or at least she’s not warmed into compliance by Randi the way Vee is. Her “great-aunt” line made me bark a laugh, but it also put a tiny chill under the scene, because she sees cleanly. I don’t know yet if she’s a witness, a warning system, or just the one person in the room whose social bloodstream hasn’t been sweetened.

This chapter wasn’t explicit, but it had heat. Not body heat exactly, more like the first electrical contact. The math metaphor could have gone corny so easily, and instead it worked on me: two things at right angles becoming one thing you can finally solve. Yes, okay, I see you, book. I’m leaning in.
