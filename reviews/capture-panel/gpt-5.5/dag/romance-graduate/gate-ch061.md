REACTION —

Oh, this got me in the ribs.

I was so ready to hate Sheri. Not even hate her fairly, just reader-hate her on Vee’s behalf because my body has been braced since “Sheri” first floated in as a threat. And the book knew that. It let me build the blonde right alongside Vee, tall and golden and unbearable, and then opened the door on this tiny loud queer woman with bad roots and too much makeup and more reality in her first five seconds than the fantasy rival ever had. I laughed when Vee laughed. That release felt physical. Like, oh, the monster under the bed is not the thing. The thing is still jealousy, but it’s not the woman you invented to carry it.

And Sheri looking at Vee. God. That was such a good little turn of the screw. Not a threat-look, not a ranking-look, not a woman assessing another woman for male ownership, but pure appreciation. Vee came armed for war and got admired. The heels, especially, being both ridiculous and sacred because they’re Randi’s shoes, and then Sheri’s eye catches there and Vee suddenly understands she is being enjoyed. That is so deliciously complicated because the Randi current is in those shoes whether Vee admits it or not. She dressed against Sheri using Randi’s armor, and Sheri looked at her like a woman. The book is basically laying tripwires under every “just girls” category Vee owns.

Also Pace blushing. I am not made of stone. Sheri saying “motorboat them things” and Pace going red to his ears did something undignified to me. I love that his competence finally has a place where it can be punctured without him being diminished. With Vee’s friends he was careful and generous and a little astonishing; with Sheri he is known. Teased. Claimed in the way old friendship claims you. She has a license nobody else has, and instead of making me jealous it made him fuller.

The chapter did something I badly needed after the Randi/Pace reveal: it gave Pace back some moral weight without excusing him. I am still angry. I have not forgotten the locked room. But Sheri’s story is devastating because it shows that his tenderness is not performance, not seduction technique, not just something he spends on Vee because she is the chosen object of the plan. He does this. He shows up. Coffee, donuts, a lock, a faucet, a bike ride, no fake comfort words. Waiting with headlights on until the porch light comes on. That is not sexy in the obvious way, and yet it is wildly sexy because it is character under pressure. It makes the betrayal hurt worse, honestly, because now I have even less room to flatten him into villainy.

Sheri herself: instant trust. Not safe as in bland, safe as in she knows exactly where the knives are and doesn’t wave them around for fun. Her volume is camouflage and invitation at once. The moment she drops it to tell Vee the real story, I felt myself lean in. And the line about strangers saying “it’ll be all right” for free, while the people who owed her words weren’t spending any — ouch. That’s grown-up writing. That’s not trope wallpaper. That is a whole life appearing at the table.

And Vee. Sweetheart. Dressing like a weapon, then realizing she misunderstood the battlefield entirely. I love that she gets to be ridiculous and tender and a little drunk and not punished for it. I love that she sees Pace’s back differently after Sheri tells her what he did. She is collecting evidence of who he is, and all of it is true, which is the knife. “Every yes was freely given” keeps getting heavier because the world around the lie is so real.

This was a quieter chapter in terms of heat, but not cold at all. It had charge everywhere: Vee’s jealousy, Sheri’s look, Pace’s blush, the secret history, the truck ride with Vee tucked against him. No explicit sex, but honestly I didn’t miss it here because the chapter moved the emotional architecture hard. It widened Pace’s world, replaced a fake rival with a real woman, and made me want the dance hall immediately. Also, yes, Sheri is going to see more than people think. She may already have.
