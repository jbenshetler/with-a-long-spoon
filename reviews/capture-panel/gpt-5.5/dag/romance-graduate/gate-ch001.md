REACTION — Oh. Well. That did not throat-clear.

I’m a little rattled, honestly, in the best and worst way. The jacket told me the book was going to let me be ahead of Vee, but this opening with Randi is such a nasty-smart choice because I’m immediately inside the machinery before the “main” girl even arrives. And it’s not coy machinery. It’s not vibes and a tasteful hand on a waist. It is a custom-built bench, apple slices, a man who notices how thin she’ll let herself eat them, and a woman who has spent her whole life being a face suddenly forced into a body. That is exactly the kind of charged I’m hungry for: not just “hot thing happens,” but “hot thing happens because somebody’s defenses have been mapped with terrifying care.”

The sex worked on me, very much. The slowness, the position, the checking in, the way his restraint is the erotic engine instead of a pause before the “real” scene. I especially felt the difference between him refusing her first “please” and waiting for the voice underneath it. That could have been corny or dom-man theatrical, but it landed because the book had already shown me her face as armor. So when she breaks, it feels psychological before it feels sexual, which makes it hotter. Annoyingly hot. Like, yes, fine, I am seated.

But I’m also on alert. Pace is attractive in a way that is dangerous to me as a reader: tender, precise, observant, and maybe too pleased with what he can see. The chapter is smart enough to let me enjoy him and then make me distrust the enjoyment. He thinks this was a remarkably good evening while she is literally rebuilding the mask in the bathroom. That gap is delicious and awful. He’s not a cardboard manipulator; he has ethics, he checks in, he respects the locked bathroom door. And still, he misses the thing that matters most because the thing that matters most is what she hides best. That’s a much more interesting danger than “bad man bad.”

Randi surprised me. From the blurb I had her filed as glamorous puppetmaster friend, but this chapter makes her wounded and hungry and not nearly as in control as she probably thinks she is. I’m already suspicious of how her future steering of Vee is going to be tangled up with this moment: jealousy, displacement, wanting to watch someone else survive what she couldn’t, wanting access to the hunger without being the one on the bench again. I don’t know, but my brain is doing that pleasant feral Kindle thing where it starts making theories before I’ve even turned the page.

The only thing that gave me a tiny “hmm” was the density of observation. It is lush, almost relentless. Most of the time I loved it because the whole point is being seen past performance, but once or twice I could feel the prose pressing the bruise with a very deliberate thumb. Still, I didn’t skim. Not once. And for a chapter this long and this interior, that says a lot.

What I’d text a friend at 1 a.m.: “This is filthy but in a therapy-wound way and I think I’m in trouble.”
