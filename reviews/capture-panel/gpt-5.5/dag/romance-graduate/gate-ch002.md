Oh, this got under my skin in a completely different way than the bench did.

Chapter 1 was heat, obviously, but it was also that very specific erotic nightmare of being *found* by someone who knows exactly where to put his hand and exactly when to stop. This chapter is the morning after, and somehow it made me more tense than the spanking. Because the danger isn’t Pace in the room now. It’s the girls. It’s Brooke with the laptop and the friendly voice and the “Pace, that’s actually his name?” It’s the whole house smelling blood while pretending to smell coffee.

I hated how much I recognized that kind of feminine social violence. Nothing “happens.” Everyone smiles. Every question has plausible deniability. Brooke doesn’t have to accuse her of anything because the hierarchy is already doing the work. And Randi knows the machinery because she *is* part of the machinery. Standards. God. Of course she is Standards. That landed like a slap.

And Randi coming home in last night’s clothes after what happened to her emotionally, sexually, almost spiritually, and having to convert it instantly into a socially acceptable anecdote? That was so awful and so good. The girls make Pace smaller before we even see him through their eyes: truck, grad student, “short-cute,” math recalibrated into something still faintly embarrassing. After all that intensity, all that “there you are,” she has to stand there and let him become a little joke in the common room because admitting his power over her would be worse.

The second half almost hurt more because nothing dramatic happens. She goes to her room and can’t perform even for herself. Closet, vanity, sink, bed. All those little stations of control, and none of them working. The palette in the closet, the single room as a reward for Standards, the clutch going where the clutch lives. She is so curated that even her breakdown has nowhere to go. Lying on top of the made bed in her shoes felt devastatingly young, too. Twenty-one suddenly felt very twenty-one.

And that line turning on her — “He’s hard to say no to” — oof. In the kitchen it was flirtation, social cover, a cute way to explain wanting him without seeming hungry. In her room it becomes something she can’t look at directly. I don’t read this as “he violated her,” exactly, and that’s what’s making the book interesting and uncomfortable. She said yes. She wanted it. She was alive in it. And also something in her is terrified because yes has consequences when someone sees the person behind the face.

Still completely in. Maybe even more in, because this chapter proves the book isn’t just going to stack erotic set pieces. It’s going to make her live with them.
