REACTION —

Oh, this one got me in that very specific post-holiday, cafeteria-light place where nothing dramatic is happening and yet the whole hidden life is sitting between the trays.

The title is doing so much work on me: *Cropped*. The photo is cropped for her parents, yes, but also Pace is cropped. Vee has to cut him down to the shape her family can hold: math PhD candidate, hiking, dancing, boyfriend with a question mark. Not the man who made the dress. Not the man who takes it off. Not the man whose house she is half-living in. Not the man whose shirt she is sleeping in for scent like a Victorian lunatic in the best possible way. She is not lying exactly, and that is what makes it worse. She is editing.

And God, her father “pricing it” without saying so. That is so real it made my stomach drop. The way parents can read objects as danger. A dress is not just a dress; it is money, obligation, sex, adulthood, some man’s claim, some part of your daughter you didn’t authorize into existence. And Vee knows him well enough to manage every possible reaction before it happens. That’s love, but it’s also exhausting. She is doing all this internal translation for everyone.

The sewing detail hurt in a surprising way. Because she’s right: a father might survive “he wants me” more easily than “he made something beautiful for me with his own hands because I wanted it.” Sex has a drawer in people’s heads. Gifts have a drawer. Boyfriends have a drawer. But Pace sewing the dress doesn’t go in the drawer. It’s too intimate, too domestic, too strange, too much evidence of attention. And Vee is proud of it, fiercely, but she can’t bring it home whole.

Cassie remains my oxygen. “So what is he. Not to your dad. To you.” That is exactly the question. No sparkle, no harvesting, no performance. Just: stop arranging the story for someone else and tell me what you know. And Vee can’t. Or won’t. Both. That answer felt painfully honest. It also made me want to shake her gently, because the inability to name him is starting to matter. She can live inside the thing, but she cannot describe it from the outside. And there is still another enormous cropped-out piece: Randi.

This was quiet, but not empty. It’s the book reminding me that Vee’s secrecy isn’t only Pace and Randi’s fault. She is learning to crop herself too. Not maliciously. Not even consciously, half the time. But she is becoming very good at choosing which version of the truth each person gets, and that skill is going to absolutely ruin me when the truths stop staying in their assigned rooms.
