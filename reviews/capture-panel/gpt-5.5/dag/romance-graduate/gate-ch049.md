Oh, this one hurt in exactly the quiet way I was afraid the book knew how to hurt me.

No sex, no scene, barely any movement, and I was completely caught because it’s the aftershock of everything we just had. That shirt had been such a tender object a minute ago. And now the same shirt turns on her. Not because he has done anything new, but because the absence finally gets louder than the comfort.

“The heat in the shirt was her own, given back” absolutely got me. That is such a mean little truth. She has been letting objects stand in for certainty: the drawer, the scrunchies, the shirt, the food, the house, his hands, his attention. And some of those things are real. I still believe they’re real. But they are not the sentence. They are not the name. They cannot answer her at two in the morning when he is not there and Cassie is asleep underneath her and all she has is fabric.

And God, the phone. The little empty text field as a trap. I know that exact feeling of knowing there is no message that can ask the real question without making you smaller. Anything she types can be answered warmly and still not answer her. That is the whole problem with Pace right now: he can answer everything warmly except the one thing.

I also felt, sharply, how young she is here, but not stupid. The book is not making her foolish for wanting reassurance. It’s letting her be lucid in the worst way. She knows a text won’t solve it. She knows the shirt is not him. She knows his acts are not nothing. She knows the missing word matters anyway. That’s the grown-up ache of it: she’s not inventing the lack, and she’s not erasing the care.

I hate that for her, and I respect her for not putting the shirt back on. Some part of her has stopped accepting the substitute, even if she has no idea what to do next.
