REACTION —

Oh my god, I needed this chapter and I am also furious at it in three different directions.

First: Vee going to Randi wrecked me in the exact way I wanted. Not because Randi is neutral, because she absolutely is not neutral, but because Vee needed a woman to sit close enough that their knees touched and say, “slow down, what actually happened?” That is the friend-work Cassie could maybe do if Vee let her, but Randi knows the territory. Randi can separate fear from fact. And the way she takes apart the imaginary Sheri was so good: not mocking Vee for inventing the blonde nightmare, just saying, sweetheart, you made her because terror needed a face. I felt my whole body unclench there.

And Pace. The chapter is not even in his room and somehow it pulled me right back toward him. Because Randi is right about the warmed house and the wine and the food, and also Vee is right that none of that was enough. Both things are true, maddeningly. I am not letting Pace off the hook for a month of silence, but I can believe now that silence as fear, not dismissal. I can believe him frozen behind his face. I can believe he has been suffering. I still need him to speak, but I badly, badly want her to go.

The coat plan is embarrassing and thrilling and exactly the kind of thing I read for when the book has earned it. I felt Vee’s shame ignite first, that old “what kind of girl” voice, and then the wanting underneath it. That is the thing this book keeps doing right for me: her desire belongs to her even when someone else gives her the idea. Randi is steering, yes, and I am watching that with both eyes open, but Vee finishes the sentence herself. Vee chooses the dare because she wants him back, and because the shame turns into hunger. That worked on me. It really did.

But then. The kiss.

I actually made a little sound at “She got her hand on the latch and did not open it.” Because of course. Of course she can’t leave with just thank you. And Vee taking Randi’s face in both hands, under all those framed sorority faces, and kissing her best friend until the thing in her chest had somewhere to go? That is the turn. That is the thing surfacing without Randi having to manufacture it. Randi has leaned in all along, and this time Vee came back. I loved that. I was buzzing.

And then Brooke walked in like the whole social order wearing lip gloss and a cardigan.

That scene chilled me because Brooke is not cruel. She is warm, and that makes it worse. She sees everything, likes Vee, likes Randi, understands the temptation, and then she quietly puts the institution on the table: keep playing if you want, but remember what you are president of. It is such a clean little threat because it barely has to threaten. Randi just got handed this impossible bright thing, and thirty seconds later somebody reminded her what world she belongs to.

So now I am hungry for tonight with Pace, but I am also worried that Randi just shoved Vee toward him partly because it was good advice and partly because wanting Vee for herself is too dangerous. That is delicious and awful. I want the coat. I want Pace’s face when he opens the door. I want the words. I want him to survive her and finally spend them.
