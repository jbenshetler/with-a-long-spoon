REACTION —

Oh, this chapter got me right where I live, and also made me nervous in exactly the way this book keeps making me nervous lately.

Vee coming back from break crying in the car felt so real I almost hated reading it. That particular humiliation of having made a brave stand and then sitting alone with the silence afterward until your own mind starts inventing another woman to torture you with — yes. I believed every ugly inch of that. And I loved that she went to Randi. I loved that Randi’s first instinct was to get her out of the public room, up the stairs, behind a door, and hold her. That hug had more ache in it than some sex scenes. The smell of Randi’s neck, Vee breathing it in and steadying from it, the hug going past where a hug stops — there’s the thing. Vee wants her. She still doesn’t quite know what she wants, but her body knows, and mine as a reader absolutely knows.

Randi was wonderful here, and Randi was dangerous here. Both. She did the thing Vee needed so badly: she sorted fear from fact. She didn’t just soothe her with “of course he loves you”; she made her walk back through the actual evidence: warm house, wine poured, food made, “most important thing in my life.” I needed someone to do that for Vee, because Vee was spiraling herself into a blonde ghost. And Randi being the one to say, basically, do not hand him to an imaginary woman — I could have kissed her for that.

But then. Then she turns the whole thing toward showing up naked under the coat, and I am split right down the middle. As heat? Yes. Completely. I felt Vee’s shame catch fire into wanting, and that is one of the things this book does best. It understands that the forbidden feeling is not separate from the desire; sometimes it is the spark. Randi walking her through “who’s going to see you?” and making it about courage instead of degradation worked on me because it worked on Vee.

But emotionally? I’m scared. Because Pace’s failure was words. Vee needed words. And Randi’s advice is “don’t do it with words. Words are the whole war.” That is seductive advice, and maybe it gets Vee through the door, but it also risks letting Pace off the exact hook he needs to hang on for once. I do not want sex to patch the hole where the name should be. I want the coat to open, yes, obviously, I am alive. But after that, I need him to speak. Not cook. Not touch. Speak.

And Randi telling the John story to Vee nearly broke my heart because Vee hears the fairy tale as proof of everything she lacks, while I know from Randi’s side that the fairy tale was empty. That is deliciously painful. Vee thinks Randi is sharing discretion, maybe romance; Randi is hiding loneliness and the fact that “perfect” didn’t reach her. The line about the bedroom being the room she lives in made Vee laugh, but it made me sad. Randi is joking right over the wound.

I am absolutely reading on, but I’m bracing. Tonight could be gorgeous. Tonight could be the thing that gets them back. Or it could become another private, blazing, wordless room that leaves Vee with no name in the morning. And if Pace opens that coat and still cannot say what she is to him, I may personally need to lie on the floor.
