REACTION —

Oh, this one got under my skin in a quieter, more dangerous way than the porch chapter did.

I went in expecting the delicious Randi debrief, and I got it, but the book is doing that thing now where the erotic charge between Vee and Randi is no longer hiding behind friendship successfully. Vee still thinks she’s walking to class, sharing Lifesavers, telling dirty stories, kissing a ticklish neck because it’s funny. But my whole body is sitting there going, girl. Girl. You are on a date. You dressed for her. You skipped dinner for her. You are bringing her candy. You are letting her ask you the exact questions that put your body back in the truck and the bed. And Randi knows exactly what she is doing, except also maybe not entirely, because that neck kiss landed on her too.

The anthropology lecture was almost unfairly perfect. Gifts, grooming, the comb, the thing that passes hand to hand and carries obligation and touch and social order. Vee and Randi sharing wintergreen without looking at each other felt so intimate because nobody in the room would name it as intimacy. That is the trick of them. Their bodies are already having a conversation in little public gestures while Vee’s mind is still pretending it’s just “girls.”

And Randi. Oh, Randi. I am suspicious of her and weak for her at the same time. She refuses to let Vee make the story smaller, and part of me loves her for it because Vee’s shame needs someone who will not collude with it. But part of me is watching Randi get turned on by being the one who draws the confession out, the one who makes Vee say the harder version, and I can feel the old plan breathing in the walls. “Give me the real one” is loving, and it is also control. With Pace, that kind of control has become mutual and reverent. With Randi, the consent is warmer and blurrier and more socially hidden, and that makes me lean forward and tense up at the same time.

The mouth thing was so good because it wasn’t just raunchy. It was a door in Vee. She wants the answer to be “gross, obviously,” because then her first recoil can stay the whole truth. But her body has already betrayed that answer, and Pace handled it so beautifully. He stopped. He read which no was which. He wiped his mouth, accepted the boundary, and let her come back on her own terms. That is exactly the kind of dominance/care that keeps me here. He likes her so much, all of her, even the parts she thinks are too much or wrong. And Randi saying “I don’t” with that maddening calm cracked the little tidy box Vee had built around the incident. I felt Vee’s laugh die in the air.

Also: “He likes it. Actually — he’s enthusiastic.” That sentence is going to live in her now. Not because Randi explained anything, but because she didn’t. She just made room for the possibility that what Vee thought was disgusting might also be wanted, powerful, erotic, and already hers. That is the best version of this book’s engine: shame turning into heat because someone receives it without flinching.

But I am feeling the gap again. Pace is wonderful here by absence, almost. We’re living off the afterglow of him being public and attentive and brave enough to say boyfriend. That bought him a lot with me. But the longer we stay in Randi’s booth, the more I feel how much power she has over Vee’s interpretation of herself, and how much Vee does not know about why Randi is there in the first place. The warmth is real. The wanting is real. That’s what makes the secret worse.

And that neck kiss. That was not nothing. Vee weaponized it playfully, yes, but Randi’s body did not only tickle. The “catch and then a rise” went right through me. I would have texted a friend at 1 a.m.: she is absolutely kissing her best friend’s neck in the street and telling herself it’s a bit. It is not a bit.
