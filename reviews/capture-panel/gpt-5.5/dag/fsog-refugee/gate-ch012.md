Oh. Oh, this got me.

This is exactly the kind of chapter I keep reading for, and also exactly why this book has me nervous in my whole chest. Because on the surface it is just a hike, a blister, lunch, rain, a truck. But underneath it is all appetite and permission and being taught without being humiliated, and that is basically my weak spot with a pulse.

The boot thing undid me first in this quieter way. I was braced right along with Vee for shame. The sock, the foot, the mother-voice coming in like a slap before anything has even happened. And then Pace just fixes it. No teasing, no little sexy comment, no making her feel fussy or incompetent. He sees the actual problem and tends to it. I cannot overstate how erotic that kind of plain competence is when it has care in it. He doesn't make the blister into a scene. He makes it stop hurting. God.

And then the summit. I loved that the world was bigger than him for a minute. That mattered to me. Vee gets to have an experience that isn't only Pace, even though Pace arranged the outing and is right there watching her. The book lets her be swallowed by beauty, not just by a man. But of course he is watching her see it, because of course he is, and I am apparently not immune to a man who wants the face more than the view.

The food again. This book is going to kill me with feeding. The sandwich, the shared bottle, the cherries. The way hunger is allowed to be honest because the climb made it necessary first. That line, “the plain animal rightness of needing something and being handed it,” is basically the whole seduction in miniature. And the cherry pit moment could have been awful. It could have been a tiny humiliation. Instead he corrects her so gently that she gets to learn the rule and keep her dignity. I felt my shoulders come down. He keeps doing that: giving her a standard without using it to reduce her.

But the dress. I clocked that. He is absolutely going to do something with the russet silk dress, isn't he? And I don't know how I feel about it yet. If he buys it for her, that could be devastatingly romantic or it could be another piece of the arranged room closing around her. Probably both. “That sounds like a beautiful dress” was perfect because he did not pounce on the wanting. But the book made sure I saw him see it.

And then the truck.

I mean. I had to put the book down in my lap for a second, in the good way and the alarmed way. Because what I wanted from this genre was always this: the heroine choosing the charged thing from inside herself, not being maneuvered into it by a man pressing on her no until it gives. Here, Pace literally gets out of the truck. He gives her privacy without making her ask for it. He stands in the rain with his back turned, and that restraint is hotter than half the explicit scenes I've read.

And Vee calls him back.

That is the difference. That is the whole thing. She wants him to see her. She chooses the shame-word, almost. She lets the bad voice become heat because it is hers to transmute. It is dangerous territory for me as a reader because shame-to-heat can so easily become cruelty in a prettier dress, but here it felt interior, specific, owned by her. He doesn't tell her what she is. He doesn't name her down into it. She shows him, and he looks because she has called him to look.

His stillness in that cab. Whew. The fact that he does not touch her, does not lean in, does not cash the moment in. Just looks, wants, receives. I was absolutely gone.

And then the hand shaking. That brought the tenderness back so sharply it almost hurt. Because for all his control, he is a wet, freezing man who gave her the dry shirt and stood in the rain so she could decide what to do with her own body. That is dominance I can believe in: not performance, not cruelty, not taking. Containment. Attention. Cost.

The ending with the campus cop made me laugh in this awful strangled way because Cassie’s “state troopers” came screaming back into my head. Of course the outside world crashes in right when Vee is about to say something too big. Of course she runs, because shame and authority and sirens are all one system in her body. And now she is standing there in his flannel, soaked and interrupted, with no closure. Maddening. Effective. I want the next chapter badly.

I am still not forgetting the original sin of this arrangement. That hasn’t gone away. But chapters like this make it harder, because inside the moment he is so careful. The trap, if it is a trap, is lined with actual tenderness. That is the problem, and the book knows it.
