REACTION — Oh, this one got me in a different place.

After all that heat and scheming and Pace-and-Randi voltage, I did not expect the chapter that makes me clutch my chest to be Vee and Cassie at the pool, but here we are. I loved seeing Vee without either of them in the room. Not chosen by Randi, not being watched by Pace, not being arranged into the plan yet — just her, in that terrible old public-girl-body gauntlet of walking past boys in a swimsuit and having to pretend comments bounce off. I hated those boys immediately, obviously, but the book knew exactly how that kind of comment lands: not catastrophic, not “scene-worthy,” just another little hand on the air around her body.

Cassie, though. Cassie! I already liked her, but this chapter made me fiercely protective of her. The way she refuses performance with her whole body, the way she keeps the joke low just for Vee instead of making it a public comeback, the way she notices Vee come up changed and does not make her explain herself. That felt like actual friendship. Not glamorous, not seductive, not useful to anyone’s plot. Just: I know you, I’m here, take this ridiculous splash to the face and come back to me.

And Vee underwater hurt me. That “before she had a body to be a problem” feeling — yes. That landed. It made me ache for her, because so much of what Randi and Pace are going to touch in her is already here: the shame, the watching, the hunger to be outside herself and fully inside herself at once. But this wasn’t eroticized, and I’m glad. It felt like the book letting me know Vee has a private inner country that existed before either of them found her. That matters to me a lot, because if this is going to get dangerous, I need Vee to be a person and not only a beautiful appetite waiting to be unlocked.

I did feel the gap from the central seduction, but not in a bad way yet. This was the kind of quiet chapter I’ll forgive because it deepens the ache. It also made me slightly dread what Randi’s entrance into Vee’s life might cost her with Cassie. Cassie sees too much. Cassie says “Goodbye, Vee” like she already knows something is leaving. I am now reading partly with my hand over Cassie’s head, spiritually speaking.
