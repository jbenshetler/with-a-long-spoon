I felt that. I felt it embarrassingly hard.

And Cassie. Cassie saying "Goodbye, Vee" actually made my stomach dip. That was the closest thing in the chapter to a hand grabbing my sleeve. It is so plain and so ominous because Cassie is not dramatic. She isn't jealous, she isn't needy, she isn't trying to win a scene. She just sees something, maybe not all of it, but enough to say goodbye like Vee is walking through a door that will close behind her. I don't know whether I trust Cassie's instincts yet, but I trust that the book wants me to notice her, and I did.

Randi is terrifyingly good here. Not cruel on the surface, not even fake in a simple way. That's what gets me. She *is* warm. She does give Cassie the full beam too, at least socially. But with Vee, the warmth has aim. And Vee is so ready for it. The whole coffee shop sequence is almost erotic without touching sex: Vee walking into a room she has always felt outside of, reading every girl in it, judging and wanting and measuring herself, then looking at Randi and having the whole room fall away. I missed Pace a little, because yes, I am watching the gap since the two of them were alone in a room. But I didn't resent the chapter for it. This was necessary baiting of the hook, and it worked on me too. The bond here is not romantic yet in the soft way, but Vee's attachment starts blooming so fast it scared me. Randi saying "Love you, girl" should be nothing. It should be a throwaway girl-language thing. But to Vee it is practically a sacrament. That is dangerous, and I want the next chapter badly because I want to see whether Randi feels even one honest pang about this girl she is pulling in.
