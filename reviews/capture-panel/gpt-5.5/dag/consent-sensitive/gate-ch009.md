That matters to me so much with this book, because the premise is a trap, yes, but the moment-to-moment seduction keeps being built out of actual room to breathe. I am still watching that distinction like a hawk, but here it worked on me.

The dinner made me embarrassingly soft. Food in this book is doing a lot, and it’s not subtle exactly, but I don’t mean that as a complaint. Vee not eating all day because the date swallowed the afternoon, then being taken somewhere where she doesn’t know the rules, and instead of humiliating her or educating her like a project, he asks “May I choose?” That hit the exact nerve. Do I trust Pace? No. I trust his manners, which is different and more dangerous. He is exquisitely good at leaving the last inch. He does not push past a stated boundary; he receives it as true. That is hot, and it is also part of why he is so effective. The chapter knows both. When she says she doesn’t kiss men she’s known a couple of days, and he simply accepts it, I believed him. And then I watched her pull herself back into the kiss because the space he left was more compelling than pressure would have been. That is exactly the kind of consent dynamic I’m here for and afraid of: every step is hers, and the architecture around the steps is his.

The handholding almost did more to me than the kiss. The right hand that tore bread all night, that kept some rule she didn’t know, suddenly around hers like it had been missing from her life. I’m suspicious of “destiny” feelings in romance because they can be used to steamroll a girl’s judgment, but here it felt like Vee’s own interior shock, not the book ordering me to agree. She is lonely in a way she barely has language for. She wants to be carried, and he has figured out how to make being carried feel like dignity instead of diminishment.

And the kiss was gorgeous. The first one small because she makes it small. Him answering only what she brought. Then the space. Then her choosing again. I am not immune to “he leaves the last inch where it is.” Apparently I am extremely not immune. Damn it.

I’m still carrying the jacket copy in the back of my mind, so every tender thing has teeth. Randi and Pace have planned this, and Vee is walking into something staged. But this chapter did not make me feel like the author had forgotten that. If anything, the tenderness is the trap and the book knows I know it. That’s the thing keeping me reading: I am falling for it, but I can feel the book watching me fall.
