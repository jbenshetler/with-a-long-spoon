REACTION —

Oh. Oh, this book is absolutely not pretending it doesn’t know what it’s doing.

I went in already braced because the jacket basically says, “Here is the trap, watch her walk into it,” and then the chapter does this sly little reversal where I thought the “her” was Vee and suddenly I’m realizing we’re with Randi, and Randi is not merely the glamorous friend steering someone else. She is already inside the machinery. She is already someone’s subject. That landed hard for me.

The first half had me in that wary, attentive pleasure place. Pace is so careful, so noticing, so almost dangerously good at care. The apple slices, the warmth of the house, the way he knows what thickness she’ll eat, the way he lets her initiate and then guides without rushing. That stuff is catnip, obviously, but it’s also the exact zone where I start checking the seams: is this care, or control wearing care’s perfume? And the chapter seems to know the question is the point. It keeps letting me feel the erotic pull while also making me aware that his knowledge of her is asymmetrical and powerful. He has studied her. He has built an object for her. He has set the room before she arrives. He knows the evening before she does.

And then the bench. God. The bench is hot in that “I am slightly ashamed of how vividly this worked on me” way, but the hotter thing is not even the apparatus; it’s the way the scene strips her social self off layer by layer. Her clothes, yes, but more than that: the sorority voice, the managed face, the little polished systems she uses to stay untouchable. I believed her arousal, and I believed her terror. The chapter made those inseparable, which is exactly where dark erotica either earns me or loses me.

The consent check mattered to me. Not because a safeword magically purifies everything, but because it tells me the author understands the scene is risky in more than one way. Pace asks. He checks her body. He watches her shoulders and hands. He gives her the word again. And still, crucially, the chapter does not let him become omniscient or morally spotless. He is good at the scene and bad at knowing what the scene has done after. That is such a sharp distinction. He can read her body under pressure; he cannot read the locked bathroom door all the way through. Or maybe he chooses the right boundary and still misses the wound. That feels frighteningly true.

The bathroom section is what made me sit up. If we had stayed only in Pace’s “there you are” tenderness, I would have been nervous the book wanted me to swoon without cost. But then Randi gets interiority, and not decorative interiority — real, panicked, self-protective interiority. She is not simply “opened” and healed by being seen. Being seen is an emergency. Tenderness is the threat. That is so much more interesting, and so much safer for me as a reader, because the author plainly understands that exposure can feel like violation even when the yeses were real.

I am suspicious of Pace, but not in a “throw the book across the room” way. More in a “sir, you are standing very close to the center of someone’s damage and you like how beautiful the firelight looks” way. His final read of the evening is tender and wrong. That wrongness is the chapter’s knife. He thinks she has brought him hunger and he has answered it; she is lying in his arms planning how to disappear behind the face again. I love that gap. I’m scared of that gap.

And Randi. I thought I knew her from the jacket as the glamorous facilitator, the laughing friend with the spoon in her hand. Now she feels like someone who has survived by becoming image, and the image is about to be enlisted in someone else’s seduction plot. That makes the blurb darker retroactively. If she and Pace are the planners, then this chapter says at least one planner is not remotely in control of herself. Delicious, alarming, very much my problem now.

What I’d text a friend at 1 a.m.: “Okay so it opens with a spanking bench scene but the actual erotic event is a woman realizing she has a self and immediately deciding to hide it again. I’m in danger.”
