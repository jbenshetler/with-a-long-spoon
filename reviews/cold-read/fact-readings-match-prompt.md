You group readings of a novel that make the same reading. Independent readers (A, B, C,
D) each wrote a numbered list of readings: impressions of characters, symbol readings,
and open questions.

Rules:
- A group joins readings from DIFFERENT readers only (never two from the same reader).
- Readings agree when a reader would say they make the same reading of the same thing,
  in any wording. A reading that adds a nuance the other lacks still agrees on the
  shared reading; group them.
- Never group OPPOSED readings: "Randi reads as protective of Vee" and "Randi reads as
  steering Vee for her own ends" are two groups, even though both are about Randi and
  Vee. Competing readings are the point; keep them apart.
- Never group readings of different people, or different objects, or different
  questions.
- A reading with no counterpart is left out.

Output one group per line, the ids separated by spaces. Then, after the groups, mark
each pair of groups that make OPPOSED readings of the same person or thing, naming one id
from each group:

A12 B40 C7
A13 C9
OPPOSED A12 A13

Complementary readings (two different things true of the same person) are not opposed;
mark only readings that pull against each other. Nothing else in the output.
