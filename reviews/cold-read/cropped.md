# Cold read — Cropped

## Reader reaction

A quiet chapter, and after the stats-class ambush of last time it felt like exhaling. Two roommates, athletic cafeteria, Sunday-night-everyone's-back. I liked being here. No Randi, no Pace in the room — just Vee trying to render Pace to her parents and to Cassie, and discovering he doesn't fit any frame she's got.

**Vee.** This is the most sympathetic she's been to me in a while, because I got to watch her *do the cropping* in real time and understand it isn't only about a photo. She sent her parents the version that "stops at the waist," and then the whole telling of Pace is the same operation: "I told Dad the parts that fit." The math-PhD-who-hikes-and-teaches-you-to-dance part fits. The cooking, the owning-the-house, the *sewing* — those get cut, and her reasoning is precise and a little heartbreaking: "Too good is its own kind of suspicious." I felt the loneliness of that — she's proud of the most extraordinary thing about him (a man hand-sewed her a dress) and there is literally no one in her old life she can hand it to. The line "my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it" is genuinely good and got a real reaction out of me — funny and sad at once, and it lands because I already know about the dress.

The tell I'm sitting with: "the strange thing is, I'm not ashamed of it. The opposite, if anything." She's not ashamed of the weird — she's *proud* of being the one it happened to. That's the shame-fused-with-heat thing running quietly under a dinner conversation instead of a sex scene, and it works.

**Cassie.** Still the person I trust most, and this chapter deepened it. Home was "good to see my parents" and "good to leave, too" — that little bit of adult friction, the sister's baby, the dork brother-in-law — makes her feel like a whole person and not just Vee's moral floor. And her two questions are surgical: "So what is he. Not to your dad. To you," and then "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" That second one is the sharpest thing in the chapter. She's naming the exact machinery Vee runs — the difference between an unsayable thing and a *withheld* one — and Vee admits "Both. Probably both." Cassie caught what nobody else would.

**The ending got me.** "She had a gift for knowing when a door was closed... Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not." That "a little, not" is the whole book in six words. Vee *wants* to be pushed. Cassie's respect for the closed door — her best quality — is now the thing that lets Vee keep getting away with silence, and some part of Vee resents being let off the hook. That's the same protect-by-silence instinct I've been dreading, but here it's turned around: for once Vee is the one who half-wishes the door would be forced. I didn't expect the chapter to make me feel that Cassie's virtue is quietly *failing* Vee, and it did.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Nobody new to distrust. This is the warm register almost pure — no Randi to read twice, no Pace to fall for. If anything it's a relief valve. The only unease is structural and I brought it in with me: I keep waiting for the secret Vee's now carrying about Randi (the PPP outlier) to press against this new confession-that-isn't. It didn't come up. Interesting that in a chapter *about* what she can't say, she says nothing about that either — but the text didn't point at it, so I'm not sure that's a plant or just me.

**Erotic charge.** Deliberately low and I didn't mind. The nearest thing to heat is the dress-off-vs-dress-made line and the mother touching her own collarbone looking at the shoulders — that gesture was a nice oblique flush, the older woman recognizing something about the garment she won't name. No slack from boredom; it's a talk scene and knows it.

**Friction.** Very little. A couple of the "he'd think it was weird / weird is worse than wrong" beats border on Vee over-explaining her own father to Cassie — it's a touch essayistic, Vee narrating the sociology of her dad rather than just feeling it. But it stayed in her voice and I bought it as her working something out aloud, so it didn't tip into the author's thumb.

**What I want next.** I want the secret she's holding about Randi to finally have somewhere to go — this chapter reminded me how many doors Vee keeps closed, and Cassie is the one person who'd force one open if she pushed. *Guess from here:* the "a little, not" is a crack, and I suspect the next time Cassie doesn't push, it'll cost more. I also still want the photo question to detonate — a mother printing the cropped version and showing it around is a soft, warm image, but I haven't forgotten there's an *uncropped* one loose in the world.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Sunday dinner, athletic cafeteria. Cassie back from home (new-baby visit, parents still treat her like a kid). Vee recounts her own visit: parents proud of the honor-society induction; her mother printed the *cropped* photo (stops at the waist) and kept showing it around; her father kept staring at the dress, doing the math on its cost and getting quietly wary. Vee told them Pace was her boyfriend (with an audible question mark), gave her dad "the parts that fit" (math PhD, hiking, taught her to dance) and cut the rest — the cooking, the owned house, and above all that Pace *sewed* the dress ("weird is worse than wrong"). She admits she's not ashamed of it — the opposite. Cassie asks what Pace is *to her*; Vee can't/won't answer ("Both. Probably both."). Cassie, respecting the closed door, drops it — and Vee is grateful, and also "a little, not."

**How I feel.** Warm, quiet, a little lonely — a relief-valve chapter after the stats ambush. Still golden-on-top, cold-underneath, but here the cold is Vee's own isolation: she can't hand the best of Pace to anyone in her old life. Cassie is still the one I trust most, but the chapter opened a hairline crack — Vee *wanting* to be pushed, resenting the mercy of the closed door.
