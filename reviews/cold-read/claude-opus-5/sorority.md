## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one is funny. I laughed out loud at the canoe-sized yeti shoe and the pink rhinestone platforms — "Do these go with burgundy?" — and at the flat flapping loose from its sole, "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." And I want to be honest that the comedy worked on me completely, because the thing sitting under it is one of the ugliest moves in the book so far and I didn't see it coming until the card was on the counter. Randi got a real laugh out of herself, the one that costs her something, folded over a try-on bench in a bypass discount store. That was not performed. Then twenty minutes later Vee put the emergency credit card down for a number with nothing behind it, knowing there was nothing behind it, and smiled, "because the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch." Twenty chapters of Vee being given things and this is the first time something got *taken* from her, and it was taken with a true sentence.

Because the sentence is true. "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." Vee cried in that sun porch over exactly that and had no word for it, and Randi handed her the word, and I felt the little click of it land on me too. That's the whole horror of Randi in one gesture: she is not lying, she is *naming*, and the naming is the hook. The tan shoe never had a chance — Randi never argued with it, she just said "What size?" and let "practically a marriage" do the work, and then delivered the line Vee had been running behind her own eyes all week ("walk in late and stop the room cold") in someone else's mouth, which made it real. Vee followed her out without answering. I would have too.

The toe. That's where the chapter lands hottest and it's a thumbnail moving once across a nail. Randi down on one knee on the pale floor in her own beautiful clothes, "I've got her," lifting Vee's foot and *not* reaching for the box — turning it to the light, looking at the frosted plum she herself excavated the reason for at brunch. "Mm." And Vee's floor tips and her hand shoots out to Randi's shoulder to stop the room swinging, and Randi looks up and smiles and says nothing at all. Nothing at all is devastating. She knows that polish was picked to match the silk. She's reading her own intelligence back off the girl's body and letting the girl feel it go all the way up. My stomach dropped and I was also, plainly, turned on, and the two were the same feeling, which is the position this book keeps putting me in.

And then the shoes *hold* her — "less like wearing a shoe than being stood up by one," the arch firm as a hand under her foot. Same grammar as Pace's whole tenderness, the hand under her, the being carried rather than balanced. She rises into four inches the way she rose onto her bare toes in his mirror, and this time the line finishes. It's lovely. It's also the first time being held has had a bill attached.

What I don't have any suspicion of, still, is the *warmth*. Randi ordering the dress kept in the bag with the top foot unzipped so the shoulder shows. Randi careful with it on the brass hook. Randi genuinely, helplessly wrecked by the sandal joke. "You can put it in the trunk, you know. It's not going to *spoil*" — and Vee's laugh coming up whole and unrehearsed, and the last line, which broke my heart a little: *she had never once in her life had a friend like this.* She hasn't. That's the sick part. The affection is real and the extraction is real and they're the same afternoon. Set against it: Randi signing for loafers at several times Vee's number without glancing down, mid-anecdote, hands going, and wearing them out of the shop — while Vee carries her box against her chest with both arms because the rope handle isn't enough. Not one word of comment from the page. It didn't need one.

Friction: almost none, which is rare for a shopping chapter. "Which was its own kind of going under" is a touch pat, and I noticed the arithmetic paragraph flexing a little, but Vee's an econ major so I let her have it. Title: **Sorority** — Randi's house never appears, and that's the point for me. This is the sisterhood Vee actually got inducted into, matched size sevens, "practically a marriage" — and a sorority is a thing you pay dues to join. She just paid hers, on credit, with nothing under it. The board line, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, has never felt more precise: the invitation this time was "Come on. I know just the place," and it was perfectly polite, and she went. From here I dread the induction more than I want it — the gown, the mother in the room, the walking in late on purpose — and I want to know who's going to be standing in that high room when Vee stops it cold.
