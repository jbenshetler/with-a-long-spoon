# Cold read (grounded) — Sorority

## Reader reaction

**No jacket copy or tagline came with this volume** — all I have on the cover board is *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*, so I'll take that up at the end.

---

This chapter made me so happy and then quietly put a hand around my throat, and the hand was a credit card.

The stats class is the best three-way scene the book has given me. Vee comes in "fit to burst" with two girls in one room, and the chapter lets her be exactly as giddy as she's earned. Cassie opens with "Your bed's made," which is the whole of Cassie — no preamble, case already cracked, and she *lets Randi have it anyway*, lets Randi do the detective bit she already solved hours ago. And then the thing that actually got me: Vee surfaces from forty honest seconds of Simpson's paradox and realizes Cassie never went down into the numbers at all, has been turned a few degrees in her seat the whole time, because "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." I love her unreasonably. "Was it a good night?" — "Oh, yes." — and *something eased in Cassie's face, and she picked her highlighter back up*. That's the warmest beat in the chapter and it's four words and a highlighter.

And the professor, meanwhile, is reading Vee's life off the board in that flat cereal-box voice. "Each year on its own, the first man is the better hitter. Put the two years together and the second man is. Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them." I sat up. Nobody has touched one of Vee's numbers. Every single thing she's been handed is true at the level it happened — the moleskin, the sheet at midnight, "to you," the laughing fit in the shoe aisle — and the aggregate is a reversal she cannot see from inside any one year of it. The book has done this before (latent variables, error-correcting codes) and this is the sharpest one yet, because it doesn't accuse anybody of lying. That's exactly my unease: nobody's lying.

The shoe store is just a delight and I'm not going to put a shadow over it. Randi coming down the aisle a full head taller on six-inch pink rhinestone platforms, grave as a woman at serious work — "Do these go with burgundy?" — and the flat with the seam grinning loose, "this one's one brisk walk from becoming a sandal," which folds Randi over the bench into "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give." Vee feeling like the funniest woman in Virginia. The shared size seven, "God help us both, it's practically a marriage." I was grinning like an idiot. And *on Vee's ground*, the store she knows, where she's the expert on which racks lie. That mattered.

Then the boutique, and my stomach went. The number written small on the leather sole. "She could not say it... and stood there holding a quiet the exact shape of what was in her account." That sentence is terribly good. And then Randi kneels — on the pale floor, in her own clothes, "I've got her" — and lifts Vee's foot and *doesn't reach for the box*. She holds it, turns it to the light, looks at the frosted plum, and says "Mm," and runs one thumb across the big toenail. Vee's floor tips and her hand shoots out to Randi's shoulder to stop the room swinging. That's where the chapter's heat is, all of it, in a thumb on a toenail in a hushed store — and Randi *looks up and smiles and says nothing at all*, which is worse and better than saying something. She harvested those toes at brunch and now she's touched them. Vee still has no shelf for what her own body does around this woman.

And the card. The half-second with the debit between her fingers, "the one that only ever spent what was already behind it," and putting it back. "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it." She has spent the whole book reading the right-hand column first, and Thursday surviving the mimosa was a small won battle; this is the column losing. What I can't fully pin is whether Randi engineered it or simply *is* this — she never looks at a number, she walks out in the new loafers mid-afternoon "like shoes were a thing you simply traded," at several times Vee's price, while telling a story about a ruined coat. I don't think she priced the cruelty. I think she can't see the arithmetic from where she stands, and that the not-seeing does the same work as malice. The page earned that read and no more; I'm not going to call it a trap.

One beat I'm still holding open and want to: Randi's hand stays at the shoulder seam when Vee says *he was up all night finishing it*, and "for a breath Randi was somewhere else, behind her own face." Vee's heart goes out to her — Randi, who's never had anyone sit up a night over anything of hers. And I know that the man who sat up that night is Randi's. Vee is comforting her for the absence of the exact thing she's being told about. That's the sickest and most tender thing in the chapter and the page doesn't underline it once, which is why it lands.

Friction: almost none. "You lucky slut" landing lower than Randi meant, with Vee's body "keeping the night's account since eight," is a fine save on what could've been a throwaway. The older couple watching them laugh is a tiny flare I couldn't place — maybe just the one reminder that they're visible. And Randi's "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you" is the one line where I felt the author's thumb — it's *too* good, too neatly the word Vee cried over without having — except Randi being the person who hands Vee clean names for her own feelings is established, so I let it go.

**The titles.** "Sorority" — Randi's actual sorority is nowhere in this chapter, which is the joke and the point: the sorority is the three of them in a lecture hall with the middle seat saved, Vee's own two-woman chapter, happy in the middle. It recolored for me on the last line: "she had never once in her life had a friend like this." As for the cover board — *With a Long Spoon* is the proverb, and nobody has to finish it for me: you sup with the devil using a long spoon. That tells me someone at this table is the devil and I am being invited to guess, and the cruel part is I've watched all three of them be genuinely kind. *A Polite Invitation* points dead at Pace's "May I," and at every door Randi has opened for Vee by holding it. Politeness as the method. That's the promise and I believe it.

**What I want next:** the induction, the shoes, the mother in the room — and the bill arriving next month. And Randi's half-breath behind her own face. Guess from here: the invitation of the book's title gets made before Vee ever learns what she's inside of.

**Romance:** 2 — Cassie's easing face and the highlighter; "he made it *to* you"; "practically a marriage"; a friendship avowed outright in the last line.

**Motifs & images:** *Shoes* — the whole chapter, and a major repeat (paired row in his closet, ch 14; Meg's wrong boots, ch 11; scuffed good pumps, ch 17; the pumps rejected as "an apology" in Peekaboo, now answered). *Frosted plum toenails* — repeat (18, 20) and now touched. *Reading the right-hand column / small arithmetic* — repeat (17, 20), now broken. *Rising onto her toes* — repeat (the riser, ch 15; the mirror, Peekaboo) now finished by four inches. *The Mercedes window dropping its half-inch* — repeat (17). *Hair lifted off the face* — Randi's gesture, repeat. *The made bed as tattletale* — first. *Simpson's paradox / latent variables / cereal-box professor* — repeat. *"Baby" / "gorgeous" / "There's my girl"* — repeat.

**Symbolism:** Simpson's paradox, flagrantly and well — true in every part, reversed in the whole, nobody touched a number. The dress zipped back into gray nylon "tooth by tooth," shown to not one living soul. The credit card with nothing under it as the first debt Vee takes on for being looked at.

**Characterization:** Cassie deepened most, with the least page time — the non-reader of dresses who only reads the wearer. Randi deepened: the rhinestone bit is the first time her clowning cost her nothing to give, and the breath behind her face is the second crack. Vee deepened by spending past her means with her face held perfectly still. Nobody thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Earned its length. Three clean movements (class, dorm, two stores), each with a different temperature; the first store runs a touch long on gags but the laughs are the point.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building, and smartly varied — after the Pace-heavy "Portion" and "Peekaboo," this swings back to the girls and spends the dress instead of making it. First real material consequence in twenty-odd chapters. The only repeated beat is Randi-extracts-over-a-table, and this time it was Randi's knee and Vee's foot, so it didn't feel like a rerun.
