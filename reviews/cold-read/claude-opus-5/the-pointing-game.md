**Felt read**

That opening got me completely. He goes down on her, lets her finish, then comes up *into* the aftershocks — "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing" — and kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth, and she takes it off his lips and kisses him harder for it. I was gone. And then the thing I didn't see coming: he asks a question and *that's* what makes her come. "The question reached her body before it reached her." A sentence doing more than his hips were doing. That's the hottest thing in the chapter and it's language, not friction, which is exactly my weakness.

And here's where my stomach dropped, because I remember the bathroom. I remember the mirror, the smile settling, *he could have the face, the one he had found was going back behind it and she was not coming out again.* So watching her body answer before she can dress it — the flinch, the tongue at the lip, then the beautiful save, "Only in a threesome. With you," handing him the appetite so she doesn't have to own it — I'm reading two chapters at once. He thinks she's opening. She's rerouting. He even sees the rerouting, names it precisely ("The same act. A different owner"), and *is charmed by it*, calls it beautiful. He's the most attentive man I've read in ages and he's reading a woman who made a vow three days ago to never be read again. That gap is the whole chapter for me, and it aches.

Randi on the quad is the best she's been. The stride dropping out from under her — "the half-stride dropping out" — and the bare "Yes" arriving with nothing around it, before she can get to it. Then the scramble: "Uh — yeah. Yeah. I like her." And the footwork on the brick she didn't decide to do, and the squeaked *yes*, and then the lower *yes* in the register she only uses in his bed. I rooted for her so hard in that minute, which is strange, given what she's rooting *for*. I don't think she's lying when she says she's being a good sport. I think she genuinely doesn't know she stopped being one a quarter mile back.

And then the cold runs under it. They are shopping. The pointing *is* socially obscene and the page says so through her first recoil, and then she gets a taste for it. "I point. You pick." Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — the one quality neither of these two has — and she is immediately selected. And the collision: thirty feet of geometry, half a head of weight, executed so cleanly that *Vee's* hand flies to her own mouth, *Vee* apologizes. I admired it the way you admire a pickpocket. Then Randi's hand stays on her arm a half-beat too long. Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I actually put the page down. That is a girl who three nights ago couldn't survive being seen, already arranging for someone else to be put facedown.

Trust: Pace is where my unease sits, and it's not the kink — the bench chapter was scrupulous, the check-ins, the word, the closed bathroom door he wouldn't touch. It's that he's begun to enjoy *authoring* her. "He had decided this beforehand." "He had no name for the game yet." The warmth is real and so is the appetite for his own accuracy. I also notice the prose keeps hurrying to acquit him — "that mattered more to him than the answer did," "She deserved that gentleness. He wanted it for her" — and that insistence is the one place I felt a thumb on the scale. Nobody needs to tell me he's kind; show me and let me worry. The long quad-architecture paragraph ("composed two centuries ago by men who wanted the eye to travel exactly this way" — pretty, and a bit pleased with itself) slowed me, though in hindsight the men arranging the view is a joke I liked. And the "I can't eat that" to the cheeseburger, after the thin apple slices, after the bottle cap under the thumbnail — that's the thread I'm most frightened for, and "Let's go feed you" as the last line is tender and also a man who thinks he's already solved it.

Titles: "The Pointing Game" was a nothing-phrase going in, faintly playground, and that's about right — it gave away a little (someone will point) but not what, and reading it after, the ugliness is in *game*: a stranger turned into a round of something two people play. The cover board, "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION," has teeth now. Sup with the devil and use a long spoon. I don't know who's the devil in this house, and after this chapter I'm genuinely less sure than I was — Pace points, but Randi is the one who walked across the lawn. "A POLITE INVITATION" lands square on that collision: the most polite social gesture I've read, wholly engineered. No tagline in my packet.

What I want: Vee, in her own right, not as an item on a list. I want to know whether she's the laugher or whether she's got her own locked bathroom. Guess from here: Randi will fall for her and be far more frightened of *that* than of any bench, and the threesome she offered as a gift will turn out to be the thing she wanted least to want. I'd also bet the stats class is not a coincidence she needed.
