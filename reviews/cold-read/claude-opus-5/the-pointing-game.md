## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I spent this entire chapter with my stomach in a knot, and almost none of it was Pace's fault, which is the horrible part.

Because I know something he doesn't. I watched Randi stand in his bathroom two chapters ago and decide — "quiet as a deadbolt going over" — that he could have the face and nothing else, not ever. And now here he is, four weeks in, lying in bed thinking *She had been performing, the first week. She was not performing now,* and I wanted to put my hand through the page. He is so *good*. He reads her breath going "from held to spilled." He asks the question when the heat has left the room so she can weigh it free. He wants her "gathered back into herself" before she answers. He hopes there's "a way for her to say something true without having to sit up and put a name to it" — "She deserved that gentleness. He wanted it for her." That is tenderness of a very high order, and it is being spent on a woman who has already closed the door and locked it, and he's congratulating himself on the quality of his listening. I've rarely felt this much dread inside something this warm.

And yet — and this is what made the chapter genuinely great rather than just ironic — she *did* want it. The body answered before the manager could get there. She came, hard, off a *question*, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." So he isn't simply wrong; he's half right in the most dangerous possible way. And the thing she does next is the single best move in the book so far: "Only in a threesome. With you." I actually said *oh* out loud. "She had not taken back the wanting... She had moved it." Not a woman who hungers for a woman, which is unsurvivable — a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which is merely sexy. "The same act. A different owner." That's the face doing exactly what she decided it would do, and Pace *sees* the mechanism, admires it, and calls it delightful instead of a warning. He is the most attentive man I've read in ages and he keeps reading the right data into the wrong frame.

The quad sequence swept me up completely, and I want to be clear that it swept me up as *pleasure*, not just as apprehension. Cruising strangers hand-in-hand in the four-o'clock sun, his mouth at her ear, "What about her?" — the three-beat breakdown of her response (social wrongness, then confusion, then the hunt up the path after the swinging ponytail) is thrilling. "I point. You pick." She laughs "before she could decide whether to," and that's the whole game: he keeps getting ahead of her permissions. And then the taxonomy of her refusals is doing gorgeous characterization work — too expected, the sealed pair, and the *fast* "God, no" with real feeling in it at the blowout who runs the room. She's rejecting versions of her own performance. She keeps saying *maybe* only to the girl on the library steps who's warm and unsealed and comfortable in the room she takes.

Then Vee. "Her," and he's watching *Randi* when he says it, so we get the arrest of the walk, the not-looking-away, "the follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step." The bare "Yes" with nothing around it. Yes, fine, I melted. Then the recovery and the retrofit — "Yeah. I like her" — which is the face reasserting ownership within one breath. And I have to say the collision on the walk is the most purely delicious thing in the chapter: thirty feet of invisible geometry, shoulders meeting, and *Vee's* hand flies to her own mouth, *Vee* apologizes. Randi is a predator with a decade of dance behind her and she's just discovered what she can do. Then the little half-second of footwork on the brick, the squeaked *yes*, the low bedroom *yes* in daylight — I was rooting for her, wholeheartedly, which alarmed me.

The knife: "Do you want a cheeseburger?" / "No. I can't eat that." And then the last line, "Let's go feed you," from the man who sliced the apple thin. He thinks last night's lesson was that she can be fed. I don't think she's going to eat.

Suspicion: not of Pace's *motives* — I still trust him entirely, which is what makes this frightening. My unease is structural and the text absolutely earned it: he has taken the one genuine, unmanaged thing she let slip and handed it back to her as a game, with a stranger's name and class schedule attached. Vee is now a real person somewhere in this book who has no idea she was pointed at across a lawn. That's the part that sat coldest.

Friction: very little, but the chapter is dense with Pace glossing his own moves, and twice it tipped into essay — "If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her, good enough that the good feeling and the wanting became one fact" made me feel the thumb. And "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did" felt like the author underlining a thesis Pace was already demonstrating.

Titles: **"The Pointing Game"** is exact and a little chilling — it names the flirtation *and* the fact that a person got pointed at. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**: the spoon is "sup with the devil," so somebody here is being warned about somebody, and I can no longer tell which of these three is the devil — Pace with his built furniture, Randi behind her face, or whatever Vee turns out to be. "A polite invitation" now reads as the shoulder-check on the walk. My packet gives no jacket copy and no tagline.
