## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

That opening is the hottest thing in the book so far for me, and it's hot because of *timing*. He comes up over her while she's still pulsing, slides in before he kisses her, kisses her with his mouth still wet — and then asks a question into "the warm disorder of her" and the question makes her come. Not his hips. The question. "The question reached her body before it reached her." I felt that land in my own stomach. It's the cleanest demonstration yet of what he actually does to her: he doesn't touch her past her defenses, he *talks* past them, and her body confesses on a delay she can't control. And then the later kiss on the library walk, in daylight, her own taste arriving on her mouth in public and her breath catching — that's a filthy, elegant piece of cruelty-that-isn't-cruelty and I loved it.

But I am reading this chapter with a stone in my shoe the size of the last two chapters, and Pace isn't. I *know* what he doesn't. I watched her stand in his bathroom and decide, quiet as a deadbolt, that the woman he found was going back behind the face and not coming out again. So when he lies there thinking "he wanted her gathered back into herself when he asked, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did" — that's genuinely tender, it's *the good version of a man*, and it's also him carefully handing a choice to a person who has just privately resolned never to be a real person in front of him again. Her "Only in a threesome. With you." — he sees the move! He reads it exactly right, that she's relocated the appetite from *hers* to *his*, that she's made herself the generous instrument. And then he says "Okay," easy, and calls it the best idea he's had in a month. He thinks he's building her a room to be honest in. She's building an exit. The gap between those two things is where my dread lives now, and it's delicious.

The walk swept me up more than I expected, given it's essentially window-shopping for a human being. What worked was watching her *drop the frame*. She starts by being offended — pointing at a stranger, the "bald social wrongness" — then goes to "I'm being a *very* good sport about your little game," then, a quarter mile later, "she had stopped being a good sport. She was shopping." The turn is rendered through her hand in his and the coil of a dancer leaning into a step not yet called, and it's much sexier than the inventory of the candidates, which honestly went a little listy in the middle (leggings, scrunchies, sage athleisure, the beanie, the blowout — four paragraphs of outfits). Her verdicts are the best part: "Too expected." "Those two go everywhere together. You'd get both or neither." The furious speed of "God, no" at the woman whose ease is performed *outward* — she can't stand a better mask than hers.

Then Vee. And the page earns her — the laugh with her head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Of course. Of *course* that's what undoes Randi, who has never once forgotten she can be seen. The bare "Yes," with nothing around it, before she remembers to dress it up — I got chills. And I want Vee, on sight, which makes what happens next very hard to enjoy cleanly: the shoulder collision. Thirty feet of geometry made to look like chance, and the beautiful horror of it is that *Vee* apologizes. Vee's hand flies to her own mouth. Randi's hand on her arm, staying a half-beat longer than the apology needs. That's a hunt, executed with a decade of dance and three years of sorority, and Pace stands at his light pole "with a deep warmth in his body" watching the woman he loves be very, very good at it. He admires the instrument and doesn't ask what it's for.

So: suspicion, yes, but precisely located. Pace still reads as kind to me, scrupulous, attentive — and newly, worryingly delighted with himself. "I point. You pick" is a great line and also a man who has built a game in which he never has to own a want either. Randi I trust not at all and sympathize with completely; she's more alive in this chapter than she's ever been, and everything that makes her alive she is converting into camouflage. "I'm hungry." "Do you want a cheeseburger?" "No. I can't eat that." — and the chapter closes on "Let's go feed you," which after the apple slices reads as him thinking he's still answering a hunger she keeps trimmed. And her last line, low, into his chest: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I went cold. Vee isn't a fantasy anymore; she's a thing to be brought somewhere.

Titles: **The Pointing Game** did give something away — I read the chapter waiting for the pointing, and the name of the game arrived before the game. It pays off anyway because the title is *his* framing, the harmless wrapper, and "point" turns out to also mean aiming. **WITH A LONG SPOON** is doing more work than anything else on that board: he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon. I'd assumed Pace held the spoon — he's the one with the safeword and the water bottle. After today I think the spoon might belong to nobody in the chapter and the one who needs it hasn't been warned. And **A POLITE INVITATION** — "Go meet her," delivered with a smile and a nod, is exactly that. My packet gave no jacket copy or tagline, only the cover board.

What I want: Vee, in her own light, not as an object someone pointed at. What I dread: that stats class.
