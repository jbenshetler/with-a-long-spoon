## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The first half undid me and the second half made my skin crawl, and I don't think those are in conflict — I think that's the chapter.

The bed scene is the best sex this book has given me, and it's better because of what I know that Pace doesn't. He comes up over her *while she's still coming*, "into all that grip and flutter, before he kissed her," and kisses her with his mouth still wet — and she takes herself off his lips and kisses him harder for it. That's the whole thing in one gesture: he wants her to have her own taste, her own wanting, handed back to her. And then the question, asked into "the warm disorder of her," and her body answers ahead of her, a third orgasm arriving out of nowhere that his body didn't earn. *Would you like to taste another woman?* — I felt that land in my own stomach. It's hot and it's also a kind of theft, and he notes it himself: "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That sentence is where my trust in him cracked, quietly. Not because he's cruel. Because he's decided her body is a more honest witness than she is, and he's *right*, and being right that way about another person is its own trespass.

And I'm sitting here holding what she decided in that locked bathroom two chapters ago — *he could have the face, she was not coming out again* — while he lies there admiring the beauty of her deflection. "Only in a threesome. With you." He reads it exactly correctly: she moved the appetite, handed it to him, made herself the generous instrument. He calls it beautiful and lets the camouflage stand because "it was the wrapper the wanting needed to come out in." But he thinks the wrapper is temporary. I don't. He thinks he's giving her room; from where I sit he's just handed her the perfect instrument for staying hidden — a want she gets to have as a gift to her boyfriend. God, that's a good trap, and nobody built it on purpose.

Then the walk, and my pleasure went complicated. The game itself is genuinely delicious — "I point. You pick," and her laughing before she can decide whether to, and the *maybe* on the library steps rewarded with a kiss that puts her own taste back in her mouth in broad daylight. Her verdicts are wonderful characterization: *too expected*; *those two go everywhere together, you'd get both or neither*; the fast furious **"God, no"** at the blond girl who performs her ease outward. Randi knows exactly what she can't stand, which is women doing what she does. I loved watching her stop being a good sport a quarter mile before she'd admit it — "she was shopping."

And Vee. The page gives her to me the way it gives her to Randi: head thrown all the way back, throat bare, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." I *melted*, and then I got cold, because that's the audition criterion. Unselfconsciousness is what got her selected. Randi's bare, unadorned "Yes" — the word arriving "with nothing around it" — is the most honest thing she's said since the bench, and she immediately puts a costume on it. Then the bump. That collision is the most frightening thing in the chapter and it isn't sexual at all: thirty feet of invisible geometry and it's *Vee's* hand that flies to her own mouth, *Vee* who apologizes, Randi's hand staying on her arm a half-beat past the apology. Three seconds so clean it looked like nothing. I admired it the way you admire a knife.

So: suspicion, earned, and not of anyone's sincerity. Pace's care is real — I believe the water bottle, the "tell me your word," the "let's go feed you" against her "I can't eat that" (the apple hunger, still running). Randi's delight is real; the little half-second of footwork on the brick, the squeaked *yes* and then the low bedroom *yes* in daylight, both hands in his hair — that's the most alive she's been out of bed. But nobody in this chapter asked Vee anything, and the last line is Randi asking, low, with her head still down: *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* He doesn't answer. He kisses her head and feeds her. That non-answer is the coldest moment in the book so far.

Friction: the narration is a touch in love with its own reading of her. The dance figure lands beautifully once ("a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves") and then comes back twice more and starts explaining. "It was the best idea he'd had in a month" is charming but the page keeps assuring me the game is working. And the candidate parade sagged a little around the blazer girl — a lineup, with the fourth entry doing less than the first three.

Titles. **"The Pointing Game"** told me someone would be pointed at, not at whom, so it cost me nothing and now it reads worse than it did: he says outright he "had no name for the game yet," and the chapter supplies one — a finger aimed at a stranger in daylight, the thing Randi first recoiled from as "bald social wrongness" and then went hunting inside of. **With a Long Spoon** — that's the devil-supper proverb, and I keep trying it on each of them and it won't stay put. Pace, sure; Randi, maybe more; and now Vee, who is about to be invited to dinner. **Book One — A Polite Invitation** is what the whole chapter is: the courtesy is the delivery system. I'm uneasy and I want more immediately.

What I want: Vee's own head. I want her to be a person and not a gift. Guess from here, marked as a guess: Randi's "never coming out again" and this threesome are the same decision — a way to be wanted at a distance forever — and Vee is the one who'll pay for it.
