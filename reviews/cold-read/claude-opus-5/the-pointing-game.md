## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this chapter still carrying the bathroom mirror from two chapters ago — Randi deciding, *quiet as a deadbolt going over*, that the one he found was going back behind the face and not coming out again. So the first line, her slowing at the long table with its top back on and saying *Let's do something different. Today.*, had my stomach already dropped. He hears a girlfriend asking for fun. I heard a woman shopping for a place to put the thing that happened to her on that bench.

And then the sex worked on me anyway, which is the trouble. Him coming up over her *while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing*, kissing her with his mouth still wet, her taking herself off his lips and kissing him harder for it — that's as hot as anything in this book. And the question. *Would you like to taste another woman?* — and she comes, instantly, *far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it.* A woman detonated by a sentence. I actually sat back. That's the cleanest piece of erotic writing I've hit here: her body answering before she can make the wanting mean something safer. And the care around it is real — he refuses to take the body's answer as her answer, waits for the heat to leave the room, asks again when she's *gathered back into herself, free to weigh it and choose*. I believe he means that. I've trusted him this whole book partly because of exactly this.

Which is why the chapter hurts. She flinches — barely, *the kind of thing he might have missed entirely if he had not been paying attention* — and then she hands him the costume: *Only in a threesome. With you.* And he sees it. He names it precisely: she moved the appetite, made herself its generous instrument, *not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift.* And then — *He let the camouflage stand.* He admires the lie. He takes yes for an answer because the yes is beautiful. I don't think he's doing anything cruel; I think he's doing something enormous and not knowing it, and I know it, and that gap is where I spent the whole afternoon on the quad.

The walk itself is where my feelings split. The pointing is genuinely erotic as a *game* — the three-beat he watches move through her (propriety offended, confusion held, then the hunt), the rule he invents on the spot — *I point. You pick* — the kiss on the library steps where he hasn't wiped his face and she tastes herself again and her breath catches. Her *I'm being a very good sport about your little game* while having stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. That coil in her hand, *a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves.* Lovely. But the catalogue got long on me. Four, five women graded by waistband and trainer and *built to the proper scale*, and somewhere in there the heat went to appraisal and I was reading a spec sheet. One line felt like a thumb on the scale too: *If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her* — I don't need the thesis read out to me when the body already said it.

Then Vee, and the catalogue was worth it. *Laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen.* That's the knife. That is the exact thing Randi swore off in his bathroom — and it's what she stops dead in the middle of a step for, and says a naked *yes* to with nothing around it. The stride dropping out from under her, the eyes that won't come off. I was moved and I was scared. The shoulder-collision is the most unsettling thing in the chapter and also dazzling: thirty feet of invisible geometry, and *it was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth*, apologizing for being hit. I enjoyed the skill of it and my skin crawled. Vee is lovely, unguarded, warm, and has just been pointed at and acquired by two people with a bench.

Trust: I still trust Pace's intentions entirely and his premise not at all. The text earned that, not my paranoia — he *watched* her dress the want as a gift and chose to be charmed. My suspicion is of Randi, and it's sympathy-shaped: I think she's found a way to put someone else in her place. The last line confirms the dread without resolving it. *Do you think she'll like the bench?* — said into his chest, glowing. And he answers *Let's go feed you*, which is tender as hell (after *I can't eat that*, after the thin apple slices) and completely beside the point of what she just asked.

Titles: **The Pointing Game** is just the mechanism, and it gave me the shape of the afternoon a little early — I was waiting for pointing. It recolors after, though: he thinks he's pointing at women; she's pointing away from herself. The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — now reads like a warning with a bow on it. Sup with the devil and use a long spoon. I can't tell who the devil is, and that's the good kind of not-knowing. The *polite invitation* is surely that collision on the walk: a stranger apologizing for being chosen.

What I want: Vee, in her own right, with her own interior. What I dread: that she's an instrument. I'd keep reading right now.
