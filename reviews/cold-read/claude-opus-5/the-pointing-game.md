# Cold read (grounded) — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The opening three pages got me before I had my guard up. He comes up over her *while* she's still going, slides in while she's still fluttering, kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth — and then asks. "Would you like to taste another woman?" And the question lands in her body before it lands in her, and she comes on the question. Not on anything he's doing. On being asked. I felt that in my stomach. It's the hottest thing in the chapter and it's entirely a sentence.

And then, because I remember the mirror — because I was in that bathroom with her four weeks of story ago and heard the deadbolt-quiet arrival of *not ever* — I spent the whole afterglow half a page ahead of him and sick about it. He waits for the heat to leave the room. He wants her "gathered back into herself when he asked, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did." That is genuinely beautiful of him, it is the exact opposite of pressure, and it is also the trap: gathered back into herself is precisely where the face lives now. So she hands him the wanting dressed as a gift. *Only in a threesome. With you.* And he sees the move, admires the craft of it, reads it as her needing camouflage to say a true thing — which is right, and which is also him accepting the face and calling it her. "The shape she gave it felt right." I wanted to put a hand on his arm. You are being so careful with a door she has already locked.

The quad is where I came apart a little. It's a gorgeous chapter of walking — the one sugar maple gone over all at once in a field of things that haven't decided, the underside of the maples showing pale, the bass arriving a half-second late. And under it, this filthy elegant game. "I point. You pick." Randi laughing before she'd decided whether to. The *too expected*, the sealed pair you'd get both or neither, the *maybe* on the library steps in the green beanie — and him stopping right there to kiss her on the mouth with her own taste still on him, in public, so she has to taste what she's shopping for. That's the peak for me. Cruel and generous in the same gesture. And her "*Oh.* You dirty boy" with the seam showing in it, the play not quite covering the actual heat.

Then Vee comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown all the way back, laughing like someone who has forgotten she can be seen — which is the one thing Randi has never once forgotten in her life — and Randi's stride just drops out from under her. "Yes," with nothing around it. I loved her there. Fully, uncomplicatedly loved her, for about four seconds, and then the costume came back on top of it: "Yeah. I like her." The *adding* of it. God.

Where I went cold is the collision. Thirty feet of geometry made to look like accident, and the beautiful part — the part he'd stand an hour to see again — is that *the redhead apologizes*. Randi runs a con on a laughing stranger with a hand that stays a half-beat too long, and Pace watches from a light pole "far enough back to be nobody," warm all over at the sight of it. That's the first time this book has let me see what these two are together rather than what he is to her, and it's predatory, and both of them are happy. I don't think the text is confused about it either. The last line — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — arrives before Vee has had a second conversation with either of them, and my whole body said *she doesn't know she's been picked*.

Trust: no suspicion of Pace's intentions — I've never had any, he's the most careful man I've read in a while — but I've stopped trusting his accuracy, and that's worse. He's built an entire afternoon on the reading that her body tells the truth and her mouth corrects it, and he's *right*, and it still won't save him, because she's decided to be all correction from here. Randi I trust completely to be exactly what she says she is on the surface and nothing underneath, which is the saddest sentence I could write about her. The one crack is that the bare *yes* got out anyway — her vow is already leaking. That's the thing I want to watch.

Friction, small: a couple of Pace-interiority lines over-explain what the scene already did — "If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her" nudged me, and "It was the best idea he'd had in a month" is a wink I didn't need. And the parade of graded women gets slightly catalogue-y in the middle stretch (lavender, sage, sprinter's chin), though the sheer specificity is also the joke — Randi's whole grammar is grading women.

Titles: "The Pointing Game" told me nothing going in, then meant everything — and the chapter earns the second word harder than the first, because it *is* a game, with rules he invents mid-walk, and games have losers. He "had no name for the game yet," which I felt as a small warning. The cover board says WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION, no tagline supplied, and it's ringing now: you sup with the devil using a long spoon. Four weeks of politeness, an invitation extended to a girl who hasn't been asked yet. I can't tell who's meant to be holding the spoon and I'd like that to stay unclear a while.

What I want next: Vee. On the page, talking, with her own inside. And I dread the stats class.
