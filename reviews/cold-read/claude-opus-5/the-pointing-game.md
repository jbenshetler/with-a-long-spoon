**Felt read**

The opening got me before I had my guard up. Her hand flat on top of his head "not pushing, just holding, as if to confirm he was there" — that's the whole of her in one gesture, and then him coming up into her while she's still pulsing, kissing her with her own taste on his mouth, and her taking it off his lips and kissing him *harder for it, ardent before she could think better of it*. That phrase is doing a lot of work on me. And then the question, and the way it detonates — she comes from the *question*, not from his body, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." I believed that completely. It's the hottest thing in the chapter and it's not an act, it's a disclosure.

And then the thing I've been carrying since the bathroom mirror two chapters ago came due. She relocates it: *"Only in a threesome. With you."* He sees exactly what she's done — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner." I love and hate that he reads it that clearly. Because here's what's cracking me open: he's the most attentive man I've met in a book in a while, he reads her breath, her shoulders, her *flinch* — and he still thinks the bench *opened* her. He's lying in bed "quietly happy about her," thinking she finally let someone feed her, and I know she went back behind the face and decided *not ever*. I'm reading him with real affection and a sinking stomach at once. He thinks he's building something with her. He's building it with the face.

The cheeseburger. "I'm hungry." / "Do you want a cheeseburger?" / "No. I can't eat that." Easy, automatic, not even a glance. After the apple slices cut thin, that landed like a small cold stone. And "Let's go feed you" as the last line — tender, and also the one thing she will not let him do.

The pointing game itself is where the chapter is most alive and most uncomfortable, and I don't think the discomfort is me being prissy — the page earns it. Her first recoil is *propriety*, "the exposure of being two people who pointed at a stranger," and I felt that with her. Then the game becomes genuinely delicious: "I point. You pick." She stops being a good sport a quarter mile back and starts shopping, and the follow-gathering-before-the-lead image is gorgeous — I know that coil from the dance floor and I felt it transfer. The verdicts are funny and revealing and I was grinning: "Too expected." "Those two go everywhere together. You'd get both or neither." "God, no," fast, with real feeling, at the one who runs the room.

Vee arriving is the swoon. Head thrown all the way back, throat bare, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That line is the chapter's heart and also its knife, because the two people watching her are *exactly* the ones who will see her. And Randi's "Yes" — bare, alone, with nothing around it, the stride dropping out from under her, the first woman all afternoon she could not look away from. My chest actually went. Then the kiss with her taste still on him, "Close your eyes. Can you imagine kissing her? Go get her." — "For us?" "For us." I was flushed. That's a 3 for me and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

And the shoulder collision, executed so cleanly that *Vee* apologizes — "It was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth" — is the most thrilling and most chilling two minutes in the book so far. Randi's hand staying a half-beat longer than the apology needed. Then the grin breaking the managed surface, the half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do, the squeaked *yes* and then the low *yes* she only uses in bed. She's radiant. I rooted for her so hard right there, and then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I went cold. She's already placed Vee on it, before Vee knows her name is in two mouths.

Trust: I don't distrust Pace's intentions — his care is consistent, he asks the question twice and refuses her body's answer as her answer, he waits for the room to go ordinary, he wants her free to choose. But I don't trust the *project*, and I notice nobody in this chapter has thought about Vee's consent for a single second. The cover board says **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** (no tagline in my packet), and after this chapter the spoon proverb is ringing — sup with the devil, use a long spoon. I can't tell yet who's the devil here, which is exactly why I want to keep reading. "A Polite Invitation" now reads straight off the page: *no, no, my fault, it's fine.*

Friction: almost none, but the chapter does tell me what it's doing more than once. "She was shopping" is great; "Her body wanted this; her mind had not yet caught up to it" is the author tidying what the scene already proved. And three paragraphs of Pace's taste-theorizing ("Not the expected, not the matched pairs, not the ones who ran the room") sagged slightly — I'd worked that out from her nos.

The title gave nothing away and then recolored everything: a *game*, named by him, with rules he sets and a camouflage she maintains — and a finger pointed at strangers who never consented to be in it.

What I want next: Vee, on the page, with her own interior. I dread that she has one.
