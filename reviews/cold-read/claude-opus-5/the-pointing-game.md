# Cold read (grounded) — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

**No jacket copy was supplied — only the cover-board line, and no tagline.** So I'm reading this with just that and my memory of the last two chapters.

---

Okay, the first third of this chapter undid me. The structure of it — making her come with his mouth, then coming up into her *while she's still going*, undefended, and kissing her with her own taste on his mouth so she takes herself off his lips and kisses him harder for it — that's filthy in the exact register I want filth in: specific, generous, attentive. And then the question. "*Would you like to taste another woman?*" and "the question reached her body before it reached her" and she *comes* on it. No friction accounting for it. I made a noise. That's the hottest thing in this chapter and maybe the hottest single beat I've read in this book, because it's not an act, it's a woman being detonated by a sentence and having no cover for it.

And then — and this is what makes Pace the strangest, most unsettling person on the page — he does the *scrupulous* thing. "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." He waits for the heat to leave the room. He asks again when she can weigh it. He even hopes she can say something true "dressed as play, before she had to look it full in the face." That is real care. I believe it completely. And it is also, from where I'm sitting, catastrophic, because I know what he doesn't: she locked that bathroom door Friday night, decided *he could have the face,* decided *not ever,* and lay in a cold sorority single in last night's clothes and didn't cry. So when she purrs "Only in a threesome. With you," and he sees through it — sees her move the appetite, hand it to him, make herself its "generous instrument" — and calls it *beautiful*, and **lets the camouflage stand**... I felt my stomach go. He thinks he's giving her room. He's actually accepting the mask on the mask's own terms, which is the one thing that guarantees he never gets her back.

The pointing game itself is a delight and a horror at once and I couldn't stop reading it. The craft of her taste — not the mirror of herself ("too expected"), not the matched pair, and a hard fast *"God, no"* at the one whose ease is "performed, aimed outward, a thing she did *at* people," which is of course exactly what Randi is and exactly why she can't stand her. The *maybe* for the round-faced girl on the library steps with the split earbuds, loose-limbed, "comfortable in the room she took." That's the tell: Randi doesn't want a woman like herself, she wants a woman who isn't guarding anything. And she doesn't know she's revealing it. "I'm being a *very* good sport about your little game" — and twenty lines later, "She had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." I laughed, and then didn't.

Vee. God. The whole afternoon sharpens to her and the page earns it: the hourglass the year's clothes aren't cut for and don't care, the athlete's center under it, the copper the wind won't leave alone, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That last clause is the whole book pressed flat — it is the precise inverse of Randi, who has *never once* forgotten. Randi's stride drops out from under her. She doesn't look away. The bare `"Yes," she said. The word came out alone, with nothing around it` — I was swooning and dreading in the same breath. And then the recovery, the costume going back on in real time: "Uh — yeah. Yeah. *I like her.*" Adding the part that makes it a gift from a girlfriend.

The collision is my favorite piece of pure character on the page so far. Thirty feet of geometry made to look like chance, half a head given, enough weight that the accident appears to happen *to her* — and the beautiful detail that it's **Vee's** hand that flies to her mouth, Vee who apologizes. Randi's whole trained apparatus, dance and sorority both, folded into three seconds. And then Randi comes back across the lawn *lit up*, doing involuntary happy footwork on the brick, squeaking "*Yes*" — and I rooted for her so hard it hurt, because that was the real girl out in daylight for about four seconds. Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Cold water. She's already converting the one thing she wanted for herself into something she can hand over.

Trust and suspicion: I don't distrust Pace's intentions at all, and that's what frightens me. He's never once been careless — he waited, he checked, he asked twice, he gave her the choosing ("I point. You pick"). But he's steering a woman who has secretly gone behind glass, and he's enjoying how well he reads her: "He liked when he guessed right about her." A man that good at reading someone who has decided to be unreadable is going to drive them both off a cliff while believing he's been gentle the whole way. Randi I trust not at all and sympathize with enormously — the cheeseburger ("No. I can't eat that"), the scrunchie as "the one flourish," deciding that morning "how much of herself the day would get." And Vee I feel pure protective tenderness for, three paragraphs in. She has no idea she's been pointed at. She apologized.

Friction, small: a couple of the pointing vignettes read a touch catalogue-y — the sage/lavender pair in particular felt like a costume list before it became a person. And I noticed the thumb a little on "the whole art of it was that it stay a game," explaining a technique the scene was already showing me beautifully. Minor.

Titles: **"The Pointing Game"** gave nothing away — it meant nothing before I read it and now it means everything, including the ugly part, the "bald social wrongness" Randi recoils from for exactly one beat before she starts hunting. Pointing is what you do at objects. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — now has a taste in my mouth: you sup with the devil using a long spoon, and the invitation about to be extended to Vee over stats class will be extremely polite. What I don't know is who's holding the spoon and who's the devil, and I'm no longer sure it's Pace.

What I want: Vee's interior, immediately. What I dread: that she gets it, all of Randi's practiced warmth, and never knows it was aimed. *Guess from here:* Randi's going to fall for Vee, and the thing she can't survive isn't wanting a woman — it's being seen by one.
