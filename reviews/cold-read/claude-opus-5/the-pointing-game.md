# Cold read (grounded) — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The first few pages worked on me without resistance. He comes up over her *while* she's still going, slides in through the flutter, and kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth — "she took herself off his lips and kissed him back harder for it, ardent before she could think better of it." That's the whole book's engine in one line and it's also just plain hot. And then the question, asked into "the warm disorder of her," and her body answering before she does — coming hard, helplessly, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." I love that he registers it and then *doesn't* take it: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." That restraint is the thing I've come to trust in him. He waits for the heat to leave the room. He asks again when she's gathered. That's more care than most men in most books get near.

And yet. The afternoon is where my stomach turned over, and it turned over *while I was enjoying it*, which is the uncomfortable part. The pointing game is genuinely delicious — "I point. You pick," and her laughing "before she could decide whether to," and the slow transformation from offended propriety ("the bald social wrongness of it") into a woman who "had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." The dance metaphor is the best writing in the chapter: "a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves... already leaning into a turn that hasn't been called yet." I felt that in my own spine. The kiss on the library walk, when she tastes herself again and her breath catches — that's the peak for me, more than the bed.

But I keep looking at what he's actually doing. He reads her completely and she doesn't know she's being read. "He let the camouflage stand." He watches her hand him her own appetite dressed as a gift — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy" — and he finds it *beautiful*, and lets her keep the costume. Is that mercy or is that custody? He says he wants her "free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did," and I believe he believes it. I also notice he's spent the entire walk building a want she's not permitted to own, and that at the end the only question left in her is "*is this one mine to take?*" — she turns to him for permission. He gave her a choice inside a frame he built. After the bathroom chapter, after "he could have the face," I can't un-know that she's performing a yes to a man who is counting on it being real.

Randi moved on me hard, and not warmly. The shoulder-check is the coldest thing in the book so far. Thirty feet of geometry "so gradual they looked like chance," and then — the detail that made me go still — "it was the *redhead's* hand that flew to her own mouth. It was the redhead who pulled up short, stricken, apologizing." A decade of dance and three years of sorority folded into three seconds, aimed at a stranger who was laughing with her head thrown back. I'd been feeling tender toward Randi since the bench. Here she was *frightening*, and the little happy footwork on the brick afterward ("there and gone") made it worse, not better — a woman delighted with herself. Also: "Do you want a cheeseburger?" / "No. I can't eat that." Two words and I was back in the kitchen with the thin apple slices.

Vee is the only person on this page I have no suspicion of and total sympathy for, which is exactly why I'm afraid. She's rendered the way nobody else in the book has been — not graded by the grammar of labels, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That line is the warmest in the chapter and it's also the sentence that told me she's prey. She's being *selected*, by two people, out of the current, and she apologized to the woman who hit her. And then Randi, head down against his chest, low: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I actually felt cold. Not *will she want this* — will she *like* the apparatus we already own. And Pace doesn't answer. He says "Let's go feed you." The non-answer is the loudest thing on the page.

The one place I felt a thumb: the inset fantasies. "The sprinter was getting her out of her clothes from behind, and the soft one stood in front of her and did nothing but hold her eyes while it happened." Hot in isolation, but four of them in a row started to feel like a catalogue, and the chapter's own momentum stalled each time. And "It was the best idea he'd had in a month" is a little too pleased with itself for a man I'm meant to be partly worried about.

The titles. **The Pointing Game** reads to me now as the sweetest-sounding name for the most calculated thing he's done — a man made a game so a woman could want something without being accused of wanting it. It did slightly flatten my surprise: knowing a game was coming, the walk felt pre-arranged before his hand came up. The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — detonated for me here. *He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon.* Someone at this table is the devil, and after today I genuinely cannot tell whether it's Pace, or Randi, or the two of them as a unit. And "a polite invitation" is now unmistakably a shoulder in a stranger's arm, an apology accepted, a stats class in common. Vee has already been invited, politely, and doesn't know it. No tagline was supplied beyond that line.

What I want: Vee's own head, desperately. What I dread, as a guess from here: that the woman I'm rooting for is the one with the least information in the room.

Dance-as-reading: the follow breaking frame (first). Deadbolts/locks — absent here, but her "yes" arriving before thought is the opposite of that key turning.

**Symbolism:** The single sugar maple "burning orange in a field of things that had not yet decided to change" — one body changing ahead of schedule, announced twice. The 200-year-old colonnade "composed... by men who wanted the eye to travel exactly this way" sitting under a man arranging where her eye travels; the page put that there on purpose and I took it.

**Characterization:** Pace deepened and darkened in the same move — the care is real and so is the management, and he cannot see the difference. Randi deepened hardest: the shoulder-check gave her a competence I hadn't seen and didn't like, and the bare "Yes" gave her a want she can't hold. Vee arrives vivid in three paragraphs on charm alone. The five pointed-at women are sketches, fine for what they are.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The bed and the quad both earn their length; the fantasy inserts are where it pooled, and the middle two pointings are one more than needed.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building, and turning outward at exactly the right time. *The Bench* went all the way inward, *Standards* held her in airless stillness, and this opens the door and puts a third person in the frame — a real escalation rather than another pass at the same beat.
