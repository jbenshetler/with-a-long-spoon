# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one worked on me and I resented how much. Vee reading the text three times, pressing the phone to her chest, grinning at a dark screen — I was right there with her, and the thing that got me wasn't the date, it was *Saturday, he said, and he didn't ask her to answer that night.* "The other ones always made you answer. Right then, on the phone... So they'd know where they stood." That's the whole chapter in one sideways line: she's not swooning over romance, she's swooning over not being on the hook. And the awful part is she's right — he did build the quiet in, deliberately, and it is kind, and I've watched him do exactly this kind of patient carpentry on another woman's nervous system in a room with a bench in it.

Because I know what Vee doesn't, and it sat in my chest the entire dinner like a stone in warm water. He was pointed at her across a lawn. Randi said *Yes* before she could dress it up, and then came to his house and made him say it with wine in her mouth — *Tell.* So when Christine warms at the door, and the little dishes crowd in, and Vee stops watching herself eat for what is clearly the first time in her adult life — that was the most tender passage in the book so far and also the one I could hardly breathe through. The mirror to the apple is ruthless: he sliced Randi's apple thin because thin is what she'd permit, and he orders Vee a table too big to police. Both are attention. Both are true kindness. Both are a man learning exactly where a woman's hunger lives, and neither woman knows he's keeping two ledgers.

Pace — I don't have a clean feeling about him and I don't think I'm supposed to. Nothing he does on this page is cruel; he leaves the last inch where it is, twice, and I *melted* at that. "He wasn't going to take it any further than she had. Whatever came next was hers too." That's the most genuinely erotic consent I've read in a while, and the kiss landed hot precisely because she had to choose it out loud, twice, against her own rule, with the grin still on her mouth. But he came to this lobby freshly shaved because his girlfriend pointed at this girl, and he has not told her, and the not-telling is now a load-bearing wall. The froyo chapter let me almost forget. This one wouldn't, because it's *so* good to her.

Vee I love and fear for in equal measure. The elevator nearly closing on her, the borrowed shoes, the tabbouleh going down into a hollow older than noon — she's so hungry to be chosen that being chosen is all the verification she needs. "She stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong... and she let it not be there." Oh, honey. And Cassie, ten words on the page and still the truest person in the book: she doesn't tease about this one, she says "That's not a boy. That's a man," which is both the most loving thing anyone's said to Vee and, from where I'm sitting, the worst possible news. Her flat *Goodbye, Vee* from the lecture hall keeps ringing.

Erotic charge: peaked in odd, non-genital places and I'm not mad about it. His hands tearing hot bread. The drip caught off the side of his hand and licked without a pause in what he was saying — I actually felt that one. The right hand every time, the rule he keeps that she can't read. The tea glasses in little brass cages, "made... so you could hold them while they were still too hot to hold" — that's the most charged object in the chapter and it's a cup. The kiss is warm rather than scorching, and it should be; the heat here is the yielding, not the friction.

Friction: very little, honestly. The food writing flirts with over-explaining its own metaphor — "one to wake you and one to fold you under" is the author's thumb leaning in, and "she'd thought she knew how hungry she was. She hadn't" comes close to underlining what the scene had already made me feel. The "What've you got on this man?" line from Cassie reads a touch sitcom-bright against her usual flatness. Small prices.

The titles. "May I Choose" is doing double duty and the second duty is nasty: it's the courtesy of *May I call you?* — his whole grammar, and what Vee falls for — but it's also a menu and she is on it, chosen off a lawn in a game where she was pointed at and someone else said yes. The chapter gives away nothing, but the title recolors itself the second he says the three words and her relief "went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." **WITH A LONG SPOON** I now can't unhear — sup with the devil and use a long spoon — and tonight there were no spoons at all, only hands and bread, and the one cup that was too hot to hold came with brass built around it. **A POLITE INVITATION**: the politeness *is* the mechanism. I'm braced.

What I want: I want Vee to have this, actually have it, which is the trap. What I dread is Randi walking into it — not as villain exactly, because I saw her face on that couch holding a want too big for it. What I expect (guess from here): Vee finds out she was pointed at, and the finding out will hurt worse than a lie would have, because everything on this page was real.
