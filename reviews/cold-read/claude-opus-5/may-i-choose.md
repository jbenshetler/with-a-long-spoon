# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-opus-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch008 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter was lovely and I hated how much I loved it. Vee's whole evening is written from inside a person being handled beautifully and having no idea, and I'm the one at the table who knows. I know she was *pointed at.* I know Randi sat on his lap with wine in her mouth and said *tell,* and I know the girl who built the too-big froyo cup is a story told back in a warm house. So every good thing here arrives doubled: the shower and the shave and the polished old boots — *he'd brought the real things, the ones he already looked after* — genuinely moved me, and simultaneously read as a man who knows precisely what a girl in that lobby will register. I could not get either reading to cancel the other, and I don't think I'm supposed to.

And it *worked on me* anyway. The restaurant is the best sustained warmth in the book so far. The single dog-eared page she can't read, the soft foreign words she lets stay closed — "each soft sound a closed door with something behind it, and she sat there with her mouth watering at promises she couldn't have read aloud, wanting things she had no names for" — that's the whole book's erotics compressed into a menu. Then the hunger that "kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon," and the one that undid me: "Somewhere in there she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong. She didn't notice she'd been waiting until she wasn't." I felt that in my chest. She loses the thread of what he's saying and nothing bad happens. Nobody has ever let her drop the thread. Whatever else Pace is, he is the first person in Vee's life to give her a place to put down the work of being likable — and I ached, because the gift is real and the ledger it's being entered into is not hers.

Pace: my feeling has moved, and moved toward *more* alarm, not less, because he was more tender here than he's ever been. "May I choose?" is the same instrument as "May I call you?" and the same instrument as the bench — build the position, hand over the word, let her say yes to something she can't see the shape of. He weighs what he chooses for her, he asks the one question (anything you won't eat), he doesn't explain the menu to her. It's *good*. It's also, precisely, what he did to Randi with the apple slices. The detail that snagged me hardest was the smallest: "It was the right hand again, a pattern now, enough times over to be one: always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." Vee files it as charming mystery. I read it as a man who runs rules on himself and I do not yet know what the left hand is for. And when she names her rule — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — he doesn't push, doesn't charm, doesn't wait her out. He *lets it close and walks her on.* That's the most frightening thing in the chapter, because she comes after him. The refusal was the last handle she had and he took it by declining to take it.

Cassie I love without reservation and I'm now worried about her. After the pool, she's the one person in this book who wants nothing from Vee. She doesn't tease about this. But "That's not a boy. That's a man." is the one line that felt like the author's hand on my shoulder — too clean, too plaque-ready, and from the character whose whole register is four flat words. And there's the older, unresolved chill: Cassie's *"Goodbye, Vee"* in the lecture hall, which nobody has explained and which sits in me like a stone every time Cassie is warm. Christine at the restaurant is new and I liked her instantly — an older woman whose face *eased* at who walked in. That reads as a real, unperformed reference for Pace. It's the only outside testimony about him I've gotten, and I noticed how badly I wanted it to mean something.

Erotic charge: real, and almost entirely displaced into food and hands. The bread torn with care for the heat, the drip caught off the side of his hand and licked away mid-sentence without ceremony, her eyes going to his hands *before* she reaches for food herself. That peaked for me higher than any undressing would have. The hand-taking outside is where it tips into something else: "less like something beginning than something resolving." Gorgeous, and the one place I felt the prose push a half-step too hard — "It was too much for what it was" tells me what the paragraph had already made me feel.

**The titles.** "May I Choose" is the hinge, and it gave nothing away before I read it — it detonates on arrival and then recolors backward, because *choose* is what Vee hands over, and the title is his line, not hers, even though the chapter is hers. **WITH A LONG SPOON** I now hear as the old warning about supping with the devil, and **A POLITE INVITATION** is doing exactly what the chapter does: the courtesy *is* the mechanism. *May I.* He never once takes. She keeps handing.

What I want next: I want to know what Randi does when the *later* she promised arrives — she's the one holding a want too big for her face, and she's the one who'll have to be in the room. Guess from here, clearly a guess: whatever Randi builds, she'll build to keep from having to say she wants it, and Vee will be the thing she builds it out of. I dread the moment Vee learns she was pointed at. I don't want it to come. I want another dinner first.

**Symbolism:** The unreadable menu — closed doors, promises without names, wanting what you can't pronounce. The bright dish and the dark dish, "one to wake you and one to fold you under," which is a fair description of what he does to women. The bread torn *hot* and handed around.

**Characterization:** Vee deepened a lot — the braced-for-the-bill reflex made explicit, and her competence at holding conversational threads shown as *labor* for the first time. Pace deepened by getting kinder, which is the most unnerving direction available. Cassie held, with one line that flattened her.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Earned its length. The dinner's slow drift ("the evening went soft at the edges") is the point, not a sag. The dorm opening is brisk and does its job.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building. Coming off the pool's quiet and the wine-and-tannin heat, this is the first full Vee-and-Pace scene and it lands with weight — but three consecutive Vee-POV chapters have me hungry for Randi's side of the ledger again.
