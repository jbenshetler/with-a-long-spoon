# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This one swept me. It also frightened me, and the fright is entirely because of what I know and Vee doesn't — but let me do the swoon first, because the swoon is the chapter.

The text arriving with no demand in it: "He didn't say where, and he didn't ask her to answer that night." And then Vee sideways, half to herself — "The other ones always made you answer. Right then, on the phone. So they'd know where they stood. He just left it with me. Like it could keep." That's the whole thing, isn't it. That sentence made me ache for her, because it's a girl discovering that nobody has ever handed her time before. And Cassie — Cassie, who teases about everything — not teasing: "That's not a boy. That's a man." I trust Cassie completely and I'm glad she's the one in the room.

The lobby. He'd showered and shaved for her and worn the old polished boots instead of new ones, and Vee reads all of it correctly: "the boys in this building bought new shoes to look like they'd lived, and he just wore the ones that had." The elevator nearly closing on her while she stares. Her *begging the doors for mercy*. I was so fond of her right there. And then the restaurant, which is the best-written swoon in the book so far for me: the unreadable menu, and the three words, and the small collapse of relief — "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." He didn't take it lightly. He asked her one question and then built the meal. The soft sounds she couldn't repeat, "each soft sound a closed door with something behind it, and she sat there with her mouth watering at promises she couldn't have read aloud." God. Yes.

And then she *eats*. She eats without the part of her that watches her eating. "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That landed on me hard — that's the hunger the book keeps making literal, and here it finally gets fed instead of portioned. She loses the thread of what he's saying and *nothing bad happens*. The low background readiness for the bill to come due isn't there. Then the mint tea in its little brass cage, made so you can hold what's too hot to hold — lovely object, and it woke her the way it woke me. Then the hand, unasked, "less like something beginning than something resolving," her hand wrong all its life for want of his exactly. And the tree-shadow, the rule spoken out of reflex, him not arguing — no lean, no charm turned up, nothing she'd braced for — and her feet not going. "I'm making an exception." I melted. Straightforwardly. That's a beautiful ending to a date.

Now the other thing, which never left me for a line of it. I was at the dining hall. I watched him point and I watched Randi's stride drop out from under her and I heard the bare *yes*, and I know Vee's collision on that lawn wasn't a collision — Randi executed it, clean enough to look like nothing. So every tender thing here has a second face. He hands her the choice, and I've seen him hand Randi the evening and call it her choosing; I've seen him work a woman past the end of her own apparatus and then say *there you are.* "May I choose?" is the same key as "May I call you?" — and I remember Vee heard the *may* as a word from another vocabulary. The care is real. I believe it's real; that's what makes it unbearable. He genuinely is a man who notices the shallowness of a shelf and the thinness of an apple slice. He's also a man running something he hasn't named to her, and Randi — absent from this chapter entirely, not mentioned once — is somewhere with her phone. That absence rang louder than a scene would have. In *Substitution* Vee thought Randi was bored, thumbing at "some boy." I know who the boy was.

So: no suspicion of *Vee*, no suspicion of Cassie, enormous fondness for both. Toward Pace I'm split in a way the text earned rather than hinted — he behaves beautifully on this page and I still flinched at "she was already learning she could trust where he was taking her." The froyo chapter had the same doubleness but gentler; here the meal, the feeding, the surrender of choice stacked it up.

Friction: almost none. If anything the prose leans a half-degree hard on telling me the wanting was too much for what it was — "It should have been nothing, and it wasn't. It was the first time." Slightly over-narrated for a moment that was already doing it. And "Tossers" Cassie's absence of a bunk-scene payoff is fine; I wanted one more beat with her at the end, and got none.

Titles: the chapter title gave away nothing and then arrived mid-scene as three spoken words, which was a small pleasure. But the cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON** — is the half of a proverb about who you sit down to eat with, and this chapter is a *meal*, shared with bare hands, in which she eats what she cannot name. **A POLITE INVITATION** now reads like a plainer threat than it did before: this whole book's danger arrives with a *may I*. That recolored the swoon without cancelling it, which is exactly where I want to be.

What I want: for her to have this. What I dread: that "See you later" was already scheduled in a calendar she can't see.
