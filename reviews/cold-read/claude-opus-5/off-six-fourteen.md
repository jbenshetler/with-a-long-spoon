# Cold read (grounded) — Off Six-Fourteen

*scene: scenes/off-six-fourteen.md · model: claude-opus-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch008 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is a short, warm, almost weightless chapter and it hurt me more than the bench did.

Vee comes in still lit, talking before her coat's off, and I loved her here — the brass cages she can't explain right, the spin that nearly takes out the closet door and is "over before it was really a dance at all," the "*Look at me*" she can't stay seated for. I have been rooting for this girl since she stood outside the Chi Latte sign not going in, and this is the first time the book has let her have something and just *have* it. Cassie is the whole reason it works. Cassie, who "did not go to a pool to be admired," who fired that low sheet of water across ten feet instead of shouting at the bleachers, gets off the desk chair for this, chin on knees, sock feet, asking the small questions that keep it coming. I trust Cassie more than anyone in this book. She's the only person on the page who wants nothing.

And then Cassie asks the question. "How much did he give you to drink?" It landed on me like a hand on the back of my neck — because it's the *right* question, the one a real friend asks, and because Vee's answer is the cleanest statement of consent the book has given me: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." She's telling the truth. That's what wrecks me. The kiss under the tree *was* hers — she stopped, she pulled him back, he'd already given it up without a lean or a wait. Nobody drugged her, nobody pressured her. It's just that the man she chose to kiss was pointed out to her by the woman he'd fucked that afternoon, on a quad, in daylight, as a game. Randi arranged the collision, the seat, the coffee, and was sitting three feet away holding a cup she kept not sipping from while he asked for Vee's number. Vee's "on purpose" is real and it's also the smallest true thing inside a much larger arrangement, and she has no way to know that, and Cassie — who reads rooms flat and fast, who clocked "he looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" — has no way to know it either. I sat there wanting to shout through the page and knowing it wouldn't help.

Then the dossier. "His name is Pace, he is *lovely*, and I am not going to go missing." Vee counts it off "like plenty," and Cassie repeats it back — "*That's the whole address. Off six-fourteen*" — and I went cold in a way the chapter is completely relaxed about. It's played as a bit. It *is* a bit; Cassie's state-trooper joke is exactly the joke friends make. But the page lets Vee discover, mid-sentence, that she doesn't know whether he's rich, has never seen the house, can't say where it is past where the road goes to gravel — and she files that as "the best pile there was," the pile of things she gets to find out. Meanwhile I know that house. I know the too-warm rooms, the quartersawn oak, the deadbolt Randi turns first thing, and the thing in the bedroom that is a table when its top is on. Vee's happy ignorance is furnished, for me, with all that. And Cassie's "borrow good ones" is such a small tender ordinary send-off that it made the gap worse.

I'm not suspicious of Pace's *behavior* in this chapter — he isn't in it, and everything Vee reports of him is scrupulous: no argument, no charm turned up, no patient wait, he let it close and walked her on. I believe he meant it. What's off is structural, and the text earned it in earlier chapters, not by hinting here: he asked Randi a question in bed, got a body's answer, and built a game out of it, and Vee is the answer. He's kind at every individual moment and the whole is something else, and I can't get those two facts to sit in the same shape. That's the most interesting problem the book has given me.

The title. **Off Six-Fourteen** is the address that isn't an address — the thing Vee offers as proof she's safe, which is proof of nothing, and she says it twice and Cassie says it twice and by the fourth time it's stopped being a road. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it recolored *hard* on the last page. **With a Long Spoon** I can't unhear — you sup with the devil using a long spoon — and **A Polite Invitation** is Pace exactly: "May I choose." "May I call you." The politeness *is* the invitation, and it's the thing there's no defense against, because who refuses a man who asks properly. My packet gives no tagline or jacket copy, so that's all I have to go on.

What I want next: the hike. West of town, past the gravel, "a trail he goes to," real shoes. I want it to be good — I genuinely do, I want her to have the day — and I'm braced. Guess from here: she ends up at that house, and the long table is in the middle of the room with its top on, and she won't know what she's looking at. I also want to know what Randi does while this happens, because the last I saw of her she was lying against Pace's neck deciding never to come out again, and this girl is her gift and her rival and her project all at once.

**Heat:** 1 — the kiss is recounted, not lived; the charge is residual warmth in a friendship scene.

**Romance:** 2 — Vee's giddy "Me." over her own chosen kiss, and the real tenderness is Vee–Cassie: gladness with a dry floor under it.

**Motifs & images:** Cold thin line of air from the cracked window, present twice and unfelt by Vee both times (first sighting — and it closes the chapter). Brass tea cages (repeat, from the restaurant last chapter). Borrowed shoes (repeat — she borrowed heels for the date; now real shoes for the hike). Cassie folded up with knees to chest, feet not reaching the floor (repeat, from the bunk in "May I Choose"). Cassie's flat single-word register (repeat — "Cassie," "Goodbye, Vee"). Food/hunger as the measure of what she'll let herself have — absent here, replaced by appetite for the unknown. Gravel where the road stops being mapped (first).

**Symbolism:** "Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" as the edge of what Vee knows, offered as if it were knowledge. The window's cold laid over both girls while only the reader feels it.

**Characterization:** Cassie deepened most — the drink question gives her a protective intelligence the pool chapter only implied, and she still doesn't tease about the thing that matters. Vee deepened: her joy is specific, physical, self-mocking, and the "best pile there was" line tells me exactly how she meets a blank. Pace absent, unchanged. Randi's absence from Vee's account is itself loud.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Brisk and right-sized; a four-page scene doing one thing. The only place it slackens is the middle beat of the address bit, which repeats once more than it needs to — though the repetition is also what makes the title land.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building. A short exhale after the long dinner, but it converts that dinner's swoon into dread without breaking the warmth — and it plants a destination (the hike, the house) that pulls hard.
