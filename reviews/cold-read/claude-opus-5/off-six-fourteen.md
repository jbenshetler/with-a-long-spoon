## Reader reaction

This one is short and it's a glass of cold water thrown on me, except the chapter doesn't know that. It's just Vee, home, spilling. "She was already talking." And I loved her in it — the brass tea cages she'll "never explain right," the spin that nearly takes out the closet door and "was over before it was really a dance at all — the room too small to hold what she had to do with it," then the heap in the bean bag going "*Look at me*." That's the purest happiness anyone in this book has been allowed yet, and it's all hers, and the thing she's happiest about is not that he kissed her. It's that *she* did. "He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me." After Randi, whose entire apparatus is arranging a face and handing the day to him, watching a girl come home glowing because she authored one inch of her own night was genuinely moving.

And Cassie. Cassie is the best person in this book and I'm not sure the book knows how much I've started needing her. "How much did he give you to drink?" — asked flatly, in the middle of being delighted, because that is what you ask. Not a shadow, not a plot device; a friend running the check. And when Vee says nobody did anything to me, I wasn't talked into it, I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose — Cassie *believes her* and moves on. "Okay. Look at you." The gladness with the dry floor under it. Then the state-trooper joke, which is a joke, and the way she keeps turning the same stone over: "Off six-fourteen." "That's the whole address. *Off six-fourteen*." "Is he rich?" She's the only person on the page pressing, and she's pressing in a register everyone can laugh at, and Vee laughs, and the pressing goes nowhere.

Which is where the dread sat in me the whole four pages, because I know what Vee is telling this happy story about. I watched him point at her outside the dining hall while his girlfriend went still and said *yes* before she could dress it as anything. I watched Randi straddle him on the couch and say *tell* into his ear, hot, and drink the wine and take nothing else. So Vee's dossier — "I know where he lives and everything," counted off "like plenty" — landed on me as terror. She doesn't know the last name; the sorority doesn't either. The page itself flags the thinness: it hadn't occurred to her she didn't know if he was rich, "and it didn't trouble her now that it had; it went into the pile of things she'd get to find out, which was the best pile there was." That sentence is so tender and so open-throated I winced.

I want to be honest about my divided feeling on Pace, because the chapter doesn't complicate him — it only lets Vee report him, and everything she reports is genuinely good conduct. He didn't argue, didn't lean, didn't turn the charm up, let her rule close. He asked for hiking and said one thing: real shoes. My suspicion of him isn't built out of this scene; it's built out of the quad and the wine, and it's specifically this: he takes the body's answer for the true one, and he's very sure he's handing Randi a gift. Nobody in his accounting is Vee. She's the gift. That's the off-ness, and it was earned two chapters ago, not manufactured here.

The title. "Off Six-Fourteen" gave nothing away going in and everything coming out — it's the sum total of what she knows about him, said twice, flat, by the one person who hears it as thin. A road past where it goes to gravel. And the cover board — WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION — is now doing real work on me. He who sups with the devil. The *politeness* is the whole trap: "May I choose," "May I call you," the held door, the pencil handed back. Every courtesy has been real and every courtesy has also been the invitation. I don't know yet who's supping and who's serving; Randi may be as much on the menu as Vee.

Friction: almost none, though the borrowed-shoes beat ("You don't own real shoes." / "I'll borrow real shoes.") is the second borrowed-shoes exchange in three chapters and the second time Cassie plays verdict-giver from a folded-up position, and I felt the repetition slightly. Erotically this chapter is a rest stop, which it should be — the charge is all retrospective. And I noticed the cold: the cracked window, "the window let its cold thin line in over the both of them, and Vee lay back in the bean bag warm and did not feel it." Randi lay under an inadequate duvet in a cold corner *not crying*. Vee lies in the cold not feeling it. I don't think that's me straining. What I want next: the hike, the house off six-fourteen, the bench she'll walk past and not understand — and I dread the moment Randi and Vee are in the same room with him. Guess from here, clearly marked: Vee is going to be told it's a gift, and she'll say yes before she knows what she's agreeing to.
