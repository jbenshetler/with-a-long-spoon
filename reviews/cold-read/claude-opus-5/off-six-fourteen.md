# Cold read (grounded) — Off Six-Fourteen

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This one is short and it hurt. Not because anything bad happens — nothing bad happens, it's eight minutes of a girl being happy in her own room — but because I'm carrying the shape of it and she isn't. Vee comes in still warm, already talking, and the thing she most wants Cassie to have is: *"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose."* And she's right. On the path, under that tree, she was. He left the inch there and she crossed it herself. That part is clean and I don't want to take it from her. It's just that I watched Randi stop walking on the quad and say *yes* with her whole body, and I watched Pace point, and I know the collision outside the dining hall was executed, and so Vee's proudest sentence is true about the kiss and false about the week, and she has no way to know which.

The spin is what got me. *"the room too small to hold what she had to do with it"* — up out of the quiet, arms up, nearly taking out the closet door, down into the bean bag in a heap. I grinned at that. I've been in that room. And the way her voice drops before the tree — the prose actually slows with her, *"the part wanted a different voice than the brass-cage voice"* — so Cassie has to lean the last inch in. That's lovely writing about intimacy that isn't sexual at all, and it's the warmest thing in the chapter.

Cassie is the person I trust most in this book now, and it isn't close. She gets pulled out of the desk chair by good news. She asks the small questions that keep it coming. And then — because it's Cassie — *"How much did he give you to drink?"* Flat, dry, straight at the only question that matters, asked by the one person in Vee's life who will ask it. And when Vee says nothing, no wine, *tea*, Cassie takes her at her word immediately: *"and this time the okay had her believing it."* That's what a friend is. Then the state-troopers joke, which is a joke, and which went through me like cold water, because Cassie is *playing* at the thing I'm actually afraid of. *"a man you met at a coffee shop."* She doesn't know she met him too. She waved at him from the next table.

And then the address. *"Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel."* Vee counts it off *"like a dossier, like plenty"* — that clause is the whole chapter — and Cassie says the quietly devastating line: *"That's the whole address."* Not suspicion, just accuracy. Vee doesn't know his last name's weight, hasn't seen the house, doesn't know if he's rich, hasn't been out there. And the thing is, I've been out there. I know the room at the end of that road and I know what's in the middle of it with a top on it, and I know a woman asked, in that house, *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* So "off six-fourteen" is, for me, not a charming gap in Vee's knowledge. It's a direction with a destination I've already toured.

Where I'm honestly unsettled, and I want to be careful here because the page hasn't given me cruelty yet: Pace has been *good* to Vee on every page he's shared with her. Giving back the pencil. The *may I choose*. Not pretending her problem was hard, not pretending it was easy. Leaving the last inch. Not telling her where they were going but also not telling her where she'd end up, which are different things. None of that was fake — that's what makes it bad. The care is real and it was also *aimed*, and he aimed it at a girl his girlfriend picked off a walk because she wanted her. He didn't lie. He also hasn't said one word. Randi has said a great many words — *love you, girl*, *see you later* — and sat in his lap drinking wine to hear about her. The *politeness* is the part that's getting under my skin: every move in this courtship has been impeccably mannered, and nobody has told Vee anything.

What stops this from being only dread is how little the chapter cheats to get there. It doesn't put a shadow on the wall. It lets Vee be happy, lets Cassie be funny, lets the window hold its cold thin line over both of them while Vee lies back and doesn't feel it — and *that's* the whole image, really. Cassie keeps the cold, Pace keeps his house too warm, Randi gets cold after and brings a blanket. Vee's just warm. The one place I felt a thumb, barely, was *"which went into the pile of things she'd get to find out, which was the best pile there was"* — a half-beat too explanatory, telling me the innocence I could already see. And the "borrow good ones" button is a touch neat. Small complaints.

The chapter title is the address, and after reading it means: this is all she's got. It's also the first physical pull toward the house — the book's center of gravity — and she's going there in borrowed shoes. The cover board says **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, and I keep hearing the proverb under it: you need a long spoon to sup with the devil. I've got no idea yet who's holding the spoon, and I genuinely can't tell whether the devil is Pace, Randi, or the face Randi put back on in his bathroom and swore she'd never come out from behind again. *A Polite Invitation* feels exactly right for this run of chapters — every single thing has been asked beautifully. Nobody's been told what they're agreeing to. (My packet shows no tagline or jacket copy — just the cover line.)

What I want next: the hike, the house, Vee seeing the long table with the top on. What I dread: Randi standing in that house when Vee arrives. Guess from here, clearly marked: Vee finds out what she's been chosen for from somebody other than Pace, and it's Randi who tells her, warmly.
