# Cold read (grounded) — Dear

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The chapter opened on a small wound and I didn't clock how much it would cost until the last page. Cassie up first, cap on, "with the readiness of a person who has somewhere to be and is glad of it" — and then that look going past Vee to Randi and being *folded away* before it came back. Cassie didn't say anything. She never does. And then Vee, out loud, cheerfully, closes the door herself: "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one. All the—" with the little hand-motion. I actually made a noise. The book let her have the reasonable thought right after ("nobody ever called that anything but healthy"), and then put in the one clause that gutted me: "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's Vee's whole method — read the room perfectly, then decline to read yourself — and this is the first time I've watched it cost her the one person who has never asked her for anything.

Then the smoothie place, and Randi runs a beautiful, horrible little operation and I could see every seam and Vee couldn't see one. First the fake catastrophe date: Friday, tall, dreamy, dressed like money, talked about himself all night — a photographic negative of Pace, complete with the thesis dropped in like a pearl: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." It's a key cut to fit Vee's lock, and it's handed over as *vulnerability*, as "hear my catastrophe first so you know what you have." And the "Paul?" with the finger-snap — she made herself grope for his name. That's the coldest beat in the chapter and it's four words long.

And then she takes the thing. Vee's whole treasure from Chapter 10 — *nobody did anything to me, I wasn't talked into it, I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose* — the one-turn spin in the dorm room, "Look at me." Randi gets her within inches of it and turns it around: "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." And Vee, who had "the dignified version sitting right there ready," hands it over: "…Yeah. Because of him." That's a theft, and Vee helped, and neither of them will ever call it that. I felt sick and I also completely understood why she did it — Randi's face came up "a whole notch," and Vee "sat up a little under it." She'll trade anything for that notch. Same with "You let him choose for you. And you liked it," said slowly, held to the light — Randi gets Vee to admit the pleasure of handing herself over, and then generously doesn't press, and the not-pressing lands as *kindness*. That's the move I most distrust in this book and it worked on me too; I felt the warmth before I felt the hand in the pocket.

What I can't resolve, and don't want to: whether "Vee's my dear friend" was a play. It arrived with Brooke's eyes on it and Brooke's "We missed you Thursday" already sitting on the table — Thursday being, as I happen to know, the night Randi couldn't wait and drove out to the house and said *Tell*. So Brooke is keeping a ledger, and Randi answered a ledger entry by taking a red-haired stranger's hand in public. But the hand was cool and damp from the cup, and the page says Vee knew *without being able to say how* that it cost something. I think it cost something. I think it was also useful. The book let both stand and I'm leaving them standing. The unnamed tall one with the coffee, who never speaks and whose gaze finishes "its tour of Vee and handed back nothing but more smile" — she frightened me more than Brooke did. Somebody is being surveyed and it is no longer only Randi.

Heat-wise this ran quieter than the last few but it isn't cold: the two-block walk with "two small skies where her eyes should have been" and the side of Vee's face gone hot with the sun as an alibi — "This was not fine. This had color in it" — is the most nakedly the book has let Vee's want for Randi come up to the surface, and she still won't look at it. The high chair is the erotic engine of the scene and it's not even about sex: "she had been set up on a shelf," feet not reaching the floor, the room able to turn and look. Exposure she didn't consent to, then a hand on hers that makes the wrong-size feeling *stop mattering*. That's the trade this book keeps making and it's working on me. Small grim note I couldn't unsee: Randi's order is beet, wheatgrass, **egg white**, oat milk, ordered "as though it were her usual" — she's drinking her mother. And Vee said oat milk because Randi was standing there, and let herself not look at that either.

Friction: almost none. The one place I felt the hand on the scale was "to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi, to be a girl from a stats class and no one's business at all" — a beat more explanation than Vee's "We have stats together" needed; the line had already done it. Otherwise the withholding is exact.

**Dear** as a title is a small knife. I read it as the address on a letter until "Vee's my dear friend" arrived, and then it recolored twice — *dear* as beloved, *dear* as expensive, a thing that costs. It gave nothing away beforehand and reorganized the chapter after. Against the cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — it sits about right: sup with the devil and use a long spoon, and the invitation keeps being polite, keeps being lovely, keeps being *offered* rather than pushed. (My packet gave me no jacket copy and no tagline this time, only that board line.) What I want next is Cassie, and what I dread is that Vee gets to the house off six-fourteen before she gets back to the dorm and notices who she left in the aisle. Guess from here, clearly a guess: Brooke's "We should all get dinner sometime" is going to come back and not be nothing.
