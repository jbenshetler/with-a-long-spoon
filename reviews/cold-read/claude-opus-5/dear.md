## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The first page hurt before anything happened. Cassie up with her cap on, "with the readiness of a person who has somewhere to be and is glad of it," and that look going *past* Vee to Randi and then folding itself away before Vee could catch it. "I'll see you at the dorm." Easy. Already going. And Vee not watching her go — because Randi had her by the wrist. Then Vee does the thing that actually made me put a hand to my mouth: she supplies a reason Randi didn't ask for. "It isn't really her place, the smoothie one." That's Vee's own shame about that room, handed to Cassie to carry so Vee doesn't have to. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." I didn't need the narrator to flag it — I'd already felt the floor tilt — but God, that's the first time Vee has *done* something to someone instead of to herself. Cassie is being let go of in forty-minute increments, politely, and Vee is calling it healthy.

Randi's Friday-night French-restaurant catastrophe is the coldest, most skillful lie in the book so far, and I enjoyed it the way you enjoy watching a card trick you can't catch. Snapping her fingers after her own boyfriend's name — "Paul?" — then "Pace," said back and already past. And the invented lacrosse bore exists to deliver one line: *a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else.* She built a frame and then asked Vee to fill it, and Vee filled it so gladly. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Held up to the light, exactly the way Pace held Randi's own yes up in bed — then the warm "Mm," the letting-go, which Vee counts as a kindness. She's using his hands on Vee. I don't know whether she knows that.

And then the real theft, so quiet I almost missed it. Vee had the dignified version ready — *I made an exception* — the sentence she gave Cassie, the sentence that was the whole triumph of that night: *nobody did anything to me, I did it on purpose.* Randi got there first with "Because you couldn't not. Because of him," and Vee's own sovereignty came out of her instead as "the smaller, truer thing." The page calls it truer. I don't believe it's truer. I think it's the version that puts the doing somewhere other than Vee, and Vee has spent her whole life being relieved to be let off that hook. That's where I ached.

But I also laughed, and the chapter earned every bit of its warmth. "No bra is not an option for me" — and the *lift,* once, to make the case. That's the first joke Vee has made about her own body out loud instead of rolling her shoulders inward, and the two of them howling on their high shelf in a room of girls who all got dressed together was pure swoon. And then the line I'll carry: "Ask and then ravish," a beat late, low, "the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." Randi slipped. For one second the register from the bench came out, and Vee — hands running slow down the tops of her thighs, already up the trail — "felt her there in it beside her." They are in the same fantasy about the same man and only one of them knows it. That's the hottest thing in the chapter and it's two women drinking smoothies.

Brooke arriving in person moved my fear forward a notch. "We missed you Thursday" — friendly, and it sets an entire private week on the table that Vee has never been invited into, and Vee "went very slightly the wrong size for the room." Then the taller one who never speaks, whose eyes make a slow circuit of Vee twice, and the exit look: "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over." I don't know her name. I don't like how much that look rhymes with a finger pointed across a lawn. And Vee's instinct — shrink, not to hide from humiliation but "to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi" — is the most generous thing anyone does in this chapter, and nobody notices.

"Vee's my dear friend," with the cool damp hand taken and left on the white tabletop in plain view. I think that cost Randi something real, and I think it was also useful, and I can't separate those, and I don't think she can either. What I'm sure of is the chapter's title. **Dear** is the word she says, the salutation on an invitation, and *dear* as in expensive — "she did not know what it had cost Randi to say it." The jacket board says **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, and this chapter is the invitation being politely extended: not to the bench yet, just to a table, a hand, a *we should all get dinner sometime.* The long spoon is the thing Vee doesn't have and doesn't know she's supping without. Last line: "She felt picked." She has no idea how right that word is.
