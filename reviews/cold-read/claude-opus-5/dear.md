# Cold read (grounded) — Dear

## Reader reaction

**"Paul?"**

That's where the chapter got me, and it got me in the stomach. Randi snapping her fingers twice, *reaching after it and not catching it* — for the name of the man whose bed she was in yesterday, whose bench she wept on, who pointed at Vee across a lawn and said *go meet her*. I knew she'd lie. I did not know she'd do the little theatrical grope for it, the tiny added flourish of forgetting. That's not self-protection anymore. That's craft. And I sat there with Vee saying "Pace" helpfully, *correcting her*, and felt genuinely sick.

Then the Friday-date story. Tall, dreamy, dressed like money, talked about himself all night, *a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else* — and it's a beautiful piece of construction, because it's a lie built entirely out of true material. She knows exactly what being taken care of looks like; she found out on a bench with her face in a leather cradle. She's spending that knowledge as bait. "Tell me he is nothing like that" is a fishing line with her own humiliation on the hook, and Vee, delighted, bites.

And the extraction. *"You let him choose for you. And you liked it."* — said slowly, held up to the light. That is precisely what Pace did to Randi: ask the question the body has already answered, and wait for the mouth to catch up. Randi got the "…Yeah" out of Vee, small, twice, the second time with *"Because of him"* substituted for the dignified line Vee had ready — and then said "Mm," *warm, and let her have it, and went no further.* She's learned the technique from underneath and is now running it from above, on a girl who has no idea there's a technique. I don't think she'd call it that. That's what frightens me: the page doesn't show her plotting, it shows her enjoying herself, and I can't tell how much of the warmth is real. I don't think she can either.

But I have to be honest about how much of this chapter was lovely. Vee walking out into the clean light *feeling, frankly, like a person things happened to* — "Vee had been fine for two years. This was not fine. This had color in it." That's a sentence I felt in my chest. The froyo boy talk, the six-year-old-about-dinosaurs bridge story, the pride in "I kissed him" coming out flat and proud — all of it is a girl handing over the best thing that's ever happened to her, gladly, with both hands. And she *wants* to hand it over; "that was the strange part, how much she wanted to put all of it into Randi's hands." I ached. I've been that person at that table.

The hand on the white tabletop is the chapter's real seduction, and it worked on me even knowing. Cool and damp from the iced cup, taken *the way she might have reached for anything on the table*, held in plain view. *"Vee's my dear friend."* And Vee, sitting up on a shelf in a pilled cardigan with her feet not reaching the floor, feeling it *cost* Randi something. It may have! That's the part I can't close. Brooke filing it — Brooke, who two chapters ago was pleasantly interrogating Randi about a boy named Pace while three girls pretended not to listen — took in the hand and the word and *did not change.* Something was banked there. I don't know what.

Cassie is the wound. That first paragraph — the look going past Vee to Randi, and "by the time Cassie's eyes came back to Vee's, whatever had been in them was folded away." Then Vee doing the excluding herself: "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one," the little hand-gesture that means *the leggings, the matched hair.* Vee is trading a person for a room. And the book lets her not notice: "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." Same move as Randi's non-crying under the duvet, same move as the mother's voice closing the picture at Chi Latte. This book keeps showing me people declining to look at the thing, and it keeps being the most intimate thing on the page.

Friction: almost none, but Randi's monologue is *slightly* too well-shaped — "she set it up like a story she had told before and would tell again" earns most of it, but the Switzerland/lacrosse details are a beat too neatly awful. And the "dear friend" epiphany paragraph explains its own weight a shade more than it needed to; "she knew only, without being able to say how, that it had cost something" is Vee's confusion rendered very legibly for me.

The title. **"Dear"** on its own is one of the most brutal things in this book so far — because as a word it's warmth, and as an adjective in *dear friend* it's a currency, and the third meaning is sitting right there in a story where a girl is being acquired. I only saw it land when Randi said it, which is the right way round; it didn't give anything away going in. The cover line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — I finally feel the spoon. You sup with the devil with a long spoon, and nobody here has one; they're all leaning in over the bowl. "A polite invitation" now reads as the whole engine: *May I choose. Go meet her. Come to Chi Latte with me. We should all get dinner sometime.* Every door in this book is held open courteously.

What I want next: Cassie. I want her to say something flat and true and I want Vee not to be able to smooth it. And I dread, from here (a guess), that the first time Vee learns Randi and Pace are attached, it will be Randi who tells her, arranged, warmly, as a gift.
