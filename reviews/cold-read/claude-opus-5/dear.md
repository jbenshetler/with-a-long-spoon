# Cold read (grounded) — Dear

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one hurt in a way I didn't see coming, and it did it in a smoothie shop.

The first wound is Cassie. Three lines: Vee catches Cassie's look go past her to Randi "turned in her seat and lit up, already reaching for her," and by the time it comes back "whatever had been in them was folded away." Then "I'll see you at the dorm. Easy. Already going." And then Vee — my Vee, the one I trust most in this book — excuses her absence to Randi with *it isn't really her place, the smoothie one. Not her thing*, complete with the little hand-motion at the leggings and the matched hair. She's not being cruel. She's being *comfortable*. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." I sat up at that sentence. That's the first thing Vee has done in eleven chapters that I didn't want her to do, and it cost her nothing to do, which is exactly why it landed. "Goodbye, Vee" got one inch closer to making sense and I still can't reach it.

The second wound is smaller and worse. Randi opens with a fake catastrophe — Friday, tall, dreamy, dressed like money, talked about himself all night — and I *watched her build it*: it establishes her as single, it warms the table, and it hands Vee the exact frame she needs. And the punchline is the tell: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She's describing Pace by negation. She knows precisely what it is she's selling. Then she goes fishing, and gets everything: the menu, "May I choose?", *I stopped keeping track of myself*, and when Vee tries to get in front of her own face — "It was just nice. Not having to be in charge of everything for once" — Randi just says "Mm," warm, and lets it go, and Vee reads the not-pressing as a kindness. It *is* the technique. I felt sick and admiring at once.

But here's the cut. Vee came into this with the dignified version ready, the one she gave Cassie on the bean bag and meant with her whole chest: *Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it on purpose.* And Randi, soft, near tender, says: "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." And Vee says "…Yeah. Because of him." One sentence and the single thing Vee owns outright — the thing that put her on her feet spinning into the closet door — is taken off her and re-titled in Pace's name. I don't think Randi did it maliciously. I think that's the only grammar she has: wanting belongs to whoever you hand it to. She just taught it to Vee in under four seconds.

And *still* — the chapter is warm and it worked on me. The bra joke is the best laugh in the book: Vee flat-voiced, lifting once to make the case, "I'd black my own eye," Randi barking loud enough to turn a head, the two of them up on their shelf laughing at Vee's chest in a room full of girls who got dressed together. That's real friendship-shaped heat, whatever it's sitting on top of. And the high white table — the same window seat that at Chi Latte meant *you belong here* — this time reads as "set up on a shelf," "a thing the room could turn and look at." Same furniture, inverted meaning. That inversion is the chapter's smartest move and nobody had to explain it.

Erotic charge: real, and it peaks late. Randi hangs the picture — hair down, no bra, "let your mountain man ravish you against a tree" — and Vee, after the laugh has gone by, says quietly "He'd ask, though," with her hands running slow down the tops of her thighs and back, and lets the trail come back on purpose: "Before he ravished me." And Randi's answer comes "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume": *Ask and then ravish.* That's Randi's own want coming up through the floor of the bit, and Vee feeling her "there in it beside her" without knowing what she's feeling. I got hot on it. It also frightened me, because it's the only unperformed thing Randi said in the whole chapter.

Brooke's entrance dropped the temperature exactly right — "We missed you Thursday," friendly, and it sets a whole week Vee has no part of down on the table. The taller woman who never speaks, who finishes her circuit of Vee and "handed back nothing but more smile," and gives her at the end "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — I don't know who she is and I'd like to. Then: "Vee's my dear friend," and the cool damp hand closing over Vee's in plain view, unhurried. I *felt* that. And I can't settle what it is. Vee reads it as a cost paid on her behalf. I can also read it as a claim being staked in front of the chapter president, or as Randi, for one second, not managing anything. The page leaves it ajar and I'll leave it ajar. Either way Randi was the one standing between Vee and deep water, and Vee walked home "picked."

Friction: only a little. The paragraph reassuring us it's fine to have your own people ("nobody ever called that anything but healthy") nudges me toward the irony a half-beat before I'd have gotten there. And the closing "She felt picked" is a touch tidy — though *picked* is the book's word, so I'll allow it.

The title: **"Dear"** is *dear friend*, obviously — but it's also the salutation of a letter, and the cover board says **A POLITE INVITATION**, so I'm now waiting for something written down and addressed to her. And *dear* means costly. Three meanings, all live, none given away in advance; it recolored on the last page rather than spoiling anything. **WITH A LONG SPOON** — sup with the devil and use a long spoon — keeps getting truer, and here Vee is drinking through a straw with no spoon at all.

I want the hike, and I'm afraid of it now in a way I wasn't at the end of chapter 10. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the invitation the cover promises is going to come from Randi, in writing, and Vee will say yes for the same reason she said "because of him."
