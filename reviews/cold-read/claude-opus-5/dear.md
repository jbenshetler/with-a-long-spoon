## Reader reaction

**Dear**

This one hurt in a way I wasn't braced for, because almost nothing bad happens in it. A girl comes home happy and tells her best friend. Then she tells somebody else.

Vee spinning in the dorm room — "a clumsy whole-body turn that nearly took out the closet door and was over before it was really a dance at all — the room too small to hold what she had to do with it" — is the best thing in the chapter and I'd defend it against anything. I loved her there. And I loved that the first thing she wanted Cassie to have was the consent of it: *Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.* That's the sentence the book put in her mouth, and the book knows what it's worth, and I know what's standing twenty feet behind it.

Cassie. God. Cassie asked the only question anyone has asked in this whole book: "How much did he give you to drink?" Flat, dry, unembarrassed, and *checking*. She is the one person in Vee's life running a safety protocol, and she runs it badly on purpose so it plays as a joke — the state troopers bit, "a man you met at a coffee shop" — and then the address comes out and it is *not* an address. "Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." "That's the whole address." Cassie says it; Vee laughs it off. That landed cold on me. Cassie also asks *is he rich* and Vee doesn't know, and files not-knowing in "the best pile there was." And then the second movement: Cassie's look going past Vee to Randi, lit up and reaching for her, and "by the time Cassie's eyes came back to Vee's, whatever had been in them was folded away." Then Vee cuts her out of the smoothie place with a hand-wave at the leggings. And thinks it's *healthy*. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." Cassie said *Goodbye, Vee* a few chapters ago and I have not stopped hearing it.

Randi I now watch the way you watch a hand at a card table. She snapped her fingers at his name. *"Paul?"* She told a long fluent lie about a Friday date — tall, dressed like money, candlelit French place, a summer in *Switzerland*, which is the country Vee invented for Randi's own accent — and the lie's moral was *a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else.* She built the frame and then Vee walked into it carrying the bread and the brass tea glasses. And the move that made my stomach drop wasn't a lie at all: Vee had "I made an exception" sitting right there, dignified, hers — the one line that is *about her own agency* — and Randi got in front of it. "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." And Vee said *…Yeah. Because of him.* She was handed a smaller version of her own best moment and took it as a gift. Twice, actually — "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." / "*…Yeah*" — and then Randi, warm, *let her have it, and went no further*, and Vee read the restraint as kindness. It's the apple-slice thing again from the other direction: someone noticing exactly how fast you'll eat it.

And then "ask and then ravish," a beat late, low, "the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That's the one place Randi's own want got out from under the face, and it's also the only moment in the chapter where the two of them were in the same room. It did something to me.

Heat sits mostly in Vee's body, not in anyone's hands: her hands running slow down the tops of her thighs, the picture of the trail coming back and her letting it, "He'd ask, though. Before he ravished me." Also the no-bra bit, which is genuinely funny and genuinely erotic in that way a woman's frankness about her own body in front of another woman is erotic — "I'd black my own eye," the bark of laughter, a head turning. Two girls on a shelf in a window laughing at Vee's chest in a room full of girls who got dressed in the same room. That's heat, and it's social terror, and it's the same thirty seconds.

The Brooke beat was superb and I did not need anything explained to me. Brooke smiles, the smile "take[s] her measure, top to bottom, and stay[s] warm the whole way," and sets down the fact of a Thursday Vee has no part of. Vee makes herself small — "we have stats together" — explicitly to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi. And Randi says *Vee's my dear friend* and takes her hand, cool and damp from the cup, in plain view, and doesn't hurry. I felt the warmth. I felt it as warmth even knowing what I know, which is the chapter's whole cruel trick. "No one had chosen her out loud like that in a long time."

So: **the title.** *Dear.* It's the word, obviously — but the second meaning arrived with it and won't leave: dear as in *costly*. The text all but says so: "it had cost something, that Randi had spent it on her without appearing to count it." Dear friend, dearly bought. And a third, fainter: *Dear —* as the top of a letter, the opening of something addressed to someone. Vee is being written to. She thinks she's being written to.

Friction: very little. The one place I felt the thumb was Vee's narration telling me outright that the *dear friend* had cost Randi something — I'd already felt it; being told dulled it half a notch. Otherwise I had no complaints and no boredom.

**Cover/volume:** WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION. I know the proverb's shape — *he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon* — and after this chapter the title is doing cold work: everyone here is eating from someone else's hand. The apple slices. The bread. The bruise-colored smoothie Randi ordered like a sentence handed down while Vee drank strawberry banana. And *A Polite Invitation* — that's "May I choose?", that's "see you later," that's "Vee's my dear friend." All of it asked beautifully. No tagline in my packet.

What I want: I want Cassie to say one more true thing and be heard. I dread the hike (guess from here: a house out past where the road goes to gravel, a long table with a top on it). And I notice I still *like* Pace, which is the most uncomfortable thing I'm carrying.
