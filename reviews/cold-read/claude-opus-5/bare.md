# Cold read (grounded) — Bare

*scene: scenes/bare.md · model: claude-opus-5 · memory: ck-ch040 + raw ch041..ch044 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Three pages and my chest hurts. This is the smallest chapter in a long while — one girl, one bunk, one shirt, no dialogue at all — and it's the one that finally says out loud what the last three have been circling. She's got the flannel back. He wore it all weekend because she asked him to, two shirts in his own warm kitchen, and that image made me melt at the time. Now it comes back *working exactly as designed* and it still doesn't do the job, and that's the cruelty: the fix wasn't broken, the thing it was fixing was.

The detail that got me hardest is the shirt worn over her sleep shirt. "Over her own shirt it was just a thing she wore. It hid what it was." She's put a layer between herself and the one object in her life that means *him*, so that Cassie won't have to see a girl too far gone in a boy. She's hiding it from the one person who'd never say anything. That's the same editing she's been doing since the fra diavolo — ladling the middle out of every story — except now it's down to the level of fabric against skin, and she's doing it lying six feet above her oldest friend in the dark. Cassie asleep and breathing easy under her, the window at its two inches, is the loneliest staging the book has given me.

Then the phone. She scrolls to his name, holds her thumb over the empty field, and finds there's *nothing that can be typed*. "There was no text that made it come clear; the ones she thought of were all small, and would be answered small and warm, and would leave her exactly here." That's the whole shape of this stretch of the book, stated cleanly and without anyone explaining it to her. It also rhymes, horribly, with the laundry-room verdict: the one person who could resolve it is the one person she can't ask. She's not stupid and she's not in denial — she has run the experiment, twice (the cracker sentence, the Chili's fight), and both times got warmth back with a hole in the middle. What's left is arithmetic.

And then the last move, which is genuinely superb and which I didn't see coming: she takes her own shirt off, puts his on bare, and the smell works — *and then it stops working, because she figures out the heat in it is hers.* "It had promised him close and there was no one close." That single sentence recolors months of motif for me. The flannel has been the book's most reliable warm object since the rain on the mountain, and here it gets exposed as a closed loop: she warms the cloth, the cloth gives it back, and she calls it him. I can't not read that as the verdict on the whole arrangement she can't see. Everything he does for her is real and it's *also* something she is doing to herself. Then she takes it off into the cold and stays in the cold, and leaves it in a heap, not folded, not back on the pillow — she who has rationed this thing for months. I made a small noise.

My feeling about Pace has moved, and this is the first time it's moved on *feeling* rather than on the structure I know and she doesn't. "Across town in his own bed, gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." He always sleeps. She never does. That asymmetry has been on the page since ch 21 and I never once read it as an indictment until this chapter put the two bodies side by side in one sentence. I still can't find a lie in him. But the line I keep chewing is from two chapters back — "I love how good we are together" — and the way she caught, weeks later, that the two sentences *were not the same shape*. He answered a statement about being fed with a statement about the pairing. I don't know whether that's a man who doesn't feel it, a man who can't say it, or a man who *cannot say it to her because of what he has with Randi* — and the chapter is scrupulous about not telling me, and so am I.

No suspicion earned here of anybody new; the ache is entirely Vee's and entirely legitimate. Erotic charge: basically nil, deliberately. There's a body bare in a cold room and it's the least erotic nakedness in the book — which is exactly right, and I'd be lying if I scored it otherwise. The chapter's whole point is that the smell of him, which has been reliably arousing for forty chapters, has gone inert. That's the loss registered in the one currency she trusts.

Friction: almost none. I braced slightly at "not the big one and not the small one," because that formulation lands verbatim from the end of the previous chapter, but as a reprise in a four-page coda I'll take it — it read as her mind grinding the same groove, not the author reminding me. The title **"Bare"** I first took as the obvious body-in-the-cold, and by the end it had turned: bare as in *stripped of the thing that was standing in for him*, bare as in the un-layered honesty she can only manage when nobody can see. Nothing given away in advance. As for the boards — *With a Long Spoon*, Book One, *A Polite Invitation* — the spoon keeps getting longer in my hands the further in I go; "polite" is precisely the weapon here, the unfailing courtesy that never once gives her the word. No tagline was supplied.

What I want next: I want Cassie to wake up. I want somebody in that room to be awake at the same time as Vee for once. And from here — a guess only — I dread that the next person she takes this to is Randi, because Randi is the one who'll know exactly what to say, and exactly what it's worth.

**Symbolism:** The shirt as a closed thermal loop — she heats it, it returns her heat, she reads it as presence. The book has been handing me his objects as proof of love for forty chapters; this is the first time one is weighed and found to contain only her. Also the layered shirt: a covering worn to make a covering deniable.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens hard — she's now the one dismantling her own comforts, clear-eyed, and choosing the cold over a lie that works. Cassie is present only as a sleeping body, which is characterization by absence and stings. Pace appears only as an inference (the easy sleeper), and the inference is doing more damage than any scene of his has.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Four beats, no fat: phone, shirt off, shirt on bare, shirt off for good. Earned every line of its short length; a longer version would have wrecked it.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building, and correctly quiet. Three chapters of escalating unnamed dread (the sentence, the fight, this) is a lot of the same beat in a row, but each one has gone a full step further inward and this is the one where the last prop gets kicked out. I'd now like the plot to move — Randi, Cassie, the outlier, anything external — because the interior line has run as far as it can go alone.
