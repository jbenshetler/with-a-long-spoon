## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Oh, this hurt. Beautifully. I spent the whole chapter with my stomach in a fist, because I was in Vee's head and Vee doesn't know anything. I watched her reconstruct the collision — "it had genuinely not been clear, one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it" — and I *do* know: I stood at the light pole with Pace and watched a decade of dance fold down into three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing. Vee has been handed the one interpretation Randi built for her, and she's holding it like a borrowed hour in a brighter life. Every warm thing in this chapter is a thing I've already seen from the outside, and the gap between the two views is the whole engine. I've never been so happy to be complicit and so sick about it.

And I *liked* Vee instantly, which makes it worse. She's the first person in this book who reads rooms out of hunger rather than command. The right pen. Writing the date at the top of a clean page "so that her hand would have somewhere to be" — God, that's a lonely, precise little sentence. Her standing outside the Chi Latte joke for two years and never sounding out the Chi is the most naked class detail the book has given me yet; the joke isn't funny until you're allowed in. She's not naive, exactly — she clocks that Randi has a single room and instantly reads the whole hierarchy off it — she's just *starving* in a way she's fluent enough to keep off her face for one beat before she says yes. "She made herself wait one full beat so it would not come out the size it actually was." I know that beat. I have done that beat.

My feeling about Randi moved, and not toward her. Two chapters ago I ached for her, locked in Pace's bathroom, the face going back on for good. Now I'm watching that face at full operating power and it is *terrifying*, because it's excellent. The warmth that swings over and lands on Cassie "total, undimmed." The bit about her name with its built-in door for you to walk through. And then — this is the line that put ice down my back — "***There* you are.**" That is Pace's sentence. That is what he said to her at the bottom of the bench when she broke open and cried on the leather, *there you are, Randi.* She has taken the single most naked moment of her life, the one she locked a door over and swore never to have again, and she is now using its words as a greeting. She's handing out counterfeit of the only real thing that ever happened to her. She isn't only recruiting Vee for Pace. She's doing to Vee what Brooke did to her in the common room — warmth as an instrument, the seated authority, the kindness you can't refuse — and what Pace did to her on the bench, which is find someone and be the first person who ever looked. She's learned it and she's spending it.

Trust: I don't distrust Randi, I *know* better, which is different and worse. But Cassie — Cassie is the one who made me sit up. Flat one-word name into the gap. Declining the coffee with no sulk in it. And then "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word. Vee's face "did the thing it did with wrongness, which was to smooth it," and *she didn't fix it. She let it stand.* I don't know what Cassie saw — the page holds it shut and I'm leaving it shut — but she saw something, and she said the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday. That's the chapter's cold spot and the text absolutely earned it.

Heat, for me, wasn't Randi at all — it was the floozies paragraph, and it's the best interior writing in the chapter. The word arrives "whole and in her mother's voice," and then, "riding under it where she didn't look," a longer beat of looking than the word accounts for: the sprinter's cut of a stomach, how low a waistband sits. Then the second voice moves into her mouth and closes the picture, and she keeps the cardigan on — the cardigan she put over the better shirt on purpose and let the room think was an accident. That's shame arriving as heat, and it's the first time in the book someone else's wanting has been rendered from inside with the same honesty Randi's was. The "flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance" — sitting there "warm, unexamined, glad" — is the sound of a girl who has no idea yet what she's feeling, and I felt it in my chest.

Friction: almost none, but the professor's "*latent variables* — the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can," with Vee underlining it once and letting it go nowhere, is the author's thumb waving at me. I smiled, and I also saw the hand. And a couple of narrations tidy what the prose already did: "she did not look at either fact straight" — I'd got there, I didn't need telling. As for titles: **See You Later** looked like Randi's warm promise, and it is — "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see" — but on second thought it recolors into Cassie's *Goodbye*, the farewell that got substituted out. It didn't give anything away going in; it curdled on the way out. **WITH A LONG SPOON** — sup with the devil — is now pointing straight at a yellow room full of spoons ringing against cup bottoms, everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom of their drinks. I don't think I was meant to miss that, and I didn't. **A POLITE INVITATION**: Vee has just been politely invited, and she thinks she's been chosen.

What I want: Vee's next chapter, immediately. What I dread: that the bench question — *do you think she'll like the bench* — gets answered, and that Vee walks toward it grateful.
