**Felt read**

This is the first time I've been inside the girl on the other end, and it hurt in a way I wasn't braced for. Vee is instantly, embarrassingly lovable — the third minute of not looking at the door, writing the date on a clean page "so that her hand would have somewhere to be," the way *the right pen* "warmed her like praise she'd worked for." She's not a mark in her own head; she's a girl who looked at Chi Latte through the window once and didn't go in because it felt like walking into someone's party uninvited. And I know what she doesn't. I know Randi came up that aisle because Pace pointed at her by the dining hall doors and Randi said *yes* before she could dress it as anything. I know the last thing Randi said about this girl was *do you think she'll like the bench?* So every warm beat landed double on me: the arm squeeze, the hug, the hand flat between her shoulder blades, *love you, girl* — all of it genuinely warm, I think, and all of it aimed. That's the sick brilliance of the chapter. I couldn't find the seam. Randi's warmth is real warmth being used, and I can't tell how much of that Randi herself knows.

My feeling about Randi moved, and not simply. I'd been given her from the inside — the face, the mirror, the key turning in the lock — and I had sympathy for her. From out here she is *terrifyingly* good. The entrance she chose: "*There* you are." The exact three words Pace used on the bench, the words that cracked her open and that she went into the bathroom and locked the door against — and here she is spending them on a stranger in a lecture hall, warm and free, as currency. I don't think she clocked that. But I did, and it went through me cold. The woman who decided *he can have the face* has taken the face out to work.

The thing I keep turning over is Vee's own looking. The two girls in sports bras get the mother's word, *floozies*, arriving "whole and in her mother's voice before she had finished forming the picture" — and then, "riding under it where she didn't look, a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for," the sprinter's cut of a stomach, how low a waistband sat. Then Randi, and "all Vee could do was look at her with a flat helpless want." Vee doesn't name it. The page doesn't name it for her either, and I'm grateful — it just leaves the cardigan buttoned over the better shirt and lets me sit with how much that costs her. Two women in this book now with something they won't look at squarely, and one of them is walking the other toward it on purpose.

Cassie is the most interesting new thing on the page. Four words, flat *Cassie*, declining the bit's open door, declining the coffee with no sulk in it — "just a person declining a thing that wasn't hers in a voice with nothing in it but the fact." And then "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word. The page tells me it caught, that Vee's face "did the thing it did with wrongness, which was to smooth it," and that she laughed it off — *it's coffee, weirdo.* I don't know what Cassie saw. The text earned my unease there without explaining it, which is exactly how I want to be unsettled. Something passed over her face "that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to," and I can't name it either. I'm holding it open.

The swoon is real and it's not the sex kind. It's the being-chosen kind, and I was defenseless against it: the sign finally resolving — **Χ ΛΑΤΕ** — "she had stood outside this exact joke," and now she's walking under it with the door held. The high table she'd thought was "where you sit if you belong here." The hour whose transcript is blank because "she had not been keeping the words. She had been keeping the feeling." That's the best paragraph in the chapter and it ached. Erotically the chapter simmers rather than burns — the charge lives in the helpless looking and in Vee's body suddenly "presented at the height the whole room could see" — but after two chapters of sustained heat, a chapter that runs on social hunger instead felt like the right trade, not a drop.

Friction: very little. The one place the thumb showed slightly was *latent variables* — the professor's last line, written down, "underlined once, going nowhere." It's a pretty wink and I noticed it as a wink. I also mildly wanted one more hard edge from Vee, something less sweet, so she isn't purely a lamb; the mother's voice is doing that work, but it's the only sharp thing in her.

The title did its job twice. Going in, "See You Later" was nothing. Coming out, it's Randi's last line — "not *let's do this again,* which was a thing people said and didn't" — "a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see." It *is* already scheduled. I've read the calendar. That's the knife. And against Cassie's *goodbye*, the two partings sit side by side: the one who means a later and shouldn't, the one who meant goodbye and might. The volume board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — finally rang: literal spoons, "all over the room, an irregular ringing, everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom of their drinks." Sup with the devil, use a long spoon. Vee has no spoon at all. And the polite invitation is *come to Chi Latte with me... I'm not asking, I've decided.* No tagline was supplied.

What I want next: Vee and Randi again, and I dread it. Guess from here: Pace will meet her, and I think he'll be kind, and that's what will make it unbearable. I also want to know what Cassie knows.
