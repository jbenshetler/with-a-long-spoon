# Cold read (grounded) — The Long Way

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Short chapter, and it got me where I was most defenseless. The whole first half is three friends at a table deciding whether to answer a text, and I was grinning — Vee reaching for the flaw ("Is he desperate? Texting this fast?") because sitting in the wanting is unbearable, Randi taking the phone right out of her hand, Cassie not looking up from her coffee. "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again." I laughed out loud. That one line did more for Cassie than anything since she said *Goodbye, Vee* — a whole semester of Vee up till two keeping a business major alive in a video game, remembered fondly and dryly by the person who watched her do it. Cassie loves her. Cassie is also the only one at that table not running an angle, and I notice I've started reading her as the chapter's weather vane.

And then the thing that made my stomach drop harder than Vee's did: Randi looks at the phone and says *He looked like a man who wants you.* **She doesn't say he's hers.** That omission sat on the page like a held breath. I know what Vee doesn't — that Pace pointed on the quad, that Randi said *yes* before she could dress it as a gift, that Vee is the chosen one and does not know she's been chosen *for* anything. So "The rule's for boys you're not sure about. You're not unsure" lands as kindness and as a push, both, and I couldn't tell you which Randi believes she's doing. The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar — her hands around that coffee cup in the last chapter, the sips that didn't happen, she's not simply generous and she may not know what she is.

Then the froyo shop, which I did not expect to be the tenderest scene in the book so far. Dusty plastic ivy, lemon cleaner under the sugar, machines humming — unglamorous on purpose, and that's why it worked. "Tart on the bottom holds up under the heavy ones. You put chocolate on tart and it's an argument the whole way down." *I've thought about most things,* said without apology. That's the same man who built a bench and sliced an apple thin because she'd eat it that way — the pleasure of the engineered small kindness — and here it's deployed on froyo and it's charming instead of ominous, which is exactly what unnerves me.

But the beat that actually got me, the melt, was the cup. She builds it without thinking and it gets away from her, leaning, too much — and *usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing. Then: "She bit her lower lip, knowing the cup would drip, and then found she didn't care." After a whole chapter of Randi slicing herself down to apple-thin portions and another of Vee keeping the cardigan buttoned over the better shirt because of a voice in her mother's mouth that's moved into hers — a girl overfilling a cup and not caring is a *huge* event, rendered as nothing. I felt it in my chest. And "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo." That's naked, and she knows it's naked, and she tries to take it back — *See. Pretend* — and he doesn't pretend and doesn't inflate it either, he just lets the true thing have room and then says "Your tart's holding up." I swooned. Fully. I also wanted to put my hand through the page, because she's being held this carefully by a man who went looking for her on behalf of another woman, and the care is *real*, that's the problem. He asked her things "the way her grandfather used to ask things." She wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on. That's the cruelest sentence in the chapter and nobody in it means it cruelly.

Trust: I don't distrust Pace's *feeling* — the page keeps showing me a man genuinely delighted by whoever he's with — I distrust the frame he's set her inside, and that he thinks a body's answer is the true one. Randi I now watch sideways. Vee I just want to protect, which is a bad sign for me.

Erotic charge is low and I don't hold it against the chapter — it's a date, not a bed. The only real heat is secondhand: Randi grinning, "He wants to see you naked," and Vee's flat helpless want from the last chapter still echoing. Friction: almost none, though "It was easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting" is the one place the narration explained a thing I'd already felt from the dialogue. Let me have it.

**The titles.** "The Long Way" — they "walked the long way, not toward anything," and Vee went home the long way after Chi Latte, warm the whole way; it's her pleasure now, the unhurried route. And it's Pace's creed from the bench: *I'm not stopping. I'm taking my time.* The long way is the method. It's also, read cold, the shape of the thing being done to her: nobody is going at her directly. The title didn't give anything away, it recolored on the last line. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — "he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon," and this chapter literally has her grabbing spoons and a fistful of napkins and eating out of a cup he paid for. *A Polite Invitation* is *May I call you?* and *whether she liked frozen yogurt*. The politeness is the invitation; I've been told what the courtesy is for, and I'm going anyway. No tagline in my packet.

What I want: more Vee, and Cassie in the room when the truth lands. What I dread (guess from here): that Vee finds out what the quad was, and that the discovery costs her the one hour she's had in months that felt like being chosen.
