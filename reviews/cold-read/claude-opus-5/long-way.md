# Cold read (grounded) — The Long Way

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Short chapter, and it worked on me almost entirely because of what I know that Vee doesn't. On the page: a girl gets a text, her friends tell her to go, she goes, it's lovely. Underneath: I watched Randi take the phone *out of her hand* — "Randi took the phone out of her hand to see for herself" — and say, grinning, "He wants you. He wants to see you naked," and I felt the floor tilt. Three chapters ago Randi stopped mid-stride on a brick walk and said *Yes* with nothing around it. This is that yes, being handed off through a boyfriend, dressed as a girlfriend cheering on a crush. Randi's "Why would you do that?" when Vee floats waiting a day — that's not friendly counsel, that's impatience. She wants this moving. And Vee reads the whole thing as luck.

I am, against my better judgment, swooning at the froyo. That's the trick of the chapter and it got me. The tart-on-the-bottom system, delivered with the same gravity he gave her calculus; "I've thought about most things," said without apology and then the *contained* pleasure, "as if he'd set something down exactly where he meant it to go." And then the beat I actually felt in my chest: "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo." Followed by him not pretending, not inflating it, just walking beside her and then — "Your tart's holding up." That is the best line in the chapter and possibly the kindest thing anyone has done in this book. It let her be sincere without paying for it. I melted, and I'm aware I melted, and I don't want to take it back just because I know how he found her.

The cup is what undid me, though. "She built her cup without thinking, and it got away from her — too much, the cone of it leaning. Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." Set that against Randi on Pace's lap with thin honeycrisp slices, apples being *permitted*, the calculation running behind her face — and against Randi on the quad: "No. I can't eat that." Two women with a portioning voice in their heads; one of them just had the voice go quiet for an evening, and bit her lip and didn't care about the drip. That's an enormous amount of feeling loaded into a plastic cup, and it never once announces itself. Also: they carry *spoons*. In a book called *With a Long Spoon*. I noticed and I did not enjoy noticing.

Cassie is the only person in this book currently not enchanted, and I trust her more with every appearance. The healer line — "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again," a whole semester of Vee up till two keeping a business major alive — is the first real history we've been given for the two of them, and it's exactly the shape of Vee: someone who will pour herself into keeping another person going and call it fun. Cassie says it flat, without moralizing, and doesn't look up. She's the one who flagged how Pace looked at Vee "like he'd forgotten there was anyone else in the room," and looked at Randi like a great-aunt. She said *Goodbye, Vee* in a lecture hall. I don't know what she sees. I want her in more rooms.

Suspicion, honestly: my unease isn't about Pace's cruelty, it's about his *certainty*. He has now been right about everything — the integral, the pencil, the lights, the bench, the maple, the apple slices, the tart under the chocolate. A man who is never wrong is a lovely thing to be dated by and a frightening thing to be selected by. And the selection is the part he's keeping from her: the whole evening is real and warm and she does not know she was pointed at. He didn't lie to her. He also hasn't told her, and the pleasure he took at the light pole watching Randi collide with her is not something this froyo shop knows about. So: I have no suspicion of his *feeling*. I have a lot of suspicion of the arrangement. With Randi I'm past suspicion — I saw her face in the mirror decide *not ever*, and this chapter is her first move with the face back on.

Erotic charge is low and I don't mind; the chapter isn't for that. The closest it comes is secondhand — Randi's "he wants to see you naked" landing on a girl who's been keeping her cardigan buttoned over the better shirt on purpose. Knowing what Vee said to herself in the Chi Latte mirror — *she had more than they had, and better* — that line is a door being opened a half inch. Nothing in the froyo shop is hot. Something adjacent to it is.

The title: **"The Long Way."** It recurs — Vee "went home the long way, in no hurry at all" after Chi Latte, and here "they walked the long way, not toward anything." It's her signature: the happy unhurried detour of someone with no one waiting for her. But it's also *his* — "I'm not stopping. I'm taking my time," on the bench. The same words mean her joy and his method, and the chapter lets them sit on top of each other without saying so. That's the best kind of title: not a spoiler, a double exposure. The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — I now read as the proverb about supping with the devil, and this chapter hands us actual spoons and an actual polite invitation (*May I call you?* / *do you like frozen yogurt?*). No tagline was supplied. What it promises me from here: that the courtesy is real *and* the long spoon is necessary, both. What I want next is for Randi to make her move on Vee directly, and what I dread is that Vee will be so grateful to be chosen that she'll say yes to anything either of them offers.
