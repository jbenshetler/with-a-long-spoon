## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The first half is just a pleasure. I came out of the measuring chapter wrung out, and this hands me Vee on Cassie's bed with a pillow against her chest, grinning at the underside of the bunk. Cassie is magnificent here — the highlighter going down, the knees pulling up, "He *sews*," and then the slow walk back to the shirt: "You wore it to breakfast on Tuesday." She lets Vee pivot when Vee needs to pivot ("Cassie let the pivot happen. She was good at that."), and she asks the one question that matters — *Did he ask?* — and gets the true answer: "He didn't use the word. But yes." That exchange is Cassie doing, in one beat, the thing I've been doing nervously for ten chapters. And then she caps the whole house of cards with "Vee. Are you sure he's not gay?" and I laughed out loud, and then Vee patting her own ass like a witness indicating a diagram, and then the reveal that *she* was the one grinding. The chapter's funniest and warmest minute is also Vee finally being able to say the shameful thing to someone and have it be a joke they own together. "Like I was supposed to be there." Cassie's hand on her knee, once, taken back. That restraint killed me.

Then the Mercedes, and I went cold by degrees, and I want to be precise about why, because it wasn't dread arriving — it was recognition. Randi told a story about being tied to a headboard with a scarf, laughing until she cried, helpless, and then "I went off like a *rocket*." That's the bench. That's the bench with a beautiful idiot painted over it. And when Vee asks, small, "Weren't you scared, though?" Randi says the truest thing she's said in twelve chapters — "You spend your whole life holding the reins so tight your hands cramp... it turns out that's the only time some of us ever put them down" — and then "the smile came back and closed the thing up." She can only tell the truth in costume. I was moved and appalled in the same breath.

And then she goes to work, and the craft of it is horrible. "Nobody measures over a bra... it's not a *thing*, it's just how it's done" — said with the easy authority of money, so the shame releases and Vee hands it over. Then the compliment that converts shame to pleasure ("The tit goddess was good to you"). Then "*challenges*," with its little hook. Vee's panicked "It was cold in the house" and Randi's flat "Cold. Hmm." I actually flinched for her. Then the laughs going up together, "Vee's first, raucous... and Randi's a half-beat behind" — that half-beat again, the face arriving late. And when Vee is finally all the way open, the thing she has told no one rising on its own because "the room was so safe" — "You don't have to tell me." Gentle. Kind. "A door closing very softly." That's the cruelest beat in the book so far and nobody raised their voice.

I don't think it was only tactics. Randi's thumb went under her nail on the rim of the water glass while Vee said *he looked at me and I was fine* — the bottle cap again, the hurting-herself-where-no-one-sees. She is jealous, and feeding, and in pain, all at once, and she still spends Vee's secret for her own hour. The thing that gutted me most is Vee catching the tightness, reading it first as *she thinks I'm awful*, then choosing the kinder reading because she wanted it to be true — "she was sitting across from someone glad for her." That's the whole mechanism of this book in one paragraph and Vee walks into it with her hands open.

The kiss worked on me and I didn't want it to, which is exactly right. "Not the dry tight press Vee gave her grandmother. Soft, and in no hurry, and then — just as Vee thought it was ending — not ending." The small drawing-back and the second press with something in it. Then Vee at the curb, two fingers at her own mouth before she decided to, filing it as *French, that's just how women like her say goodbye* — and then the cold turn: scanning the dark dorm windows, counting the ones where someone could have been standing. The shame arrives before the wanting does, and the wanting is going to arrive anyway. Heat-wise, the chapter peaks not in the kiss but in the picture of Randi tied up and laughing, and the heat Vee "had no shelf for and would have died before naming at this table." She's looking right at it now without seeing it.

Friction: very little. The one place I felt a thumb was the paragraph spelling out Vee's charitable re-read ("Maybe Randi was *moved*... That had to be it") — I'd already felt it from the thumbnail and the tight smile, and the narration told me twice. Everything else is money: the right-hand column of the menu read "the way you read a road in fog," the scuffed toe tucked under the booth, Vee putting her own card down because the one thing she won't do is let Randi buy her seat at the table. And Vee's good joke — "overdue reserve" — earning "God, I love you," which costs Randi nothing.

The title: before I read it, "How It's Done" meant nothing — maybe Pace's joinery, his codes, patience and paying attention. After, it's Randi's lie, verbatim, the sentence that unlocks Vee. It recolors the whole first half too: this book is full of people with methods, and two of them are being practiced on the same girl. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** keeps getting more literal — sup with the devil and use a long spoon; the invitation is always polite, always "may I," and the politeness is the delivery system. I don't know yet which of them is the devil, or whether that's the wrong question. No tagline was supplied beyond the cover line.

What I want: the dress finished, and Vee in it with her name read at the front of a room. What I dread: Randi at that induction dinner. And Pace standing between two women who have each handed him something they kept from the other.
