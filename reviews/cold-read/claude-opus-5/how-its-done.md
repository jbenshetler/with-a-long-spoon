# Cold read (grounded) — How It's Done

*scene: scenes/how-its-done.md · model: claude-opus-5 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch016 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**The Cassie half first, because it's the one that made me happy.** Vee can't sit down — bag on the floor, bag on the chair, up, window, back — and Cassie puts the highlighter down, which is Cassie's whole vocabulary of love. "He *sews*," set down somewhere like a fact she'll need later, and then the pounce: "You wore it to breakfast on Tuesday." I laughed out loud at the gay interrogation and at Vee indicating her own ass "brisk and clinical, like she was indicating it on a diagram," and then at the actual confession — "*I* was grinding on *him*" — which is the most unguarded Vee has ever been with anyone. But what got me was the quiet in the middle: "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*," and then "Like I was supposed to be there," and Cassie's hand on her knee, once, and taken back. *Once, and taken back.* Cassie keeps putting her hand down and picking it up again, and I don't think Vee has ever noticed the picking-up.

**And then Randi, and I felt the chapter change temperature under me.** That lunch is the most frightened I've been in this book, and nothing happens in it. It's a conversation. Randi warms the booth up, hands Vee a scarf-and-tickling story that sounds like another Friday-at-the-French-place — the third one now, and I no longer believe any of them — and then goes to work with the gentlest tool in the world: *it's not a thing, it's just how it's done.* That's the move. She doesn't ask Vee to confess; she tells her there's nothing to confess, and the shame drops its guard, and Vee hands over the bra. Then "were there any *challenges*," with its little hook in it, and the "it was cold in the house" that gives everything away, and the laugh of the absolutely cornered. I was sick for Vee and I could not look away. I also couldn't stop noticing the thumb — the rim of the water glass set in under the nail, bearing down — which is the bottle cap from the bench night, and it meant Randi was in pain the entire time she was so warm. Vee looks right at it and decides it's an older friend being *moved* for her. She reaches for the kinder reading because she wants it true. God.

**Then Randi shuts the door herself, and I don't understand it, and I love that I don't.** Vee is one breath from giving up the thing she has told nobody — the real thing, the soaked satin, the deepest-folded secret — and Randi says *you don't have to tell me.* Gentle. Kind. And the hush ends, and she looks for the check. I genuinely cannot tell whether she stopped because she couldn't bear to hear it, or because jealousy came up her throat, or because she'd already got what she came for and taking more would have cost her the girl, or because — maybe — some part of her refused to take that one. The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar. It's the most interesting thing she's done since the mirror.

**Swoon, where it landed.** The warm house arriving secondhand at the lunch table — "he turned it up hours before, probably" — hit me harder in the retelling than it did live. And then there's the thing Randi says about being tied: "You spend your whole life holding the reins so tight your hands cramp. And then somebody takes them, and you *can't* hold them even if you wanted to, and it turns out that's the only time some of us ever put them down." That is the bench. She's telling the truth about the most naked night of her life inside a story she made up, to a girl she's grooming, and I felt a real ache for her in the middle of being afraid of her. The car with the top down and the song and both of them singing swept me up completely and I let it — Vee catching the couple in the crosswalk and not minding being looked at, not one bit.

**The kiss.** Soft, unhurried, and then the *second press* — the drawing back and coming back with more. That broke my heart a little, because the first press could have been French and the second one could not, and Vee has it explained away before her mouth is her own again. *That's just how women like her say goodbye.* And then the cold turn: scanning the courtyard, counting dark windows for who might have seen. That's the first time Vee has had something to hide. The flannel on the pillow upstairs and Randi's green smell in her hair at the same time.

**Friction, small.** Almost none. The one place I felt the author's elbow was Vee's interior reach-around to the kind reading — "Maybe Randi was *moved*, a little, holding back a smile" — which spells out an inference I'd already made from the thumb on the glass. And "all the best ones start at the left hip," which meant nothing at all and landed like a benediction" explains its own trick a half-beat too helpfully. I'd have believed it unlabeled.

**Titles.** "How It's Done" is Randi's line lifted straight out — *it's not a thing, it's just how it's done* — and it's a picklock: the sentence that makes a shame ordinary so it can be handed over. On the second thought it's also the book's method, and maybe Pace's: everything in this story is done *correctly*, courteously, by people who know how. The cover line — WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION — has stopped being decorative. You sup with the devil with a long spoon; nobody in that booth has one. And "a polite invitation" is now at least three things: Pace's two-o'clock text with no other word in it, *may I look*, and *tell me everything, don't you dare clean it up*.

**What I want next:** for Vee to keep the dress and keep Cassie. What I dread: that the next thing Randi draws out of her is the one she stopped short of, and that she'll come back for it. Guess from here, marked as a guess: Randi didn't report this lunch to Pace, and that's the first thing she's kept.
