# Cold read (grounded) — How It's Done

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

That lunch made me sick with admiration. I mean it as a compliment to the book and as an accusation against Randi. This is the first chapter where I watched her *work* at full extension, and it is a beautiful piece of machining. The car door glass that drops half an inch and seals itself, the booth with three velvet walls that eats sound — "You could have said anything in that booth" — the Sinatra, the *witchcraft* song humming in the wallpaper. She built a confessional and drove Vee into it with the top down.

And the crowbar was so kind. That's what undid me. Vee balks at saying *bra*, the words piling up "vague and quick around the shape of the thing she wasn't saying," and Randi hands her the absolution: "Nobody measures over a bra… it's not a *thing*, it's just how it's done." Delivered "the way you'd tell someone the salad fork goes on the outside." I felt Vee's chest unclench through the page and I wanted to shout at her. Then the escalation, one *yeah* at a time — the hip, *which hip*, "all the best ones start at the left hip," which the book flatly tells us "meant nothing at all and landed like a benediction." Randi knows exactly what a fitting is like from the inside. She doesn't need Vee's information. She needs Vee's *saying* of it. And then, at the exact instant Vee finally opens her mouth to give up the one thing she's told nobody, not even Cassie — "You don't have to tell me." Gentle. "A door closing very softly." That's the cruelest line in the book so far, and it's made entirely of kindness. She took the confession out of Vee's mouth and left the wound open, and then reached up and tucked her hair back and said "Big day."

Did anything move in her, though? Twice, and I can't close either. When Vee says he'd warmed the house for her, and *I wasn't even embarrassed, he looked at me and I was fine* — Randi goes still, the smile holding over "some muscle she was holding," her thumb working the rim of the glass. That's the bottle cap under the thumbnail again, and it arrived at the precise sentence where Vee describes being given what Randi decided in a bathroom mirror she could never survive. Whether that's jealousy of Vee, or of Pace, or grief at watching her own best night happen to someone else in daylight, I genuinely can't tell, and I love that the page doesn't tell me. And the other: "That's the whole point, baby. The letting go… you *can't* hold them even if you wanted to, and it turns out that's the only time some of us ever put them down." *Some of us.* That's the bench talking, wearing a scarf and a beautiful idiot as a costume. I don't believe there was a Saturday man. I believe she took the truest night of her life, stripped Pace out of it, put a stranger's hands on it, and used it as bait — and it worked, and it also slipped, right there in that sentence.

The heat landed. The tickling story got me the way it got Vee — not the man, "the man was nobody," but *Randi* bound and laughing and cracked open, and the book is honest that the picture "put low in Vee" a heat she "had no shelf for and would have died before naming at this table." Vee is falling and has no vocabulary to fall with. The kiss at the curb is the chapter's best-executed shiver: the cheek-thing that isn't, the hand at the hinge of her jaw, and then "a small drawing-back, the barest pause, and then back again with a little more." The second press is the whole book. And then Vee's reflex — *French, it's a French thing, that's just how women like her say goodbye* — "explained away almost before her mouth was her own again," followed instantly by the cold scan of the dark dorm windows. That scan is her body knowing what her mouth won't say. I ached.

What I don't feel is any pull to root for this pairing, and I notice the difference. With Pace on the riser last chapter I was swooning outright; here I was leaning forward with my stomach cold. The tenderness is real — "God, I love you," "the tit goddess was good to you," the way Randi makes Vee's lifelong shame into luck — and it is all load-bearing for an extraction. Both true at once, again, which is this book's whole trick and I'm tired of being good at it.

The money details were quietly devastating and I didn't see them coming: Vee reading the right side of the menu first, "the way you read a road in fog, looking for what was passable"; the scuff on the pump she rubbed at that morning; one glass of Rombauer costing more than her entire order; the debit card and "the number that would be smaller tonight in a way she would feel on Thursday." And she pays anyway, because "the one thing she would not do at this table was let Randi buy her seat at it." Randi watched all of it and didn't reach again. That's dignity Vee is buying at a price, and Randi let her — filed it, I think.

Friction: almost none, which is itself a little uncanny. The one place I felt the author's hand was Vee's interior weighing of Randi's tight smile — *she thinks I'm awful* → maybe she's *moved* → "That was the look" — a beat more explicit than it needed to be, though I'll grant that Vee talking herself into the kinder reading is exactly her disease. The rearview-song montage ran a touch generic ("one of those songs… the first four notes enough") but it earned the release.

**The titles.** "How It's Done" is the sharpest chapter title yet, and it gave nothing away going in — it only bared its teeth on the way out. It's the exact phrase Randi uses as the skeleton key ("it's just how it's done"), it's the etiquette Vee has never been taught, and it's the chapter's own demonstration: *this* is how you do it to someone. Three meanings stacked, all cruel. **WITH A LONG SPOON** I've been holding since the cover — sup with the devil and use a long spoon — and today Vee ate from the same booth with no spoon at all. **A POLITE INVITATION**: everything in this book arrives as a courtesy. *May I choose.* *Get in, are you hungry.* *You don't have to tell me.*

I want the next chapter badly and I dread Cassie's face when Vee doesn't tell her about the kiss.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Randi. Also present, unnamed and non-speaking: the host, the waiter, two girls on the Hahn steps, a couple in the crosswalk. **Mentioned only:** Pace, Cassie, Brooke, Vee's father, the "beautiful idiot" of Randi's story (unverified, possibly invented).

**Heat:** 2 — the booth ran hot the whole way: the tickling story landing "low in Vee," the wet-satin confession dragged into the light, the second press of that kiss.

**Romance:** 2 — a real turn in the bond; the kiss is tender and Vee is warmed all the way through, even as I can see the hand on the lever.

**Motifs & images:** *Being fed / plenty* — "here is plenty" before you read the menu (repeat: "Let's go feed you," the honeycrisp, the bread on date one). *Portioned food & money* — Randi reads only the left side of the menu; Vee reads the right (new, and brutal). *The thumb worrying an edge* — the water glass rim (repeat: bottle cap under the thumbnail, ch 1; the curled problem-set corner, ch 5). *The face* — the tightness "folded away somewhere," the smile "gentler, the performance gone out of it" (repeat). *Cold citrus / green perfume* — "something bright and green" in Vee's hair (repeat: the hugs, ch 4, ch 10). *Bound wrists / helplessness* — "a little helpless can be a very good thing" (repeat of the bench, in disguise). *Fire lane* — Randi parks in it, "like fire lanes were a thing that happened to other people" (repeat: Pace in it, ch 11, and a cop came for him). *Cream cashmere* (repeat, ch 1 — folded onto a chair in his bedroom). *Doors* — the door swung open, the door "closing very softly" (repeat). *The safe black dress* (repeat), worn here. *Being looked at* — the girls on the steps, the crosswalk couple, the dark dorm windows. First sighting: the plain heavy white-gold band Randi never removes.

**Symbolism:** The white-gold band — the one fixed thing in a wardrobe that changes daily, and nobody has named it. The self-sealing car window: a thing that closes around you, silently, on its own. The green velvet booth — soundproofing sold as luxury. "How it's done" as etiquette weaponized against a girl who was never taught any.

**Characterization:** Randi deepened enormously — technique, appetite, and a hairline fracture, all in one scene, and the book still won't tell me which is driving. Vee deepened painfully: the class shame is now as sharp as the body shame, and her habit of choosing the kinder reading is now visibly a vulnerability, not just a sweetness. Nobody thinned.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Excellent. The drive breathes, the extraction escalates without a wasted line, "Big day" cuts it off at exactly the right instant, and the curb kiss is two sentences long and does everything. The song montage is the only slack.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building hard, and smartly varied — after two chapters of Pace's warm slow hands and two rounds of Cassie-debrief, this swerves into a different room with a different predator and pays off ten chapters of groundwork. Best chapter since the fitting, and possibly since the bench.
