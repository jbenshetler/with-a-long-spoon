## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

The chapter had me before she got out of the shower, and it kept me, and I'm faintly annoyed about how completely it worked — which I think is the correct annoyance to have here.

The mirror section is the best solo scene in this book so far and it isn't close. What got me wasn't the touching, it was the *sequence of the drawer*: her hand going to the black everyday bra out of habit, correcting to the good lace because "Pace deserves the pretty one," and only then catching up to the fact that Pace is getting neither. That's a woman whose body has more manners than her plan does. Then the shoes: "last thing, only thing," and the real giggle alone in the room. The frosted plum she kept touched up over Christmas *for a man she'd no longer been sure would ever see them again* — that's the whole ache of the break in one lacquered toenail. And then she edges herself in front of the glass and stops, on purpose, because "it was not hers to finish." She wanted him to be the one. I felt that decision in my stomach. Her mother's word coming up "and finding nothing to hold to" — after fifty chapters of *floozie* arriving as heat, the word finally arriving and failing to land is a genuine payoff, and the chapter didn't underline it.

The drive is where the heat peaked for me — bare on a cold seat, the satin lining keeping the ache lit, hand off the wheel on an empty county road, and then the cruiser. The siren callback (the campus cop's single bark in the truck, back in November) landing as a cop who doesn't even want her, who blows past her toward something else entirely — that's funny and cruel and it wound her tighter. The stain bloomed into the good coat, the church coat, and her *not* being ashamed of it: "it only told her she meant it."

The doorstep undid me. The formal voice — "It's late. Is everything alright?" — the same wall from December, and her answer: she says nothing at all and opens the coat. "She had brought something the wall could not refuse." And it doesn't. He looks at her "like a starving man, and… like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look." I've been waiting since ch 50 to see his grief land on her, and it lands without either of them naming it. The sex is graceless and fast and I bought every step of the three hobbling paces toward a bed neither of them was going to reach. The afterward is where it turned romantic on me: him on the floor with his head on her breasts, mouthing her gently just to keep her lit, asking for nothing, and then "You sure know how to make an entrance. Vee." Her own name — the same name he said as she walked out in December, the one that wasn't enough then — and this time she laughs and then cries. Then pancakes at midnight, made not poured, and a grown man going down on one knee with his voice cracking: "I missed you so much." I melted. Fully. "Tonight there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given."

And underneath all of it, the cold thing I can't put down: she is wearing **Randi's shoes**. Randi wrote this night — the coat, the nothing, the timing, the footwear — and shipped the woman she loves out a county road to the man she's still sleeping with, and did it while holding her too long in a hug and pulling a breath off her neck. "Those stay on," he says. The page doesn't tell me whether he knows whose they are. I'm leaving that ajar, and it's the most interesting locked door in the chapter. The dramatic irony runs hot too — Vee's imaginary blonde is *Sheri*, built entirely out of fear, and Randi kindly dismantles her while being the actual other woman. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." She's safe advising that; she knows exactly what the real one's face looks like.

Friction: almost none. The one place I felt a thumb was the doorstep interiority — "For once she was not the one being shown; she was the one showing" — true, earned, but stated a beat more plainly than the scene needed. And I'll admit the oral-on-the-porch beat asked me to forget how cold January is on bare knees; I forgave it because the wet-nipples-in-the-air detail is so specific it bought back the realism it spent.

The chapter title gave the coat away before I read a word — I knew the whole plan from "Nothing Underneath" and from Randi's dangling sentence in the previous chapter, so there was no reveal, only execution. But it recolors afterward: *nothing underneath* is also the question the book keeps refusing to answer about him. He fed her, knelt, cracked his voice — and still not one word she can carry outside that house. Tonight she chose not to need it. That's not the same as it being there. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — I know the proverb about supping with the devil, and this is the first chapter where I felt the spoon: a polite invitation extended by Randi, accepted by Vee, with the devil unspecified. No tagline or jacket copy came with my packet.

What I want: I want them to have this and I know they can't keep it. Guess from here — the reconciliation holds for a while, Randi keeps brunching, and the shoes come back to the sorority house with a story in them.
