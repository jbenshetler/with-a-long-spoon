## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

That lecture landed on me like a hand on the back of my neck. "The first kind sits on a single point… you lift it out, and the line goes on like it was never there." Then the second: "It comes in through the part that carries forward. It's in everything that comes after." I don't think Vee heard a word of it — she copied the word down without reading it, gave the board "another honest minute," and went back to the kitchen in her head. I heard all of it, and I sat there with the cold feeling of a book telling me plainly which kind of thing has already happened, while the girl it happened to is busy noticing a knee against her knee.

The reversal got me before anything else did. For twenty chapters Randi has done the collecting — the hand at the elbow, *come here a second*, the cutting-out-of-the-herd. This time it's Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist, *walk me out?*, "To Randi. Only to Randi," and Cassie saying *see you in the room* **to the side of Vee's head.** That detail is small and it's the cruelest thing in the chapter. Cassie saved the seat with her backpack and lifted it away, and got a profile for her trouble. I'm sad in a way I can't do anything with.

And then the practice room, which is the best-built scene in the book for pure charge. Soundproof — "Not muffled. Gone. The kind of quiet you could hear your own blood in." Vee's own half-said *those are where everybody used to…*, and Randi's flat "I do know," which is the single most loaded line she's ever given. Randi doesn't touch her once. She sits in a chair and asks questions, and Vee's body does everything: the voice dropping into something "she didn't entirely recognize," the arms crossing over nipples that have gone tight from a memory, the hot slow drop of realizing *she wasn't only telling it. She was doing it again. Here. Now.* That's a 3 for me without a hand being laid on anyone. The bathroom is where it peaked and then turned to ache — "Her hand knew the way. It moved before she did," and she pinned it flat to her knee, because "to do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." Oh, Vee. You already did; you're just negotiating about where.

Randi is where my trust goes to die and my sympathy keeps living anyway. The questions are surgical: *A game of slap-and-tickle* → *did you deserve it?* → and when Vee laughs out "probably I deserved it," **"Randi's smile brightened."** Then *his hand was wet* — she reconstructed the physical fact from a detail Vee didn't offer, and put Vee back in the kitchen with it. And then the mouse-that-roars story, the counting out loud, the skirt up, the hand that "would slip," landing on *welcome* exactly when Vee's body gave out. I don't know if a word of it is true. Her stories have never been verifiable and this one arrives shaped precisely to the hole Vee just showed her. But the thing that keeps me off the easy read is Randi's own stillness — "the way a cat goes still" — and the way she let it go afterward "as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And Vee couldn't tell whether she had." Neither could I, and I loved that the page refused to tell me.

Pace isn't here and he's all over it. The *leave them on* speech got reported secondhand and still worked on me — "I'm not going to insult the loan by complaining I have to look up." What snagged me is that Vee handed Randi the whole thing except the middle: "she heard herself skid past the middle of it, the part she had no words arranged for." She's editing for Randi now too, the way she edits for Cassie — and Randi is the one person who won't let the edit stand. My only friction was tiny: the professor's outlier speech is doing a lot of pointing, and "It's in everything that comes after" is about a half-inch past what the scene needed. I forgave it because the whole thing is delivered "in the flat voice of a man reading a course catalog aloud" and Vee genuinely doesn't listen.

The title, before and after: **The Practice Room** read to me first as a place — and it is, a piano closet with a worn square of carpet — and by the end as the other thing, the room where you rehearse. Vee practiced the apology in the car all weekend; here she practices a wanting she won't name, out loud, on a woman, in the one room in the building where nobody can hear you. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on the way out, which is the good kind. **WITH A LONG SPOON** I know the proverb for — sup with the devil and bring a long spoon — and that cover board has been quietly promising me the bill for twenty chapters while everyone is being unbearably nice to each other. **A POLITE INVITATION** is doing awful work now: every step of this has been an invitation, politely extended, and Vee has accepted every single one. I dread the ledger. I want to keep reading anyway.
