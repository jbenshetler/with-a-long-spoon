# Reader reaction — *broken-in*

I came into this chapter braced for the apology and got a morning of dress-up instead, and it worked on me exactly the way it worked on Vee — which is the uncomfortable part.

First: the opening ten minutes are the best small domestic writing in a while. Vee playing asleep on the top bunk while Cassie moves around below her, tracking the whole morning by sound, and Cassie's low **"*Hrm*"** — "the sound Cassie made instead of a question" — and then the door. That's the second night in a row Vee has had someone three feet away who would have sat up for her, and hasn't taken it. Then she gets up and *shuts the window Cassie cracks every night*. I don't think the book meant that as a knife, but it landed as one. Cassie is being closed out one small gesture at a time and doesn't know it.

Then the walk. Bare legs in October, an apple from the dorm bowl, "the day was a show-off," the stride lengthening when she thinks *I'm going to fix this.* I loved her here. This is the Vee of "I wanted it, and I took it" — resolve as a physical fact, cold up the backs of her legs and liking it. After the awfulness of the Rusty Nail I was so relieved to see her upright.

And then she goes to Randi, and I sat there with two feelings that refuse to resolve. Randi's counsel is *good.* Genuinely: the reframe (jealousy as evidence she's in it), "you don't go in there sorry — sorry's easy — you go in there sure," the refusal to let her run at it in a panic. If a friend of mine said that to me I'd be grateful for a decade. But **"Not today"** stopped me cold. Randi just bought herself a day of Pace not being apologized to, and she did it in a voice so kind it read as wisdom. And then: *"He won't text you, by the way... he doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing."* Vee hears an oracle. I heard a woman describing her own boyfriend's habits to the girl he's dating, and my stomach dropped. Every piece of that advice is true *because she sleeps with him.* Brooke opening the door with "Oh — you're the stats friend" — "Vee had told this house nothing in her life and the house had a folder on her anyway" — was a colder little chill than any of it.

The heat is in the shoes, and it's real. The long skirt falling over the whole operation so Vee can't see any of it — "only the feel — the cool quick hands somewhere down past the hem" — a hand coming up under the hem to the outside of her thigh to steady her, and then the thumb **pressing once into the soft hollow behind her knee** on the way down, and Vee's breath doing "something she hoped wasn't out loud." That's the best erotic passage Randi has had with her. It rhymes deliberately with Pace's fitting — being tended to, being held still, not being allowed to look — and with the boutique, where Randi knelt and thumbed the frosted plum toenail. And the shoes themselves: "being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." That's the dance floor sentence from last chapter, said again about a different person's hands. The book keeps telling me that Vee's deepest pleasure is being *led*, and it keeps letting two different people be the one leading. The scarf, hands over hands, "tension — it has to hold its shape or it'll go limp on you" — I could not not think of the Hermès scarf and the wrists and the tickling. Randi is teaching her a knot.

Where it truly ached: the kiss at the door, and Vee catching up to it "a half-beat late: she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed... It was only what they did." Three chapters ago she stood on a curb counting dark windows. That's not a French thing anymore and nobody has had to say so. And **"Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet"** — offered as a joke, taken as a joke, and it is the loudest line in the chapter. The volume's cover board is *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*, and the proverb behind that title has never been this audible: sup with the devil, bring a long spoon. Vee is eating with her hands. (No tagline or jacket copy was supplied to me, so that cover line is all I have to go on.)

The title. Before reading I assumed leather — the tan lace-ups "low and worn soft," the borrowed ropers. After reading, it's the shoes *and* it's her: broken in the way a horse is, or a glove, made comfortable to wear by someone else's use. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on the way out, which is my favorite kind. Friction was minor — Randi's pep talk runs a beat long and slightly too articulate, and "which was a surrender wearing the coat of a defense" is the one sentence where I felt the author's elbow. The *Edna Mode* laugh, on the other hand, was perfect and bought the whole scene.

What I want next: Vee at his door in the black dress, and the honest apology she owes. What I dread: that she'll walk in *styled by Randi*, and that Pace will recognize the hand.
