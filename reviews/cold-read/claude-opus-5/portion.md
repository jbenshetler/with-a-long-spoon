# Cold read (grounded) — Portion

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one hurt, and it hurt in a place I wasn't braced for. Twenty chapters of Randi setting every scene, and here she walks into the one room where she doesn't get to, and the whole evening comes apart over a bowl of stew she doesn't eat. The first wrong note is so quiet I almost missed it: *"It's wonderful."* And Pace catching it — "She did not say things like that to him. She said them to waiters, to girls in her house on the phone, to Vee, he would have bet, all day long." The face has come into his kitchen. She has never brought the face in here before, or if she has, this is the first time he's been able to see it as a thing arriving. He has no idea he's watching his own defeat; I did, and I wanted to put my hand over the page.

The spoon drawn *along the top* of the sauce, not down into it. Tasting what it brought up and setting the spoon on the cloth. God. That's the whole woman in one gesture — take the surface, leave the substance, and make the not-eating look like manners. And he sets the bread on her plate anyway, and the small bowl, *the one he sliced her apples into*, sits there all night with one line through the skin. When he covers it with a plate and puts it in the refrigerator, *for her*, at the end — that undid me more than anything else in the chapter. The man does not know what he did. He goes back through the evening from the gravel forward looking for it, the way he'd check a proof, and it isn't there, because the answer isn't in the evening. It's in a bathroom mirror a month ago that he will never be shown.

But here's what I don't have settled, and I don't want to settle it: I cannot tell what Randi is jealous *of*. "You've got her Friday to Sunday. You've got her all weekend, every weekend, and you're in no hurry, and I can't think why you would be." That reads one way as *you're hoarding Vee from me*, and another way as *she's taking you*, and a third as sheer unbearable proximity to a thing she watched on a sun porch that morning — a girl glowing and saying *I wanted it and I took it*, which is the sentence Randi cannot say about anything. The text keeps all three live and doesn't pick. Her calling it **selfish** is the tell that she's furious and reaching, because it's the one word that's plainly wrong about him — and the page knows it, landing it "somewhere to the side," in the man who knelt an hour at a pedestal and finished the hem.

The wine. He has poured every glass she has ever drunk in that house, and she takes the bottle by the neck and pours her own full one, and he watches her hand do it "and it went in somewhere and stayed, and he could not have said where." That's the divorce, right there, before they even go down the hall. Then the sex — fast, eyes shut, nails in his chest, no *tell me*. He lies under her *waiting* for it, and it doesn't come, and after a while he stops waiting and "gave her what she was taking." That's the coldest, saddest line about a man in this book. It was erotic — she's ferocious and it's unmistakably want — but it's want with the person scraped out of it, and the going-off-him-at-once, the cashmere blanket, the back turned, hit me as grief dressed as an orgasm. She ate the whole salad down to the dressed leaves afterward, too, which I noticed and which chilled me: a permitted food, all of it, once she had done the taking.

**Portion** as a title is exact and it didn't give anything away before I read it — it kept getting better as I went. The small bowl. The apple slices. The share of him she gets, or the share of Vee, or the measured amount of self she has decided anyone may have. And then it rings against the cover board, *WITH A LONG SPOON* — the spoon she drew along the surface and set down, the spoon you sup with when you're eating with something you don't intend to get close to. I'd been reading that title as a warning about Pace; tonight it read as Randi's own operating instruction, and that flip felt earned rather than clever. *A POLITE INVITATION* still sits ahead of me like a dropped shoe; nobody has issued it yet. (My packet gives no tagline or jacket copy beyond the cover-board line.)

No friction to speak of — this is the tightest chapter in a while, all kitchen-object choreography, no one explaining anything. If anything I wanted *one* more beat of Randi's interior and was denied it, correctly; being locked in his head while she becomes illegible is the point of the pain. What I want next: Vee, unknowing, walking into whatever this becomes. What I dread: that Randi's answer to being outpaced is to speed the thing up herself — to stop waiting for Vee to arrive on her own. *Guess from here:* the invitation comes from Randi, soon, and it comes early, and Pace lets it happen because he can't read what he's agreeing to.

**Cast present (in person):** Pace, Randi. *Mentioned only:* Vee.

**Heat:** 2 — furious, eyes-shut taking that runs hot and lands cold; the charge is real and doing work, but it's grief-shaped.

**Romance:** 2 — the wine poured at the sound of gravel, the house smelling of it since three, the bowl covered and put in the refrigerator *for her*; tenderness at full strength, aimed at someone who has left the room.

**Motifs & images:** The small bowl / portioned appetite — repeat, straight from ch 1's thin-sliced apple and the egg-white mother. The apple bowl itself, named. Wine he pours for her (ch 1's water set out in advance) vs. the glass she pours herself — first sighting, and a rupture. The cashmere blanket at the foot of the bed — repeat from ch 1, but now *she* takes it, alone, back turned. The deadbolt going over — repeat (ch 1). Cold citrus perfume arriving a beat after her mouth — repeat. Music low under rooms — repeat. The missing *"tell me"* — new absence of a thing I now realize was hers every night. The white-gold band, noted on her hand at the table.

**Symbolism:** The line drawn along the top of the sauce — surface taken, depth untouched, exactly her. The skin formed over the peas and carrot by the time he comes back to it. The refrigerated portion saved for someone who won't want it.

**Characterization:** Both deepened hard. Pace's limitation finally has a shape — he can read a person past their face and still be unable to see a person who has decided to stop being read; his exactness ("How do I get her from where she is to us, on your schedule?") is the most defended I've heard him. Randi cracks in the only place she can't manage the lighting. Nobody went thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Beautifully judged. The long, slow inventory of a cooked dinner earns every object it later uses; the sex is fast because she is, and the chapter ends on a kitchen light going off rather than a line of dialogue, which is right.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A sharp, necessary acceleration after two Vee-glow chapters in a row (Toenails, Fed). The first real fracture in the triangle, and it arrives from the direction I wasn't watching. Momentum way up.
