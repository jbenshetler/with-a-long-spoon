## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

That bowl. He gave her the small one — "the one he sliced her apples into" — and I was already braced before she sat down, because I've watched this man measure a woman's appetite with his whole attention and get it right. He got it right again. He portioned for her exactly the way she has trained the world to portion for her, and tonight that care arrived as an insult neither of them could name. She drew the spoon *along the top of the sauce*, tasted what it brought up, set it down on the cloth. "It's wonderful," she said. "You shouldn't have." I felt sick. Not scandalized — sick, the low kind, the way you feel at a table where somebody is being unkind in a dialect only two people speak.

Randi moved on me more than anyone has in twenty chapters. This is the first time since the bathroom mirror that the face slipped, and it didn't slip into the real woman — it slipped into something worse, a flatness with nothing on it. "She's not ready." / "She's not ready. Or you're not." The page told me outright what I'd stopped expecting to be told: *the thing at the corner of her mouth that came a beat late when she was holding something was not there, because she was not holding anything. It was out.* And what's out is jealousy, and I did not see it coming from this angle. I thought her tightening jaw at *he looked at me and I was fine* was about Vee. It is about Vee. "You've got her Friday to Sunday. You've got her all weekend, every weekend, and you're in no hurry, and I can't think why you would be." She built this. She pointed, she walked into the girl on purpose, she harvested the toenails and the bought panties lunch by lunch — and she is now starving at a table while the two people she arranged feed each other. I feel for her so much it's uncomfortable, and I still don't trust a word of her warmth, and this chapter made both of those *more* true at once.

Pace broke my heart in a quieter way. "It's selfish, Pace." And it lands — but *to the side*: "in the place that had knelt an hour at a pedestal not touching a girl who had been wet through her clothes for him, and finished the hem." That's the most defensive thought he's ever had on the page, and it's defensive because it's a receipt. Then "How do I get her from where she is to us, on your schedule?" — and that *us*, said out loud, flat, in his own kitchen, was the first time I've heard him speak the plan in plain language, and it chilled me coming from the mouth I trust most. He hears it too: *He did not take it back, because it was true, and because he did not know how.* He's not lying. "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything" is, I think, genuinely his law — and it's also the law that lets him have her Friday to Sunday indefinitely while the arrangement waits. Both. I can't make it only one.

The thing that gutted me was small and domestic: *She reached across the table and took the bottle by the neck and poured her own glass… He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house. He watched her hand do it and it went in somewhere and stayed, and he could not have said where.* That's a divorce in one gesture. And then she ate the entire salad — all of it, down to the dressing-heavy leaves — in front of the stew she wouldn't touch, which is the most articulate refusal in the book. She fed herself. She would not let him feed her.

The sex ran cold and I think it was meant to and it worked on me as dread, not heat. No *tell me* — every night in this house she'd said it, and he lay there waiting for it like a man waiting for a word in a language he's been learning. "Her eyes were shut." No inch-and-stop, no eyes. She used him and rolled into the cashmere blanket with her back to him and the chill took her, as it does, and nobody wrapped her this time — she wrapped herself. And he *did the thing he does*: laid each piece beside the next, got nothing, didn't force it, didn't put his hand in her hair, respected the closed door one more time. The man who reads people faster than they can hide read this one wrong — "it was the want, he thought, with an edge on it tonight, from somewhere." No, Pace. I knew and you didn't, and that reversal is new and awful.

Then the fridge. He covers the bowl with a plate and puts it away *for her*, and turns off the stove light. I didn't expect to be moved by Tupperware. He is saving a portion she will never eat, for a woman who has already decided not to come out, and he does not know he has done that twice now.

**Trust/suspicion:** no new suspicion, a new *fear* — that Randi will accelerate this herself, and that the acceleration will be aimed at Vee's ignorance rather than her consent. "How long are you going to take" is a woman reaching for the throttle.

**Friction:** almost none. One beat I noticed as authorial underlining: *"and he was who he was, and it did not taste like anything"* — I liked it, but it's the chapter telling me the mood twice. I'd also have traded the explicit *because she was not holding anything. It was out* for being allowed to find that myself; the gesture work was already doing it.

**Titles:** "Portion" is the best chapter title yet — the small bowl, the share of Vee she's been allotted, the appetite rationed since girlhood, and the plain second meaning: *her portion of him*, shrinking. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on the last page. And the cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — finally bit. Sup with the devil and use a long spoon. Tonight the spoon was laid on the cloth beside an untouched bowl, and I can't tell which of these two is the devil, or whether it's the invitation itself. "A polite invitation" now reads like a countdown: somebody is going to ask Vee something very courteously, and I am dreading it in a way I wasn't an hour ago.

**Want next:** Randi alone, and Vee walking into whatever Randi does with this. Guess from here: Randi makes a move on Vee without Pace, to take something back.
