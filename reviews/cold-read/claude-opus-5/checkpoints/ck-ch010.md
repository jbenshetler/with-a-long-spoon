### Impression
**Vee** — I'm wide open to her and a little afraid for her. She's the one whose interior I trust most: she reads every room accurately and then puts herself last in it, and her mother's voice arrives before her own opinion can. What's moving is how small the good things are that undo her — the right pen, a high stool by a window, a Greek joke she's finally in on, a hand around hers. The two strongest moments in the book so far are hers and both are private: the pool bottom, where she has a belonging nobody took from her, and the shadow under the tree, where she stopped, made him stop, and took it. I want her to keep that. I also know exactly how she got to that tree, and she doesn't.

**Pace** — I like him enormously and I can't stop watching him. His care is not a technique I can catch out: the safeword offered twice with permission attached, the water bottle left in advance, the locked door he wouldn't knock on, the pencil handed back at the exact line, the last inch left standing twice on a dark path. He kept Vee's most exposed sentence to himself because it was hers. None of that reads as cover; it reads as who he is. And he pointed at a laughing stranger across a lawn and said "Her," and is now courting that stranger while his girlfriend asks whether she'll like the bench. Both things are true at once and I have no idea yet which one governs. My unease about him isn't suspicion of dishonesty — it's that he may be entirely sincere in every direction at the same time, which could hurt people worse.

**Randi** — the one I ache over and the one I'd warn Vee about, and those are the same page. Her chapter-1 mirror scene bought her all my sympathy: a woman who has been portioning herself since childhood, who got fed once and immediately decided never again. She lies with total competence, she manages her own sisters by the second, and she pressed a bottle cap into her own nail bed without knowing it. She wants Vee — badly, growingly, and she will not let herself hold that for one second — and the only way she's found to want anything is to hand the wanting to a man to own. I find her more desperate than predatory right now, and I don't think that makes her safe.

**Cassie** — clean trust, no reservations. She's the only person in the book who has asked nothing of Vee. Her love shows up as an insult pitched downward instead of up, a splash instead of a question, and one demand for an address. That "Goodbye, Vee" is the single sound in ten chapters I can't place, and it's sitting in me like a stone.

**Brooke** — no evidence of malice, plenty of evidence of surveillance. She ran four minutes on Randi without ever raising her voice or standing up, and she's the one thread already pulling.

Where trust sits, plainly: I have no reason to doubt Cassie, and no reason to doubt that Pace's tenderness is real. What I doubt is that anyone in this arrangement has told anyone else the whole truth, and the only person operating with no information at all is the one it's all pointed at.
