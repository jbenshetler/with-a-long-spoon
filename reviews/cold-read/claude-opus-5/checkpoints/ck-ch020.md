### What I know that they don't
- **Vee does not know Randi and Pace are lovers.** She believes Randi is single and unlucky in men, because Randi feeds her stories (the lacrosse monologuist, the scarf, the hurried ex). She does not know her boyfriend's girlfriend is the woman holding her hand across lunch.
- **Vee does not know she was chosen.** She thinks Pace crossed a coffee shop because he saw her. He crossed it because Randi pointed at her by the dining hall doors and he said *Her*, and because Randi then engineered a collision, learned Vee's name and class schedule, and asked, with her head on his chest, *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* The jacket (marketing, held loosely) frames this as a plan Randi and Pace started in bed, aimed at the best year of Vee's life, which she doesn't know is planned — and warns that it will get real for each of them.
- **Vee does not know the point of the pointing game was for her to be wanted by a woman.** Pace asked Randi in bed whether she'd like to taste another woman and her body answered before she could; she repackaged it as *only in a threesome, with you.*
- **Caring side, fully as real:** Pace's tenderness is not technique. He left the water out in advance because she always drinks more than she expects; he sliced the apple thin, cut-side up, and was *sorry* about the thing that polices her eating; he went down on one knee to undress her and refused to spend the night early; he kept out of the margin of Vee's calculus so the solving stayed hers; he carried out her cherry pit without making her small; he gave Vee his only dry shirt and stood out in freezing rain with his back turned; he turned the thermostat up hours before she arrived so she could stand bare in his house and not be cold; he spent an hour on his knees not touching a woman who was all but handing herself to him, because he'd said he would make the dress right; he matched the silk to *her* and not to the dress she described. He is, as far as the pages show, exactly what he appears to be — and he is also the instrument of a plan.
- **I know what happened on the bench and Vee doesn't.** Randi begged in a voice she hadn't used since she was a child, cried without knowing it, flinched when he called her beautiful, locked a door she'd never locked, and decided in the mirror to go back behind the face forever. Vee sees only the glamour.
- **Pace doesn't know that either — the decision part.** He thinks that night was a breakthrough she's still digesting. He does not know she resolved never to be found again. He also does not know why she left after sex in ch 13, or why she wouldn't eat and turned her back in ch 20; he looked for what he'd done and it wasn't there.
- **Randi doesn't know what Pace keeps back.** He did not hand over Vee's true line on the walk (*this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time*), or that Vee bared herself in the truck. "She's easy to listen to"; "Her stomach is soft." He keeps some of Vee for Vee.

Her belly went board-tight on top of Pace while he described Vee's hair. She said *"I want us"* into his ear, torn out of her. She kissed Vee on the mouth twice with a second press in it. Vee has filed all of it as a French thing.
- **I know Vee wants Randi too, and she doesn't.** The flat helpless want at Chi Latte with nowhere to put itself; the long beat of looking at the bare midriffs before her mother's word arrived; the heat the picture of Randi tied and laughing put low in her, which she immediately covered with a joke.

- **Nobody but Pace and the reader knows about Daphne's door.** It is why he did not knock in ch 1.
- **Nobody but the reader has seen Randi press a sharp edge into her own nail bed.** Three times now, always right after something real has gotten through.

### Symbolism
- **The bench that is also a table.** A purpose hidden under a domestic surface; she'd been laying her underwear on it for weeks without seeing it. The top goes back on in the morning and the room is innocent again — exactly what she does with her own face. The pages invite this directly in the last paragraph of ch 1.
- **The face as a thing that is not her.** "What was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing." The whole economy of Randi, stated on the page.
- **Euler's formula.** Two separate hard things, pushed at and giving nothing, put together at right angles and turning into a single plain thing. Offered as math; it sits over Randi-and-Pace, and over Randi-and-Vee.
- **Error-correcting redundancy.** *"Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it."* Also what Pace does with tenderness: the water left out, the heat turned up, the second press of a kiss, *you are doing so well, you are.*
- **Signal in noise.** The patent finds the true picture under the junk faster, and gets a frightened person out of the tube sooner. It is also the thing he does to women, and the thing Randi could not survive having done to her.
- **The riser.** A pedestal built by hand, finished smooth as furniture, that puts a woman's most shamed part at a kneeling man's eye level — and he never looks down. She rises onto her toes afterward toward something she has no name for.
- **The locked bathroom door.** Hers, not his; you don't open it because you can hear through it. Daphne's door, relearned.
- **Water as the untaken self.** Weightless, unseen, nobody wanting anything; the body that is a problem waits up on the deck.
- **"With a long spoon"** (series title, from the jacket — marketing, held loosely): the proverb about who you need one to sup with. Every meal in this book so far is somebody being fed, or refusing to eat.
- **The uneaten bowl in the refrigerator.** Her portion, covered and kept, in case she wants it later.

Everything he does is the real thing: the moleskin, the one dry shirt, the thermostat turned up hours early, the pencil put back in her hand, the cherry pit picked up without making her small, the hour on his knees not touching her because he'd said he'd make the dress right. He asks before he takes, every time, and he means the question. And he is the hand that reached for a girl because another woman pointed at her, and he's running a plan he hasn't told her about, and both of those are true at once — which is exactly where my unease lives. The Daphne line tells me he's already paid once for going somewhere he wasn't invited, and that he learned the lesson almost too well: his great virtue, not putting a hand on a closed door, is also why he can lie behind Randi for an hour with all the pieces and not see her. I trust his intentions and I don't trust the arrangement he's inside.

**Randi** — the one I can't put down. I find her glamorous and I find her unbearable to watch, in roughly equal measure. She is the book's predator — the engineered collision, the *it's not a thing, it's just how it's done*, the soft *you don't have to tell me* that closes the door at the exact instant Vee tries to give her the real thing — and she is also, plainly, the person in the most danger. Everything she extracts from Vee she takes home and burns as fuel on top of Pace. The thumbnail pressed into the nail bed three times. The bowl with one line drawn through it. *I want us*, torn out of her. The decision in the mirror never to be found again, which she is keeping with total discipline against a man whose entire gift is finding people. I feel more tenderness toward her than I expected to, and more wariness than toward anyone else in the book. If something breaks, it breaks in her first.

**Cassie** — I love her without reservation and I'm braced for her to get hurt in the dullest possible way, by inches, by not being invited. She's the only person in the book who has said a true thing out loud at a table where it cost her something, and nobody picked it up. *"Goodbye, Vee"* is still sitting there unanswered.

**Brooke** — mild, bright, friendly, and the most quietly dangerous presence in the sorority half of the book. She hasn't done anything. She's filing.

Where trust sits: nobody has lied to Vee about who they are except Randi, and Randi's lie is enormous and growing — the concealed boyfriend, the stories of other men, the harvesting disguised as sisterhood. Pace's silence is a different species: real love running on a withheld fact. Three people are now genuinely in it, none of them has the whole picture, and the thing that worries me most is that every yes so far has been freely given.
