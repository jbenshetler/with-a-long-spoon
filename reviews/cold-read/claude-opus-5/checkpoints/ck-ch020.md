### Symbolism

### Story so far
**Ch 1 — The Bench.** A warm house west of town; Steely Dan low. A black-haired, blue-eyed sorority girl lets herself in with an overnight bag, locks the deadbolt behind her, straddles her boyfriend at his kitchen table and eats the apple he sliced thin for her, bite-kiss-bite. He leads her to the bedroom, where the long table she had been laying her underwear on is standing bare — a bench he built for her, for tonight: face cradle, chest shelf, hand grips, leg rests on pivots, nothing under the middle. "Show me." He undresses her from one knee, slowly, folds each piece, looks at her with the lights up. On the bench he sets her safeword — **red** — and works her for an hour: spanking in patterns she can't predict, long slow passes of his hand, kisses up her spine, edging her repeatedly and stopping each time. She asks the way she'd ask a waiter for water, and he says no; her whole apparatus of performance runs out, and what comes next is a real, ugly, broken *please* from below. "Good girl." The orgasm is hers in a way none before were. She cries without knowing it. He carries her to the bed, wraps her in the cashmere blanket she'd left there, gives her the water he'd set out in advance, holds her and interprets nothing — and remembers Daphne, whose closed door he once opened, and doesn't knock when this one locks. In his bathroom she looks at the face she has used since thirteen, understands she was seen past it and that the tenderness waiting in the next room is aimed at the woman behind it, and decides: *he could have the face; the one he found is not coming out again.* She arranges the smile, goes back, curls into him, says yes to staying. He falls asleep pleased, thinking a door opened. Her name is **Randi**.

**Ch 2 — Standards.** Saturday morning, sorority house, last night's clothes. Brooke, seated at the chapter laptop, stops Randi without standing: how's the paper, where did you work, *Pace — that's actually his name?*, grad school, engineering? (Math. PhD.), you're not in his section, are you. Sloane mentions the white pickup behind Hahn; Kinsey calls him short-cute. Four minutes, mostly smiling. Upstairs in her single, Randi can't find what she came to her closet for, lies down in her clothes and shoes under a thin duvet, hears herself say *he's hard to say no to* in a key she can't sing in, and does not cry.

**Ch 3 — The Pointing Game.** Pace's bed, four weeks in. After bringing her off with his mouth, he enters her still pulsing and asks, into the warm disorder: *would you like to taste another woman?* Her body answers with a hard, helpless orgasm before she can make it mean anything safer. Afterward, gathered back into herself, she reframes it: "Only in a threesome. With you." He sees exactly what she's done — the appetite handed to him so she can be its generous instrument — and lets her have the costume. Then: "Let's go for a walk on campus." On a brilliant fall quad he points at women, one at a time, and she picks. *I point. You pick.* She rejects the mirror-image sorority girl (too expected), the sealed pair, the room-running blonde (a hard *no*), and holds longest on the beanie girl on the library steps (*maybe*, rewarded with a kiss that still tastes of her). Then a tall redhead comes out of the dining hall with her head thrown back laughing, and Randi stops mid-stride and cannot stop looking. "Yes." "Go meet her." Randi engineers a shoulder-collision so clean the redhead apologizes to *her*, touches her arm, learns her name, discovers a shared class, and comes back across the lawn grinning: "Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together." "Let's go feed you."

**Ch 4 — See You Later.** Vee's side of it. She sits in stats not looking at the door; Randi arrives late through the back — "*There* you are" — and takes the aisle seat beside her. Cassie gives her name flat and won't play the bit. Vee spends the hour half-listening (*latent variables* is the only thing she writes down) and then is swept to Chi Latte, the yellow place with the Greek sign she'd never sounded out and had never gone into alone. Cassie declines — "I've got a thing" — and says "Goodbye, Vee," the whole word, which lands slightly wrong and is smoothed over. At the high table in the window Randi buys her a lavender oat latte and talks, and afterward Vee cannot reconstruct a single sentence, only the feeling. On the sidewalk: a full hug, "Love you, girl," "See you later." The best hour she's had in months.

**Ch 5 — Substitution.** The coffee shop on Wilson. Vee is beaten by a challenge integral; Randi thumbs at her phone; Cassie works two tables over. "That cute guy in line is looking at you." Pace crosses the room to her, offers help, gives his name, learns theirs — Randi supplies hers as one bare word, a stranger. He rolls his cuffs, reads the problem properly, asks what her professor said the trick was, writes the horrible closed-form in the margin and tells her not to memorize it, marks two short strokes at a right angle, names Euler's formula, and **hands the pencil back** at exactly the line where she can finish it herself. She does, cleanly. He asks Randi a plausible stats question and answers it briskly; he asks Vee *what's drawing you to it* and then listens for five minutes without touching his phone. He is a math PhD, nearly finished, which mortifies her delightfully. "But I'd love to see you sometime. **May I call you?**" He takes her number and goes — leaving his place in line and his coffee behind, as Randi points out. Cassie: "He looked at her like he'd forgotten there was anyone else in the room. He looked at *her* the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt."

**Ch 6 — The Long Way.** He texts twenty minutes later: frozen yogurt. Randi overrules the wait-a-day rule. That evening, neon and plastic ivy, he explains his topping system ("I've thought about most things"), her cup leans over and for once no voice tells her that's plenty, he pays before she finds her wallet, and they walk the long way. She says the embarrassing true thing — this is the nicest anyone has been to her in a long time, and it's froyo — and he neither pretends nor inflates it: "Your tart's holding up."

**Ch 7 — Water Wings.** The rec pool with Cassie. Catcalls from the bleachers ("You won't need floaties with *those*"), Vee's game face, Cassie's low "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman," and the held breath going out in a laugh. Cassie stands planted at rib depth; Vee crosses into the deep, goes under, and the room switches off — weightless, sculling, the old belonging she lost when her body changed. She surfaces with her face wrong, and Cassie, who never asks, shoots a sheet of water at her head instead, and the splash fight fixes it.

**Ch 8 — May I Choose.** Friday: *Saturday. Dinner.* No venue, no demand for an answer that night, and the built-in quiet is the part that undoes her. Cassie: "That's not a boy. That's a man." He comes to the lobby showered, shaved, in a pressed broadcloth shirt and polished old boots; she nearly rides the elevator back up staring at him. A block she'd never turned down; a small Middle Eastern place; Christine, who knows him. The menu defeats her happily and he says: **"May I choose?"** He asks only whether there's anything she won't eat, then orders — soft, rounded words she can't repeat, promises she can't read. Bright tabbouleh, smoke-dark baba ghanoush, blistered bread, the right hand always. She stops watching herself eat, loses the thread of what he's saying and lets it go, and nothing bad happens. Mint tea in brass cages brings her back to a half-emptied room. Walking home he takes her hand — not a question — and under a tree he stops and it's obvious what's next, and out comes her rule: *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days.* He doesn't argue; he smiles and starts them walking. She doesn't go. The hand pulls taut. "But — I'm making an exception."

**Ch 9 — Off Six-Fourteen.** Vee tells Cassie everything, slowing at the tree. "Nobody did anything to me... I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." Cassie, glad, asks how much he gave her to drink; nothing, tea. Vee spins around the room. He's asked her hiking. She can hand Cassie a dossier: his name is Pace, he lives off six-fourteen past where it goes to gravel, he'll have water. Real shoes required; she'll borrow them.

**Ch 10 — Dear.** After stats, Cassie peels off to practice and Randi takes Vee to the Smoothie Bird. Randi opens with her own catastrophe (the beautiful lacrosse bore) and then turns the whole beam on Vee, who tells the dinner: *may I, not can I, who even says that*, and "you let him choose for you — and you liked it." *…Yeah.* And the kiss, two days in, which Randi reframes from *I made an exception* to *because you couldn't not — because of him*, and Vee lets it be said the truer way. Then Brooke arrives with a taller, silent woman; "We missed you Thursday" sets down a world Vee isn't in; Vee shrinks herself to *we have stats together*; and Randi says **"Vee's my dear friend,"** taking her hand on the white table and keeping it there. Vee doesn't know what it cost, only that it cost something. "Love you, girl." She walks home feeling picked.

**Ch 11 — Leave No Trace.** The trailhead: a wall of trees, borrowed boots a half-size big. He sets a pace slower than she braced for. When her heel goes hot she says so; he sits her on a boulder, takes the boot and sock off, finds the spot, moleskins it, re-laces it differently, and apologizes for not asking about the boots. The summit opens all at once — black rock, a silver lake, burning ridges — and it doesn't care that she's standing there, which is the best part; when she looks over, he's watching her face and lets her catch him, then gives her the rest of the looking alone. Rye sandwiches with horseradish that makes her eyes run, one water bottle shared, cherries. She spits a pit; "we take nothing and leave nothing," and he sets both pits on wax paper without watching her learn it. Over the cherries she tells him about the honor society induction, and the russet silk dress in the lobby magazine she can't afford, and the good-enough black one in the back of her closet. He says only, "That sounds like a beautiful dress," and lets it lie where she set it. Coming down, the rain arrives all at once and soaks them both. In the truck he hands her his only dry shirt — the flannel he'd packed for himself — then gets out and stands with his back to the cab in the rain so she can change. She knocks on the glass, crooks a finger, and when he's back in she holds his eyes and unbuttons her wet shirt all the way and pushes it off and lets him look. He doesn't move; his face is want and gladness and stillness, and that's the whole answer. Reaching for his hand to start the next thing, she finds it shaking — he's freezing — and sends them home instead. At her dorm he pulls into the fire lane under the overhang; she starts to say something too big; blue-and-white lights, one bark of the siren, and the sentence is gone. She keeps the flannel.

**Ch 12 — Rye.** Told to Cassie: the blister, the kit, the forearms, the lake, the sandwich, the rain, the shirt, the knock on the glass, *and he didn't touch me.* "Borrow real boots next time." "I'm keeping the shirt." "Keep the shirt."

**Ch 13 — What to Wear.** Dinner with Meg and Kayla in the athletic dorm's gold light. Theo waves. Pace's day: lifts first thing, writes six hours, stops; powerlifter. The shirt story again, better in the telling. The count: **one kiss, twelve days ago**, and nothing since — "it's making me lose my mind... and I can't even explain why. He's not pushing. It's the opposite. He asks me first." Meg, kindly: everybody's different at the start; sometimes they even are. Then the text: *his house, Saturday, two o'clock,* and nothing else — no what-to-wear, no what-for. Kayla: there's no meal at two; bring a change of clothes.

**Ch 14 — Two Towels.** She drives past the gravel driveway and comes back for it. He opens before she reaches the top step; she kisses him first, and he is the one who ends it. The house is too warm and she doesn't ask why. The tour: a closet of paired shoes, a working kitchen, an office drowning in graph paper and marked-up drafts ("I didn't realize you knew Sanskrit"), one closed door — his bedroom, named and left shut while she leans toward it in the satin she wore hoping — and a den full of white oak he built himself, with joints that hide their own seams. Books: cracked fantasy paperbacks, used cookbooks, ordered math. He explains error-correcting codes with "the dogs *are* barking." She asks how he affords it; something closes at *parents*; he licenses a patent — faster MRI reconstruction, which means a frightened kid out of the tube sooner — and lights up like a six-year-old about it. The shop: a garage swept to bare concrete, tools in painted outlines. Then the sun porch, glass and mountains, and a bolt of burgundy silk on the table. He holds it to her face; it isn't the magazine shade because he matched *her*. She says "I can't sew" and then, finally, works it out: **he made the flannel shirt** — that's why there's no tag. "I'd like to make it for you. The dress. If you'll let me." He'd measure her first, in what she'd wear under it, in good light; two weeks; ready before the induction. Her eyes go hot. "Yes — of course, yes."

**Ch 15 — A Round.** The fitting. Jeans off, shirt off, and then: "The bra too. It changes the line. It'll lie to me." The room no longer permits not-knowing; the word *stripper* arrives in her mother's register; she takes it off anyway and stands in the satin panties she chose with hope. He doesn't ogle — he admires — and her arms come down. A riser he built, set where the light falls. The rhythm: close, two touches, withdrawal, pencil, look, return. Neck, arm, waist (she flinches, ticklish, and he grins before he can stop it), under the bust, across the bust — where she asks whether the measurement changes when they're *not like this*, and he laughs, then genuinely doesn't know, and turns it over as a real problem. Inseam. Then tea and a fleece blanket while he cuts, and the discovery that the satin has gone slick, and the thumbprint, and the spreading. Pinning, on his knees, working around her, his hands flat on her ass for the first time — and then the geometry closes and his face is about to arrive at the worst of it. She shuts her eyes and waits for the grin she's braced for her whole life. There is silence. She opens them: he has craned his neck all the way up to meet her eyes and has been waiting there, smiling like a man handed a gift. **He never looked down.** Her stoop comes undone; she rises onto her toes; he says *hmm* and she comes back down laughing. After that she spends her new power: leaning, turning, and finally pressing her bare breast to his cheek on purpose — "Oops. Sorry." He goes red, fights a grin, loses. A pin nicks her ribs; he kisses the bead of blood before he decides to; then, low and a beat too late: **"Keep still."** She keeps still, and he finishes the hem with small deniable touches, and she stands in pinned burgundy with her whole skin singing, knowing both that she nearly toppled him and that he stayed on his knees to do it right.

**Ch 16 — Turned Up.** Told to Cassie, out of order and at speed: the dress, the silk held to her face, the riser, *the bra too*, the box, and — landing hardest — the warm house, turned up hours early so she wouldn't be cold. "Oh, Vee," says Cassie. Cassie works the puzzle and asks whether he's gay; Vee reports, gravely, feeling his package while he pinned the back seam — and then, delighted with herself, the truth: "*I* was grinding on *him*."

**Ch 17 — How It's Done.** Randi collects her in a dark green Mercedes with the top down, and the smile is different today, slower, less performed. A green-velvet booth off Prices Fork, Sinatra, a near-alcove where you could say anything. Randi orders salmon and a Rombauer without reading the prices; Vee orders tomato soup and water and pays her own check. Randi's story: a beautiful idiot, her wrists tied to his headboard with a scarf, being tickled helpless, and then the switch — "the letting go... a little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." Vee laughs, jokes to cover the heat the picture puts in her, and then asks, from nearer than she likes, whether she wasn't scared. Then her turn. She means to tell the arranged version and doesn't. Randi supplies the permission — "Nobody measures over a bra, sweetheart... it's just how it's done" — and out it comes. Then, lighter, the hook: "Were there any *challenges*?" — "It was cold in the house." — "Cold. Hmm." Caught, Vee gives it up laughing: "actually it was really *hot*." Randi walks her down, one *yeah* at a time — the pedestal, the knees, the left hip — to the very edge of the thing Vee has told no one, and then closes the door very softly: **"You don't have to tell me."** The hush ends; the check comes. Driving back with the radio up they sing at a light, and Vee is happy. In the fire lane Randi takes her jaw and kisses her on the mouth — soft, unhurried, a small drawing-back and then a second press. "See you soon, gorgeous." Vee files it as a French thing, then stands on the curb counting dark dorm windows in case anyone saw.

**Ch 18 — Famished.** A quiet week; Pace at a Chicago conference; then *Dinner Friday?* She sleeps in the flannel chasing his fading smell, hears *floozie*, feels the heat arrive a beat behind the shame. She buys a green satin-and-lace set for the color and a bottle of frosted plum polish; she paints her toenails Friday afternoon, in October, early enough to dry. At his house he's in a canvas apron with everything prepped and a Chianti already breathing. "Are you hungry?" — "**Famished.**" She kisses him, pulls the apron strap, asks to see his bedroom, and takes them there. His chest and shoulders and the woodworker's forearms; a large scar high on the inside of his thigh. When he takes the satin down and sees the crystal strings of her, he asks, seriously, "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — and she says no and makes it true. He brings her off standing with his mouth and his hand under her, and the shame runs the whole length of it and never closes her. Then the bed: plain white cotton, his actual smell behind the proxy she'd been sleeping in, long unhurried strokes, his eyes on her face the whole way with nowhere to hide, and the moment the careful thing in him lets go. They finish together. Afterward he pulls on jeans and cooks the fra diavolo from where he stopped while she sits at his table wrapped in his top sheet, drinking the wine at last, near midnight, nothing missing. Later she fetches the soaked green panties, folds them in thirds with the spot centered, drops the sheet, and holds them out: **"Trade."**

**Ch 19 — Toenails.** Home in the morning with no bag, no brush, no makeup, and a smile that came back at every red light. Cassie spots the October toenails. Vee tells the story and lifts the middle out clean — the apron, the Chianti, *he's a good kisser* — and Cassie doesn't pull at the seam. Then Vee starts to undress for a shower and stops with the zipper half down: she has nothing on under the skirt, because she gave them away, and that would be a fact rather than an edit. She zips back up and lies about forgetting something. Cassie leaves for a game. "She had never in her life been so happy."

**Ch 20 — Fed.** Brunch with Randi on a bright bistro sun porch; Vee prices a mimosa, finds Thursday survives it, and orders. Randi's sad story: an old boyfriend, beautiful, in too much of a hurry. Then Vee's — the same clean version she gave Cassie, but further: the middle waved past, and then the part she wants to keep, sitting in his sheet at his table at midnight watching him cook. "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." Then the extraction, fond and expert: *you keep saying "he,"* — did you do anything to get ready for him? The nails. **The toes.** "You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands." The polish name, *frosted plum*, and the thing Vee doesn't say — that she picked it to match the silk he's sewing her. The underwear: *you bought them.* "That's not nothing, that's a campaign." And when fear shows in Vee's face at how far gone she is, Randi covers her hand: "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." Vee says the truest sentence she has said all book: "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." Another hour, another mimosa she doesn't price. At the curb, the goodbye kiss again — expected this time, and met — and she doesn't look down the street to see who saw. "See you soon, gorgeous." She drives home lighter than she came.

### Impression
**Vee.** Enormous tenderness, and a real pull — she is being handed, one week at a time, the exact things she'd stopped asking for, and she is receiving them bravely: the knock on the truck glass, the exception under the tree, "I wanted it and I took it." It is genuinely lovely to watch her stop watching herself. And I am uneasy on her behalf in a way she has no access to, because I know where the door she walked through was installed, and by whom, and I am aware that her happiness and my dread are the same object seen from two sides.

**Pace.** I trust his hands and I do not know what to do with that. Nothing he does to Vee is false: the thermostat, the moleskin, the pencil handed back, the shirt in the rain, the face turned up and waiting while she died of shame. That is not technique, or if it is, technique has stopped being the right word for it. But he asked a question in bed to see what it would do to a woman and then took her out to a quad to point at strangers, and he is fond of the moment a person runs out of apparatus. He reads people faster than they can hide, which is the tenderest and the most frightening thing about him. I like him. I would not want him aimed at me without knowing it.

**Randi.** The one I feel for most and trust least. Chapter 1 gutted me: she was met, completely, and her response was to bolt the door and never come out — a decision made in a bathroom mirror with no one to see it, which is the loneliest thing in the book. Everything since is her working that decision outward, spending her own real want for Vee by disguising it as a gift to her boyfriend, and running lunches that are equal parts love and harvest. I think the warmth toward Vee is real and I think she will use it anyway, and I don't think she's decided which of those is the truth.

**Cassie.** The clean water. Flatly, undramatically good, and currently being lied to by omission, which is the only development so far that makes me actually sad rather than merely wary.

**Where trust sits:** I have no reason to doubt Pace's feeling for Vee or Randi's affection for her; I have every reason to doubt the frame both of them are keeping her inside. The dishonesty so far is one specific withheld fact — that the three of them are already a triangle — and it is load-bearing for everything. Nobody has been cruel. That is exactly what unsettles me.
