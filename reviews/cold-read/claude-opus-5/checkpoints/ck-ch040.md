### Symbolism
- **The bench with its top on** — a piece of furniture that is one thing in daylight and another with the top off, sitting in the middle of the room full of what it is, waiting. Offered by the pages themselves as the shape of the whole arrangement.
- **Error-correcting codes** — "say it more than once so the noise can't kill it." Redundancy so a message survives being chewed up on the way. He explains it to Vee off his own shelf.
- **The paint paradox and the coastline** — a shape you can fill with a cup and never finish painting; an edge as long as you have patience for. He gives them to her as beauty; they sit oddly well against a thing whose depth keeps increasing the closer you measure.
- **Euler's formula, ch 5** — two things that won't give on their own, set at right angles, becoming a single plain thing you know what to do with. His first gift to her was a way of turning an unsolvable shape into a familiar one.
- **The dance frame** — the closeness she fought as too fixed turns out to be the only thing that makes the next step possible; the space she was keeping was what got her stepped on.
- **The riser / pedestal** — she is literally put up on a box, in the light, at exactly the height where a kneeling man's face arrives.
- **Simpson's paradox and the outlier** — reversal without a single number being touched; and the point that is invisible unless you know to look for it, which is where Randi's name turned out to live.
- **The jacket's series line, *With a Long Spoon*** — carried forward from the marketing copy only, not from the pages.

### Impression
**Vee.** I'm all the way in with her, and it's uncomfortable, because I can see the floor she's walking on and she can't. What's happening to her is real: the shame is genuinely turning into heat, the appetite is genuinely hers, the courage of the induction stride and the truck window and *I wanted it and I took it* is her own. Nobody is faking the good she's getting. That's precisely what makes me wince — every yes is freely given, and the freedom of it is the load-bearing wall. Tenderness toward her, and a low steady dread.

**Pace.** I can't find the seam, and I've looked. Everything he does is exactly what a person who loves someone would do, down to the thermostat and the drawer and the pencil handed back. He is also a man with two women, one of whom he pointed his other girlfriend at across a lawn, and he has never once told Vee the truth of his own house. The Daphne story is the most frightening thing about him, not because of what he did, but because of how well he has defended it: he was handed something at fifteen that hurt him and he has filed it under *luck*, in good order, in a careful voice. I trust him with Vee's body completely. I don't trust his accounting. Fondness, pull, and a specific unease.

**Randi.** She's the one breaking. She started as the hand on the wheel and she's now the person in the story with the most to lose and the least room to say so. The bench told me everything: she went further into herself than she's ever been, and within twenty minutes she'd decided never again, and nothing in her life since has contradicted that decision except Vee. "It doesn't," in the booth, was the truest thing she's said. I feel enormous tenderness for her and I do not think she can be trusted, and I don't think those are in conflict.

**Cassie.** The one person in the book asking plain questions with no angle in them. *Do you love him.* *You don't come home school nights.* *Goodbye, Vee.* She's being edited out of her best friend's life in small kind increments and she knows it, and she doesn't make Vee pay for it. No reason whatsoever to doubt her.

**Overall.** Suspicion sits almost entirely on the structure — the arrangement, the debrief in Pace's bed, the lunches that open Vee one question at a time — and almost not at all on any individual's feeling. That's the trap I'm in: I've been ahead of Vee since chapter three and I still want the thing she's getting for her. The outlier record was the first time the floor creaked audibly, and I'm braced now — for the day Vee finds out, for what Randi does when the game and the love stop being the same object, and for whether Pace has any idea what he's built.
