# Cold read (grounded) — In His Hands

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

That opening paragraph got me before I had my guard up — the red curtain of her hair drawn against the lamp, kissing him blind inside it, and then the soreness she'll be glad of tomorrow. And then he does the thing that undid me: rolls all the way across her, feet off the far edge of the bed, and lays his head on the lower slope of her breast like a man setting a weight down. *He takes such comfort there. They're not about sex for him* — and then her own body contradicts her under his mouth, and the corners of his mouth pull up because he felt it happen too. That's the exact register this book is best in: her theorizing herself one beat behind her own skin, and him not correcting her, just smiling against her.

And then the photograph, which I have been turning over since I finished. Everything in that scene is warm. He says he takes care of it himself thinking of her, and when she asks what about, he gives her the first bite of something he made her and *lying there glorious in my bed* — not an act, a face. She taps her tooth three times and offers. He says *you don't have to* and catches her wrist only to say it. He poses her himself with a kiss at every place his hands set her — shoulder, hip, knee — and then fusses with the lamp shade like a boy with a camera, and looks at *her* over the top of the phone and grins. I melted at that grin. I also want to be very plain about the other thing, because it's the loudest sentence in the chapter and nobody in the room heard it: she says *only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone,* and he says *Never. I'd never share your photograph.* He narrowed it. She asked about herself; he answered about the file. I don't think he lied — I think he answered exactly, the way a precise man answers, and the thing he did not promise is the thing this whole book was set in motion to arrange. *Delivered,* in that little grey word under the bar. My stomach went cold and my chest stayed warm and I hated how well that worked.

There's a second thing under it that's quieter and sadder. She goes to the bathroom and *puts her face back on,* the poppy-red mouth, works at herself in his mirror "until she'd found the version of herself she wanted him to keep," and comes back composed — a girl who has decided to be looked at. That's Randi's move. That's the mirror from chapter one. And Pace says *no, not like that. You'll have to wait,* and arranges her himself. He keeps refusing the offered face and taking the woman. With Randi that was the miracle she fled. With Vee it's still working. I don't know what to do with the fact that the same gesture is a rescue in one bed and a haunting in the other, and I suspect the book knows.

The kitchen is a long unhurried pleasure and I didn't resent a minute of it — the pan going loud and then settling to a steady talk, the lemon zest in a fine bright rain, turning the chicken without looking because the pan said so. And the small, enormous line: *She had used to just eat. Now she tasted, and there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was.* That's the feeding motif finally naming its own cost. He hasn't fed her hunger; he's rebuilt her palate. You can't un-know a thing like that. Then two hits in a row: *I don't go back,* each word set down clean and apart from the last, a door with no handle — the parents thing closing for the third or fourth time, and she takes her hand off it, which was kind and which I wished she hadn't. And Sheri at Thanksgiving, *she's alone,* said with such plain unguarded fondness that I believed it completely, while knowing Sheri is the one person who's done the arithmetic out loud. Vee's little flicker and how fast she put it out — she is getting *very* good at extinguishing her own alarms.

Trust: I still can't catch Pace in cruelty, and that's what frightens me. Every single thing on this page is generous and most of it costs him something. The only genuinely off note is the seven-word promise, and it's off because it's *careful*, not because it's cold. Vee I love and ache for — she was brave in the right way (believing him was the brave part, and the text says so) and she has now handed over an object that exists whether or not she consents to its next use. Erotically the chapter ran hot but not at its hottest; the peak for me wasn't the sex, it was being posed, kiss by kiss, and choosing to be kept. No friction to speak of — maybe *and it landed warm under her breastbone* is a half-beat of the author underlining a landing I'd already felt. Tiny.

**In His Hands** — before reading I assumed it meant the dress, the cooking, being held. After, it means the phone. She is now a thing that exists in his hands, portable, reproducible, out of her control. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on me at *Delivered.* The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — is all I was given (no jacket copy, no tagline), and the spoon proverb has never been louder than in a chapter where the devil cooks you lemon chicken and grates the zest so none of the white gets in. "A polite invitation" now reads like the photograph itself: an invitation she extended, politely, to a party whose guest list she hasn't seen. What I want next: for her to ask the question she didn't ask. What I dread: that picture on a screen with Randi's blue eyes over it. (Guess from here, marked as such.)

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Pace. Mentioned-only: Sheri, Pace's parents, Vee's folks.

**Heat:** 2 — sustained warm charge, peaking not at the sex but at being arranged kiss by kiss and choosing to be looked at.

**Romance:** 3 — the giving-over: *she gave him herself to keep*, the wrist caught only to say *I like you exactly like this*, hands held at the counter saying nothing.

**Motifs & images:** Feeding, and now *tasting* — the spoon-tastes, the watched first bite, explicitly named as his doing (ch 8, 11, 18, 21). "That look on your face when you take the first bite" — he told Randi almost the same thing in ch 24 about what Vee does to a room. Mirrors: Vee putting her face back on (repeat — Randi ch 1, Vee ch 14/21/27/30), and Pace refusing it, as he did with Randi. The scar, still unasked (repeat, ch 18, 25, and last chapter). Hair in his hands (repeat — the nape gather ch 29, the scrunchies). Wine in the pan. The door that won't open: *I don't go back* (repeat of the closing at *parents*, ch 25). First sighting: the photograph, the shutter, *Delivered*.

**Symbolism:** The photograph — her, made portable, in his keeping, promised about only in its narrowest form. The posing: he sets her limbs and kisses each placement, the shoe-and-dress lesson again (being given no say makes her surer). The lemon zest taken without the pith — precision that leaves the bitter part behind.

**Characterization:** Both deepened. Vee: newly able to *taste*, newly brave, newly good at snuffing her own flicker of suspicion. Pace: exact where it matters — *I'd never share your photograph* is perfectly in character and that's what makes it awful. Nobody went thin; Sheri offstage stayed consistent.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Earned its length. The cooking passage is long and I'd not cut a line; the only slight sag is between the photo and the water glasses, where the adrenaline drops faster than the prose does.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building, after a deliberately quiet stretch (the swim, the drawer, the dorm room). This is the first chapter since the brunch to put a live grenade on the table, and it did it in the middle of the tenderest scene in the book.
