## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came in braced for Vee and got a bait-and-switch I didn't see coming — the woman being undressed in this room is not named until "There you are, Randi," and I made an actual noise at that line. Before it, I'd been reading her as the jacket's twenty-one-year-old: sorority, monogrammed overnight bag, apples portioned against a mother who lives on egg whites. The jacket told me Randi was the glamorous friend who *steers*, and instead I got Randi facedown, begging, crying on leather without knowing she was crying. That recolors the blurb hard. Whatever the plan is, the woman who has one is the one who just came apart.

Pace is the surprise of the chapter for me, because I expected to be watching for the hook and instead he's — genuinely lovely? Alarmingly so. The apple sliced thin, cut sides up, *because he had noticed that she ate them faster when the cut sides showed*. The water bottle left in advance. The cashmere blanket she brought in week two because she gets cold. "Tell me your word." "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset." He turns his chair when she circles a finger, and *being directed by her pleased him. He kept that off his face, mostly.* That last clause is where I fell for him and also where the small hair stood up: he curates what she sees of him, too. And the Daphne paragraph — a door he didn't respect once, that cost more than he knew anything could cost — is doing something enormous with about four sentences. He has been *educated* into this patience. I trust the patience and I don't know what it's built on top of.

The heat worked on me start to finish, which I did not expect from a chapter this technically detailed. The apple game is the best erotic writing in it — bite, kiss, bite, and then his hands going *up, not lower*, each pass turning back a little higher and leaving before it arrives, until "You're doing that on purpose," and "Doing what?" and he does it again. I was grinning and squirming. The undressing from one knee, pressing his face to her stomach through warm silk before a single button. Her hands taken off him twice and then putting them behind her back *herself*, so that "it became, without her deciding it, an offering." The bench-reading is its own slow burn — she reads the thing part by part, finds the handholds, finds *no place at all* where a surface should be, and her head goes back and her throat bares before she's touched. "That's not a table." "Not only a table." "You said it was a table." "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you." That exchange is the most charged dialogue in the chapter and it's about carpentry.

The edging sequence peaked me and also slid, once, toward inventory — right, then left, right, then left, the pink deepening — and around the eighth pair I felt the prose start narrating a procedure. It pulls back up sharply at the two *pleases*: the first one "still being said in the voice she said *please* in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water," the second one *from below*. That distinction is the whole chapter and the page earns it without explaining too much. "Good girl" landed on me like a struck bell. I'll admit the interior essay in the bathroom — *the face took the being-looked-at... what was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing* — is the author's thumb a little visible; it tells me in clean paragraphs what her body had already told me in a flinch and a locked door. But it's beautiful thumb, and I didn't resent it much, because it ends somewhere genuinely cold: *He could have the face.* That's a woman deciding never to be seen again, immediately after the best experience of her life. And she presses the bottle cap under her thumbnail without knowing she's doing it — the one detail that scared me.

The tenderness/dread split at the end is the finest thing here. He is sitting there *quietly happy about her*, moved by the girl who let someone feed her, resolving to answer her hunger as often as she brings it — and she is upstairs of him in her own head, sealing the door, "quiet as a deadbolt going over." She locks his front door first thing, before anything else, every time. She locked the bathroom door for the first time ever. The hum in her ribs "the size of a key turning in a lock." I've got no suspicion of *Pace* yet — the page hasn't given me one, only that he enjoys knowing her better than she knows herself — but I'm frightened of what Randi does with this, and the jacket's "game" now reads as something she might build out of tonight rather than something she built before it. That's a guess from here, nothing more.

Titles: **"The Bench"** is plainly literal and then quietly not — an object he *built*, disguised as a table, that she'd been laying her underwear on for weeks, and that goes back to being furniture in the morning while she walks past without looking. It gave nothing away in advance; it deepened after. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**: the spoon is the sup-with-the-devil proverb, and after this chapter I don't know who's holding the long handle, which is exciting. "Polite" now has teeth — everything tonight was asked, checked, offered. The blurb's closing line, *Every yes was freely given. That was the problem*, is already true of a chapter where nothing was taken.
