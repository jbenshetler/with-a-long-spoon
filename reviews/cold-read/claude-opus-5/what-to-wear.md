# Cold read (grounded) — What to Wear

*scene: scenes/what-to-wear.md · model: claude-opus-5 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch012 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one is the sound of a girl being happy in public, and I loved it and I could not enjoy it cleanly, because I know what's in the middle of the room at that house.

Vee at the corner table, gold light down the west windows, laughing so the next table wants in — that's the best I've seen her. What struck me is that she's *good* at this. She has friends I hadn't met (Kayla is new to me, curls and an unbroken stream, funny and instantly real), she has a table, she has a boy she turned down in September who still waves and whom she defends: "He took it like a gentleman. He's nice." Vee is kind in small unwatched ways, and I keep noticing it.

And the retelling — the shirt, again, third time now (the truck, then Cassie, now here), polished a little brighter each pass: "I could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care." That's a wonderful line about how a girl makes a good thing into a possession. The detail that undid me was the shirt "folded on her pillow at this exact hour." She didn't say that to the table. That one was for her.

The confession under the noise is the chapter's real heat: *once*. Twelve days, and she knew the number without counting, and it's killing her. "He's not pushing. It's the opposite. He asks me first... and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it." Then: "I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." That's exactly right about her and it's exactly what was built into her on the bench-adjacent evidence — the *may I* vocabulary working on someone who has never been asked. It's arousing precisely because she can't account for it. I felt the ache of that, not the tease of it.

And then the phone. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." No *for what*. I got a genuine jolt — my stomach dropped and my pulse went up with hers, and the two feelings were not the same feeling. Hers is the light stupid quick thing she lets herself have. Mine is that I have been in that house. I have seen the long table in the middle of the room with its top put back on, "full of what it was… waiting for the next time." Kayla, who knows nothing, reads the summons correctly on the first pass: "Two in the afternoon is not dinner. It's not lunch either... He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes." The room full of girls decoding a text is the oldest comedy there is, and here it doubles as the most accurate reading anyone has made, and neither Kayla nor Vee knows how accurate.

Suspicion: nothing new landed on Pace here, and that's its own unease — the whole chapter is testimony *for* him, given by a happy witness, and every word of it is true. He did stand in the rain. He does wait for the real answer. What's off isn't him at this table; it's that Randi and Cassie are both absent from it. Cassie isn't mentioned once. Randi isn't mentioned once. Vee has a whole other set of friends who don't know those names, and the thing that's about to happen to her is being workshopped by people with no data. Meg's "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny... Sometimes they even are" is the closest thing to a warning in the chapter, and Vee "let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." That line is the thumb I felt most — but it's a light thumb, and it earned itself, because it's a true thing a friend says.

Friction: very little. It's a talk chapter and it knows it; it's short, warm, well-paced, and the jokes land ("that's not a message, that's a ransom note"). Minor drag: the powerlifting reveal is a bit of an inventory beat, a spec sheet handed over — though I'll take it, because "I'd just spent a whole mountain climbing up behind him, so I'd had the time to work it out" is very funny and very her.

The title. **"What to Wear"** is doing three things at once and I felt all three. It's Kayla's practical panic — he didn't say. It's the russet silk dress from the mountain, the thing she wanted out loud and then set back on the shelf, which I am now certain is going to come back (guess from here: he bought it, or he will). And third, colder: on that hike she learned to be dressed *by* him — boot relaced, menu chosen — and the answer to what to wear may turn out to be *nothing*, or *what he lays out*. The title didn't give anything away; it recolored on the last page. The book's cover line, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, finally rings like a bell here: this chapter *is* the polite invitation, twelve words with no subject in them, and the spoon proverb says you sup with the devil at arm's length. Vee is being invited to the table without one.

What I want: for her to have the afternoon she thinks she's getting. What I dread: Saturday at two, and where Randi is at two.

**Romance:** 2 — the shirt story retold as a treasure, "he's different," the flannel folded on her pillow; devotion recounted rather than enacted.

**Motifs & images:** *The shirt* (repeat — the truck, Ch "Leave No Trace"; Cassie's room, "Rye"; now the table, and folded on her pillow). *Food and being fed* (repeat — she's at a dining hall, eating easily, stealing fries; no mother's voice anywhere in this chapter, which is new and lovely). *"May I" / being asked* (repeat — "he asks me first... and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it"). *Gold room / the good light* (repeat of the window-table motif: Chi Latte, Smoothie Bird — but here for the first time it's a table she belongs at without being set on a shelf). *Doors* (repeat and escalated — "never once been through his door, and now there was a door, and Saturday behind it"). *The russet dress* (repeat from the summit, invoked by the title, not by the text). *The tree kiss, the fire lane, the siren* (repeats as anecdote). First sighting: Kayla; Theo; Kayla's "bring a change of clothes."

**Symbolism:** The unsigned invitation — the least information she's ever received, read a thousand times; a blank the reader can fill and she can't. "Two o'clock" as a time with no meal in it: an appointment disguised as a social call, the way the bench was disguised as a table.

**Characterization:** Vee deepened — she's socially fluent, generous about Theo, self-aware enough to hear how her own story sounds ("I can hear it sounding like nothing"). Kayla arrived whole in a page. Meg got promoted from a name to a person with a fork and a dry, kind skepticism. Pace exists here only as testimony, which is its own characterization: everyone who meets his effects loves them.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Brisk, right-sized; the banter earns its length and the text lands at exactly the point the scene would otherwise start repeating.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building hard. "Rye" was the debrief, this is the summons — two low-stakes talk chapters in a row is the most stillness the book has run, but the text at the end is a starter pistol and I'd have turned the page immediately.
