## Reader reaction

Four pages and I'm sitting up. This is the shortest thing the book has given me and it's one of the hottest, and the heat is almost entirely made of a woman wanting a *report*.

The opening got me before I'd decided to be got: the deadbolt going over before he's off the couch, no hello, no back-and-forth at the door, "she gave him no room to back off." After the bench chapter, where he ran everything at his own unhurried pace, watching Randi arrive already halfway up the stairs of her own need is a real reversal, and the page lets it be one. Then the wine. He'd poured one glass, red, an hour early, and *didn't pour himself one* — "Everything he was going to have of it he was going to have like this." She drinks, looks at him over the rim, comes down and kisses it into his mouth. "the tannin catching at the inside of his lips, the taste of it drawn across his tongue by hers, her mouth already darker for it." I felt that in my own mouth. It's the single most erotic image in the book so far for me, more than the bench, because it's *transferred* — he only gets it through her, and he prefers it that way.

And then that one word. She takes his hands off her hips, puts them under her top and closes his fingers over her breasts — the exact callback to the kitchen chair where he spent all those minutes not taking them, now weaponized — leans in and breathes "***Tell.***" into his ear. She has learned what he likes and is spending it. He laughs into her shoulder because he knows and likes that she knows. That's the closest these two have come to meeting as equals, and it's over in a paragraph.

What I can't stop turning over: "She built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop." He offers it as a charming detail about Vee. It lands on the woman who eats thin apple slices with the cut sides up and said *No. I can't eat that* to a cheeseburger without looking at it. And the page gives me her body first — breasts pressing harder into his palms, "the insides of her thighs going tight against his and staying tight" — before her face catches up a half-beat late. I don't know what that is. I genuinely don't. Desire for Vee, or hunger to *be* someone who can build a cup like that, or both fused so tight they can't be pulled apart. Pace reads it as one clean thing — "she had come here tonight wanting to hear about a girl" — and he may be right, and the page does not confirm him. That gap is where my whole reading pressure is now.

Then it turns. "Did she make you laugh?" "No. I made her laugh." And Randi goes quiet — "her thighs did not tighten now; they set" — and the smile arrives "a beat after it was called for." Pace reads it as wanting held, and it moves him. I read it through the bathroom mirror, where she decided he could have the face and never the rest, and I felt cold in the middle of a hot scene. He is being tender toward a performance and calling it her restraint. *"She's fun. You'll like her." "I already do,"* bright, and then she drains the glass in one swallow. That swallow is the whole chapter.

And he keeps something back — Vee's embarrassing true thing on the froyo walk. "It was hers." I loved him for it and it chilled me at the same time. He's already curating a private channel with Vee and handing Randi the edited version, while believing himself perfectly straight with everybody. Nobody in this book is lying outright and everyone is withholding, and I think that's the design of the hurt coming.

My feelings have moved: I *like* Pace less easily than I did two chapters ago. Not because he's done anything cruel — he hasn't, he's the most careful man I've read in ages — but because his confidence in his own reading of people is starting to look like the thing that will do the damage. Randi I'm frightened for and increasingly fascinated by; she's the most alive character in the book and the least visible to anyone in it. Vee isn't on the page and is all over it, which is its own kind of dread — she's the warmest person here and she's being discussed as a dish.

No friction to report, honestly. It's too short to drag and it earns every line. The only place I raised an eyebrow is "he had known she would come; he had known since about the second block of the walk home" — a small flex of authorial certainty on his behalf, though it's consistent with how he thinks.

**Tannin** — perfect after the fact. The dry astringent catch, the thing that grips the mouth and dries it, the taste he only ever gets secondhand. Also what makes a wine keep, and what makes it bitter before its time. It gave nothing away going in; it recolored everything coming out. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* — I keep hearing the proverb about supping with the devil, and I no longer know which of these three is holding the spoon. "A polite invitation" now reads straight at Pace's *May I call you?* — the courtliest possible opening move into something nobody is going to survive politely. No tagline or jacket copy was supplied to me beyond that line.

What I want next: Vee and Randi in a room with Pace in it. What I dread: Randi handing Vee to him as a gift and discovering what she actually wanted only after it's unrecoverable. *(Guess from here, nothing more.)*
