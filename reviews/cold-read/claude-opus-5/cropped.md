## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

A quiet one, and after the pizza-and-flannel warmth of last chapter I was glad of the cold air. Two girls, trays, a cafeteria that isn't theirs, Sunday night when everyone comes back. And the whole chapter turns out to be about editing — which is the thing I've been watching Vee do for twenty chapters without her ever once saying the word out loud. Here she says it in the first minute: *I cropped it before I sent it. The version they have stops at the waist.*

That landed on me harder than any of the sex has lately. Because I remember where the uncropped frame went. Her mother got the shoulders and a hand drifting to her own collarbone. Her father got the dress and a slow mental pricing of it. Randi got the whole thing — legs, toes, everything — because Vee refused to be a girl who flinches. The people who made her get the top half; the woman who pointed at her across a lawn gets all of it. Nobody in the chapter says that and I doubt Vee has thought it once, and it sat in my chest the entire read.

Her father is the best thing here, and he's not even in the room. The little inventory of what he could take and what he couldn't — hiking, yes; a doctorate, yes; concrete and a boom box before the dance hall, *very* yes; the house, no, he'd start hunting the catch — and then the line I actually put my hand over my mouth at: *my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would.* And then the turn under it, which is sharper than the joke: *weird is worse than wrong. Wrong is something he understands and rejects.* That's a real girl knowing her real father, with love in it and no illusions, and it's the most grown-up Vee has sounded all book. Also the burned rolls. *On purpose, I'm pretty sure.* I liked him instantly and I suspect he's going to break my heart eventually.

Cassie got something this chapter I've been wanting for her: an inside. A sister, a new baby, a dorky brother-in-law, parents who don't quite see her as an adult yet — *not overtly, but I can tell.* Ten chapters of four-words-a-day and here she is with a whole small life she's never bothered anyone with. And then she does the thing only she does. Not *who is he*, not *are you okay* — **"So what is he. Not to your dad. To you."** And when Vee can't answer: *"Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?"* That question is a scalpel and Cassie holds it perfectly steady, and then — *she didn't push at it* — puts it down. My feeling about her hasn't moved so much as hardened: she is the only person in this book who asks without an angle, and she is being handed less and less to work with.

The last beat is the one that got me. *Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not.* Oh, that's true. She wanted to be pushed. She has wanted to be pushed for a while now — she wanted Pace to fight her over the five nights too, and he didn't, and *the plain grace of that had cost her more than a fight would have.* Same shape twice in two chapters. Everyone in her life keeps opening the door and waiting on the far side, and what she actually wants is someone to reach in and take the thing out of her hands. Randi is the only person who does that. Randi walks her down question by question until the secret is half out. That's not a coincidence I'm enjoying.

No heat here at all, and I don't hold it against the chapter; it's a breather and it's doing real work. But I felt the shape of it — Vee gives a long uninterrupted aria about her father while Cassie eats. A few paragraphs run as continued speech with no tag, and there's a stretch where it stops being a conversation and becomes an essay on Midwestern paternal epistemology. Very good essay. Slightly thin as a scene. And *"I'm not ashamed of it. The opposite, if anything"* is the one line I'd cut — she's already shown me that; saying it flattens it into a thesis statement.

The title told me almost nothing going in and quite a lot coming out: I assumed "cropped" meant a haircut or a field, and then it turned out to be the photograph, and then it turned out to be *her*, the version of herself that stops at the waist for anyone who might be hurt by the rest. No spoiler in it, all payload. The volume board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* — still hums quietly; I know how the spoon proverb ends even if nobody in the book has said it, and "a polite invitation" fits everything about how Vee was approached: a door opened, courteously, and she walked through it herself. No tagline in my packet.

What I want next: Thanksgiving at Pace's house, which I was promised and didn't get — Sheri, the store-bought pie, and whatever those two said about Vee and Randi with no one to perform for. And I'm braced for the anthro class in spring, and for the outlier to come back. *From here, a guess only:* Vee's own father, proud and pricing the dress, is going to meet Pace, and I dread it the way you dread a good man walking into a room full of things nobody's told him.
