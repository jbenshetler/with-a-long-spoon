## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Oh, this one got me in the throat. First chapter in a long while from inside Pace's house with *Randi* in it instead of Vee, and the first thing I noticed is how little he says and how much she can't stop saying. "Everywhere else she filled the air… In his house the machine switched off… But tonight it had been running since the kitchen counter." That single observation reframed the whole evening before the sex even restarted: she came here loaded, carrying something she'd been holding for two days, and she needed four orgasms' worth of cover to get it out.

The stats-class story is the most deliciously awful thing in the chapter. Randi leaning over to tell Vee she'd come to class still full of him — I felt that go through me the way it went through Vee, and I also felt the small cruelty in it, the pleasure Randi takes in making her go that color. And then the tell, tossed off like nothing: "I got my hand in it once in October, before I knew better, and I have not been the same about it since." *Before I knew better.* She's been in trouble since October and she's dating it precisely, which is what people do with the thing they can't stop thinking about.

The interrogation is where I sat up. Pace asking "How did you know, that day? … How did you know to pick her," and his answer — *you taught me, your hand went cold, your breath changed, I was reading you and not them* — landed on me as both the most romantic and the most chilling thing he's said. He never chose Vee at all; he read Randi and aimed. And then, right after: "Is she all right?" / "Is she happy?" / "She is **radiant**." / "That isn't the same answer." He *is* checking the gauge, and he's the only one in this book who asks that question, and he gets it wrong anyway — because he's asking Randi about Vee when the person coming apart in front of him is Randi. "Stop checking the gauge. I know exactly what I'm doing. I have *always* known what I'm doing." Nobody says that twice unless it's stopped being true.

The sex ran *hot* on me, and specifically because of what it was being used for. Her turning to face the back of the couch so he couldn't see her face — "she had forgotten the framed mirror on the wall past the end of the couch, which gave him her face the entire time" — is the best-constructed erotic move in the book so far. She hid, and she didn't, and I got to watch her not know it. The clench arriving on the word *mouth*, involuntary, "she had not decided to do it and could not have stopped it, and he felt all of it happen from the inside." That's the whole book in one sentence. And the duel underneath it: "I'm doing it for *you*." / "I didn't ask who it was for." / "It's not a treat, it's *work*—" / "Sounds grueling." He is laughing at her and he is also handing her the exit she doesn't want, and when he says the kind, lethal thing — "You don't have to do it for me" — she comes on it. "Oh, but I'm *doing the work*." God.

And then: "God, I want her," into the wood, with nothing around it. "She heard it leave her. He felt her hear it." Then the immediate walk-back, "For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant," and the need for him to co-sign it: "Aren't you?" I don't believe her for a second, and I don't think she does either. What moved for me this chapter is Randi — she's no longer the hand on the wheel, she's a woman describing a goodbye kiss on a sidewalk in a voice that's come "down out of its own register," and the detail she chose to carry home was that Vee's lipstick goes soft at the edge. That's not a campaign. That's being in love.

The ending undid me more than the sex did. After ch 1, where she locked that bathroom door and decided he would never get the real woman again — here she is saying "You see more of me than anybody ever has… It makes me feel *real*. And some nights it frightens me." She breached her own vow and I don't think she noticed. And his "Then I'll be careful with you," which he means entirely, from a man who built this entire arrangement by pointing at a girl across a lawn. Then "Say something nice to me" — the most naked line she's ever had — and he *thinks about it, because she would know the difference*, and gives her "You are extraordinary," and she holds his forearm and pulls it tighter, and tighter again. I ached. Both of them are being tender inside a machine neither will turn off.

Friction: almost none. The one beat I noticed the author's hand on was "which is a girl who left somewhere late and did the rest in the car" — a touch too neatly deduced for conversation, though it's in character for her to perform cleverness at him. No suspicion that's new; what's new is sympathy. **"Four"** meant nothing before I read it and now it's perfect and small — her counting out loud in his house, "That's more than three," and the fourth one arriving slow and crossing over into hurting. A tally as a title, and a man keeping his own numbers like his lifts. Against the cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* — the long spoon says you can sup with the devil if you keep your distance, and this chapter is three people steadily shortening the spoon. "A warm reception" now reads double: the thermostat up two hours early, for whoever's coming.

What I want next: Vee, and whether Randi can keep sitting across from her. What I dread: Randi deciding to speed it up because she can't stand the waiting.
