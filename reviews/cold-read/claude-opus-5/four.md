## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

So this is the room I've been outside of for forty chapters, and it is worse and better than I'd braced for. Pace's POV, Randi folded into the end of his couch in dark red silk, the deadbolt already over, the heat up two hours early, three times already done — and the whole conversation is *Vee*. Not as a target. As the thing neither of them can stop talking about. Randi has come carrying a Monday-morning detail for two days the way you carry a present: Vee walked into stats with wet hair pinned half-up and a stolen coffee and a grin, and Randi tucked her hair behind her ear "because it was an excuse and I took it," and then leaned over and said the filthy thing just to watch her go red. I felt that in my stomach. It's affection and it's a hand on a lever and I genuinely cannot get the two apart, and I've stopped believing I'm supposed to.

The answer to the big question lands, and it's not the answer I expected. He didn't read Vee. He read *Randi* — the slack hand, the changed breath on the library-steps girl, the fast *God, no*. And then the paragraph he keeps to himself: that he'd already chosen her out of those doors with the wind in her hair, that he built the picture of the two of them on his pillows, Randi narrow and bound against all that sprawl, himself kissing Vee with his hand on Randi's face — and that he went hard on a public lawn with Randi's hand in his. "He had already chosen; the pointing was only to find out whether they had chosen the same." That's the first time I've caught Pace *withholding*, and the withheld thing is small and specific: he never pointed at a woman he didn't want, and he let her believe it was her game. My unease about him has always been about accounting, and here is the ledger with a line scratched off it.

And yet — and this is why I can't put the book down — he's also the only person in the chapter who says the thing out loud. "She consents — every time — or I'm done." Then, before Randi can take it personally: "It isn't you I'm checking on." I believe him. I also notice he asked "Is she all right?" and got "She is *radiant*," and said, flatly, "That isn't the same answer." He knows the difference between those two questions and he keeps asking anyway, and he keeps not stopping. The chapter lets both facts stand without resolving them, and I'm grateful it didn't tidy it.

Randi destroyed me. She turns to face the back of the couch "as though the position were a thing the sentence required" — because she's about to describe kissing Vee and cannot have her face looked at — and she forgets the mirror, so he has it the whole time. Then the goodbye kiss, told from the inside: the hug, a foot shorter, *her hair in my face, she runs so warm, you could stand in it*. The drawing-back. "And she waits." And the clench arriving on the word *mouth*, which "she had not decided to do and could not have stopped," and he felt all of it from the inside. That is the hottest thing in this book so far and it is made almost entirely of tenderness. Then the defense — "I'm doing it for *you*… somebody has to get her used to my mouth… it's not a treat, it's *work*" — and his terrible gentle "You don't have to do it for me," and her "but I'm *doing the work*," and coming on it. And later, face down in the wood with nothing around it: "God, I want her." She *heard it leave her.* He felt her hear it.

And the counting. "That's more than three." "More than three what?" "Nothing." She's measuring her day against Vee's brunch report — twice, three times a night — and she said *I'm inspired by her*, and I went cold in the middle of being turned on. She's competing with the girl she loves, for the man she's giving her to, and the scoreboard is her own body. Also "Well. She told me first," about the mouth-asleep line — flat little flare of ownership she couldn't swallow. And "That's the one that got me. Twenty-one years old and she found out this autumn that a person can sleep." She is not performing there. She is grieving over something good.

The end is where it turned romantic on me against my will. She pulls his arm tighter and then tighter again. "You see more of me than anybody ever has… It makes me feel real. And some nights it frightens me." "Then I'll be careful with you." And then she *asks* — "Say something nice to me" — which is the same woman who flinched at "You're beautiful" on the bench and went into the bathroom and shut the door on herself. She's figured out she can take a compliment if she commissions it. He thinks about it, "because she would know the difference," and gives her "You are extraordinary," and she keeps hold of his arm a long time. I don't think she came out from behind the face. I think she rented a window.

The title: "Four." The fourth time, worked for until it crossed over into hurting and he stayed in it anyway. Also four nos on the lawn, and four people now — Vee, Randi, Pace, and the Vee who exists in these two people's mouths, who may be the one they're both actually in love with. It gave nothing away beforehand and recolored nicely after. *With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception* — I still only have the spoon as a warning about eating with the devil, and *A Warm Reception* has gone bitter on me: this house is warm two hours before anyone arrives, and the reception is a selection.

What I want next: Vee, badly, and some innocence to stand in. What I dread: that the next thing out of Randi's mouth to Vee won't be a tucked strand of hair.
