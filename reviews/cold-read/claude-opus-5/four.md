This is the first time in a long while I've been inside Pace's head, and it does something I wasn't ready for: it makes him *smaller*. Not worse — smaller. He's not running anything tonight. He's lying there taking inventory of a woman who is coming apart in his house while telling him she isn't. "I didn't," he says, when she asks how he knew to point at Vee. "I pointed. I watched your face." I believed him completely, and it rearranged the whole origin of this story for me. I'd been carrying chapter three as his scheme with her consent. It wasn't. It was her appetite and his willingness to be the instrument of it. He was the pointing finger. She was the one who said yes.

And Randi — God. She came in with a question she'd been carrying, asked it, got an answer she couldn't do anything with ("Hm," and she let it lie), and then talked for the rest of the night about Vee's hair. "I got my hand in it once in September, before I knew better, and I have not been the same about it since." *Before I knew better.* She knows. She has known since September. The thing that undid me wasn't the sex, it was "She told me that one." / "Did she?" / "Well. She told me first." That's not strategy. That's a woman staking a claim on a sentence. Same with "Is she happy?" — "She is *radiant.*" "That isn't the same answer." Pace I trust and don't respect tonight, which is new. He hears her count the fourth time and is privately pleased with himself about his own capacity, which is such an ordinary young-man thing that it almost reassured me. He is not the spider. He's a very kind man with excellent hands and a catastrophic blind spot, and he has now heard, in detail, the best thing in Randi's week, and filed it under preparation.

The ending gutted me. "Say something nice to me." He thinks about it, *because she would know the difference*, and gives her "You were extraordinary tonight" — a compliment about performance, which is the only kind she can hold. Chapter one he said "You're beautiful" and she flinched and went and locked the bathroom door. She's holding onto the arm of the man she's decided can never reach her, because the woman she actually wants gets a hug on a sidewalk once a week. No suspicion from me this chapter — only pity, and a creeping certainty that she's the one who detonates.
