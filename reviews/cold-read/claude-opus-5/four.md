# Cold read (grounded) — Four

## Reader reaction

**Four.** I counted with her, and then I understood what the title was counting, and then I didn't like how much I'd enjoyed getting there.

This is the first time in a long while I've been inside Pace's head rather than Vee's, and it did something to me I resent a little: it made him more sympathetic and more frightening in the same sixty seconds. He answers Randi's "how did you know to pick her" honestly — her hand going slack, the breath change on the library-steps girl, the fast *God, no* — and that's genuinely impressive reading of a person. But the part he *doesn't* say is the part I now have to carry: "he had never once pointed at a woman he did not want," and "He had already chosen; the pointing was only to find out whether they had chosen the same." So the game Randi thought she was running, and that I thought she was running, she was being walked through. He went hard on a public lawn with her hand in his, holding a picture of the two women on his pillows. That's not a man assembling a gift for his girlfriend. That's a man who'd already set the table and let her think she picked the chairs. And then — "She consents — every time — or I'm done. It isn't you I'm checking on." He says it low instead of loud, which is his whole character, and it's the most honest and most self-serving line in the book so far. He has an airtight audit of the consent he's actually asking for and no audit at all of the one he isn't. I believe he believes himself. That's the problem.

Randi wrecked me. She came in running her mouth — the machine she usually switches off in that house was going from the kitchen counter — and I clocked it before Pace did. The stats story (Vee arriving at eleven with wet hair and a steel cup and a grin she couldn't hide; Randi tucking her hair back *because it was an excuse and I took it*; leaning over to tell her what she was still full of) is the most naked she's been about what she's doing, and it made me hot and then it made me cold, because Vee lived through that and has never once mentioned it to me. There's a whole Monday Vee isn't telling. Then: "I got my hand in it once in October, before I knew better, and I have not been the same about it since." And "She told me that one." "Did she? … Well. She told me first." That's jealousy of *Pace*, over Vee. And "Saturdays are mine." She isn't managing an appetite anymore. She's competing.

The sex is the best-constructed thing in the chapter and I say that as someone who was thoroughly worked on by it. He pulls the goodbye kiss out of her a sentence at a time, and the pacing is diabolical — she turns around and kneels facing the couch back *mid-sentence*, "as though the position were a thing the sentence required," which is the truest thing on the page: she has put her face where he can't reach it so she can say it. And she forgot the mirror. He has her face the entire time. She never looks back at him once. The clench arriving on the word *mouth*, before she decided anything — that's the same body that answered his threesome question in chapter three, answering again, and this time about Vee specifically. Peak for me: "I'm doing it for *you*." "I didn't ask who it was for." And then she comes on "You don't have to do it for me," mid-protest, with *but I'm doing the work* — that's funny and devastating at once, the lie collapsing under its own orgasm. "God, I want her," said down into the wood with nothing around it, and the half-second where she hears it leave and reels it back — "For us. When she's ready" — ached.

And then the last page undid me. The blanket arriving before she reaches for it. Her hauling his arm tighter, and tighter again. "You see more of me than anybody ever has. It makes me feel real. And some nights it frightens me." That's the woman from the bench, briefly out, without her deciding to come out. "Then I'll be careful with you," and he means it entirely, and he cannot be, structurally. Then "Say something nice to me," and he *thinks about it, because she would know the difference*, and doesn't say *beautiful* — the word she flinched at in chapter one — and gives her "You are extraordinary," which she takes. He knows her well enough to route around the wound and not well enough to know she locked a door on him months ago. That's a tenderness I can't fault and can't trust, and I don't think those are in conflict either.

Friction: almost none. If I'm picking, the gauge exchange ("so you can stop checking the gauge. I know exactly what I'm doing. I have always known what I'm doing") is the one place the thesis gets a little close to the surface — Randi declaring her own competence three ways in a row reads slightly like the author making sure I noticed she's lost it. But she's also a woman who talks when she's rattled, so it mostly holds. And I still don't know how much of Pace's "I'd have taken you right there on the quad" is kindness and how much is pocketing her again.

The title: *Four* is the count — "That's more than three," his fourth, the one that had to be worked for until it crossed over into hurting and he stayed in it anyway. But four is also how many of them there are now in that room: him, her, the girl on the pillow in his head, and the woman behind Randi's face who got let out at the end. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it got better after. The cover board, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION*, is sitting much heavier now — he keeps the house warm hours ahead of whoever's coming, and the reception is warm, and the spoon is for something you don't eat with your hands.

What I want next: Vee's side of that Monday. And I dread the Saturday Randi can't hold it in a booth.
