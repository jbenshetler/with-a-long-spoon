# Cold read (grounded) — All the Time

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one ran on me like a slow warm hand. It's just brunch — no dress, no swat, no afternoon that turns — and Vee says so out loud, which I loved: "There's not — it's not a thing that happened. It's more like. It's just how it *is* now." That's the chapter's whole nerve. She's been keeping her happiness in installments, in scenes she can hand over, and now it's become weather. "The amount of it." I got a dumb grin reading her try to describe him and fail — *he takes forever*, *he's strong*, *a minute later he's so gentle I could cry*, *I don't know how one person is both* — and then the small panic under it: *am I turning into some kind of sex pervert?* Randi's answer is the best line she's ever been given: "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud… You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*." That undid me a little. The mother's voice has been the loudest thing in Vee's head since chapter one, and someone finally said the opposite sentence back to her in daylight. Absolution from the person doing this to her. That's the book in one bite.

And then the yolk. The egg breaking and running gold over everything and Vee's hand going back for more before she decided anything, and "this is the best part. The middle, when it goes everywhere." I laughed, and then I didn't, because she's been fed and fed and fed through thirty chapters and the hollow keeps widening — and she's ordering off the left side of the menu now, on a card with nothing behind it, and she put it down before Randi's hand could drift. She signed and "said nothing about the number." That sat cold in an otherwise sunlit scene.

The moment I'm still holding is Randi's. Vee reaches for the unnameable thing — "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around" — and Randi doesn't answer, and her eyes go bright and unfixed, and there's a catch in her breath she doesn't quite smooth over, and she says, low, "It doesn't." Oh, God. Vee reads it as a good story landing well and goes warm with pride. I read it as the bench. That is the exact thing Randi got once, on a padded table in a too-warm house, and decided in a bathroom mirror never to have again — being wanted past the face. Her own lover's own method, described back to her in the mouth of the girl she picked off a quad, and she can't hold her face. Then the beam comes up full and she cracks a joke about his playlist, and I wanted to put my hand through the page. She is starving in public and the only person who could see it is congratulating herself on being moving.

The photo. I felt the whole choreography of it in my stomach: the two-week-old "dusty" pictures, the "You don't have to show me" that slides the phone *back* — that withdrawn door again, the exact ch-17 move — and Vee's thumb finding the cropped one before the rest of her votes. Then "you only gave him *half*?" and Vee, refusing to be the girl who flinched, handing over the whole uncropped length of herself. "I gave him *all* of it." And Randi looks past what a verdict needs, and lands on "Showing him your toes, eh?" — the shoe-whore thread pulled tight, the frosted plum, the hand on the bare foot in the boutique. The heat in that exchange is real and it's also awful, because I'm sitting there with the thing Vee doesn't know: Pace promised he'd never share her photograph, and he didn't have to, because she carried it to his other lover herself. Nobody broke a promise. That's worse.

Trust: no new suspicion, just the old one getting heavier, and my sympathy for Randi getting heavier with it. She's not running a con this morning — she's not extracting, she's not steering, she genuinely forgets her drink. The only engineered thing is the anthro section, and *Vee* proposed it, and Randi said "Done. Obviously" before it cost her a thought, and then "Just us" came out with "a small roughness under it, there and gone." Two nights a week, seven to eight-twenty, that Vee's weekend can't touch. I don't think she planned that. I think she grabbed it. And the goodbye — Vee leaning up the last half-inch *after* Randi, taking a second for herself, "noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." That's the first time the kiss has been hers. Randi holds her eyes past what a goodbye needs. Something has quietly changed hands and neither of them will name it.

Friction: almost none. If anything I wanted one honest line of Randi eating — last week's single neat bite was such a good cold detail that "They ate" felt like a missed beat. And "I used to be a person with thoughts" is a tiny bit modern-sitcom against the rest of Vee's voice, though I forgave it because it made me laugh.

**Titles.** "All the Time" read at first as the amount — *we have so much sex, Randi*, two, three, mornings and afternoons. By the end it had turned over: it's Randi's ration. One hug to carry her the whole week. Saturdays set down in a dark green room for the small news of a life that happens somewhere else. Vee has him all the time; Randi has this, and just signed up for Tuesdays and Thursdays. The book title, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, keeps promising a devil at a supper table, and this chapter is nothing but supper and invitation — the politest possible version, both women reaching across the cloth with no spoon at all.

What I want next: that anthro class. And I dread, cleanly and specifically, the day Randi has to look at that photograph again in a room where Pace is.
