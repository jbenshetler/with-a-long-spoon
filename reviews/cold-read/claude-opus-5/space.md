# Reader reaction — "Space"

Pace alone in a cold garage on a Friday, truing tenons with shavings up his forearms, is such a quietly devastating open. He didn't know what to do with the Friday. He said he wouldn't text her, and he hasn't, and rather than sit with the empty evening he went and made something fit. "The garage was cold and the work was warm and there was nothing in his head but the fit." I felt tender toward him in a way I haven't quite before — not admiring, protective. And then the engine on the gravel, which is not a Friday sound, and the deadbolt going over in somebody else's hand.

What got me first was Randi arriving *pleased and letting it show* — "she had never once come through that door pleased and let it show like this." After "Portion," where she drew one line through the sauce and poured her own wine and rode him with her eyes shut, this landed as relief, and I distrusted the relief while feeling it. Because the source of the gladness is Vee. She's happy because Vee cried in her desk chair at nine in the morning. And then she takes that morning to bed and *spends* it — narrating the black lace bra, walking all the way around her, taking her time about it, her hand under the long skirt, the thumb in the back of the knee, "She made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it. … I heard it." I was flat-out turned on and simultaneously cold at the back of my neck, and the chapter knows it: the heat and the harvest are the same motion. She is giving him a thing and taking a thing at the same time, and the page says so.

The hottest thing in it isn't the picture of Vee, and Pace clocks that too — it's Randi getting off on her own telling, "enjoying the telling so plainly that it was coming up in her body while she spoke," the grip tightening on every word. That's the peak for me. And directly against it: she pulls him up off her mouth *before* she goes over, hard enough to hurt. "Not like that. Not yet. Lie down." She will come while talking, on top, in charge, delivering — she will not come while being given to. After chapter one's bathroom mirror that read to me as the whole shape of her in one gesture, and it ached. So did the table: she carries her folded clothes to it, keeps her hand flat on the surface, and looks at it, "and it was not the look you give a table." She didn't ask. She's never asked again. Neither of them says the word *bench* and I nearly said it for them.

Pace, meanwhile, holds a line I respect and can't entirely forgive: he won't tell Randi what Vee said at the bar, because "those were his, and that was Vee's." He keeps Vee's confidences while receiving Vee's undressing as pillow talk. He doesn't ask Randi anything about the pleasure she's showing; he doesn't take up what she set down. He's decent inside a frame that isn't. And "Give her a day," repeated, "as if the day were his to give, and he set that down beside the rest" — he noticed. He's keeping a list of things that don't fit, the way he kept one after the untouched bowl. That's the seam I'm watching now: he sorts people faster than they can hide, and he has three or four pieces of Randi that won't go together.

The chill arrives at the end, in a sentence that reads as devotion: "She's going to be so beautiful when she walks in. You wait." Half asleep, "as if she were promising him something." She is producing this reconciliation. She told Vee *not today, tomorrow night*, picked the dress, the neckline, the shoes, the scarf, taught her the knot — and she's telling Pace to wait, she's coming. Both of them think they're leaving Vee room to choose. Vee will walk into that house tomorrow dressed by Randi, certain the choosing was hers. I dread that scene and I want it immediately.

The title. "Space" — nothing in the chapter says the word, and it kept reaching back to the dance: *that's what gets you stepped on, the space you're keeping; close it, come all the way in.* Here it's the space on Pace's Friday that Vee's absence opened and Randi walked into; the space he's leaving Vee on purpose and calling patience; the space Randi keeps around the one part of herself she won't let him have, even naked on top of him. Oblique but it hums, and it didn't give anything away. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** still reads to me as the warning about supping with the devil, and this chapter is the first where I couldn't tell which of the two of them needs the long spoon; the "warm reception" now has a room and a date attached — tomorrow night, at his door. Small friction: "The tit goddess was generous with that girl" tipped a half-step past Randi's register for me, the only line I heard as written rather than said.
