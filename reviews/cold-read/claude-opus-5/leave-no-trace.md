# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

*scene: scenes/leave-no-trace.md · model: claude-opus-5 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch011 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. All the way. I went up that trail braced for something to go wrong and what happened instead was the best hour I've spent with these two, and by the end I was sitting in the cab with her not breathing right.

The boot. I want to start with the boot, because that's where the chapter told me what it was going to be. She says "I think — my boot" with half a laugh on it, and he *turns at once*, two steps back down, pack off, and then the thing I didn't expect: her stomach clenching before she knows why, her mother's verdict arriving in advance — *what kind of girl lets a man* — and her mouth already opening to head it off, and he's simply got the boot unlaced already. Peeled the sock. Bare foot in his hand in the cold air. *Hmm.* Moleskin held under his thumb to warm it. Relaced tight at the ankle, loose across the top, a pat on the toe that means only *done*. And then: "Should've done that at the lot. I'm sorry. I didn't think to ask about the boots." He takes the fault for a boot he never saw. Somewhere behind her the clench let go without her catching it go — that's the sentence. That's the whole method of this man in one clause, and I have stopped being able to catch him out. I've tried.

Then the summit, which I did not expect to move me as much as it did: the trees ending all at once, the lake holding the silver sky "flat as a poured thing," the ridges burning, and the thing that undoes her being not the beauty but the indifference — "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it with her heart going and her mouth open, and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it." That's the pool bottom again, in daylight and huge: the one kind of belonging that doesn't require anyone to be looking. And his blue shirt swallowed by it. And him a few steps off, already looking at her face in front of the valley, and *letting her catch him*, and then turning away to give her the rest of the looking alone. God. The horseradish gasp, the water bottle he drinks from where her mouth was, the cherries — the whole food sequence is doing that thing this book does where hunger and being fed are the erotic event. And the pit. She spits it, he says "We take nothing and leave nothing," and her face goes hot and the voice hands her the *no one taught you* word — and he's already reaching for his own cherry, laying his own pit down beside hers, not looking. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." I could have wept at that line. That's the whole grammar of how she's been taught things her entire life, inverted.

The russet dress I'm still holding. She names the color three times trying to get it right, hears the wanting in her own voice, and sets it down herself — "There's a black dress in the back of my closet." And he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress," puts no weight on it, and leaves it lying on the rock between them. I believe him entirely and I also know he is a man who remembers exactly what someone eats and leaves water on a nightstand in advance. *Guess from here:* that dress arrives. I don't know yet whether I'll be glad.

The cab. I'm not going to be coy: the cab is the hottest scene in this book so far, and it's hot because of the shame, not despite it. His only dry shirt handed over with nothing said, and then him getting back out into the rain and standing with his back to the truck, shoulders set, blue going dark — "His shirt, and then the truck. Both." Then her wanting, arriving in two layers: *I want him in here with me,* and under it, slower, *I want him to see me.* And the voice comes up with its whole catalogue — three dates, girls in trucks, your mother — and her body answers each count with heat, "the heat that knew the voice was right, that wanted to be the very thing the voice said." That's the most honest thing anyone has written about her yet. Three knuckles on the glass. The crooked finger. And she keeps her eyes on his for four buttons *because she put them there*, and then looks down anyway so his eyes go with hers and she can see what he sees — the sheer bra, the gooseflesh, the drop running out of her wet hair — and her thighs press once on their own at the word *floozy*. She gives it to him to see. And he doesn't lean in, doesn't lift a hand, and the gladness on his face *is* the answer. Then the turn that made this a 3 on both axes: she reaches for his hand to start the next thing, and it's shaking, and everything in her stops and she puts his hand on her belly under the warm flannel and says "You're freezing." She was the one being given to all day and she is the one who notices. He leaves the hand there a beat longer than he has to. I'm undone.

Friction: almost none, which is rare for me. The campus cop is the one place I felt a hand on the scale — the siren squawking her sentence in half right as she says "Pace—" is a *very* tidy way to keep her from saying the big thing, and I felt the author's timing there. Small. And the honor-society induction arrived a touch like a set-piece being installed. Otherwise the prose earned every inch of its length; even the "push and lift / push and drop" repetition worked as legs, not as style.

Trust: I have no fresh suspicion of Pace *inside this chapter* — and that's exactly the problem, because the only voice on that trail besides theirs was Randi's. Her *ravish* was up there with them, the picture she hung for Vee and Vee "had not taken down since." Randi arranged the mood of a date she wasn't on. And I still know what Vee doesn't, and this chapter's whole mode of tenderness is the same mode as the apple slices and the water bottle left in advance. The man is real and the frame is a trap and both stay true.

**The titles.** "Leave No Trace" is his line about cherry pits, and on the page it's a gift — a small decency taught without humiliation. On second thought it turns cold two ways: it's also the operating principle of whatever Randi and Pace are running on her, no evidence anywhere, a girl who cannot find the seam. And Vee walks off with his flannel smelling of soap and gym bag and her own wet hair — she *did* leave a trace, on him, and took one. The volume line, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*: supping with the devil, and the spoons ringing in every cup in that yellow coffee room. "A polite invitation" now has the chapter's *May I* and *We should all get dinner sometime* both inside it. I feel the spoon getting shorter.

What I want: her to have this. What I dread: that I'm being taught to love him at exactly the speed she is.

**Symbolism:** Leave no trace, in both directions. The summit's indifference as the only safe kind of being-looked-at. The flannel from a gym bag that lives behind the seat — a dry, warm thing kept ready, which is his whole signature and also exactly what I can't stop noting about him.

**Characterization:** Vee deepened enormously — she has now twice chosen her own wanting on purpose (tree, cab) and once set a want down herself with dignity (the dress). Pace deepened without resolving: warmth total, sincerity total, and he has never once been caught. Nobody went thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** earned every line. The ascent could have sagged and didn't because the boot broke it exactly right; the summit got its silence; the cab was paced at button-speed. Only the cop rushed.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** building hard. After "Dear" — which was Randi managing Vee at a white table with a probably-invented date story — this lands as the first chapter in a while that's purely Vee's, and it's the biggest step yet. No repeated beat, and the heat finally moved onto Vee's side of the book.
