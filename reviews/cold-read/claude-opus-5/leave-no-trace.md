# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I went up that trail completely on Vee's side and came down it wanting to warn her, and neither feeling canceled the other.

The boot. That's what got me first. Not the summit — the boot. She sits up on a boulder with her feet not reaching the ground, and the thing that spikes in her isn't fear of him, it's *shame about her sock*: "the word came up in her mother's voice, the whole verdict, *what kind of girl lets a man*—" and before the sentence can finish he's already got the lace open, the moleskin cut, his thumb warming it against her heel. Then he apologizes — *to her* — for not asking about the boots at the lot. "Somewhere behind her the clench had let go without her catching it go." That's the whole engine of this book in one line: relief arriving before she's registered the fear, care landing exactly on the spot the shame lives. I melted and I felt the mechanism, at the same temperature.

The summit is the best pure prose in the book so far. The valley, the lake "holding the silver sky in it flat as a poured thing," and then the turn I didn't expect — "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it... and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it." A girl whose entire interior is organized around being looked at, handed a thing that doesn't look. And then the counterweight: "he was already looking at her — not at the valley, at her face in front of the valley — and he didn't look away when she caught him doing it." Gorgeous and slightly awful. The world doesn't want anything from her. He does.

Cherries. "We take nothing and leave nothing." He lays her spat pit on the wax paper next to his own and doesn't look at her while she learns. He hands her a class marker without making her small for not having had it — I believe that entirely, it was tender, and I also noticed he has now taught her to leave no evidence. Which is the title, and the title recolored the whole chapter for me on the walk down. Trail ethic, yes. Also: a description of a man who conducts an operation on a person's life and leaves nothing behind that could be read back.

The russet dress is the one place I felt the author's hand — not clumsily, but deliberately: "the dress stayed where she'd set it down on the rock between them, and he didn't pick it up again." That's a gun on a rock. He said only "That sounds like a beautiful dress," with no weight on it, which was the correct and lovely answer, and I am now certain that dress is coming, and that its arrival will be the sweetest and most expensive thing anyone has ever done to her. Same with "The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." She's describing appetite engineering and calling it a trick and she means it fondly. I flinched a little on her behalf.

The truck. The truck is the hottest thing in this book outside of chapter one's bench, and hotter in a different way, because nothing is done *to* her — she does all of it. He gives her his only dry shirt, says nothing about it being his only dry shirt, and gets out and stands in the rain with his back turned so she can change. And her body answers with "*I want him in here with me.* And under it, slower — *I want him to see me.*" What made it burn was the fusion: "her body answered each one with a low pull of heat under the cold, the heat that knew the voice was right, that wanted to be the very thing the voice said." Her mother's *floozy* arriving as arousal. She could have taken the fourth button by feel and kept his eyes — "She looked down anyway, and his eyes went with hers." That one sentence is direction, and she's the director. And then he sits there and doesn't lift a hand and "the gladness in him was the whole of the answer, and it was right." God, yes. Consent as the erotic content itself.

Then his hand is shaking and she stops. *She* stops, for him, and puts his cold hand against her bare belly under the flannel. That's the romantic peak of the chapter and maybe the run: she got to be the one taking care. Which is, if I'm honest with myself, also the most effective thing that could possibly happen to a girl who has never been on that side of it. I keep having to hold both.

Suspicion: nothing *he* did here was off, and I want to say that plainly — this chapter earned no new distrust of Pace on its own terms. My unease is entirely carried in from what I know and he doesn't say: Randi's name did not come up once, and the absence was seamless enough that I nearly forgot to notice it, which frightened me more than a slip would have. The campus cop killing her sentence at the curb made me laugh and then made my neck prickle — a man with no last name, blue lights, taking her "Pace—" away with a siren squawk. Small bill, arriving.
