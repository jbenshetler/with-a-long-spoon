## Reader reaction

**Underneath**

Oh, this one got me in the gut. Two chapters of Vee glowing — the mountain, the blister, the shirt, Cassie saying her name like setting something down where it wouldn't break — and then we cut to his house and the deadbolt goes over and I'm watching the other half of the machine run. I read it fast and hot and then sat there feeling slightly sick, which I think is exactly the deal on offer.

The sex is the best-written sex in the book for me since the bench, and it's the least tender. "Every garment was an interruption" — I loved that; his hands coming off her only for as long as it takes the next thing to leave her body. And her lowering herself an inch at a time with her eyes on his face and not on what she's holding, making him watch, until the groan comes up out of him that he hadn't decided on: "She had earned it and she knew it." That's Randi at full competence and it's genuinely thrilling. Then "Tell me," and my whole body changed temperature. He doesn't make her work for it. "Her hair smells sweet... and under it, faint — chlorine. Like she'd been in a pool." I *know* where that chlorine came from. I was at the bottom of that pool with Vee, in the one place nobody wanted anything from her. It's being spent here, as fuel, in a bedroom she's never seen. He feels it land in her body. She laughs and it comes out thin, not her laugh.

And then "Her stomach is soft," and the narration says the thing that made me put my hand over my mouth: *he did not say how he knew. That she had given him herself, in the truck with the rain coming down, and he kept it.* Three times now he's kept something of Vee's back — the froyo confession, and now this. I keep wanting to read that as decency, and it is, and it's also the only thing in this arrangement he owns outright. He's curating her. He hands Randi the sweet hair and the chlorine and the soft stomach — three sensory items, portioned like apple slices — and keeps the shirt off her shoulders for himself. Meanwhile Randi clenches, eyes shut, and doesn't move, and won't let go, and he has to say her name to get her back. She isn't in the room. The most naked thing she says all night is "I want us," torn out of her at his ear, and *us* is doing an enormous amount of work there — him and her, or her and Vee, or some third thing she can't name — and the page doesn't tell me, and I don't want it to yet.

Then she leaves. God. The chair by the door with nothing on it — he notices that before she's even in the room and *sets it aside*. No overnight bag. She takes the blanket, but to get dressed in, not to be held in. She won't eat. "No thanks," bright, from inside the camisole going over her head. "Stay anyway. Get warm." "I'm good." And she turns the deadbolt *back* — the lock that has been her first act in that house every single time, undone by her own hand on the way out. She's never left after. Not once. "He set that beside the empty chair and left them both there." That line broke something open in me. He sees it. He sees all of it. He just puts it down next to the other thing he saw and keeps standing in his warm house. And then — the detail that finished me — he eats alone at the table and *is still hungry after and makes more.* The whole book has been him feeding her in thin slices. Tonight he's the one with the hole in him.

I ached for him and I don't fully trust the ache, and that's the honest state. He was standing in freezing rain twelve hours ago so a girl wouldn't be looked at; tonight he narrated her body to his girlfriend during sex because she asked him to. Both are him. And Randi — I have never felt her further away. In ch 1 the vow was a decision; here it's operational. She came to get something, got it, and did not stay to be a person afterward. The want she can't hold has now eaten the one hour of the week she wasn't on duty. She is starving herself of the only thing that ever fed her. I find that unbearably sad and also a little frightening, because the want is growing and it's pointed at a girl in an oversized flannel who thinks she has a place now.

Small thing I clocked: Blondie low in the front rooms instead of the Steely Dan that's been there twice. The house changed its music and nobody remarked on it. I don't know what to do with that and I like that I don't.

**Titles.** *Underneath* is doing three jobs at once and I felt all of them: undressing, the layer under the face — "the sharp citrus and the cold thing under it" — and the way the bench spent a chapter underneath a tabletop nobody corrected. Also, quietly, he's *underneath* her this whole scene and it changes nothing about who's being used. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on the last page, which is the good version. The cover board is all my packet gives me — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, no tagline, no jacket copy in front of me now. The long spoon reads plainer every chapter: this is what supping with something looks like, and nobody's spoon is long enough. And "a polite invitation" — I remember a blurb saying an invitation arrived before she knew there was one. After tonight I think the invitation is nearly out of Randi's hands and into the light.

**What I want next:** Vee in that house, and whether she recognizes the blanket. And Brooke, who has now seen the hand on the white table. *Guess from here:* Randi asks for the threesome out loud before Pace decides anything, and it will not be a gift.
