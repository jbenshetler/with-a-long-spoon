# Cold read (grounded) — Above Him

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one is small and it wrecked me a little. After the bistro — after the stall door and the blue light and the glaze on Vee's lip that she never earned — I came into this chapter braced, and the chapter just... let me put my hands down. A week apart, the door swinging wide before she got to it, her feet leaving the boards, and the porch light wheeling once behind his head. "He didn't set her down so much as let her slide the front of him until her toes found the boards." That's the whole book's method in one clause and I don't care, it worked. I was grinning.

What I love about the first section is that the sex is already over when we get there. We're in the cooling sheet with the whole day loud in her body, and what she's actually inventorying isn't positions, it's *evidence*. The slowness. The coming back an hour later. "It always came around to her first." And then, underneath, the sentence I've been waiting for her to be brave enough to think: *She loved him.* Set down plainly, in her own chest where she can't take it back, and then — this is the part that got me in the throat — she does the accounting that lets her not need the words. The door, the spin, the slowness, the salmon, the warm tomatoes. "He felt it in everything he did and said it in nothing, and she found, tonight, that she could live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry." God, Vee. That's a girl who has been hungry her whole life teaching herself that a full plate is the same as being told she's fed. It's generous and it's a little bit of a survival skill, and the page lets both be true.

And then "*Peter*," breathed at a sleeping man who doesn't stir. She uses the name he gave her by the fire, the one that isn't the name anyone else has — and she says it where it can't be answered. That's the ache of the chapter. Not a misgiving, an ache: she's found the safest possible way to say it out loud.

The drawer. I'll be honest, I read the bare counter with my stomach dropping — *he minded, he tidied her away* — and the reveal landed exactly as designed and I didn't resent one inch of it. His things "pressed in and fitted too tight together," folded down into half the space they were built for; hers standing upright in the top drawer with "a clear run of liner with room to spare, room for more of her." A careful man giving up his order without narrating it. "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead." That's the most romantic thing in this book so far and there's no skin in it. Same grammar as the pencil back in her hand in the coffee shop, the same as the heat turned up hours early. He *builds* rather than says, and I believe the building completely — which is the problem, because I've watched him build for two women, and the top drawer is a finite object.

Because here's where I cannot be clean about it: that is the only bathroom he has. That mirror is the mirror Randi locked herself in with and decided to disappear behind. That counter is the counter she'd have laid her things on if she ever left any. Vee's kit is now sprawled all over the one room in this house that has a history she doesn't know about, and Pace watched it happen in the glass and held there a second, "taking it in," and said nothing. I don't think that second was distaste. I think it was a man doing arithmetic. He never says, and this chapter makes a point of it twice. I love him more than I did last chapter and I trust the arrangement less, and both of those went up at once.

The one thing that put a cold finger on me otherwise: the weight. She eats like a field hand now and her bras have moved up a band, "her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, and she'd never asked it to." Written as joy, and I took it as joy — she's finally eating, she's finally *lit*. But she's shrinking inside clothes bought a size up by a girl who never believed her own build, and Randi lives on egg whites, and the book keeps putting feeding and thinness beside each other. I'm not accusing anyone. It just registered.

Friction: almost none. The paragraph explaining the racks and the budget and the tailor is a beat long and reads as the author making sure I do the math on the bras. "There. She'd said it, or not said it" is a touch over-supervised. That's it.

**Above Him** — I took it first as the whisper, her mouth at his ear over a sleeping man. Then the drawer arrived and reset it: her things in the top one, *above his*, which he pressed down into a single drawer to make room. A love-declaration in cabinetry. And a third reading I can't shake off, given the induction and the heels: this is a woman learning to stand at a height. None of it gave anything away beforehand, which I appreciate — the title only paid out on the last page. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** still hums under everything: you need a long spoon to sup with the devil, and the politeness of this house — the asking, the drawer, the juice — is exactly the invitation. No tagline was supplied in my packet.

What I want: I want her to have the top drawer. I want to be wrong. What I dread (guess from here): that the next person to open that bathroom drawer is Randi.
