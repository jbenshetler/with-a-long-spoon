# Cold read (grounded) — Turned Up

## Reader reaction

This is the debrief, and I came into it grinning already, because the previous chapter left me wrung out and I wanted somebody in Vee's life to hear it. What I didn't expect was how much of it she'd *keep*. She tells Cassie the dress, the silk, the riser, the bra, the box, the geometry of him on his knees — and she does not tell her the wetness, the panic, the shut eyes, the pin, the kiss on her ribs. The hardest hour of her life gets handed over as a comedy set with two or three trapdoors in it. Watching her decide, live, which parts stay hers was the whole pleasure of the chapter for me. "It was — I don't know. It was a lot." That's a girl standing at the edge of the real thing and stepping back on purpose, and I didn't feel cheated by it; I felt how new the having-something-of-her-own is.

The two places she does go all the way down got me badly. First: the heat. "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold." — and then, "He turned it up hours before, probably. As soon as he knew I was coming. He thought of it then." That's her doing the arithmetic out loud, and I did it with her, and Cassie going *very still* before "Oh, Vee" is the perfect reader-surrogate beat: this is the one that undoes a person, not the silk. And I'm the one sitting here knowing that house is kept evenly, excessively warm *all the time* — that Randi has stood in that same heat in a cardigan for weeks — and Vee has read it as a thing done for her. It may well be both. That's the shape of this whole book: care that is completely real and completely not what she thinks it is, and the page refuses to make me choose.

Second: "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*." And then the room going quiet, and Cassie's "No. I think I have it." That's the best line in the chapter and it's Vee finding language for what happened to her a day late. Set next to "Like I was supposed to be there" — the answer to *how did it feel* after a pause long enough that it isn't about finding the word — and I'm just gone. That's the same register as the pool bottom for me: belonging she isn't apologizing for. She pulls her knees up "smaller than she usually sat" to say it, which is the body still not believing the mouth.

Cassie is doing something I want to name carefully. She puts the highlighter down before a word is spoken. She lets the pivot happen when Vee jukes away from the heat detail — "She was good at that." She says nothing at the knees-and-riser moment, "which was the right thing." She touches Vee's knee once and takes it back. And then, having held all that, she's the one who brings the room back down with the funniest line in the book so far: "He's a bodybuilder who makes dresses… Vee. Are you sure he's not gay?" I laughed out loud, and so did the chapter, and Vee's brisk clinical pat of her own ass as courtroom evidence is the most unselfconscious she has ever been on the page. But underneath — Cassie is *working a puzzle when the pieces wouldn't fit*, and she is the only person in this book assembling a picture of Pace from the outside. She lands on the wrong answer. I notice she went looking for an answer at all. That "Goodbye, Vee" is still sitting in me like a stone and this chapter didn't move it either way.

Trust and suspicion, plainly: nothing on this page earned suspicion. It's a warm dorm room. What I have instead is dread with no local cause — Randi is not mentioned once, not even in passing, for the first time in a long while, and the silence where she'd normally be ("Randi says," "Randi thinks") felt like a held breath to me. Also this: "the answer in her hands the whole time, and she'd decided it was a riddle and stopped looking." She's talking about the shirt tag. I heard it about everything else. That's the page doing it, not me — Vee herself says it.

Erotic charge is low and I don't mind. It's recall, not event, and most of it gets routed into laughter. The one place it flickers live is the grinding reveal — "*I* was grinding on *him*" — and it's less hot than triumphant, a girl discovering she's a person who *does* things. It's the third chapter in a row where Vee's own agency is the punchline she's proudest of, and the throughline from "I made *him* stop. And I kissed him" is doing real work.

The title. "Turned Up" reads three ways and all three landed: the thermostat turned up hours early; Vee turned up at his door and on the box; and her own dial turned up past where she can manage it. It didn't give anything away — I only felt the pun settle after Cassie went still at the radiator line, which is the good kind of title. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** is the thing I keep chewing. You need a long spoon to sup with the devil. Book *One*. "A polite invitation" — and every invitation in this book has been immaculately polite: *May I choose. May I call you. May I look.* The courtesy is the mechanism. Nothing this chapter contradicted that; the warmth of the room only sharpened it. What I want next is the dress finished and worn, and what I dread is who else is in the room when she wears it.
