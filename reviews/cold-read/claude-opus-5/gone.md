## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter still laughing on that sidewalk with the shoebox, and then Randi opened her mouth and told the story wrong. That's the thing that happened to me here, and it happened in the first minute. I *was there* Saturday. Vee found the canoe shoe on the clearance wall and set it down in front of Randi — "Found your size. Do yetis shop here?" — and it was *Randi* who came back down the aisle six inches taller in pink rhinestones, and the older couple watched two girls being silly. In this bed the story is: Vee got the ugly shoe on, did the full bigfoot, stomping, arms out, the little growl, and turned around and *saw them*, and went scarlet, hand over her mouth, wanting the floor to take her. "It was so cute. She was *so* embarrassed." And then, softer: "That was kind of hot." She didn't just borrow the bit. She moved the humiliation off herself and onto Vee, and then she got off on it. My stomach went cold in a way it hasn't yet in this book. Nobody has been cruel — that was my line about this book — and this is the first thing I'd call cruel, and it's small enough that nobody in the room will ever know it happened.

Which makes Pace's half of the conversation almost unbearable to read. He's being handed a doctored girl and he's holding the real one anyway: "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body." Flat, certain, no heat, and exactly right — he got there off a cardigan. And then the passage that actually broke me open: "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp... she spends the entire time sure she's too much, and she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her." He wasn't looking at anything; he was seeing it. That's a love speech. He doesn't know it's a love speech. He's giving it, naked, to his girlfriend, about the girl his girlfriend picked off a quad for him.

And Randi went still on him. "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding." God. That's the second crack — the first was her jaw going tight when Vee said *he looked at me and I was fine* — and this one is wider and she can't get her face over it fast enough. "Yeah," she said, when she'd been quiet a beat too long. And then she fixed it the only way she has: she found him hard and rode him. The whole chapter she has one move for anything true that surfaces — put a body over it. Same as the bathroom mirror. I felt so much for her and I trust her less than I did an hour ago, and both of those got stronger at once, which is the thing this book keeps doing to me.

But I want to be honest that it's also warm in here, and that got me too. "She talked less here than anywhere. Whatever she ran on out in the world, she set it down at his door" — that's true and it's lovely and it costs her nothing to be true in his bed, which may be the only room where she's cheap about it. "I didn't think I'd like her this much... She got past me before I could help it." I believe that completely. "Pace this, Pace that... She's got it *bad*. It's a little pathetic." Beat. "I love it." That's real affection and real appetite and I couldn't separate them, and I don't think she can. And when she said "It's *criminal*, Pace" and dragged herself down the length of him to *show* him Vee's body with her own — that was the hottest thing in the chapter and also the saddest, because the only way she can want a woman out loud is through a man, in the dark, as a demonstration. He clocked it exactly: not the picture of Vee, but *Randi wanting something out loud and not able to stop it*. And "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." He keeps building her doors and she keeps bolting them.

The line I'll be carrying: "She's not ready, though. She'll get there. You'll want to take your time." — "I want her to get there on her own." Something crossed her face and was gone. I don't know what crossed it. It could be tenderness or it could be a scheduling conflict. Pace means that sentence purely, I think — he genuinely wants the yes to be hers — and it is *still* a sentence about steering someone toward a destination they haven't been told exists. The most frightening kind of decency: he will not push her one inch, toward a place she was pointed at from across a lawn. "God, you two," she said. She's half-outside it now, watching them, and I couldn't tell if that was fondness or exclusion.

The title. "Gone" is Randi's word — "that's why she's so gone over you" — and she says it like a diagnosis of Vee, a bit pathetic, a bit adored. Then the chapter hands it right back to her with that stillness, and I finished thinking *which one of you is gone.* And the cover board, all I have: WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION. No tagline, no jacket copy in my packet. The spoon proverb has been sitting there the whole time and I've mostly let it alone; tonight, with Randi warm in a warm bed telling a lie the shape of a girl's blush, I notice nobody in this house is holding a long spoon at all. *A polite invitation* reads like a promise the book intends to keep exactly — politely, warmly, with everyone smiling. What I want next is Cassie. What I dread is the invitation arriving, and Vee saying yes, happily, in the dress. Guess from here: Randi's plan and Randi's heart part company before Vee ever learns anything, and that's what detonates.
