## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the first chapter I've had from inside Pace since the pointing game, and the thing that struck me before any plot-thought was how domestic it is: the kettle, the chicken flattened under the flat of the pan, the couscous, fifteen minutes and "nothing in it he had to think about." And Randi in it. Randi *staying down* after, loose on his chest — she has never stayed down. Randi crossing to the chest of drawers and opening the second one down "without looking for it" and putting on his undershirt, which means she knew which drawer, which means she's opened it before and thought about it. Randi asking, for the first time in her life as far as I know her, *is there anything to eat in this house* — and then eating it. All of it. Every stalk of the asparagus. Against the woman of thin apple slices and the egg-white mother and the bowl of stew with one line drawn across the top of the sauce two chapters ago, that plate wrecked me a little. And he doesn't say so. "It was the most he had seen her eat at his table. He did not say so." He has learned exactly where the door is and he stays off it.

And the lever that opened her isn't him. It's Vee. "I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come... She got past me before I could help it." That's the confession of the book so far and she makes it into his sternum with her hair spread over him. The one who vowed in a bathroom mirror never to come out got walked past by a redhead who didn't know she was doing it. I felt that as warmth and as a low alarm at the same time, which is where this book keeps putting me.

Then the thing I caught, and I want to be careful because I only just read it: **she told the shoe store wrong.** In the store it was *Randi* who came down the aisle a head taller in pink rhinestone platforms doing the grave-serious bit, and Vee who found the canoe with the snow-tire sole and handed it over; the older couple watched two girls being silly and Vee got a flush, "there and gone." In Pace's kitchen it becomes: Vee does the full bigfoot, arms out, the little growl, Vee turns around and sees them, Vee goes so red she wants the floor to take her, hand over her mouth. "It was so cute. She was *so* embarrassed." And then: "That was kind of hot." She moved her own clowning onto Vee and moved the audience's eyes onto Vee and kept the humiliation as the part worth telling — and she told it to the man she's steering Vee toward. That is the first outright lie I've caught Randi in, not an omission, and it isn't a lie about logistics. It's a lie that manufactures a scene of Vee being caught and looked at. It went cold down my back.

And the very next beat is why: Pace says the room thing. "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp... she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her." And behind him the chair makes no sound, and Randi has gone still — "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding" — and she can only get out "Yeah. She is." after a beat too long. Same tell as the tight jaw at *he looked at me and I was fine.* Then she crosses the kitchen, has him through his jeans, takes the undershirt off on the floor of his kitchen and drags herself the length of him describing Vee's body in a voice gone hoarse — "It's *criminal*, Pace." That's the hottest moment in the chapter and it's hot for the reason Pace names to himself: not the picture of Vee, but *Randi* wanting something out loud and unable to stop. It is also, unmistakably, a woman burying a jealousy she cannot afford under an offering. Both at once. I don't think she could tell you which.

Pace: "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." God, that's good, and it's the whole problem with him in one line. He sees it. He declines to open it. It's the Daphne rule applied to a woman who is drowning behind glass and smiling, and it's simultaneously the most respectful and the most useless thing he could do. He is also the man who said "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body," flat and certain, and got it right on the first pass with no help. I trust his eyes completely and I no longer think his restraint is safety.

**Titles.** "Gone" — I expected it to be Vee's chapter, since *gone* has been her word (*how far gone she already was*, and here, "she's got it bad... I love it," "that's why she's so gone over you"). It's Randi's. She's the one gone, in the falling sense and maybe the vanishing one, and the title only turns over on you after the still chair. Nothing given away; a real recolor. The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — reads more ominous every chapter: you sup with the devil with a long spoon, and nobody in this house is using one; the *polite invitation* has to be the threesome, and after tonight I think Randi will be the one issuing it, courteously, for the wrong reason. No tagline or jacket copy was supplied to me beyond that line.

**What I want and dread:** I want Randi to eat again. I dread her asking Vee something she's already decided the answer to. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the invitation comes before the induction, and Randi proposes it not to give Vee to Pace but to stop being the one outside the room.
