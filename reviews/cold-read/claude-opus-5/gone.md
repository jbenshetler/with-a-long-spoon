I believe it's a fact about her, and I think it's the second half of the bathroom-mirror decision: if nobody reaches you, nobody stays interesting. Vee reached her by accident, through a wall Randi built on purpose, and she can't make it stop. I am so unexpectedly moved by her tonight, and I don't trust a single thing that will come of it.

That line is the one place I felt the thumb, faintly — it explains his delicacy where the silence would have done it. Everything around it is clean. The cooking paragraph I expected to skim and didn't: the pan leaned flat on the chicken to even it, turning it by the sound, "fifteen minutes, all of it, and nothing in it he had to think about." She hasn't come round to his patience; she's come round to wanting Vee for herself and having nowhere to put it. They are now agreeing in two different languages, and the only person who could tell them apart is the one who doesn't know either of them knows the other. Also: *"She was so embarrassed… That was kind of hot."* Said tenderly, in bed, about a blush. I felt the cold edge of that even while I understood it.

**The titles.** "Gone" is doing triple duty and I only caught the third on the second pass: Vee is "so gone over you" (Randi's word, approvingly); Vee herself said *how far gone she already was* at brunch; and tonight the person who is gone is Randi — past her own guard, "she got past me before I could help it." It doesn't give anything away; it recolors. A fourth reading hums under it that I can't shake: gone as in *lost*. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** keeps getting more literal and more sinister — he who sups with the devil uses a long spoon, and this is a book of people being fed by hand at close range (the apple, the small bowl, the spoon laid down beside it, tonight's plate she actually cleared). "A Polite Invitation" now reads like a countdown: the invitation hasn't been issued, and both of them want to issue it for different reasons. My guess from here, marked as a guess: Randi will be the one to propose it, and not for Pace.
