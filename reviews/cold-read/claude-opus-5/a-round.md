# Cold read (grounded) — A Round

## Reader reaction

I want to say first that this chapter made me put a hand flat on the table. Not because anything happened — nothing *happens*, he never kisses her, the man stays buttoned to the collar the whole hour — but because the wanting in it ran so hot and so unrelieved that I was holding my breath along with her. "She had not, she realized, been entirely breathing." Same. This is the most erotic chapter in the book so far and it's a sewing appointment.

The architecture of it is what got me: the rhythm of **closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil.** That she expected the measuring to be the exposure and discovered "the measuring was only half of it" — that the unbearable part was standing on a box being looked at from across a small room by a man with a pencil — is exactly right about how being wanted works, and I've never seen it put that way. And then her body does what bodies do, and the chapter turns into something I found genuinely agonizing to read: the thumbprint on the satin, the bargaining ("Not at conversational distance"), "She tried, *please, please, just stop* — asking her body a thing she'd long since learned it doesn't answer to." That's her mother's whole inheritance made physical and visible on the one garment she picked with hope. "The hope was visible. The hope was sized to be seen across the room." God.

And then he's waiting with his chin tipped all the way up. I melted. Flat out. The whole sequence — her eyes screwed shut, the silence where his working sounds should be, the catalogue of faces she dreads (disgust, the gentleman's pretense, "his face working not to laugh"), and then: *he hadn't looked down once.* He had spent her entire private death with his face turned up to where her eyes would be, waiting. That is the single most loving thing anyone has done in ten-plus chapters, and it cost him a word count of nothing. The involuntary un-stooping afterward, her weight resettling, rising onto her toes toward "something she had no name for" — and his *hmm* and the sideways glance at her heel, the two of them not saying it — I'd read this book for that beat alone. His little grammar of *hmm* goes back to the blister on the trail; it's becoming his sound for *I've seen, it's fine, I'm handling it.*

Then she spends the power, and the chapter gets *funny* and hot at once, which is rare. "Does this measurement change? Like. When they're not — like this." — and he laughs, and then goes somewhere behind his eyes to actually *work the problem*, and she thinks *I made him laugh, and then I stumped him. Standing here like this, I did both.* That's the best line in the chapter. The third breast-against-his-cheek, on purpose, "Oops. Sorry" — I cheered. Watching him go red, watching him lose the fight with his own mouth, watching the hands stay steady while the breath fails: "the maddening difference" is exactly the engine of the heat here. And the pin, and him kissing the blood off her ribs before he'd decided to, and then **"Keep still"** — low, a beat late, after a silence she watches him spend deciding. That's the hottest line of dialogue in the book and it's two words of restraint.

So: trust. I have none of my usual unease about him *in this room* — his care isn't technique, it's load-bearing, and the thermostat turned up hours early is the same man who left the water bottle on the nightstand in advance. But two things sat cold under all my swooning, and the page put them there, not me. One: she thinks of him as the man "who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking," and I know what he actually did, which was point at her across a lawn and say *Her.* That sentence hurt to read. Two: she is standing on a low smooth riser, beautifully finished, built by a woodworker for a woman's body to be arranged on — and I have already met the other thing he built for a woman's body and called a table. He builds furniture that holds women still so he can attend to them completely. In this chapter that's tenderness. I don't think it's *only* tenderness.

Friction, honestly: a couple of places the prose reached in and told me what I'd just felt. "That was the bravest thing she did all day, the not being able to stand it" — I'd gotten there; the narrator didn't need to pin a medal on it. And the closing "she had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting" states the thesis out loud when the scene had already delivered it bodily. The *stripper* / *floozie* / *girls like that* voice also came around once or twice more than it needed to. Small things against a chapter this good.

**"A Round"** — the tape going around her, and him knee-walking around the base of her like a circuit, and (I think) her own roundness, the body the swim squad had no use for. It didn't give anything away; it reads flat until you're inside it and then it's the shape of the whole hour. The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — keeps getting worse in the best way. *He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon*, and this chapter is the most lavish supping yet, with nobody holding any spoon at all. And "a polite invitation" is now unmistakably that text: *would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock.* The politeness is the mechanism. No tagline was supplied to me.

What I want next: for her to keep that un-stooped posture. What I dread: Randi hearing about this afternoon, and Randi's face when she does.
