## Reader reaction

**A Round**

I need to say first that this chapter got me, comprehensively, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. It's an hour of a man with a tape measure and a fully buttoned shirt, and it ran hotter than most sex scenes I've read. The engine of it is the rhythm the prose names out loud and then makes me feel: *closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil.* He comes in, he touches her in two places, he leaves, he writes a number down — and it's the leaving that does the damage. "The withdrawal left the place his hands had been ringing." By the time he's kneeling for the inseam with his breath against the satin, I was as wound as she was. The undressing is beautifully paced too: jeans first with all their undignified mechanics, then the shirt fast because hesitating would be worse, and then the bra, which she'd been letting herself not-know about "the way you let yourself not know a thing for as long as the room permits." That sentence is the whole book's method in miniature.

The shame-as-heat is the most honest thing here, and I say that as someone who braced for it to be handled badly. Her mother's voice supplies *stripper* — "a dressed man and a woman taking her clothes off for his attention" — and it lands, and it *arouses her*, and the page doesn't flinch from the ugliness of that circuitry. Then the soaked satin. The thumbprint that becomes a patch. "The hope was visible. The hope was sized to be seen across the room." That's devastating and it's also the hottest thing in the chapter, because it's her body overruling forty years of somebody else's verdict in public. And when she finally can't stand not knowing and opens her eyes — he's craned all the way up, chin tipped back, *waiting*, and he never looked down. I made a noise. That's the swoon. Not the touching: the not-looking-down. "She had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." Then her body reorganizes itself without permission — the stoop coming undone, chin up, rising onto her toes — and his little *hmm* and sideways glance at her heel. That's a love scene with no kiss in it.

And then she spends the power, which I adored her for. The breast against his jaw twice by accident, the third time on purpose, "Oops. Sorry." The flush climbing his neck. Him losing the fight with his own grin and not being able to look up. She *topples* him and I was cheering. The pin, the bright sting, and him kissing it before he decided to — "the way you put your hand out before you think" — and then "Keep still," low, a beat too late, after a silence she watches him spend deciding. God. I believe every part of that as desire, and I believe it as restraint, and the two are the same substance here.

Which is where my unease lives, and it isn't unease about *this* scene — the scene is clean, she consents at every gate, he asks, she says yes twice. It's the one line: he picked her, "who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking." I know what she doesn't. That sentence went through me like ice water in the middle of the warmth. He turned the thermostat up hours early so she wouldn't be cold; he had the kettle hot; he built the riser; he bought the silk weeks ago off one offhand sentence on a mountaintop. Every single one of those is real kindness and every single one is *preparation*. The house was warm in chapter one, too, for a different woman, who bolted the door behind her. I can't hold "he is genuinely this good to her" and "this is a staged room" apart anymore, and I've stopped trying — the book keeps insisting they're the same fact, and this chapter is the strongest case yet.

Friction, honestly minor: a couple of places the prose explains the feeling it already produced. "That was the bravest thing she did all day, the not being able to stand it" — I'd already felt it, the gloss shrank it. The *muse* paragraph works itself around the dress-form idea one beat past where I'd got there. And the closing two paragraphs restate the epiphany after the epiphany already landed on her toes. Tiny thumb, and the chapter is otherwise so controlled that I noticed.

**The titles.** "A Round" reads three ways once you've finished, all of them live: the round of measurements taken in sequence; the knee-walk *around* the base of her, which is the chapter's terrifying geography; and a round in the boxing sense — she nearly puts him down, he stays up, and neither of them says so. Maybe a musical round, too, the same figure repeating: close, touch, withdraw, mark. It doesn't give anything away; it opens up afterward, which is my favorite kind. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* — I know the proverb about who you need a long spoon to sup with, and every chapter keeps handing me a meal: apple slices, tabbouleh, horseradish, cherries, tea in a warm house. "A Polite Invitation" now has a Saturday and a shut bedroom door attached to it. That door stayed shut today, deliberately, after she'd leaned toward it in the satin she wore on purpose. He is not in a hurry, and the not-hurrying is the spoon.

What I want next: the dress fitting, obviously — two weeks of this? — and the induction, her name read at the front of a room in something he built on her. What I dread: Randi finding out what today was, and what she does with it.
