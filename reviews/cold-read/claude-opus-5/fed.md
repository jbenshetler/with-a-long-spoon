# Cold read (grounded) — Fed

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one is quieter than the last few and it got under me anyway, mostly because of what Vee says about the sheet. "I sat at his table in his sheet and finally drank it, and watched him finish making our dinner. The oil going in the pan and starting to tick, the window black behind him, near midnight, neither of us saying a word and nothing missing." That's the whole book's ache in four clauses. She isn't describing the sex, she's describing the *after*, which is what she's actually starved for, and she says out loud "I don't think I've ever felt anything better in my life." I believe her. I'm so happy for her I could weep, and I'm the only one at that table who knows what the happiness is made of.

Randi. God. Her opening story — the beautiful ex, the boy in a hurry, "at least I got dinner out of it ahead of time" — landed on me as a cover story before I'd finished the paragraph, and the tell is how *useful* it is. It manufactures a deficit ("I'm running on fumes here"), which manufactures a debt, which opens Vee's mouth. She's done this every time and I still watched her do it and only caught it a beat late, like Vee. What's new is that she isn't only harvesting. "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky" — I don't think that was tactical. Neither was "So why are you telling me like it's a confession?", which is the single kindest sentence anyone has said to Vee in this book, and it comes from the woman running the con. The two facts sit right on top of each other and won't separate, and that's the thing I can't stop turning over.

The polish is where my stomach dropped. Vee "had the answer and couldn't give it" — the frosted plum chosen in a bright Ulta aisle to wake up the color of the silk. And Randi asks *what's it called, why'd you pick it*, and watches it arrive in Vee's face. Randi has stood in that sun porch, or knows the bolt, or will. The reader is the only one who gets to feel the two colors meeting. It's a beautiful, sickening little rhyme and the page never underlines it once, which I loved it for.

Trust: nothing here made me newly suspicious of anyone. It confirmed what I already carry. The thing that's *moved* is my sympathy for Randi, which keeps rising against my will, and my dread on Vee's behalf, which is now specific rather than atmospheric — Vee is handing over the inventory of her own wanting (the toes, the bought underwear, the *I took it, for once*) to the one person who can price it. And she does it because she wanted to tell somebody. That's not a flaw in her. That's just being twenty-one.

Erotically this ran cooler than "Famished" and it should have — it's a recounting, not an event. The charge that's actually live is Randi's, and it's never named: the stillness when she listens, the leaning in, "do not insult me, what did you have on." And then the curb. "Vee had half-expected it this time, and met it, and when Randi let her go she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them." The whole turn is in that clause. Last time she scanned the dorm windows with her heart going. This time she just took it. Nobody on the page remarks on it. That's the hottest line in the chapter and it's about a habit dropping away.

Friction: structurally this is the third debrief-over-a-meal in a short span, and I felt the shape before the scene started — Randi orders, tells a story against herself, then "tell me everything." It's saved by being *shorter* and by not ending on a slammed door the way "How It's Done" did. But I notice the pattern now, and if the next chapter is another lunch I'll be checking my watch. Small thing I didn't quite buy: the paragraph explaining what Randi is to Vee — "the friend she'd wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting to get" — is true but it's the author telling me a thing the mimosas had already shown me.

The title. "Fed" is the meanest one yet, and I didn't see it until the end. Nobody eats at this brunch. Two mimosas and a friend's whole weekend. Vee is fed in the sense Pace means it — fra diavolo at midnight, "let's go feed you" — and Randi is fed in the other sense, the parasitic one, on a story she asked for because she was "running on fumes." Same word, two appetites, and the book has been laying that fork down since a man sliced an apple thin in chapter one. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — I finally hear properly here: he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon, and a polite invitation is exactly what a brunch invitation is. The politeness is the mechanism. That's what I've been reading.

What I want next: the dress finished, and Vee in it, and Randi in the room when it happens. What I dread: the moment Randi tells Vee something true — because I think that's how this breaks, not with a lie.
