## Reader reaction

**Fed**

The first thing that got me wasn't the brunch — it was Cassie's face. "Something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name." I've been carrying her "Goodbye, Vee" around for a long time now, and here it is again: a flicker she doesn't get to keep long enough to say. Then she reads Vee "fast and whole" and matches her mouth to Vee's grin, because that's what Cassie does — she gives you the thing you came in with. And then Vee, three feet away, edits her. The ladle image is lovely and awful: "the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness. It was a kindness. She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." That's the most honest sentence about self-deception I've read in this book, and it's not cruel to her, which is why it lands.

The zipper stopped my breath a little. She can narrate the night with the middle lifted out, but bare under the skirt is not a story — it's "on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." That's the exact seam of the book: Vee can survive telling, she can't survive evidence. And Cassie, oblivious, hands her the mercy of the wrong diagnosis — "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend," said "kindly, certainly, wrong." That one adverb-stack is doing more damage to me than any villainy. Then the cleats, the clatter, "Don't wait up, lover," the door sticking and giving, and Vee alone in the cold room letting the towel fall and being happier than she's ever been. I felt both halves at once and I couldn't pick one.

Brunch swept me up and scared me in the same motion, which is apparently just how this book works now. The mimosa arithmetic — reading the right column first, finding Thursday survives it — is such a dignified way to show being poor, and the way the drink lets her be "for the length of a sentence, a woman who has a mimosa at brunch and thinks nothing of it" made me ache for her. And Randi is *good*. Not slimy-good; genuinely good company. The sad story offered first to clear the air, the self-mocking "before he, ah, lowered my expectations," the way she "made everything that had cost her into a story that had cost her nothing." Then the beam swings around. "You keep saying *he*." The toes. "You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands." *That's not nothing, that's a campaign.* I was grinning with Vee and simultaneously watching an inventory being taken — the polish, the purchase, the sureness she drove over with — by a woman who is going home to the man it was all for.

The heat peak isn't the remembered sex, it's **frosted plum**. Vee has the answer and can't give it, so it comes up her throat into her face instead: "slow and complete, the heat of a thing being seen before she had decided to show it." Randi watching it arrive and saying "That face isn't about a color, is it" — that's the most erotic exchange in the chapter, and nobody touched anybody. The secret she's protecting is *the dress*, which means she's protecting Pace's gift from the person who steered her to him, and she doesn't know that's what she's doing. And then "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky," with the hand covering the painted one — and damn it, I believe Randi meant it. That's the problem. The warmth isn't fake; it's just also the instrument.

And the one thing that put the hair up on my arms: the voice. "The esses running long, the a's lifting, more of whatever it was than there had been when they sat down," eyes gone liquid — and Vee checks the glass, and it's one mimosa, mostly untouched. "Nothing came along to take its place, and the light moved on the porch, and Vee let the question go." I did not let it go. That's the first time the page has shown me the *mask* slipping in Randi's presence rather than in her mirror, and it slipped toward something with an accent in it. Is the whole Randi register — the "honey," the "darling," the money-ease — a built voice, and what's underneath it something else entirely? I have no idea, and I love that I have no idea. Also: she's the one who went still and all-listening at the sheet-at-midnight story, and she's the one who said "Keep that one." I keep thinking she's harvesting and then she says something that sounds like a woman guarding the only beautiful thing in the room.

Swoon-wise: "I wanted it. And I took it. For once," with her hand drawing her own hair slowly through her fingers — that's the line I'd underline. And the curb kiss, half-expected and met, and not looking down the street for witnesses. She's stopped checking. That's growth and that's exposure and the chapter refuses to tell me which. The title, **Fed**, does good work both ways: Pace fed her, she fed herself at last, and she spent ninety minutes being eaten by somebody fond. The cover line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — rings the old proverb about supping with the devil, and this chapter is literally two meals and an invitation delivered entirely in affection. The spoon is long because the thing across the table is warm, not because it's cold. Friction: almost none; if anything the Cassie half felt like ground I'd just walked (I remembered it before I'd finished it), and "She had never in her life been so happy" is a shade of thumb-on-scale — the towel falling said it already.

**Symbolism:** The zipper halfway down — the exact boundary between a story and a fact. The sheet taken off the bed and worn to the table: being wrapped in him, warmth borrowed and carried out. The porch glass — bright, public, everything visible and nothing seen.

**Characterization:** Vee deepened hard — her self-knowledge is now ahead of her honesty, which is a real place to be. Randi deepened most, via the voice-slip and the unperformed stillness at the midnight story; she's less legible than before, not more. Cassie is thinning *because she's being thinned* — one flicker, three flat lines, gone to a game — and that reads as design, not failure, but I miss her.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The dorm scene is tight and earns every line. The brunch takes its time and should; the only slack is the "another hour… other things" summary, which is fine as a cool-down.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Holding rather than building — this is the second consecutive told-not-lived chapter after the big night, and the beat (Vee narrates, a woman listens) has now run three times. But the voice-slip and the zipper gave it new ground, so I didn't feel stalled. I want the triangle to crack next; my guess from here is the induction does it.
