## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one took the top of my head off, and it did it while being *kind*, which is the trick this book keeps pulling and I keep falling for.

The opening move is Vee texting first — "she had never once been the one to reach first" — and then coming at three for a four o'clock, and being willing to sit in the car. I felt the nakedness of that before any clothes came off. And then the whole den sequence is the most purely *fun* Vee has been on the page: the poke, the "Get up. Get up. Get up.", the shake down the front of his shorts, her dragging them off and waving them over her head like a flag over a taken field. I laughed out loud. I also clocked the thing she clocked: she hit his thigh and **he flinched**. "She had just watched it flinch." That is the first crack in the wall in thirty-one chapters and the chapter treats it as an almost-nothing, which is exactly how it would land in the moment and exactly why I noticed it.

Then the discovery that she's *faster than he is* — "quick and light… able to cut and reverse while all that mass of him had to gather and come around like a ship" — and her flashing him to make him come after her. A clumsy yank, the bra up with the top, "some small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it." That's the shame converting into play, and it's the happiest version of that conversion I've seen from her.

The bet is where it went from delightful to *scalding*. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." And then the hand on the inside of her knee, going up slow, and her "Don't" — and it's **gone**, instantly, palm turned up like a man showing empty hands. And then: "She had shooed it away, and she wanted it back. / 'Yes,' she said. / 'Yes?' / 'We find out.'" I have rarely read consent rendered so hot. The withdrawal is what makes the yes burn; he gave it back to her to hold, and she picked it up herself. The finger going in with no threshold at all — "Too easy. The ease went through her worse than any sting had. Her body had decided without her" — and then the whole length of it held up gleaming, "the old shame doubled, the wet and then the shameful plenty of the wet." That's her mother's voice without the mother having to speak a word, and then he disarms it by looking at it the way he looked at her under the tree the night she said she doesn't kiss a man she's known a few days. Like it was true, like it was fine, like it was hers, and good. I melted.

And then the spanking itself, which the chapter sets up beautifully to *defy*: Randi's story rises in her while she's bent and waiting — the counting, the theatre, the humiliation — and "What came was nothing like the story." Quiet. His hand resting until the skin "began, on its own, to want the thing it was afraid of." The first one almost nothing, a question. His mouth pressed to the warm pink of her. Her hips lifting into it while her mouth keeps saying *it's not, I don't* — "she could not have said which one of them she was lying to." That's the best sentence in the chapter. And the strap-line about the sheet afterward, her skin reading "every separate thread of it," is the kind of physical detail that made me put the page down for a second.

"Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness" — and inside that list of what was absent: *no Randi to be told*. That snagged me hard, because it's the one moment the chapter admits how much of Vee's life is now lived twice, once for herself and once as material for a lunch. Here she got a room with nobody in it but them, and she was, for once, not ashamed.

Then the aftercare, which is what actually undid me. The juice — "Juice. Sweet and cold and faintly ridiculous" — and the fact that he clearly *hadn't planned it*, "and that was the thing that undid her." Her head in his lap, "wonderful, wonderful," quieter each time. "Sated and safe and, for the first time she could remember, treasured." And the scrunchies: a whole form of them, deep green with white dots, no note, "more of them than a person could go through quickly, far more, and the plenty of them said the thing no note would have, that he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." That's the feeding motif and the *plenty* motif and a proposal in the shape of hair elastics. I got a lump in my throat over hair ties.

Suspicion: honestly, near zero *inside this chapter*. What I carry is structural, not earned by this page — I know Randi handed her the vocabulary she went in braced with, and I know the contrast between "the story" and what actually happened is only visible to me. The one live thread the chapter touched and left ajar is the scar — now "a clean surgeon's line," which is new and more specific than before — and she deliberately didn't ask, "the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep." I want that door opened and I trust that she doesn't.

Friction: very little. The bathroom monologue is the loosest register in the book — "My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky" is funny and true to her, but it's the one line that sounds like a person from a slightly different novel. And the closing curry paragraph goes a bit inventory ("green and red and yellow, carrots a hard bright orange") and then the chapter simply *stops* on a beer and a late heat, with no turn. I didn't mind the softness, but I looked for a last beat that wasn't there.

The title: **"We Find Out."** Her line, her yes, the whole chapter in three words — and it's a title that only pays off *after* you've read it, which I liked; going in it meant nothing, coming out it's the exact hinge. It gave nothing away. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**: the spoon is the one that keeps me cold in a warm chapter — you sup with the devil with a long spoon — and *A Polite Invitation* still reads like an invitation not yet issued, which after Randi's practice-room work I assume is coming for Vee, in words, from someone smiling.

What I want next: her to keep this. What I expect: to be told about it at a lunch.
