# Cold read (grounded) — Two Towels

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I spent the first half of this chapter happily wandering a house with her, and the last third with my stomach somewhere around my knees. The house is smaller than either of us had built it — post-war, vinyl siding, a dark sconce bulb waiting for night — and I liked Vee enormously for how she reads a man off his objects: the shoe closet ("Depends how many things he does"), the knives that aren't a set, the chipped white KitchenAid worn to a shine. That's her econ brain, the gap between what people say they're doing and what they're doing, turned on a man who, for once, matches. The office pleased me most — the one room that "had thrown over order entirely," green graph paper in square handwriting, and her joke, "I didn't realize you knew Sanskrit," and him not explaining it down to her size. She is *glad he stayed exactly as far past her as he was.* That's a real sentence about desire.

And then the title did its work before the chapter did. "Two towels on the rod." One clause, in passing, in a plain clean bathroom, and I went cold, because I've been in Randi's chapter and I know she stays over. Vee walked straight past it. So did the prose — no weight, no pause, on to the office. That is the cruelest, most elegant thing this book has done to me yet. And it rhymes with the other thing she almost said and didn't: the house was *too warm*, and she nearly asked *did you leave the heat on*, and filed it "the way you walk past a wrapped box on the counter without asking who it's for." Ch 1 opened with that same too-warm house and a woman bolting the door behind her. Vee is standing inside another woman's evening and reading it as a small mystery of him.

The closed door at the end of the hall undid me twice. She'd dressed for it — "the satin a girl wears hoping the man won't hand her a reason not to show it to him" — she *leaned*, and waited for his hand to go to the handle, and he said "My bedroom" flat as he'd said kitchen, and left it shut, and "Something in her dropped," and she didn't let her face do a thing. I ached for her there, genuinely. And then twenty minutes later he asks to measure her "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light," and she says yes twice with no hedge, and I realized the shut door wasn't a refusal, it was a price. Whether he *meant* it that way I honestly can't tell, and the page doesn't tell me — that's the vertigo. Both readings fit him perfectly, which is what he does to me every chapter.

The dress is the swoon and it's a big one. I love that her first beat was disappointment — wrong red, not the magazine red — and that the turn came in the mirror: he hadn't matched the dress she described, he'd matched *her*. Cherries, silver sky, guard down, a thing she named once and set back on the shelf inside the same breath, and he picked it up off the rock without ever picking it up. Then the shirt with no tag — I gasped out loud at that, because I'd read that detail twice in her own voice and filed it under "counterfeit, sample" right along with her. "I couldn't find a shirt I liked, so I made one," and then reaching for the tape measure, done with being admired. He is *so* good at this. And the error-correcting codes speech is the same gesture in math: "Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it." A man whose whole method is redundancy — say the kindness in a hundred small forms so no single one has to carry it. And whose patent is finding the true picture buried in noise in a fraction of the passes. I don't think the book is winking; I think it's just handing me his mind and letting me feel how well it would work on me.

Two small cold spots. "It does other things. That's the part I like" — he likes the *general* tool more than the application, and the application is what makes people cry. And the flicker at *parents*: "a small closing, there and gone, a door easing shut two rooms away." First thing he's withheld from her on the page, and the book chose door imagery to do it. I noticed. She didn't.

Friction: the middle sags a little. Shoes, kitchen, office, books, garage, sunporch — each stop lovely, but the codes lecture and the MRI lecture land back-to-back and the chapter stops moving for them. And the summit flashback is re-narrated more fully than I needed ("She'd hung the idea back up beside the good-enough black dress") — I remembered the cherries fine; the paragraph is the author making sure. Also notable by absence: her mother's voice never once speaks in this chapter. Out of the dorm, off campus, in his house, the voice goes quiet. I don't know yet whether that's freedom or anesthesia.

Titles: "Two Towels" meant nothing going in, put me faintly on alert, and then detonated in one clause. It didn't spoil — it *aimed* me, and I'd rather have been aimed. **WITH A LONG SPOON** I keep hearing as the proverb about supping with the devil, and **BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** now reads as this exact text message: "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." The least information a human being has ever sent her. The politeness *is* the mechanism. No tagline in my packet.

What I want next: the measuring, and I want it badly and with dread in equal parts — she will stand in the brightest room in his house in satin she chose for him, and he will be perfectly correct, and it will be the most erotic thing in the book. *Guess from here:* the shut bedroom door has the bench in it, top back on, waiting. And Randi doesn't know about the dress.
