# Cold read (grounded) — All Told

## Reader reaction

**All Told**

Here's the thing that got me, and it got me sideways: this is a chapter where almost nothing happens — a girl is late, a girl sits down between two friends, a professor draws a table of baseball numbers — and I finished it colder than I've been since chapter one.

Because the professor says it out loud. "Each year on its own, the first man is the better hitter. Put the two years together and the second man is. Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them." And *he let it sit*. I have been reading this book one year at a time. Every single scene, taken on its own, is genuinely what it looks like: Pace's tenderness is real, Randi's warmth is real, the dress is real, Vee's happiness is real. Nobody has touched one of the numbers. And put together, the answer reverses. That's not a metaphor I went looking for — the man in the front of the room put it up on the board over Vee's head while she was busy being happy, exactly the way *latent variables* went up over her head in chapter four, exactly the way she wrote down the one phrase and then went off with Randi. Twice now this professor has described her life to her and she's taken forty honest seconds and come back up.

But the part I actually loved — the part that made me put my hand over my mouth a little, the way Vee did at the dress — is Cassie. "It was when she came back up out of the numbers that she understood Cassie had never gone down into them at all." Cassie hadn't looked at the professor once. Cassie doesn't care about the dress; Cassie has worn the same fleece through two winters. Cassie was watching *what the dress did to Vee*, because the friend is the only part of any of it Cassie has ever been able to see. God. And then the exchange that's the whole chapter for me: "Was it a good night?" — "Yes. Oh, yes." And *something eased in Cassie's face, and she picked her highlighter back up.* That's it. That's love with no extraction in it at all. Cassie cracked the made bed hours ago and let Randi have the reveal anyway. I would walk through fire for her.

Which makes the seating arrangement almost too on the nose to bear: Vee dropped into the seat they left open between them, "turned between the two of them, happy in the middle." One of them wants her to be all right. One of them wants the details. Both of them are getting what they came for.

Randi's beat did two things to me at once, and I'm still sorting them. "You lucky slut," low and delighted from the aisle — and it lands lower than she meant it to, because Vee's body has been "keeping the night's account since eight, a good soreness no gym had ever given her." That's the mother's word arriving in the beloved friend's mouth, and for a second Vee is certain the room can read her. It's the closest this chapter comes to heat and it's a real flush: the soreness inventory, the nipples against the bra, the fear of being legible. Then Randi finds the one snag in the gold of the day — the shoes — inside a single breath, and converts it into Saturday. "That's not a problem, that's a *date*." "Don't you dare tell me no." I felt the warmth of it go through me the way it went through Vee, and I also felt the machinery: Randi is going shoe shopping for a dress her boyfriend sat up all night finishing. She knows what she's buying heels for. And Cassie's eyebrow went up and came back down, and Cassie has never once cared what was on anyone's feet — which is the book telling me, quietly, that Cassie can see the shape of Randi's attention even if she can't name it.

Do I suspect anyone new? No. Nothing moved; everything hardened. My unease isn't about a revealed lie, it's about the aggregate, and this chapter handed me the word for the aggregate.

Friction: one line snagged. The last clause — the dress "still shown to not one living soul." She showed it to Pace, gloriously, in a window full of gold light, and I had to reread to decide the sentence means *nobody in her world* has seen it. A fractional wobble on a closing line I otherwise loved, because the rest of it is perfect: happy and starving and "on time, now, for nothing."

The title. **All Told** — the sum. That's Simpson's paradox exactly: *all told*, the answer flips. And the second meaning is the crueler one, because all is precisely what is *not* told: Vee tells, and skips the middle; Randi listens, and is the middle; Cassie asks four words and gets the one true thing. It didn't give anything away going in — it meant nothing to me on the first line and recolored the whole chapter on the last. That's the good kind.

The volume line, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**: I keep hearing *he who sups with the devil*, and I keep seeing spoons ringing in cups, everybody stirring up the sweetness at the bottom. And *A Polite Invitation* — this chapter ends on one. "You and me, Saturday." It's the politest thing in the book and I don't want her to go, and I want her to go, and I know which of those the book intends to give me.

What I want next: the shoe trip, with dread and appetite in equal measure. What I dread: that Randi will put Vee in front of a mirror in heels and Vee will be the most looked-at she's ever been, and the person doing the looking will be the one who pointed. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the induction is the room where the two seasons get added together.
