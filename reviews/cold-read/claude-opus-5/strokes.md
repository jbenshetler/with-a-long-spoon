# Cold read (grounded) — Strokes

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one is just her, alone, in the one room in the book where nobody is doing anything to her — and after thirty-odd chapters of being fed, measured, dressed, coached, buckled, spanked and *seen*, the relief of that was physical. No Randi. No Pace. No Cassie. Just lane three, chosen by nobody, held by unspoken agreement between three strangers who have never once discussed it. That detail landed on me harder than it should have: it's the only arrangement in Vee's life that nobody engineered.

The thing that got me in the throat was the suit. "The green-and-white suit had nowhere left to go on her and hadn't for years. She looked at the water instead of at that." One sentence, and the whole mother's-voice apparatus is still running, even here, even in the place the checkpoint told me was safe. The pool isn't a refuge from her body's shame so much as a place where she can decline to look at it for seventy-five minutes. And then the payoff — "her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here. It only worked." That's the closest thing to peace this book has given her, and it's not love, it's *labor*. I found that moving in a plain, unsentimental way.

Structurally the chapter is one gorgeous trick: the erosion of language by effort. *Do you love him* gets in off the third wall. Then it's a pressure under the ribs with dread riding it. Then by the sixth "she had *him*, and the ache, and the count. / Then the count." That paragraph break did the work of a whole scene. And the four strokes in the quiet where she can't tell "whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good" — *that* is the live wire, and the book is honest enough to give it exactly four strokes and then take it away with the wall. It doesn't let her solve it. It doesn't let her avoid it either. I'm sitting here with it instead, which I suspect is the point, and I don't resent it.

Trust: I have no new suspicion of anyone, because nobody's here. What I have is a colder version of an old one. She asks herself *did he mind* about her brush and pins on his counter, and she's been turning it over since Saturday — and I know she's been given the top drawer, the easy one, the one your hand goes to first, with room to spare for more of her. So I'm holding a kindness she can't feel, which is the exact inverse of the thing I usually hold about these two (a manipulation she can't see). That was an odd, sweet ache. And yet: *he never says.* She keeps having to read him. A man who communicates only in unsigned gestures keeps every gesture deniable, and she keeps doing the interpretive labor. The page doesn't push that; I just noticed it in the silence.

The *maybe* is the bruise. In "Above Him" she said it to herself flat out, in the dark, unretractable — she loved him. Then Cassie asked her the plain question and out came *maybe*, and here she still has "no idea in the world" why. I don't think she's lying to Cassie. I think saying it to Cassie would make it a thing in the world, and things in the world can be taken. That reading is mine, not the page's — the page leaves it genuinely open, and I like that it does.

Friction: one real snag. The counter-and-brush worry reads as still-unresolved *since Saturday*, but I've already watched her open that drawer and find her place made. Either this chapter sits earlier than the one before it, or she's carrying a worry the drawer should have answered. I couldn't tell which, and I re-checked the lines twice. Small, but it put a hitch in an otherwise seamless run. Otherwise almost no thumb on the scale — the closest is "It never answered anything, the swimming. That was the whole use of it," which is a hair too neatly aphoristic for a woman with her lungs still going, but it's earned enough that I let it pass.

**Strokes** — swim strokes, and the count of them, obviously. But this book has spent three chapters on a hand coming down, on Randi's *counting out loud*, on a bet paid in strokes on a bare bottom, and the title knows it. It also caught me sideways as brushstrokes: the four-stroke doubt about whether she painted him. Three meanings, none announced, none cheap. Best chapter title so far. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — the proverb's second half is the one nobody prints: *he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon.* Vee has been supping for two months with no spoon at all, and every course has been delicious. "A polite invitation" gets more sinister the politer everyone gets. My packet has no jacket copy and no tagline, only that cover-board line.

What I want next: her to be asked again when she isn't out of breath. What I dread: that the next person to ask her is Randi, in a warm room, with a door offered and then withdrawn.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee. Background only, unnamed and unspeaking: the butterfly man, the woman with the good flip turn, a lifeguard with a radio. *Mentioned-only:* Pace, Cassie.

**Heat:** 1 — faint; "him, and the ache" flickers, but the body here is engine, not appetite, and the suit is looked away from.

**Romance:** 1 — the love-question dominates, but as unresolved private ache; no one is in the room to be tender with.

**Motifs & images:** **Water/the pool** (repeat — ch 7 with Cassie, the place her body was never a problem; here striped into lanes and made a discipline). **Counting** (repeat, and pointed — Vee counting on the dance floor and ruining it, ch 26; Randi's *counting out loud*, ch 30; here the count is what's left when words burn off). **The black line / the lane** (first sighting, and it rhymes with the returned pencil and the dance frame: a rule that carries her). **The mother's voice** (repeat, by absence — the suit sentence is it, unspoken). **Doors and drawers/order** (repeat — his one clean counter). **Streetlight** (repeat from ch 31/33, in Cassie's remembered voice). **Wet hair, chlorine, the scrunchies' cousin** — hair-as-wreck-to-be-managed (repeat).

**Symbolism:** The lane you take by no arrangement anybody made, that the others arrange themselves around — the one unengineered place in her life. The set that "never answered anything" and takes the words off things until only the shape is left: the chapter's own method, and a quiet argument that Vee knows something she can't yet phrase. The four strokes of *is he that good or did I make him* — a doubt with a wall coming.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens — first chapter to show her competence, her private discipline, a self that predates and excludes everyone courting her, and the one question she can't answer awake. Cassie, in one remembered flat sentence, is still the only instrument that reads true. Pace exists here only as something she turns over, which is exactly right.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Earns every line; short, tight, no drag. The set's structure *is* the structure. Only the shower-philosophy beat runs a half-sentence long.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate breath after a heavy run — induction, the bet, the hall walk, the bistro, the drawer, Cassie's question. Momentum holds rather than builds, and I wanted the hold; four consecutive Vee-interior chapters is as many as I'd want before we get back to Randi or Pace unobserved.
