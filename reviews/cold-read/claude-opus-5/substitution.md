## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this one hurt in the good way and the bad way at once. The whole chapter is Vee being given the best forty minutes of her life, and I'm sitting outside it with the door I can't knock on. She's stuck on the integral, the thumb-curl in the corner of the page, the pencil set down so she won't snap it — I liked her instantly and completely there, the private stubbornness of *"I want to do it,"* said to the page and not to Randi. And then a man crosses a coffee shop and looks only at her, and the room settles a half-degree. I *felt* that. I felt her shoulders change. And I knew whose boyfriend he was, and the knowing sat in my chest like swallowed ice for eleven pages.

Pace — God. He is genuinely, infuriatingly good at this. Reading the problem twice, slowly, like he's never seen it. Asking what her *professor* said the trick was. The margin note instead of the rewrite. Handing the pencil back at exactly the line where the solving becomes hers. *"She hadn't known she was allowed."* That sentence broke something open in me. And the *may* — "May I call you?" — landing in her like a word from another vocabulary. I swooned. I want to be clear that I swooned, because that's the trap the chapter is: he is not faking the attention. That's what's so unbearable. The attention is real and it is also *aimed*, deployed, the end of a game that started with a finger pointed across a quad. He told Randi *I point, you pick.* This is him collecting.

And Randi. Randi, who I have loved and ached over for two chapters, is sitting right there with her hand on a coffee cup. Twice the cup comes to her mouth and no sip happens. That detail is the whole chapter for me — the water-bottle cap under the thumbnail, the same body doing small private damage where nobody is looking. She's texting before he arrives; she's the one who suggested Wilson. I think she arranged this and I think it is eating her alive in real time, and I cannot tell — the page won't tell me — whether what's eating her is jealousy of Vee, jealousy of Pace, or the horror of watching her own wanting get handed to her boyfriend to carry. She asks him the Bessel's correction question, which is a bid, a small flare shot up: *look at me.* He gives her a textbook answer and turns back. "Right. Right, I knew that." She knew that. She asked anyway. That's the loneliest line in the chapter.

Cassie, who I already half-trusted after that flat *Goodbye, Vee*, does the thing nobody else will: says it out loud. *"He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt."* And then Vee's own quiet note — that Cassie knows that look because it's the one that slides off her every day. Cassie is the only person at that table with clean hands, and she's also the one who pointed him out: *"That cute guy in line is looking at you."* She handed her over. She doesn't know. I'd like to protect her from ever finding out, which is not an option available to me.

The thing that most unsettled me wasn't the seduction. It was Vee reaching for Randi's bit. "Vivienne Thorne. V.T. Yes, like the university." She *borrowed it* — tucked it away in the lecture hall to try on later — and it lands, and she feels the lift of borrowed poise, and then she says "Vee, people call me Vee" because she suddenly wants her own name back. One page later Randi hears her own routine come out of Vee's mouth and answers with a single bare word: *"Randi."* She would not take the bit. She has stopped doing the bit. I don't know if she's furious, or ashamed, or watching herself be replaced by a warmer copy. The page leaves it shut and I'm leaving it shut.

Friction, honestly: the chapter over-explains Pace's technique. That long paragraph parsing his tutoring — "the path of a person who wasn't performing — not showing off, not hiding it either" — does in four sentences what the returned pencil already did in one gesture, and I felt the author's hand steadying my chin. Same with Euler's: "Put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain — easy to name, easy to know what to do with." Beautiful, and *awfully* convenient as thesis-in-costume for what three people are about to become. I enjoyed it and I saw the wires.

**Titles.** *Substitution* is the sharpest chapter title yet — the calculus trick (turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do), and then everything else: Vee substituted for the woman Randi can't admit she wants; Pace substituted in as the one who does the wanting out loud; Vee substituting Randi's own line into her own mouth. And, coldly, Vee substituted *for Randi* in his attention, in the chair, in the looking. It didn't give anything away going in — it recolored hard on the way out. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**: the spoon is louder now. He that sups with the devil — and I keep hearing the spoons ringing all through Chi Latte, everyone stirring the sweetness up from the bottom. *A polite invitation* is exactly what *May I call you?* is. Politeness as the delivery mechanism. I dread it and I'm turning pages fast.

What I want: for Vee's phone to light up, because she's lovely and she deserves this, and simultaneously for it never to. Guess from here: Randi will discover that a gift given away is still gone.
