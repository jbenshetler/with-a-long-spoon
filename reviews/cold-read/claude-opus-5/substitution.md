## Reader reaction

# Substitution

Oh, this one hurt in the best way. I spent the whole chapter inside Vee's chest being delighted, and knowing.

Because I was in the room on the quad. I watched Pace point and Randi say *yes* before she could dress it up, I heard her ask *do you think she'll like the bench*, and now here's Vee at the place on Wilson — *the place on Wilson*, the same address Randi handed Brooke as an alibi, which made my skin prickle — being stuck on a problem while Randi thumbs at her phone with "the private, absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention." Vee thinks: *some boy.* I thought: the bell over the door is about to ring. And it did. That gap is the whole chapter and it is delicious and it is awful, and the awfulness didn't cancel the delicious, which is the thing I want to be honest about. Pace was *good*. The tutoring was good. He read the problem twice like he hadn't seen it, asked what her professor said instead of what he'd say, wrote the ugly formula and then told her not to carry it, and put the pencil back in her hand at exactly the line where she could finish it herself. "She hadn't known she was allowed." I melted at that. I have been taught by people who couldn't keep themselves out of it, and I felt what she felt.

And then the chapter told me what it was doing, in Vee's own mouth, and I got chills: *the trick to applied math is always to turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already knew how to do.* That's the title. That's the method — and it's being run on her, not just with her. "On their own these two are a struggle — you push and they won't give, and neither one quite looks like what it is. Put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain." He's talking about Euler. He's also describing Randi and Vee. I don't think the page winks; I think he means the math. That's what makes it land instead of clang.

What moved for me: Pace. I have loved his attention for three chapters — the apple sliced cut-side up, *tell me your word*, the closed bathroom door he refused to open because Daphne taught him what that costs. He is scrupulous. And here that same scrupulousness is deployed on a stranger who does not know she was *chosen off a walk* two days ago, and I couldn't stop hearing *may I.* The word is real. The frame around it isn't. He isn't lying to Vee — but he crossed that floor already knowing her, and she thinks she's meeting weather. That's not a consent I can rest in, and it's the first time in this book I've felt genuinely uneasy about him rather than fascinated.

Randi is the one I can't read, and I love that the page won't let me. The cup. Twice up to her mouth, twice down, no sip happening. One bare word for her name — *Randi* — when she has a whole practiced bit for that, the very bit Vee borrows and performs to Pace a half-minute earlier, having "tucked it away to try on later." Vee is wearing Randi's manner at Randi's man while Randi holds a cup she isn't drinking from. Is that jealousy? Arousal? The Bessel's-correction question — a thing she surely knows, asked to make him turn — reads to me like a hand reaching for the wheel. But she also says *you should go out with him*, and does the accounting about the abandoned coffee. I genuinely don't know if she's steering, competing, or sick with wanting, and I'm not going to decide for her.

Cassie is my favorite person in this book and she's barely in it. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." Said flat, from the next table, by the girl the look slides off every day — Vee notices that and the page doesn't underline it further. Cassie sees Randi clearly ("She's not serious," or that's how Vee read it) and says four words and they're all load-bearing. After her "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter I've been carrying a small dread about her, and this chapter didn't soothe it.

Heat: this isn't a sex chapter and it ran hot anyway, in the way being *chosen* runs hot. The rolled cuffs — "They were not a math tutor's forearms" — and her eye staying a half-second past where she'd have chosen. The unfinished sentences: "He is here for the math help. He is also—" and "The way he had—". Twice her body outruns her and the thought just stops. That's the most erotic device in the chapter and it's about a pencil. The *may I* did it to me too, frankly. Where it went slack: the Bessel's exchange is deliberately flat, which is the point, and the econ monologue summarized rather than dramatized ran a touch long.

Friction: very little. The one place I felt a thumb was the stacked catalogue of what he'd done right — "a margin note, not a rewrite… gave her back the pencil at the exact line" — which is Vee thinking in the author's voice a bit. But she *is* the girl with the eye for how smart people handle problems, so I forgave it.

Titles. **Substitution** is the best chapter title yet: the calculus move, and Vee as substitute — for what, or for whom, I don't know, and the ambiguity is the knife. **A POLITE INVITATION** just clicked audibly: *May I call you?* And **WITH A LONG SPOON** — I know the proverb, and I now feel it hovering over Vee, who has no spoon at all, no idea she's at the table. No tagline in what I was shown.

What I want: for Vee to be happy, absurdly, and to keep the pencil. What I dread: that she finds out how she was arrived at — and that Randi, who handed her want to Pace as a gift because owning it was "unsurvivable," will be the one who has to watch someone else receive it.
