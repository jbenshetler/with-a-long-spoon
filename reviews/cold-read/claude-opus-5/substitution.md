## Reader reaction

**Substitution**

Oh, this one got me, and then it made me feel slightly sick, and both of those are compliments.

I came into this chapter knowing something Vee doesn't, and the whole hour at the coffee shop is lit by that. Randi *suggested they move to the place on Wilson.* Randi is on her phone, "thumbs quick, eyes down, the private, absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention," and Vee thinks *some boy, amused,* and I thought: no, sweetheart, that's the delivery instruction. Then the bell over the door. I actually sat forward. The staging is immaculate and I was inside it on two levels at once — swept up in the meeting *as Vee experienced it* and watching the floor get built under her.

And here's the thing I didn't expect: the seduction **worked on me**, straight, no irony. The math scene is the most romantic thing in this book so far and there isn't a hand on a body in it. "What did your professor say the trick was, with these?" — asking her teacher's method instead of supplying his own. The margin note instead of the rewrite. *"He set the pencil back in her hand."* And Vee's "She hadn't known she was allowed." I melted. Then the little cross-mark — two things that won't give on their own, put at right angles, turning into one plain thing — is maybe the best-made image in the book, because it *is* the chapter's method and the book's, and the page never once says so out loud. And "Vee" getting her own name back after she borrows Randi's bit: *"Vee," she added, quickly, suddenly wanting her own name back.* Lovely.

My feelings moved most about **Randi**. I came in holding her with enormous tenderness after the bench and that locked bathroom door. Here she is the one *arranging* an ambush of the softest person in the room, and I still can't hate her, because the page keeps her hands busy: the cup held in both hands a second longer than a sip needs, twice to the mouth with no sip happening. Vee reads it as boredom. I read it as something with no name yet — jealousy, nerves, the weird vertigo of watching the man who found *you* do the exact thing to someone else — and I'm grateful the chapter doesn't tell me which. It leaves it ajar and I'll leave it ajar. Also: when she introduces herself she gives Pace **one bare word**, "Randi," no adjective bit, no parents-had-a-sense-of-humor. She handed that bit away to Vee ten pages ago and here Vee is wearing it while Randi goes flat as Cassie. That inversion did something cold to the back of my neck.

**Pace** is where my trust genuinely cracks for the first time. Every single behavior on this page is exemplary — the attention, the restraint, "*may* I call you," giving back the pencil — and every single one of them is being deployed at a woman who does not know she was pointed at across a quad two days ago. The consent architecture he built so carefully around Randi (the word, *tell me anytime*, the closed door he wouldn't open) has no equivalent here. Vee has no safeword because Vee doesn't know she's in a scene. That's not me hunting for a shadow; it's the plain arithmetic of what I was shown last chapter and what I was shown this one. The warmth is real *and* it is aimed. Both.

**Cassie** is the only person in the room with clean hands and she gets the best line in the chapter: "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." And then the page quietly breaks my heart — "It was the one that slid off Cassie every day of her life, and Cassie had named it without a flicker." After her flat "Goodbye, Vee," I'm now actively worried for Cassie. She sees everything and says four words a day.

**Vee** I simply love, and that's the problem. She's warm, sharp, hungry, cataloguing a room for where she fits, keeping her cardigan buttoned over the better shirt. Her elation at the solved integral — "the private elation of a person who has just been handed something she is going to use for the rest of her life" — is pure joy and I was happy for her without reservation for about four seconds.

**Friction:** almost none, but Vee's long interior appreciation of Pace's tutoring technique ("the path of a person who wasn't performing — not showing off, not hiding it either") runs a beat past what she could plausibly articulate mid-swoon, and the page half-admits it with "the thought did not finish." Twice, actually — the unfinished thought happens twice in a row and the second one felt like a device rather than a mind. Minor.

**The titles.** *Substitution* is doing at least four jobs and none of them spoiled the chapter — it's a calculus term (turn the thing you can't do into a thing you can), it's Randi substituting a safe story for an unsurvivable want, it's Vee substituting Randi's borrowed persona for her own, and — the one that landed last and hardest — it's Vee being substituted *in*. **WITH A LONG SPOON** I finally feel: you sup with the devil with a long spoon, and the devil here is charming, patient, and hands you back the pencil. **A POLITE INVITATION** now reads as the dryest joke in the book. Everything that has happened to Vee has been polite. All of it has been an invitation.

What I want: for Vee to be told. What I expect (guess from here): she won't be, not for a long while. What I dread: Randi discovering her own want has teeth, with Vee in the room.
