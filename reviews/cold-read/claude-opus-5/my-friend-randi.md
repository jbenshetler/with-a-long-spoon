## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter did something I didn't expect: it made me *grateful* to Randi and sick about it in the same breath, and it never once let me separate the two. Vee comes off a red-eye with her face wrecked and walks across campus to the one door she trusts, and what she gets behind it is real. Randi comes down those stairs *fast* — gets her past Brooke and past the eyes before Vee has to be a spectacle — sweeps the Gstaad dresses and the coat in its plastic off the bed onto the floor without a glance, and Vee registers it: "none of it rated a look, that the wrecked girl in her doorway came first." That's love, and the page lets it be love. I felt it in my chest.

And then Randi uses it. Not coldly — that's the thing. She takes Vee's catastrophe apart with genuine skill and most of what she says is *true*: the house warmed hours early, the wine poured out and waiting, the phone in the other room. "That's not a man who's done with you, honey. That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." She's right. She's also the woman in his bed. She is advocating for her own lover to her own beloved, and she does it so well that I was nodding along with Vee. The one that got under my skin: *"You don't do it with words. Words are the whole war."* Coming from the woman who decided in Gstaad that she will never, ever say hers — that's self-prescription dressed as advice, and the page doesn't underline it, which is why it landed so hard.

The biggest thing I don't understand, and I'm turning it over: she *had* the opening. Vee handed her a phantom blonde and begged to be told Pace was gone. Randi could have let that stand and kept her. Instead she dismantled it — and she's met Sheri, she knows exactly how fictional that blonde is. So why push Vee back toward him? Because she wants Vee happy. Because she can't survive being the reason it ended. Because the old game is still running and she wants them both. I genuinely can't tell, and the chapter leaves it open rather than tipping. That's the suspicion the text earned: not that Randi is lying here — she mostly isn't — but that I don't know what she's for anymore, and I'm not sure she does.

The erotic charge is all in one place and it's hot: the coat. Randi stops at "You've got that good long coat" and *waits* — makes Vee finish the sentence herself, so the obscenity is in Vee's own mouth — and then the mother's voice arrives on schedule, the burn up the neck, "It's such a slutty thing to do," which Randi makes her *say*. And then: "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's the book's whole engine in one clause and it worked on me. Shame as the accelerant. Then the black heels pressed into her hands — of course the shoes, Randi always does the shoes — and Vee grateful that one piece of tonight is already decided for her. Earlier, smaller and almost better: Vee pulling a breath off Randi's neck, "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet," and taking another, "and didn't want to stop," and Randi holding the hug "past where a hug stops." Neither of them names it. Vee never names it. I'm starting to think she's chosen not to.

Pace isn't here and I missed him, which is new for me. After him alone with the chicken and the truck that didn't slow, I wanted someone to tell Vee what I know. Randi more or less did — the right conclusion from the wrong person, for reasons I can't audit. I'm rooting hard for tonight and dreading it, because Vee is walking out there bare under a coat on the strength of a plan given to her by his other lover, and whatever happens on that porch, Randi will have engineered it.

Friction, honestly: Randi's counseling is almost *too* good — the therapeutic sequence ("Not questions. Just setting each thing down where Vee could see it") is virtuosic enough that I felt the author's hand briefly. It earned it back with "like a raccoon who lost a fight," which is exactly the deflation that keeps her human. And the Gstaad retelling is beautifully placed — Randi performs the fairytale, Vee takes it like a knife ("a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it"), and then Randi hits the hotel room and just stops. "And then it got very late, and we went up." Vee reads discretion. I read the empty room, the face put back on, the nothing. That gap is the best thing in the chapter.

**The title** — "My Friend Randi" is Vee's only working name for anyone. She can't name Pace, the sorority files her as "the stats friend," and the one label she *has* is the one that's most wrong. It doesn't give anything away; it recolors everything afterward, bitterly. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**: the spoon is for supping with the devil, and I'm now watching Vee hold the handle by the wrong end. "A warm reception" lands triple — the sorority hall, Randi's arms, and whatever Vee is about to open that coat on. No tagline or jacket copy was supplied to me.
