## Reader reaction

This is the one the whole book has been leaning toward, and it landed hard. After chapters of him stopping — the withholding, the "just looking," the closed door — this is the scene where the stopping ends, and the release of that pressure is real. I felt it in my body before I could think about it.

**Vee.** I'm all the way in with her, and she's never been more vividly herself. The dressing scene — the bed filling up with what she tried and took off, the kitten heels that "said out loud the thing she was trying not to," the ballet flats "flat and easy and nothing to answer for" with the frosted plum "gone dark and hidden inside them" — is the best rendering of her I've had. She's performing not-having-tried while having tried enormously, and I love her for it. The green satin she bought "without trying it on, for the color," the matching bra, the crystal-string panties she chose without asking herself why: she's dressing a secret for a man and pretending to herself she isn't. And the shame engine kept working on me — "her body announcing itself ahead of her," the *floozie* voice arriving a beat behind the heat, "she could not find the seam between the heat and the shame because there was no seam, they ran on the same wire." That's the thing this book keeps doing and it hasn't gone stale.

What actually moved me was the last third of the sex: the shame *not closing her*. Every prior scene, the shame crests and shuts her down. Here — "she waited for the shame to crest and close her, the way it always had, and it didn't." "The shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her." "It was the first time that had been true." I felt that as a genuine liberation, and I'm uneasy about how glad I am for her, given what I carry about the two of them.

**Pace.** God help me, he works. The apron over the henley, everything mise-en-place'd and *stopped* — "He'd done all the preparation and stopped" — that's his whole signature rendered as cooking, and it's beautiful and it's ominous at once. The line that detonated for me: "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" A real question and something else, and the text tells me she knew both. His whole erotic mode is engineering her into producing proof of her own wanting and then *treasuring* the proof — "nothing in how he held her wanted her to be any less than she was." That's the most seductive thing a man's done in this book and it's also exactly the machine I've been dreading. I fell for him in the scene. I distrust him out of it. The book wants both and got both from me.

**Two cold objects I clocked.** The scar — "surgical and old," inside the thigh near the hip. She "filed the scar in the place where she kept facts without frames." I filed it too. I don't know what it is. Given the earlier "right hand only" and the "hidden real name" and the flinch at "parents," I've got a small growing pile of things about his body and history that don't have explanations yet. *Guess from here:* something about that scar connects to the right-hand-only eating, or to whatever the MRI/patent money or his past is — but I'm genuinely just holding it, not solving it.

And the panties. "Trade." She folds them with the spot centered and gives them to him, and "something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private." On the page it reads as her boldest, most owned gesture yet — she's giving him the evidence instead of hiding it. But I carry from before that Pace and Randi are *collecting* Vee, orchestrating her, and a folded, wet, chosen-green trophy handed over reads differently in that light. I can't tell if his private shoulders are tenderness overwhelming him or something being logged. The book made me unable to tell, deliberately, and that's the cold under the warm.

**Erotic charge.** Working, strongly, and cleanest in the standing oral passage — "he met it before he had bared her at all," the crystal strings "obvious and excessive," her hips moving without her telling them, "the sound that came out of her with no word in it." It peaks there because the shame and the heat are fused in real time and she's burning through instead of shutting down. It goes slightly abstract at the actual intercourse — "the answer to his question arrived, unmistakably" — which is a deliberate pull-back into interiority (she's not narrating it, which is the point), and I didn't mind it, but it's the least sensory beat of the sex.

**Friction.** Very little. The one line I noticed as maybe a touch too neat is "Like a short story, she told the mirror, long enough to cover the subject and short enough to keep you reading" — it's charming and it's *her*, English-student self-aware, but it's the one moment I felt the prose being clever slightly above where she'd actually be. She half-catches it herself ("a nervous thing to say to an empty room"), which mostly saves it. Also "The evening had used her well" — I liked it, but "used" is doing quiet ominous work and I noticed the thumb, just barely.

**Randi absent, and I felt it.** No brunch, no Randi. And yet the whole scene is downstream of the fitting she extracted, and Vee dressing a secret is exactly the material Randi's been mining. I kept half-expecting the phone to buzz. It didn't. That absence felt loaded — this is the body track (Pace) running its scene clean while I know the word track (Randi) exists and Vee doesn't know they touch.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the morning-after, and I want to know what she does with the scar, and — most of all — I dread the next brunch, because Vee is going to *retell this* to Randi, and Randi is going to extract it. That's the collision I've been set up for: the most private, liberating night of Vee's life becomes raw material for the woman secretly co-running her. I turn the page wanting Vee to keep this to herself and knowing she won't.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Pace texted "Dinner Friday?" before flying to Chicago; the week went quiet (he doesn't text much). Vee bought green satin lingerie "for the color" and frosted plum polish, agonized over dressing to look good without looking like she'd tried, and drove to his house. He'd prepped a fra diavolo and *stopped* — everything ready, pan dry. She kissed him first ("Famished"), pulled him to the bedroom, and they slept together — his signature move ("Would you be bothered if I was too hard?"), standing oral, then intercourse. For the first time the shame ran the whole way through and never shut her down. Undressing him she found an old surgical scar high on his inner thigh; he wordlessly moved her hand off it; she filed it "without a frame." Afterward they ate in silence, she wrapped in his sheet. At the end she fetched her worn green panties, folded them with the wet spot centered, and handed them to him — "Trade." Something in his shoulders went private; she let it.

**How I feel.** Swept up and uneasy in equal measure — the book made me fall for the night and distrust the man in the same breath. Genuine joy at Vee's shame not closing her, shadowed by everything I carry: they're collecting her. The folded panties and the private shoulders read as tender to her and possibly as trophy-logging to me; the book won't let me decide, on purpose. Mood: the warmest, most consummated chapter yet, cold running quietly underneath.

**Open questions.** What is the scar (ties to right-hand-only? his past? the money?) — the pile of unexplained body/history facts is growing. What was "private" in his shoulders when she handed him the panties — moved, or collecting? When does Vee retell this to Randi, and what does Randi do with it? When does Vee learn Pace and Randi already know each other? The dress he's making, and what night it's for. The closed bedroom door / two towels are now partly answered (this is his room) but the collaboration behind the scenes is not.
