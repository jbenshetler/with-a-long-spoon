## Reader reaction

Oh, my chest hurts. Because on its own, inside Vee's skin, this is one of the loveliest, dreamiest first dates I have ever read — and I *cannot* read it on its own, because I know how he ended up at her table in `Substitution`, I know Randi is at home imagining lifting Vee's breasts up to him in his bedroom, and I know he already has a girlfriend. That split in me ran the entire length of the chapter: melting with Vee in real time while my stomach knotted underneath it.

Start with the swoon, because pretending it didn't work on me would be dishonest. Everything Vee feels here makes complete, devastating sense. After the bleachers and the boys who sit down like dropped backpacks and make you roll a healer till 2 a.m., here is a man who tells her not to answer until tomorrow ("He just left it with me. Like it could keep"), shows up freshly shaved in polished boots he actually takes care of, brings her somewhere warm where Christine knows his name and loves him, and feeds a girl who spent all afternoon starving out of nerves and who is *always* policing her own appetite. Watching her stop watching herself eat — "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" — got me in the throat. And the restfulness! The moment he licks the baba ghanoush off the side of his hand and she loses the thread of what he's saying, and instead of panicking to keep up her end, she just sinks into the sound of him like warm water, until the sharp mint tea brings her back up as if from a trance — I felt that softness right along with her. And then the kiss under the tree shadow: her blurting her rule ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"), him accepting it cleanly without a trace of pout or pressure and starting them back down the path, her planting her feet until their joined hands pull taut, and then him doing *the exact move* we watched him do with Randi at his front door in `The Bench` — giving a small warm kiss, easing back a breath, and letting *her* lean in and take the real one. Smile inside the kiss, mint in both mouths. Pure swoon.

And that exact echo is also where the unease lives, earned inch by inch by the sequence of chapters. In `See You Later`, when Randi charmed Vee at Chi Latte, Vee left unable to remember the actual words, holding only the dizzy warm feeling of being chosen. Here at dinner with Pace, the evening goes "soft at the edges" and Vee literally drops the thread of his words and floats in how restful it is to let him carry it — it's the gentle, grounding mirror of Randi's bright dazzle, and poor Vee doesn't know these two people are together and picked her out on the quad like an item on a menu. Worse — or more complicated — nothing about Pace on this date reads as fake! Christine's fondness isn't staged. His six hours of writing and third-place powerlifting (there's the answer to Vee's wondering about his forearms and thick knuckles!) are just his real life. He isn't twirling a mustache; he is genuinely, attentively giving Vee a wonderful night and letting her choose every step... except the massive foundational fact he's omitting, which is that he and Randi orchestrated meeting her so Randi can have a threesome (and asked if Vee would like the bench!). How can consent feel *this* scrupulous beat-by-beat — "Don't answer tonight," "May I choose?", turning away when she states her kissing rule, backing off after the first brush of lips — inside a setup Vee hasn't been told the shape of? That contradiction has me completely hooked and deeply unsettled on Vee's behalf. And Cassie's quiet, un-teasing verdict — "That's not a boy. That's a man" — made me wince, because Cassie is their sharpest observer (remember her odd "Goodbye, Vee" in `See You Later` and her clocking Pace's "great-aunt" look at Randi in `Substitution`), and even Cassie is disarmed by his patience here.

The erotic charge is a slow, sensual simmer rather than explicit heat, and it works beautifully: the razor-blush on his jaw and the promise of smelling soap on him up close; the soft Arabic words like "closed doors" making her mouth water; watching his right hand tear hot blistered bread and then the quick, unthinking lick of smoky dip from the side of his hand that makes her lose her train of thought completely; the heat of the tea through the worked-brass cage; and finally the taut pull of their hands in the dark and her stepping across that last inch to take the second, deeper mint-warm kiss. Zero slackness, zero clinical beats, no reader friction at all — every detail felt lived-in.

The titles hit hard once you put them side by side. `May I Choose` echoes his polite `May I call you?` from `Substitution`, and on the surface it's that lovely relief at the restaurant when Vee gladly hands over the menu ("more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would") — her first tiny taste of the dynamic we already saw full-grown with Randi, where letting him lead feels like laying down a lifelong burden, paired with him leaving the kiss choice entirely to her. Underneath, knowing `The Pointing Game` ("I point. You pick"), `May I Choose` takes on an eerie double edge about who is choosing whom. And look at the cover board: `WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION`. Everyone knows the proverb about supping with the devil ("he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon"), and here we literally are at a supper full of impeccable manners — *May I call you?*, *Don't answer tonight*, *May I choose?* — *A Polite Invitation* in the purest sense. It makes me dread for Vee even as I ache at how badly she needed a night where someone fed her and let her rest. Next, I want — and dread — finding out how and when the curtain drops: does Randi stay the "friend" cheering from the sidelines while Pace dates Vee, or do they bring the real invitation into the open soon? My guess from here is that Vee is going to tell Randi all about this Saturday date on Monday, glowing, and watching Randi's face over that is going to be electric.
