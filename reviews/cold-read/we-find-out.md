# Cold read — We Find Out

*scene: scenes/we-find-out.md · read after: practice-room*

## Reader reaction

This is the scene the whole book has been leaning toward, and — I have to be honest — it worked on me hard and it broke my heart at the same time, which is exactly the register I've been carrying.

**The play at the top is genuinely delightful.** The shorts-and-skirt chase, her flashing him to *make him come after her*, "waved them like a flag over a taken field" — this is the lightest and most alive Vee has felt in the whole book. And the discovery that she's *faster than he is* — "quick and light… while all that mass of him had to gather and come around like a ship" — landed as a real, physical fact, and gave me a flicker of hope: for once she's winning, she has power in the room. Then "the running was the mistake, because the hall gave onto the bedroom and the bedroom was small and had no floor to lose him on." That sentence closed like a trap and I felt it close. Her one advantage runs her straight into the one place it doesn't matter.

**The flinch is the most striking new thing.** "She had never once seen anything she did land on him… and she had just watched it flinch." For a moment the wall is a person. I loved that. And then the whole rest of the chapter is the wall reasserting itself with total control, which makes the flinch feel almost like something he *let* her have.

**Pace.** My feeling about him did not move so much as deepen and darken in the same motion. The "bet" — "Either you admit you lost, or we find out" — is beautifully, horribly clean. He constructs a situation where her own body is the evidence against her, and then reads the verdict off her. That's exactly his signature: he removes her decision and lets her arousal make it, then cherishes the proof. When he adds rules that "hadn't been part of any bet he'd named" — "No. Slow. That's part of the bet too" — I caught the thumb on the scale *inside the fiction*: he's rewriting the terms live because she's too far under to object, and the text let me see that without announcing it. That's the tell that stayed cold under the warmth.

And yet the aftercare undid me the way it undid her. The juice — "Juice. Sweet and cold and faintly ridiculous" — the "wonderful, wonderful" said "like a plain fact he wanted on the record," the head in his lap. "He hadn't planned any of what came next, she could tell, and that was the thing that undid her." That line is the hinge of my whole divided response: *she* reads the unplannedness as proof it's real, and I want to believe it too, and I can't fully, because I've been in his POV on that bike ride. So I'm treasured and I'm afraid in the same breath. The book keeps doing this to me and it keeps working.

**Randi's ghost in the room** — "Randi's story rose up in her like something she'd swallowed: would she have to count out loud?" — paid off the practice-room scene exactly as I dreaded it would. The template got fitted. But the payoff has a twist I didn't expect and found chilling: what Pace does is *nothing like* Randi's story. Quiet, no counting, no theatre. So the story Randi planted doesn't script the event — it scripts Vee's *dread going in*, primes her to expect one thing so the gentler real thing lands as revelation. If Pace and Randi are coordinating, that's a fiendishly good division of labor; if they're not, it's an eerie coincidence that these two are running the same girl from two sides. I still can't confirm from the inside that they're working together, and this chapter gives Vee no hint of it — but the resonance is louder than ever.

**The erotic charge.** Peak for me was the finger: "she braced somewhere low for it to be difficult, for there to be at least the dignity of a threshold to cross. There was none." And the held-up finger — "not just the tip… the whole length of it was coated… the shameful plenty of the wet." That's the book's core shame-engine (her body announcing its wanting, the *plenty* of it) at full voltage, and it did not go clinical once. The spanking itself is rendered as tenderness weaponized — "his mouth, once, pressed to the warm pink of her" — which is hotter and sadder than any hard version would've been. The only place the heat cooled slightly for me was the second penetration, "there was so much more of him to ask it with now" — a touch of restatement, since the finger had already made the point about her body having decided. But the "small lit country of his bed… no such thing as a witness" passage earned the cooldown; it reframed the same ease that was her humiliation as, privately, "only true," and that's the most generous the book has been to her wanting.

**Friction.** Very little. The one spot my eyebrow went up was the tidiness of "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no longer any such thing as a witness, anywhere" — it's the closest the chapter comes to stating its own thesis out loud rather than letting me feel it. It's lovely writing, but I noticed the author's hand phrasing the idea for me. Minor.

**The scrunchies.** "a whole form of them, deep green with small white dots, the kind of thing you found online and never in any store in town… the plenty of them said the thing no note would have, that he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." Objectively a gorgeous, tender gesture. But it rhymes with the burgundy dress and the shirt: he *provisions* her, stocks his house with her, and the very abundance ("more of them than a person could go through quickly, far more") reads to me, from where I sit, as much like a man laying in supplies for a long campaign as a man in love. Vee reads it as being kept in the good sense. I read a faint chill under it. The book let me have both, again.

**Her bathroom monologue** to the mirror — "My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky," "I'm going to start bringing a bag" — is the most self-aware and funny she's been, and it's the sound of a woman deciding to stop resisting and move *in*. It's charming and it's the trap tightening, and she narrates only the charming half.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread the moment these two tracks touch — the first time Randi's words and Pace's hands are visibly the same hand. *Guess from here:* the "bring a bag" line means Vee is about to functionally live at his house, which is the staging ground for whatever the induction/threesome turns out to be. And the scar he still won't explain, plus the withheld family, plus right-hand-only eating — I keep waiting for one of those to crack open and tell me who Peter actually is under Pace. I want that crack and I'm afraid of it.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Vee broke her own rule and texted Pace first, then arrived early. A play-fight — stripping his shorts, flashing him to bait the chase — turned when she discovered she's *faster* than he is but ran into the bedroom, the one room too small to escape in. He caught her, swatted her, and proposed a "bet": if she wasn't enjoying it he'd stop; if she was, she'd let him spank her "properly." He proved it by sliding a finger into her already-soaked, already-yielding body and holding up the copious wet as evidence. She conceded. He undressed her slowly (heels kept on), spanked her gently — quiet, no counting, *nothing* like Randi's story she'd braced for — soothing and kissing between each, then took her, easily, twice. Afterward: unplanned-seeming aftercare (juice held to her mouth, "wonderful, wonderful," head in his lap, she fell asleep treasured). She woke to find a huge supply of green polka-dot scrunchies stocked in his bathroom, no note; gave a wry mirror-monologue about her "Victorian LARPing" and resolved to start bringing a bag (i.e., moving in by degrees). He cooked her a curry.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee / Vivienne** — crossed the last line; now fully in his bed and his house by her own choosing, narrating the surrender as love. Shame/heat fused; her body decides ahead of her and she can no longer pretend it doesn't. Newly funny and self-aware — which reads as her giving up resistance, not gaining defense.
- **Pace / Peter** — tender-plain, total control; wins by making her arousal testify against her, rewrites the "bet" rules live while she's too far under to object. Aftercare felt unplanned and real *to her*; I still carry the bike-ride knowledge that he's working two women on purpose, so I hold his tenderness as real-and-instrumental at once. Chest hidden under too-big clothes; the inner-thigh surgical scar still unexplained (she's decided to stop asking); withheld family; right-hand-only eating.
- **Randi** — not present, but her practice-room spanking story surfaced in Vee's head as dread going in — and the real event was pointedly *unlike* it, so the planted template shaped Vee's expectation, not the act. Coordination with Pace still unconfirmed from inside; resonance louder than ever.
- **Cassie** — absent; moral floor being routed around. **Sheri** — Pace's blunt riding partner (source of my outside knowledge). Orbit: Brooke/Kayla/Meg.

**How I feel.** Aroused and grieving in the same beats, as ever, but tipped further: this was the consummation and it was tender enough that I half-believe him and cold enough (the live rule-rewrite, the provisioning scrunchies, the "bet" as a rigged proof) that I don't. Saddest note: she feels *treasured* for the first time and reads the unplanned aftercare as proof it's real — and that reading is what fully dissolves her.

Now that Vee is effectively moving in ("bringing a bag"), how close is the induction/threesome, and when do the two tracks finally touch? Will Cassie force her way back in before it's too late?
