## Reader reaction

This one unhorsed me. I came in expecting Vee — the chapter before had left her mid-answer to Cassie's question about what she and Randi are — and instead the chapter turned and gave me Randi, and the whole machinery she built turning around to eat her. I have never felt for Randi like this. Since chapter one she has been the one who locks the door, who keeps the face on; tonight the door got locked on her. The moment that landed hardest: the open chair. "The only seat left was the one that faced the table, the one the girl under review sat in, pulled a little out from the wall… a glass of water set out in front of it by someone who was not Randi." She put girls in that chair. She knows every lever on that page. And she sits there and takes it because the thin truth costs more than the fat lie — because to correct Kinsey about the bra would be to hand the board the wanting, and the wanting is the one thing she has spent twenty-one years hiding.

Brooke is the scariest thing on the page. "I want you to know I held it off as long as I could." Warm, soft, a door swinging wide — and it's steel all the way down. Compare it to the girl who'd hugged Vee and called her *sweetheart*: the same warmth, weaponized. Sloane's "careful, fair" testimony and Kinsey's greedy lie are exactly what terror looks like among ninety girls.

The opening is gorgeous and hot in a settling way. Randi thumbing a lime Lifesaver into Vee's palm — straight back to salt and lime and the kiss — and Vee sucking it to nothing and keeping her leg still. The hug: Randi's head "settling heavy on her chest, the small certain weight of it over her breastbone." And then, catastrophic and perfect: *"A girl needs a spanking!"* — which detonates inside Vee and inside me both, because I have the whole private history of what that word does to her. That's real charge, and it's doing real work; the heat is tangled with shame and memory the way it has been all along.

Then the chapter goes cold, and the cold is the point. The erotic charge drains out because the hearing is anti-desire: a room where wanting someone is the unspoken crime under the named ones. But Randi's love for Vee floods the back half — "She would give up the house before she gave up Vee; she knew that the way she knew her own name." That is a devotion beat. The August memory of meeting Pace reframed everything for me too: *she* drove it, took the sex fast, and only later saw "the long approach… the want given time to grow" as the part she spent without knowing. That lines up with her impatience in ch18, and it makes her someone who has been burning down her own chances for a long time.

Her courage is real and does not advertise itself: she refuses Brooke's offer, the one that would have meant setting Pace and Vee down in front of the chapter. She keeps them and loses Thursdays — the hour after class that was Vee's. The dropped lipstick in the runner is the most quietly devastating image in the book since the cap under her thumbnail: she opens her clutch to do her face because you go back in with your face right, and the gold tube drops, and she does not bend for it. There was no time. That single small crack is worth pages.

**Title.** Inversion — and how. The enforcer made the accused; the head chair taken by someone else's body; the warm public self turned inward as a cage. But also this: Kinsey's lie inverts the mixer into Randi's aggression (her "hand up the skirt") when what actually happened was Randi letting Vee be looked at and losing herself. The title recolorsthe whole chapter, and it hurts.

**What I want next.** I want to know what Vee is told, and what she isn't. I dread the slow theft of Thursdays. And I am more afraid, now, of what happens to Randi — the girl who has never once let anyone see her want.
