## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction — Tannin**

I came into this chapter still warm from *The Long Way* — the froyo walk, Pace holding the door with his back, Vee's "the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time" — and this chapter turns that sweetness dark and hot before its first sentence has finished landing. Randi's entrance undoes me a little: no hello, none of the door-playing from *The Bench*, just her mouth on his and then "Tell me." The whole chapter is her wanting the details of his date with Vee, and the wanting itself is the content. I felt swept up immediately, and the pull never slackened. The wine kiss is the erotic peak and the title's meaning at once — the tannin, dry and dark and catching at the inside of his lips, and the last line landing in my chest: "He had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else." The swoon is real. I was not pushed into it; Randi's hunger is on the page and it worked on me.

Suspicion: I don't doubt anyone's goodwill here. Pace reads tender, careful, even generous — he withholds Vee's vulnerable line, the true thing she asked him to pretend she hadn't said, and that withholding reads as an act of respect. But the chapter gives me unease it doesn't force. The line "She's fun. You'll like her. I already do." sits wrong: Pace has already met Vee — he took her out for froyo himself. Randi speaking as though she's the one introducing Vee to him reads as her re-owning the story, making Vee her find, her gift, so the wanting can stay survivable. That matches what I saw at the end of *The Bench*: the face over the want, the locked bathroom, the decision not to be seen again. Here the face is still on, the wanting bigger, the holding harder. I feel for her, and I also don't trust the arrangement being built — not because anyone is cruel, but because none of them is naming what this is. And I'm aware, uncomfortably, that I read Pace's POV and felt warm while he orchestrated it.

Friction: the "You'll like her" line made me stop and re-read; it's the one seam where the performance shows. On second thought it reads as intended — the tell, not a mistake — but it did briefly feel like the author's thumb, as though the reversal were underlined for me. The chapter is short and tight; no drag, no boredom. If anything I wanted it to go a scene longer, which is not a complaint.

Titles: "Tannin" — before reading I expected wine, something dry and dark; after, it's the taste of the wanting itself, the astringency that catches and draws. It illuminates the chapter without giving anything away. The volume line, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*: the old proverb — *he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon* — promises a book about an invitation into something you shouldn't take at face value, and the need to keep some distance. "A Polite Invitation" echoes Pace's "May I call you" and every invitation in this book so far — the coffee, the froyo, the pointing game, now this couch — polite all the way down while the thing underneath is not polite at all. No separate tagline was supplied in my packet. The title doesn't recolor this chapter so much as confirm where the book is taking me: someone will need the long spoon. Probably more than one of them.

What I want next: Vee back — her POV, her not-knowing. The sweet moment from *The Long Way* is already being metabolized into Randi's fantasy, and I feel oddly protective of Vee even though nothing threatening has happened on the page. I expect the next chapter to cross the streams further — Randi pursuing Vee again, or Vee texting Pace. I dread Vee learning she's inside something she wasn't shown the whole shape of. And I want to see Pace's house in daylight with Vee in it, just to have the contrast drawn.
