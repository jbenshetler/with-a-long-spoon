## Reader reaction

I came in smiling, and the chapter let me. This is the debrief scene the book has been building toward since the first kiss — Vee back in the dorm, still warm from him, the cold off Cassie's cracked window not touching her — and it's all glow, the good kind. The detail that got me first was the spill of it: talking before the coat was off, the brass cages of the tea, the word "supposed" in *you could tell what was supposed to happen.* Vee slows when she hits the tree, and the chapter slows with her, and the slowing felt like a friend leaning in. I leaned in too.

Vee is the reason this chapter works. The line that landed hardest — *"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose"* — is the thesis of the whole book in one breath, and it's spoken to Cassie as proof, almost as relief. Her agency is the thing she keeps reclaiming: she stopped him, she made him stop, *Me.* And then the spin — quick, clumsy, nearly taking out the closet door, over before it's a dance — that's the most alive Vee's been on the page, and it's not about him. It's about *look at me.* I was happy for her, plainly.

Cassie is the floor under the glow, and I love her in this. *"How much did he give you to drink?"* is protection wearing a dry joke, and the way she absorbs Vee's answer and then just says *Okay* — that second *okay* — is the entire friendship. Then *"Is he rich? Rich people hike. Poor people walk."* It's a joke and it isn't; Cassie is the only person who notices what Vee's giving away, and Vee laughs it off because she's in it. I felt the same tug as Vee — charmed, ready to go along — and the same faint resistance, because Cassie's questions are good ones.

Here's the thing I can't unknow: *off six-fourteen* is Pax's house. Wait — Pace's house. I've been inside it. I've watched the bench come out of its table, watched Randi break and lock herself in the bathroom, heard Steely Dan low in the front rooms. So when Vee says, giddy, *"I know where he lives and everything,"* and Cassie answers flat, *"That's the whole address. Off six-fourteen,"* it landed on me with a chill the scene itself doesn't produce — it's the dramatic irony, not the chapter's own note. The chapter's prose stays warm and funny; the unease is mine, carried over from *The Pointing Game* and *Tannin*, where I watched Pace select this exact girl and Randi want her. Vee thinks a road is a proof of safety. I know what's at the end of it. That knowledge made the title sit over everything.

Friction, barely any. The chapter is slight — one beat, two people, a room — and it knows to be short. The dossier count-off (*"He'll have water, he's not going to walk me off a cliff. See. I know where he lives"*) is a touch on-the-nose about her naivety, but it's earned by giddiness. The ending — the cold line from the window returning while Vee *lay back in the bean bag warm and did not feel it* — is almost too neat a closing image, but it worked on me.

The title recolors the chapter rather than giving it away: *Off Six-Fourteen* is Pace's house to me, the real address underneath her happy road-name. And it promises the book means to take us there. The cover line — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — has been quietly ominous from the start; I hear the old proverb, the long spoon for supping with the devil, and "a polite invitation" is exactly the machinery of this book: the *may I's*, the *permission*, the courteous descent. No tagline was supplied, so that's all I've got to read. Nothing here changed my sense of where I'm being taken — toward the house, toward the arrangement, toward politeness doing darker work.

What I want next: Randi. She's been offstage since *Tannin*, and her absence here is conspicuous — Vee tells Cassie everything and never mentions the girl who introduced her to this life. I want the two lines to cross, and I'm guessing, from here, that the hike out past the gravel road is where Vee finally meets the house — and maybe the woman who's been behind the invitation all along.
