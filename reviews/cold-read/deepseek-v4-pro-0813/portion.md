## Reader reaction

This chapter landed on me as a slow, quiet grief. It starts with the house as a warm, prepared thing — the pot, the bread, the salad, the music — and Pace's whole rhythm of welcome, and I was bought all the way in. I wanted to sit at that table. The line "He poured her glass when he heard the gravel" told me he knows her at the level of approach, and that the evening had a shape. The title has been working on me since the first sentence: it sits right over Randi's bowl, over the serving, over the giving, and by the end it has cut away to something else — the portion as what you won't accept, what you withhold, the measurement of how much of yourself you're willing to be fed. I thought I was being given a dinner chapter; I was being given a withdrawal.

Randi. I don't know who this Randi is, and that's precisely what the chapter wants me to feel. From the first beat she's different — "You cooked. A beat. You shouldn't have." She never says things like that to him, and he knows it, and the prose doesn't let me forget it. Then "It's wonderful" in the wrong voice, the voice with nothing on it, and she doesn't eat the stew, only the salad, and she draws one line through the sauce and leaves it. The flatness of "She's not ready. Or you're not." — I didn't recognize her. I have always seen Randi perform; this is Randi with the performance let go, and what's underneath is cold and hurt. I'm afraid for her in a way I haven't been. The woman who used to say "love you, girl" and hold Vee's hand in front of Brooke is sitting across from Pace with a bowl she won't touch. I felt her leave the table before she stood up.

Pace. I still trust him as a person — the chapter doesn't give me a reason to stop — but for the first time I felt his blindness as something more than a limit. He replays the whole evening searching for what he did, and "it was not there," and I believe him, and that's the bleak thing. He truly cannot see that serving her a small bowl and being visibly glad about Vee's weekend, to the woman who picked Vee with him, is the wound. "It isn't for me," he says, and it's true and it's awful, because it tells her she is not what any of this is for. The line that undid me was at the very end, after she's pulled away and he's alone with the bowls: "He covered the bowl with a plate and put it in the refrigerator, for her." The *for her* — he's still operating on the old script, the one where she comes back and eats. He doesn't know she's gone.

The sex did not work on me erotically, and I don't think it was trying to. It was mechanical: "She put him inside her in one motion and drove." Eyes shut, fast, no *tell me* — and that italicized phrase is the chapter's hinge. For months their intimacy has been built on her asking *tell me*, wanting the story of Vee. Tonight she won't take the story. She takes his body instead — uses it, maybe, or uses it as the only portion of him she can still touch — and "he gave it to her anyway" made me feel his generosity and his servitude at once. He lies under her and waits for the old word and it doesn't come. That's the erotic charge of this chapter: absence. A sexual ritual not performed. And then the back, the rolled-away back, the breathing that changes only after he thinks she's asleep. The heat is all bereavement.

The music: *Violator* — Depeche Mode, and the chapter names it, "a dark voice over machines, every sound in it polished." I've been noticing these music cues since the first night at his house: Steely Dan, Blondie, Fleetwood Mac, now this. Each names the weather before the dialogue does. This one is the house at its most machine-like, the polished surface over the ticking thing. It fits the too-warm, too-prepared house, which here felt less like care and more like a stage. The title now reads to me as one of the darkest in the book: "Portion" is the small bowl, the single line through the sauce, "as long as she needs," the sex he gave her anyway, the part of himself he doesn't know he's withholding. I want to know what Randi actually came to say. I don't think it was about Vee — I think that was the cover. I think she came to break something, or to ask Pace to want her specifically, and couldn't. I dread the moment she tells Vee a version of this, or Vee finds out what Randi and Pace are to each other. That's the bomb under the table, and tonight it ticked louder than ever.
