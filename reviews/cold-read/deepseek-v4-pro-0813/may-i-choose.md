## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I keep waiting for something to go wrong — and this chapter won't let me. It's the most disarmed I've felt as this book's reader, and that's saying something, because I've been reading Pace with one eye narrowed for chapters now. But "May I Choose" landed on me the way the meal landed on Vee: I went in braced, carrying the whole accumulated worry of everything I know, and the page just kept feeding me this warm, unhurried, *specific* thing until the brace quietly stopped mattering.

The froyo in the last chapter had already done a lot of this. But that was daylight, and he asked her out for frozen yogurt — it had an escape hatch. This is evening, it's dinner, and Pace has come to *get* her. I noticed him before she did — standing in that ugly lobby, not looking at his phone, not looking for her, just somewhere in himself. And here's the thing that moved me: he had showered, she reads the "recently" on his jaw, and then she says he hadn't dressed to *impress* — he'd brought the real things, the ones he already looked after. I felt the difference she feels. It's the opposite of buying new shoes to fake a life. He's not performing having lived; he's the one whose boots have actually been kept. That's the entire seduction in this chapter, and it works on me deeply.

And then the restaurant. *May I choose.* The title was sitting there even before I started, and I thought — three times in this book, he's chosen *for* a woman, or steered her toward a choice she didn't know she was making. The chapter title being the thing he says at the table made me read the whole dinner with that word *choose* ringing. But it didn't recolor the scene the way I expected — as manipulation. It felt — merciful. She's been portioning herself, hedged against every meal since her mother. Then she hands him the menu she can't read, and he doesn't explain it to her like a tour guide; he just *chooses*, and builds the meal slowly, and the food that comes is better than anything she'd have known to ask for. I'm still not sure whether that's seduction or rescue, and the chapter doesn't make me decide. It lets me be Vee — wanting things I have no names for, and for once, not needing to know.

The erotic charge for me was all in his hands. The right hand always, the pattern — tearing bread, folding it, the drip of the smoky one caught on his palm, brought to his mouth without ceremony. That's the hottest image in the chapter, and it's just a thumb and a piece of bread. The charge doesn't come from skin; it comes from seeing a man who moves without self-consciousness, who eats without apology, who handles a thing with exactly the right amount of care and no more. I lost the thread — the same way Vee did — and the page made me not care.

The kiss was the part I was most afraid of, because this is the scene where the book could fail Vee or cheapen the date. It didn't. The rule arrives — *I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days* — and I flinched, because I thought Pace would charm her out of it. He didn't. He just smiled like she'd told him something true about herself, and let it close. That moment undid me more than any kiss could have. Because it was exactly what he did with the math — handed it back to her as hers. And the kiss, when it came, was *hers*. She makes the exception, she closes the gap, and he meets her "all the way" only after she's chosen it twice.

My feelings about Pace have moved. I've been troubled by the bench night — the way he read Randi, the lock on the bathroom door, the fact that I know something he doesn't. I still am. But this chapter doesn't feel like the same machinery. It feels like the genuine article. What unsettles me most right now is that I'm not unsettled. I've seen him do this before — the patience, the watching, the waiting for the real *yes* — and on Randi it ended with her locking a bathroom door against tenderness she couldn't survive. I know that. And yet here, I wanted Vee to stay under the tree and kiss him longer. I wanted it for her.

That's the duality this chapter made me sit in: I don't need a happy ending, but I *ache* for Vee to have kept this exact evening. It's the first time in the book I've wanted a woman to get to stay somewhere, rather than wanting her to get out of somewhere. That's not a shadow I can push away. It's just true.

What I want next: I dread the collision. The book has Randi's hand on this, somewhere underneath — she told Pace everything she knows about Vee, and I keep waiting for that to surface, for the pointing game to become a cage Vee doesn't know she's walked into. And the title *May I Choose*, which feels so gentle in this chapter, is the same question that'll eventually have to be answered by all three of them. I'm holding my breath for the *no*.
