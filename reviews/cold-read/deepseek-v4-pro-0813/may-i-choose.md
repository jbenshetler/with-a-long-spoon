## Reader reaction

This one landed as the most disarming chapter in the book so far, which is exactly what makes it the most frightening to read. On its own, "May I Choose" is a flawless courtship — the best first-date chapter I can remember. The food, the surrender, the hand, the almost-kiss: I was in it, warm, hungry, completely seduced along with Vee. And the whole time I carried The Pointing Game in my stomach like a stone. I know how this began. I know she was chosen on a quad before she ever knew his name, with Randi's "yes" as the arrow. So the tenderness here reads double — as real and as instrument — and that double vision is the chapter's whole effect.

Vee undid me. The hunger is literal and not: "the hollow had been opening in her since midday," and then it "went down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the line I'd underline twice. This isn't a girl enjoying a nice date; it's a girl who has been portioning herself for years, who finally stops doing "all of the things she normally did" and can rest. When she said "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" and then, a breath later, "I'm making an exception" — I wanted the kiss for her and wanted to stop it in the same instant. Both are true.

Pace is the problem, and the problem is that he is flawless. He doesn't explain the menu until she asks for rescue; he asks only "anything you wouldn't eat"; when she trots out her rule he just "smiled, like she'd told him something true and fine about herself" and starts to walk her back. That's the horrifying part: the giving-her-room is what makes her walk toward him. Knowing what I know from The Bench and Tannin — a man who reads a woman's body as truth, who points and withholds and waits to hear *please, please, please* — his polish here isn't warmth the way she receives it; it's the same engine in a gentler gear. The line that hooked me mid-page: "he was already learning she could trust where he was taking her." *Where he was taking her.* I know where he's taking her.

Cassie gets barely a page and she's still the only person I trust without reservation. "That's not a boy. That's a man." She doesn't tease this time. That's the note the book wants me to hold. Randi is not on the page at all — not even mentioned — and her absence hums. This dinner is the fulfillment of the game she pointed Vee toward; somewhere offstage she's waiting for the report in wine-dark terms, the "*Tell*" from Tannin still hot in my ears. I felt her behind every course.

The food is the sex in this chapter, and it's better than the bench was for pure heat, because it's all wanting and no consummation. The right hand only; the bread torn with care for the heat; the drip licked off without a pause. "Content for once not to know" — that's the peak of the swoon. Then the hand-holding: "less like something beginning than something resolving." That line leaned a half-beat too explicit for my taste — the one place the prose told me what to feel — though Vee's romanticism might genuinely run exactly there. The withheld kiss at the end is the chapter's best move; I finished wanting the next page badly.

"May I Choose" — on the page it's the relief of the menu, of surrender, of being allowed to let him carry it. Reading it with the quad in my head, it turns cold: he chose her already; what she's receiving is the experience of choosing him. And the volume title, "A Polite Invitation," now reads as it was always going to read — this dinner *is* the polite invitation, and "With a Long Spoon" warns me how to sup with the devil wearing a good shirt and asking *May I*. I expect the kiss next, or its aftermath; I dread Vee learning what she's inside; I can't look away, and the dread and the want have become one pull.
