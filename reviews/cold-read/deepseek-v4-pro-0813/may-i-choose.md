## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I read this chapter as a kind of earned exhale after all the hunt-and-tension of the recent run — and it nearly works on me completely. The date is *good*: the little narrative of her choosing dresses and a borrowed pair of shoes, Cassie cross-legged on the bunk delivering verdicts, the hollow of hunger she's been carrying since noon opening into this small warm restaurant she's never found on her own. "May I choose?" — three words, and I felt the relief go through me the way it went through her: the surrender handed across a table, accepted, made into a meal she couldn't have ordered and then *loved*. The bread, the smoke-dark dip, the mint tea in its little brass cage. The whole scene is about being fed and not having to perform — and the book has built Vee as exactly the girl who is always performing, always braced for the bill, always catching the thread before it can drop. Letting it drop, sitting there with food she has no names for but trusts: that's the swoon. I wanted it for her.

But I can't unknow what I know. Pace chose her at the dining hall as the "most alive one in sight," pointed at her, sent Randi to collect her. So the tenderness here — and it *is* tenderness, or the texture of it — is also machinery running on schedule. The chapter itself doesn't flag any of that; on its own page, Pace is a man who showers and shaves and comes to get her, who asks "may I choose" and holds her hand like a thing resolving. The unease lives entirely in my memory, not in this chapter. That's an honest distinction, and it's also what makes the chapter a little hard to sit with: I'm rooting for Vee and watching her be folded in, and the folding is a plan she doesn't know she's inside. That doubleness is the book's whole trick, and it works.

The erotic charge is real but indirect — the *right hand* pattern, the drip of smoke-dark dip licked off his skin, the hand-holding that felt less like beginning than resolving, and then the kiss. The kiss is the peak, and it's earned: the rule she sets and breaks, the inch he leaves, her taking it twice. "This time he met her all the way." That landed. My only mild friction: "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" — the metaphor pushed just past what the moment needed; I felt the author's thumb for half a second. Otherwise the slowness is the pleasure, and it didn't sag.

The book's title sits over all of it like weather. "With a long spoon" — I can't not hear the old warning in it, the one about supping with someone you'd be wise to keep at a distance. And here she is, close enough to kiss him. "A Polite Invitation" — the whole chapter is an invitation she didn't know she'd been sent. I want to keep reading, and I dread what the spoon is for.
