## Reader reaction

**The Long Way** — felt read.

This chapter is short and clean, and it works on me mostly as charm — Vee's charm, the date's charm, the blessed quieting of her mother's voice. The first thing I felt was continuation: the phone lighting up facedown, her stomach going "like a stair missed in the dark." That's a lyric, accurate thing; I've had that feeling. Then the little friend-scene confirming what I already carried from the study session: she's in, she's already hoping, she doesn't want to seem too eager. *"Is he desperate? Texting this fast?" It was easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting.* That line is real, and it re-earns her whole register in one sentence.

The date swept me into Vee's corner. I was happy for her, all through it. The froyo shop is rendered with just enough specificity — the plastic ivy gone dusty on top, the lemon cleaner cutting through the sugar. And then the moment that got me: she builds her cup too big, and *usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, "that's plenty, that's enough now." Today there was nothing.* The mother's voice gone quiet — that's the same voice, in different words, as Randi's mother on egg whites and tofu, the food-police inheritance both women carry, and Pace somehow turns it off by being there. That's the sirens' song he is, and I felt it working on me too, because the relief in it is so plainly what both of them have been starved for.

The grandfather line landed deep. *He asked her things the way her grandfather used to ask things, as though the answer mattered and there was no wrong one, and then he waited through all of it.* It renders him safe and unhurried, and I believed it of her. The swoon peaks where it should: *"This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo."* I winced with her at "See. Pretend." And then he doesn't pretend and doesn't make it large, just walks beside her and says, *"Your tart's holding up."* That line laid me open more than anything in the chapter. It's the perfect deflection that's also a blessing — he won't pick up the huge fragile thing she put on the table, but he'll confirm she's okay in the small concrete way. If I didn't carry the earlier chapters, I'd read this as the best first date in the world.

But I do carry them. And the unease sits exactly at the join of what I know and what Vee doesn't. The chapter itself never makes Pace wrong; on this page, he is exactly as good as Vee experiences him. My unease is entirely from memory: he and Randi picked Vee out on the quad, Randi engineered the collision, and now Pace is on a date with her. So when Randi takes Vee's phone, reads the text, and grins, *"He wants to see you naked,"* I hear that line twice — once as a friend being a friend, and once as the woman who said *Yes* when Pace pointed at Vee, watching the plan come together. Does the page of *this* chapter earn the double hearing? No. The book so far earns it. I want to say that plainly: nothing on this page tips Pace's hand. So my trust in him on the page is intact, while my trust in the situation Vee is in is not. That's the honest dual feeling.

On friction: the chapter is very short, and I found myself wanting one more beat of the walk. The cut at "Your tart's holding up" is strong, but the brevity meant I didn't get to feel the date settle into anything longer. The opening with Randi and Cassie also reads fast; Randi's *"The rule's for boys you're not sure about. You're not unsure"* is doing a lot of shapeshifting work in a line and a half, and the page glides past it. That's not a complaint about craft — it's the right glide for Vee's POV — but it's the one place I wanted to sit a beat longer.

The title. *"The Long Way"* is the phrase from the end of *See You Later* — Vee going home the long way, warm the whole walk. Here they walk *the long way, not toward anything.* So the chapter title means the unhurried path, the not-rushing, and it points at the tempo of the whole pursuit — the man's name is Pace, and the long way is what he does. It doesn't recolor the chapter darkly; it just names the rhythm. But taken with the cover-board line — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* — the two talk to each other. A long spoon is what you sup with when you don't want to get close to what you're eating with. And this is Book One: a polite invitation. So the book is telling me, before anything happens, to keep my distance from the polite invitation. From here, the titles are promising a courtship that is also a careful approach to something already flagged as untrustworthy. That's the uneasy contract I'm reading under.

What I want next: I want Vee to have more of this — I root for her to get a good thing, and I want a second date. And I dread the frame closing on her: the moment she learns Randi was not an accident, or the moment the game becomes explicit. My guess from here (a guess only) is that Pace's sweetness is not fake, but that it is in service of a plan Vee has no vote in, and that Randi's encouragement is the same plan wearing a friend's dress. I would love to be wrong.
