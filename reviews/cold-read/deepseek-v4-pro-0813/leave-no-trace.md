# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Vee has me entirely. By the time she's on that rock in his warm flannel telling Cassie what she did, I'm not just rooting for her — I'm protective of her in a way that makes the tenderness of this chapter ache. She was already the girl who needed to be seen, fed, told she was enough, and this hike gives her all three: the blister he fixes without making her small, the "we take nothing and leave nothing" delivered gently when she spits a cherry pit, the dress she can't afford and the way he says only, "That sounds like a beautiful dress," and lets it be. The summit scene is the swoon — the lake holding the silver sky, the ridges burning, "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it," and that not-caring as the most beautiful part. And him letting her catch him watching her face, then turning away to give her "the rest of the looking alone" — that's the pull, clean and earned.

The truck is the peak. Not because there's explicit anything; because Vee chooses. The voice — her mother's, always — calls her "floozie," and she answers it with heat and goes ahead anyway. Her unbuttoning that wet shirt on her knees on the bench, holding his eyes, then looking down so he looks too, and his face going "want and gladness and stillness" — it's one of the hottest scenes I've read in this book, precisely because it's a woman taking what she wants while terrified of it. And then the reversal: his shaking hand, her pulling it under the flannel against her belly, "You're freezing." Care running both ways. I melted there.

Which is why the shadow sits so heavily. I cannot unknow, from the earlier chapters, that this hike is Pace and Randi's shared project — the pointing game, "go get her," the watcher at the pole. So while this chapter, on its own terms, reads as genuinely tender and patient and good, I read every kindness double. The dry flannel, the standing in the rain, the not touching — all real, all also a lure. The dread isn't in the page; it's in the gap between what Vee experiences and what I know. The page does not make this feel off. That's the horror of it, and it makes me feel for her more, not less.

Trust: Vee I trust completely. Within the scene, Pace gives me no reason to doubt him — he's exactly the man Vee sees. But the larger frame gives me a reason to doubt what the scene means, and that unease is earned by the chapters I've already read, not manufactured from this one.

Friction: small, and mostly swallowed by how well it works on me. "His only dry shirt" is underlined a little hard, the author telling me to be moved — but I was moved. The cop-car ending is abrupt, and the siren felt like a device to cut her sentence off and get her home with the flannel; still, "the two sets of taillights went down the drive and onto the road and were gone" lands as a clean, slightly ominous comedown.

The title "Leave No Trace" — I read it before the chapter, and by the end it's recast. The ethic is to take nothing and leave nothing; Vee takes the flannel, keeps a trace of him, and the chapter closes on that. It's the beginning of her refusing to leave no trace, refusing to disappear. It also whispers the darker reading: what Pace and Randi leave behind on their way through her. The volume title, "With a Long Spoon," and "Book One — A Polite Invitation" — the long spoon is the proverb for supping with the devil: you keep your distance while partaking. "A Polite Invitation" has gone from courtly to ominous on me, because I now know who is being invited and who is doing the inviting. They promise me a story more complicated than a romance, and they're taking me exactly there.

What I want: for Vee to keep arriving, to keep taking what she's told she can't have. What I dread: the moment she finds out about Randi and Pace, and the possibility that all this tenderness is also a trap. What I expect: the next chapters will draw her closer, and Randi will become a live third player rather than a warm echo.
