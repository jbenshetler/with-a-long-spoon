## Reader reaction

I read this chapter with my stomach slowly tightening and not letting go. The trail itself is an ordeal the prose makes me *do* — the wrong boots, the hot spot, the sweat gone cold — and because it costs her something, the summit actually pays. I believed the view because I'd climbed to it with her. The lake that "did not care that she was standing on the edge of it" landed on me harder than any of the pretty language, because it names the thing she's climbed away from: being a body that is looked at, measured, found wrong. And Pace — his blue swallowed by the world, his eyes on her face instead of the view — is doing the chapter's central thing: he keeps choosing *her* in front of the enormousness, and she keeps not quite having the language for why that undoes her.

The shoe moment undid *me*. I felt the clench before she did — the mother-voice about her sock, her foot, "what kind of girl lets a man" — and then he took off her boot and her sock and touched her heel with a square of foam, and the chapter gave me the single most intimate moment so far and it was a blister. That's the whole book's method: desire lives inside care, and care undresses you without asking for anything back. When she stands and says, "It's better. Thank you," the clench has already gone and she didn't even catch it go. I felt myself let out a breath I didn't know I'd taken.

The meal was heat without a single touch. The horseradish that ambushes her, the eyes watering that she doesn't care are watering, the cold metal bottle passed and drunk from where her mouth was — the chapter understands that feeding someone who has been taught not to be hungry *is* foreplay, and gives it all the unhurried space it wants. The cherry pit moment almost broke my heart: "We take nothing and leave nothing" is the purest line in the chapter, and he says it so gently. Then he lays his own pit beside hers and doesn't look, and she learns the rule without being made small. That is what Vee keeps calling "kind" — the mercy of being taught without ceremony. But "leave no trace" also went down a darker corridor for me: here is the man who, with Randi on a bench, selected this girl and set their collision in motion and said nothing. The phrase is the trail ethic, but it is also the whole silent arrangement around Vee, and I read the title with a slow chill after I finished. Something here is being consumed without a trace, and the chapter keeps serving us cherries while it happens.

The truck scene was the chapter's peak, and it burned in a way that surprised me by how much it honored consent. She calls him back with a crooked finger, holds his eyes, unbuttons her own cold wet shirt — and he stays still through all of it, "want and gladness and a stillness." That stillness is the opposite of being taken; it's being *received*. She chooses him seeing her, and the voice that says "floozy" in her mother's exact word is answered by her body pressing its thighs together once, hard, because the shame and the heat are the same current now. Then the turn — his shaking hand, his shivering, and her impulse breaking off its mouthward reach and tucking his cold hand inside the flannel against her belly. I went from arousal to tenderness so fast it almost winded me. She puts the shirt on, doesn't button it, holds it shut, and asks him to get warm. There's still no kiss. Somehow the withholding makes it hotter — the thing that happened is "in the cab with them, holding still in the warm air." That is what heat does when it trusts the wait.

One small friction: at the truck she buttons the flannel "to the collar now" before the drive, yet during the drive she's "holding it closed across her front with one hand" — a tiny continuity wobble, a garment both buttoned and held. It didn't break the scene for me; I read it as memory dreaminess, or perhaps the last unbuttoned thing. But I noticed it, and the editor in me paused. Otherwise the pacing is the chapter's second-best achievement: the long climb earns the fast truck scene, and the cop's lights landing on the final beat is exactly right. Her body hears the siren and goes before the sentence does — a body trained to comply with the outside before the self gets a vote. That's what the voice *is*. It unmake a little of what the summit healed, and the chapter knows it.

I have more feeling for Pace after this. The shoe, the sandwich made this morning, the dry flannel behind the seat, the door opened before she reaches it, the man standing in the rain with his back turned so she can change — he's almost pathologically good at the *silent* care, the care that doesn't ask. And that's what sits uncomfortably against the benchtop plan. The con is not that the care is fake. It's that it's real, and she will have no trace of its design. Randi isn't in this chapter at all, but her word follows Vee up the trail — "ravish" — and I read that as a ghost in the frame: the other set of hands behind the mountain.
