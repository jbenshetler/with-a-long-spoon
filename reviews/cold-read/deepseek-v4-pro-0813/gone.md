This chapter landed warmer than I expected after chapter 18's rift. Randi stayed. That's the first thing I felt: from chapter 11 I'd been holding the open question of why she'd started leaving straight after sex, and here, in the very first lines, she is "still astride him where she'd stayed after, down onto his chest, loose as she only went afterward." She stays down, she lets him feed her, she sleeps over. I melted into that, honestly — the blanket drawn over her back, the Sade low in the other rooms, her asking, sleepy, "Will you make me something?" It felt like something between them thawing.

The conversation about Vee is the chapter's real center, and it moved me more than I expected. "She got past me before I could help it." That line, so undefended, did something to my chest. Randi, who has read for twenty chapters as a woman rationing herself and steering every intimacy, says out loud that Vee slipped past her defenses, that she keeps wanting to tell her stupid things just to hear her laugh, that she's *lovely* and *extraordinary*. The beat "That was kind of hot" about Vee's mortification in the shoe store is the old Randi — discovered desire arriving as a little cruelty — but "I didn't think I'd like her this much" is new. That's not performance. I rooted for her there, maybe more than I have all book.

And then Pace says the thing, and the whole warm room shifts. "She spends the entire time sure she's too much, and she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her." Randi goes still — not the loose stillness of after, "the kind that takes holding." The text earns that unease completely. He has just said, to Randi, in Randi's bed, with Randi's body still on his, that Vee is the most alive person in any room. And Randi holds it, beats too long, says "Yeah. She is." Pace registers the stillness and does not ask what it was. That is the ch18 blindness again — he doesn't know what he's done, and this time I could see it happening in real time. The chapter title pressed down on me right there: *Gone*. What's gone? Her ease? Her primacy? Her control? Something in Randi tightened and didn't come back.

The erotic charge peaks in the "Tell me" and the slow ride. That word — *Tell me* — has been Randi's arousal-kicker since chapter 6, and now it's aimed at hearing about Vee's effect; he describes Vee brightening a room and Randi asks for more, and then she finds him hard and smiles "So soon?" The ride is charged and specific, and the best image in the chapter is a repeat: "the blue on her toes the last composed thing left of her." The blue toes from chapter 1, chapter 17, chapter 20 — now her composure's last flag. That's heat with real feeling in it. It cools into the kitchen, but not clinical: it turns into being fed.

That dinner scene is where I felt the biggest turn. In chapter 18, Randi drew one line through the stew and refused to eat. Here she eats everything on the plate — "there was nothing left on it." The inversion lands as healing, or as something she's allowing because of Vee, or both. And she sits in his undershirt with "all of her inside it but her face and her hands and the blue of her toes" — tucked into him, but also hidden, the found self drawn back inside again.

The book's cover line — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — now reads double-edged. This chapter is full of the polite register: "Darling," "Will you pour me a little more?", "I'm going to let you feed me first." All that courtesy sits around a shared fantasy of a third person, and an invitation being drafted under the warmth. Chapter title *Gone*: going in it made me braced for a loss; by the end I think the loss is happening inside Randi, quietly, during the "most alive person" beat. I want the induction next, and I dread the moment Vee sees the two attentions as one design.
