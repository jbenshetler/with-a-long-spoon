## Felt read

Right away, this chapter has the pull of a door finally opening that the whole book has been circling. The kiss she takes at the stoop — the one she'd been owed "since the mountain" — lands because she *takes* it, and because for the first time in her life it's the man who stops. That's the hinge of the chapter for me: **"no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs."** It isn't played cold — his breath is uneven, his hand comes into her hair — but he stops, and then he leaves the bedroom door shut *not because he doesn't know she's wearing satin for him, but* — and this is the part I felt in my chest — *as if he does.* "Something in her dropped" is the truest line in a very composed chapter. It tells me she came dressed hoping to be invited, and the withholding is, for her, already a kind of wanting.

The swoon is real, and it peaks late, at the bolt of burgundy. I believed the small disappointment — "oh, it's not the right—" — because I've been carrying the russet-red since the summit too. Then he holds the cloth up to her face in the mirror and the shade flips: "He'd matched *her*." That's a declaration wearing an object's clothes. And then the reveal stacked on it — the flannel she's been wearing has no tag because *he made it* — made that earlier gift retroactively enormous. The dress offer, the measuring, "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light" — that's where the erotic charge crests. Her "yes" isn't to a gown; it's to being looked at, the exact thing Water Wings taught me she both dreads and hungers for. I felt it as scary and warm at once.

I have no real suspicion of him in this chapter — but I'm not unalert. The *parents* beat is a hairline crack: "a small closing, there and gone, a door easing shut two rooms away." That's the only time the tour hits a locked room he won't name, and it rhymes with the actual closed bedroom door. I read both as the page honestly offering something with a door on it, not as proof of anything. The thing I'm watching now is whether his restraint — which makes me *trust* him — is also the same machinery that withholds. The book has already told me what a Pace-shaped door can do, with Daphne. I'm holding the spoon.

The title. **"Two Towels"** is exactly the kind of quiet, recursive detail this book trusts me to catch — it appears once, on the bathroom: "plain, clean, two towels on the rod." It gave nothing away beforehand, and after, it means: the house was already readied for a second person, down to the spare towel. That's the whole seduction of the chapter, the care prepared *before* she arrived. I liked it better for being so small. The cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** (no separate tagline supplied) — keeps doing what it's done for me since early on: the proverb behind it is *dine with the devil, but keep a long spoon*, and every lovely exact courtesy in this book — "May I choose," "would you like to come to my house," "if you'll let me" — is, I'm starting to think, the spoon. The politeness is real and it's also how you survive the thing being polite. From here, that feels suggested, not yet proven.

What I want next: the fitting. What I dread: finding out the dress was always supposed to happen — that the plan Vee knows nothing about has been running underneath her "yes."
