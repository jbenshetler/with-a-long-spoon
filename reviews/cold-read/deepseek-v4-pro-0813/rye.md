## Reader reaction

What a warm chapter to sit in after the storm of the last one. I ended "Leave No Trace" aching for Vee with her balled wet shirt and Pace driving off under the cop's lights, and here she is in her room, safe, wearing his flannel like a whole new skin, telling Cassie everything — and my tenderness for her expanded all over again. She's not smaller for having been through it; she's bigger. "I got up a mountain." The pride in her voice — "Me." — landed harder than any of the heat, because it's her claiming her own wanting and her own daring out loud, to her best friend, without the shaming voice narrating over it. That's the Vee I've been hoping would show up.

Cassie, who I already trusted harder than anyone, outdid my trust. "No windowless van." "I had your blood type ready." She's dry and watchful, and then she does the thing no one else in Vee's life is doing right now: she goes still and listens. "Vee," she says, like setting something down where it won't break, and then "Keep the shirt." She blesses it without claiming it. My feeling about her hasn't moved so much as been confirmed all the way down.

Pace is offstage and enormous. The page made me swoon for him completely — the blister fixed without making it weird, the dry flannel, getting out into the rain with his back to the cab, and above all "he looked at me like I'd handed him something. And he didn't touch me." That not-touching is the most romantic thing in the chapter. But I can't read it cleanly the way Vee and Cassie do, because I know what they don't: this good true thing is also the thing Randi and Pace collaborated to start. So my swoon and my unease sit side by side. The page didn't earn suspicion of Pace here — he's cleaner than ever — but the reader's position makes the savoring ache a little. That's not the chapter's shadow; it's mine, from memory.

Where the chapter landed on me deepest was the tenderness. "He was shaking, he was so cold, and he just looked" — the restraint there is the devotion. The erotic charge is real and sustained — "His hands, though," "I think there's a whole situation under there," the way she says "we ate" the way other girls say *and then we kissed.* It peaks at the shirt removal recounted, "On purpose. Me," then goes soft rather than slack, which felt exactly right.

One small friction, a noticing more than a fault: Vee's telling edits out the cop. Last chapter ended on the siren cutting her sentence and him pulling away under the lights, and here it's gone entirely. I don't distrust her for it; she's telling the version she wants to keep. But as the reader who saw the other ending, I noticed the erasure. It's honest, even endearing — and it reminds me she doesn't know how that night actually ended for Pace either.

The title "Rye" — the bread. Not the shirt, not the rain, not the man: the sandwich. The seeded, sour, substantial bread that had no name in the soft-white world she came from. It's the thing she tells Cassie about with the most wonder before the bigger thing. It names being fed — fed well, taught gently, not shamed. "We didn't leave a pit. You carry out what you carried in." And yet she kept the shirt. Leave no trace of the picnic; keep the evidence of the care. That tension feels like the whole chapter.

Book title: *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation.* That proverb — he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon — sits under every courtesy in this book. "A Polite Invitation" fits Pace exactly: may I choose, the asking, the not-touching, getting out in the rain. It all reads as invitation, and the long spoon is the warning the invitation doesn't carry on its face. From here the titles promise something dangerous under all this warmth.
