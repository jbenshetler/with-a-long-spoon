## Reader reaction

This chapter lands on me warm and lopsided — warm because I am inside Vee's happiness and it *is* real on her, and lopsided because I know one crucial thing she doesn't, and the page keeps brushing against it without letting her feel it. The "dear friend" moment in front of Brooke is the heart of the chapter and it nearly undid me: two women, one of them holding the other's cool damp hand on a white table in plain view, spending something on her "without appearing to count it." I believed it. I believed Vee's whole account of what it cost Randi to say it. And at the same time my stomach is sour because I, the reader, know Randi selected her like a fruit at a stand. That double exposure — the swoon and the sickness held in one beat — is the chapter doing exactly what this book does to me.

I come away loving Vee more, and more protectively. She is so glad to be picked, and so unaware that the whole bright table is a stage. Her old habit of making herself smaller is still in her; it arrives at Brooke's approach before she can stop it, and the passage where she shrinks "not to get out in front of her own humiliation, but to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi" — that's a precision cut, and I recognized it, and I wanted to put my hand over hers. I also notice, and believe, how often Vee simply does not look at things: the oat milk, the not-watching-Cassie-go, the little thing turning over under the comfortable thought about having her own people. She keeps letting truths lie, and the book keeps letting her, and it makes me tender toward her and also a little afraid for her.

Cassie. That opening beat — Cassie already up, cap on, then the look going past Vee to Randi, and by the time Cassie's eyes come back "whatever had been in them was folded away." That landed on me as a small, sharp grief, much bigger than its size on the page. I trust Cassie from my memory of her as observant and protective, and here she sees something about Randi's claim on Vee, and folds it away, and goes. I felt the loss of her leaving the scene, and I want to know what she saw. That's one of my strongest pulls forward.

Randi: I have to be honest. The page makes her radiant and funny, and the "Bruce" line about the bruise-colored drink is a real barb, and the closeness of the forty minutes feels like Vee's good fortune, not a trap. *In the scene,* I like her. That is what makes the understory so uncomfortable. When she says "your man" and Vee lets it sit and it fits, I felt how good it would feel to have Randi hand you a story about your own life and have it fit. I am not immune to that. But I never stopped hearing, underneath, the fact that this friendship is Randi's construction. The chapter does not let me forget it, because I carried it in with me. So my feeling about Randi has not moved so much as deepened into something harder to hold: I believe she is sincere in the moment, and I also believe she is, knowingly or not, moving Vee toward something Vee has not chosen to see. The "Ask and then ravish" line — playful, intimate, a little bit like a nudge — felt like the first time in the chapter I could feel the missing third person in the room, Pace, as a shared project between them.

The heat is real but it is mostly carried in Vee's telling. The highest point for me is the confession: "I wasn't watching myself the way I always am. I just wasn't." That is the erotic core of the chapter — not touch, but the relief of being relieved of self-audit. Her voice goes down when she says "Yeah... Because of him," and I went down with it. The "rampant" joke about the tree and the no-bra aside never quite blunts that softness; it burns at the edges, not the center. There is also a private charge between Vee and Randi — not sexual exactly, but the hand, the knee, the full wattage of Randi's attention. I'd call it romantic more than erotic.

The title "Dear" — I now read it in two ways. It is the word that does the chapter's work: Randi claiming Vee as *dear friend* in front of witnesses; Vee feeling addressed, held, chosen. And it is also the salutation of a letter. Something is being addressed to Vee that she cannot yet read. That connects to the volume's "A POLITE INVITATION" — a social invitation, all surface charm and manners. "WITH A LONG SPOON" sits heavier now; I am keeping a wary distance from someone at this table, and I don't yet know which one of them I should be most afraid for.

Friction? Very little. Maybe one small note: Vee's thought about having her own people, with the "nothing turning over" left untended, is repeated in structure to the oat-milk beat just before it — she lets herself not look at things twice in a row. It earned its place, but I noticed the pattern and felt the author's hand, lightly, in the repetition. Not a true flaw, just a rhythmic tell.

I want, now, to go hiking with Vee and Pace. And I want the week to stop being bright and private without her. And I dread the moment the polite invitation closes.
