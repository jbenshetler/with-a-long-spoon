## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I opened this book expecting Vee — the jacket's girl, the one waking to a man and wanting the woman beside her — and the first page hands me instead Pace and Randi, alone in his bedroom, in the deep end of a scene that has apparently been both prepared for days and been building between them for weeks. That reframing landed immediately: my first chapter isn't the girl's awakening. It's the unmaking of one of the two people the jacket says started it. And that makes the tenderness louder, not quieter, by the end.

The first two-thirds swept me up on the heat alone, and the heat is real. The whole evening is engineered — the apple sliced thin because she won't eat thick, the cut sides up because she eats faster; the slow passes up her sides that turn back just before her breasts; the bench revealed where her underwear has been lying all along. The withholding, the edging, the three *pleases* in a voice she hadn't used since she was a small child crying at her mother — I wanted her to get what she was begging for, and the orgasm that finally came, "uneven," "longer than her orgasms went on," with the shudder she couldn't have produced if she'd been producing it, landed as a gift. And then he says "There you are, Randi," and the chapter pivots on those four words.

The bathroom section is the truest page of the book so far, and it turned the whole night inside out for me. Randi at the mirror, the face she has been using since she was thirteen, the face that has just said *I'm begging* out loud — and her decision, arrived at "quiet as a deadbolt going over": he can have the face; the one he found is going back behind it, **and she is not coming out again.** She flinches when he tells her she's beautiful. That flinch is the hinge of everything. It's not that he did something wrong — within the scene he is careful, exact, patient, scrupulous about the safeword, respectful of the locked bathroom door. It's that the tenderness he is waiting to give her is precisely what she cannot receive, and the chapter makes me feel that cruelty from inside her ribs.

Do I trust anyone? I trust Pace *within* the scene. He isn't violating anything; he checks in, he waits for her weight to come forward, he leaves the closed door closed. But the chapter earns a slow, diffuse dread that has nothing to do with a safeword being ignored. He reads the evening as a triumph — a girl who finally let someone feed her hunger — while she is in the next room deciding never to show him that hunger again. The asymmetry of interpretation is the thing I can't shake. And the jacket's whisper, that the invitation "began as a game between the two people now closest to her," makes me read his confidence and her concealment as the long spoon itself: two people handling something dangerous and believing, or needing to believe, they are holding it at a safe distance. Randi's locked door tells me the distance has already collapsed.

*The Bench* now means exactly what the chapter hides in plain sight: the table that is not only a table, the furniture that conceals an instrument of exposure. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, sings off the page — the evening is exquisitely polite, from "I sliced this for you" to "Just checking," and the invitation is the exposure itself, the thing Vee's jacket promised arrives "long before she knew there was one." *With a Long Spoon* I feel as the old warning made architecture: you sup with the devil by keeping far enough away not to burn — and this chapter shows the spoon not being long enough. The blurb's closing line, *every yes was freely given, that was the problem*, is already humming inside Randi's yeses: they were real, they undid her, and now she must disown them.

What I want next is Vee, and what I dread is the repetition. I expect her to arrive soon, and I now carry foreknowledge Pace and Randi don't share — I have seen Randi lock a door on herself, and I have seen Pace misread the sound of it as peace. When the two of them turn their attention to a girl who doesn't know there's a plan, I'll be reading from inside the plan, ahead of him and unable to warn her. The closing image — the bench waiting under its tabletop, "full of what it was, doing what it would continue to do, waiting for the next time" — is not afterglow. It's architecture.
