## Reader reaction

I opened this cold, expecting — from the jacket — to meet Vee, and instead the chapter is entirely Pace and Randi, the two people the jacket told me are the planners. That disorientation is part of the pull: I'm dropped into a relationship already deep, already strange, with no preamble. The bench scene is explicit and psychologized at the same time, and it worked on me precisely because of that combination. I was inside Randi's dawning horror and arousal together, and Pace's cool, measuring tenderness beside it. The heat was real, and then the bathroom delivered the turn that reframed everything before it felt cheap.

Pace unsettles me while I also understand him. He's a man who builds furniture to hold a woman exactly where he wants her, who slices apples thin because he's noticed how she eats them, who says "good girl" at the moment she breaks, and who then sits back and feels "the small warm pleasure of how much further she had gone than he had thought she would." That line made my skin prickle — it's the pleasure of a craftsman admiring his work, not just a lover. But he also respects the locked door, citing Daphne, and his restraint is real. So my suspicion is not that he's a predator hiding it; it's that he's a man who loves the architecture of control, and Randi is the current project. I don't trust him entirely, and I don't think the chapter wants me to.

Randi is the one I'm carrying. The chapter gives me her interior in that locked bathroom, and it's devastating. The face, the mask, the "slight permanent surprise" she's built her life around — the man has looked past it, and she cannot let him do that again. "He could have the face. The face was very good." That's not a woman basking in afterglow; that's a woman slamming a door inside herself. I feel for her profoundly. She's discovered something real — the begging, the aliveness — and her response is to hide it, because being seen that way is more terrifying than being used the old way. The small key-turning hum at the end is perfect and awful.

The swoon was there, mostly in the aftercare: the water bottle, the blanket she'd brought herself, the stroking of hair, the two quiet "kisses with no pressure behind them." And then the scene flips, and the swoon curdles into something closer to dread. The erotic charge was high — the edging, the begging, the shuddering orgasm, the wet sounds described without embarrassment — but it was always shot through with Pace's observational coldness. He is not lost in it; he is watching, timing, choosing when to deny. That is the point, obviously, and it was hot, but it also kept me a little outside the heat, like I was reading over Pace's shoulder. The peak for me wasn't the sex; it was "There you are, Randi," because it landed as both salvation and violation.

Any friction? The chapter is long, but it earns the length; the only moment that pulled me out was near the end, when Pace thinks "That's not what this night was for" about his own erection and then quietly sets the fact down. It felt like the author nudging me to admire his restraint, and I didn't need the nudge. Also the sudden switch to Randi's POV mid-chapter, though set off with a break, was a jolt — effective but a jolt. No boredom; I was held.

The chapter title, "The Bench," is literal but quickly becomes a figure for exposure: her facedown, open, everything a surface usually covers hanging free. And at the end, the bench sits there "full of what it was, doing what it would continue to do," which makes it feel like a third character. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, now reads as ironic: this chapter is anything but polite, and the "invitation" is a machine built to strip away politeness. The series title, *With a Long Spoon*, evokes the proverb about supping with the devil — appropriate, since I'm already not sure which of these two is the devil, or if the devil is the intimacy itself. The blurb's closing beat, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," hangs over Randi's "yes" to staying and the locked door of her interior: the yes is free, and the problem is everything she isn't saying.

I want to see Vee now, because the jacket promised me her story, and this chapter has made me desperate to know how she fits between these two. I expect the next chapter will shift to her, and I dread the moment the plan — whatever it is — reaches her, because I've just seen what these two do to each other when there's no outside witness. My guess from here: Pace and Randi are each other's long spoon, and Vee is the supper.
