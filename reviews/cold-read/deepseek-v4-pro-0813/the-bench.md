## Reader reaction

## The felt read

This chapter caught me off guard in a good way. The jacket set me up to expect Vee's story, and instead I'm dropped into a fully established, deeply specific intimacy between Pace and a woman I don't get to name until late — and when the reveal comes, "There you are, **Randi**," it recast everything I'd just read. I'd been inside the scene without knowing whose scene it was. That withholding worked: for most of the chapter she's simply *her*, the woman, and I was reading the texture of a couple that already has its own private language — the unlocked door she always locks behind her, the apple sliced thin because she'll only eat thin slices, the Steely Dan. It's lived-in in a way erotica rarely bothers to be, and it pulled me in before I'd decided to trust it.

The heat is real and it peaks early. The apple game — bite, kiss, bite, feed him — is tender and hot and strange, and his restraint is the charge, not a brake on it. Her directing him to turn the chair, straddling him, the way she "had stopped saying" *Jesus, Pace* — the chapter knows how to make me lean in. The line that landed hardest for me in the early going: her having worn the matching set "to chapter dinner because chapter dinner had only been a stop on the way to him," that she'd been thinking, since waking, "about being undressed in this room." *That* is desire rendered, and it earned several paragraphs that followed.

I have to be honest that the undressing flagged. Every garment folded to the chair, every byway enumerated — it was meticulous, and the meticulousness *is* the point, but at moments it tipped from liturgical into checklist, and I felt the author's patience rather than Pace's. "The walk was part of what he was watching" was one line too many; I didn't need to be told.

Then the bench scene took over and the chapter earned everything. The edging, the not-knowing-when, her bracing — the page makes you *feel* the reorganization of her nervous system. And the turn is devastating: the *please* that changes registers, that "came from below," that's "almost ugly compared to the bright sorority-house voice." He hears it, and he knows it's hers, and I believed him. The crying without sobbing, the tears "pooling on the leather under her cheek," and then — "There you are, Randi." That was the chapter's real orgasm, not the one he gave her.

And then it flips. The bathroom section is the best writing here, and the most punishing. The face as armor, "what was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing." The horror is exact: the most alive she's ever felt came from being seen, and being seen is the thing she can't survive. She decides — quiet as the deadbolt she's never before locked — to go back behind the face and not come out. The final irony is total: Pace sits on the bed thinking this was a remarkably good evening, that he *fed* her, and she lies against him thinking she has to figure out what to do about this. The bench, "full of what it was, doing what it would continue to do, waiting for the next time." That closing sentence is a promise and a warning, and it made me want the next chapter immediately.

Do I trust Pace? He's not sinister in any ordinary register — he's warm, patient, and genuinely believes his patience *is* care. But the chapter doesn't let me be entirely cozy: "it was not the right *please* yet. It was still her *please*" — that's a man engineering a breakdown he has a theory about. And "the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go" names the conquest plainly even if he doesn't feel it as one. The Daphne line — the door he once didn't respect — gives him a conscience, a lesson learned. And then he applies it perfectly, and the woman behind the locked door uses that very respect to seal him out. That's elegant, and sad.

The chapter title gave me the object before I read it, and knowing "The Bench" was coming built anticipation without spoiling what it was. It landed as the book's first emblem: the table that's "not only a table," the built thing that waits. The volume title — *A Polite Invitation* — sat over this chapter heavy: the politeness is everywhere, the asking, the "tell me your word," the courtly restraint, and the jacket's back-cover whisper, *"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,"* let me see the whole scene as a mechanism. Randi's yeses are free. The problem is exactly that they are. And *With a Long Spoon* — the devil's supper, the long spoon of distance — reads here as the exact thing Pace and Randi are failing to keep between them. The polite invitation is the seduction's form, and this chapter shows it working on someone it will cost.

I want the next chapter. I want to see what Vee's entry looks like, now that I've seen what Randi is running from and what Pace believes he's offering. My dread: that the bench is not the worst of what's waiting, and that Randi's quiet resolve — *not ever* — is a door the rest of the book will have to open.
