## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter carrying The Pointing Game and See You Later fresh, so I knew before Cassie pointed him out that the "cute guy in line" was going to be Pace. And it was. That knowledge didn't deflate the chapter; it gave it a second, buried track. The whole time I'm inside Vee being charmed, I'm also watching Randi's hands, and the two registers rub against each other like a wrong key in a right lock.

Vee is the one who owns the chapter, and I feel for her more sharply than I did in See You Later. She's smart and working hard and so open to being chosen that it hurts a little. The "borrowed poise" when she tries out Randi's name bit — "Yes, like the university. No, my parents didn't plan it" — is tender because it's a girl reaching for a social self she wants to be, and it lands, and then she quietly wants her own name back. I noticed the single word: *"Vee," she added, quickly, suddenly wanting her own name back. "People call me Vee."* That small retreat from the borrowed Vivienne is a whole character in one beat. And "She felt, absurdly, chosen" — that's the ache. She is being chosen, in the most mechanical possible sense, and she has no idea. My protectiveness toward her is real.

Pace is harder for me now. In The Bench he was patient, attentive, even moving in his attention. Here I see the same instrument aimed at someone who doesn't know she's in a scene. There are tells only I can catch, because I was in the room for The Pointing Game: when he asks Vee what she's studying, he says *"Apart from stats"* — she never said she was in stats at this table; he knows because Randi told him. That one landed cold on me. The tutor's choice — not solving it in two lines, making the margin note, giving the pencil back — read to Vee as him keeping himself out of it so the solving stayed hers, and on the surface that's generous. Underneath it, I recognize the man from the bench: he doesn't rush, he lets her arrive, and the arriving is the part he controls. I still understand why Vee melts. I melted a little too, at "May I call you?" and the way he says "You already know every piece of this." But I don't trust a single warm word out of him right now.

Randi is the buried live wire of the chapter. She gets almost no interior — but her hands won't stop moving to her coffee cup, and twice "the cup came up to her mouth and went back down, and no sip happened." Vee files that as *she's bored, this is going on too long,* and I caught myself doing the opposite: that's not boredom, that's a woman sitting beside her boyfriend while he courts the girl she picked, performing the wingwoman with absolutely no room to feel whatever she's feeling. I wanted her interior badly. This chapter keeps her a closed door, exactly like the bathroom after the bench — and that consistency is part of what makes her sad to me. She planned this, and she's going to sit through it with a smile fixed on.

Cassie is the one I trust. Her observation is the sharpest thing said aloud in the whole room: "He looked at her—" *a tip of the chin toward Randi* "—the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." It's funny, and it's also a knife, because of course he looked at Randi like a great-aunt — she's not the object of the performance, she's its audience. Cassie doesn't know that, but her almost feral plainness keeps her honest in a room full of hidden geometry. I like her more every chapter.

The pull of the chapter landed hardest for me at the end: Vee's hand on the solved page, the marginal cross he left, her phone facedown, and *"She let herself want it to light up."* That ache is completely real on her side, and I felt it, even though I also wanted to reach through and tell her not to. It's the same ache I had for her in See You Later, now sharpened by the fact that the thing she hopes for is already a script.

I trust nobody in the scene except Cassie, and possibly Vee's own unknowing self. The suspicion isn't introduced by this chapter — it was born in The Pointing Game — but this page feeds it. The Wilson coffee shop is the same one Randi named to Brooke as her alibi in Standards; when she suggests "the place on Wilson" here, it stopped being a random nice spot and became loaded for me. The "Apart from stats" slip. The cup held without sipping. These are small, and Vee misreads all of them, which is exactly the design.

Erotically, the chapter is a steady simmer rather than a blaze. It peaks for me at "May I call you?" — the formality of it in a room of boys who'd say "can I get your number" — and in Vee's body answering before her mouth catches up. The forearms moment, the shirt that "would not come," the way she keeps trying to find the shape under him and can't — that's desire doing real work. It never goes slack because the dramatic irony keeps it friction-hot even in the quiet math explanation.

The only small friction I felt as a reader was the twice-repeated *"The thought did not finish"* — it read as a deliberate stylistic tick, but honestly I liked it; it matches Vee's own refusal to complete the thought about what he was doing, and mine too. The chapter earns its length. The math scene could have dragged; it didn't, because the seduction is in the listening, not the integral.

The title. *Substitution* — before I read, I thought it would be about the math. It is, beautifully and then chillingly: two things that don't work alone, "at right angles," become one plain, nameable thing. Pace explains it to Vee exactly that way — "You already know every piece of this." That is the plan, stated in the vocabulary of calculus: substitute until the problem becomes something you already know how to solve. Vee is the substitution. The chapter title didn't give anything away early; it landed after, like a click.

The volume title has been sitting with me all along: *With a Long Spoon*. I know the proverb — "He who sups with the devil should have a long spoon." It promises intimacy with something dangerous, closeness kept at a safe remove. And *A Polite Invitation* is exactly what Vee just received. That pair of titles feels like the book telling me, politely, that I'm being invited to watch something polite on its surface and not at all polite underneath. From here, that's where it's taking me: into the invitation and what it costs.

What I want next is the phone to light up, and I dread it lighting up. I want to see the date, and I want to see the moment Vee learns who Randi actually is to Pace. I expect the next turn is the courtship deepening while Randi's composure thins. My guess from here: the "substitution" will keep working cleanly until it suddenly doesn't, and the chapter where Vee sees the full geometry will be the one that hurts most.
