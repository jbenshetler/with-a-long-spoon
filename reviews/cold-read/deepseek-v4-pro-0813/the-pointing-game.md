## Reader reaction

In the opening bed scene I had to anchor myself — the time-skip moved us past the Saturday of "Standards" without ceremony — but once I found my footing the chapter had me. Pace asking the threesome question only after she'd come down earned some of my trust: "he had never needed to fill a silence" is the warmest thing he's thought so far, and the afternoon going ordinary before the real ask felt deliberate and kind. But the moment he speaks the question, she comes before she can answer, and the page is explicit: "her body had said so before she could make the wanting mean anything safer." That's the needle threading the whole chapter — his care and his power at once.

Randi moved me most. After "Standards" left her on the bed holding her breath, this chapter lets the valve open. The bare "yes" when Vee appears — "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — is the most unguarded we've seen her since the bench, and I rooted for it. Then the recovery, "the kitten finding her feet," and the devastating cover: not a woman hungering for a woman, which was "unsurvivable," but a girlfriend offering her man a gift. She flinches when he asks "Do you actually want to taste another woman?" and I felt that flinch in my own chest. Yet she's not only a victim of his game now — the collision is hers, the hand staying "a half-beat longer than the apology required." She's becoming an instrument of it.

The swoon is real and specific. "He had not wiped his face since the bed" — the kiss where she tastes herself on his mouth in daylight, while students pass — is the chapter's hottest image, and it fuses the bedroom and the quad into one transgression. The ending landed on me fully: her failing-to-hide grin, "Her name is Vee," the unplanned footwork, then the second "yes" — breathy, bedroom-register, in daylight — and her asking "Do you think she'll like the bench?" That last line is funny, dark, and proof of a private language between them; I wanted them to have it.

My unease is earned, and it's for Vee. She's a real woman who doesn't know she's being auditioned. "Go get her" — "For us." The collision is staged so cleanly that "the redhead's hand flew to her own mouth… stricken, apologizing" — Vee's first, unguarded instinct is to take blame, and Randi harvests the warmth of that. Pace stands "far enough back to be nobody," watching the whole engineered pickup as "the beautiful part." The book has trained me to watch Pace read a person and move her; now he's doing it outward, with Randi as his instrument, and Vee as the appetite they've already begun to consume. That's not paranoia — the page shows me the staging and calls it beauty.

Friction, honestly felt: the opening time-skip took a reread to place. And Vee's entrance is allowed to thunder — "drew the eye as nothing else on the quad had all afternoon" — which is gorgeous but nakedly mechanical, the author's thumb telling me *this one*. I noticed it, didn't resent it. Same with the fantasy of "that mouth at Randi's breast" arriving before Vee has spoken on the page; it collapses her to appetite even as the prose insists on her aliveness.

"The Pointing Game" now means exactly what it says, and more: the game of pointing at women, the social wrongness made private fun, "I point. You pick." It promised a game and delivered a recruitment. "WITH A LONG SPOON" reads like the old warning about supping with something dangerous — keeping a measured distance — and "A Polite Invitation" matches the book's register precisely: Brooke's warm inquisition, the sorority etiquette, Pace's mildness, all mannerly surface around an appetite none of them can name. Next I expect the invitation to reach Vee herself; I dread the collision being treated as consent, and I want to see her from the inside.
