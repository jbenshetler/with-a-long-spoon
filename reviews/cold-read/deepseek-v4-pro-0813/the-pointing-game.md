## Reader reaction

I read this chapter with the pull and the unease running together from the start, and the place they merge is one sentence: "that mouth at Randi's breast, all that loose red spilled down over her, and Randi's face doing something he had never seen it do — her first woman, and him there to watch her find out." That swept me up and raised my hair in the same breath, because the picture is gorgeous and the astonishment gets wired to *his watching* — the swoon is real and so is the thumb. The opening worked on me the same way: her coming at a question before she can answer it, "too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." I was in the heat. I also heard the engineering under it — "I had decided this beforehand," "while she was still... undefended" — and the chapter lets both be true. She says "Only in a threesome. With you," and the narration names exactly what she did: handed him the appetite and made herself its instrument, "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift." That line is the chapter's spine, and it's him seeing her move *and* keeping the move at arm's length. I don't distrust him as a villain; I distrust the gap between what he says he's doing — "free to weigh it and choose," "she deserved that gentleness" — and what he does: ask it into her hair right after sex, then run a game where he points and she picks under his eye. "I point. You pick" is exactly half a gift.

Randi broke my heart and lit me up in the same three beats. The bare "Yes" at Vee is the single most alive thing anyone does in this chapter — no costume, no managing, just wanting arrived before the mind. Then the recovery: "Uh — yeah. *Yeah. I like her.*" He watches the whole recovery without blinking. And then, back at him, *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* That line made me ache because it shows her trying to pour the wanting into the one container that made it survivable — the bench, the place she was found, with him present. She can't want Vee alone, so she wants Vee *with them*. The grin that "broke the managing surface" is real happiness, and I rooted for it completely, which made the frame around it sharper.

Vee: I liked her immediately, and that's the danger the chapter wants. "Clothes chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task" made me laugh and want her; the laugh "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" is the thing all of Randi's grammar exists to prevent. She reads as *the answer* — the one the grammar can't touch — and she's also just walked into a collision she thinks is her fault: "the redhead's hand... flew to her own mouth... stricken, apologizing." That told me she's kind before I know anything else. I dread the game reaching her, *because* the chapter made me like her. He's staged the "accident" and she'll carry the apology for it.

Heat peaked twice: the opening question (a 2's burn), and the bare "Yes" at the dining hall doors — the heat of wanting given a name and a copper head. It thinned in the middle catalog, "the one in lavender... the one in sage," which read as inventory for a stretch. Friction, small: "Sex-kitten" pulled me out of the register once; and "good enough that the good feeling and the wanting became one fact" is the author stepping forward to say the thesis — beautiful, but it names what the scene had already shown me.

The chapter title "The Pointing Game" gave away nothing before I read it, and after the read it recasts itself: pointing is *aiming her wanting* — he points, she picks, but only within the direction he aimed. "Game" is the cover over a real hunger. The volume line, "With a Long Spoon — Book One — A Polite Invitation," now reads to me as a promise that the manners *are* the mechanism: a polite invitation is how the unpolite need reaches for you, and the long spoon is the distance required to sup with something you can't fully hold. I don't know the proverb's exact stakes from the page, but that's the felt echo. A Polite Invitation, after this chapter, has teeth: the collision was an "accident," the question was asked "into her hair," the door was held. Politeness as leverage.

From here I want the stats class to be the lie that becomes real — and I dread it becoming the bench instead. A guess from here, and only that: Vee won't fit the grammar, which means she may not fit the *game* either, and I half expect the game to break in her hands. I want Randi to get one hour of wanting her without a costume. I don't expect the chapter to give it to her.
