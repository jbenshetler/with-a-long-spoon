## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The chapter opens on exactly the thing the last one taught me to dread: Randi, back in Pace's bedroom, looking at the bench, and saying *let's do something different* — handing him the day "as she handed him every one." Since we're inside Pace, the sentence lands as tender observation. It also lands, after *Standards*, as the sound of a door locking from the wrong side. She does not hand him things because she is all in; she hands them because the thing she actually wants would have to be asked for in a voice she just heard herself use and has sworn never to use again.

The sex works on me; I can't say it doesn't. The question asked *into the warm disorder of her* — *would you like to taste another woman?* — and her coming hard, too fast, ahead of herself: that's genuinely hot, and it's also terrifying to watch from inside Pace, because he narrates her body's answer as *the true one* while narrating her mind as slow, behind, needing to be led gently past itself. "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." I wrote that down. It's the most frightening sentence in the book so far, because it's dressed as love. The erotic charge peaked for me right there — heat and dread braided. Then it cooled during her recovery: *"Only in a threesome. With you."* I had to set the page down for a second. She flinched when he asked it again in daylight. She flinched, and then she reached for the silk voice and gave her wanting to him as a gift. The prose admires her for it — "it was beautiful" — and I think that's the point, and it makes me want to shake them both. His patience now reads not as kindness but as a man building a hallway she can only walk one direction down, while telling himself it's a game with no named winner.

And God help me, the pointing game swept me up anyway. Not in the way it swept *her*; I kept feeling her readiness through his description of it, the girl on the library steps getting the first real *maybe*, the kiss with her own taste in it, his quiet *"maybe," I agreed.* But Vee. Vee works on me the way Vee works on Randi: the chapter spends all afternoon grading narrow correct girls and sealed pairs and performing warmth, and then Vee comes out of the dining hall throwing her head back laughing like she forgot she could be seen, and the grammar has nothing to do with it, and I felt the drop right along with Randi. *"Yes," she said. The word came out alone, with nothing around it.* Then the amendment, the costume: *Yeah. I like her.* That's the chapter's whole tragedy in miniature and it happens in a paragraph. The collision — the redhead's hand flying to her own mouth, apologizing — I saw it from Pace's pole and I was appalled and completely taken by the craft of it, the same way he is. The grin she can't hide. The happy footwork. I wanted it for her, this bright thing, and then I remembered what *the bench* meant to her alone in the bathroom, and I wanted someone to run up and take it back.

Trust-wise: I don't trust a single person on this page, fully, and I have evidence for all of it now. Pace's care is real and his design is real and he is a person who says "let it come out laughing, dressed as play, before she had to look it full in the face" about another person's desire, as if managing it into softness were a kindness. Randi's wanting is real and her flight from it is real, and the flight is deciding faster than the wanting. I have no suspicion yet about Vee herself — she's allowed to be just a laughing girl — but I already dread what a girl who laughs like that is going to become in this house.

Friction: the campus scenery paragraph ran long for me — the maple, the colonnade, the frisbee, the speaker — lovely, but I felt the author's thumb holding me still while the real event waited. The *"Let's go feed you"* at the end stopped me; it's doing too many things — tenderness, power, the apple from *The Bench* — and I don't believe Pace is naive to any of them anymore.

The chapter title is too knowing for comfort. *The Pointing Game* — I'd have laughed if I'd heard it before reading; after, it names the thing Pace is actually doing in the name of play, and it feels like the book winking at me from the jacket. The volume title, *With a Long Spoon*, sits heavier now: the polite distance you'd keep from a devil you're choosing to dine with. *A Polite Invitation* — the whole book is an invitation issued in someone else's voice, with the manners kept up the whole way down. The titles aren't promising rescue; they're promising complicity, and keeping step with it.

What I want next, and what I dread, are the same thing: I want to see Vee again from anywhere but Pace's mind. I want Randi to say yes in her own voice or break under the weight of not. And I'm afraid the book is going to give me exactly these, unsoftened — the way *The Bench* did, with a woman crying into a stranger's towel and locking the door behind her.
