## Reader reaction

I went into this chapter already wary of the distance between what Randi feels and what she lets herself show, and this chapter made me feel that gap as heat and as unease at the same time. The opening sex lands hard — the question mid-thrust, *"Would you like to taste another woman?"*, hitting her body before her mind, and her coming at the words alone. That was the most destabilizing beat in the book so far, and it worked on me. Then he asks again, calm, afterward, and I watched the flinch: *barely. He felt it rather than saw it.* I was with her, recognizing that flinch from the bathroom mirror and the duvet. She's still putting the face on. He sees it too, and he admires the recovery even as he sees through it. That's the knot: he sees everything, and he lets her keep the costume.

The pointing game swept me up against my judgment. The kiss with her own taste on his mouth — her breath catching — was a spike. And then Vee comes out of the dining hall, and the chapter changes key. The catalog of sorority bodies had started to go flat for me, but Vee is rendered as alive in a way none of the others were: *laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen.* Randi's bare *"Yes"* — *with nothing around it* — made me ache for her. That's the swoon I came for, not the game, not Pace's watching: the follow breaking her frame before the lead calls the step. I wanted her to have Vee, and I wanted her to be allowed to want it.

The unease is earned, not reflexive. This chapter is entirely Pace's POV — after *Standards* gave me Randi's interior, I'm now shut out of it and left leaning on his reading of her. He's not wrong about her; he's exquisitely right. But he frames everything as a thing he's giving her — *"I point. You pick"* — when he planted the question, kept it hanging, and then stood at a pole watching her execute the pickup like choreography. *Far enough back to be nobody, a man waiting on someone.* That line is full of his control and of my own position as reader: I'm at his shoulder, not hers. The chapter wants me there. I don't fully trust that seat. The last line, *"Let's go feed you,"* swings from the ecstatic *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* back to the man who slices apples thin because she eats them faster that way — tender, and the same hand.

Friction, mostly small. *"Went to the root of him"* clanged — the prose reached for a euphemism it didn't need. The rejected-women stretch sagged a little, a type-list between *"Maybe"* and Vee. But the pickup paid it all back.

The title gave nothing away before I read it — I didn't know what the game was until Pace pointed. Now it reads as the chapter's mechanism and slightly the book's: pointing is naming, choosing, making her see what she's already looking at. The cover-board line — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — didn't steer me, but it hums now. The proverb promises intimacy at a deliberate remove, exactly the distance Pace keeps while seeming to close it. *A Polite Invitation* is the sorority surface, the civil grammar, and also a quiet second meaning: everything here is offered, and the offering has a hand behind it.

I want Vee to become a person, not a redheaded wish. I want Randi to get more of her own bare *yes*, and I'm afraid the threesome framing will turn Vee into a gift from Randi to Pace rather than something Randi gets to have for herself. What I most want next is Randi's interior, unread by him. What I dread is that the politeness will hold and the cost will arrive later, small as a key turning in a lock.
