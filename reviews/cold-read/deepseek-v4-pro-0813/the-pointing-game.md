## Reader reaction

The pull of this chapter is immense, and most of it is the pull of watching Randi meet her own want. The opening sex is warm and careful — Pace's tenderness shows in the pacing, the holding, the way he wants the day to go ordinary before he asks again — but what stays with me is the question planted mid-coitus, and the way Randi's body answers before she gets a chance to dress it. *She came, hard, her sex clenching around him,* before she could make the wanting mean anything safer. I believe that want is real. The chapter lands it as true, and it lands it on me too: the rest of the afternoon — the pointing, the scanning, the bare *Yes* on the quad — all reads as a woman tasting something she has been holding under her own surface for years. I am rooting for the wanting. I am also, increasingly, watching who gets to decide what it means.

That's where my unease lives, and this chapter earned it. Pace narrates everything, and his certainty is the engine. He takes the body's answer for the true one, he reads her flinch as proof, he calls her words *her mouth still busy correcting*, and the prose is so confident it nearly persuades me. But the page keeps showing me the counter-evidence he reads past: the flinch in the afterglow, the recovery into the kitten voice, the answer that only arrives as a gift to him — *Only in a threesome. With you.* That is the only verbal yes we get, and it is a deflection, and Pace narrates it away as the *wrapper the wanting needed.* The line *He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did.* is the one place the chapter tips its own hand too hard for me — the thesis announced. I wanted a little more air in the ambiguity, because the ambiguity is what's alive here: she does want it, and he is also deciding what her yes means before she has held it herself. Both true. That's the book's charge for me.

But God, the walk. The pointing game is the warmest, most alive sequence in the book so far. Randi wakes up in it — the scanning ahead, the *follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves*, the dry *Is that a yes or a no for you?*, the strong jealous *God, no* at the performer. The library-steps *Maybe* is the first real crack: she holds the girl's face longer than the rest, and Pace kisses her with her own taste on his mouth, and she cracks. Then Vee comes out of the dining hall and the chapter goes molten. The description of Vee is the most generous thing on the page — the lavish scale, the red hair sparking copper, the laugh with the whole body, *head thrown back, throat bare*, the grammar with nothing to grade. And Pace points, and Randi *stops*, and her yes arrives bare, with nothing around it. That is the peak of the chapter. The engineered collision, the redhead apologizing, Randi's hand on her arm staying a half-beat too long — I was fully there, rooting for them. The return, the failing-to-hide grin, the happy footwork, the two *yeses* — one bright, one breathy — it's the most unguarded Randi has been outside of sex, and I ached for her.

Until the last line. *Do you think she'll like the bench?* lands with a small chill. It's her extending the place where she was truly seen into the new wanting, handing Pace the same shape that undid her. I don't think she knows how much she's handing him, or to what. And *Let's go feed you* — the feeding motif again, the apple, the portioned appetite. Tender and troubling at once.

The chapter title undersold nothing — it is exactly the game, and it deepened on me as the game behind the game: Pace pointing her toward her own desire and watching it land. I want Vee's interior next, desperately, and I dread that she may be only a target. I want to know if Randi's locked-away self is still driving anything, or whether the yes is being curated for her.
