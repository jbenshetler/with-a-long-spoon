## Reader reaction

I’m sitting with this chapter still warm. It’s the most alive I’ve felt reading in this book so far, and also the most uneasy, and the two things are tangled in a way I don’t want to separate too fast.

Pace — I’ve liked him across these chapters. He’s patient, he sees Randi, he gives her room. But here I started to feel the weight of how much he directs the seeing. He’s not just watching her want; he’s engineering the conditions under which her want can show itself, and he’s narrating that engineering to himself as generosity. The line that landed on me hardest was: *He hoped the game might give her a way to say something true without forcing her to sit up and put a name to it — let it come out laughing, dressed as play, before she had to look it full in the face. She deserved that gentleness.* And I believe him — I do — that he means it kindly. But it’s still a man choosing when and how a woman meets herself, for her own good. That sits differently after “Standards,” where Randi locked a bathroom door and decided the found self was going back behind the face. Pace is now, in this chapter, actively inviting that self out in public. Maybe he’s right that the play is kinder than the mirror. Maybe. But it’s his porch, his rules, his pointing finger.

Randi — my heart’s split for her. The opening bed scene: *She had been performing, the first week. She was not performing now.* And then the question — *Would you like to taste another woman?* — and her body answers before she can dress it. I felt that in my stomach. And then the recovery, so fast and so practiced: *“Only in a threesome,” she said against his throat, smiling, all silk. “With you.”* I almost laughed, not because it’s funny but because it’s so perfectly her — the want moved into a shape she can survive. Pace sees it as “beautiful,” and it is beautiful, and it’s also a locked door. The whole chapter is her body and her mouth in a race, and her body keeps winning by a half-stride. The moment she says *“Her”* — the bare word, nothing around it — that’s the chapter’s peak for me, not the sex. Then she hears herself and reaches for the costume: *“Yeah. I like her.”* The costuming is the tragedy, and also the heat.

Vee — she’s only on the page for a handful of paragraphs, and she already feels like a weather system. The description is doing a lot: *lush in a way that had nothing curated about it and nothing apologized for*, *clothes chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task*, *the surface offered the grammar nothing*. She’s everything Randi’s vocabulary can’t grade, and that’s exactly why Randi goes helpless. The laugh — *laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen* — that’s the unmanaged life Randi has never allowed herself. I’m already dreading and wanting what happens when the game stops being a game and Vee becomes a person.

The swoon is real. The kiss on the quad — *he had not wiped his face since the bed and he knew the moment her own taste reached her because her breath caught against his lips and held* — that’s the hottest beat in the chapter for me, more than the opening scene. It’s public, it’s secret, it’s evidence. And then the whole final sequence: Randi drifting, the collision that isn’t, the hand on the arm longer than the apology needs, the names, the *happy footwork*, the squeaked *“Yes.”* I was grinning. It worked on me. It worked on me even while I could see the strings.

Trust vs. suspicion — I don’t distrust Pace the way I would distrust a predator, and I don’t think the text has made me. What I feel is closer to wariness of a man who is very good at reading a woman and genuinely enjoys guiding her arrival at herself. He gives her the choice and he points at the women, but he also frames the whole thing as *play* so she never has to own the wanting. And at the end, *“Let’s go feed you”* — he’s still feeding her. He was slicing apples in “The Bench.” It’s tender; it’s also a man who manages a woman’s appetite, even her hunger for another woman. I’m not saying he’s wrong. I’m saying I’m watching.

Erotic charge: high. The opening is explicit and intimate, and the question-in-bed moment is a real spike — her coming from a sentence, the *hard afterclench around him*, the *helplessness* he sees. Then it cools into the walking game, which is its own kind of charge: sustained, public, edge-of-exposure. It builds and builds to the Vee encounter. For me the chapter pegged at a 3 — not because of the sex acts but because the wanting is the point and it’s everywhere, in every step.

Friction: the middle of the walk, the parade of pointed-at women, started to feel slightly mechanical the fourth or fifth time — *the girl on the library steps, the pre-law girl, the matched pair, the chair-running blond* — each one a type, each verdict a data point. I understood it was building a profile of her taste, and it was, but I felt the author’s thumb on the scale a little: the women were all so legible, so typed, until Vee arrives and is the opposite of everything. The contrast worked, but the setup was visible. The other small friction: *He let the camouflage stand. It was the wrapper the wanting needed to come out in.* That’s Pace’s interior, and it’s the book telling me exactly what the game is for, in case I missed it. I didn’t miss it.

The chapter title — “The Pointing Game” — lands differently after reading than before. Before, I thought it would be about selecting a woman, maybe about objectification. Now it’s clearly the game that makes Randi’s desire unspeakable-but-doable: you point, you choose, you don’t have to say *I want her*, you just answer. The title gives away almost nothing, but it sat in my mind the whole chapter as the mechanism of her permission. It’s a good title. It’s also a little cruel, that her awakening has to be a game with a referee.

The volume title — *With a Long Spoon* — and the series line, *A Polite Invitation* — they’re starting to pull together for me now. “A Polite Invitation” is the threesome, the walk, the game: it’s all couched in manners and play and consent, a very civilized invitation to transgression. And “With a Long Spoon” — I know the proverb, *sup with the devil with a long spoon* — it suggests you can engage with something dangerous as long as you keep your distance. Pace is the long spoon. Randi is being fed, carefully, from a safe remove. But the spoon isn’t hers. That scares me a little. The jacket tagline wasn’t in my packet, so I’m going on the board alone.

What I want next: I want the stats class. I want to see Randi next to Vee without Pace in frame, or with Pace holding the other end of the leash. I want to know if Vee is really as unmanaged as she looked across the quad, and whether the game survives contact with someone who doesn’t know she’s in it. I’m half-dreading that Vee will turn out to be more body than person — the lush redhead as symbol — and half-hoping the book is smarter than that. I expect the threesome to actually happen, and I expect it to be the thing that cracks the game open. I don’t know if Randi will survive that. I don’t know if I want her to come out of it as herself or if I’m more scared of what that would look like.
