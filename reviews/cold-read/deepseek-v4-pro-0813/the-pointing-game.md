## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I opened this chapter directly from the aftershock of "Standards" — Randi on her bed in last night's clothes, the breath held, *I can't, I can't* — and the first thing I notice is that she isn't there anymore. Not her interior. The camera has gone behind Pace's eyes and stayed there. I am being shown her only from outside now, and for a long stretch what I feel is myself pressed against the glass. That is a deliberate, unnerving choice, and it makes me read everything she does as something he is allowing me to see.

The sex at the top is good and honest — her hand "flat against the top of his head, not pushing, just holding" got me. So did the kiss where he hasn't wiped his face since the bed, and "the moment her own taste reached her because her breath caught against his lips and held." That was real heat, and it worked on me. But the question — *Would you like to taste another woman?* — is asked *before* this, in the afterglow, in the one moment she is "undefended." He waits for her to be gathered back into herself, and then asks again. Which he presents as ethics. And I almost bought it, because the ethics are real: he wants her to *choose.* But look at what the choosing is asked into. She gives him her wanting dressed as a gift (*Only in a threesome. With you*), and he accepts the wrapper and then spends the entire afternoon peeling it back off. That is not consent coaching. That is orchestration, and it disturbs me, even as I am enjoying it.

The campus walk is the chapter's engine, and it's electrifying. *I point. You pick.* She is "shopping." Her failed recoveries — the dropped stride, the bare *yes* — crack the polish exactly the way the bench did. The woman on the library steps with the beanie ("maybe") is the first honest consideration. The chairing, blow-dried girl gets a God, no "with real feeling in the no," and I loved her for that. But then Vee comes out of the dining hall, and the whole chapter tips. The description of her laugh — "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — is the most alive sentence in the book so far, and it's a direct counterweight to Randi's entire managed existence. The bare *yes* that comes out of Randi is the chapter's peak. I felt it below my ribs.

And then: "Her name is Vee." The book gives her a name like a hook setting. And Randi's feet do "a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do." That's the first unmanaged joy she's had since the bench. The two *yes*es at the end — one squeaked, one in the bedroom register — and then *Do you think she'll like the bench?*, asked low, without lifting her head. That's the door from "Standards" creaking open, and I feel for her so much it aches. Then: *Let's go feed you.* And I am left with a vision of Pace as a man who would like to be her healer, and who is in fact the same man whose house she fled to her own bathroom to cry in.

I have no full suspicion of him yet — he's not a villain, he's a craftsman — but the book has planted the word *exposure* in my brain. The game is public pointing, daylight, the path. He feels her first-beat recoil — the "social wrongness" of it — and lets it pass because it's part of the game. That told me everything I needed about whose gratification the game is finally for. The chapter title works doubly: the game of pointing, and the book pointing me at Pace's hands, pointing.
