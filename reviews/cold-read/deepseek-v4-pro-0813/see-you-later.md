## Reader reaction

The chapter lands on me as a quiet, breathless dread. We're inside Vee now, and I read every warm moment with a stone in my gut, because I just watched this exact warmth being manufactured — the staged collision, the "go get her," the question about the bench. None of that is in this chapter, but it sits under every line like a held breath. Vee is so achingly, recognizably lonely. I don't flinch from her; I ache for her. The way she runs *we should sit together* both ways until it wears a groove, the way she says *yes* and has to fold the eagerness down to keep it from showing its real size, the way she stands under the Chi Latte sign and spins a little — she wants to be chosen so badly that she can't feel the choosing has a purpose she does not know. I want to shake her gently and also just let her have it, the hour, the table, the hand flat between her shoulder blades, because I understand why it would feel like the best hour in months.

Randi is terrifying in this chapter, and the terror is entirely that the warmth works. On the page she's nothing but lovely — the bit about her name, the warm wave that swings to Cassie with "nothing held back," the *love you, girl,* the backward walk that lands *see you later* like a fact. But I know the warmth is a tool from the walking game, and the chapter keeps showing me its seams without ever letting Vee see them. The blank transcript is the killer: Vee can't reconstruct a single thing Randi actually said, only the feeling of being a person who got to sit at that table. That's the mechanism of the seduction, and the prose quietly stages it as what seduction does — replaces content with a feeling. So I trust nothing Randi says, and I also understand exactly why Vee would.

Cassie is the one clean instrument in the room. Her flat *Cassie* refusing the bit, her plain *I've got a thing,* her *Goodbye, Vee* landing with the whole weight of a longer parting. I flinched at that word. Cassie sees something, or simply is something — the one person present who isn't performing. Her absence leaves Vee wholly exposed to the warmth, and the chapter knows it: Vee half-forgets the oddness because "the better thing was moving and Vee went with it."

The swoon is real and it works on me. The high table at the window, the spoons ringing, the lavender latte, the key that goes "in like a lock" — I felt Vee's hunger and her pleasure, and I let myself be pulled. The café scene has its own charged looking: Vee cataloguing the girls in barely anything, the word *floozies* arriving in her mother's voice, and under it "a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for." That's where the chapter's erotic charge lives — not in acts, but in Vee's suppressed, unexamined wanting, her private thought that she has more than they do, better than any of them, and then the second voice closing the picture. The cardigan is the chapter's quiet grammar of self-censorship; she is "covered to the collarbone" and lets the room not know it's a choice.

Friction: the line about latent variables sat a little heavy — "Next time we get into the interesting part — *latent variables*." I felt the author's finger on the scale there, the term too perfectly naming what these three are. And the Chi Latte sign — the Greek you only get to read once you're inside the joke — is almost too apt, a threshold Vee has stood outside forty times and walks under now because Randi is holding the door. It works on me despite the neatness; I'd rather feel the neatness than not have the image. The middle stretch, the conversation Vee can't reconstruct, is a deliberate hole, and I trust it as honesty about how seduction erases content — but it also means the chapter gives me no *words* to hold against the feeling, which is exactly Vee's problem.

The title. Before I read it, *See You Later* sounded warm; after, it sounds like a predator's promise — the "later" is the bench, the thing Pace wanted Vee to like, and Randi delivers it walking backward, holding Vee's upper arms, "already half-turned toward wherever the meeting was." It recolors the whole chapter: the coffee is the *see you*, and the *later* is the trap, scheduled in a calendar Vee can't see. The volume title — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — now feels like a warning I'm watching someone fail to heed. The long spoon is what you use to sup with the devil; the polite invitation is exactly this coffee, this seat, this door held open. It promises a long game, and I believe it.

I dread the next chapter. I expect Randi to keep being warm, to bring Pace in gently, to make Vee feel it was her idea. I fear Vee's hunger is the exact shape of the thing being fed. I want more of the high table, and I want someone to stop this.
