## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter hit me as a slow, warm, terrible thing to read, because for the first time I'm inside Vee's whole sweet unexamined wanting while I know exactly what it's being wanted toward.

I liked Vee immediately and ached with her a little. The not-looking-at-the-door routine — decided three times and maintained in its third minute — is so precisely the texture of a crush you're trying not to admit to. And when Randi appears, the chapter makes me feel the whole apparatus of being chosen from inside: the right pen, the seat beside, "wait is this going to be on the thing," the cold-citrus at her elbow. Vee is not a fool; she's a person whose hunger to belong is the keyhole, and Randi's warmth is shaped exactly to it. I want the swoon for her even while I can't unhave what I know. That's the central friction: it's genuinely swoony — the high table, the lit face, the hand flat between the shoulder blades — and I feel it through Vee while also reading every beat as the performance Randi has been running since The Pointing Game.

Cassie is the rope the page throws me. She refuses the bit, hands her name flat, and then says "Goodbye, Vee" — full-worded, wrong, not "see you later." The page makes Vee notice the wrongness and smooth it, and it lands like a small warning bell hung offstage. I don't know what Cassie sees; I suspect she sees *something* about the warmth, or about what Vee becomes around it, but the chapter honestly leaves that open. I'm holding it open. But the one person who won't be charmed is the one whose parting word is the heavy one.

The café did real work on me. The sign joke — her finally being *allowed in* — got under my ribs, and then the room cataloguing did. Vee reads the bare-midriff girls with her mother's word "floozies" and then keeps looking under the word, and the prose leaves the door open to something she won't name: she could wear what they're wearing, she has more than they have, and then the mother-voice closes the picture and the cardigan stays. That's the chapter's erotic peak for me — not an act but a suppression, a body that wants to be seen and wants to look, dressed to the collarbone, cataloguing other girls' stomachs from inside the thing she won't wear. And the want she has for Randi — "flat helpless want" — sits in her chest "warm, unexamined, glad," which is the most dangerous phrasing in the chapter.

The latent-variables line from the professor landed heavy and a little too pointed: "the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can." It's the chapter telling me how to read it — Vee's want, Randi's hidden self, the whole book's method — and I felt the author's thumb on the scale right there. Then Vee underlines it and it "sat there... going nowhere," which is exactly what she's doing with her own want. I'd have liked the echo a little quieter. Same small push on "the queen having decided there would be a later" — the power move named too cleanly.

The title hit me after Cassie's goodbye: Randi says "see you later," and Vee takes it as "a thing already scheduled in some calendar," which for her is a promise and for me is the trap. Cassie's "goodbye" is the honest version. So the chapter title holds both words' difference — the false warm *later* that will in fact happen, just not the way Vee hears it.

The momentum builds — this is the first chapter from Vee's inside, and the book needed it; now the arrangement has three legs. I dread the bench, and I want Cassie to be right about something.
