# Cold read — My Friend Randi

*scene: scenes/my-friend-randi.md · read after: stranger*

## Reader reaction

This is the chapter I was dreading, and it's worse — smoother — than I feared. It reads as pure comfort and it lands as management. I watched it happen and I still felt the pull of it.

**Vee.** Total sympathy, and now a kind of ache watching her be walked. She arrives raccoon-eyed, wrecked, wanting only "somewhere with a door that shut," and the thing that steadies her is Randi's smell off her neck — "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" — which she pulls in twice and doesn't want to stop. That detail did something to me: it's the body reaching for Randi before the mind does. And the moment that gutted me was quiet — Vee admitting the blonde rival "Sheri" was built in her own head "on the nights I felt stupid for missing him this badly." So the imaginary rival I carried from before was *her* invention, and Randi dismantles it in three sentences. That should feel like relief. It felt like a hook being reset.

**Randi.** Here's where my feeling moved hard. On the surface she is magnificent — fast down the stairs, arms around Vee before a word, the mess swept off the bed without apology so the wrecked girl comes first. Every single thing she says is *true and kind*: the fear is a liar, you haven't slept, you ambushed him, that's a boy holding himself still. I can't point to one false note in the content. And that's exactly what unsettled me. She never once said "maybe he *is* seeing someone." She had the truth available — I know, from before, that Pace really is running two women, that "Sheri" isn't the rival but Randi herself is — and she used every ounce of her warmth to steer Vee *away* from the correct suspicion and back toward the man. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." The reader knows the woman with a face is sitting on the bed.

The coat move is the chapter's spine and it chilled me. Randi doesn't *tell* Vee to show up naked under a coat — she says "You've got that good long coat," stops, "and let the rest hang in the room, and waited for Vee to find it." Vee finds it herself: "And nothing under it." Then Randi laundered the shame out of it beautifully — "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do." That's the engine I was warned about in my own memory: *she produces with words what he produces with his body.* She's authoring tonight's scene and making Vee feel she wrote it. The pressed heels — "Randi always knew the shoes" — is the same hand, gloved into a gift.

**Brooke and the blonde.** Brooke's warmth is starting to read as a texture of this house rather than a person — "The warmth arrived ahead of her… and gave back nothing but more warmth." The unnamed wet-haired blonde who "calculat[es] whether a crisis was hers" and the half-unpacked room full of new things with tissue paper and a coat still in plastic — that's a house of women, and I don't fully trust its coziness.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No longer neutral. The text *earned* my suspicion this time, not by any tell but by the gap between what I know and what Randi does with what she knows. The craft is that nothing she says is a lie — it's the selection. My unease is specifically about the coat plan being *her* plan wearing Vee's face.

**Erotic charge.** Present but strange — it runs on shame, exactly as before. The heat peaks at "she felt the other thing turn over under the burn, as it always did once the shame was lit, the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's Vee's whole erotic wiring in one line and it worked on me. The longer hugs and the goodbye kiss "unremarkable now, only what they did" carry a low current too — I'm increasingly aware there's something between these two women that isn't only friendship, even if Vee reads it as nothing.

**Friction.** Almost none — it's disturbingly frictionless, which is the point but also the only thing my critic-brain flagged. The one beat that pulled me out slightly: "let it sit," "let it hang," "let the quiet sit" — Randi's technique gets described in that pausing rhythm a few times close together, and I started to *see* the method, which nudged toward the author's thumb. But it's faint.

The New Year retelling landed well. Vee asks for it to "give something back into the warmth," and Randi's Gstaad story — John, naming three children on the first lift, the dress "the kind you tell your daughter about" — is charming and does its damage sideways: it's "the exact thing she didn't have." And then Randi hits the same wall I already knew about: "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." From my memory I know that night was hollow, that she measured John against Pace and Vee and felt nothing — so "the bedroom's the room I actually live in" reads to me as a confession disguised as a quip. She lives in the room where the only two people who move her are Pace and the girl on this bed.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the coat scene, and I dread it. *Guess from here:* Vee shows up tonight naked under the coat and Pace — the man who "goes behind a wall" — either opens it and folds, which would mean the rupture gets erased and she's fully back inside the machine, or the cold holds and she's humiliated in the one posture Randi promised was brave. Either way Randi has arranged the meeting between her two lovers without either knowing she did it. I'm bracing for the peace-sign to *work*, because that's the crueler outcome — it teaches Vee that Randi is always right.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Wrecked from the door scene, Vee drove to Randi's — a house of women (Brooke; an unnamed wet-haired blonde; Randi's room half-unpacked, full of new things). Randi rushed her upstairs, held her long, and talked her down: the fear's a liar, you ambushed him with no warning, "that's a boy holding himself still," the blonde rival "Sheri" is one Vee invented in her own head. She never once said Pace might really have another woman. Then she steered Vee — letting Vee "find it" herself — into a plan: drive back *tonight* showered and dressed, naked under her long coat, a wordless "peace sign he can't misread." She reframed Vee's shame ("slutty") into "the bravest thing a woman can do," and pressed a pair of her own black heels into Vee's hands. Vee, shame-and-wanting fused as ever, agreed. Then Vee asked for the real New Year story; Randi told the Gstaad/John fairy tale (naming children on the first lift, the daughter-dress, the ball), went quiet at the bedroom door, and closed it with a quip — "perfect everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." Long goodbye hug, the now-baseline goodbye kiss. Vee drove to the dorm to sleep, plan intact: tonight she goes to him.

**Who's who.**

- **Pace / Peter** — provisioner who never says love; met her stranger-cold this morning (Randi reframes it as a wall, not the end); really is running two women. Offstage this chapter; tonight's target.
- **Randi** — the brunette confidante; secretly loves Vee, is Pace's other woman; here she performs flawless comfort while withholding the one truth that would save Vee, and authors tonight's coat scene while making Vee feel she chose it. Everything she says is true; the cruelty is the selection. My suspicion is now earned, not neutral. Her Gstaad quip reads to me as buried confession.
- **Sheri** — Pace's real blunt friend, whom Vee wrongly cast as the rival (now dismantled). **Brooke** — warm-surfaced sorority girl, "always passing through." **Cassie** — moral floor (absent). Unnamed wet-haired blonde in Randi's house.

**How I feel.** Golden-on-top, colder-underneath than ever — this was comfort as capture and I felt the machinery even as it soothed. Warmth toward Randi curdled into dread. Body-note: Vee reaching for Randi's scent, the too-long hugs, the goodbye kiss — something between these women isn't only friendship.

**Open questions.** Does the coat scene work — Pace folds and the rupture is erased — or does the cold hold and Vee is humiliated in the pose Randi called brave? Either way, Randi has arranged a meeting between her two lovers, neither knowing. Still open: the scar; right-hand-only eating; sealed family; the nude photo; Randi's locked bracelet; what Randi actually wants with Vee's body; the threesome timing I keep sensing under all of this.
