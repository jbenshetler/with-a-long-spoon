## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

The POV shift hit me like a door opening in the wrong direction — I've been living inside Pace's head and Randi's for three chapters, and now I'm suddenly *Vee*, the copper-haired girl from the dining hall doors, and she has no idea. That's the whole engine of this chapter and it worked on me completely: I stood at Pace's light pole and watched Randi engineer that collision, watched the hand linger a half-beat too long, and now I'm inside the mark, feeling the con arrive as the best morning of her life. "She'd carried the morning of it like an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter life." God. My stomach hurt the whole way through, and it hurt *because* the chapter is so tender — Vee writing the date so her hand has somewhere to be, the three minutes of not looking at the door, the *yes* she folds down to a smaller size so it won't show. I know exactly what Randi is doing — I watched her learn to do it — and Vee just knows she got chosen.

And Randi. My feeling about her has moved, and moved in a direction I didn't expect: not softened, *sharpened*. From the outside, through Vee's eyes, she's dazzling — "whole face lit, as though the thing she had been hoping for had in fact been this." And it lands as warmth, genuine-feeling warmth, on Cassie too, the question left open, the delight at the flat one-word name. But I've been in Randi's bathroom mirror. I've seen her arrange her face after the bench. So when Vee tries later to reconstruct what they actually talked about and *can't* — "the leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed" — I felt the chill under the swoon. That's not a conversation. That's an administration. Randi is doing to Vee exactly what was done to her, and she's doing it beautifully, and the worst part is I believe some part of her means it.

Cassie is the person I now trust most in this book, on about twelve lines of evidence. The flat *"Cassie."* The refusal of the bit's open door. And then *"Goodbye, Vee"* — the whole word, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday," and she doesn't fix it when Vee laughs it off. I clocked that the way you clock a floorboard going cold. Cassie *sees* something. She couldn't possibly know what I know, but the page earned my suspicion through her anyway — she's the only person in the chapter whose warmth isn't wattage. I want more of her and I'm also afraid of what's being set up around her.

The Chi Latte scene is where the chapter got under my skin in a different register. Vee's interior inventory of the room — *floozies* arriving "whole and in her mother's voice," then the longer look the word doesn't account for, the stomach cut, the low waistband, "she had more than they had, and better than any of them" — and then the cardigan staying buttoned, the room not knowing it's a choice. That's a whole woman in one paragraph: appetite and shame and pride and self-concealment running at once, none of it looked at straight. It rhymes with Randi portioning herself over the apple slices, and the rhyme makes the pairing feel fated in the worst way — two girls with locked interior rooms, except one of them has learned to pick locks. The heat in this chapter is all unspoken, all crush — Vee's "flat helpless want" with nowhere to put itself — and it *worked* on me precisely because it's innocent. It's the hottest kind of simmer: desire that doesn't know its own name yet, directed at someone who knows exactly what name it will eventually answer to.

The titles are doing heavy, gorgeous work. "See You Later" is Randi's exit line — "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see" — and from Vee's side it reads as a promise, the queen decreeing a future. From my side it reads as a *booking*. And it sits against Cassie's "Goodbye" like the two poles of the chapter: one woman speaking a future she controls, one speaking a farewell she can't explain. The cover board — WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION, no tagline supplied — snapped into focus here: I've been turning the proverb over since I opened the book, and this chapter gave me the spoons, literally, "an irregular ringing, everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom of their drinks." Vee is supping. She does not have a long spoon. And "A Polite Invitation" is precisely what this chapter *is* — the smile, the arm-squeeze, the lavender thing, *love you, girl*. My one quibble: the professor's "latent variables — the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer" landed a touch plated, theme spoken aloud by a lecture, but it also gave Vee underlining it and "going nowhere" a small ache, so I'll allow it.

What I want next, marked as a guess from here: the later Randi promised. I want it and dread it in the same breath. I expect the bench to come back into the frame — Randi asked Pace "do you think she'll like the bench?" and Vee is now walking toward a room whose furniture she can't imagine. What I dread most isn't the seduction; it's that Vee is already half-gone on a feeling built from nothing, and that when the transcript stays blank it will be too late. Also: Cassie said goodbye. I don't think the book said it carelessly.
