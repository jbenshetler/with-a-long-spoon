## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

The POV shift knocked the wind out of me in the best way. After three chapters inside Pace's patience and Randi's performance, I'm suddenly inside Vee — and Vee doesn't know. I watched that collision get *built* last chapter, watched Randi's shoulder meet hers on a line adjusted by degrees, and now I'm inside the girl who "had genuinely not been clear" who walked into whom, who carried the morning of it "like an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter life." That's the chapter's whole engine: everything that dazzles Vee, I've already seen from the other side. The warmth that swings over "total, undimmed." The bit with the built-in laugh slot. I kept wanting to put a hand on her shoulder through the page. And yet — I don't think Randi's warmth is fake exactly, which is worse. I saw the bare "Yes" on the lawn. The wanting is real; it's just been dressed as friendship, as recruitment, as anything but what it is. Vee is being handed a gift whose giver won't admit what it's for.

Vee herself undid me. The girl who gets to lecture early, who writes the date at the top of a clean page "so that her hand would have somewhere to be," who decides not to look at the door and then spends three minutes not looking at it — I know her, I have been her. And then the Chi Latte room read: the "floozies" arriving in her mother's voice, and *underneath the word* the longer look at the sprinter's stomach, the low waistband, "the easy ownership of being mostly bare." She has more than they have and better, and she's the one covered to the collarbone, and "she did not look at either fact straight." The page is telling me her wanting has been pointed at women for a long time and has never once been allowed to arrive. Pace saw it in thirty seconds of a laugh. Nobody in her actual life ever has.

Cassie. Four words a day and every one lands. "Goodbye, Vee" — not see you later, not text me, the whole flat finished word, and then "she let it stand. She didn't fix it." I trust Cassie completely and it frightens me, because Cassie plainly *saw* something cross the room and choose not to name it. That goodbye is the only honest word in the chapter, and Vee smooths it the way she smooths everything wrong. I want more Cassie. I'm also now braced for Cassie to be right about something terrible.

The swoon here is real but it's a swoon I can't enjoy cleanly — that's the book doing exactly what it wants to me. Vee taking the high table stool "in a single clean motion," the lavender latte ordered *for* her (and I noted: Pace ended last chapter with "Let's go feed you," and here is Randi feeding Vee, the same care, the same erotics of being handed something good to consume), the hug with the hand flat between her shoulder blades. It's lovely. It's exactly the being-chosen Vee has starved for. And I know the transcript is blank because the words were never the point — "she had been keeping the feeling, which was the feeling of being a person who got to sit at this table." Randi does this on purpose. I've watched her do it to Pace's questions. The chill is that it's the *same instrument*, now aimed at someone with no defenses at all.

The erotic charge is a low hum rather than heat — Vee's "flat helpless want" with nowhere to put itself, the unexamined gladness, the body-awareness she polices in real time. It peaked for me in that one buried beat of looking at the underdressed girls too long. Nothing is consummated and nothing should be; the wanting is all subterranean, which given this book's habits feels like a fuse, not a reprieve.

The titles have started talking. "See You Later" is Randi's exit line, which Vee hears as a queen's promise — "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see" — and the chapter sets it directly against Cassie's "Goodbye," so the title now reads as both vow and threat. And the volume title finally showed me its object: the whole room of spoons ringing against cups, "everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom of their drinks." *With a long spoon* — reaching sweetness at the bottom of something you haven't sounded. And "A Polite Invitation" — that's exactly what Randi's warmth is, an invitation whose host hasn't disclosed the terms. I also caught "latent variables" underlined in Vee's notebook, going nowhere: the factors you can't measure directly, inferred from the ones you can. That's the whole book's method, and it's sitting in her stats lecture.

What I want next: Friday. I want to know what the "later" contains and I dread it — my guess from here is that Randi will introduce Vee to Pace's frame slowly, the way the bench was introduced: a table first. I also want Cassie's four words to keep coming.
