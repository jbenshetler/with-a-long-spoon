## Reader reaction

Oh, this one *melted* me. After the hush of the MIRH chapter, the book swings back to Pace's house and gives Vee — and me — a chapter that is almost pure warmth, and I let myself be swept. The doorway kiss "as though five nights were nothing," her body's "running tally her mouth would never once have read out" — I felt the ache of that counting right alongside her. The undressing-as-slow-defeat down the hallway is genuinely funny and genuinely hot: she gets two buttons, he gets all the way down her back with one hand; the bra held like a spool so she has to unwind herself out of it; the thong thrown, "green on the low table." It's erotic writing at its most alive — playful, unhurried, utterly consensual, her wanting bigger than her dignity and the book loving her for it. Heat peak: the sound that comes out of her "too loud and far too soon," her body "publish[ing] the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private." Gorgeous.

And then the pizza scene nearly undid me in a different register. "He'd have made the pizza exactly like this for an empty house" — the care as "only the shape his attention took when it came down on a thing" — and the strawberry/cotton-candy tasting moment, where her palate itself seems to have been educated by this relationship. This is the book's hunger motif at its most tender: she can finally taste everything, separately, and count it.

The shirt. God, the shirt. "It stopped smelling like you" — her confession that she slept in his flannel every night away until it went to nothing, washed it, and drove out here to ask him to refill it with himself. His face "com[ing] open," pulling it on over the shirt he's already wearing, "You'll have it back Sunday." I teared up with her. This is the most intimate thing in the book so far, and no one is naked.

Trust vs. suspicion: on the page, nothing earned suspicion this chapter — Pace is grace itself ("Take the time you need, I'll be here"), and even the flicker where he almost thinks she's returning the shirt is instantly repaired. My unease is entirely inherited, not fresh: the "whole weekend" promise lands against the standing Thursday knowledge, and I notice he never fought for the five nights — she *wanted* him to want them out loud, and "he never said so." The reader in me who holds the ledger hears the silence differently than she does. But the swoon is real and I won't manufacture a shadow over it.

The title, "The New Ordinary": a drawer of her in his bathroom, feeding her afterward as routine, a scent-relay shirt as standing arrangement — domesticity become the baseline. It's also quietly the book's title logic: this extraordinary arrangement wearing the costume of the ordinary. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* still hum underneath for me as the price-tag on all this sweetness, but this chapter asked me to just be happy, and I was.

What I want next: Sunday, the shirt returned "smelling like me." What I dread: the weekend overlapping a Thursday; Randi, who just absorbed her own blow, somewhere off-page in the same man's life.
