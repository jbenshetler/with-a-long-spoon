## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt in a way no other chapter has — quietly, surgically, in a laundry room. The porch scene itself is gorgeous: the cool porch, the quilt that "smelled like him," the second coffee already positioned, the moon clearing the mountains. I was completely swept in. Her sentence — *"I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal"* — took my breath. She built a cathedral around the words she can't say, and the text tells us exactly that: "a structure she could push into the air that would carry what the words meant without being the words."

And his answer is where the crack runs. *"I love how good we are together."* It has "I love" in it! That's the cruelty — it's the closest he's ever come to the word, and it's aimed at the *performance*, not at her. "What you do — what your body does — I've never." My stomach dropped exactly where hers did, the small *wait* behind her breastbone. Her sentence was about being fed; his was about how good the meal tastes. And the text shows him feel the landing and redirect — the two aborted hand-responses, the two breaths she'd never heard before — before he produces the assembly. That detail chills me: he *registered* the mismatch and chose his answer. Chose, or couldn't do otherwise — the page leaves it ajar, and Vee's own "Maybe he understood and chose to say it like that. Maybe it had gone past him entirely" holds both.

And then the tenderness after — the held kiss on her crown, cheek against hair — is real warmth, and the book lets it be real warmth, which is what makes the whole thing so devastating. She is cherished in every register except the one she just asked for. The laundry room aftermath is the loneliest scene in the book: her sitting on the folding table running the two sentences side by side, "the two things had not been the same shape," and either end of that thought hurting. And the pile — *"The place was not empty. There were other things in it already"* — that's the most ominous line in the chapter, the first time the book admits her storage system has a backlog. "Small repair work to keep functioning." That phrase is doing enormous work: she repairs *her knowledge of him* so it can keep working. That's a confession of a pattern.

The title, "Seconds," reads triple to me now: the second cup of coffee waiting for her, the second-sentence exchange (hers, then his), and — darkest — second helpings, the meal metaphor turned on itself. Against "A Polite Invitation" and the long spoon: she extended the spoon, and what came back was *almost* the food she offered. My wariness, inherited and quiet for chapters, is louder now — but the page earned it this time, in his two breaths. What I want next: I want someone — Cassie, Randi, anyone — to ask her the question she can't ask him. And I dread that she never will: "the one person who could resolve it was the one person she could not ask."
