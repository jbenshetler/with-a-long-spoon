## Reader reaction

This one aches. The whole chapter is one small domestic scene — a cool porch, a quilt, a moon — and inside it, the thing I've been waiting for: she finally says something *aloud* to him that is built entirely around "I love you," and he answers it with a sentence that is *almost* right and quietly, precisely wrong. "I had been eating crackers my whole life, and you fed me a meal" — it's about him, about being known and met as a person. His answer: "I love how good *we* are together... what your body does." We. Body. Performance. Not her. And the page lets me feel it before she does — "a small *wait*" behind her breastbone, "the kind her body produced before her mind had caught up." That's the book's deepest trick and it lands here perfectly: her body registers the miss instantly, her mind takes two days and a laundry room to name it as *I do not know what just happened.*

How I feel about Pace has genuinely moved. Not into coldness — his stillness before answering, the "two different responses with his hand" he began and put down, the second breath she'd never heard before — the page is careful to show that something *registered* in him. This wasn't obliviousness; it felt like a man who heard the shape of what she offered and chose the safer answer, or whose vocabulary for love genuinely only runs through doing and bodies. Either reading is on the page and I hold both. But it's the first time his warmth has felt like a *limit* rather than a shelter. She notices too — "received warmly, she would remind herself, in the small private way you remind yourself of evidence whose weight you are not sure of." That line broke me a little.

The swoon is real but it's the sad kind: the pre-dawn porch, the coffee already poured at the right temperature, the kiss held on her hair longer than usual. The chapter seduces and withholds in the same motion, which is exactly what he does. Heat: none, and it doesn't need any — this is intimacy's aftermath, not appetite.

Trust vs. suspicion: I now suspect not malice but *grammar* — that "the rule was that he said it first" may be a rule only she is following, and that his not-saying may be permanent, structural, part of the same precision as the vow. The pile she doesn't look at — "small flickers, moments across the fall when what she knew about him had had to do small repair work to keep functioning" — that's the chapter's quietest bomb. There was already a pile.

Titles: "Seconds" works three ways and all of them hurt — seconds at the meal (she asked for more than the first serving of love and was refused), the seconds of his stillness before he answered, and the sense that she got a secondhand version of the sentence back. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, reads newly ironic: she extended the most polite possible invitation to say it back, and he declined — politely. The series title *With a Long Spoon* keeps its old warning hum underneath. (I have no tagline in my memory to react to.)

What I want next: I dread and want the pile to get tall enough that she looks at it. And I notice she filed this where she files things — the same place the Randi-kiss lives, the same place everything unspeakable goes. That place is getting crowded.
