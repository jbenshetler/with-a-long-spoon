## Reader reaction

This one hurt. Quietly, like the chapter itself — no scene of conflict, just a quilt and a moon and the wrong-shaped answer.

Her sentence is perfect. "I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal. I didn't know what a meal was until you fed me one. And now I know." I believe her completely. And I understood immediately what it was — *I love you* in a costume built to survive the rules. What got me first, bodily, was the rule itself: "she did not know the rule was a rule until she had bumped against it from the inside." That's the creepiest sentence in the chapter, and the tenderness around it doesn't soften it. A rule with no stated author, discovered as gravity, "by now, part of her shape." Given what I know from the jacket — that the terms of this whole arrangement were set by two people who told her nothing — a rule she can't locate the source of makes my skin tighten.

His answer. He said the word — *love* — and aimed it at "how good we are together." Then made it worse or better, I genuinely can't tell: "What you do — what your body does — I've never." She offered him her whole restructured life; he answered in the register of her body's performance. And yet — the stillness "a beat longer," the hand that "had begun two different responses with his hand and put both down," and the two breaths, "She had not heard him do that before." That's the chapter's whole mystery in one unprecedented breath. Something happened in him. I do not believe it went past him. Whether what happened was *I can't say it* or *I won't* or *that's not what this is*, I can't know — and now neither can she, ever, because "the one person who could resolve it was the one person she could not ask." That closed loop is the saddest architecture in the book so far.

My feeling about Pace moved this chapter — the first time the page itself, not the jacket, gave me the gap. He's still the man whose coffee is positioned and cooled for her before she wakes, whose quilt was his own bedding first. His love is legible everywhere except in words. But she asked for words once, once, and he had two breaths' worth of something and spent it on "how good we are together." My suspicion is earned now, by the text: he heard more than he answered.

The "place where she put things she did not know what to do with" — "not empty... small flickers, moments across the fall" — nearly undid me. She's been keeping a ledger too, in her body, without admitting she keeps it. And the last line is a small death: "The cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech." No seconds. That's the title landing: the second cup of coffee, the second breath she'd never heard, the second thing that's entirely hers (after the whispered "Peter") — and no second helping, no second saying, no sentence said back. Maybe also: she is the second woman, getting seconds.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and right — the charge is tenderness, which makes the miss land harder. Friction: almost none. The repetitive cadence flirts with mannerism but earns it; the chapter trusts me to feel the miss without underlining it past "He had not said the sentence back."

The book's title and tagline sharpened for me here. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — her *not-saying* was also freely given, self-enforced, and that's the trap's inner chamber. *A Polite Invitation* is now also about her own politeness to him. And *With a Long Spoon* clicks against the feeding motif in a way I don't love, in the best way — you sup with something dangerous using a long spoon; she was fed the meal, and she's just found the distance built into the spoon.

What I dread next: the next extraction. If Vee ever tells Randi this scene, Randi will hear her own brunch-language — "finally hungry out loud," "He woke you up" — come back as Vee's love declaration *to Pace*, unanswered. I don't know if Vee will keep this one. I hope she keeps it. (Guess from here: she keeps it, and keeping it changes what the brunches are.)
