## Reader reaction

This chapter is a slow bleed, and I felt every drop of it. No heat at all — and yet it's as interior and as naked as last night's bench scene, just turned inside out. Randi coming home in last night's clothes, walking into a common room ambush that isn't an ambush at all — that's the horror of it. Brooke's interrogation is *friendly*. "Brooke's voice was always warm. She had a voice the way other people had a perfume." Nothing in that room was hostile, and it still reads like a cross-examination under a heat lamp, because I know what Randi is carrying and the sisters don't.

What gutted me is how the chapter keeps proving last night's bathroom resolve in real time. Every "fraction-of-a-second" pause before the smile arranges. The pitch-matching with Kinsey she didn't choose — "She had not had time to choose the pitch." The face doing its job flawlessly while the person behind it can't figure out why she opened her closet. The room reads like the true cost of Pace's warm house: here, the duvet "was a duvet. It was light and synthetic and adequate" — and the sentence that breaks off, "It was not — it was a duvet," is her refusing to think *it was not his*. The cold corners of the lovely old house against the house he keeps warm. She's made her choice and the choice is freezing.

And then the chapter turns the knife I didn't see coming: *"He's hard to say no to," she had said, in the kitchen* — the line she fed the girls, the version of the story where she has no agency and no desire, the face's version. Hearing it back "in a different key… not a key she could sing in" — that's the closest she comes to the truth this whole chapter, and "she did not let the thought continue." What is that key? My gut says it's the key where the sentence means something closer to *he's hard to say no to* as in *I didn't want to say no* — as in last night, the begging, the wanting that was hers. But the page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar.

The ending undid me: "the not-going did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." After a whole book-so-far of locks and deadbolts and doors, the scariest sentence is the one where the door is gone — the held breath that stops feeling held. She lies there in her shoes under the adequate duvet and does not cry, and I wanted to shake her and hold her at once.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** I now actively distrust Brooke — not as a villain, but as a woman whose warmth is an instrument. The not-standing, the "engineering, isn't it" with its built-in notch down, the math-recalibration "absorbing it without conceding the notch," the *thank god* exhale about his sections — every line is a small status operation wearing a smile. And Sloane's "I love that you don't care about cars" is a compliment that's also a measurement. The whole house is a measuring device; Randi sits on its board (Standards — the title!) and is also its finest product. My suspicion isn't that anyone means her harm; it's that this world would eat the real her, and Randi knows it, and that's *why* there's no door.

**The title:** "Standards" is doing triple work and I love it — the Standards board she sits on (her single room, her authority), the sorority's standards (the scale, the vetting of boyfriends: his truck, his height, his field, whether he's near her math section), and her own standards — the face's standards — the discipline of never letting anything reach her. The chapter is all three at once.

**Friction:** almost none. The middle section — her standing at the closet, running water she doesn't use — risked monotony but the accumulation *was* the point; the paralysis earned its length. The only thing I noted was that the chapter gives Vee nothing again; two chapters in and the jacket's central girl hasn't appeared. I'm not impatient exactly, but I'm watching for her.

**What I want next:** I want to know what the two phone notifications are — she looks at the phone and doesn't read them, and I caught that. My guess from here: one is Pace. I dread his warmth arriving as a text she can't answer. And I keep holding the jacket's promise — Randi is meant to steer Vee toward Pace — and wondering how a woman sealing herself off this hard does that.
