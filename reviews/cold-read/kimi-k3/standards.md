## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt. Not the way the bench chapter hurt — that was ecstasy curdling — this is the flat, cold morning after, and I felt it in my shoulders the whole time. I was braced from the first line: "Randi did not close doors quietly." Two sentences in and I already know she's diminished, because her signature — "the small unhurried click of a woman who knew the room was hers" — is gone.

The four-minute interrogation by Brooke is the most tense scene in the book so far, and not one person raises their voice. Brooke is terrifying in the politest possible register. I believed her instantly: the voice like perfume, the not-standing as a power move, "I kept thinking you'd said *Chase*" — she's been holding his name, waiting. "Like she was filing it." That word did something to my stomach. And the probe about whether he teaches her section — that's not curiosity, that's the actual rulebook being quietly consulted. But the text keeps it exquisitely ambiguous: Kinsey and Sloane are right there doing the normal version ("he's *cute*, right? Like — short-cute?"), so Brooke reads as only one degree too filed. I can't tell yet if it's governance, suspicion, or even care in her idiom. "Sorry to grill you. Go." might be genuine. What tells me how Randi experiences it is the line that knocked me flat: "The release was the cleanest hit." Addiction language for being excused from a conversation. That's the whole house in five words.

And the detail that chilled me most: her laugh matching Kinsey's pitch — "The matching was very fast. She had not had time to choose the pitch." The face is now automated below the level of choice, and earlier it was "a fraction late." The machinery is degrading and accelerating at once.

Then the room sequence, which is the loneliest thing I've read in this book. Walks to the vanity, doesn't sit. Walks to the bed, doesn't sit. Opens the closet, can't remember why. Runs the water and doesn't put her hands in it. Nobody tells me she's depressed; the blocked actions do all of it. And the duvet — "It was not — it was a duvet." She can't even let herself finish the comparison to his weight, his warmth. The geography of the book has inverted on me: her world — lovely, old house — is the cold one now ("the corners were always cold"), and his is the warm one. And she eats nothing. The girl raised on portioning comes home from being fed and doesn't eat.

The turn of the knife: "He's hard to say no to" — her own cover-story laugh-line to the girls — coming back "in a different key," and "It was not a key she could sing in." Because it's true. He is hard to say no to, she said yes with her whole self last night, and the joke has become a fact she can't carry. And the ending — "the way a held breath stops feeling held. The breath was held." The text insisting on the held breath when she can't feel holding it. That's the tell that she is not okay even by her own system's accounting.

The two unread notifications — phone facedown on the vanity — I read them as Pace, probably glowing, probably tender, and she's lying under a duvet with her shoes on not reading them. That's the new engine of dread for me.

Complication to my sympathy, a good one: "she sat on the board herself, Standards." She's not just the machine's product; she's an officer of it. She polices other girls with this same instrument. The book isn't letting her be a pure victim and I respect it.

**Title:** "Standards" lands triple — the board, the measuring (everything here is rated: "rated a single," "a notch down," the spreadsheet), and her own slipping scale of what she's allowed to want. White pickup, math PhD, "short-cute" — her body measured him one way last night; this house measures another. It doesn't give anything away; it deepens on reread. And *A Polite Invitation* — this was the politeness chapter: a smiling four-minute invitation to confess, politely declined. The tagline cuts both ways now: the yeses she gave Pace were free; the yeses she gives Brooke ("Yeah. It's his name." "He's cute. Yeah.") are extracted. *With a Long Spoon* — I keep hearing it under Brooke's breakfast table, Kinsey's spoon literally held at her mouth.

**Friction:** honestly none. Maybe the litany of blocked actions risks one beat too many, but each is different — the untouched running water is the scariest — so no.

**What I want/dread next:** I want Pace's Saturday morning — what those two texts say, whether he feels the wrongness through the phone. I dread Brooke checking whether "the place on Wilson" exists. And a new paranoia I can't shake: *any* girl in that house could be Vivienne. Pure guess from here, marked as such — but the jacket said Randi picked her out across a quad, and now I've seen how this world watches girls.
