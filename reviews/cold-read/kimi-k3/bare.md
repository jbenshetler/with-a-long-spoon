## Reader reaction

This is the loneliest chapter in the book, and it got me. Nobody in it but her, awake, and a man who exists only as a smell in a shirt — and it's the most devastating portrait of what this relationship actually is that we've been given.

**How I feel about them.** Vee: my heart is cracked open for her. This is the most honest hour she's ever had on the page. Every layer of self-management is gone — no surrogate sentence, no forgiveness-rug, no *he's all mine* — just a girl in a top bunk discovering that the comfort she's been breathing off her own shoulder is, literally, her own heat. "It was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back." That sentence did something to me. It's the whole book in one line of physics. And then the choice: she takes the shirt off, the cold is "so much worse, and she did not put it back on." She would rather be cold and true than warm and lied-to-by-laundry. That's the bravest thing she's done, and nobody will ever know she did it.

Pace: he never appears, and his absence is the scene. I want to be fair — I've seen his costs, the putting-away, the two breaths, the fork-squaring — so when she imagines him "gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him," I know that's her picture, not a fact. But the narration itself has told me he sleeps easy, always, while she has now lain awake grinding and then not slept at all. The asymmetry is becoming the loudest thing in the book.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Nothing new to suspect him *of* — no cruelty, no lie caught. What I feel instead is a slow dread about his silence: "She had everything he did and not the one thing he wouldn't say, and there was no name in her for it, not the big one and not the small one." The not-saying has gone from mystery to deprivation. The tagline is fully legible now: every yes was hers to give, she gave them all, and the one thing she needs is the one thing no yes can extract.

**Erotic charge.** Zero, deliberately — and that's a gut-punch, because her nakedness has always been *witnessed* before, charged, wanted, "she did not cover one inch of it." Here she's bare with no one to see, and the body is just cold. The inversion of all that earlier nakedness is the saddest thing in the chapter.

**Friction.** None, honestly. It's short, three beats — the unsent text, the shirt on, the shirt off — and it earns every line. "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them" — I've felt that exact thumb hovering over that exact field. If anything I notice the book has now given me two interior chapters in a row; this one is distilled enough to feel like the floor of the descent rather than repetition, but the floor better crack soon.

**The titles.** *Bare* is perfect and multi-pronged: she ends bare in the cold; the comforts are stripped bare; and her nakedness — the book's recurring erotic offering — is here made bare of any witness, bare of purpose. It's also an antonym to all the dressing chapters — the sewn dress, the two costumes. No garment left works. As for *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, and "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — this chapter is the trap's mechanism made visible: the invitation stays polite, the answers stay small and warm, and the spoon was never long enough to reach the word.

**What I want/expect/dread next.** She has now burned through every comfort: the photo, the drawer, the retired sentence, the rug of forgiveness, the shirt. There is nothing left to self-soothe with. So something has to break — either she says the word to his face (dread and longing in equal measure), or she pulls away to see if he chases, echoing the bet chapter, or some outside thing — Sheri, Randi, the photos — detonates it for her. All guesses from here. I turned the page immediately.
