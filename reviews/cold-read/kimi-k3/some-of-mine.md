# Cold read (grounded) — some-of-mine

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This one is barely a page long, and it hit me like a held breath. The whole scene is two girls still in their coats — Vee can't even come into the room; Cassie came in too recently to have taken hers off — and the question just standing there "like a third person nobody had offered a chair." After the storm of the mixer and the Boyfriend confession, the book slows down to almost nothing, and the nothing is awful.

Cassie asked it last chapter — "What was that with you and Randi?" — and here Vee tries to answer and can't. She reaches for "the sorority thing" and the words literally crater: "She ran out. She never ran out." I caught my breath at that. Vee is the most verbal girl in this book — she narrates her whole life to Randi across white cloths — and in front of Cassie she has nothing, because Cassie is the one person she can't perform for.

And then Cassie asks the cleanest question in the entire book: "Is this what you want?" Not what happened. Not what is she to you. And Vee — I need to sit with how the page renders this — actually goes inside and looks: "There was a shape there, close and large, and she came up against the edge of it and stepped back off before she could make out its size." That's the whole machinery of this girl in one image. The want is there; she will not look at its size. And the irony is crushing me, because I know what Vee doesn't — that Randi spent months engineering that shape, that the "friendship" was a pointing game, that the courtship itself was steered. Cassie is the only person in this book who asks Vee what she wants with nothing riding on the answer. Pace asks "Will you?" inside a courtship that was built for her. Randi builds the wants and then harvests them. Cassie just asks. And Vee can't hold the answer even from her.

So she lashes out — "You don't like her... you don't get to stand there and look at me like that" — and it's unfair and she knows it's unfair, and Cassie does the most merciful thing in the chapter: nothing. "Cassie stepped over it without once looking down." She doesn't fight, doesn't take the years-old bait, just keeps her own face and waits. Then: "You didn't have dinner." And the Cocoa Pebbles. I think I love Cassie more after this than after all her dry one-liners. She sees Vee can't answer the big thing, so she hands her a small thing: "not the other question... only this one, small enough to hold in two hands." A want the size of a bowl. And Vee takes the box and that's all either of them can do tonight.

What undoes me is the food inversion. Pace has been feeding Vee blood oranges peeled down to "the jewels" and persimmons salted like Japan; Randi pours bottles of red without looking at the price. And the love that asks nothing back arrives in a box of kid cereal from a bunk bed. Dr. Marsh's lecture from Tuesday is still ringing in my head — the hau, the gift that drags its giver's claim along with it — and Cassie's Cocoa Pebbles may be the first free gift Vee has accepted in this whole book. Nobody's collecting. It just says: eat, you're home, I see you.

The image that'll stay with me is the neck as evidence — Vee standing there convinced Cassie can "read the whole night off her skin like a postmark." This girl is a walking trail of evidence: the dark bloom on the coat lining, the soaked thong, the Live Neck. Her body keeps mailing the truth and she keeps trying to intercept the letters. There's no heat in the scene itself, but the whole chapter is about a burn — Randi's mouth-print that won't finish fading.

Title "Some of Mine": it's Cassie's cereal, obviously, and it's luminous — sharing as love, the thing Cassie offers of what is actually hers to give. But it also points at Randi and Pace's whole economy of "yours": Randi's skirt, Randi's shoes, Pace's sweats, Pace's cashmere — everyone dressing Vee in *some of mine*, everyone staking a claim through hand-me-downs. Cassie's version is fourteen cents of cereal and no claim at all. The volume title "A Warm Reception" keeps promising welcome-as-trap (reception = the party where Randi received her; reception = how her people receive Pace). And the series title "With a Long Spoon" — I keep reading it as the warning it sounds like: if you're supping with something dangerous, keep distance. This chapter is Vee discovering which table is safe — and it's the cardboard one.

What I dread: Saturday brunch with Randi, which Vee invited precisely because running scared was worse. Randi got denied a kiss and defaulted to a neck-kiss with an audience; she is not going to accept a closed door. What I want: Cassie's question to keep working on Vee like a seed — and some day, someone Vee can answer it to.
