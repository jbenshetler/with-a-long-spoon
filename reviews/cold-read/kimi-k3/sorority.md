## Reader reaction

God, this chapter landed on me like warm water and a cold stone at once. The shoe-shopping farce — the canoe shoe, the rhinestone platforms, the limp flat flapped like a fan — is the happiest stretch of pages in the book so far. Two girls being idiots over shoes, an older couple stopping to watch, and I believed every beat of the friendship in it. "She had never once in her life had a friend like this" — I felt Vee feel that, and I didn't want to take it from her.

And then the boutique, and everything in me went quiet in a different way. The thumb across the frosted-plum toenail — the polish Vee chose *for Pace*, to catch light like the silk — and Randi's "Mm," and the room tipping. Vee's attraction to Randi has been simmering all book, but this is the most physically charged moment between women so far, and it worked on me completely — "she felt it go all the way up the length of her, felt the floor of her tip." Randi going to one knee, fitting the shoe, doing the buckle: it's a proposal tableau and the book knows it. "Same feet, baby... it's practically a marriage." I swooned and I braced simultaneously, because "same feet, same everything" also means Randi can now wear the induction shoes herself, and I noticed myself noticing that.

The credit card is the wound of the chapter. The debit between her fingers, "the last careful thing in her give way," signing for money that isn't there while Randi rings up a multiple of it without glancing down — that's the class gap made flesh, and it aches precisely because Randi isn't cruel about it. Randi is generous, dazzling, *right* about the shoes ("It wants to walk in late and stop the room cold" — she said Vee's secret daydream out loud, and Vee heard it as being known). But I know the design underneath, so "I know just the place" reads to me as another pointing: the sensible shoe argued out of her hand, the budget blown, the daydream fed. Is it earned suspicion? Yes — I know things Vee doesn't. But on the page, taken straight, this is also just a rich friend being magnificent and a poor friend going under quietly. The book holds both open and so do I.

"He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." That line undid me a little — Randi naming the exact thing Vee cried over wordlessly. Her gift for naming remains her most loving and most dangerous quality.

Friction: almost none. Maybe Randi's perfection strains slightly — the flawless verdict ("Charmeuse... a good weight"), the knee, the card — but the rhinestone-platform goofing earns her humanity back.

Titles: "Sorority" now reads doubled — the literal induction ahead, and this chapter's truer sisterhood: same size, shared shoes, "practically a marriage," two girls laughing on a sidewalk. The book's title *A Polite Invitation* and the spoon keep pointing at courtesy as capture — Randi never forces anything; she invites, and Vee follows her out of every store. Next: the induction, the late entrance, the room turning — and I dread what it costs, in money and otherwise.
