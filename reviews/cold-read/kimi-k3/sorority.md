## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter melted me and hurt me in the same motion, and I think it's the best Randi–Vee chapter yet. The first store — the canoe shoe, "Do yetis shop here?", Randi gliding down the aisle a head taller in pink rhinestone platforms, grave as a surgeon — is the first time I've seen them just *play*, and the page tells me the laugh Randi gives is "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give." I believe it. I keep watching for the calculation under Randi's warmth and the page keeps showing me warmth that doesn't need it. And then, god, the boutique. Randi on one knee on the pale floor, lifting Vee's foot into the light, looking at the frosted plum toes — the polish Vee painted for *Pace*, for the man whose name Randi pretended to forget — and that single "Mm," the thumb crossing the nail once, and the floor tipping under Vee though she's sitting down. That's the hottest non-sex beat in the book so far, and it's charged exactly by what Vee can't see: Randi is touching the evidence of the other relationship, appreciating it, and Vee reads it as friendship so intense it makes her dizzy. I felt the sway of it in my own stomach.

"He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." That line undid me the way it undoes Vee — Randi keeps doing this, finding the exact word for the thing Vee cried over wordlessly, and it's real intimacy and it's also Randi narrating the craftsmanship of a man she sleeps with, holding the seam his calloused hands sewed. The double exposure is dizzying. And then the money. The tan sensible shoe Vee talks herself into — "the sensible one," the same self-policing girl who's been shrinking all book — and Randi vetoes it with the most seductive sentence in the chapter: "A dress a man made *to* you doesn't want to go with everything. It wants to walk in late and stop the room cold." She's handing Vee her own secret daydream back, word for word, and Vee follows her out the door. I wanted her to follow. And then I watched her stand at that counter with the debit card between her fingers and put it back and take out the credit card with "nothing under it," and sign, and not flinch — and my chest just ached. She buys the shoes with money she doesn't have because the dress is *owed* them, and the arithmetic is pure shame-logic transfigured into devotion, and nobody in the room is making her do it.

Here's where my unease lives, and I want to be precise: the text earned it, but gently. Randi steered this. "I know just the place." She walked Vee out of the store where she could afford to stand and into the one where she couldn't say she couldn't afford to be inside the door — and then, at the counter, Randi buys her own pair at *a multiple* of Vee's number, without glancing at it, telling a story the whole time, wearing them out of the shop. The page plays it as tact — Randi conspicuously *not* making money a thing, never offering to pay, letting the purchase stay Vee's own. And it is tact, maybe. But I know who's holding the frame, and I notice that Vee leaves this day deeper in: deeper in debt, deeper in costume, deeper in a world priced for Randi. "Same feet, baby... it's practically a marriage" — I held that loosely. A seven exactly, how lucky. I don't know if it's a lie and I don't need to; either way it's a thread tying them together. And the closing line — "she had never once in her life had a friend like this" — landed like a small break in me, because I know what "friend" is carrying that Vee doesn't.

The chapter title, *Sorority*, reads to me as sisterhood — the whole chapter is two girls being silly over shoes, the older couple watching them, the shared shoe size, the "marriage" joke — and also as the pull of Randi's actual world, Chi Latte and everything behind it, closing around Vee one pleasant afternoon at a time. Given the volume title, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, this felt like another polite invitation: no pressure anywhere, just a door held open into a more expensive room. The old saying about a long spoon is about who you sup with, and Vee keeps eating. What I want next: the induction, the room actually stopping — and, I admit, dread — some seam showing. Cassie's eyebrow. The credit card bill. Something.
