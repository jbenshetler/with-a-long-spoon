## Reader reaction

A whole chapter with no Vee and no Randi in the room, and it still warmed me. That's the surprise. I've been living inside Vee's head for four chapters straight — the fight, the sleepless night, Randi's closet — and now the book pulls back and shows me Pace from the side, through the eyes of somebody who has two years of Saturdays on him. And what she sees is a man who cannot stop talking about the redhead.

"Listen to you" might be the tenderest line in the book so far. He doesn't even know he's doing it — the walnut chair, her thumb on the joint, the math book she didn't put back "like it had burned her" — and Sheri has to tell him he's lit up. After watching him be so controlled, so composed, so deliberate with Vee and with Randi, seeing him *unknowingly* smitten lands different. It doesn't absolve anything — the frame is still the frame — but it confirms what I've been hoping against my own wariness: his regard for Vee is not performance. You don't perform for your riding partner on a cold climb. There's no one to fool out there.

Sheri herself arrived fully built in six hundred words. Faster on the climbs and never lets it go unmentioned; remarks on women like weather; can turn her heat on a person "inside a breath and keep it burning for years" and what that heat is worth on the far side is *everything*. I trust her instantly the way I trust Cassie — another blunt one whose loyalty is the whole of her. And her two lines do the chapter's quiet work: "You want me to not like her?" and then, "Two of them at once. If that's not already complicated, it will be." There's the first outside witness to the arrangement — and notably, she does *not* seem to know it's an arrangement. She knew Randi ("the pale sorority chick"), thought maybe that was finished, and reads Vee as a second girlfriend. Even Pace's oldest friend sees only the surface. His "That's not finished" about Randi sits there flat and unrevealing, and I can't tell what he means by it — ongoing love, ongoing project, or both.

The detail that got me: Sheri watched Vee after the fight, unasked as far as I can tell. "All she did was sit by herself, glaring at the floor, nursing the one drink. Danced with nobody. Left alone." It's the bartender's account confirmed from his side — Vee's miserable night, witnessed and reported back with a compliment attached ("Hell of a backside on her, though"). Nobody sent anyone after her; Pace left her the room exactly as Randi predicted he would. And the timing lands hard: this is presumably Saturday, meaning while Vee is being dressed in Randi's shoes and scarf and told to arrive "sure" tomorrow night, Pace is out on the road glowing about her to Sheri. Both parties are being readied for the same door.

Suspicion: none of Sheri — she's the cleanest presence in the book after Cassie. My unease stays structural: Pace keeps every relationship in its own sealed lane (Sheri doesn't know the game; Vee doesn't know Randi; Randi doesn't know what Sheri is to him), and a man this compartmentalized is either disciplined or practiced at something.

The title, "hills-and-valleys": the rolling road that governs the talk — conversation on the flats, silence on the climbs where the breath goes. It reads as the rhythm of this friendship, and maybe of the whole book right now: we just came off the drop of the Rusty Nail fight, and this is a flat, easy stretch before whatever climb Saturday night is. Nothing gave anything away; it meant itself.

I want Saturday night badly now. Vee in the black dress, the cognac heels, the scarf she learned to tie — walking into the house of a man who's been talking about her thumb on his chair joinery all morning. *Guess from here:* the reconciliation will be good, maybe too good, and Randi will have engineered every inch of it.
