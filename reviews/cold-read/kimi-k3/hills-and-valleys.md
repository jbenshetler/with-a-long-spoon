## Reader reaction

The first chapter lived entirely inside Pace's head, with neither Vee nor Randi in the room — and it turns out that's exactly what I needed. After chapters of knowing him through Vee's dazzle and through the afterglow scene where the game was spoken aloud, here he is on a bike with a friend, cold in his fingers, and the jacket's promise — "as real for the two who started the game" — stops being marketing and starts being fact. He gives Sheri "Vivienne" and then he's "past the name before he'd decided to be," telling her about Vee's thumb running down the grain of the walnut chair, the joint he spent a weekend on, and the math book she pulled off his shelf and *didn't* put back like it had burned her. (I stumbled on that sentence's construction once, then it landed: the math didn't burn her. She reached for his language and held it.) "He heard himself going and didn't stop." Sheri's "Listen to you" — and he genuinely doesn't know what there is to listen to. That's a man in love who hasn't heard himself yet. My suspicion of Pace doesn't vanish — the game exists, and he doesn't mention it to Sheri, and that silence is its own tell — but it changes temperature. What rises in its place is a worse dread: if he's really falling, the blast radius gets bigger.

Sheri arrives fully built in one ride. Faster on the climbs and never once lets it go unmentioned; remarks on women like weather; offers, in a single breath, to dislike Vee on his behalf forever — "he'd seen her do it, and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything." I liked her instantly and I believed her instantly. His answer — "No. She couldn't take your heat" — protects Vee and flatters Sheri in the same motion, and Sheri laughs, pleased, and lets it go. This is the first person in the book who belongs wholly to *his* world, and the chapter lets me watch him look at a woman's body — the small engine of her on the pedals, the ponytail — without wanting her, which calibrates by contrast what wanting looks like for him: it looks like the chair story.

Trust vs. suspicion: Randi is absent and somehow colder for it. "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" / "Randi. That's not finished." *Not finished* — not "we're good," not "she's great" — a thing with an ending pending, phrased almost like a project. And he lets "two of them at once" stand uncorrected. So in his own head, to his oldest on-page friend, the game goes unmentioned and the shape of it goes uncorrected. I notice. I also notice Sheri was at the Rusty Nail Thursday and "kept an eye on her" — and I catch myself checking her bleached ponytail against "the blonde" who wounded Vee. The text gives me no wink; Sheri asks Vee's name like a stranger. I won't convict her. But the question is on my shelf now.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and the chapter doesn't want any. "Hell of a backside on her, though" is weather-report appreciation, characterization of Sheri, and Pace's silent agreement — "the same thing he'd have said, if he were the kind to say it" — tells me he's not the saying kind. The heat in this chapter is all romantic and all directed at an absent woman.

The title, "Hills and Valleys": it's the literal road — talk on the flats and descents, silence on the climbs, "which took the breath for themselves" — and it's the book's terrain. Thursday was a valley; tonight, if my calendar's right (the ride is Saturday; Randi set the reconciliation for "tomorrow night"), is the climb. And Sheri out-climbs him and waits at the top, soft-pedaling — friendship as the person who beats you up the hill and waits. The book's title and tagline hum underneath differently now: the trap's jaws are *both* falling. Every yes was freely given — his too, apparently.

What I want next: tonight. Vee walking into that warm house dressed head to toe in Randi, while the man inside spent his morning narrating her thumb on his chair joint to a friend. Whether Randi told him she's coming — unwitnessed, and it matters enormously. Whether he recognizes the shoes, the silk. And more Sheri — a person whose heat is "worth everything" on the far side has been installed like a device.
