## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

After two chapters inside Vee's wreckage and repair, landing in Pace's shop felt like being handed the other half of a secret — and the chapter used it immediately. Him truing tenons with shavings "thin enough to read through" climbing his arms, nothing in his head but the fit — I recognized the lift-mind from the quilt night, and it hurt, because I knew what he was out there not thinking about. "All day the leaving had been the hard part." He gave her the space she asked for and it cost him, and he never once reached for the phone. I like him. I keep liking him, and the book keeps making that cost me more.

Then Randi arrives — unannounced, on a Friday, *glowing* — and I clocked it the same instant he did, except where he "did not ask it anything," I asked everything. She's never come through that door pleased and let it show. And the chapter waits a long time to tell me what the glow is made of: Vee, at nine that morning, in her bra on Randi's rug, letting herself be looked at. The wine he'd been keeping for her anyway, the "I'm glad it was you" — that line landed on me like a blow, because I know exactly what Vee walked into when she walked into that white-columned house, and he doesn't, and his gladness is completely sincere.

The moment that stopped me cold was her hand flat on the table with the folded clothes, "and it was not the look you give a table." She's remembering the bench. And then his version of the same memory — "a voice he had not heard from her before or since." *He has noticed.* Dimly, without a name for it, he knows something was given once and never again. That quiet "before or since" ached more than anything in the chapter.

And then the sex, which is where the chapter gets its teeth into me. It's genuinely hot — the stop-hand in his hair, "Not like that. Not yet. Lie down," her riding him slow while she unwraps the morning for him piece by piece — and it is also the wrongest the book has ever made me feel while aroused. Because what she's feeding him is Vee. Vee's black lace bra, Vee's bare legs under the skirt, Vee's involuntary sound — "She made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it. I heard it." That is the single most intimate thing Vee owns, a thing she tried to keep even from herself, and it's being spent as bedroom spice between the two people she trusts most. Pace *stopped* when she said "there's more" — I watched him stop — and then he let it continue, and drove up into it. The narration tells me it was Randi's telling that fired him, not the picture; I don't fully believe the distinction, and I don't think the chapter does either. She's consummating her morning with Vee through his body, and some part of him knows and takes it anyway. Also: she never says *tell me*. She doesn't ask anymore. She tells. The whole power current of their bed has reversed since the bench, and he's fucking her like nothing changed.

The ending undid me quietly. "Give her a day" twice, the second one half-asleep, "as if she were promising him something" — and "She's going to be so beautiful when she walks in. You wait." Randi has dressed Vee for tomorrow night — her shoes, her scarf, tied snug, tension — and is now lying in Pace's bed savoring the unwrapping in advance. The blue lacquer on her toes, "the only color on the bed" — the same sky-blue toes Vee sat across from that morning. And his closing beat, "he would not text her... It was only what he was" — which is exactly, word for word, what Randi promised Vee he would do. She knows him like a mechanism she's already wound. She told Vee what Pace is, and she was right, and that rightness is the scariest thing in the chapter.

**Titles.** "Space" — the space Vee asked for, the space Pace gives as his nature, the space Randi *administers* to both of them from the middle. It's a quiet title and it's about control. The cover board reads WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION; no tagline was supplied. "A Warm Reception" keeps doubling: the warm house, the warm welcome Vee keeps receiving from both of them, the induction to come — and "with a long spoon" carries that old proverb about supping with the devil, which after this chapter feels less like a joke about coffee spoons and more like the distance Vee doesn't know she needs.

Next: Saturday night. Vee in the black dress and Randi's shoes and Randi's scarf, walking into Pace's house to make it right — a reconciliation choreographed by the woman who just used her as an aphrodisiac. I want her to fix it; I dread how well it will go, and that the going well is the trap closing.
