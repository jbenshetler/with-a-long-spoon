## Reader reaction

This chapter is one warm room and two sock-footed girls, and it undid me more gently and more completely than anything yet. Vee comes in "still warm from the evening, from the walk, from him," and the book lets the cold line off the window find her and fail — "she didn't mind it." The confession scene is perfectly pitched: her voice dropping "without her deciding" into the kiss part, the words "coming smaller and one at a time," and then the thing itself — "He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me." That *Me* is the whole book's promise kept. And then the spin. A clumsy, unglorious, full-body turn that nearly takes out the closet door, ending in the bean bag, "*Look at me*," and Cassie's "I'm looking" — the two most romantic lines of the chapter are between the roommates. I am wholly swept.

Cassie is my heart and my spine at once now. Glad "all the way in," and then the flat dry floor: "How much did he give you to drink?" She asks exactly what I, carrying everything I know, need asked — and gets the true answer and the loaded one at once. Vee's "I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose" — I believe her entirely, and I also know the conditions her purpose flowered in were built. The chapter holds both without flinching, and so do I.

But notice what Cassie keeps returning to, and what the chapter names itself for. "What's his name, so I know what to tell the state troopers." It's a joke; everyone treats it as a joke; and the only address in the dossier is "off six-fourteen" — repeated until its thinness shows. "See. I know where he lives and everything," Vee says, counting off a name, a road number, and a promise of water, "like plenty." She doesn't know if he's rich, hasn't seen the house, and — the new, lovely, frightening thing — the not-knowing *delights* her: it goes into "the pile of things she'd get to find out, which was the best pile there was." The hiking invitation moves her off her streets (which he revealed) onto his ground, past where the road goes to gravel, with one instruction given — "real shoes" — and I cannot unread that as the design laying rail, even as it is also obviously just a man planning a hike and telling her what footwear to bring. My unease is real but proportionate: nothing on this page is menacing; the menace, if any, lives entirely in the gap between what she has (a glow) and what Cassie could hand the troopers (a road number). The book put its title on the gap.

Heat: none to speak of, and right — this is afterglow, romance high, kiss retold in summary. No friction; the chapter is short and earns every line. **Titles:** "Off Six-Fourteen" is the vaguest homing signal imaginable — the chapter could have been named for the kiss or the spin, and instead it's named for the one thing she can't answer. That recolors the whole glow on second thought, deliberately. *A Polite Invitation* keeps extending itself — dinner, now a hike at his place, each invitation warmer and farther out; and *With a Long Spoon* suddenly feels literal: the spoon is the distance from here to his doorstep, and she's about to eat farther from help than she's ever been. (No cover tagline survives in my retained memory, so I'll hold that loose.) Next: the hike. I want it and dread it in exactly the book's proportions, and I'm waiting — guessing from here — for Randi to re-enter, and for what she'll know about any of this.
