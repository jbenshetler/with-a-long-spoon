## Reader reaction

I came in braced for the kiss's aftermath and got a girl spinning in a too-small room, and it undid me — because the joy is real, the page earns every bit of it, and I'm the only one in the room who can hear what it rhymes with.

Cassie first, because this is her chapter and maybe her best one. The gladness with "a flat dry floor under it" — and then the question, the one that made me exhale: *"How much did he give you to drink?"* Somebody finally ran a check on the evening. And the evening passed clean: tea, no wine, nothing. That's the elegant horror of it. Cassie's instrument is *good*, and it found nothing, because at the level Cassie can reach there is nothing to find. The design doesn't need wine. "This time the okay had her believing it" — my most trusted instrument has now cleared him, and she cleared him honestly, on true data, and it means nothing.

And then Vee says it. The tagline, out loud, in her own defense, at the happiest moment of her life: *"Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose."* Every yes was freely given. She's testifying to the trap's perfection as proof there's no trap — the cover copy walked onto the page wearing her voice and played as triumph. Then the spin, the near-miss of the closet door, "Look at me." / "I'm looking." The body that stayed up on the deck, the body that was a problem, airborne and witnessed gladly. I loved her so much there it physically hurt.

The cold center of the chapter is the address beat. "See. I know where he lives and everything." "Off six-fourteen." "*That's the whole address.*" She knows a road. She doesn't know if he's rich — it never occurred to her to wonder — and the not-knowing goes into "the pile of things she'd get to find out, which was the best pile there was." The flaw-hunter is gone. In her place is a girl for whom ignorance has become a treat, and he taught her that, and Cassie repeating the road number twice is the only person in the scene who hears how thin "plenty" is.

Pace never appears and advances everything: the hike invitation (his ground, his trail, "past where it goes to gravel"), the one instruction (real shoes — she owns none). Froyo shop, hidden-block restaurant, and now the map's edge, the jacket's long drive about to become visible. And Randi — absent and unmentioned a third straight chapter, and now the absence is structural: the debrief, the best friend's due, the thing her whole role was built on, went to Cassie in full, with the spin and the whole face. Every chapter without her makes her re-entry worse.

Heat is banked on purpose — the kiss retold in the dropped voice ("He was already walking away and I pulled him back and I did it. Me.") carries more charge than most explicit scenes. Friction: none I can honestly report. If the thesis-line chimes too perfectly with the tagline, Cassie's question earns it, and the chime is the point. The state-trooper joke is pure Cassie and also quietly lodges his name and a road with an outside party for the first time — I don't fear violence (the jacket promises seduction, not thriller), but the book just built a tripwire and I noticed.

"Off Six-Fourteen" meant nothing picking the chapter up and everything by the end — it's the whole of what she knows about where she's going next, offered as knowing, and it points straight at the house. *A Polite Invitation* — the hike is the newest one, each yes a door deeper into his terrain. *With a Long Spoon* keeps its proverb-shape; the supper moves ever closer to his kitchen.

What I want next: the hike, the house, whether his study finally shows (does he mention swimming?), whose shoes she borrows — if it's Randi's, that's the re-entry mechanism (a guess from here). What I dread: Randi hearing about the exception in Vee's new voice, and Cassie's audit running forever on material that can't show her anything.
