## Reader reaction

This one hurt. It's the quietest chapter in the book so far and it landed harder on me than the bench, harder than the first night with Vee — because I finally got to stand inside Pace's head, and his head is a place where every instrument is precise and every reading is true and the sum of them still won't add up to her. He notices *everything*: the spoon drawn along the top of the sauce and never down into the bowl, the bread untouched, the glass she pours herself, the missing *tell me*, the turned back. He sets "each piece beside the next and they would not go together into anything he could name." And I know the name. I watched her make the resolution in his bathroom in chapter one. The cruelty of this chapter is that it's a man conducting a careful post-mortem on a body that is still sitting across from him eating salad.

The meal wrecked me. He cooked all afternoon — pot on since three, bread from the machine, dressing in a jar — and he ladled hers into the small bowl, "the one he sliced her apples into," which reached straight back to chapter one and the apple cut sides up, and then she drew one line through the surface and didn't eat it. The whole book runs on feeding as loving; Vee lets herself be fed and it transfigures her; Randi takes the spoon and skims and sets it down, and eats only salad, "down to the leaves at the bottom that had taken the most of the dressing" — taking the richest part while refusing the meal. That's her whole self right there. And then the line that actually gutted me: "He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house." She pours her own. One gesture, and the entire liturgy of their care is quietly revoked, and he feels it "go in somewhere" and can't find where.

The fight — if you can call something this flat a fight — is the moral center of the book standing up and arguing with itself. "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything." And he means it, and it's his best self speaking, and I *also* know she was chosen at a pointing game and that every "choice" has been staged across a table she's never seen. "He did not take it back, because it was true, and because he did not know how" — that *how* is devastating; he's convicted by his own creed and can't locate the crime. And Randi calling *him* selfish — "You've got her all weekend, every weekend, and you're in no hurry, and I can't think why you would be" — is that the co-author impatient for the payoff, or a woman starving? She told Vee at brunch she was "running on fumes," living off Vee's accounts of him. Then she watched him be glad about Friday — "Randi watched him be glad" — and something in that watching went cold. I read her as jealous and can't quite say of whom, and I think the page wants it exactly that open.

The sex is the hottest and the coldest the book has been at once. She takes him in one motion, eyes shut, nails in his chest, comes loud — her hunger is fully real and fully withheld. No inch-and-stop, no eyes, no *tell me*, the thing she'd said every night in that house. She's taking the body and keeping the self, which is precisely the ch 1 vow — he gets the face from now on, or less than the face. I felt the physical charge and I felt it being used as a wall, and both feelings were the point. My trust in Pace's tenderness went *up* — the chapter ends with him covering her bowl with a plate and putting it in the refrigerator, for her, still feeding someone who won't eat — and my alarm about the structure went up with it. These two are fraying, and Vee is the rope.

Title: "Portion" is perfect and multiplying — her small bowl, her measured serving, the portion of herself she's now willing to give, the portion of Vee's week each of them gets, who gets how much and on whose schedule. And with the series title *WITH A LONG SPOON* in my ears, I couldn't miss it: she takes the spoon and never goes down into the bowl — she's supping at a distance now, keeping the meal, and him, at spoon's length. *A Polite Invitation* keeps promising the reveal Vee doesn't know is coming; this chapter tells me the inviters may not hold together long enough to extend it. What I dread next isn't the reveal anymore — it's what a starving Randi does to feed herself. (Guess from here: she accelerates things, or takes something from Vee that isn't Pace's to give.)
