# Cold read (grounded) — Portion

## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This one hurt, and it hurt in the quiet way — not because anything exploded but because I could see everything and Pace could see almost none of it. We're in his head now, and his head is a good place: exact, attentive, tender. He sets out her small bowl, *the one he sliced her apples into*. He pours her glass when he hears the gravel. He hears "Friday" and is "glad all the way down" — no jealousy, no flinch, just glad that Vee is happy. And he's decent even in the frame: "He did not ask what Vee had said. That was Vee's." I keep liking this man and I keep not being able to excuse him, and this chapter sharpened both edges at once. His creed — "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything" — is sincerely held and is also, structurally, a lie, because the choosing happens inside a room she doesn't know has walls. He says it like scripture and I believe *he* believes it, and that makes it worse, not better.

Randi wrecked me. The whole chapter is her hunger wearing clothes. "It's wonderful" — a word she gives waiters and girls on the phone, never him — and he hears the wrongness and can't place it. She draws one line through the sauce with her spoon and eats only the salad, down to the leaves at the bottom, and that image is doing everything her mouth won't: she has lived her whole life on portions this size, egg whites and tofu, and here is a man who keeps cooking her more than she lets herself take. And the real starving isn't the stew. She got the *story* of Vee's Friday — "I'm running on fumes here," she said at brunch — while he gets Vee herself, Friday to Sunday, every weekend, unhurried. "How long are you going to take?" isn't really a question about logistics. "Or you're not" is. Something in her is accusing him of keeping the meal to himself, and I don't think even she could say which meal she means.

The sex is the coldest hot scene in the book so far. It works on me precisely because it's wrong: the one motion, the eyes shut, the nails, and the absence that the chapter builds into a presence — "Every night in this house she had said it... and he lay under her with his hands on her hips and waited for it and it did not come." I didn't know *tell me* was their ritual until its absence told me, and that absence is the loudest thing in the chapter. She takes him the way she poured her own glass — he has poured every glass she ever drank in that house, and he watches her hand do it and "it went in somewhere and stayed, and he could not have said where." She's repossessing things. Ch 1's resolution is on the page without ever being named: she gives him her body at full speed and nothing of herself at all. "Whatever this was, it was not for him. He knew that much and not the rest, and he gave it to her anyway." That line broke me a little — his generosity and his blindness in one breath.

And the ending undid me: he stands in the kitchen looking at the skin forming over her untouched bowl, searches the whole evening "from the gravel forward" for what he did wrong, and can't find it — because it isn't in that evening, it's in the bench night, in the locked bathroom, in everything he was never shown. Then he covers the bowl with a plate and puts it in the refrigerator. *For her.* He's still feeding someone who has stopped eating at his table. I wanted to shake him and hold him simultaneously. His tenderness is real; his frame is fiction; both, always, at once.

No friction for me as a reader — this is lean, controlled, every object loaded. If anything, I marvel at how much the chapter trusts me to already know (the kisses, the lunches, the vow) without ever winking. The title *Portion* lands on at least three levels: her small bowl, her share of Vee's story versus his share of Vee, the portion she serves herself in that bed. The volume title, **With a Long Spoon — Book One: A Polite Invitation**, reads darker every chapter now: supping with the devil requires a long spoon, and Pace thinks he and Randi are at the same table while she's already measuring the distance. No tagline was supplied beyond the cover board.

What I want next: I dread and crave the moment Vee's orbit touches this fissure. And a guess from here — Randi's hunger isn't going to stay channeled through stories much longer; she's going to want her own portion of Vee, directly, and "only in a threesome, with you" is going to curdle into something she never offered. I also want to know whether Pace ever asks her what *tell me* meant — or whether he'll keep covering bowls for a woman who's already left the table.
