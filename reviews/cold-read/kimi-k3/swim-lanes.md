## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction — "Underneath"**

This chapter flipped the lights on in a room I'd only ever seen from the doorway, and what it showed me was hot and faintly awful at the same time. This is the first time I've been inside Pace's head — and the first thing I learn is that he notices everything and files it: the empty chair by the door, the question she never let him answer, the skipped aftermath, the deadbolt turned back. "He set that beside the empty chair and left them both there." He is a man who keeps ledgers, and he is keeping one on the woman he loves. That's tender and it's surveillance, and the book won't tell me which, and I've stopped asking it to.

The sex itself worked on me completely, which is almost embarrassing to admit given what the sex *is*. The inch-by-inch descent with her eyes on his face, "until he understood that she meant him to watch" — that's the hottest staging in the book so far for me, and then it turns out to be the coldest thing in the book so far, because what she means him to watch is her using him as a listening device. "Tell me." And he knows exactly what she's asking for, "had known since the quad," and pours Vee into her ear — the sweet hair, the faint chlorine, "her stomach is soft" — and Randi's body answers each detail before her face can. The debrief-as-foreplay from ch7 has stopped being a game they play and become the *only* game: she didn't ask how the hike was. She asked once, into his mouth, and took the answer back off his tongue before he could give it. She never asked again. The trail, the summit, the rain, the girl — none of it exists for Randi except as kindling. I felt the heat and I felt the use, simultaneously, in the same paragraphs, and I think that's exactly the temperature the page wanted.

Randi frightens me a little now, and I say that as someone who ached for her hardest two chapters ago. "I want us" torn out of her at his ear — and I notice she didn't say who *us* is. Her and Pace? Her and Vee? All three? She came on the sentence. And then the chapter does the thing that genuinely chilled me: she skips the loose, slack aftermath that "she kept for herself," shivers, dresses in front of him like a woman leaving a hotel, refuses food in that bright voice — "No thanks," from inside the camisole, the trimmed appetite intact even here, even now — kisses him *thoroughly*, and goes. She has never once left after. The deadbolt, which she has always thrown to seal herself *in* with him, she now turns back to let herself *out*. The lock motif just reversed direction and I felt it in my stomach. She's not withdrawing the way she did after the bench — she's doing something brisker and scarier: taking what she needs and leaving nothing of herself behind. The want for Vee has outgrown the gift-frame, and her solution is to consume it through him and walk out fed.

And Pace — Pace deepened for me in a way I didn't expect. He withholds the truck. "He did not say how he knew. That she had given him herself, in the truck with the rain coming down, and he kept it." That's the second time he's kept a piece of Vee from Randi — "It was hers" — and now I see it's a principle with him, not an impulse: some of Vee is *Vee's*, even inside a scheme built on Vee knowing nothing. I don't know whether to find that decent or obscene. Both. He is fully complicit — he fed his lover another woman's softness as dirty talk — and he is also the only person in this book guarding anything of Vee's interiority. And then the ending gutted me quietly: the man who has spent ten chapters feeding everyone — apples, cherries, sandwiches, "Let's go feed you," wine he only drinks from her mouth — standing at his own refrigerator, eating alone at the table, "still hungry after and made more." His hunger is the one nobody answers. That's the loneliest last beat in the book so far.

Blondie instead of Steely Dan — "that cool voice riding the beat" — even the music has gone cooler tonight. And the title, *Underneath*: it's everywhere. The cold thing under Randi's citrus perfume. The want under her bright "No thanks." The real conversation under the fake one ("How was it?" meaning *tell me about her body*). What Pace keeps underneath what he tells. And the volume title — **With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation** — keeps accruing: spoons, feeding, and I know the proverb it bends toward, the one about who you need a long spoon to sup with. "A Polite Invitation" is the whole courtly machinery — *May I choose, May I call* — and after this chapter it reads less like courtesy and more like the most elegant snare ever set. Nobody has invited Vee to anything yet, politely or otherwise. That's coming, and I dread it and I want it.

What I want next, honestly, is the collision — Vee in that warm house, walking past the bench without knowing, Randi in the same room as the girl she can no longer safely want. My guess from here: the "polite invitation" gets issued soon, and Randi will be the one who delivers it smiling.
