## Reader reaction

This one is quiet and small and it bruised me a little — not because anything bad happens, but because for the first time I watched Vee *manufacture* rather than omit, and I watched Cassie let her.

Up to now, my memory of Vee-and-Cassie was omissions, ladled versions, the "Goodbye, Vee" flatness from way back. Here, Vee comes home boiled pink and glowing, sits on the bean bag, and tells the weekend — and the weekend she tells has a shrunken cranberry sweater in it, a dye accident, a doll-sized ruin, a whole invented laundry catastrophe to explain why Sunday found her in his t-shirt. The truth — that she drove out there in a coat and heels and nothing else, that there was "nothing important" in the bag — can't be said, so she builds a fable, and it's *fluent*. Cassie turns the full chair around, folds up her feet, gives her the entire front of her attention, and then: "Pace. Shrank your sweater." "To a doll size." A silence "about the length of a held breath." Then Cassie laughs — the rare full one — and the text tells me, in so many words, that "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody."

Corrected by nobody. That line sat in me like a stone. The chapter is telling me a correction was available and was withheld. Cassie clocked it — the held-breath pause, the highlighter "turning end over end... uncapped nothing" — and chose, for now, to laugh with her instead of press. And somehow that almost makes it worse, because Cassie is the person I trust most in this book, and Vee is now managing her too — extending the same choreography Randi ran on Pace's porch into her oldest friendship. I felt tender toward Vee the whole time — her happiness is real, "sun she'd been out in," happy even under the dorm shower's "polite trickle" — but I ached. The leaking-away the checkpoint warned me about is now on the page as fabrication, and it has a flavor of dread: when Cassie finally does correct something, it's going to matter.

And the mother beat — Cassie's, in the same dry register the snowshoes got: "My mother says thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again." Vee's answer comes out small — "Spring break. Sometime. Tell her I said hi" — and the narration marks it as "not the size of it at all," while Cassie "could hear the size of things" and closes the subject "the exact moment before it would have needed anything from either of them." So Vee sent Cassie's mother a card over break, and Cassie's mother wants her, and Vee can't take in the warmth of a mother who wants her — she rations it exactly the way she rations the truth. Given what her own mother's voice does in her head, that little folded-away moment hit me almost as hard as the lie. Two rationings in one scene: the over-explained sweater and the under-answered invitation. The chapter is called "unpacking" and both girls unpack selectively — Cassie's duffel sits half-emptied, and Vee unpacks the permissible parts of the weekend while the real thing stays packed. Even the title is wry about it.

Cassie herself deepened here — she's comic on her own material now (the brother-in-law's snowshoes "scratched the floors from the door to the kitchen," held "like she'd towed his car"; "The baby is fine. The baby is a baby"), and the two of them laughing with heads back is the warmest beat in the chapter, which makes the uncorrected record underneath it land even sadder. She's also the one with defended territory — the window open its two inches, "all winter, every winter" — while Vee's boundaries keep going porous. I trust Cassie completely, and I now trust *her perception* most of all. She just won't spend it yet.

Pace is mentioned-only, but there's one neat thing: Vee invents for him a flaw (ruining the laundry) to make the story plausible, because the real Pace never errs — except, I know from the last chapter, burning toast because she existed. She gifts him a fictional flaw as cover. And at the end, the flannel shirt goes over her nose — "him still, but going faint" — launching the recharging ritual again, and then the hand going out across the one-girl-wide bunk to find cold cinderblock, "and the wall was only a wall," because he's out there west of town and hers again. That hand-to-the-wall rhymes deliberately with the empty-bed hand at the start of "Missed a Spot," and it's the chapter's grace note: the dorm is rationed, the draft is "defended," and she's happy anyway because the weekend overflowed.

No heat here to speak of — a faint residual warmth in the shirt-smell and the afterglow — and romance is the friendship kind, real tenderness running alongside the first outright lie. On titles: the volume line I have is "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION," and there's no separate cover tagline in my packet. "A warm reception" keeps doing double duty — the literal heated house he keeps for her, the warm reception the dorm gives back, Cassie's mother extending one Vee can't accept. "With a long spoon" still sits oblique for me — reader association to the old proverb about supping carefully is available, warm as everything is. And "unpacking" — the duffel, the curated retelling, the folded-away mother warmth. The chapter title earns itself on second thought.

What I want next, marked as guesses from here: the meet-the-people dinner Pace volunteered to "handle" is now loaded — Cassie, Meg, Kayla, Theo, and Randi all potentially at one table, with Randi and Pace's "nine words at the coffee shop" due to become a performance in front of Vee. Tuesday's anthro-class dinner hangs in the near future too, where Vee plans to tell Randi "nearly everything," including that strange small aftertaste question from Sunday morning that she couldn't name. And the Cassie lie is now on the ledger — I don't think "corrected by nobody" is a permanent condition. I dread the correction and want it at the same time.
