# Cold read (grounded) — Toenails

## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This one is small and almost nothing happens — a girl comes home, talks to her roommate, can't quite take her skirt off, stands alone in a cold room smiling — and it absolutely leveled me. Because I know exactly what the night was. I read *Famished*. I watched the green satin get folded in thirds, "the spot centered," and handed over with the word *Trade*. So when Vee draws the zipper halfway down and stops, and the page quietly says *Nothing under the skirt. She had given them away* — I felt it in my stomach. The comedy of it (Cassie three feet away drinking brown cereal milk, oblivious) and the intimacy of it (I am the only other person in the book who knows what's missing and why) landed at the same time. That's the whole chapter in one gesture: Vee has a secret now that isn't shame. She's *keeping* something, and the keeping feels like riches instead of the usual lock-up.

The mother's voice failing is the thing I want to hold onto. "Frosted-plum toenails her mother would have had a word for. The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." After ten-plus chapters of that voice arriving on schedule — *floozie*, the whole liturgy, running even through the sex itself last chapter — here it finally comes and bounces off. Not defeated, not examined, just... declined. Because the night was so good there's no room for the word to land. I believe it completely and it made me want to cry a little, and I also don't trust it for a second — not because the text failed me, but because I know what she doesn't.

And God, what she doesn't know is heavier now than it's ever been. She slept with him. She's calling him her boyfriend in her head, driving home bare, grinning at red lights — and he is Randi's lover, and Randi picked her off a sidewalk for both of them. The chapter doesn't say a word about Randi. Not one. And that silence is screaming at me, because the arrangement of this whole book has been Randi extracting reports — "Start at the beginning and don't you dare clean it up" — and there is now a report that cannot be given. Vee edited for Cassie, "lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole" — that image is gorgeous and it is also the exact motion she's been trained in by every Smoothie Bird debrief she didn't know was an extraction. Somewhere, Pace will tell Randi, or Randi will ask, and I am genuinely braced. I don't know if I'm braced for Randi's face or for Vee's.

Cassie's little moment at the top — "something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name" — I noticed it and I don't know what it was. She's the only clear eyes in this book and even she reads it wrong on purpose, kindly: "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend." *Wrong*, the page says flatly. He's not her boyfriend. He's somebody's something, and the somebody isn't her. Cassie choosing kindness over precision is a small new note for her — she's usually the one who says the flat true thing — and it made me ache for her too, standing guard with jokes over a door she doesn't know is open.

The title is perfect and it gave me nothing away — "Toenails" is Cassie's tell, the tiny evidence Vee left lying around in October when sensible girls have given up on bare feet: *I have eyes.* The whole chapter is about the evidence a body leaves of a night it won't narrate — the painted toes, the missing panties, the smile that "would not come down." Evidence Vee is both flaunting and hiding in the same breath, which is also exactly what the green satin was for in the first place. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* sits underneath all of this like a held breath — every invitation so far has been exquisitely polite and quietly engineered, and I keep waiting to find out which part of this one was.

Heat-wise: it's afterglow, not fire — the night runs under everything "like a second channel," but the chapter is deliberately chaste, a girl fully clothed and grinning. It works as cool-down after the peak of *Famished*, and I didn't want more than it gave.

What I want next: I want and dread the Randi scene in equal measure — the lunch, the extraction, the moment Vee either gives the report or finds she can't. And I want to know what Pace does with the folded green satin. The trade has to land somewhere.
