## Reader reaction

This was a small chapter and it broke my heart a little, gently. Vee comes home bare and grinning, "the smile would not come down," and the whole thing is one long afterglow — and the most telling thing in it is what she does with the story. She lades out the middle, "clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole" — and calls it a kindness, and believes it "the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." The book has always shown Vee's self-deception working on her; here she *watches herself do it* and signs off anyway. I ached for her and worried for her at the same time.

Cassie lands exactly as she always does — the one person with no design — and that makes her small misreads almost painful. "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend" — said "kindly, certainly, wrong." Wrong because Vee doesn't have a boyfriend, she has a married-to-the-design lover whose actual girlfriend is Randi; wrong because the "brain" isn't gone, it's the happiest it's ever been. And Cassie's opening beat — "for half a second something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name" — snagged me. She read Vee "fast and whole" and matched her grin, but something flickered first. I don't know what it was. Concern? The folded file stirring? I hold it open. And "did not pull at the seam. Whether she felt it there, Vee couldn't have said" — I think she felt it.

The zipper moment is the chapter's heart and it's perfect: she can tell Cassie about the man, but the skirt coming off with nothing under it "would not be a story she was editing" — the body again telling the truth the mouth won't. So she zips it back up and only undresses alone, in the emptied room, where "she had never in her life been so happy." The happiness is real. I refuse to contaminate it. But the last detail does the quiet work: the green is gone, traded, and I know whose palm it's in and whose lover that palm belongs to.

Title: "Toenails" — the frosted-plum polish, the frivolous premeditation she couldn't quite admit (she couldn't pack a bag, but she painted her toes; Cassie caught it instantly — "In October. I have eyes."). It's the tell she gave herself permission for. Lovely, oblique, earned. No erotic content on the page, but the night runs under everything "a second channel that had not shut off once" — simmering, not slack.
