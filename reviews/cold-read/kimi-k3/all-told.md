## Reader reaction

A small, golden chapter — and a quietly loaded one. On the surface it's the happiest triad scene in the book: Vee in the middle seat between her two girls, underslept and overfull, "happy in the middle." I felt that happiness with her. The "You lucky slut" beat is warm and vulgar and exactly right, and Randi snatching the shoe problem and turning it into a *date* — "That's not a problem, that's a *date*" — melted me even as I noted, with the part of me that's been watching, that Randi has now scheduled herself into the induction costume. Of course she has. The woman who runs the approach doesn't let the shoes go unchaperoned. But the text didn't push that at me; the warmth read as warmth, and I let it.

The thing that actually landed hardest was Cassie. "She had not looked at the professor once" — Cassie spending the whole lecture watching *what the dress had done to her friend*, because "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." That undid me a little. Cassie doesn't care about the dress, has worn the same fleece two winters, and her one question is "Was it a good night?" — and "something eased in Cassie's face" at the second, fuller *oh, yes*. That's the book's only undesigned care doing its quiet work, and Vee finally *noticed her noticing*. But the scene doesn't go sour; Vee receives it as love, and so did I. The made-bed tattletale and Cassie's eyebrow staying in reserve are as close as the seam comes to showing, and it stays affectionate.

And then the professor hands the chapter its title and its thesis in a cereal-box voice: "A thing can run one way across every short stretch you measure and the opposite way across the whole of it." Vee underlines *short stretches can lie against the whole* because it sounds like an exam question, and I sat there with my whole irony ledger lighting up. Every short stretch of this story — the collision, the froyo, the hike, the bench, the dress — has run one way, toward romance, toward being chosen. The whole, if anyone ever puts the two years together, runs the other way. The chapter is called "All Told" and ends with the dress "still shown to not one living soul" — nothing yet all told, not to Cassie (the ladled middle), not to Randi (the trade, the tissue square), not to Vee (everything). The title did give me the shiver on second thought: this is the chapter about how the total reverses the parts, delivered as the chapter where nothing totals.

"Slut landed lower than Randi meant it to" — Vee suddenly sure the whole room can read the soreness off her body — is the one cold fleck, and it's the old shame-voice in a new costume, the room-as-witness. It passes in a second. Erotic charge is low but present: the good soreness carried all morning is a lovely embodied echo of the night.

What I expect next: the Saturday shoe date with Randi — a full chapter alone with her, which historically means elicitation, naming, a seal. And the induction drawing nearer with the dress still unseen. My guess from here: Randi sees the dress before anyone, and the seeing is also a fitting of the design.
