## Reader reaction

This is the chapter after the gold, and it's built like a trap I'm not supposed to notice being built — except this time the book put the trap on the blackboard and made *me* the only one in the room who can't stop looking at it.

Cassie broke my heart this chapter, cleanly. I'd had her filed as the wary roommate with the crossed face, the surveillance habit. Now: "Cassie did not care about the dress… the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." Forty seconds of Vee being a student, and Cassie spent them watching what the dress had done to her friend. Her one question is "Was it a good night?" and when the second yes comes — "Oh, yes" — "something eased in Cassie's face." That's love, real love, and it's aimed exactly where the danger isn't. She reads the made bed hours early, cracks the whole case, and then *lets Randi have it anyway*. That small abdication told me more about the trio's power structure than three chapters of brunch.

Randi — the narration gave her warmth a mechanism this time: "turned the full warmth of her attention on, like a lamp finding you." A lamp. Something switched and aimed. Her "Tell me everything. Leave nothing out" is the brunch liturgy in a classroom whisper, and I heard it. Then the shoes: "That's not a problem, that's a *date*… Don't you dare tell me no." And Vee: "It went through her warm, and she didn't." There it is — a yes to Randi, running on the exact same rail as the yeses to Pace. My suspicion of Randi hasn't gained one new fact; it has gained *ownership*. Pace built the dress; Randi has now claimed the finishing of it. His dress, her shoes, Vee's body in the middle — the middle seat she's so happy in, dressed by committee.

And then the professor. "A thing can run one way across every short stretch you measure and the opposite way across the whole of it." Vee writes *short stretches can lie against the whole*, underlines it, "and gave it no further thought" — and I nearly put the book down. Every golden afternoon, every freely given yes, each short stretch running one way. All told: the other way. And "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them" — nothing about any single yes has to be touched for the sum to reverse. The title does the rest: **"All Told"** isn't a confession chapter (I'd half-expected one); it's the sum nobody in the room is performing except me. And the professor "smiled the smile of a man who had laid the same trap a hundred times" — the word *trap*, the tagline's own word, spoken by a man talking about baseball. Is it the author's thumb? A little — a stats lecture delivering the thesis while the heroine underlines it and misses it is conspicuously convenient. But it's played so deadpan ("the flat unbothered voice of a man reading the back of a cereal box") that I took it as a shiver, not a lecture. The chapter even performs its own paradox: every short stretch of it Randi wins — the sparkle, the case-cracking, the date — and by the end of the whole, Cassie is the one who saw. Same numbers.

Erotic charge: low-watt but real, and all aftermath — the soreness inventory, "down low, and up the inside of both thighs, and in her nipples where they sat against her bra," and her flash of certainty that the whole room could read it off her. "You lucky slut" landing "lower than Randi meant it to" is the most charged beat; the public-legibility frisson connects straight back to the induction daydream.

The book's title keeps accruing: an invitation so polite that "Don't you dare tell me no" arrives as affection and lands as a yes. *With a Long Spoon* still sits opaque — I keep waiting for the meal it's warning about.

What I want next: Saturday, badly — what Randi chooses, what it costs, whether the dress comes out of its bag for her. What I dread: the induction, where the daydream says the dress debuts in front of her mother's room — and, it now occurs to me, a roomful of sorority sisters that presumably includes Randi. Guesses from here: the shoe date becomes another extraction with better lighting; and someone — Randi, the mother, maybe both — is going to recognize that burgundy in a way Vee won't catch.
