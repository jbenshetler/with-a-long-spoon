## Reader reaction

This is a small, quiet chapter and it held me completely — maybe the most quietly devastating one in a while, and nobody even does anything in it. Three girls in a stats lecture. That's all. And I ached the whole way through.

The thing that got me is Cassie. "It was when she came back up out of the numbers that she understood Cassie had never gone down into them at all." And then: "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." I had to stop there. Because I know — and Vee doesn't, can't — that on her other side sits a woman who can see everything *but* the friend, who is reading the dress, the campaign, the case. "She left at eight," Randi told Cassie, "like she'd cracked a case" — and the narration hands that simile to *Randi*, the one literally gathering intelligence, while "Cassie had cracked it hours ago off the made bed, and let Randi have it anyway." Cassie doesn't need to crack anything. She just watches the person she loves and asks, "Was it a good night?" — and when the second "Oh, yes" comes out with more in it than the word usually carries, *something eased in Cassie's face*. That's the whole of her love: your happiness is the variable I'm tracking. I trust Cassie more than anyone in this book and this chapter made me love her outright.

And Vee is so happy — "happy in the middle," "turned between the two of them," the seat left open between her two girls — that the dramatic irony nearly crushed me. She thinks this is the one room where she gets both her people. I can see that she's sitting in the exact seam of the design, warm between the woman who loves her and the woman who *placed* her, and that neither side knows about the other. The book has been building toward that geometry since Euler's formula, and here it is rendered as three chairs, two-thirds back.

Simpson's paradox, though. Each year on its own, the first man is the better hitter; put the two years together and the second man is. "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them." I'll be honest — the book's lecture-as-gloss habit is starting to show its hand to me a little. Latent variables, error-correcting codes, and now this, dropped into the exact chapter where Vee sits between two partial views of her life. It's elegant, but I felt the elegance this time; the professor's "smile of a man who had laid the same trap a hundred times" is nearly the author winking. And yet — it works on me anyway, because the trap is real: Cassie's read of Vee is true, Randi's read of the situation is true, and *all told*, aggregated, the truth flips into something neither woman can see. The title lives there. "All Told" — what the total shows when you finally add the columns up. It also means what Vee is doing in that seat: telling, but not all. The dress goes home "still shown to not one living soul" — she keeps the window-and-sunlight moment back from both of them, keeps the middle of things, the way she's learned to. Even her happiness now has an editor.

The "lucky slut" moment is the one sour note deliberately struck and I registered it exactly as placed — it "landed lower than Randi meant it to," ringing against the body's own ledger of soreness, against *floozie* on the same wire. Randi doesn't know what word she just put in Vee's ear; or maybe some part of her does. I still can't settle Randi, and I notice I don't want to. Her delight reads genuine — "scandalized, loving every second of it" — and the shoe date is real warmth *and* another claim on Vee's Saturday, and I believe both without being able to weigh them. Meanwhile Pace is entirely offstage and I didn't miss him; the chapter is about what his world looks like from inside Vee's happiness.

Almost no heat here — just the low hum of aftermath, the body "keeping the night's account," which is its own tender kind of erotic echo. What I want next: the shoe date with Randi (which I half-dread — Randi alone with Vee, shopping for the outfit Pace built), and the induction, where the dress finally gets shown to a living soul, plural, with her mother in the room. And I keep watching Cassie. The eyebrow went up and came back down. Someday it's going to stay up.
