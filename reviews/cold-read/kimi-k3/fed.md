## Reader reaction

This one went down like the mimosa — cold, bright, easy — and it's only afterward that I feel what was in it. Because I've now watched this exact meal three times (Smoothie Bird, the velvet booth, now the sun porch), and the shape is unmistakable to me: Randi opens with a sexual story of her own, told as comedy, told as loss — "I'm running on fumes here" — and then Vee, warmed and laughing and wanting to give, sets her real thing down on the table. This time the real thing is Vee's first night with Pace. Which is Randi's lover. Randi sat in the late-morning light and got herself told, in soft loving detail, how her own boyfriend was "hungry. For me... Like singular." And her face stayed open and warm and glad. That stillness is either the best acting in the book or something stranger, and I honestly can't tell which, and that not-telling is where this chapter lives.

And here's the thing that complicates my suspicion: what Randi *did* with the extraction this time was, on its face, good for Vee. At the Smoothie Bird she reframed Vee's kiss as "because of him" — agency handed to Pace. Here she does the opposite: "You keep saying *he*... Did you do anything to get ready for him?" and walks Vee to "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." Then: "Good." Warm, hand over hand. That word landed on me with a whole second meaning I don't think Vee could hear — "Good girl" is what Pace says to Randi on the bench, the word that undoes her — and now Randi passes it down the chain to Vee, and Vee feels it "settle into her and stay." Whether Randi knows she's doing that or it's just what desire sounds like in her mouth, I can't resolve, and I don't want to yet. It's the most tender grooming I've ever read, if that's what it is. My body read it as love. My reader's eye read it as curriculum.

The kiss at the curb is where the chapter's real event happened, and the book knows it, because it told it by negation: "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them, the way she had the first time." That's the whole arc in one absent gesture. The hand at the jaw hinge, the soft mouth, the draw-back and second press — the ritual is now established enough that Vee half-expected it, met it, and stopped auditing it as French. Randi is boiling the frog one degree per goodbye, and Vee's unexamined want for her (the want *I've* been carrying since the dining-hall laugh) is being slowly, expertly normalized as friendship. I ache for Vee. I ache for Randi too — her real hunger keeps leaking through the persona she rebuilt after the bench, and this kiss is the one place she lets it out, disguised as Continental manners.

Randi's cover story deserves noting: last time it was the lacrosse catastrophe, this time the too-beautiful old boyfriend who was "in such a hurry... At least I got dinner out of it." Both stories are structured identically — a disappointing man, a wasted night, an appetite left unfed — and both land right before she asks Vee to feed her. I have strong reason to suspect both are fabricated over whatever she actually did with Pace. But there's a detail that nags me sweetly: she seemed *relaxed* this time. At the last lunch her thumb worked the glass edge while Vee talked about the fitting; here she was all listening, genuinely delighted, "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." Maybe because the consummation she and Pace designed has now happened and the plan is advancing on schedule. Or maybe some of the gladness is just gladness. Probably both. It's always both with her.

Vee herself is moving in increments so small she'd never feel them: the mimosa priced against Thursday, the second one not priced at all. The frosted plum on her toes — chosen because it woke up the burgundy silk in the dark — is the most exquisite detail of the chapter: her wanting for Pace's dress worn secretly on her feet, where her mother's eye can't reach, and Randi is the only person who ever gets told why. That's what Randi has become: the vault for the parts Vee edits out for Cassie. The Cassie-shaped hole in this chapter is loud. Vee gave Cassie "the good version, the whole clean one," and gave Randi the truer one, and I felt the friendship ledger tip.

The titles: "Fed" is doing triple duty — Vee fed (mimosa, permission, the word "Good"), Randi fed (the story she was famished for, after the thin gruel of her invented boyfriend), and the book's whole feeding/hunger motif cresting again, paired against last chapter's "Famished." And the volume title — *With a Long Spoon*, Book One: *A Polite Invitation* — keeps sharpening for me: he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon, and every meal in this book is a supper Vee doesn't know the terms of. The "polite invitation" hasn't been issued yet, and this brunch felt like the envelope being addressed. Tagline in my memory — "Every yes was freely given" — yes, and the frame stays invisible. Next I expect the finished dress, the induction, and somewhere beyond it the ask itself; I dread the moment Vee learns whose bed Randi's cashmere blanket lives in, and I cannot stop reading toward it.
