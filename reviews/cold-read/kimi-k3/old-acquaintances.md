This one hurt, and it rearranged the furniture of the book. I've been carrying Pace-and-Randi as the central pair, with Vee as the friend who supplied glosses and showed photographs — and this chapter puts Vee in Pace's kitchen, Pace's fight, Pace's silence. I spent the first pages recalibrating: the induction dress was built for *her*. The "She's alone" Sheri line was said to *her*, in November, "when she was the one he cooked for." And the porch answer I'd logged as tender — "I love how good we are together" — I now hear as the "warm and level and wrong" answer to her mother's question in her own mouth: *what am I to you.* The re-sort isn't confusing so much as sickening, in the good way: the same scenes, re-owned.

Vee has my whole heart right now. She's brutally honest with herself — envying Randi's Gstaad while knowing exactly what bought it ("even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had"), building the blonde "from spite" and believing her anyway, "what you believe at midnight." The line that undid me: the Christmas food "had never once been the thing Vee was hungry for, not then and not now; she just hadn't had the word for it then." And the crop — "she was the one who had divided it" — she scissored away her own leg, "the exact thing that had made her feel like herself." I trust her narration completely precisely because she convicts herself in it.

Pace moved — colder. He's absent the entire chapter and everywhere in it. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That single turn recolors every restraint I'd admired as possibly just leaving. No word across the whole break, no coming-after, nothing about Christmas — "a whole week she couldn't see into." Yet the chapter plays fair: the fra diavolo memory is genuinely warm, the wrongness is structural — no name, no standing, "nothing scheduled, nothing promised."

Randi is, on the page, sweet — the midnight reply, "miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋" — and "dad's got us" is a fiction Vee sees through and politely doesn't. My memory makes every warmth of Randi's land doubled, because Randi holds Vee's entire archive (the bet gloss, both crops) plus her own named history with him, and Vee is inventing a stranger to be jealous of.
