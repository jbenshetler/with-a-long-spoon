# Cold read (grounded) — Old Acquaintances

## Reader reaction

This one hollowed me out. After the heat and noise of the last run of chapters, the book puts Vee alone in a twin bed her legs outgrew in middle school, in a room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her," and lets the year run out on her. The opening image did it — the trophies, the gold-fringed ribbons, the clarinet in the closet, the friends who haven't texted in a year — a whole preserved Vivienne that the house keeps dusted while the actual woman in the bed has been breathing bigger air for months. "She'd been breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." That's the whole chapter in one line, and it ached.

The cruelty the book builds here is all dramatic irony, and it's almost unbearable. Vee lies there constructing Pace's hypothetical other woman out of spite — "blonde, tan, a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's" — handing him the exact opposite of herself, while I know the actual other woman is petite, dark-haired, and the very person Vee just texted. "Randi, the only girl she could talk to about real things." I had to put the book down at that line. And the memory that gets stolen from her — she reaches for the fra diavolo night to warm herself and the blonde takes her seat at the table mid-memory, "the sheet slipping off a body that had never in its life needed building for" — that's one of the truest renderings of jealousy I've read, the way it colonizes even your own archive. Her best night won't hold her.

And the Randi thread is worse, because I've read "Fairytale." Vee imagines Randi with "a devastatingly handsome billionaire" living "the whole lit spectacle of a life lived out loud," and envies it even knowing what bought it — and I know Randi was lying awake in that hotel room feeling nothing, cold everywhere she wasn't touching a stranger, composing the fairytale text *for* Vee while the one true line in it was "miss your face most though, gorgeous." Both chapters end the same way: a woman in the dark, turned phone, a word unsaid, a warmth borrowed or imagined that won't hold. Vee thinks she's the one in the cold room. She doesn't know Randi's was just as cold, at the good end of the good table. The mirroring is doing devastating work.

What moved most in me this chapter is Pace's silence, and how the book makes his one real virtue into the wound. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." I know why he doesn't come after her — Daphne's door, the lesson he paid for, he does not knock — and Vee reads it as indifference, as maybe-it's-over, and there's no way for her to know it's the opposite. "No answer and no coming-after, and nothing since, not a word across the whole break." My dread isn't suspicion of him, exactly — it's the machinery of two people each honoring a rule the other can't see. That, plus the ticking exposure: Vee's whole comfort is "the one sure thing the new year held" — Randi, a class, a face that calls her gorgeous — "and Pace came with none of that." She's holding both of them and setting neither down, and she is the only person in the book who doesn't know they're the same hand.

Small frictions: the opening lines slip into second person — "and you burrowed under the blankets against it. *Her* sheets were pulled up to her chin" — and it recurs later ("He left you the room"). Later it's clearly Randi's quoted voice bleeding in, which is lovely, but that first "you" snagged me; I reread twice to check who was being addressed. And the beige-days paragraph (casserole, Wheel of Fortune, the mother's question at fresh angles) runs long — though honestly the flatness may be the point, the chapter *is* the beige. The title: "Old Acquaintances" — New Year's, Auld Lang Syne, but the old acquaintance being forgotten is Vivienne herself, the girl in the ribbons. And the cover-board promise, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps recoloring: this is what the invitation costs so far — a girl too big for her old world, not yet named in her new one, texting the architect of both.

Next I want the term to start as badly as Vee does. I dread — guessing from here — that Pace will keep leaving her the room until she knocks, and that she'll walk back into Randi's warmth first, which is the wrong door in the right dress.
