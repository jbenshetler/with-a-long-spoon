## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

The chapter opened soft and slow — swim, shower, closet — and then the sentence that quietly set the stakes for everything after: dressing for Randi, Vee "stood in front of her clothes with the same keyed-up gladness she saved for getting dressed for Pace." I stopped there. That's the page almost saying it, and Vee walking right past it: getting dressed for Randi has entered the same category as getting dressed for her boyfriend. And the sweater she picks hugs her body on purpose — "a good stretch to it, which was the point." She's arming herself for a dinner she keeps telling herself is nothing. From the first page I was tender with her and uneasy for her, and it never once resolved.

Dr. Marsh's lecture did the thing these lectures now do: it set the key. Last time it was gift-*hau*; this time it's the lice comb and grooming — one-fifth of a baboon's waking life, "watch who grooms whom in a troop for one week and you can draw the whole politics of it." And then Vee shares a Lifesaver with Randi in the dark — "one lecture, one flavor" — a feeding ritual so small neither of them votes on it. As a reader I heard Marsh naming the mechanism of this entire friendship while Vee misses it completely, week after week. It reads like texture; it's also the book's whole thesis under the plot, and the page has earned me noticing it rather than manufactured it.

The dinner itself is the heart, and it is hot in a way I want to be precise about: there is no sex on this page, and I was flushed the whole way through anyway, because the entire middle hour is Vee retelling explicit sex — the blowjob in the truck, swallowing, being fingered on the county road, being eaten out — to a woman I know is in love with her, across a white cloth, while both of them pretend it's just the debrief. The charge lives in that exact double exposure. When Vee tries to fold it small — "you can fill in the—" — Randi cuts in with the line that names her method to my face: *"Don't you fade to firelight and leave me wondering. You just made it smaller. Give me the real one — the harder one."* And Vee gives it. And gets flushed, wet-under-the-cloth, present-tense, while Randi sits "very still, arms folded, all her lights aimed." I know, from what I already hold, that this is harvesting — Randi extracting the explicit version to keep the account full — and I simultaneously felt the intimacy of it as real, because it is real too. That's the whole sour sweetness of reading this book.

The "mushrooms / did you swallow all of it and wipe your mouth" joke was the one pure laugh, and it's also the food-and-sex motif going explicit and self-aware: *"Are they?"* / *"They are ADJACENT!"* I laughed, and then caught myself noting that Randi has just told Vee her appetites are one appetite.

But the hinge — the thing the whole evening has been banked toward — is the kiss-tale. Vee has been polishing this punchline since Tuesday: the kiss with Pace's mouth wet with her, her "Ugh — gross," her "That's — no. Wrong." She's built it as a closed comic bit, thirty seconds, and she lands it across the table: *"You think it's gross too, don't you."* She says it as a statement, because she's filing the case shut. And Randi, "considering the question exactly as long as it deserved, which turned out to be longer than Vee had budgeted," answers: *"I don't."*

That flat, level, two-word answer is the most quietly consequential thing Randi has ever done at one of these tables — because Vee checks with her body immediately (she "clenched hard to it, right there in the booth"), and because when Vee asks *"Did you like it?"* Randi answers: *"It was powerful. It turned me on."* I hold Randi's interiority from earlier chapters — Vee is "gorgeous," the most important hour of her week — so when Randi says "It turned me on" with the register of a restaurant recommendation, I read it as a smuggled confession aimed at Vee, dressed as neutral data. Vee takes it as arbitration on the taste question. The dramatic irony is doing exactly what the whole courtship has been doing: a true thing delivered in a form that misses visible.

And then Randi's single question back — her "only question": *"How does he act?"* — confirming the mechanism, not the propriety. Vee answers without vetting: *"He likes it. Actually — he's enthusiastic."* And Randi, "warmly, the sound of a woman finding everything in order, and ate the bread." That *ate the bread* — consumption right after the admission — got me: she got what she came for. The whole exchange undoes Vee's tidy rationalization from the previous chapter ("he must have forgotten to wipe," one fluke) and reopens the door the earlier chapter left ajar ("it did not go all the way; it settled somewhere under the affection and stayed"). Randi is engineering the question that will keep Vee's body returning to this, in Randi's direction. Suspicion: fully earned, right there on the page, no machinery needed.

And then the goodbye beat that closed my throat. *"You picked well, you know. I don't think I ever told you that."* Vee glows, and only the smallest part of her "went to the one word that wasn't quite true" — she hadn't picked Pace; he'd walked in decided before she knew there was deciding. Reader, I know from the earlier chapters: Randi picked her in the pointing game. So Randi is now crediting Vee with the choosing Randi herself performed, as a compliment — and Vee swallows it because "you didn't hand a compliment back on a technicality." I physically hurt for her there. It is the kindest, cruelest beat in the chapter.

Then the escalator: the neck-kiss. Vee does it *deliberately* this time — "already looking at the neck," the kiss "landed before any of her had convened on the question" — and Randi's body betrays her cover: "a small quick thing, a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long." Randi masks it with "Ticklish," and Vee catches the response, pleased. The ratchet between them has now moved from recurring accident to chosen ritual, and I both wanted it — I do, I'm in; the pull of these two is on me — and dreaded it, because every inch of the wanting is being steered and neither of them will look at the steer.

The chapter closes perfectly: Vee walking into the wind with two sentences on repeat — *"He likes it. Actually — he's enthusiastic."* and, beneath, *"I don't."* She "let it," and I closed the chapter letting it too, because that's what this book does: it leaves the door ajar and I leave it ajar.

Friction: the opening closet passage is a hair padded (the fit-arithmetic of the sweater is over-explained), and Marsh's chorus-device is a touch on the nose this week — but the device is established texture, so it lands. Everything else earned its length; the dinner is the body of the chapter and I didn't want it shorter.

Titles. "A Clean Plate": Vee keeps serving clean, sanded versions and Randi keeps sending them back for the messy one — and the literal plate Vee polishes clean while Randi abandons hers after one bite: appetite as the real subject, tidiness as falsification. Volume/cover: "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION" — a long spoon is for supping with the dangerous, and "a warm reception" is exactly what Randi gives Vee's stories (and what Vee gives Randi's steer). No tagline shown in my packet beyond the cover board; I'm following the title as the true north.

Next — guesses from here: I want Vee to stop filing the "not quite true" word; I dread that "It turned me on" + "You picked well" is Randi's opening move into the next phase, and the neck-kiss will not hold at "ticklish" much longer; and I expect Marsh's lecture to keep naming the mechanism while Vee misses it. I'm braced, and I'm pulled.
