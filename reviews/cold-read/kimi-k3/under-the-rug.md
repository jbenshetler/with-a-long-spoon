## Reader reaction

This one hollowed me out. It's the saddest chapter so far and also one of the hottest, and the book makes those the same event.

**Vee.** My sympathy for her deepened and complicated at once. The opening paragraph is a knife — the word "right there, simple, wanting out," and her fear "of the distance it would have to cross to reach him... a room that didn't say it back." She never even risks it this time; last chapter's retired sentence has become a held breath. And then I caught her doing something I hadn't seen her do before: lie, knowingly. "You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail" — and the page says, quietly, *it wasn't true*, "some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold that night... and she said it anyway, because she needed the line of it to hold." I didn't love her less for it. I recognized it — the meanness of the needier person — and I trusted the book more for letting her be that. But something in her is hardening: "she told herself the thing that let her go: that she had forgiven him." Forgiveness as a coat she puts on, "warm and large and easy to wear." I didn't buy it and I wasn't meant to.

**Pace.** This is the most protective of him I've ever felt. The Chili's scene undid me in his favor: the eyes that lift and slip and that he *always* brings back, now at a visible cost; the fork squared and re-squared to the table edge; the flicker of irritation he drops his eyes to hide "as if he didn't want her to have it." When he finally explains — "In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you. Somewhere quieter, I can. And I do. I'm listening now" — I believed him completely. There was never deception in that scene; there was a limit. And yet. Her verdict lands on me too: "It wasn't the word. It would never be the word." He will go out, solve, explain, undress her, worship her, lose himself in her — "he'd given her his control and all of his body and every last thing but the one." I no longer suspect him of withholding it out of coldness or strategy. I suspect he *can't* — that the word lives outside the set of things he can do — and I'm honestly not sure which would be kinder.

**Swoon / pull.** The sex worked on me entirely. Her inward begging for his control to *give* — and then the book actually gives it to her: "took her like a man who'd run clean out of the strength to wait," after staying "patient past where patience should have broken." The patience-then-collapse architecture is the exact fantasy, executed at full heat, and the detail "her passion reaching up and pulling him over after her" gives her agency inside it. And then the aftermath turns the same heat into grief: him asleep in seconds, her awake in the cooling sweat, "marked all over with where he'd been," and the want of the name coming back to find her. The line that cracked me: "She came with his name in her mouth and not the other thing."

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I have no real suspicion of anyone — that's the strange ache of this book. I trust Pace's tenderness as fully as she does; I just also believe her "never." The one thing I hold that she doesn't: in the truck, when his ease returns "like a light turned up," she reads it as relief at being shut of being seen with her — "some small wrong note she'd have had to stop and turn over to hear properly, and she didn't stop." I stopped. He was relieved to be out of the *noise*, and she was too tired and too needy to hear the difference, and that's a misreading the book handed me and let her drop.

**Friction.** Almost none. The nachos passage runs long — billboard/painting, then the whole muddy-palette riff — but I loved it too much to resent it: "He had ruined it for her, was the thing. Not just this." That's the whole book in seven words.

**Titles.** *Under the Rug* is the best-fitting title yet. She asks "Is this what we are now?" — the definitional question of the whole book — and it is never answered; it's answered with logistics ("Okay. Where do you want to go tonight?"). The false accusation gets swept (he never litigates it, just: "You know what happened after the dance"). The forgiveness gets swept. The word gets drowned "as it always did, as she let it." The rug is where this relationship's truth lives. *A Polite Invitation* recolors for me: his love is a series of perfect invitations that all lead inward — to the house, the truck, the bed — and never once outward. "It lived only here, in the dark, in this bed, and stopped at its edges." *With a Long Spoon* keeps humming under the feeding motif: she is fed exquisitely, at a distance the spoon enforces.

**Next.** I dread the pile. I want someone to say the word and I now think no one will, which is worse. Guesses from here: this much unsayable pressure has to vent somewhere — toward Randi, whose thread has been quiet a long time and whose kiss lives in the same unsayable file, or toward a rupture from outside, someone *seeing* them, which is what she asked for and didn't get.
