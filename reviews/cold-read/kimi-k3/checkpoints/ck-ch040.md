### Symbolism
- The bench disguised as furniture: a hidden purpose inside an ordinary object — the double life everything in this book keeps.
- "Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it": every event arrives twice, once as the characters live it and once as the reader can read it; redundancy as survival — and as cover.
- The statistics sequence: aggregation hides the truth (Simpson), the things that matter are latent and must be inferred, and the outlier is the secret sitting in plain sight — the MIRANDA point, and Vee herself, one point off alone in the white.
- The dress made *to* her, not *for* her: desire tailored directly onto a body; the slit that permits nothing under it = the secret worn closest to the skin, invisible to the room, known only to her.
- The two bathroom drawers: love as the quiet compression of an ordered self to make first-shelf room for another.
- The pool: the one element where her body is not a problem; the place she takes questions to have the words taken off them.
- The high table in the window: belonging as display.
- Doors respected and doors locked: consent's architecture, and the one locked door (Randi's) the whole plot is quietly leaning against.

No note. There is never a note. Cassie, dry: "You don't come home school nights... Do you love him." "Maybe... It's all so fast." The pool takes the words off the question. Then the evening of three times: his mouth at her breast, "Remembering you is a pale thing," her offer — "Would a picture help?" — his condition-free acceptance and her one condition ("never share me"); he poses her like a calendar girl, kisses each placed limb, shoots on her phone, and she sends it, delivered, believing him — the brave part. Lemon chicken; "Are you going home?" — "This is my home... I don't go back"; Sheri for Thanksgiving, with a pie she'll pretend she made.

**The last stretch (ch 39–40).** Brunch in the dark green velvet: mimosas, eggs Benedict ordered past her old ceiling. "We have so much sex, Randi... am I turning into some kind of sex pervert?" — "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud... you're *my* sex pervert." Vee shows her the photos — the cropped one, then, stung by the hint of flinch, the full one: "I gave him *all* of it." They plan the anthro class, just the two of them; the goodbye kiss Vee this time prolongs, and Randi holds her eyes a beat past goodbye. Then the stats project: PPP loans, Cassie's thesis (money went up, jobs went down — her father was laid off in 2020), Randi's certainty her father's businesses used the money right. The aggregate proves Cassie. And Randi's own county produces the outlier — the biggest point on the graph, off alone in the white, tagged MIRA, opening onto a record whose first word is MIRANDA: several loans, every payroll cut anyway. Randi goes still; "Cheating bastards," ragged and too near. Cassie, unable to see the screen, takes it as vindication. Vee sees everything, says nothing, and quietly builds Randi a shelter of busywork. They walk out into the gray — Vee now holding the one secret in the book that belongs to her.

### Impression
I am completely disarmed and completely alarmed, at the same time, and the book means me to be. **Pace**: every observable act is tenderness — the drawer, the dress, the patience, "Thank you for telling me," the water left out in advance — and none of it ever slips, which is precisely what frightens me; the warmth is real *and* it is a medium he designed. I do not think he is false. I think he is sincere and deceiving her, and I cannot resolve those, and neither, I suspect, can he anymore. **Randi**: magnetic, and lonelier than she lets anyone see — the woman who locked the bathroom door on her own breakthrough. Her hunger for Vee is the most honest thing about her, and she is spending it inside a management plan. My wariness of her keeps getting tangled in pity. **Vee**: pure sympathy, and joy, and dread. Her waking up — food, sex, being seen, the long stride across that stage — is glorious to watch; I want all of it for her; I want her to have it without the architecture underneath. **Cassie** I trust entirely, and I am worried for her — she is being outmaneuvered without ever being fought. No reason at all to doubt Cassie, Meg, Kayla, Sheri, Christine, or the bartender; the blonde at the Rusty Nail was the only malice on the page and she was small and ordinary. Trust versus suspicion sits exactly here: I trust everyone's feelings and no one's arrangements.
