### Impression
Vee I ache for and am rooting for — her hunger is so much larger than her permission, and watching the permission catch up is the book's real thrill; her courage is quiet and consistent (the window knock, the "exception," opening her eyes on the pedestal, walking the pie up the hill). Pace I trust *and* distrust in nearly equal measure, which feels like the intended vertigo: the tenderness is demonstrably real — the moleskin, the sliced apples, "I want her to get there on her own," the held door of the scar — and yet he has never once told her he shares a bed with her best friend, and his patience functions perfectly as a trap whether or not he means it as one. The Daphne shadow and the ch-1 admission that he once forced a door keep my wariness alive under the warmth. Randi I find magnetic and the most dangerous person in the book *and* increasingly pitiable: she is using Vee with real skill, and Vee is quietly ruining the using — "she got past me before I could help it" is the truest sentence she's said. My suspicion sits mostly with the design, not with any single cruelty; no one has yet taken anything Vee didn't light up to give, which is exactly what makes it unsettling. Cassie I trust completely and wish Vee would use; her flat "Goodbye, Vee" still sits in me like a stone. Brooke I do not trust an inch. Overall: I am falling for all of it exactly as instructed, and the dread is entirely made of love.
