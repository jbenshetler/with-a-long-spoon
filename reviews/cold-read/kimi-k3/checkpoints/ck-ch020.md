### Impression
Vee: deep tenderness and protectiveness. Her hunger — for food, for being seen, for wanting without apology — reads as wholly sympathetic, and watching it get fed is genuinely sweet even as I hold what she can't see. Her courage is quiet and real (opening her eyes on the riser; "I wanted it. And I took it").

Pace: I trust his tenderness and distrust his honesty, in the same breath. Everything he does for Vee is careful, patient, exact, and appears to come from real regard — he *sees* her — and he is also, with Randi, the author of the fiction she's living inside. He asks before he takes and never tells her what the question costs. The Daphne lesson reads as sincere; the deception is nonetheless structural. I like him and I do not excuse him.

Randi: the most complicated pull. The bench chapter gives her wound a shape — a girl whose perfection kept her safe until a man saw past it, and whose response to being truly met was to lock the door. Her steering of Vee is skilled and calculated, and her feeling for Vee keeps breaking the surface of the calculation (the gentler smile, the stillness, "You're lucky"). I am wary of her and sorry for her, and I believe both her tenderness and her appetite are real.

Cassie: the person I trust most in the book. Her flatness is love in another register; she is the only one whose instincts point at the truth. I want her to be wrong to worry and fear she'll be right.

Brooke: mild unease — warmth as an instrument; her probe of Randi suggests she notices more than she says.

Overall: the book has made me fall for the courtship exactly as Vee does, while keeping the frame visible — and the ache of the book right now is that both things are true at once: nothing has been taken from her that she did not hand over, and she has never once been shown the table she was handing it across.
