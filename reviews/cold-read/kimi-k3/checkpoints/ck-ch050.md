### Impression
I am thoroughly, willingly compromised, exactly as the jacket promised I'd be. Toward **Pace**: deep tenderness and real frustration in equal measure. The care is not an act — the top drawer, the moleskin, the juice, "You're always beautiful," the way he answered Vee's cracker-sentence with a held press and two breaths he didn't know she could feel. But ch 50 costs him my patience and buys back my sympathy at the same time: he's not withholding love, he's a burn victim afraid of the stove, and Sheri is right that from Vee's seat the distinction doesn't exist. Toward **Randi**: the most magnetic person on the page and the one I watch with my hand on my wallet — she engineers arousal, harvests confidences, and hands them to Pace — and yet ch 47 broke me for her. Her love for Vee is the only thing in her life with no currency in it, and her decision to protect it by never saying it is either noble or the game's deepest move; I genuinely cannot tell, and I don't think she can either. Toward **Vee**: pure rooting, aching sympathy — a woman whose every "yes" is real and whose map of the room is wrong in one place: the room was built. I want the reveal to come and dread what it costs her, because nothing was taken that she didn't hand over, and that's the tragedy, not the defense. Toward **Cassie**: total trust; the only clean lens in the book, and I flinch at how Vee keeps editing for her. **Sheri** I would follow anywhere. Trust vs. suspicion, plainly: I have every reason to hold both at once — the love is real *and* the invitation was written before she knew there was one, and the book has earned my belief that both of those facts will survive whatever comes next.
