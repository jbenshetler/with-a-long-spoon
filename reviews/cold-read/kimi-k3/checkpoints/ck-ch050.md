### Story so far
A woman arrives at Pace's warm house, locks the door behind her, and is taken through an elaborate, consensual bench scene — spanking, edging, begging — ending in her first unperformed orgasm and tears. She is **Randi**: Pace's partner, a sorority senior who has spent her life behind a flawless face. He found her behind it; in the bathroom she decides he must never have that access again. The next morning she endures a status audit from her chapter president, Brooke, and goes numb in her single room.

Weeks earlier/later interleaved: Pace and Randi, in bed, play a question game — "would you like to taste another woman?" — and Randi comes helplessly at the thought, then reframes it as a threesome gift for him. On a campus walk they play the pointing game: he points, she vetoes, until **Vee** — tall, red-haired, laughing with her whole body — walks out of the dining hall. Randi says yes before she can stop herself. Randi engineers a collision, learns they share a stats class, and befriends her.

Vee, a junior econ major from Ohio — ex-synchronized swimmer, body-shamed by her mother, invisible beside her roommate Cassie's flat dry loyalty — is dazzled. Chi Latte, the high table, "love you, girl," *see you later*. At a coffee shop, Pace "happens" to hear her stuck on an integral and teaches her Euler's identity as substitution — giving her back the pencil at exactly the line where she can finish herself. He takes her number; Randi notes he forgot his coffee. Froyo, the long way home, a Middle Eastern dinner where he asks "May I choose?", a hike where he fixes her blister, feeds her rye and horseradish and cherries, teaches leave-no-trace without shaming her. In the rain he gives her his only dry shirt and stands outside the truck; she calls him back in and takes her wet shirt off for him, eyes locked. He doesn't touch her. She keeps the flannel.

First house visit: she discovers he builds furniture, sews, cooks, holds a patent, and has bought burgundy silk to make her the dress she once described and released. The measuring/fitting is an hour of sustained near-naked exposure in a warm house; she wets through her satin in mortification; he waits on his knees, smiling up at her like she's a gift, and something in her permanently unstoops. She nearly topples his composure on purpose; he finishes the hem anyway.

Randi meanwhile cultivates brunches: confesses her own escapades (tied with a scarf, tickled), draws out Vee's, names her appetites kindly ("you *love* being looked at"), and kisses her goodbye on the mouth — a soft double press that becomes their custom. Vee's body begins answering Randi's stories; twice she flees to bathrooms and stops herself.

With Pace: the first night (green set bought for him, "trade" of folded panties), the finished dress (worn bare underneath, slit to the hip), dance lessons on a concrete pad and the Rusty Nail — where a blonde poisons her ("all us girls love him… gets a girl home safe"), Vee attacks him publicly, and the bartender later tells her the girls were jealous of *her*. Devastated, she goes to Randi, who prescribes a sure-not-sorry apology, dresses her (black dress, cognac heels, scarf lesson), and later coaches the entire reconciliation. Vee apologizes with a labeled HUMBLE PIE; a playful swat reveals her response to spanking; a lost bet leads to a formal bare-bottom spanking, stripped but for the heels, and the first sex where shame has no witness. Halloween: two costumes — the dress, and nothing under it.

The induction: she takes the long step on purpose, gives the room her leg, Cassie photographs it; she sends Pace the uncropped frame, her parents the cropped one. Thanksgiving at home: her father prices the dress. She learns Pace is 22 (college at 15, the desk in the hall, Daphne at 19), sees the scar he won't explain, hears "I don't go back." She says her cracker-and-meal sentence; he answers "I love how good we are together." The mismatch lodges. In the dark she asks what they are; "words are cheap — believe what I do"; he folds his arms; she walks out into December. A month of silence: she in Ohio imagining a blonde Sheri, he at the diner with the real Sheri admitting "I want Vee. I miss her… I can't."

Interleaved, Gstaad: Randi, skiing with her parents (whose PPP fraud she has just discovered with Vee beside her in the library — Vee saw, and silently protected her), meets John, the perfect faultless man, white-tie ball, sleigh — and lies beside him cold, measuring him against Pace ("Pace on his worst night reached further") and against what comes off Vee across a table. She decides never to tell Vee, to let her arrive at it; texts Vee the fairytale version with one true line: *miss your face most though, gorgeous*.

January: Vee returns wrecked, goes straight to Randi, who dismantles the fantasy rival, reframes the silence ("a boy holding himself still"), and designs the repair: the good coat, nothing underneath, black strappy heels. Vee executes it — edging herself at the mirror, again driving (nearly caught by a passing cruiser), arriving bare under wool at his door. The wall drops instantly; he goes to his knees on the cold porch; they reconcile on the couch with the door banging open; he makes pancakes at midnight, kneels, says "I missed you so much" with a cracking voice. Sunday she walks into the dorm in his sweats and Randi's heels; Cassie raises one eyebrow. The name is still unspoken; the triangle is still invisible to its point.

### Impression
I trust **Cassie** completely and **Sheri** nearly so — the two people with no investment in Vee's transformation. I am deeply tender toward **Vee**, whose waking appetite is real and glorious, and whose every "freely given" yes has been quietly stage-managed; I want her joy *and* dread her education. Toward **Pace** I feel the book's own split: his attention is the most cherishing thing in Vee's life, his restraint reads as genuine ethic, and his wordlessness is plainly a wound, not a tactic — yet he entered her life as a move in someone else's game, and I have not forgotten it. Toward **Randi** I feel the most complicated pull: she is the hungriest person in the book, starving inside the fairytale, and her love for Vee is the first real thing she has — and she is also the one grooming her, harvesting her confidences in Pace's bed, choreographing her arousal like a fit model. I don't doubt that she loves her. I don't trust a single thing she does with it. The jacket told me every yes was freely given and that this was the problem; fifty chapters in, I believe it, and I am braced.
