### Impression
- **Pace:** I am half in love with him and I don't fully trust him, and I think the book means for me to hold both at once. His care is exquisitely specific (apple slices cut-side-up, water left out, the unopened bathroom door, "May I") and nothing on the page says the tenderness is fake — if anything the reverse. But he pointed. He helped choose a girl who didn't know she was being chosen, and then met her as if by chance. My warmth toward him is real; so is the splinter.
- **Randi:** the one I ache for and watch most narrowly. Her damage is fully credible — the food policing, the face, the mother with a knife in it, the bathroom mirror, "not ever" — and her desire for Vee is plainly, helplessly real. Which makes the extraction scenes (the cup-tell, "Paul?", the smoothed debrief) hurt rather than simply menace. I suspect she is lying to herself more competently than to anyone else.
- **Vee:** pure tenderness and protectiveness. She is luminous, hungry in every sense, self-policing to the point of disappearance — and her smallest real choices (the solved integral, the exception under the tree) are genuinely hers, which is exactly what makes the design around them so uncomfortable. I want her to have all of it and I am afraid of what it cost to give it to her.
- **Cassie:** I trust her completely. The only clear eyes in the book; her jokes are guardrails.
- Trust vs. suspicion, plainly: nobody has yet lied to Vee about anything she asked, and every yes really has been freely given — and I am suspicious of everything anyway, because I can see the frame and she can't. The warmth and the setup are both true, and the not-knowing which will outlast the other is the feeling of the book so far.
