### Impression
**Pace** draws trust and wariness at once, and I don't think the book wants me to resolve that yet. On the page he is the most careful man either woman has met — the given-back pencil, "May I," the water bottle left in advance, the closed door he won't open, the secret of Vee's he keeps even from Randi. I believe his tenderness is real. But he and Randi built a snare and called it a gift, and he is very good at arranging people; the care and the craft are the same hands. I like him and I do not fully trust him.

**Randi** is the one I ache for. Her armature — the face, the bit, the trimmed appetite — is airtight and already failing: the bench found her, the mirror found her, and her answer was a deadbolt. Her want for Vee is genuine and she has no safe shape for it except a lie (the threesome-gift frame, the friendship-as-lure). She is using Vee and loving her in the same gesture, and she flinches at "beautiful." Sympathy, tenderness, and a held breath.

**Vee** has my whole heart right now. The hunger, the mother's voice, the cardigan, the green quiet under the pool, "I did it on purpose" — watching her be fed, listened to, and let choose is genuinely moving, which is exactly what makes the reader's position uncomfortable: I am ahead of her, enjoying what she can't see is arranged, precisely as the book said I would. No reason yet to doubt that what's growing in her is real and good — only that the ground under it is.

**Cassie** I trust completely; she is the only person on the page with no design on anyone, and the only one whose eyes have caught the seams (the "Goodbye," the great-aunt). If anyone is this story's safety, it's her.
