## Reader reaction

This one got me completely. After the last chapter left me braced and dread-heavy in their bedroom, this chapter put *me* under the quilt, and I went willingly. The fire, the glass, the cold leaning in, his palm just holding her breast "without pressing, without asking" — I melted before anything was even said.

The saffron that was "old enough to vote," the spice rack seals with their "stubborn dignity" — Vee is *funny* here in a way I've rarely gotten to sit with, and Pace receives it "so exactly that the telling itself became a pleasure." And then he gives back: Peter. The name with "no forward motion in it." The desk moved into the hall for a year — "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" is the best sentence Vee has ever produced, and his "not startled... as if she had put her hand on a place he had not offered her and had, by accident, found the right one" made my chest ache. Then the paint-shape, the coastline, "Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some" — I understand why she loves him. I felt the world tilt with her.

And then Daphne, and the floor quietly drops out. Nineteen and fifteen. Two years. I did the same arithmetic Vee did, and the same clean ugly word formed in me, and I held it down the same way. The page never says it. Vee never says it. Pace *defends* it — "the best two years I'd had," "he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck" — and that is the saddest thing in the book so far. The boy sent away at fifteen because leaving was "less than staying," tutored by the one person who was tender with him. And here is what I can't unknow: the man shaped by that arrangement is now the architect of an arrangement around Vee — older, patient, designed, "I want her to get there on her own." The page doesn't draw the line. I draw it, and it frightens me, and I also can't be sure it's the right line. That doubleness is exactly where this book lives.

The scar: her fingers find it "before she thought about whether to," "longer than her hand — more of it than she'd known," and his "Not tonight" — "no wall in it, only a door with a time on it." I wanted to know and was glad not to. And "Peter," said once into the dark, "the way you say a word to keep it" — that undid me.

The erotic charge is low and warm rather than hot — the open robe, the held breast, the hand pressed onto the scar — intimacy as heat, and it works. The title *Rock*: the ridge out the window, the thing under his voice, the thing she leans on — I take it as him, and as what's under both of them. No friction for me anywhere in this chapter; it earned every line. What I dread: every true thing he gave her tonight makes the lie underneath heavier. *A Polite Invitation* keeps feeling like the politeness is the trap.
