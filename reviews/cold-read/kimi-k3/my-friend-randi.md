## Reader reaction

The opening hurt. The red-eye, the heater, no radio, crying "because there was no one in the car to keep it from" — and the cruelest detail, that the drive matched the picture she'd built "exactly as she'd pictured it, and it was nothing." Every mile running toward a town that has Pace in it "and not one inch of him for her." I was braced for a chapter of wallowing. Instead Randi took it apart and rebuilt it, and I watched a rescue happen in real time.

Randi is extraordinary here — and I mean that warmly and slightly nervously at once. She never says "you're wrong." She re-sequences: not the last month, that last night. She sets down the pre-heated house, the chicken, the two poured wines "where Vee could see it." She asks what his face did. And her diagnosis — "You backed him into a corner in his own house. Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do" — is *right*. I know it's right; I sat in that diner with him last chapter and heard "I can't" with no reason given. Randi has never been in that diner. So when Vee asks "Do you think so?" and Randi answers "I know so," a small cold spot opened in me: her certainty about this man's walls sounds like knowledge, not intuition, and Vee has no idea where it comes from. I do, or I suspect I do — "That's not finished" was said of Randi herself. Everything Randi does in this chapter *helps*; her advice is verified accurate against his own interior; and I still can't call it clean.

The phantom Sheri scene was agony and comedy together. "Blonde. Tan. Built like a model" — and I know the real Sheri is five feet of bleach, dark roots and hell-giving who "didn't want him, wasn't impressed by him." Vee's small "I built her on the nights I felt stupid for missing him this badly" broke my heart a little. Randi's "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face" is the best line in the chapter and it's also, unknowingly, truer than she means: the real woman behind the name has no face Vee would recognize as a rival at all.

The coat plan is where the chapter got hot. Not graphically — the heat is in the mechanics. Randi hangs the sentence ("You've got that good long coat") and *waits for Vee to find it*, and Vee finds "And nothing under it" — and I noticed that this is Pace's own method, the get-there-on-her-own patience, sitting in Randi's hands. Then the shame-word scene: "It's such a slutty thing to do," said at cost, flushed to the ears, and Randi walking her down it a word at a time until the text names Vee's wiring outright — "once the shame was lit, the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's her engine, finally stated on the page. It works on me. And then Randi pressing her own black heels into Vee's hands — after the induction buckle, after "Leave the shoes," this is the third time shoes pass between these people like tokens, and this time they're *Randi's own*, going on Vee's feet to another man's door. "Randi always knew the shoes." I don't fully know what I'm looking at there, but I know it's loaded.

And then the John story — the chapter's other country. It reads like a fairy tale and I think it's meant to: Vee keeps hearing its storybook finish, "a man who was a story you'd tell before you'd known him a day," and it goes into her sideways because John names children in the first hour — the exact anti-Pace, declaration without fear. But the tell is the stop. Randi, "who could narrate anything," who has mapped men's mouths across a dozen tables, hits the bedroom door and says only "it got very late, and we went up," and looks down at her hands, and then *comes back up into her smile* — that phrasing, like surfacing — with a glib gloss: "A masterpiece. Everywhere but the bedroom." I don't believe the gloss. Something happened up there, and "the bedroom's the room I actually live in" makes the dodge land harder, not lighter. Given that I already know the chalet text was a cover and Vee has never let on, I'm now holding a question Vee isn't asking: is John the truth, or a prettier cover?

The title, "My Friend Randi," is warm on its face — this is the chapter where the friendship saves Vee. After reading, it sits doubled: the friend who catches her is also the friend with a sealed archive, a sealed bedroom, and an unexplained fluency in the man Vee is going back to. I don't think the title is ironic in a cruel way. I think it's asking me to hold both.

What I want next: tonight. The door, his face, a month of silence with nowhere to hide. What I dread: the heels are traceable — he knows Randi; will he read her staging on Vee's body? And does the grand gesture get him past the word, or just around it? The February dance is still out there ticking.
