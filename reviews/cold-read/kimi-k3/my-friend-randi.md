## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me in the gentlest way. Vee crying the whole drive in — "silence wasn't an answer. The road knew better" — and then the empty dorm meeting her "like more of the same" put me straight back inside her cold. And then Randi opened her arms and the chapter got warm. That hug held "past where a hug stops," the bitter-orange smell she drinks in twice — I felt the pull in that so plainly, and Vee doesn't even look at it. Randi, on this page, is *magnificent*: fast down the stairs, "Up the stairs," new Gstaad dresses swept to the floor without a glance, the child's-hair push off Vee's face. Her reading of the fight is the kindest, sharpest thing anyone has given Vee — "He'd cooked... He'd warmed the house for you" — and she dismantles the blonde invention in one move: "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." I believe every word of her counsel. And I can't un-know that it's a cover-artist giving it, that "I'm so jealous I could scream" passed over the truest thing in the room.

The coat plan is where the swoon and the unease braid. "You just show up already being the answer" — and Vee finishing the sentence herself, the shame-word burning up her neck, Randi walking her down: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do." I *want* it to work. I also remember whose specialty a manufactured erotic gesture is, and that Randi pressed her own heels into Vee's hands.

And then the gut-punch I knew was coming: Randi tells the Gstaad fairytale out loud, and I alone know it ends hollow — she stops at "and we went up," looks at her hands, and prices John out of "the bedroom, the room I actually live in." Vee envies it all over again, in person: "the easy forward motion of it... not be afraid of one word in it." The word! Both storylines are about a man and a word, and Vee is envying a fiction. Titles: "My Friend Randi" — this is the chapter where Randi earns the possessive in Vee's mouth while I hold everything she's withholding; the friend-title does double duty as the cover's name. The book's title and tagline recede from view here; "a polite invitation" could be the coat itself — an invitation with no words in it. What I want next: tonight, the door, his face. What I dread: Randi's engineering visible in the architecture of Vee's bravest act.
