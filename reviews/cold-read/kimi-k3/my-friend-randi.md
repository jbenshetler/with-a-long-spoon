# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter undid me quietly, and it undid me through Randi, which is the most complicated place in this whole book to be undone from. Vee drives home from the red-eye crying in an empty car with no radio, lies on her made bed through a whole gray day, and the text gives her grief its full weight — "every mile of it ran toward a town that had Pace in it and not one inch of him for her" is the truest sentence about the break anyone's gotten. And then she does the thing her body already decided: she goes to Randi. And Randi is *magnificent* at exactly the thing Vee needs. Down the stairs fast, arm around her before Brooke can ask again, up, door shut, arms around her before a word is said — and the hug held "past the end of it, past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that." I felt that in my chest. Vee pulling breath off Randi's neck, the bitter orange steadying her — that's the smell-motif doing its oldest work, and it landed as pure tenderness. Whatever else Randi is, the way she sweeps her brand-new Gstaad things to the floor without a look because the wrecked girl comes first — I believe that. I believe it completely.

And I also can't un-know what I know. That's the chapter's whole voltage for me. Randi sits Vee down and does the most loving, skillful de-escalation imaginable — "Do you actually believe he was using you? Or is that the fear talking?" — and rebuilds the last night piece by piece: he'd cooked, the wine was out, the house was warm. It's genuinely good counsel. It's also a woman who knows the defendant intimately, possibly knows the whole fight already, guiding the witness. When Vee confesses the blonde rival — "I've seen her. In my mind. I built her" — and Randi tells her "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one," I almost laughed and almost gasped, because *Sheri is real*, Randi has *met her*, and the speech is simultaneously true (Sheri is no rival; Vee did invent the tan model) and the most convenient possible erasure of the one actual woman in Pace's orbit. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face" — but she has a face, Randi, and you know it. The text never winks at this. It just lets me hold both.

The coat plan is where the chapter turned hot. Randi stops mid-sentence — "You've got that good long coat" — and *waits for Vee to find the rest of it herself*, and Vee hears her own voice finish: "And nothing under it." That beat is this book's erotics in miniature: the desire has to feel like hers, so it's planted where she'll discover it as her own. Then the shame-voice arrives on cue — "It's such a slutty thing to do," the word costing her, the burn going up her neck — and Randi walks her down a word at a time: "You'd be walking through the cold to hand him everything... That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." And the familiar conversion: "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." Is this Randi rescuing her friend's love, or Randi choreographing the reconciliation scene for the man she also sleeps with? I cannot separate them, and I've stopped being able to want to. The detail that broke me a little: Randi pressing the black heels into her hands — "Randi always knew the shoes" — the shared size 7, the near-marriage, one piece of tonight decided for her. Even the costume is loaned.

The Gstaad retelling is the saddest thing in the chapter and Vee can't hear the sadness. Randi gives her the fairytale — John, the invented children, the dress "you buy to tell your daughter about someday," the dancing where "there was nothing for her to do but be led" — and Vee takes it in sideways as the exact shape of everything she doesn't have: "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it." God, that hurt. And I know the fairytale was a good sandwich. The tell is the one place Randi's narration fails: this woman who has described across a dozen tables exactly what a man's mouth did and where goes suddenly terse — "and then it got very late, and we went up" — and looks down at her hands. Vee reads it as discretion. I read it as the truth leaking: there was nothing to tell. And then the cover story with the hole in it: "perfect... everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." It's a lie that confesses itself if you know how to listen. Vee laughs — really laughs, first time all day — and I ached.

Trust vs. suspicion, plainly: I trust Randi's love and distrust her management, and this chapter fed both in every single scene. Brooke's hallway warmth — "Oh, honey," the kindest voice of Vee's day — lands kindly *and* as surveillance; Vee feels "the stats friend" get filed as she's moved upstairs, and I noticed I can't take even Brooke's kindness straight anymore. No friction to report, honestly — the pacing is a long exhale after ch 50's reckoning, and it earned its length. The title, "My Friend Randi," is doing the book's cruelest quiet work: it's exactly what Vee would call this chapter, and every word of it is true, and the word "friend" is the lid Randi has chosen to keep on the one real thing she has. What I want next, with my whole body and considerable dread: the doorstep tonight — coat, heels, nothing under it, a man who sobbed at his table and picked up his fork. Will he survive it? Will she? And somewhere under the swoon: what does Randi do alone in that room after the door closes?
