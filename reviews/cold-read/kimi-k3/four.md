## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This chapter knocked the wind out of me, and not gently. It's the first time the book has put me alone in the room with the two of them — no Vee to translate it into innocence — and what I found there was worse and better than I had imagined. Worse, because now I *see* the machinery running at full heat: Randi narrating Vee's goodbye kiss while Pace is inside her, the two of them literally fucking through the evidence of the girl who doesn't know she's in the room. "Somebody has to get her used to my mouth." I felt physically implicated, because the brunch chapter right before this had me melting — Vee prolonging the kiss, "See you soon, gorgeous" — and now I'm forced to hold both at once: the kiss was real *and* it was being carried back to this couch like game from a hunt.

And yet. Randi. God, Randi. This is the chapter where my wariness of her finally dissolved into something more painful. "I'm doing it for *you*" — "I didn't ask who it was for." He asks her, plainly, twice, and she cannot say *I like it* even while her body answers on the word "mouth," even while "I want her" falls out of her into the wood "with nothing at all around it." The scramble to retract it — "For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant" — is the loneliest thing I've read in this book, worse than the ch-1 bathroom door, because now I've watched her do it in real time: reach the true sentence and then snatch it back and file it under the plan. She's doing the work of falling in love and billing it to the project so she doesn't have to own it. "She will not let me pay for her" and "Saturdays are mine" — the tenderness is enormous and she keeps it gagged.

Pace moved in my estimation too, in a direction I didn't expect: darker and warmer simultaneously. "How did you know, that day?" — and the answer is that he read *Randi's* hand and breath across the quad, not the candidates. He pointed at Vee because Randi's face said yes. That recolors the whole pointing game for me: it was an act of intimacy between the two of them as much as a selection of Vee. And his "Is she all right? ... That isn't the same answer" — I believe the concern is real, and that's exactly the problem, because he is checking the gauge of an experiment he is running. "You don't have to do it for me," said kindly, into her neck, the one sentence that undoes her — he keeps handing her freedom and she keeps refusing it, and then "Some nights it frightens me" and his "Then I'll be careful with you." Careful *with* you, not careful *to stop*. The precision of that is doing so much quiet work.

The sex itself is the hottest sustained passage in the book so far, and it's hot *because* it's poisoned. Randi turning around, putting her face where he can't reach it, forgetting the mirror that "gave him her face the entire time" — she has built a hiding place and the room betrays it, which is her whole self in one image. The clench on "mouth" that "she had not decided to do and could not have stopped" — the body-answers-before-permission motif, now running in Randi, the person who thought she was the one running it in everyone else. And the rhythm: interrogation as foreplay, his patience against her hurry, her taking over at the end, his fourth orgasm crossing into pain — "made a sound into her back he did not recognize." That last detail lands hard; even he is being taken somewhere he didn't plan.

The explicit accounting horrified me more than any cruelty could: "still full of your come," "I tucked her hair back," "I got my hand in it once in October, before I knew better." *Before I knew better.* She's been gone on Vee since before the plan had her name on it. And "That's more than three" — she's counting his orgasms against Vee's account of two, three times a night, keeping score she didn't mean to keep. That is jealousy wearing an auditor's visor.

Friction: almost none. If anything the chapter risks being *too* perfectly symmetrical — every line answers an earlier one — but it earned it. "The whole what?" — his returning to the interrupted sentence after a minute of her dissolving — is the one moment I felt the author's hand, and even that worked, because it's his patience as method.

The title, "Four": the count. Four times in a day, one more than three — more than what he gives Vee, or what Vee reports. It's Randi's little ledgers all the way down: she counts because counting lets her call it something other than wanting. It also quietly marks this as *their* chapter — the second of the pair (his "In His Hands," hers now) — the original relationship, the one Vee doesn't know exists. The volume title, "A Warm Reception," now reads as double to me: the warmth Vee receives, and the warmth with which Randi receives *the news of her* — both real, one unknowing. "With a Long Spoon" — supping with the devil — keeps humming underneath, though I still can't tell who's supping with whom.

What I dread and want next: the anthro class, "just us," now that I've heard what Randi does with her Saturdays afterward. And I want — as a guess from here — the moment Vee's face does something Randi can't report, can't file, can't bring back to this couch. That mirror's going to get looked at eventually.
