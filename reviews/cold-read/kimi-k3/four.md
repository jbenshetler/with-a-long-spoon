## Reader reaction

This chapter took my breath out and I don't entirely mean it kindly. After forty chapters living inside Vee's waking-up — the brunches, the silk dress, the whole saturated glow of it — the book turns me around and hands me the room I've been dreading: Pace's couch, four deadbolts deep, the heat put up at three, Randi in dark red silk with the day's news. And the devastating thing is how *good* it is. Not good as in right — good as in I couldn't look away, and I felt the floor of Vee's whole world from underneath.

Pace first, since this is his chapter and the first time I've ever been *inside* his head. The line that will not leave me: "I didn't. I pointed. I watched your face." Forty chapters of me assuming the pointing game was his predation, his method, and it was *Randis's face* that chose Vee. That recolors everything and also somehow makes it worse — there was no cold architect at the moment of choice, just two people following a want and building the machinery afterward. And yet: "Is she all right?" / "Is she happy?" — he asks, twice, checking the gauge on a girl he's never once lied to in her hearing, and I can't call him a villain and I can't call him safe. "He let that go by" when Randi claims "She told me first" — he notices everything, this man, and the question is what he does with what he notices.

Randi. God. This is the most naked she's ever been on the page — literally folded over a couch rail, and spiritually, and she still will not say the true thing. "I'm doing it for *you.*" "It's groundwork. It's not a treat, it's *work,* I'm doing the work—" and Pace, kind as a knife: "You don't have to do it for me." And she comes on the word. I actually gasped. Her whole body voted against her own argument and she knew it and could not stop it, and the postscript — "Say something nice to me," the hands clamped on his forearm, "tighter, then tighter again" — is the loneliest thing in the book so far. She is starving exactly the way she diagnosed in Vee, and her name for the hunger is labor. The chapter title is her joke, "That's more than three," and it's perfect: her register even wrecked is arithmetic, deflection, the count.

And the erotic charge — it's high and it's poisoned and the book knows it. The sex is genuinely hot: the rail, the silk, the wet of three times loud in the room. But the heat engine of the scene is *Vee* — Vee's wet hair, Vee's chest in the hug, "She stands there in the sun with her eyes shut, waiting for me to come back to her" — and I am hearing a girl's private tenderness converted into fuel for an act she doesn't know is happening. That's the violation at the center of the chapter and the book does not flinch from it or name it. What gutted me most is that the retold goodbye kiss is *faithful*. Randi didn't exaggerate it. Vee really did lean up the last half inch. So the theft is not of facts but of the meaning Vee thought she was keeping.

One friction, small: nothing. Maybe Pace's equanimity at the end — "not sorry to hear it counted out loud" — skimmed past what Randi just confessed with her body, but that might be the point: he heard "for you" and let it stand because it was kinder, or because it was easier. I'm holding that open.

The title "Four" is doing triple duty — the fourth time, the deadbolt at four, her deadpan count — and it lands as Randi's whole person in one word: keep score, never say what the score means. The volume title *A Warm Reception* now reads double-edged to me: this house is warm, the warmth is real, and it is also a reception — a thing arranged in advance for a guest who hasn't arrived yet. *With a Long Spoon*: supping with someone, carefully. I feel the spoon getting shorter.

What I want next: Vee's side of this same weekend, and I dread it — the Tuesday library longing, the waiting-all-week Randi described, now that I've heard it described *here*. And the collision risk is louder: Randi said "stats with her this morning" out loud in this house. The two worlds are one wet-haired morning from touching.
