## Reader reaction

The lesson on the concrete pad is the most beautiful scene in the book so far, and I couldn't read it clean. "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead." "Don't think. Just feel the lead." "There's no sorry unless you take off a foot" — the gravity, then the laugh coming up through it. He's a *wonderful* teacher, patient and exact and funny, and when "something gave" and she stopped guessing and the dance took her, I felt it in my own shoulders. And at the same time I know what I know: this is the measuring scene translated into music. The frame, the stillness, the surrender of anticipation — "the decision pulled from her somehow." She's being taught, lovingly, consensually, to stop anticipating and follow a lead, and I can't separate the gift from the conditioning anymore. That's just where I live with this book now.

The blonde's little speech made me put the book down for a second. "Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to... He's so good about getting a girl home safe" — and then she "moved off, pleased with herself." I clocked the malice instantly, and also clocked that it wasn't *false* — that's what makes it a good knife. And then Vee's line — "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" — hit like a glass going past the point where you can catch it, which is exactly how the text describes it. My heart sank for both of them. His straight answer — "Yes. Sometimes. When it's required." — no defense, no chase, the beer set down untouched, the wedge of cold — is the most wounded dignity he's shown. She accused him of the one thing he's actually innocent of. The real thing to accuse him of exists, and she can't see it, and I sat there holding it for her. "She'd thought she was special. She was just next." God.

The bartender undid me: "Pace don't bring dates... Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." And then the chapter's quietest devastation: the trade shirt, washed, "smelling of fabric softener and no one," and she puts it on anyway. And then the last paragraph loaded the dread: she's going to Randi's to fix it. Of course she is. She's carrying the whole fight — her jealousy, her sex-shame, all of it — straight into the designer's living room.
