## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurt. It starts so sweet — the lesson on the concrete pad might be the loveliest scene in the book. Him in a short-sleeved shirt in October cold "because he'd dressed for the dancing, not the weather," the battered boom box with the taped handle, "There's no sorry unless you take off a foot" — I was grinning. And the moment the dance *takes* her — "the decision pulled from her somehow" — I felt the swoon of it, and I also couldn't help hearing it as the book's whole mechanism in miniature: stop thinking, feel the lead, let him move you. It's beautiful and it's the design's exact shape, and the chapter lets both be true without nudging me.

Then the Rusty Nail. The warm crowd, "hey, Pace," the hugs that go longer from the women — I felt Vee's count starting before she admitted she'd started one. The blonde's little knife — "Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to. All us girls love him" — landed on me almost as hard as on Vee, because it's *plausibly* friendly and perfectly poisonous. And Vee's detonation — "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe" — I winced, hearing it the way she did, a glass past catching. His "Yes. Sometimes. When it's required" — straight, no defense — made me ache for him, and also note: he says *required*. Required by what? The page doesn't say and I shouldn't fill it.

The bartender's reversal is earned and devastating: "Leaves her at the door... Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." So the crowd was watching *Vee* — she was the exception, on a Thursday, when he doesn't bring dates. And her sleepless audit — "She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be" — is honest and I believe it completely. The mother's voice surfacing as "floozies" and "spread her legs like a slut" is the old shame-voice doing its worst work, and the chapter knows it.

And then the last line quietly detonates for me: she decides to go to **Randi's** to fix it. She is carrying her rupture with Pace straight to Pace's lover and co-designer, to the woman who has been preparing her for him — and Vee thinks that's her safest person. The book just set up the collision I've been dreading, using nothing but kindness and a dance lesson.

Do I trust anyone more or less after this? Pace's conduct at the bar is confirmed clean by the one witness who'd know — that's real. But the scene also showed me the *scale* of the apparatus around him: a whole community of women he's led, gently, home. The Daphne-rhyme sits right there under the dancing teacher who "gets a girl to do just what he wants her to." Vee holds the pieces again — "He's making me" — and lets it go again.

The title: "Lesson." The dance lesson, the lesson the bartender teaches her, the lesson she teaches herself overnight — and, darker, she is being *schooled*, has been all along. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the tagline still hang over everything: this invitation is so polite she doesn't know she's accepted it. Next: she goes to Randi. I cannot wait and I'm dreading it in equal measure.
