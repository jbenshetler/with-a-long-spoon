This chapter is called "covering," and it earned the word in about six directions while I was reading it — the bra that shows more than it holds, the coat lining turned to the wall (and then deliberately turned back), the "after-school-special version," the flinch rewritten as ticklish, the stall orgasm filed under Pace. Every surface in the chapter is doing conceal/reveal work, and the book opens with a professor lecturing about how no gift is ever free. I felt the title playing with me the whole time in a good way.

The hau lecture genuinely thrilled me — Dr. Marsh announcing to a room of sophomores that a gift is inhabited, that it pulls home toward its giver, while Randi sits beside Vee wearing *the scarf* in her ponytail — the green-and-gold silk that went along to say sorry and worked — and Vee is "absurdly glad without asking why." As a reader, that's the book speaking its thesis out loud: the flannel, the shoes, the scarf, the coat — everything lent between these women is riding with its giver's spirit, and Vee is tangled in other people's hau. It's a plant, sure, but I took it like a wink rather than a lecture.

Randi deepened enormously for me here. The mother story — "They're not mine. They're your color," delivered over Sunday dinner with the lawn boy at the table — landed like a wound being offered. And the complication is intact: she's telling this wound as part of her practiced seduction-by-confession, and at the same time it's the thing that lets me see why her love for Vee might be the first safe hunger of her life.
