This chapter detonated me, and I'm still sitting in the crater of it.

Coming off "On Her Floor" — that kiss, Randi's mouth finally doing what it had been circling for months — I thought the fallout would be awkwardness, maybe a hangover of shame. Instead Vee goes home, peels Randi's skirt off her hips, finds the thong stuck to her skin, and her traitorous hand goes down and the picture that comes up is not Pace at all. It's Randi. "An offering with the wrong name on it." And then that devastating move: *I have a boyfriend,* reached for "the way you reach for the rail on a stair." The title clicked into place right there — "Boyfriend" is not a status, it's a handrail, a word for a girl with one and only one, and the whole chapter is about whether it can hold her weight while she drives through the black county dark to throw herself at it. I know too much to watch this cleanly. I know Pace is also Randi's. I know the whole courtship was staged. So when Vee reaches for the straightest, plainest word she owns, I'm holding my breath because I know the word itself is a stage prop.

And then the doorbell scene — she rings twice, "too soon for manners," wakes him, and uses him. The book is brave enough to say it: "She was not trying to have him. She was trying to get free of something." He knows immediately. "That was angry, Vee." And it *works on me* — it's one of the hottest scenes in the book, the trading mechanism giving the shame a structure to hide inside while she pays it out a coin at a time.
