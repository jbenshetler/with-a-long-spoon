## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

The double exposure of this chapter is doing something to me that I haven't felt in a book before. From inside Vee's head it's a perfect meet-cute — the kind I'd normally just melt into: the man who crosses the room with his eyes only on her, who reads her problem twice before touching the pencil, who *asks for the pencil with his eyes first*, who hands it back at exactly the line where the solving becomes hers, who says *may I call you* like a word from another century. And it works on me even knowing what I know. That's the disquieting part: I *want* Vee to be chosen, and she is being chosen, and the choosing is real and it is also choreography. Pace's attentiveness isn't faked — I've been inside his head at the bench; this economy, this not-performing, this listening is exactly who he is — and that's what makes it worse, not better. He's not conning her with a false self. He's deploying his true self, deliberately, at a target Randi pointed out on a lawn.

Randi. God, Randi. She suggested they move to the place on Wilson — the same Wilson place she invented for Brooke as her alibi, I noticed — and then she's texting "some boy," Vee thinks, amused, and then the bell tinkles. The reader can't prove it but the geometry is the geometry. And then Randi has to sit there and watch it work. Her tells are all in the hands: the coffee cup lifted twice with no sip, held a second too long. She's the one who wanted Vee — the bare *yes* on the brick path, the helpless looking — and now she's handing the wanting to Pace, wrapping it as a gift, exactly as she did in bed: *only in a threesome, with you.* The title lands like a small detonation. Substitution is the math trick Pace teaches — turn the hard problem into one you already know how to solve, the Euler move, two things at right angles becoming one plain thing — and it's also what's happening at this table in both directions. Vee's flat helpless want for Randi gets rerouted onto a safe male object she can desire without danger. Randi's want for Vee gets rerouted through Pace, who can touch what she can't. Everybody gets the thing by substituting for the thing. Nobody has to name anything. It's the sorority grammar made into a seduction machine, and it is *beautifully* constructed and it made my stomach hurt.

Vee breaks my heart in the small ways. The borrowed bit — "V.T., yes, like the university" — her trying on Randi's practiced swerve and it *landing*, her feeling the lift of pulling it off, then reaching back for her own name. Her noticing his forearms a half-second past her own choosing. Her econ answer — "the gap between what they say they're doing and what they're doing" — which is the thesis of her own life and she doesn't know it. And the last line: her phone facedown, and "she let herself want it to light up." Randi lay on her bed in last night's clothes with her phone facedown not reading the notifications; Vee faces hers down and hopes. Same gesture, opposite hungers.

Cassie is the only clean lens in the room and she's gloriously wrong in the right way: "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She saw the truth of the looking — that Pace had eyes for one person — and misread which fact it proved. Of course he didn't look at Randi. He goes home to Randi. I love that Cassie keeps filing her plain reports and everyone keeps filing them under *Cassie being Cassie.*

The heat here is real but clothed — it's the erotics of being looked at, of "something in her chest went quiet," of *chosen*. Knowing the bench chapter recolors every restrained gesture: the rolled cuffs, the hands, the economy of movement. He's in the same register he used to undress Randi — patience as an instrument — only pointed at an integral.
