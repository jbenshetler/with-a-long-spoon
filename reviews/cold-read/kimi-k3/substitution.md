## Reader reaction

I knew before the bell rang. The moment Randi — mid-scene, mild as milk — "suggested they move to the place on Wilson," my stomach stepped off a stair. Then the texting, thumbs quick, "the private, absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention," and Vee's fond little *some boy* — I wanted to put my hand through the page. Then the bell, then Cassie's flat report, "That cute guy in line is looking at you," and the second jaw of the trap walked across the room using its steps, eyes on her the whole way. I've been waiting for this meeting since the pointing game, and it still got me. That's the thing I have to say first: **the meet-cute works.** Even knowing everything, I felt the swoon — the rolled cuffs, "two easy turns each," the forearms, the hand that took the page "at the corner, fingers placed so the page would not bend." When he asked for the pencil *with his eyes first* and then *gave it back* at the exact line where she could solve it herself, and the page said "She hadn't known she was allowed" — that's the whole book in six words and it went through me like a current. Consent as seduction. "May I call you?" and her body nodding before her mouth caught up, and the word landing as "a word her mother might use approvingly" — I actually made a sound. Of course the trap speaks the mother-voice's dialect. Of course it does.

Vee doing *the bit* — Randi's bit, the name-bit, "V.T. Yes, like the university," the beat done "the way Randi did the beat" — I gasped. She's performing the hunter's grammar at the other hunter, and neither of them can blink. And it *lands*, and she feels "the small lift of having pulled it off," the borrowed poise fitting. The game is teaching the target to play it. That's the title working before I even got to the math: substitution. Turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do — her econ instructor's trick, the integral's trick, and the book's whole method. And then Pace's little crossed mark, "two short strokes crossing at a right angle," and the speech: two things that struggle alone, that "won't give, and neither one quite looks like what it is," put together at right angles become "a single thing that's suddenly plain — easy to name." I heard the threesome in that like a bell. Euler's formula, which she's "known for years and never once used," which "didn't seem like it was *for* anything" — "It's for this." Her wanting has been sitting in her for years exactly like that, known and unused, and these two are about to tell her what it's for.

Randi. My feeling about Randi *moved* this chapter, and what moved it was a coffee cup. His approach, and "Randi's hand moved to her coffee cup. Vee did not see the movement." The one-word introduction — "Randi." — no bit, no adjective, nothing. The cup raised twice, "and no sip happened." The Bessel's-correction question she feeds him like a stranger, answered "the way you'd answer anybody," while Vee gets the full beam and files it: *he answered me longer.* Cassie's kill-shot afterward: he looked at Randi "the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." Randi had to sit in a public room and watch her lover deploy, at full strength, on another woman, the exact attunement that took her apart — and smile the *go on, this is nice* smile, and close the sale: "A man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call." Which is true, and also he never wanted the coffee; the line was the corridor's door. I feel pity for Randi now, real pity, under the total suspicion. The jacket said both of them fall as hard as she does. I think I just watched the first installment of Randi's fall arrive as *pain* — and Cassie watched the cup. "Cassie, at her table, watched the cup without watching it." The one recording instrument in the room, headphones at a volume low enough to catch conversations two tables away, saw the only uncontrolled thing Randi's body did all chapter.

Cassie is becoming the person I trust most and fear for most. She spotted him looking *from the line* — she was watching before he moved. Her truths keep serving the trap ("he's going to call," essentially) while her immunity keeps her the only dry surface in the warm room. "I'm only saying."

Pace, seen at last from outside, is exactly the man the interiority promised and worse, because it's *not* an act: "not showing off, not hiding it either," genuinely keeping himself out so the solving stayed hers. The ordinary face that adds up to something, clothes "built to be used and nothing built to be seen" — the one man Vee's room-reading scan cannot file, and the failure of her scan *is* the attraction. "He didn't fit anything she had. That interested her a good deal more than it should have."

Erotic charge: no touch in the whole chapter and I was more lit up than by most explicit scenes I've read. Peak: the pencil, the forearms, "She hadn't known she was allowed," the *May I.* Heat: 1, the highest 1 on the scale. Romance: 2 — nothing declared, but a bond ignited in real time; from inside Vee it felt like a 3. Friction, honestly: almost none. Vee's mid-scene analysis of his "tutor's path" is a lot of self-awareness for a girl established as gloriously unexamining, but the page hedges it — "the kind of beat she would not catch until later" — so it half-earns the pass. The long stuck-opening earns itself: forty-plus minutes of circling so the release lands like oxygen. The title gave nothing away going in and detonated on the way out. And the book's title landed too, here, in five words: *May I call you* is the polite invitation itself.

What I want next: the call, the first date, and — dread — the house at the end of the long drive the jacket promised. I want Randi's interiority on that cup and I'm scared to get it. I want Friday's stats seat with this new thing to tell. And I keep thinking about her phone, facedown, not turned over: "She let herself want it to light up." So did I. That's the trap working on me.
