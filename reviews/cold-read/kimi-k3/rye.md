## Reader reaction

This is a small chapter, and it lands exactly where it needs to. After "Leave No Trace" — the mountain, the rain, the truck — all I wanted was the warm room with the cracked window, and that's what I got. Cassie's eyes going straight to the flannel before anything else; Vee letting her look. "You're alive." "No windowless van." The blood-type joke. I smiled the whole way through the opening.

What moves me most is watching Vee *edit for an audience of one who deserves everything and will get most of it.* She tells Cassie the boots, the blister, "he didn't make it weird" — then the blue shirt, "I think there's a whole situation under there," and Cassie's dry "You think" and Vee's "I'm reporting what I observed." That made me laugh out loud. And then: "And then we ate. She said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed.*" That line is the whole chapter in one sentence — for Vee, being fed *is* the intimacy, the sandwich on the summit is what the kiss was for other people. Rye bread, horseradish, cherries, "you carry out what you carried in" — she's internalized his leave-no-trace rule and carries it proudly, a thing he gave her without making her small, and she's already making it hers. It's tender and I noticed the title the moment the rye bread got named: this chapter is named for the sandwich, which is really named for *being fed well by someone who thought ahead.* "Rye" is shorthand for the whole day distilled into what mattered most to her — not the view, not even the truck, but the food made that morning and handed over. That's lovely and it tells me where Vee's heart actually lives.

And then the telling slows — "as she'd slowed at the tree the other night" — and Cassie goes still, and Vee gives her the truck. His dry shirt, him standing in the rain with his back turned, and then the part she could have skipped: "I knocked on the glass and I called him back in... I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." She's owning it to Cassie the way she owned the kiss: *I did it, I wanted it, nobody made me.* And Cassie is *rapt* — the book's word — and then just says her name. "Vee." Soft, "like setting something down where it wouldn't break." I felt my throat go. Cassie doesn't tease this one. She teases everything except what matters, and this matters, so she sets it down gently. "Borrow real boots next time." "I'm keeping the shirt." "Keep the shirt." Perfect, dry, full of love. I trust Cassie completely and this chapter is why.

But here's where my reader-position gets complicated, and the chapter knows it: Vee is telling the truth *as she knows it* — a man who thought ahead, who had a dry shirt ready, who gave her the truck and the shirt both, who looked at her "like I'd handed him something" and didn't touch. And it IS true. Pace's care on that trail was real; I read it happen. But I remember that this man debriefs Randi afterward, that Randi hung the "ravish against a tree" picture for Vee to walk home under, that the whole courtship has an architecture Vee can't see. So the flannel she's keeping, the shirt she's sleeping in presumably, is a trophy of a moment that was genuine on her side and *curated* on his. The chapter doesn't push that — it stays inside Vee's glow, Cassie's warmth, the cool room — and I let myself stay there too, because the glow is earned. But the unease hums underneath like Cassie's window-crack of cold air. "Keep the shirt" is sweet and it is also, unknowingly, Cassie endorsing a souvenir of a plan. That's not suspicion the text forced on me; it's just the ledger I carry that the women in the room don't.

Erotically this chapter is low — it's afterglow retold, not heat itself — but there's a warm ember in "he looked at me like I'd handed him something," the way she reports being seen and how it was *right*. The charge is in the memory, not the page. As romance, though, this nearly peaks for me: the flannel kept without asking, unasked-for in return — "He didn't ask for it back" — is its own quiet vow, and Cassie's soft "Vee" is the second romance, the friendship one, and it's just as good.

The book's title and volume title — *With a Long Spoon*, Book One, *A Polite Invitation* — keep accruing meaning. Vee is supping deep now, and the invitation (hiking, "May I choose?", the dry shirt) stays so polite, so careful, that the spoon's length is invisible to her. "Rye" as a chapter title pointed at nothing ominous — it gave nothing away, it *celebrated* — and that restraint is part of the spell.

What I want next: the shirt on Vee's body crossing paths with Randi or Brooke — someone in that sorority world who might recognize a man's flannel, or Pace's truck, and start a thread Vee can't see being pulled. And I want, half-dreading it, the debrief Randi will demand about *this* date — because I know it's coming, and Vee's story of the truck is not one Randi can hear neutrally. Guessing from here: the kept shirt becomes the object that trips something.
