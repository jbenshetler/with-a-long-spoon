## Reader reaction

This chapter did something to me. I came into it raw from the bathroom mirror and the cold dorm room — Randi's deadbolt, the held breath that no longer feels held — and then the book handed me this sunlit, windblown, grinning chapter, and I fell for it completely, and the falling is the ache. Because the whole afternoon *is* lovely. The maple burning orange in a field of undecided green, the walk, the game, her lit-up feet doing that half-second of happy footwork on the brick — I wanted this for her. I wanted her to have the wanting.

And the wanting is real and it's hers, that's the thing the chapter keeps insisting on and I believe it. The body's answer in bed — coming *hard* at the bare question, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — that landed on me as one of the most quietly erotic moments in the book so far, more than the bench, because it was involuntary and unarmored and he *witnessed* it without grabbing it. Pace asking again only after the heat left the room, wanting her "gathered back into herself... free to weigh it and choose" — I trust him in that moment. His tenderness is real. The kiss he doesn't wipe his face for, so her own taste reaches her mid-quad — that was the heat peak for me, actually, that breath catching against his lips.

And the pointing game itself is a masterclass of seduction-as-permission. I felt the grammar of it: he points, she picks. Her vetoes teaching him her taste — "too expected," the matched pairs ("you'd get both or neither," which made me laugh), the "God, no" at the girl who performs ease *at* people — each no sharpening the yes until Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her whole body, throat bare, and Randi's stride drops out from under her. That "Yes" arriving "with nothing around it" — I felt that in my own chest. And then Vee apologizing for a collision engineered against her, and Randi's hand staying on her arm "a half-beat longer than the apology required," and my stomach dropped. It's *beautiful* and it's predatory and it is both at once, in the same three seconds of execution, and the book lets it be both without flinching. I don't know yet whether I'm watching a woman reach for what she wants with the only tools she has, or watching the hunt she's been bred for. Both. Probably both.

The cheeseburger is the knife in the chapter. "I can't eat that" — easy, automatic — while she walks toward an appetite she finally gets to have. Pace sees it; he says "let's go feed you" at the end, and the echo of the thin apple slices, the portioned hunger, ran right through me. He is feeding her the one hunger and I keep thinking about the other one still trimmed to nothing.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no suspicion of Pace as a person — his care is consistent, his patience is real, he took the body's no-permission slipperiness seriously both times. What unsettles me is structural and the book earned it: Randi has reframed her desire as a gift *to him* ("not a woman who hungered... but a girlfriend offering her man a gift"), he knows exactly what she did and finds it "beautiful," and now a stranger named Vee — vivid, warm, unguarded, the only person in this book so far who laughs like she's forgotten she can be seen — has been selected without knowing she's in anything. Vee walks into this story utterly defenseless and I already want to protect her. That final line — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — made me actually catch my breath, and then he doesn't answer it, he just offers food. The deadbolt from the last chapter and this glowing grin sit side by side in me and won't resolve.

The title: "The Pointing Game" is exactly right and quietly sinister — a game that is also an aiming, a finger as instrument, and Randi insisting on calling it *his* "little game" while doing all the choosing. The book title and cover board — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — now read differently to me: the spoon is feeding, measured, someone else's hand on it, and "a polite invitation" is precisely what Vee is about to receive without knowing what it's attached to.

What I want next: Vee. Who she is, whether the stats class is real or already a door, and whether Randi's "with you" holds once there's an actual woman in the room. My dread, marked as a guess from here: that Randi will manage this the way she manages everything, and that managing it will cost Vee something before it costs Randi anything.
