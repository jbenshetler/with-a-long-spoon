## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter did something I didn't expect after "The Bench": it made me happy. Genuinely, bodily happy — the walk on the quad is one of the most pleasurable stretches of reading I've had in this book, and I want to say that plainly before I get to the splinters. The game itself is delicious. "I point. You pick." Four words that hand Randi the keys, and watching her learn she's holding them — the laugh she laughs "before she could decide whether to," the moment a quarter-mile in when she stops being a good sport and starts *shopping* — I was grinning the way she was grinning. There's real tenderness in how Pace builds it: he asks the question in bed first, watches her body answer before her mouth can make it "mean anything safer," then waits for the afternoon to go ordinary before asking it for real, because he wants her "free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did." I clocked that. That's the consent architecture I read this book for, and it's doing quiet, careful work.

And the opening scene is hot — the question landing in her body "before it reached her," the orgasm arriving "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it," her kissing herself off his lips "ardent before she could think better of it." That phrase, *before she could think better of it*, is the whole chapter's engine: Randi is a woman whose wanting keeps arriving ahead of her permission, and Pace has built a game that lets the wanting out in costume. I found the costume deeply moving, actually — "Only in a threesome. With you," her moving the appetite rather than taking it back, making herself "a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy" instead of "a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable." *Unsurvivable.* The chapter never mocks her for the wrapper. Pace sees through it — "he admired it, a little, even as he saw through it" — and lets her keep it. That felt like love, or at least like the most attentive thing I've seen him do.

The her-by-the-doors moment landed on me exactly as it landed on Randi. Vee arrives like weather — the copper hair the wind keeps playing with, the laugh with her "throat bare to the sky," a person who has "genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Of course that's the one. Of course the woman who doesn't manage herself is the one who stops the most managed woman on campus mid-stride. The bare "Yes" with "nothing around it" gave me a real shiver. And then — here's where my feelings get complicated — the collision. It is *gorgeous* as craft and faintly chilling as fact: Randi drifts the geometry closed across thirty feet, gives half a head, and it is *Vee's* hand that flies to *Vee's* mouth, Vee apologizing for an accident that was engineered. A decade of dance and three years of sorority "folded down into three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing." Pace admires it from his light pole, and I admired it too, and then I noticed I was admiring a woman deploying her instrument on someone guileless. Vee doesn't know she's been picked. She's warm and open and walking toward two people who have already discussed her. The chapter frames all this as a gift — "the gift was moving toward her hands" — and mostly I receive it that way, but a gift needs a recipient, and the recipient is currently a stranger laughing with her head back.

My trust in Pace is intact but annotated. "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That's a confident sentence, and the chapter believes it, and I mostly believe it too — her body *did* answer, twice — but it's the kind of sentence that could come back wearing a different face. He is, once again, deciding things beforehand ("He had decided this beforehand"; he chose the wall "fast and without seeming to"). His care is real and his control is real and the chapter keeps them braided so tightly I can't pull them apart, and I think I'm not supposed to yet. No suspicion that overrides the swoon — but the swoon has a shadow at its edge now, and the text put it there.

On the titles: "The Pointing Game" is literal and it works — it names the camouflage, the wrapper that lets the wanting come out "dressed as play, before she had to look it full in the face." Games have rules, and rules are what make Randi safe enough to want things. But the volume title is doing heavier work this chapter than before: *With a Long Spoon* — I know the proverb, and "Book One — A Polite Invitation" suddenly reads less like manners and more like bait on a hook. A polite invitation is what Randi is about to extend to Vee. The spoon proverb is about dining with something dangerous and keeping your distance — and I can't tell yet who's the devil at this table, Pace or Randi or the arrangement itself. No cover tagline was supplied, so I can't read that signal. The cheeseburger beat — "I can't eat that," automatic, no glance back, against "Let's go feed you" as the closing line — ties the appetite motif to the erotic one in a way that felt deliberate and landed hard: Randi polices every hunger, and Pace has spent three chapters finding ways to feed her around the police.

What I want next: the approach, the stats class, Vee's interior — I badly want to know who Vee is when she's not being looked at, because right now she's the only person in the book we've only ever seen from outside. What I dread (a guess from here): that Vee's openness, the exact quality Randi chose, is also the quality most easily used. And a quieter dread: Randi said "with you," and Pace heard the gift in it, but I don't know what happens to the gift if the wanting ever wants to leave him out of it.
