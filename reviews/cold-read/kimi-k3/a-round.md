## Reader reaction

I read this chapter with my hand over my mouth. This is the hottest thing the book has done — hotter than the bench, hotter than the truck — and it does it with a measuring tape and a fully dressed man, which tells you everything about what this book understands about heat. The architecture is the rhythm: *closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil.* I felt every withdrawal. The book had me standing on that box with her, and by the time he knelt for the waist measurement — "his breath arriving against her skin in small warm intervals she could count" — I was as gone as she was.

The shame engine is what makes it land instead of just smolder. Her body flooding the pale satin, the thumbprint becoming "big and dark and stupid," the eyes screwed shut waiting for the grin she's braced for her whole life — and then the reveal: he wasn't looking down at all. He was craned upward, waiting for her eyes, smiling "the smile of a man who had just received an unexpected gift." I actually ached. And the book earns it again immediately — the *hmm*, the sideways glance at her heel, her coming back down off her toes with that breathless almost-laugh. He didn't just absolve her; he refused to let her turn being forgiven into a new performance. That's the tenderest beat in the chapter and it has no name in it, just a sound in his throat.

And then the chapter does the thing I didn't know I wanted: it hands her the power. "Oops. Sorry" — low, bright, not remotely sorry — and this contained, careful man goes *red*, fought grin and all, can't look up without losing the line. I loved watching her spend the new coin. But the kiss on the pin-prick and then "Keep still," a beat too late, costing him something she can hear — that's Pace in one line. He declines the easy yes she's all but handing him because he said he'd make the dress right. Given what I know about him — the edging, "Not yet," the whole first chapter — I read that refusal with full knowledge she doesn't have: this is a man whose erotic native language is *delay*. She's discovering it as restraint; I know it as craft. Both are true on the page and the doubleness is delicious and faintly vertiginous.

Which brings me to the splinter the chapter itself planted, deliberately, right in her head: "a man who picked her — who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking." I stopped reading and just sat with that. Vee is using Randi as the *proof* of being chosen — the gold standard of desirability Pace walked past for her — and Randi is the person who pointed at Vee and said yes. The one fact Vee reaches for as evidence of the love story is the exact seam of the design, and she'll never feel the irony because nobody's told her. It doesn't sour the scene — the scene is genuinely radiant — but it hums under every "he saw me" the way the whole book hums. Also: the overheated house. Vee reads it as thoughtfulness staged hours ahead for her, and it melts her before he touches her. I remember Randi noting the too-warm house in chapter one, before Vee existed in this story at all. It may genuinely have been cranked early for the fitting — or the house just runs hot, and Vee is writing tenderness onto a thermostat. The book doesn't tell me, and I notice I'm doing exactly what Vee does: wanting the kind reading to be true.

The title: "A Round." The kneeling circuit he makes around her body, one slow round from hip to hip with the front of her as the final turn — but also, unmistakably, a *round* in a bout, and she won it: she made him laugh, stumped him ("pre-aroused every time I wore it" — I laughed out loud in the middle of all that tension), flushed him, nearly toppled him, and he stayed on his knees finishing her hem anyway. Round to Vee. And the furniture motif deepens: of course a woodworker's fitting room has a riser, "smooth as fine furniture," built to set a woman at the exact height of a kneeling man's face. After the bench — furniture whose true purpose reveals itself — I cannot look at anything this man builds as innocent. The riser did its real work today: it staged her shame at his eye level and then staged her absolution at his.

The erotic charge never goes slack, not once — even the tea-and-blanket interlude is charged, because it's when she discovers the slick satin and starts counting maybes she doesn't believe. If I have any friction, it's a breath of it in the early undressing, where the mother's-voice beats stack up slightly thick (*stripper*, *floozie*, the whole register) — but it pays off so completely in the eyes-shut sequence that I forgive it. What I want next: the dress finished, the induction, and — dreading it and craving it equally — the moment the invitation finally reaches her, because after this afternoon she is so far past the point of wanting to be asked that the *terms* of the asking are going to matter more than the asking itself.
