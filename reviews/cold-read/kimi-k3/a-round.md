## Reader reaction

This is the chapter the book has been building toward since the hike, and it worked on me completely — I want to say that first, before anything tidy. A woman takes her clothes off in good light for a man with a measuring tape, he never once does anything but his job, and it is the most charged sustained scene in the book by a distance. The engine is the withdrawal: "He came close, he touched her in two places, he held her inside that closeness for the length of a breath, and then he withdrew entirely" — "the withdrawal left the place his hands had been ringing." I felt that every single time. The peaks for me: the ticklish flinch and the grin that "arrived before he could stop it"; "Does this measurement change?" and his "I don't know. I've never thought about it" — a man treating her arousal as a spec worth getting right; the kiss on the pinprick, "before he'd decided to"; and above all the smile — she'd been dying with her eyes shut and he had simply been *waiting*, face craned up, not looking down, "the smile of a man who had just received an unexpected gift." And then "Keep still," a beat too late, with everything unsaid audible in it. Nothing is consummated. No kiss lands above her ribs. It's the hottest chapter of the book anyway.

**Pace.** I've lost my ability to hold him at arm's length, and I notice I've lost it. What gets me is the involuntary ledger — the grin he couldn't stop, the laugh surprised out of him twice, the flush up his neck, the kiss before deciding to. Every leak in his control is delight-shaped. On the page he earns everything. My suspicion is exactly where it was, but sharpened in a specific way: not of any act — he's scrupulous — but of the *architecture*. The thermostat turned up "hours before she came," the riser "set where the light fell," "the room arranged around it." Vee naturalizes it ("of course there's a riser in a woodworker's house") the way she filed the towels. I can't. This afternoon is a perfect machine for producing exactly what it produced — her maximal exposure, her maximal shame, his maximal tenderness, her gratitude, her first taste of power — and he never had to do one indecent thing. Whether that's the jacket talking or the page, I genuinely can't separate anymore. Also: the jacket says *Randi* is the one who draws out the shames she was raised to bury and hands them back as gifts. Pace just did that. Verbatim. Either the blurb misassigned it, or the method has a house style, and I find the second possibility chilling in a way I can't defend from the page alone.

**Vee.** The swimsuit history gutted me — "too much woman for the sport," the end of "the swimming she'd loved," never once a bikini because her mother made sure and then her own shame made sure. Her body's responsiveness itself is the core shame, "managed out of existence in public" her whole adult life, and this is the one house where it can't be managed. Watching her count the maybes on the couch and not believe any of them hurt. Watching the stoop come undone on the riser — "She had been making herself smaller without knowing it. She stopped" — is the best thing the book has done. And then her turn: "someone who'd just found out she had a kind of power she'd never been issued. She started spending it." The deliberate third lean, "Oops. Sorry," him going red. I was *proud* of her, and the pride and the dread are now the same feeling.

**Randi** is absent a fifth chapter but named — once, at the worst possible moment: "a man who picked her — who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking." Vee is using Randi as the benchmark she was chosen over, and the word "picked" is the jacket's own verb, sitting innocently in Vee's head. I wanted to put my hand over the page.

**The mother's voice** loses for the first time. Three intrusions — "stripper," "can he tell," "Floozie. Girls like that" — and each is answered not by argument but by his face. Her loudest silence last chapter; her loudest defeat this one.

Friction: almost none. "Plausible deniability." — naming the move outright is a touch winky. The mutual-unsaid formula ("Neither of them said anything") verges on tic by its fourth use. The knee-walk geography is occasionally hard to picture. The couch section sags slightly — by design, it's dread-building, and it pays. The chapter is long and earns every page.

**The title.** "A Round" sat oblique — a round of what, drinks? applause? — and lit up at the knee-walk: "he was working lower, and now he was working *around*." The tape goes around her, he circles her on his knees. Then the other meanings stacked: a round is a song built on saying the same line more than once — which rhymes with his error-correcting codes from the tour chapter — and a round is one bout of a match: she scored (made him laugh, stumped him, flushed him), he held, and her mother's voice lost its first round. It gave nothing away beforehand. **A Polite Invitation**: politeness is now the erotic medium itself — "politely not watching," the gentle plainness of "The bra too," every deniable touch. **With a Long Spoon**: the strongest pull yet — he touches her all afternoon at one remove, through tape and pins and silk, and the one unmediated touch (the kiss) is involuntary. The idiom about dining with the devil keeps hovering; I still can't settle who sups with whom. **The tagline**: this chapter is its proof-of-concept. The jeans, the shirt, the bra, the lean — every yes was hers. The riser, the light, the warm rooms weren't.

**Next.** I want the drive home and the impossible retelling — there is no cut of this she can give Cassie or Kayla, and I *dread* the harvest if Randi asks how it went. I expect the fittings to escalate and the bedroom door to open before the induction (guess, marked). I expect the first Randi-and-Pace scene eventually and I'm bracing for it (guess). And I keep thinking about her mother attending a dinner where Vee wears *this* dress.
