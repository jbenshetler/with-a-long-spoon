## Reader reaction

This chapter *landed* on me, fully. I was braced for the reconciliation to be stage-managed by Randi's script — and it was, structurally — but the script failed in the best possible way. The clean machine ran for one speech, and then Pace asked "But what happened?" and everything Randi built simply wasn't there anymore, and what came out instead was Vee's actual confession: "She handed me a lie and I picked it up because some part of me was just standing there waiting for one." That's the truest thing she's said in the book. And his answer — "Thank you for telling me what she said" — undid me. He thanks her for the why instead of accepting the what. This is the repair happening *off-script*, and it feels more real because of it.

The heel moment is exquisite. The missed-stair wrongness of being taller than him, her instant certainty that she'd committed one more disrespect — and his response: "Leave them on. Beauty like yours made for the gods and loaned out — I'm not going to insult the loan by complaining I have to look up." That line. It does everything: it forgives, it flatters, and it quietly canonizes the *loan* — he clocked the shoes as borrowed, I'm nearly sure of it, and whether he knows whose they are the page leaves ajar. And "the scarf was working" — her small absurd vanity surfacing mid-wreckage — made me laugh with recognition. The scarf IS working. Randi's whole outfit is working, and the page knows it and lets it work.

Then Sunday morning, and this is where the chapter went from tender to hot for me. The tickle, the swat she lands on him — and then his hand landing on *her*, wet from the dishwater, and her body answering before she does: the nipples, the silent *oh*, the thing crossing her face that she has "no joke for, no doctrine, no clean machine." That is the book's signature move — desire arriving ahead of the mind — now as a spank she didn't agree to want and absolutely wants. Her response, to cover what he saw with a kiss and fumble his button open, is so her. And the punchline: the pan left soaking, "exactly where it wanted to be." The title pays off there — soaking as a recognized method is surrender as domestic science, letting the water do the work, and she pulled him away from scrubbing "things that want to be left alone" and then was herself left alone by the water of the night. It's also Randi's creed — surrender as rest — surfacing in Vee's own mouth as a joke about dishes. That gave me a shiver of doubleness: she's living Randi's doctrine and doesn't know it.

The smaller devastations: his T-shirt folded on the bed for her to find, "gone a little translucent" — the totem returned, but this time given, not stolen. Her smelling of him head to foot and going "a little still over it." And the ending — *"You're always beautiful,"* not *you're beautiful*, "which she'd have argued" — he has learned the exact grammar that gets past her defenses. I swooned, plainly. But the sour note I carry: this man had her watched Thursday and never mentions it; she stood in his hall naked in her confession while he held back the fact that he already knew she'd left alone. The asymmetry didn't ruin the chapter for me — it just sat under it, like the pie label: a joke over something too naked. Also noted, quietly: "she could not walk into a lecture hall in a Saturday-night dress" — the walk-of-shame logistics the script didn't fully cover; Monday arrived "sideways," and the dorm — Cassie — is waiting.
