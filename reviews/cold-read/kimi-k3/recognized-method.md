## Reader reaction

This chapter wrecked me in the best way — and underneath the wreckage there's a hum I can't unhear.

Start with the swoon, because the swoon is real. The apology scene is one of the most tender things in the book. The HUMBLE PIE label made at the stats-lab printer at three in the morning — I loved her so much for that, for wanting "a way to walk in carrying her contrition out in front of her where they could both look at it." And then the heel moment: the missed-stair wrongness of looking *down* at him, her composure coming apart, and him lifting the pie out of her hands "the way you take a glass from someone about to drop it" and setting the scarf back over her shoulder. "Leave them on." And later, the thing that undid me entirely: he doesn't say *it's fine*. He says "Thank you for telling me what she said." He thanks her for the *why*. And the reconciliation speech she actually gives — "she handed me a lie and I picked it up because some part of me was just standing there waiting for one" — that's the deepest self-knowledge Vee has ever spoken aloud. That's her mother's voice, her whole defensive crouch, named. I was proud of her and I was melted and the morning-after "You're always beautiful" — *always*, so she can't argue it — finished me off.

And then the kitchen. The tickle, her swat ("No. Tickling."), his one answering open hand — and her body answering before she can. This is the erotic peak of the book for me so far, hotter than the consummation in ch 18, because it's *discovery*: "her body saying two or three things at once that she had not agreed to have it say." The way she covers — kiss, motion, chatter, "a quiet room was a room with a question standing in it" — and the way he refuses to be covered from, his hand finally settling flat and warm over the print of itself, no strike, nothing new, just *This?* And her "*please*," attached to nothing, out of the bottom of her voice. I felt that in my spine. And the consent of it — oblique, wordless, but real: he asks with his hand and his eyes, she answers with please, and both of them "left it there." The second orgasm "she could not file" — because it had a cause and the cause was his hand on the spot where he had hit her — that's the book's whole shame-into-wanting engine turning over at full throttle.

But here's the hum. I know things. I know Randi dressed her for this apology — the shoes, the scarf, the *timing*, "go in sure, not sorry." I know Randi sat on Pace's chest in "Gone" and said "She's not ready, though. She'll get there. You'll want to take your time" — and this chapter is Vee *getting there*. So when Pace's door opens before her finger touches the bell, when the porch light is already on, I can't read it as pure sweet anticipation. Randi told him. Her apology was scheduled between the two of them. And "beauty like yours made for the gods and *loaned out*" — *loaned*. Vee never told him the heels were borrowed. He looked down at the cognac straps and he *knew*. The word slipped and she didn't catch it and I did, and it put ice in the middle of the warmest scene in the book.

And the tickle itself — in ch 17 Randi fed Vee the story of being tied down with a scarf and *tickled* until she went off like a rocket, and this weekend Vee walked in wearing Randi's scarf, got tickled, and found out her body wants what Randi's story said bodies want. Was the tickle spontaneous? "The twist was the wrong way, or it was exactly the wrong way" — the narration winks, and I don't know whether it's winking at me. Pace has done this before; he built a bench for it. His stillness at the sink, watching her face, was the stillness of a man who has *seen that exact face before* — on Randi, in ch 1, "the face was lying." He is finding Vee the way he found Randi. And Randi sent her to him for it. The chapter title lands like a bell: *A Recognized Method.* On the page it's about soaking dishes — "you let them sit and the water does the work" — but it's also the whole design. You don't scrub a thing that wants to be left alone. You warm the house and wait. The pan soaking in the cooling water at the end is the book's thesis in a kitchen sink.

The mirrored "This?" gutted me on reflection: in "Rock," *she* touched his scar and asked "This?" and he said *not tonight*, and kept her hand there. Now *he* asks "This?" and she says *please*. He keeps his door; hers opens. That asymmetry is the relationship in one gesture, and I don't know yet whether it's tragedy or just timing.

Cassie's absence is loud. Vee has now slept in his shirt, washed in his shampoo, "smelled of him head to foot, nothing of her own left anywhere on her skin" — a line the chapter means as swoon and I received as both swoon and quiet horror. She's being absorbed, lovingly, by two people, and the one person with clean eyes is three feet away and unawakened.

The title of the book — WITH A LONG SPOON — keeps accruing: you sup with the devil using a long spoon, and Vee is being fed, exquisitely, at a table she can't see. "A Polite Invitation" — she still doesn't know there was one. What I want next: the induction, the dress, her mother in the room with the daughter in burgundy silk. What I dread (a guess from here): that the scarf and the shoes are how Randi eventually stands *inside* the room with them — and that some piece of Vee, the piece that said *please*, is already being dressed for it.
