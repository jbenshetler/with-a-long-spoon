# Cold read (grounded) — The Reach

## Reader reaction

God, this chapter. It's eleven pages of a girl alone in a bunk bed and it held me harder than most of the sex scenes in this book have. Because it's not really a masturbation scene — it's the scene where Vee stands at the edge of something and doesn't jump, and the not-jumping is the most erotic, most honest thing I've read in fifty-odd chapters.

The structure wrecked me quietly. She *closes Cassie's window* — defends her own territory for once, shuts the two inches Cassie defends all winter — and builds herself a sealed little hothouse, and the chapter keeps telling me what the shut room is for before Vee will say it: the smell can't leave, the sound can't leave, everything she makes stays in the air with her. She's engineering conditions. She's Pace at the stove, except the dish she's preparing is herself, and she doesn't know the recipe yet. And then the reach — fingers wet to the knuckles, lifted the last breath's width to her own mouth, tongue coming forward — and *stopping*. "She could not close the distance." I made a sound out loud. The book has spent two chapters having Randi plant this seed with the lightest possible hand — *I don't. It was powerful. It turned me on* — and here is the seed in the dark, germinating, and Vee cannot name what it's growing toward.

And the thing that guts me: the reach isn't only toward her own taste. It's toward Randi. "Had Randi's looked like this? Sticky too. Shining. Had she meant a mouth carrying the wet back to her? Or her own fingers, lifted to her mouth?" That question is not about hygiene. That's a woman trying to picture her best friend's arousal and calling it research. Vee files it under curiosity about a kiss, but the chapter won't let her — the last lines refuse the filing: "A mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers." Randi is *in the fantasy's grammar now*, not as audience the way she was in the bathroom stall on Tuesday, but as the imagined body. And Vee ends the chapter with "her mouth was still empty" — which is the most loaded sentence in the whole book so far, an emptiness she's going to spend the rest of this story trying to fill, and I don't think it's Pace's mouth she ultimately wants carrying it.

The wet patch detail destroyed me in the best way. She wipes her hand on the sheet, twice — her excess, her shameful plenty, the thing her mother's voice calls *greedy* — and then, at the very end, she goes back and finds the damp patch with her fingertips and "settled her hip over the dark place." Her mess. Hers to lie in. That's the whole arc of the book in one gesture: the plenty that was her shame becoming the ground she sleeps on. She couldn't put it in her mouth yet, but she can lie in it. That's the distance she closed tonight.

And Cassie's window being opened again at the end — she shuts Cassie's cold out to do this, then reopens it herself when the room holds too much of her — reads to me like the exact shape of Vee's double life right now. The cold blade of air is Cassie, is the dorm, is the world that would smell what she's been doing. She needs it and she bars it and she lets it back in when she's done.

Trust/suspicion: my suspicion of Randi, which the last few chapters have been feeding steadily, sharpens here — because I can now see the whole design. "It was powerful. It turned me on" was not a confession offered for Vee's comfort; it was a key cut for a lock Randi has been building since October. And yet — and this is why I can't hate her — the chapter Vee has alone with that planted sentence is genuinely the freest, most self-belonging scene Vee has ever had. Randi's manipulation is producing Vee's liberation. The book keeps making me hold both and I keep holding both.

Friction: almost none. If anything the chapter is a touch patient in its middle — the fantasy-replay of the kitchen scene retreads ground I read in full two chapters ago — but I understood it as Vee's own replaying, her mind doing what minds do, so it earned its length. The one line I'll carry: "a hothouse with its doors shut, the air around a tropical flower without its sweetness." That's the whole book's olfactory imagination in one image.

Titles: "The Reach" is perfectly, cruelly literal — the reach of her hand toward her own mouth that stops a breath short — and I read it as the book's title made small: *With a Long Spoon*, supping with the devil requires a long spoon, and here the spoon isn't long enough, or she isn't ready for it to be. A reach that fails is still a reach; the distance got measured tonight, and distances measured get crossed. Book Two's "A Warm Reception" keeps proving itself — every warm room in this book is a room someone made warm on purpose, and tonight Vee made one for herself, which is new and maybe the most important thing that happened.

What I want next: I want Tuesday. I want Vee across the table from Randi carrying this — and I dread it, because Vee tells Randi everything, and Randi will hear "I couldn't do it" and know *exactly* how close the girl came, alone, in the dark. Whether Randi says the right true thing or the right strategic thing — and whether Vee can tell the difference — is where this is all going.
