## Reader reaction

This chapter is small and quiet and it knocked the wind out of me.

It's one room, one girl, one window — and it's the loneliest Vee has ever been on the page. Alone, for the first time, in her own bed, in Pace's flannel, doing the thing she's now done in how many bathrooms — but this time the chapter isn't about getting off. It's about the reach. Her hand coming up, the breath's width, and the stop. "Her tongue came forward. / And stopped." I actually held my own breath there, waiting, and when her hand dropped and she wiped her fingers on the sheet beside her hip I felt the failure land in my stomach like a stone. Not failure to come — she comes, hard, sound swallowed by the shut glass — but failure to close the distance to herself. The one her body had already closed twice: the wet kiss, the clench around him before anyone voted, the second clench in the retelling. Her body said yes in two separate rooms. Her own mouth, alone with no witness to perform for, couldn't finish the sentence.

What guts me is that she's doing all the work correctly. She talks herself through it in exactly the voices she's been given — Pace's gladness ("as if the plenty of her required no pardon"), Randi's flat verdict ("It was powerful. It turned me on"), even Randi's absolution, "hungry out loud" — which "felt like an absolution then" and now, alone, "was harder to believe." That line is the chapter's hinge. Randi's permissions work beautifully across a white cloth, with wine, with those dark liquid eyes listening. Alone in the dark they go thin. And Vee, who has been told she picked well, who has been handed every step of her own awakening as if it were hers, is left doing arithmetic with borrowed numbers and coming up short by one breath.

The detail that undid me: she wipes her fingers on the sheet, then has to lie in it. "Her mess. When she came back to bed, she would have to lie on it." That's her mother's whole lesson in one sentence — your plenty stains, and the stain is yours to sleep in. And then the window. God, the window. She opens Cassie's defended two inches — the exact thing she shut at the chapter's start, claiming the room for the radiator, for the enclosed heat she needed to do this in — and January comes in "like water through a broken door," and the room gets aired out so Cassie won't smell her. Even her shame is housekeeping. She hides the evidence in the cold and climbs back into bed, and the last line — "Her mouth was still empty" — reads like the book quietly scoring a debt. Something wanted in and didn't get in. It's still out there, one breath away, and it's not going anywhere.

And here's where my feelings move: this chapter turned the "missed a spot" morning around and showed me its underside. In that chapter Vee tidied the kiss away — he must have forgotten to wipe, poor luck, a funny footnote for Randi. I let her have that tidiness because she seemed to. Now I see the tidy explanation didn't hold four days. The question she wouldn't chase — "how many times, and how carefully" — has been quietly working on her, and the page lets me know something Vee doesn't quite: the kiss was not forgotten. His mouth came to hers wet every single time for months, and exactly once he didn't wipe first. That isn't luck. I don't know what it is — patience, curriculum, care, all three at once — but the chapter made me feel its intentionality without ever saying it, and I trust the man less for it and ache for him more for it, somehow both. He is the only person in this book who has ever looked at her "plenty" without flinching, and he is also the one engineering her toward it. Both things keep being true.

On Randi: the reach fails, notably, *after* Randi has supplied the verdict. Randi could say "I don't" across a table and make it mild and settled. Vee could not say it to herself alone. The chapter never blames Randi — there's nothing to blame; the words were kind and true for Randi — but the gap between what Randi's confidence can do and what Vee's own body can authorize is the whole tragedy of their friendship so far, and it made me sad for both of them. Vee will go back to the loft on Tuesday and report, or not report, this. I suspect (a guess from here) that she tells it — she tells Randi everything — and that Randi will hear "I couldn't" as an invitation.

The title: "The Reach." Literally the reach of her hand to her own mouth, one breath's width, unclosed. But it's also the reach Pace makes toward her across months of patience, and the reach Randi makes through Vee's own voice — everyone reaching Vee except Vee. And the book's title, "With a Long Spoon," plus this volume's "A Warm Reception," now read colder to me than they did: a warm reception is exactly what Vee gives everything served to her, and the long spoon is the distance she keeps — has to keep — between herself and what she already wants. She sups beautifully. She just can't yet put her own hand to her own mouth.

I want Tuesday. I want, dreadfully, the version of this story that gets told across that table.
