# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

*scene: scenes/leave-no-trace.md · model: kimi-k3 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch011 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

This one undid me a little. I went in braced for the arranged-ness of it — I know what she doesn't, that Randi hung this exact picture for her at the Smoothie Bird, "he's going to hand you up over the rocks" — and the hike plays out almost line for line on Randi's script. But what the chapter does with that is give Vee the reins anyway, and I found myself not caring whose picture it was, because the girl inside it was so entirely herself.

Pace moved me here, more than he ever has. The moleskin. I was as clenched as Vee was when he reached for her boot — her mother's voice arriving on schedule, *what kind of girl lets a man* — and then he just... fixed it, patted the toe, apologized for not asking about the boots at the lot. The sandwich construction ("lettuce against the bread"), the cherries, the pit-picking — God, that moment. She spits a pit onto the rock, the voice supplies *common*, and he just says "we take nothing and leave nothing," gentle, picks it up without looking at her, and lets her arrive at the wax paper herself, "not looking, sometime in between." That is the most tender piece of teaching I can remember reading. And then the flannel — his only dry shirt, handed over with nothing said, and then he gets out and stands in the rain with his back turned so she can change. I know what this man is concealing from her and I still couldn't hold out against the care. The trust is growing faster than the wariness, and I notice the book letting that happen.

And the truck scene. This is where the chapter burned. Vee on her knees on that unbroken bench seat — *bench*, I clocked the word — rapping on the window, crooking her finger, and unbuttoning for him with her eyes on his, choosing to look down so his eyes follow hers. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice" — the shame wiring straight into the arousal, *floozy* in her mother's exact word and her thighs pressing together. That's the most honest rendering of how hunger and shame braid that I've gotten from this book yet, and it's hot precisely because it's hers — her initiative, her exception, her body offered and his stillness receiving it: "he sat and looked at all of her and didn't move, and the gladness in him was the whole of the answer." And then the turn that broke my heart a little: she reaches for his hand to start the next thing and it's *shaking*. He'd been standing in the rain, freezing, giving her the dry shirt and the privacy, the whole time. Her pulling his hand under the flannel to her belly — the caretaking reversing — was more intimate to me than the undressing.

The ending stung in the right way. She's about to put "a small thing where the big thing went" — something true, I think, maybe *the* thing — and the campus cop's siren eats it. And I couldn't help hearing Cassie's state-troopers joke come back around, the joke turning real at the exact wrong second. She goes inside wearing his shirt. There's no way that shirt goes back to him without everything going further.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee and Pace, alone for the whole chapter; the campus cop appears only as lights and a siren. **Mentioned only:** Meg (the borrowed boots), Cassie (the scream in the hall, the dress money), Vee's mother and father, Randi (via the remembered "ravish").

**Heat:** 3 — the cab scene dominates; her deliberate stripping under the mother's voice is the hottest, most charged thing in the book so far.

**Romance:** 3 — a real turn in the bond: her offered body, his stillness, her warming his shaking hand; devotion passed both ways without a word of it said.

**Motifs & images:** Food/hunger again but inverted — here the hike *makes* the hunger and the food answers it (sandwich, horseradish, the shared bottle, the cherries and their pits). The mother's voice recurs as ever, but new: now it *is* the heat. Being looked at vs. seen, twice — he watches her face in front of the valley, not the valley; then she conducts his eyes over her body. His hands (the shaking one, held to her belly). Water everywhere — the lake "holding the silver sky," the rain, her pool-bred lungs fine while her legs burn. The word *bench* on the truck seat. Cassie's state-troopers joke made literal. The russet dress set down "on the rock between them" — a new image of a want she shelves. Blue shirt against the brown woods, swallowed at the summit.

**Symbolism:** The title does the work: "we take nothing and leave nothing" is Pace's trail ethic, offered gently as a lesson — but it lands double. This whole courtship is built to leave no trace (Randi's collision, the staged coffee, the debriefs Vee can't see), and I couldn't shake the dread question of what trace it will leave on *her*. Also: the cop arriving precisely as she tries to say the true thing — the world interrupting honesty, again.

**Characterization:** Vee deepened enormously — agency, hunger, shame, and caretaking all in one chapter, all consistent with the girl from the pool and the dinner. Pace deepened too: the prep (sandwiches, moleskin kit, dry shirt in the gym bag) reads as the same exactness that built the bench, now spent entirely on her comfort — and the man who won't open a closed door also won't lift a hand she hasn't directed. Randi, absent, is quietly thickened by how completely Vee is walking inside her script without knowing it.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Long and fully earned; the climb earns the summit, the summit earns the cab. No drag. One small stumble on the page — "A drop hit the edge of her sight and she didn't believe it. she Another" — a broken line that tripped me mid-scene.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building hard. After two chapters of Randi-frame intimacy (the smoothie date), this hands the book back to Vee and Pace and advances them further in one day than the whole courtship before it — and the interrupted goodbye guarantees the next chapter opens mid-sentence. I'm reading on immediately, and I'm dreading the shirt's return.
