# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter undid me, and I want to say that plainly before anything else: it undid me with *her* power, not his. The whole back half of it — the cold cab, the rain on the roof, Vee up on her knees on the bench seat with the flannel folded beside her, rapping on the glass to call him back — is the bravest and hottest thing this book has given me. She's the one who decides. She puts her eyes on his and holds them there so he can't mistake it; she does the buttons herself; she looks down on purpose so his eyes will follow hers and *see what he gets to see*. "She gave it to him to see." I felt that in my sternum. And the book braids the shame right into the heat without letting the shame win — *floozy* arriving in her mother's exact word and her thighs pressing together on their own, the voice becoming the heat instead of killing it. That's the truest rendering of desire-under-policing I've read in a long time. And then — the detail that broke me — she reaches for his hand to start the next thing and finds it *shaking*. He stood in the rain for her and never said a word. The seducer turning instantly into the caretaker, pulling his freezing hand under the flannel against her belly. I am completely swept.

And Pace. God. The moleskin scene had me already — crouched in front of her on the trail, her bare foot in his hand in the cold air, her whole body braced for a transaction (the mother's voice arriving with its *what kind of girl lets a man* —) and he just... warms the moleskin with his thumb, re-laces the boot, gives the toe a pat that means only *done*. He apologizes for not asking about the boots. Then at the summit he lets her catch him looking at her face instead of the valley, and *doesn't look away*, and then gives her the rest of the looking alone. The sandwich engineering ("Lettuce against the bread. Keeps the wet from going through") — this man's love language is preparation, and it lands on me every time. And the dress. When she sets the russet silk down on the rock between them — "It'll be good enough for this one" — and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and *doesn't pick it up* — I swooned and my stomach dropped at the same time. Because I've read this book for ten chapters now. He noticed. He will do something about that dress. And when the russet silk shows up — and I am guessing here, but I feel it coming — it will be the most romantic thing that has ever happened to her and one more gift whose cost she can't see. The tenderness is real. The design is real. I keep having to hold both.

The title is where my unease lives now. "Leave No Trace." On the page it's the trail ethic — "We take nothing and leave nothing" — and the cherry pit she spits onto the rock, the flash of the *common* word from her mother's voice, and Pace laying his own pit beside hers without looking at her do it, teaching her the rule without making her small to learn it. Beautiful. But I've been watching two people conduct an entire courtship engineered to leave no trace of itself — a collision that read as her fault, a meeting that read as chance — and I cannot hear that phrase innocently anymore. That's what a good design does: takes everything, leaves nothing. The title turned from sweet to cold in my hands and I think it's supposed to.

Randi is nowhere in this chapter — not even mentioned — and somehow that makes her more present for me, not less. Vee climbed a whole mountain with one man and never once thought of her dear friend, and I sat there the whole time knowing. Every freely given yes of Vee's — and this one is the freest, the most hers, the one she authored from hunger and rain and gladness — is also a deepening of what the reveal will cost her. The chapter is generous enough to let me feel the first thing fully; the second thing just sits in my chest like a stone.

Small frictions: almost none. The campus cop felt like a thumb on the scale — siren barking exactly through her sentence, "Pace—" swallowed, so the thing she wanted to say stays unsaid and the truck pulls away with a cop trailing it out of the fire lane. Contrived, maybe, but I'll allow it, because what it leaves behind is perfect: Vee alone under the overhang in a man's flannel, soaked, holding her balled wet shirt, the moleskin still holding on her heel. Also — small catch of dread — she is walking into her dorm wearing *his shirt*. Cassie is in that dorm. And Cassie once asked his name for a state-trooper friend and got "off six-fourteen, past the gravel." Vee still doesn't know his last name, has never seen his house, and has now undressed for him in a truck. The cop's lights strobing across the back glass felt like the world starting to notice, and Sloane already noticed the truck chapters ago.

The cover line sits heavier each chapter: *With a Long Spoon* — you sup with the devil, you want a long spoon — and *A Polite Invitation*, which is exactly what this whole book is: the politest possible invitation, "May I" all the way down, into something the jacket can't quite be saying. This chapter was the invitation working flawlessly. What I want next: Cassie's eyes on that flannel. The induction dinner — her name read at the front of a room, the black dress or, my guess, not the black dress. And I dread, with real weight, the day Vee learns who set the table. Not because the heat was false — it wasn't, that's the horror of it — but because "on purpose" was the thing she was proudest of.
