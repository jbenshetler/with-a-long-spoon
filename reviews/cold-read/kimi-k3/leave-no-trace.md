## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me. After two chapters inside Randi's bright room, Pace comes back in the flesh and he is — there's no way around it — flawless, and I felt the flawlessness as both seduction and threat, which is exactly the vise the jacket built for me. On the page he earns nothing but trust: "He walked at the speed of her not knowing how"; the crouch, the moleskin, "Should've done that at the lot. I'm sorry"; the pit picked up "and didn't look at her doing it"; "That sounds like a beautiful dress" with no weight on it — he doesn't rescue the dress, doesn't buy it, just lets the wanting exist. Then he hands over his *only dry shirt* and goes and stands in the rain with his back turned. The text gives me zero to suspect. Everything I fear about him comes off the jacket, and the dissonance has never been louder — because then the cab scene happens, she calls him back with a crooked finger, gives him everything with her eyes held on his, and he *doesn't take*. Doesn't lean, doesn't lift a hand. And then the kicker: his hand is shaking. He's been shivering the whole time, "a long way from the question of whether he was cold." The man who stops keeping track of himself — that line from the retold dinner — enacted in his own body. It's the first uncontrolled thing I've ever seen from him, and per the jacket ("as real for the two who started the game") it might be the realest.

Vee: her bravest, most self-authored hour. She builds the display herself — kneels, tucks the curl, knocks, holds his eyes, and then the move that broke me: "She looked down anyway, and his eyes went with hers." She *directs* his looking. And the voice finally speaks in full sentences — "Girls who do this. Girls in trucks" — and for the first time it isn't obeyed or filed, it's *fuel*: "the voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." That's the jacket's "wanting she'd have been ashamed to name," named, to herself: "I want him to see me."

Erotic charge: peak of the book, and it peaks without a single touch — the unbuttoning sequence, "floozy" in her mother's exact word, thighs pressing. Then the collapse into caretaking ("You're freezing") converts heat to tenderness without killing it. If anything went slack, the sandwich paragraph is the most luxuriant food-writing yet and I half-noticed its indulgence — and then the horseradish gasp won me back.

Friction: only Pace's edgelessness, and I only notice it because the jacket taught me to. And one flicker — "had no house here to go to" — which I read as *far away*, down that gravel road.

The title: it lands as trail ethic, the cherry pits on wax paper — then recolors dark. Leave no trace is the affair's ethic; nothing taken, no fingerprints. And she keeps carrying traces home — the flannel, the moleskin "holding," like the empty pink cup before them. The book's title sharpens too: this whole courtship is a series of polite invitations he never cashes in. *With a Long Spoon* still mostly sits at the edge of my hearing — something about dining carefully with what might hurt you — I'll leave it there. The tagline now has a third voice: the yes that undresses itself, freely, in a cold truck, with nothing to blame.

Next: I dread the bright room. Does she hand Randi the cab scene, and does Randi take its authorship the way she took the kiss's? The russet dress is a loaded gun — my guess: it comes back, and from whose hand matters terribly. The flannel has to be returned. And what was the sentence the siren took?
