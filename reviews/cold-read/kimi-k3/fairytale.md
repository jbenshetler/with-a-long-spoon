## Reader reaction

The cut wrong-footed me for exactly one page. After the door closing in "Believe Me" — Vee in the cold, Pace calling her name once — the book opens on stone columns and beveled glass and Randi's father being hearty in Gstaad. No Vee, no Pace. Then the chapter took me apart.

The breakfast table told me Randi's whole life in one course: "She made them up for him, the boys who chased her… The invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table." Her mother hugs "all edges and points," exquisite, tennis bracelet catching light — and Randi "kept it out of the light," her own plain white-gold band, "locked on her wrist." I noticed that before I had any idea why. And "eating her revulsion" as she hugged her father — a dark, unexplained phrase the page just leaves there. I clocked it.

John undid me as designed. The lift-line con — "I'm with her… Play along" — the invented wife-game, three imaginary children at ski camp, and then the black run where I fell for him properly: "He could have left her behind whenever he wanted, and didn't… he had the run to spare and spent none of it proving it." The waltz, the sleigh he conjured past the cab line, "here he was writing another page of it." I believed the fairytale right along with her.

Then, mid-waltz, with zero fanfare: "He was a better dancer than Pace." I stopped reading and went back. Pace. Randi knows his lead from inside her own body — "the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for and good enough to catch" — and at the hotel door it got worse: "Pace had never assumed anything in his life. Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." *Every time.* My stomach dropped. Randi has been with him. And every brunch I've logged, every extracted scene-file, every tier of photographs held in her own hand rearranged itself in one flash: she has been eating, in weekly installments, the love life of the man who "found the person she kept hidden and did not look away — and he would never once belong in the life she had built to keep her hidden." She had him, or something of him, and chose the cover. This is also the first time I have ever seen Pace from outside Vee's hunger — and Randi corroborates all of it. The sight is real. Two women, same testimony. That steadied something in me about him even as his silence stands.

The night's verdict gutted me: "a good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon: the pleasure real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared." The lipstick ring left on him, "felt her own mouth do it from a long way off." "The eyeliner had held. Of course it had. Nothing had happened to her eyes." And the cold — "the cold did not belong to the room. It was hers." The book's whole heat-and-cold current, which I had mapped onto Vee choosing cold exits, turns out to run through Randi as climate: she carries it in and carries it out. "The warmth she could borrow and could not make, and stayed." Vee walks out of warmth on purpose; Randi stays in warmth she can't make. That rhyme wrecked me.

And then Vee. "Vee was her gorgeous." The one scenario Randi can't run to the end — Vee's face while being told. "Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways." So the doubled sidewalk kiss, "best friend in the whole world" — Randi's side is love under cover, and her decision is: keep the brunches exactly as they were. The text exchange is the kill shot — Vee's real warmth from Ohio answered with the fairytale, "Every word of it true. None of it the thing," plus one smuggled true line, then the phone turned facedown.

The title "Fairytale" is the Gstaad production, the daughter-story, the invented boys, and the lie-text — and it recolors Vee's whole thread: the nameless warm house is the real thing the fairytale counterfeits. The tagline flexes to fit her: every yes all night was freely given; the trap is the life that says yes on schedule.

Friction: almost none. "Eating her revulsion" is a heavy unpaid plant; the cliff-abandon of the rupture is a real withhold I felt; and Vee's untroubled text leaves me unsure whether this night is before or after the confrontation. What I want next, dreading: the next brunch. Guess from here: the locked bracelet is Pace's mark; John gets kept as cover.
