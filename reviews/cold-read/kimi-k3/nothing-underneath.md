## Reader reaction

Oh, this one. I read the whole second half with my hand half over my mouth. The coat plan survived contact — more than survived. The reveal on the doorstep, "the wall was simply gone," and then the look — "like a starving man... like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — that's the moment I've been waiting for since December, and it landed exactly as hard as I needed it to. He went to his knees on cold boards in January. The door banging open the whole time, neither of them caring. I was rooting so hard it hurt.

And then the chapter did the thing that made me love it instead of just enjoy it: pancakes at midnight, him on one knee, "I missed you so much," voice cracking. Food as the under-language, fully paid off — "made and not poured." That's the word he'd never say, said the only way he can. She notices, too: "nothing needed saying now." And "Vee" — her name, "easy and warm in his mouth," the same name that wasn't enough in December, now enough. I cried a little with her, honestly.

The erotic charge was the highest of the book for me — the mirror scene alone, her denying herself on purpose ("not hers to finish... it left her feeling, of all things, powerful"), then the drive, the near-arrest (my heart actually spiked — the fake-out with the cruiser was cruel and perfect), and the doorstep itself, "strung the length of her between the two," cold air on wet nipples. It never went clinical; it stayed *hers*, her wanting, her authorship — even when the blueprint was Randi's.

Small frictions: Kayla's laundry monologue ran a touch long while I was dying alongside Vee, though "Don't do anything I wouldn't do" earned it. And the horn music — "no man put on music like that for a woman" — felt like the page quietly killing the blonde one more time; fine, but I noticed the thumb a little.

The title, "Nothing Underneath": literal — nothing under the coat — but also what the night proved: no wall underneath his formality, no lie underneath her offering, nothing held back anywhere. And the book's title, *A Polite Invitation* — this was the least polite invitation imaginable, and it worked where every polite word failed. The series title *With a Long Spoon* still sits ominous to me; I keep waiting for what the spoon is for.

What I dread next: the word was still never said. He said "I missed you," not the other thing. And Cassie's "Hmm" — the reckoning pending just got a rolled-cuff exhibit. Also: Vee walked into that house wearing Randi's shoes, executing Randi's plan, and Randi knows everything and Vee knows nothing. The reconciliation is real and I believe in it — and it's built on a fault line.
