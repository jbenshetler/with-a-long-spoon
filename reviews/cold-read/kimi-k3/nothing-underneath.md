## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me. I came in braced — the whole plan was one card with nothing smaller behind it — and the book spent the first half winding the tension so expertly that the door opening felt like a held breath finally let out. The mirror scene is the erotic center before the erotic center: Vee alone, doing Pace's hands for him, denying herself the finish "on purpose" and feeling *powerful* — that inversion, denial as sovereignty, is the chapter's whole thesis. And the police-light fake-out: I genuinely gasped, and the cruelty of leaving her "denied a second time" is delicious. Then the door. "The wall was simply gone... so plainly glad and so plainly wrecked" — starving man and forgiven man as one look. I was rooting for them and I got them, fully, on the doorstep, in the cold, the door banging open the whole time. The reunion earns its explicitness because it's the argument words couldn't make — "the mouth that had spent no word she needed on her in December giving her everything it had now." And "Vee." — just her name, and it nearly broke her again. The pancakes section might be my favorite: him on one knee with a cracked voice saying "I missed you so much," her with a mouth full of pancakes. I cried a little, honestly. My unease is quiet but standing: the word still wasn't said — "I missed you" is close, close enough that she decided "no room... for wanting anything he hadn't given," which the page itself flags as tonight-only. And the blonde arrived one last time at the door and "could get no grip" — the lie is dying but the Sheri error still circulates. The title "Nothing Underneath" is perfect and double: the coat, yes — but also what the wall turns out to have underneath it, nothing but gladness and wreckage. A Polite Invitation keeps recoloring: this chapter is the opposite of polite — an impolite, total offering that worked. From here I want: whether the word ever comes, and Cassie's one raised eyebrow says the homecoming edit is already written for her.
