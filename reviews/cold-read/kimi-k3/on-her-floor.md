## Reader reaction

Oh, it happened. Fifty chapters — one hundred and whatever pages of brunches and shoe-fittings and double-press goodbyes and bathroom-stall stops — and it finally happened, and the page delivered it the way I'd been half-dreading and wholly hoping: exquisitely, meltingly, and then poisoned on the way out. I am still ringing.

Let me start with the sound the chapter makes before the kiss even arrives: "Yes." Vee's yes to the party, out "before the question had finished," a yes "with no floor under it." I have now watched Randi collect yeses like this twice in two chapters — last time it was "bare," the waxing, and the yes spent before the question could be amended. I noticed the echo immediately and it sat in my stomach the whole chapter, because everything that follows — the borrowed skirt, the skirt that won't stay down, the hands Randi placed so Vee's hands couldn't hold the hem, the "show" for Harrison — was one long engineered yes. Vee thinks she went to a party. I watched her get walked, step by step, onto Randi's floor under Randi's lights with her panties out, by a woman who kept freeing her hands precisely so she couldn't cover herself. And the terrible thing is it was also the best night of her life. Both are true. That's the book working exactly as it's been built to work.

Cassie, bless her, did what Cassie does: "At a dance?" One line, arithmetic, unbothered, accurate. I've trusted her all book and tonight she again said the true thing in the driest possible register and let it lie — and even her prediction (the hem won't stay put) came true on schedule. The whole dorm sequence was warm — Vee deliberately holding her own eyes in the mirror instead of dropping them, choosing to want to be looked at. That felt like growth rendered honestly: her appetite is real, it's hers, and it's also been cultivated by exactly these two people. Both things true, again.

The walk over was delicious on its own — "it's not a skirt. It's a rumor" — and then the bracelet. *The bracelet is called Love.* Taking it off "requires tools." I marked it the second Randi said it: a locked circle that only machinery can open, worn always. Given what I know about Randi's face-locks and egress-proof affection, the page was absolutely inviting me to read it as her love — locked on, requiring tools, never informally removable. I don't feel I'm reaching; the page held it out.

Brooke unsettled me *less* this time, which is itself interesting. The hug, the recitation of Vee's whole little history — the cardigan, the smoothie shop, the Friday she "needed our Randi" — and the deliberate skip over the wrecked two-weeks-ago visit. Vee took it as kindness and I almost did too. But I remember the house having "a folder," and here it was, read aloud warmly. Also: "She runs *Chambord* through it… a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime." That's Randi describing Brooke, but it's Randi's own exact religion, and it's Pace's gift-method too (no card, no moment you're asked to thank). Money spent so care feels free. I clocked it and moved on, because the chapter then took my body entirely.

The dance. The skirt climbing. "Your panties are showing." The shame arriving and then — the page said it and I felt it — being "not only awful," turning over into heat. I believe that about Vee so completely now; the book has earned her particular circuitry. And then, "Let's put on a show," Harrison — a prop boy with no name needed — and the kiss.

The kiss itself: I melted. Fully. The held inch; Vee's eyes going over Randi's face "as they had wanted to all autumn and never let themselves"; the smell of her that Vee had "been quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping the file"; the "please" of a tongue; Vee's hand going into Randi's hair on its own; her hips pressing once before she could stop them. This is the swoon the entire book has been banking toward — Randi and Vee finally, explicitly — and the page did not cheat it. It was genuinely the hottest beat of the volume for me, and I let myself swoon even knowing what I know. Maybe especially knowing: the reader-position this book puts me in — foreknowledge making the swoon ache instead of canceling it — is the whole design.

And then the knife, delivered in Randi's brightest register: "You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it." Eleven words of cover, and Vee — body "still wide open and ringing" — accepts the folded-neat version "because it was the only thing held out to her." That hurt. Randi got the full kiss, with plausible deniability banked in front of everyone, and left Vee holding a want "so far past the size of its excuse that it frightened her." I don't doubt Randi loved it — her coming back "a moment behind," blinking, shaking her head, reads as genuinely knocked sideways — but she still chose the cover story. I don't trust a thing she does with what she loves. Again. More.

My suspicion of Randi is, again, entirely earned on the page: her hands moved Vee's hands. She lifted Vee's arms. She announced the panties. She picked Harrison. She closed the inch. That's staging, not spontaneity — and the readerly agony is that Vee's awakening toward her is nonetheless real, glorious even.

On Pace — present mostly as the split-weekend formula ("He gets me Friday… you get me Saturday night") — the dread: Vee has now kissed her best friend while attached to her boyfriend, and does not know the best friend is the boyfriend's girlfriend. The triangle's point just did something the other two vertices have a standing agreement about. What happens if Vee, brunch-honest as she is, tells Pace? What has Randi already told him? Every permutation I run is awful.

The erotic charge: the strongest in the volume, for me. The exposure arithmetic, the tantric dance-floor buildup, then a kiss rendered with real tongue-and-taste specificity. Peak.

No real friction. If anything, Harrison-as-device was transparent, but he was transparent *to* Vee the same way — Randi's jokes need props. The mirror-in-pieces sequence was a small gem.

Titles: "On Her Floor" — the sorority mixer's dance floor, Randi's floor, Randi's turf — and the page makes the title literal: everything that happened happened because Vee was on Randi's floor, under Randi's management. It didn't give the kiss away; it just quietly attested where the floor was. The cover board shows "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION," no separate tagline shown to me — "a warm reception" now reads directly as this gold-lit mixer and its welcoming warmth (and Brooke's smell-like-warmth hospitality), while "a long spoon" keeps warning me about supping with dangerous company. The promise sharpens every chapter.

Next — guesses from here: Randi will report the kiss to Pace in some managed version; Vee will either confess it (she confesses everything) and detonate against an invisible wall, or sit on it while Randi's "never telling" policy gets harder to hold. I dread the Thursday debrief and I need it.
