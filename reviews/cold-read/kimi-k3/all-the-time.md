## Reader reaction

Randi's back, and I didn't realize how much pressure her three-chapter absence had been storing until the hostess said "Randi?" at the door and Vee *warmed* at being expected. That tiny beat did something to me — the whole chapter is in it. Vee comes in blind from the glare, "could make out almost nothing," and Randi is "the first thing to come clear, before the room did." Being guided into someone's dark, by name, and feeling *lucky* about it. I wanted to put a hand on her shoulder.

The confession scene — "the amount of it" — is the happiest Vee has ever been on the page, and I believed every word of it, which is exactly the problem. Her account of Pace matches the man I've been shown: slow, builds, strong then gentle, cooks, watches her eat. It's *true*. That's what scares me now — the trap's product is indistinguishable from a good man in love. When she asks "Am I turning into some kind of sex pervert?" and Randi answers "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud" — I felt the absolution land as genuine kindness AND as the conditioning re-branded as health, both at once, inseparable. And then "…Does he have a brother?" I laughed out loud and then went cold. Randi already has the man. The joke is the only claim she's allowed to make at this table.

The center of the chapter for me is Randi's crack. "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached." — and Randi's eyes go bright and unfixed, a catch in her breath, "It doesn't," low and not quite even. This is the first unguarded Randi Vee has ever been in the room with, and Vee reads it as a compliment to her storytelling — "she went warm with something close to pride." I read it as grief, or jealousy, or guilt, or love, and I genuinely can't tell which, and that not-knowing is the most humanizing thing the book has given Randi yet. She engineered this happiness and now has to sit in a velvet booth and hear it described back as weather she can't live in. I felt *sorry* for her. I hate that I felt sorry for her. It changes nothing about what she does next.

Because what she does next is flawless and total. The photo sequence turned my stomach in the best way. "Babe, these are two *weeks* old" — boredom as lever. "You don't have to show me" — the release that functions as the hook. "You only gave him *half*?" — the flinch-lever, the exact same tool as "vanilla" last time, and Vee pays out the uncropped full-length image on it, the thing she had ready *cropped* specifically to keep the rest private. "The one friend she trusted with all of it. There was no one else, anywhere, she could have shown." The most portable file of her just traveled — on Vee's own thumb, no theft required, handed over in a burst of stung pride. And Randi's eye goes to the *toes*. "Showing him your toes, eh?" — the same detail Pace stopped at. The two of them read her body with one eye, and Vee experiences it as friendship.

Small cold note: "Is Cassie in?" — asked before "Just us" comes out soft with that little roughness under it. Randi confirmed the witness's absence before savoring the dyad. Maybe innocent. With Randi, the pattern is the point.

And then the goodbye, where the kiss's escalation passed into Vee's own hands — "Vee was the one who wouldn't let it, leaning up the last half-inch." She noticed, decided she wanted it, "and didn't bother about it again." The design no longer needs pushing. That's the scariest sentence in the chapter.

Erotically: no explicit content and yet genuinely charged — the photo-showing has a real exhibitionistic hum (her foot going under the table, "the thrill and the shame running together"), the breaking yolk is food-eroticism done right, and the kiss hums. Friction: almost none. The Benedict lingered a beat past necessary, but appetite *is* the chapter's subject, so I'll allow it. "Each waiting for its mistress" is a touch arch. That's all I've got — the chapter is disciplined.

The title: "All the Time" is the amount of sex, the sex-brain, the attention "the whole time I'm there," the wanting that starts thirty minutes out. But with the jacket in hand it's also the machinery — the game runs all the time, even (especially) at brunch. And the book's title and tagline have never fit a chapter better: this is literally a polite invitation, a meal with the woman she should be supping with at long-spoon distance, and every yes at the table — the Benedict, the photo, the uncropped one, the extra half-inch of kiss — was freely given. Each one was also manufactured by a lever. That's the trap, stated in eggs and mimosas.

Whether the Anthro "just us" term gives Randi a second weekly room.
