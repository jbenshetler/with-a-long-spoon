# Cold read (grounded) — All the Time

## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This one got me from both directions at once, and I felt the split physically the whole way through. On Vee's side of the table it's one of the warmest chapters in the book — a woman discovering that her appetite isn't a defect, saying "We have so much sex, Randi" and being answered with the single kindest sentence anyone has ever handed her: "You're just finally hungry out loud." I wanted to cry at that, because it's *true*, because it's the exact anti-mother, the absolution her whole Ohio upbringing never gave her — and because I know who's giving it and what she does with everything she collects. The horror of this book isn't that anyone lies to Vee. It's that Randi tells her the truth. The absolution is real *and* it's feed for Pace's bed, and I have to hold both in the same hand.

The moment that undid me was Randi cracking. "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around" — and Randi's eyes going bright, the catch in her breath, "It doesn't," low and not quite even. Vee reads it as her story being so good it reached a sophisticated woman. I read it as Randi hearing the exact thing she has never once had said about her — the thing Pace nearly gave her in ch 1 that she swore in the bathroom mirror she'd never let out again. And Vee, radiant, takes it as *pride*. That misread is the saddest beat in the book so far, sadder than the bench, because it's tender on both sides and true on neither side in the way the person across the table thinks.

And then the photo. God. Vee has the cropped one *ready*, which already tells me she came planning to be brave, and then Randi needles her — "you only gave him *half*?" — and Vee hands over the full uncropped length of herself to win a standing she doesn't need to win. "God, those *legs*." "Showing him your toes, eh?" I was squirming. Not because it's cold — it's hot, deliberately, giddily hot, two women gone pink over a nude — but because I know Randi has wanted exactly this since "what she keeps under there," and Vee is experiencing it as the best friendship of her life. The kiss at the end, where *Vee* leans up the last half-inch and takes a second one "for herself" — her desire for Randi has finally done something in daylight, unnamed and unbothered, and she files it as love of the morning. I believe her. That's the ache.

The detail that chilled me quiet: "I've never once heard it start." The music is always already going when she walks in — like the thermostat turned up hours early, like two towels, like the drawer emptied for her before she asked. She hears it as a house that knows her. I hear a house that is *staged* for her, every single time, and the fact that she's never once caught the switch being flipped is the whole design in one sentence. The title does the same double work: "All the Time" is the sex, the wanting that grows instead of spending, the attention that never comes off her — and, to me, the surveillance that never comes off her either.

Friction: almost none. The Benedict-and-yolk passage ("the middle, when it goes everywhere") nearly winks too hard as metaphor, but Vee is exactly the woman who would say it without hearing it, so I let it. The book's title and cover board — WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION — keep earning themselves: everything here is an invitation, extended so courteously you don't notice whose hand is on the spoon. No tagline beyond the cover board was shown to me.

What I dread and want next: the Anthro class, "just us," Tuesdays and Thursdays — a standing twice-weekly room for Vee and Randi with Cassie explicitly written out of it, and Vee *engineered it herself* out of pure wanting. That's the design completing itself through her own hands. My guess from here: Randi's mask is going to fail in that classroom before any confrontation with Pace arrives, and when it does it will be the best and worst scene in the book.
