# Cold read (grounded) — Among Friends

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one sat warm in my chest for most of its length and then did something quiet and terrible at the edges of it, and I'm still sorting which of those two things the chapter actually was.

The long middle — the dinner itself — was pure pleasure, and I want to say so plainly before I get to the rest. Watching Pace work that room was one of the most satisfying stretches this book has given me in a while. "Vee's boyfriend," said six times, flat as a grocery list, until it became the night's running joke — and Vee catching herself at the brisket saying it under her breath, "trying it on with her own mouth," and making herself stop. That undid me a little. After December, after the folded arms and the word he cannot say, here he is spending the word *boyfriend* in public like it costs him nothing, to every measuring face she brought. It's not the word, and both of them know it isn't, but it's a word, out loud, in a room, and she's been starved for exactly that. And then the way he hosts: never producing a credential, handing people their first line and standing back to laugh at their second. Danny never learns what Pace benches. Theo gets "where does it start, *for you*" — a question with no wrong answer built into it. And Cassie. Cassie glowing "drily, at low wattage, and unmistakably," giving him the cafeteria story, and then the exit line — "You can stay" — which from Cassie is a papal blessing. I believed every bit of it. Vee standing at the slaw end watching her worlds braid together, "something enormous and quiet go by underneath her, like a whale under a boat" — I felt that. I wanted this for her.

And. I know what I know. So the whole time I was swooning I was also watching Randi arrive first — first at the table, first at the chair facing the door, first even ahead of the hosts, "she beat everyone to Vee's things, always had" — and the chapter lets that read as devotion because that's how Vee reads it, and it *is* devotion, and it's also surveillance. Both. The handshake scene is where I actually felt the floor tilt: "Randi's hand closed hard around hers, hard enough to feel the bones," and Vee reads it as her best friend being happy for her at the exact moment the word *boyfriend* gets confirmed. And I'm standing outside the scene knowing Randi is Pace's lover, that she engineered all of this, and that grip is — what? Joy? Pain? Possession? The page doesn't tell me and I'm not going to fix it, but I noticed my stomach tighten, and I noticed Vee didn't.

The end-of-night table of three is where the chapter got its teeth into me. Pace delivering, at his volume for three, "Randi said something you should hear — 'Brunches with Vee are the highlight of my week.'" And Vee's flash of flat panic — because she knows what those brunches contain — and then nothing in his face but a man passing along a compliment. Reader, I sat there knowing that these two people have lain in his bed and discussed those brunches in detail, that he has heard the contents of them from Randi's own mouth, and that this little performance of *Randi told me a nice thing about you* is a scene being played *for* Vee by both of them simultaneously, in perfect unscripted sync. And then Randi one-ups it: "What I didn't tell him is that meeting Vee was the highlight of my year. To my beautiful best friend." Which is the truest thing Randi has ever said out loud and also a move in the game, and Vee has to look at the table, too full. And then — the image the chapter leaves glowing — Randi going glazed-quiet, turning her glass a slow quarter at a time, and Vee privately *proud* of being able to move her. I love Vee so much it hurt there, and I wanted to put a hand between her and all of it, and I couldn't, and that is exactly the ache this book has been building toward for fifty chapters.

The stinger moment — Vee watching Pace make Randi laugh and something going through her "with a stinger on it," the December shape of *him, easy, somewhere she wasn't, being wonderful at a woman* — that's the closest Vee has ever come to seeing it, and the text knows it, and lets her talk herself down: *this was Randi, this was her own doing.* The trapdoor check with no trapdoor found. I don't think Vee is stupid for filing it away. I think the scene is exquisitely cruel about how invisible a triangle is from its own point.

And then the erotic back half, which — God, it worked on me, and it's complicated that it did. The parking lot kiss, her pulling his cold hands up under her sweater herself, the boldness of it; then the truck, "greedy with the whole night," and the detail that destroyed me: his hand in her hair going *still*, "forgotten, every part of him pulled down to one place... The man who did six things at once, down to zero." That's a gorgeous piece of writing about what it feels like to undo a composed person, and it's hers, she made it happen, after a night of watching him compose everyone else. Then the drive home — her own hands, the zipper, the jeans worked down "a graceless honest inch at a time," pulling the sticky lace aside and *holding* it out of his way, "an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second it went on" — that's the consent signature of this whole book, desire as continuous active choosing, and it's hot precisely because of it. The callback to the rain-night in September ("it'll only blow cold till the truck warms up") and to the county-road drive and the marked coat lining — the book weaving her whole erotic history into one truck cab. Peak heat, fully earned.

But the last paragraph. "And if some small sentence in her was still standing at a window somewhere, watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time, it couldn't have made itself heard tonight over her own blood." That sentence turned the whole warm chapter over and showed me its underside. The window — the book's oldest motif, looking and being looked at — and the glass, Randi's glass, the glazed quiet Vee was proud of causing. Some part of Vee noticed and was not heard. That is the chapter in one image: everything wonderful, everything true, everything engineered, and the one witness inside her outvoted by her own happiness.

The title, "Among Friends," is doing quiet double duty and it got me. The night is genuinely among friends — the best night of communal warmth Vee has had in the book. And the deception is also among friends — her two most intimate people, sitting at one round table with her, each privately holding the fact of the other. The title doesn't give anything away ahead of time; it recolors afterward, which is the good kind. The volume title, *With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception* — "a warm reception" is literally this chapter, the pine room and the butcher paper and everyone welcomed; and the long-spoon proverb (supping with the devil, you want a long spoon) hangs over all of it — Vee is at the warmest table of her life, and the spoon she needs is the one thing nobody brought. No cover tagline beyond that in what I was shown.

What I want next: the Thursday debrief dinner with Randi, dreadfully — because Vee will now tell Randi about the truck, about the whole night, and Randi will receive it with those dark liquid eyes, and I know where those confidences go afterward. And I dread, in the good way, the first time the stinger doesn't back out of her on its own.

**Symbolism:** The three round tables pushed together with VEE on butcher paper — her name literally holding the center of a room two people built around her. Pace leaning back out of the light "exactly as he had all night for the others" — the host's generosity and the manipulator's self-erasure wearing one gesture. The buffet endlessly redealing the guests: a social world with no fixed seats, no ends — and no stable vantage from which Vee could ever see the whole table.

**Characterization:** Pace deepens beautifully — the same noticing that made the drawer and the dress now turned outward, hosting as fluency; his "they share an interest / You" is the truest, most chilling line he gets. Randi is perfectly, dreadfully consistent — devotion and design indistinguishable at every beat. Vee's stinger-and-self-correction is the first crack of perception in her, drawn with real care. Cassie lands her best beat in the book ("You can stay"). Nobody went thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The dinner's long unhurried middle earns its length — the episodic table currents are the point. The transition to the truck is seamless; nothing sagged.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Strong forward movement after the reconciliation-and-retelling stretch; the book finally puts all its people in one room, which the run has been promising since "Back," and the triangle's first shared scene lands with full weight.
