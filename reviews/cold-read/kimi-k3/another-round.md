## Reader reaction

Relief. That's what this chapter is, and it lands on me like a long exhale — I braced with Vee and then the text dissolved the thing I'd braced for, which is a compliment only because the bracing was honest. She *knew* the blonde was her own invention — she said so out loud in Randi's room — and armored up anyway ("she knew it while she was doing it, and she did it anyway"), and I believe that completely, because relief never survives contact with a body. Then the blonde walks into the taproom and there is no blonde: five foot nothing, roots going dark, "a round face under too much makeup," and a hug for Pace that is not a lab partner's hug — a hug with kinship in it, which we learn by the end is exactly what it is. The two of them are the estranged, keeping each other.

Sheri is a joy on the page. "You've got him happy. That's the whole book, far as I'm concerned" — and then the motorboat line, and Pace flushing "past anything deniable," the first true blush Vee has ever collected from him, and I felt the same stunned delight she did: the composure man, caught. Her calibration is wonderful too — loud, apparently careless, but "there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when." She waits till he's at the bar, drops her volume like a coat, and tells the story right: coming out last March, the whole tablecloth pulled with no dish caught, "Easy words, for a hard thing," and then Pace arriving with donuts and fixing the lock and buying the bacon cheeseburger. "He was there. That's all a body can be for you at a point like that. There." I teared a little, plainly.

And the reveal I didn't expect: "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen." That is the first real bite the book has taken out of the estrangement question I've had open since "I don't go back." And it's telling that it arrives secondhand — Sheri's the one place he's fully honest, and delivering Vee this sliver, with her little editorial blessing ("you don't praise a man at his own table"), reads like Vee being written into that honesty circle one guarded step at a time. I trust that. But I also hold the irony the chapter can't quite keep out: Sheri now carries the secret of Pace's wound and Randi carries the secret of the triangle, and Vee's two confidantes are keeping two separate ledgers. That prickles.

The swoon for me is the back half — the quiet machinery of being kept. Pace's arm settling as ballast ("with him, those had never been two different things"), the headlights held on Sheri's door until the porch light answers, and Vee riding home studying his ten-and-two hands "like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling." That's the chapter's real event: she got to watch his care as a principle instead of a courtship, poured outward over Sheri, and the warmth reads bigger for that, not smaller. "Bacon cheeseburgers," she says to the window, and I sighed — she's collecting evidence and she can't even file it anywhere.

Titles: "Another Round" did double duty for me — the literal rounds Sheri orders all night (and Pace's beer standing flat and witness-like through them), and another round of the gauntlet Vee keeps having to run, the next rival, the next party, the next introduction. Book Two's "A Warm Reception" now feels almost thesis-literal: this *is* the warm reception, the one where the dread was always hers. The series title "With a Long Spoon" still won't settle for me — it reads like proverb-dread, spooning with someone dangerous — and I keep holding it up against all this kindness like a watermark I can't decode yet. No tagline was supplied.

Small frictions: almost none. The "philosophy of his food" phrase recurs once more than it needs, and Pace's summary of Sheri — "Small. Loud... We cycle. She dances." — hands her facts with blades accidentally, though maybe that is the point: his economy sending everyone the same sparseness, and Vee's vacuum doing the threatening. I buy it.

What I want next: the hall, the Brooks and Dunn cover band, "leave them shoes home — can't two-step on stilts." A guess from here: some Saturday dance floor will put Sheri and Randi in earshot of each other's names, and the interest rate on the buried secret goes up. Dread and appetite, in that order — but honestly, mostly appetite.
