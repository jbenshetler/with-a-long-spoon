## Reader reaction

This one took me off guard — a whole chapter with no Pace, no Randi, no trap on the page, just Vee and Cassie at the rec pool — and I braced for a filler beat and got the chapter that's hit me hardest so far.

Cassie. She'd been a voice over a coffee cup — the audit, the dry warning. Here she became a whole person, and I fell for her completely. The suit she fights into "like it had insulted her family"; "Who designed this? Genuinely. What man?"; the refusal to be admired, "and it didn't bother her one bit." Then the care: the tossers line kept low when "she could have pitched it up into the bleachers… She'd kept it for Vee." The standing rib-deep because that's as far as she goes "and the water knew it." And the moment that undid me: Vee surfaces with grief on her face, and Cassie — who "never asked," because "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger" — fixes it from ten feet away with one cupped-hands shot calibrated to be all outrage and barely any water, "which was the point." Care as exact dosing, no words, no invoice. My trust went from high to total. She is the control group — the love that asks nothing — and now I'm afraid for this friendship, because I know what's circling Vee and Cassie doesn't.

Vee: the body chapter. The suit from sixteen, "there was more of her now," and "it gave her nowhere to hide." The bleacher gauntlet met with practiced armor — the long blink, the eyeroll aimed at the ceiling. Then underwater, the best passage in the book so far for me: "The room switched off." "Nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything. She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." And she "grieved, a little." The first grief I've seen her allow herself — self-administered, in the one place no wanting can reach her — and it ached double knowing about the seven months, the built room, the one-way channel. She has almost nowhere that isn't someone's audience: bleachers appraise, mother polices, Pace studies, Randi manages. The water is the one non-audience, and even there she can only stay until her lungs ask.

Then it blooms: she doesn't need water wings; the skill lives in her hands ("her hands still knowing how"), and what holds her up is her own old competence plus a friend at the fence-edge of the deep.
