# Cold read (grounded) — Water Wings

*scene: scenes/water-wings.md · model: kimi-k3 · memory: raw ch001..ch006 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter quietly broke my heart, and I didn't expect it to. After all the heat and maneuvering of the last several chapters — the bench, the pointing game, Pace crossing the coffee-shop floor — this is a small, still scene between two friends in a rec pool, and it lands harder than almost anything since "The Bench." It's Vee's first real interior chapter where nothing is being done *to* her — and what we find there is grief. The synchronized swimming suit from when she was sixteen, "when there'd been less of her to fill it." Her hands still knowing how to roll the towel. The body she's been building the chapter around — the lavish one Randi chose on sight, the one Pace crossed a room for — is, to Vee, "a problem," something to leave up on the deck with the towel "where it could wait." That inverted the book's looking for me. Pace looks at Randi with the lights up and it undoes her; Vee goes *under* the water to be where "nobody up there could see her" and "nobody down here wanted anything," and that's the only place she's whole. I felt that.

Cassie. Cassie has been the flat one, the ball cap and the four words a day, and this chapter shows me she was never flat — she was *watching*. "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman," pitched just for Vee, and then the cupped-hands water trick, "outrage out of all proportion to the actual amount of water, which was the point." She never asks; she just fires a sheet of water across ten feet of pool because her friend came up "with her face on wrong." That is the tenderest thing in this book so far, and it's platonic, and I want to protect both of them. It also recontextualizes "Goodbye, Vee" from two chapters back — that flat, wrong, finished word. Cassie saw something leaving and named it the only way she names things. Now I'm braced.

The bleacher catcalls made me flinch — "You won't need floaties with *those*!" — not because they're graphic but because of how *practiced* Vee's deflection is: the long blink, the eyeroll "aimed at the ceiling." She's been handling this since before sixteen. And the underwater passage is the chapter's peak for me, not erotic but sensual in the old, clean way — "the cold coming up from below and the light coming down through the surface in slow sheets," the grief arriving "a beat past" when her lungs asked. The line "she had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken" — *taken* is doing so much quiet work, especially with Pace now in her phone.

The title "Water Wings" landed with irony — the catcaller jokes she won't need floaties, but the water itself is what holds her up; Cassie, too. As for the book's cover-board title, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*: the old proverb about supping with the devil keeps sharpening. Pace's politeness — "May I call you?" — is the invitation, and Randi's "we should sit together" was one too. Vee has accepted two invitations now, one from each of them, and this chapter makes me see exactly what she'd be risking.

What I want next: I'm half-dreading Pace's call, because I now suspect — a guess from here — that his interest in Vee and Randi's interest in Vee are the *same* interest, the threesome Randi reframed as her gift to him. Vee thinks she's been chosen twice. I think she's been chosen once, twice over. And Cassie is the only person on the page who isn't choosing her for anything.
