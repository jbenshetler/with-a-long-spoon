## Reader reaction

This chapter exhaled. After the froyo date's bright nervous energy, this is Vee in the one place her body has never been a problem — and the book lets it be that simple. The bleacher boys lob their comments ("You won't need floaties with *those*!") and Vee handles them with the long blink and the eyeroll, practiced, unbothered on the surface — but the chapter quietly tells me the cost: "fifty yards of held breath," the face she puts on, the towel carried down at her side instead of against her. And then she goes under, and the room switches off, and "something in her chest that had stood at attention all afternoon quietly sat down." That line undid me. The grief she does down there — grieving, a little, the uncomplicated belonging she had before she had a body to be a problem — is the first time the book has let her mourn something instead of manage it.

Cassie. Cassie, Cassie. "They will never know the love of a woman," pitched low, kept for Vee when she had the lungs to pitch it to the bleachers. And then — the moment that got me — she doesn't ask what happened down there. "Asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger." Instead she shoots a sheet of water across ten feet of pool, barely gets her, and lets outrage do the work comfort can't. The shriek-laugh, "the ugly one," the sound Vee hadn't made in a while. I adore Cassie with my whole heart. She is the only person in this book whose care has no design behind it that I can see, and this chapter is a whole argument for her: she stands in water to her ribs "like a person," planted, so Vee can go deep and come back to someone.

No suspicion earned on this page at all — that's the strange relief of it. Pace and Randi don't appear, and their absence is palpable: for one chapter nobody is pointing at Vee, nobody is working on her, and the contrast makes me notice how worked-on the rest of her life is. The erotic charge is nil and rightly so; the swoon here is friendship-swoon, and it lands fully.

The title: *Water Wings* — the floaties joke from the bleachers, turned inside out. Vee doesn't need wings; the water is the one thing that has always held her. But Cassie is the water wing, really — the thing that keeps her up without asking anything. The book's title and the long-spoon tagline are quiet this chapter; nothing here invited or supped. This feels like the breath before something — and honestly, knowing what I know about the design tightening around Vee, a chapter this safe makes me dread what's next more, not less. (A guess from here: the stillness is the point. Whatever detonates will detonate against this.)
