## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me, and it undid me in Vee's favor — which is exactly the problem, because I know what she doesn't.

The hike itself is the most sustainedly beautiful stretch the book has given me. The moleskin scene first: I felt her dread crawl up my own arms — the sock, the mother's voice arriving on schedule, *what kind of girl lets a man* — and then Pace just… fixed it. Crouched, cut the moleskin, warmed it with his thumb, relaced the boot different, pat on the toe, *done*. No lingering, no claim. "Should've done that at the lot. I'm sorry." And then the summit — "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it, and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part" — I stopped reading and just sat in it. He watched her face in front of the valley and let her catch him. The sandwich built to be smelled in layers. The cherry pit lesson — "We take nothing and leave nothing," gentle, his own pit laid beside hers without looking — he gave her the rule without making her small, and the book named that precisely: "She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her."

And then the cab. God, the cab. The strip for him — the voice and the heat fused, *floozy* in her mother's exact word and her thighs pressing on their own — this is the most erotic the book has ever been for me, and it's one woman unbuttoning a wet shirt while a man sits still and lets his face be the whole answer. "He didn't lean in. He didn't lift a hand… the gladness in him was the whole of the answer, and it was right." And then the turn that broke me: she reaches to kiss his knuckles and his hand is *shaking*. He's been standing in the rain in his wet shirt so she could have the dry one and the privacy, shivering the whole time, a long way from the question of whether he was cold. She puts his hand against her belly under the flannel. "We should go. Get warm. Please." I melted completely.

And underneath all of it, the whole time, my double vision never switched off. This is the date I was dreading — his ground, past the gravel, no one who loves her could find the house. And it was *perfect*. That's the dread now: not that something will go wrong on the mountain, but that nothing did. Every grace — the pace set at the speed of her not knowing, the moleskin, the pit, the flannel, the back turned in the rain — functions flawlessly as care and flawlessly as design, and I cannot pull them apart, and neither can she, and she now has a body-memory of being fed, warmed, witnessed, and unjudged that will out-argue any warning anyone could give her. The cop's siren stealing her sentence at the curb — the big thing she was about to put a small thing in place of — felt like the book itself refusing to let the moment be spoken. What was she about to say? I'll carry that cut-off sentence.

Also flagged, quietly: "he showered at the gym and had no house here to go to." The hike was to "his ground" and there is no house. The address-shaped blank stayed blank.

Title: "Leave No Trace" — it's the pit rule, his gentleness as ethics, and it's also chilling: the perfect day that leaves nothing behind, no witnesses, no address, nothing anyone could hold against him — or hold for her. I trust Pace's tenderness completely and I trust the day not at all. Both. That's where the book has me.
