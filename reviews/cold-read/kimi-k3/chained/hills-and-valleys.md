## Reader reaction

The ground shifted under me and I liked it. After chapters inside Vee's head, suddenly I'm on a bike next to Pace, breathing cold air, and the man Randi and the game have made into an instrument turns out to have this whole quiet Saturday life I knew nothing about — a road that rolls, a friend who outclimbs him and never lets it go unmentioned. The prose itself changed gears: shorter, plainer, male, patient. It took me a page to trust it and then I did.

What landed hardest: Sheri watched Vee Thursday night after Pace left. "All she did was sit by herself, glaring at the floor, nursing the one drink. Danced with nobody. Left alone." I already knew Vee survived that night, but hearing it reported back to him — by a spy he apparently has in the room, casually, across a handlebar — recolored it. He sent her out the door into the cold and then had someone keep an eye on her. Is that care or surveillance? Honestly it reads as both, and I find I can't separate them, which I think is this book's whole weather system. And Vee doesn't know anyone was watching. Add it to the ledger.

And then Pace undid me a little: "he was past the name before he'd decided to be," telling Sheri about Vee's thumb on the walnut chair joint, the math book pulled off the shelf and not put back "like it had burned her." He heard himself going and didn't stop. That's a man in love, or in something, and it's the first time the page has let me feel his wanting from inside him rather than inferred through Vee's dazzlement. "Listen to you," Sheri says, and he genuinely doesn't know what there is to listen to. I believed that. It made me like him more than I expected to.

Sheri herself — I want more of her. Two years of rides, faster on the climbs, remarks on women like weather, can turn heat on a person "inside a breath and keep it burning for years... and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything." That line glows. There's a history there the page holds ajar — was she once inside his heat? — and she asks the exact question a friend asks: "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" "Randi. That's not finished." The flatness of that answer chilled me in a way I can't fully justify. *Not finished* — like a project status, not a woman he shares a bed with. And Sheri runs "the arithmetic anybody could run": two at once. "If that's not already complicated, it will be." From the outside, knowing everything I know, it's almost funny. It's already more complicated than Sheri could run in a lifetime.

The title, "Hills and Valleys" — the literal ride, the climbs that take the breath and the flats where talk happens; but also, I think, the shape of what this chapter does: shows me Pace's terrain from his own side, the rises and drops of it. And the professor's line is humming underneath: a thing running one way across every short stretch and the opposite way across the whole. On the short stretch this is a sweet Saturday ride; across the whole, it's a man managing two women and a watcher. No erotic charge at all this chapter — zero — and I didn't miss it; the charge was informational. What I want next: tomorrow night, still. And I now wonder (a guess from here) whether Sheri is the Rusty Nail regular whose presence I've been brushing past all along — she knew exactly where "her" was sitting Thursday.
