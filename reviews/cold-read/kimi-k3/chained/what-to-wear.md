## Reader reaction

God, this chapter felt like sunlight after all the cold rooms — literally: the gold dining hall, the floor-to-ceiling windows, Vee laughing "loud and easy," the kind that makes the next table "want in." She's *happy*, out loud, in public, and the book just let her be happy for a whole chapter. And I sat there loving it and quietly bracing, because I know things her table doesn't.

Kayla and Meg are a delight. Kayla with the "bright unbroken stream you climbed onto rather than followed," Meg dry and level, "From Meg, that was an event" when she set her fork down. This is Vee's world widened — three girls at a corner table, stealing fries, no design in the room. The telling of the shirt story is now on its *third* performance (to herself, to Cassie, to the girls), and it keeps getting better in the telling and she knows it and doesn't care. That line — "She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care" — landed both ways for me: pure girlish pleasure, and a tiny chill, because the story is load-bearing and nobody on her side of it knows what it's holding up.

The moment that got me: "He asks me first. The small things. What I want to order, and he waits for the real answer." She's trying to explain why it's making her lose her mind and she *can't* — "It sounds like nothing. I can hear it sounding like nothing." Reader, it does not sound like nothing. It sounds like exactly the thing a consent-careful designer would build, and it's working so perfectly that his own patience is the aphrodisiac. Meg's "Everybody's different at the start... Sometimes they even are" is the kindest and most devastating line in the chapter — Vee "let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." Of course it didn't.

And then the text: "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." His *house*. The address-shaped blank, "off six-fourteen," "he had no house here to go to" — and now there's a door, and Saturday behind it, and he didn't say what to wear. Kayla's right that it's "a ransom note." Two o'clock, no meal, no instructions. My stomach dropped the way Vee's heart lifted. The heat here is all anticipation — "climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner," one kiss twelve days ago counted without counting — and it's genuinely working on me; the restraint is the engine.

Title: "What to Wear" is Kayla's scandalized question — what do you put on for a man's house when you don't know what it's *for*? It's funny and light on the surface, and underneath it's the whole chapter's shape: she's dressing for an event whose design she can't see. Bring a change of clothes, Kayla says. I bet the afternoon has its own ideas about clothes.

What I want next: Saturday. Obviously. I want her through that door, and I dread what's on the other side of it in the specific way this book has trained me to — it will be tender, it will be careful, it will be *exactly right*, and I won't be able to tell the gift from the move. Also quietly noted: Randi absent a third chapter running, and the Pace file has now been narrated to two audiences with no Randi-shaped hole in it.
