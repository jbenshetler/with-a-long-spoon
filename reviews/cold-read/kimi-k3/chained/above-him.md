## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. After the brunch chapter — the stall, the glaze, the ache — the book turns around and hands me a whole chapter of Pace being good, and it *is* good, and I melted anyway, knowing everything I know.

The lift at the door undid me first: "she was off the ground... her feet swinging out, the porch light wheeling once behind his head... a bright stupid sound she'd have died of anywhere else." A whole week of missing him in one sound. Then the sexual ethic, deepening exactly the man I had: "it always came around to her first... usually more than once before he took a single thing for himself. She hadn't known a man could do that. She hadn't known a man would want to." Then the feeding — salmon turned once, pale green dill sauce, tomatoes warm off the sill, a mustard thing "she'd wanted to drink straight," wine "a little stony," and "she ate like a field hand at his table now." Set that against Randi's "one neat bite" from last chapter and the book is writing hunger in two different handwritings. At Pace's table: the whole meal. At Randi's: secondhand glaze.

And the drawer. The misdirect worked on me completely — I believed with her that he'd cleared her things because he'd minded, "where a guest's things went once the guest had been too much" — and then: "He'd folded his whole ordered self down into a single drawer. He'd given her the top one. The easy one. The one your hand goes to first." I put a hand to my chest. "There was no note. There was never a note." "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead."

She loves him. "She'd said it, or not said it, but let it be true in her own chest where she couldn't take it back." And then "Peter," barely a breath, into a sleeping ear — the title living right there, the word said *above* him, not to him.

And here is the counterweight I cannot put down, and the page itself hands it to me fair and square: "she lay there and counted all the ways he'd said it without saying it... A man didn't do all of that for a girl he was only passing a season with. He had to feel it." I know about Randi — "practically a marriage" — about Thursdays, the watch, Sheri, the pointing-and-picking. The counting is true, and the count is incomplete. He has never once said, and she has never once asked — the scar "still never asked about and still meant to keep" — and they're building something out of a shared courtesy toward each other's un-saids. It reads as tenderness and as architecture in the same paragraph. I won't manufacture suspicion of the *acts* — the acts are honestly tender. The unease lives entirely in the silence the chapter keeps naming: "He never said. He never said."

One detail nags gently: "her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, and she'd never asked it to" — happy on its face, and also the familiar motion of her self changing out ahead of her permission, the bras keeping the secret. Meanwhile his powerlifter chest hides under shirts a size too big while she swims in clothes bought a size up — the world mis-sizes them both; they fit each other. That's the swoon again.
