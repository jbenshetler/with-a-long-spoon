## Reader reaction

This is a small, soft chapter and it lands exactly where it means to: Vee comes home "lit up," glowing enough that Cassie calls her a public utility, and I believed the glow completely. It even warmed me — after the bistro chapter I braced for Cassie to be another version of the Randi interrogation, and instead Cassie is the undesigning witness, the one who just listens "with no face on at all" and lets her talk herself out. "Go to sleep, streetlight." I love Cassie's dry tenderness here — she names the truth ("You don't come home school nights," not a question) without one ounce of the bistro's extraction. The seam repaired is holding.

The swoon is in Vee's fluency itself — she who never runs out of words runs clean out of them, and Cassie notices. That beat got me. And the burn story: the tomato sliding off the fork, his cold cloth arriving before the first swear, fussing for days. Her hand going to the spot on the side of her breast without her knowing it moved. That's desire living in the body and telling on itself — the body ahead of permission again, but gentle now, not shaming.

But the chapter's spine is the ending. "Do you love him." And Vee, who last chapter set the word down plainly in her own chest "where she couldn't take it back," goes small: "Maybe. I think so." "It's all so fast." To a sleeping man she says "Peter" and means love; to her closest roommate she can't say it awake. That's not a retreat — it reads to me as the word being too big to say into another person's face, only safe in the dark. Cassie lets the maybe stand. I noticed what I know: the fastness is designed. "It's all so fast" is the most accurate thing Vee has ever said, and she has no idea why it's true.

Suspicion: none new earned by this page — Cassie remains the one person in this book with no agenda I can detect. The chill is all carried knowledge: every detail Vee glows about (the cooking "with intent," "he watched her eat it like that was the show," "he noticed things before she did," the house kept warm) is double-exposure for me. Warm house, warm meals, her own bed sitting empty — the architecture is working, and she describes the architecture as love.

The title: "School Nights" — Cassie's phrase, the days her own bed sits empty, the slow colonization of her week. It's rueful and tender, not ominous; it names how far in she already is without having decided to be. The book's title keeps sharpening: the warmth, the drawer, the cooked dinners are the polite invitation she's already walked through; the long spoon is the one she can't see.

What I want next: for something to break the idyll, honestly — Randi has been absent two chapters running and I've never felt her more present. My guess from here: the collision comes through the house she's stopped sleeping away from, or through Thursday.
