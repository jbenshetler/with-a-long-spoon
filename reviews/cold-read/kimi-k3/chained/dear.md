## Reader reaction

This one hurt in the most luxurious way. Randi is back after two chapters of absence, and the book lets her be *wonderful* — warm, funny, tender, claimed — and lets me watch every wonderful thing also function. I kept melting and catching myself melting.

The opening beat gutted me quietly: Cassie with her cap on, glad, somewhere to be — and the look that goes past Vee to Randi and gets folded away. Then Vee letting Cassie be excluded from the smoothie place — "Not her thing" — and the narration telling me she could have left it at "she's got practice" *and didn't*. Vee is now doing small, plausible work to keep her safest person out of the bright room, and she experiences it as health. "It was fine to have your own people." Something turned over, small, and she let it lie. That's the whole chapter in one sentence.

And then the smoothie date itself is gorgeous and unbearable. Randi's catastrophe story is delightful — genuinely funny, genuinely warm — and I noticed, the way I now notice everything, that its moral is *a man who doesn't listen won't take care of you*, which sets up Pace as the counter-proof, the man who listens. The debrief draws out of Vee exactly the two confessions the design would want on record: "May I choose" / "You let him choose for you. And you liked it" — and Randi "went no further," letting the letting-go feel like kindness. And the kiss: Vee had the dignified version ready — *I made an exception* — and Randi's soft reframe, "Because you couldn't not. Because of him," pulls the truer, less sovereign version out of her, and Vee *gives* it. Her agency from last chapter, "I did it on purpose," gets gently refiled as surrender, and it feels like intimacy while it happens. That is the most skillful and the most frightening thing the book has done yet.

The Brooke scene is a knife. The tall girl's slow inventory, the shelf of a chair, the pilled cardigan "like an apology" — and then Randi, in front of the finished women, taking her cool damp hand and saying "Vee's my dear friend" and not letting go. I felt what Vee felt. I *wanted* it to be real, and some of it is real — that's the hell of it — but I also watched what it purchased: Vee shrank herself "to leave nothing of herself worth holding against Randi," and the wrong-size feeling "stopped mattering next to the other thing," and the chapter ends with "she had a place now, and that the place was here, next to Randi." The world narrowing, experienced as belonging. "Love you, girl," and gone, radiant, back into the bright matched crowd — while Vee walks home alone with an empty cup, feeling *picked*.

The title: **"Dear."** It gave nothing away and detonated on contact — *dear friend*, said out loud, costing something, spent on her. Dear as in beloved; dear as in expensive. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the long spoon both keep gathering: this chapter the invitation is friendship itself, publicly extended, and it's the most effective one yet.
