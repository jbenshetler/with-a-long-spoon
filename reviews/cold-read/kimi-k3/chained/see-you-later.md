## Reader reaction

Being inside Vee's head for a whole chapter undid me a little. She's the most alive person on the page and now I know exactly how she works from the inside: the not-looking at the door, the *yes* arriving "fully formed, ahead of everything" and being folded down to size, the social fluency that rises "under it before she'd decided anything." She's generous, observant, funny about herself — and she is walking into a designed game with her whole heart open. I adore her, and I ache for her already.

The swoon is real and I won't pretend otherwise. Randi at the end of the row with "her whole face lit," the forearm squeeze, "you're one of those people who has the right pen," the high table, "I've decided" — I felt Vee's feet leave the ground with her. The best hour she'd had in months, and I believed it. And because I'm carrying what Vee can't, every warm thing doubled: the lit face is also a performance Randi is very good at; the collision Vee still can't assign fault for ("both people apologize and neither one did it") was choreographed; the hour whose words wouldn't stay — "the leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed" — reads to me like being loved *at* rather than known. That's the chapter's quiet horror inside its glow: Vee kept the feeling and lost the transcript, and the feeling was manufactured.

The detail that genuinely chilled me is Randi's own room, said "like it was nothing," placed right where Vee is learning to rank her. And *"Love you, girl"* after one coffee — love-bombing in its purest collegiate dialect. Vee's own inner life complicated things beautifully: the "floozies" thought in her mother's voice riding over a longer look at bare stomachs than the word accounts for, the cardigan worn on purpose over the better shirt, "she was the most of what the room was selling and she was the one with the most fabric on." Vee has her own policed wanting — for women, unexamined, "warm, unexamined, glad" — sitting right next to Randi's. Two women with managed desires, one of them being steered.

Cassie. Four words a day, doesn't take the bit's open door, declines the coffee with "no sulk, no edge," and then: "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word, flat and finished, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." My stomach dropped. Something passed over her face that Vee "would not have been able to name and did not try to." I don't know what Cassie saw or senses — maybe nothing more than a friend watching someone get swept — but the text made that goodbye toll like a bell, and the title is Randi's answer to it: *See you later*, the queen's promise versus the friend's farewell. The title gave me the chapter's whole chord before I had it, and now it's the thing I can't put down.

Heat: none on the page, but the simmer is thick — shoulder against shoulder, breath warm, the flat helpless want with "nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her.*" Vee's desire is working on me more honestly than any sex scene could, because it's so unguarded.

Friction: almost none. The stats lecture beat ran a touch long for what it carried, but the drone was the point — Vee sinking under the class because the better thing was at her elbow. The professor's parting "latent variables — the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" is the author's thumb just barely showing, underlined once in Vee's notebook "going nowhere." I noticed it; I forgave it, because it's exactly what this book is doing to me.

The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, now has its literal instance: "Come to Chi Latte with me... I'm not asking, I've decided." An invitation you cannot decline, delivered as warmth. *With a Long Spoon* still whispers its warning — supping with something, using a long spoon — and from inside Vee's skull I can see exactly who isn't holding one.

What I want next: Pace's vantage on this — does he know how well it's working? And I dread, genuinely, the moment Vee learns the collision was staged. Guess from here: Cassie's "goodbye" is the first witness the design has acquired, and she will matter.
