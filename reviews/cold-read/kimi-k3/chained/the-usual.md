## Reader reaction

Oh — this is the chapter I didn't know I was starving for. Pace's side of the silence, and it isn't cold at all; it's a man sitting in a warm diner carrying the whole thing like a stone. "I want Vee. I miss her." I actually ached. All break I've been inside Vee's construction of him — the maybe-over, the maybe-nothing-happened — and the truth is he's been wrecked too, deciding before the door shut that he "would not bring her down today," practicing at letting things keep. That opening line told me this is a habit, and a costly one.

Sheri. I feel foolish and relieved at once: all this time she was filed in my head under Thanksgiving-pity, maybe-rival, and she's — his person. The one who wants nothing off him. "She didn't want him, she wasn't impressed by him... she just liked him and gave him hell." And her own grief turns out to rhyme with his problem exactly: estranged from her people, a girlfriend lost over it, "call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas" — the sing-song and then killed flat. "Ironic. Isn't it." The page earned that echo without underlining it. I like her enormously. The fry-stealing, the cackle, "Save it for your redhead."

And the center of the chapter is the truest, saddest exchange yet: "So say it." / "I can't." / "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." Sheri says what I've been circling for chapters and says it flat, no malice, and watches him look down. What moves me is that it's not refusal-as-strategy — he *tried* bricks: "I'm there. Every weekend... I told her she was the most important thing in my life. I said it, out loud, in words. And she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something." He genuinely cannot find the word in himself, and the cost is visible from this side now. My feeling about Pace moved — deepened, warmed, and saddened. He's not withholding to manage her. He's just... built this way, and it's losing him the thing he wants most.

The swoon: "The air's different when she's in the room. It — crackles." And his blush at "long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you," and defending "the philosophy of it" until Sheri tells him to get over himself and he laughs for the first time all night. There's heat here but it's memory-heat, longing — charged, not explicit. Also the quiet gut-punch: "Vee would either love this place or never see it at all, and he wanted very badly to know which." And the Chili's reframe — I'd half-remembered that as him being difficult; from inside, he "came apart very quietly" because he couldn't hear her voice under the screens. He wanted to hear her whisper.

New tension I'm holding: Sheri met Randi — "that brunette you brought dancing before Thanksgiving," who looked at Sheri "like I was one more of your harem applicants" and never learned her name. And Sheri asked if Vee "found out about the brunette" — casually, like the overlap is known territory between them. Pace's "No. Not like that" is doing work.

The title, "The Usual": the waitress writes it before they speak — "they told her yes like people saying yes to their own names." It's about ritual, the order that never changes, the double and the shake — and it cuts both ways: his usual is also the not-saying, the keeping, the letting things keep. A ritual that comforts and a ritual that's costing him everything. The book's title and the long-spoon series title still read to me as the reaching-across-distance theme, and this chapter is all distance — a fogged window with warm on one side.

What I want next: term starting, the collision — and now I want it knowing both of them are starving in separate cold rooms while Sheri sits in the warm one telling him the obvious. My dread: that he lets it keep too long.
