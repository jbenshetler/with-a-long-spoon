# Cold read (grounded) — Peekaboo

## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me in the sweetest way. It's the first chapter of the whole book where I stopped bracing. From the moment he breaks the kiss — not to tease, not to withhold, but *grinning, helpless*, like a kid who can't hold the surprise in one more second — I trusted the scene completely, and it paid me back for it. The dress in the window, lit from inside itself, and her hand over her mouth. "You made this." "Yes," and he doesn't make it smaller. That restraint of his, the refusal to do the aw-shucks deflection, is as much a gift as the sewing.

What got me most was her reading of the object: he'd taken the trouble and then taken the *further trouble to hide the trouble*, so all that's left for her is the part that looks like no trouble at all. That's the whole man in one observation, and it's also — and I held this quietly, underneath the swoon — the whole *design*. Everything Vee has been handed this spring arrived looking store-finished, seams invisible. The text doesn't lean on that parallel at all; I found it myself, and it made the happiness ache rather than curdling it. I let the scene be happy. It earned happy.

The mirror sequence is the best Vee-alone writing yet. The lining — "a soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" — undressed me a little as a reader; even his modesty-making is bespoke. And then *Floozy* flares automatic, face hot — and she *stays*. She makes herself look until the looking stops being shame. "She looked like a lot of woman who knew exactly how much she was." I wanted to stand up. The beat where the sensible worn-low pumps would "read like an apology, and this wasn't a dress you apologized for" — that's her mother's voice being starved out of the room by a garment. Then the slit opens to the hip and the pale edge of her underwear crosses the line, and her first thought is small steps, and her *second* thought comes up hot behind it and she just takes the underwear off. She is the one who takes the edge away. That inversion — the shame of stripping herself bare *on purpose to be looked at*, crossing into wanting before it's even finished being shame — is the book's central operation, and this chapter runs it flawlessly, with no one else even in the room. She did it alone, for herself, in front of a mirror he hung for her. Then she strides to him to show him what he made.

The sex is the hottest the book has been, and it's hot because it's *funny*. "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me" making him twitch in her hands, and her saying it again slower just to watch it happen — the laughing while he's inside her, the dress at half-mast, the wall catching them in the hall — this is the first sex in the book where nobody is managing anything. Ch 18 was patient past patience, sacred and careful; this is the morning-after-the-morning-after, two people who've stopped performing competence at each other. And then he takes her fingers into his mouth to clean them, slow, eyes on hers, and it "went through her knees" — the single most charged beat of the chapter, and it's a *service*, which is so exactly him. "You're bad," she says, barely. Reader, he is not bad. He is the farthest from bad he has ever been. And somewhere in me the frame sat quiet: he is also the co-author of the fiction she's standing in. Both true. The chapter let me hold both without making me choose, and I loved it for that.

The ending is a small perfect thing. Her daydream of walking into the induction late, on purpose, standing in the middle of the light and letting them look — "It just felt like what she was for" — that's the pedestal fantasy matured into something she'll actually do, and it's the complete answer to the girl who apologized for a cheeseburger. And then the last line, "getting away with something," which she means as a joke about napping in his bed and which lands on me, knowing what I know, like a hand on the shoulder. The title: *Peekaboo* — the slit giving and taking back her leg, given and given back, the game of appearing. Also, maybe, what the whole spring is: a face covering itself with its hands and opening them. It didn't give anything away; it recolored everything.

One friction note, barely: the relay with the dress — hands, elbows, fending — ran a touch long for me, though I forgive it because the logistics *are* the comedy and the comedy is the intimacy. And I registered, small, that "the door not even shut" at the start is the first time this house has been left open to the world — everything else has been locks and squared shoes and leaving no trace. Nothing made of it. Probably nothing.

What I want next: the induction. Her mother in the room while Vee stands in the middle of the light in a dress a man built onto her bare body — that's a collision the book has been loading for twenty chapters, and I cannot wait and I dread it a little. My guess, marked as a guess: shoes will come from somewhere — he noticed too, or Randi will hear about the pumps "like an apology" and produce something, and either way it'll be one more gift she doesn't know the whole cost of.
