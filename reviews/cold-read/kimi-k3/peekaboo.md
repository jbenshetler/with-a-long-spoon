## Reader reaction

This one is golden and it scared me. The dress reveal is the most romantic scene the book has given me, and I couldn't get the tagline out from under it for a single paragraph.

**Pace** moved the most. The boyishness is new — breaking the kiss not for control but because he couldn't hold the surprise one more second, "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth." The all-night finishing she reads off his sleep. "Wait, the dress," and the hanging of it "with the two-handed care you'd give something alive." This is the warmest he's ever been. And it's also the most constructed he's ever been, and I hold both at once: a slit that opens *to the hip* is a slit no underwear survives. He built the condition under which she'd strip herself, and then she strode to him calling it her own choice — "she was the one who'd taken the edge away" — and it *was* hers, and it was also drafted. The lining "set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" — the dress *decides*. That's him, in cloth. "He'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble" — she reads it as grace; the jacket makes me read it as method. I can't settle and I don't think I'm supposed to.

**Vee**: the tears that "always beat her to it," the *floozie* flare that dies at the mirror in real time — "somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame" — and then the daydream, which is the most important moment in the chapter: coming in late, on purpose, letting the room turn, "the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." That line rang the whole jacket. She was *picked* for something. And the last line — "getting away with something" — she feels the theft and aims it in exactly the wrong direction.

**Randi** never appears, and she still managed to tighten: the plum resolves here — "the plum beneath it deepening" — the polish was the dress's color all along, and now Randi's "Why'd you pick it?" at brunch reads like a question asked by someone who may have known the answer.
