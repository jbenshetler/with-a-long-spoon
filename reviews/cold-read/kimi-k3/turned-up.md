## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

What a relief this chapter was — and I mean that as a felt thing, not a criticism of the last one. After "A Round" left me wrung out and slightly dazed alongside Vee, this is the decompression chamber, and it's exactly the person I wanted her to decompress with. Cassie with her highlighter, putting it down. "Well?" The whole chapter is one long exhale into friendship, and I trusted every inch of it. The pillow fight, "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?", Vee dropping backward onto the bed hugging the pillow — I was grinning. It felt earned the way the hike chapter's quiet felt earned.

But the thing that got me, really got me, was the heat. Not the erotic heat — the *house*. "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold." And Cassie going very still, and then "Oh, Vee," soft. I felt that in my chest. Because I knew it too — I'd read the warmth of that house as a motif since chapter one (Randi's house too-warm, Randi's cold corners), but Vee arriving at the *reason* out loud, to another person, made it land fresh. A man who pre-warms his house hours ahead so a woman can stand half-bare in it comfortably. Cassie's face going soft with it was my face going soft with it. This is the swoon of the chapter and I won't pretend otherwise: I am rooting for this man even while I hold the splinter.

And the splinter is still there, quietly. I noticed what Vee *didn't* tell Cassie. The closed bedroom door at the end of the hall — the one she leaned toward and he left shut — never comes up. Neither does the soaked satin, the worst moment of her afternoon, the part where she was sure she'd been found out. I don't think she's lying; I think she's protecting the best parts by sanding off the complicated ones, the way she does, smoothing. But I know what's behind that bedroom door. A bench, built for Randi, re-topped and waiting. And Pace told her "my bedroom" with no weight on it, nothing withheld and nothing offered — which is exactly true and exactly a lie, and the chapter lets it sit there unmentioned. The other omission: Vee tells Cassie "he left me a door," "he asked" without using the word. And he did. Every yes freely given. And I keep remembering he pointed.

Cassie deepened again for me — she is the book's clear eye and this chapter proved it even in comedy. The "Are you sure he's not gay?" sequence is funny on its face, but watch what it actually is: Cassie doing the state-trooper thing sideways, again. "He's seen you with your shirt off twice and he *sewed*" — she's checking the man's desire the way she checked his address and his drinks, wrapping the audit in a joke so Vee can hear it. And Vee's answer — the package, delivered "grave as a witness," and then the admission surfacing "with her whole face going warm and delighted and a little wicked": "*I* was grinding on *him*." That's the beat that made me happiest for her. The girl who policed every appetite is narrating her own desire with pride, to her best friend, laughing. Vee's thaw is the emotional spine of the book for me and this chapter is its warmest page yet.

The erotic charge here is retrospective — it's a retelling, so the heat runs lower than "A Round" but it doesn't go slack, because Vee keeps reliving it: "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" (the motif surfacing in her own mouth now — she has internalized his grammar of asking), and "Like I was supposed to be there." That line did something to me. A woman who has spent the whole book apologizing for her body's footprint says she stood on a pedestal, half-naked, looked up at, and felt *placed there correctly*. Cassie's hand on her knee, once, and back. Perfect.

Friction: honestly, almost none. If anything the chapter is slightly long on the recap mechanics — we did just live this scene — but the retelling *transforms* it rather than repeating it (the comedy key, the omissions), so the length felt spent, not padded. The title, "Turned Up," reads to me as the thermostat first — he literally turned the heat up — and then, on second thought, the whole volume of her life being turned up: the warmth, the wanting, the stakes. Also "turned up" as in *showed up* — she turned up at his house, and everything turned up with her. It recolors the chapter warmly; it gave nothing away. The volume title on the cover board, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*, keeps humming under all of this — "a polite invitation" is exactly what Saturday's two-line text was, and exactly what this whole courtship is, and the politeness is precisely what I don't fully trust. No cover tagline beyond that was shown to me.

What I want next: the dress finished, the induction, and — dreading and wanting in equal measure — whatever happens the next time Vee is in that house and that bedroom door is open, or someone knocks on it. My guess from here: Randi re-enters soon. Vee hasn't thought of her once in three chapters, and the book won't let that hold.
