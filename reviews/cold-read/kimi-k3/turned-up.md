## Reader reaction

I did not expect this chapter and I loved it. After the riser, the book walks straight back into the dorm room and makes Vee tell it — and I spent the whole chapter doing the math of what she'd say and what she'd keep. She says more than I feared and less than I hoped, and the shape of the cut is the whole story: everything where she was *powerful* or *cared for* gets told; everything where she was *ashamed* stays home. The riser — told. The thermostat — told, and it slips out of her almost against her will ("Vee had not meant to say this part yet, or maybe not at all"), which means her control leaks exactly the way his does, except his leaks delight and hers leaks evidence of his care. The grinding — told, with her "whole face going warm and delighted and a little wicked," because that's the version where she's the agent. But the wet spot: nowhere. Her mother's voice: nowhere. And the two things I was holding my breath over — the pinprick kiss and "Keep still" — she keeps. She hoards them. I noticed myself physically relax when she didn't give them away, which tells me how much they'd come to feel like mine too.

Cassie is the best chapter she's had. She's the sharpest reader in the book: "Did he make that too?" — she solves the tagless shirt in one move, a riddle Vee "filed under weird things about him" and gave up on. She checks the consent architecture without ever using the word — "He asked." / "He didn't use the word. But yes." — and the book's whole mechanism is right there in that exchange, witnessed now by a second person. And then, bless her, "Are you sure he's not gay?" — which is honestly the only rational hypothesis available from the evidence as cut, and it made me laugh and it made me notice my own position: Cassie reads him as a puzzle, Vee reads him as gospel, and I'm sitting here with the jacket copy in my lap knowing neither of them has the full hypothesis space. Nobody in this world has proposed "designed." Nobody would.

Where my trust sits: nothing on this page earned new suspicion. Randi is absent a sixth chapter and — first time — not even *named*. The itch goes quiet rather than away. This chapter lowered my guard, and I'm aware it lowered my guard.

Erotic charge: secondhand but real. The present scene is two friends and a thrown pillow, but the reportage simmers — the package at the back seam, "I was grinding on *him*," and especially "the image had come back, the two of them caught in the mirror, and she needed a second with it" — the narrative blushing on her behalf, withholding from *me* this time. The "Oh, Vee" over the thermostat is the emotional peak of the chapter, and notably it's about heat, not bodies.

Friction: almost none. One pattern-itch, small: Vee's interpretive confidence in his silence is total and the book keeps validating it ("May I look," "He left me a door"). Cassie's gay hypothesis is the first time anyone has read him differently — and even that resolves Vee's way within a page. Somewhere down the line I think I need one of her readings to be *wrong*, or the unsaid-grammar becomes a trick I stop believing.

The title: "Turned Up" meant nothing to me until "He turned the heat up before I got there," and then it bloomed — the thermostat, yes, but also Vee herself, turned up, lit, volume raised; facts turning up (the shirt's provenance); and — it hit me a beat later — a hem is literally *turned up*. Dressmaking term, heat, arousal, surfacing. It recolors the last chapter rather than giving anything away. The book's title keeps working: this chapter is full of polite invitations — the door left open, "May I look." The series title still sits cryptic and faintly warning-shaped; I can't parse it and I'm not meant to yet.

What I want next: the call. "He'll call me when it's ready" — the unfinished dress is now the runway, and at the end of the runway is the induction dinner with her mother in the room, the mother whose voice said *stripper* and *Floozie*, meeting the dress made by the man who turned the heat up. That collision is what I dread. Guesses from here: a second fitting before the dinner (guess); Pace and the mother meet at the induction (guess); Randi re-enters at or around it, after six chapters dark (guess).
