## Reader reaction

I came in expecting the jacket's setup — the couple, the picked-out girl, the game. Instead the book opens with just the two of them, three weeks in, and no game anywhere on the page. Vivienne doesn't exist here. What I got instead was one of the most careful, patient, quietly devastating sex scenes I've read — and by the end I understood why the book started here: because the first person caught in the tagline's trap isn't the girl who doesn't know. It's Randi. Every yes she gives is real — "Show me," "Red," "Yes" to staying — and none of it protects her from being *seen*. That's the trap, already working, inside the supposedly safe couple.

Pace unsettles me in a way I can't cleanly resolve. On the page he is almost impossibly attentive: the apple sliced thin because she'll eat thin and not thick, cut sides up; the water bottle left out in advance; "he backed off and let her decide." He notices the bracing she learned from men who hurried. He sets his own arousal down — "That's not what this night was for." And yet. The narration juxtaposes two sentences with nothing between them — "He had begun building it seven months ago. He had met her three weeks ago" — and then lets him answer her "For me?" with "For you. Tonight." That's not a lie, but it's not the truth either. And there's pride in him: "the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than she had ever been taken." The Brooke gambit — naming her sorority president mid-scene to break her script — worked on me, it genuinely did, but she never consented to *that*, and the text knows it: "a woman in a position she had not consented to even though she had consented to the position." That line gave me a chill and also felt like the one moment the author showed his hand — a thesis sentence wearing scene-clothes.

Randi broke my heart. The childhood voice surfacing — "a voice she had not used since she was a small child in tears asking her mother for something her mother was not going to give her" — gutted me. And then the bathroom. The whole book pivots in that mirror: "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." I watched her decide, "quiet as a deadbolt going over," that the girl he found is going back behind the face, *not ever* — and then walk out and lie to him with her best smile, and watched him buy it completely: "He thought that this had been a remarkably good evening." The man who can read her pulse from across the room cannot read her across a bathroom door. That divergence — his triumph, her seal — is where the chapter lives, and I know both sides of it. The flinch at "You're beautiful" is the smallest, saddest beat in the chapter.

The erotic charge is real and it peaks where the emotion fuses with it — the third round of begging, the voice coming "from below," and "There you are, Randi." The long undressing and body-inventory passages risk feeling like a catalog, but the prose's patience is Pace's patience, so I read it as character. Heat-wise this is full throttle, sustained.

The title "The Bench" meant nothing before and everything after — a hidden-purpose object sitting in plain sight, disguised as furniture, exactly like the woman on it. The last paragraph, the bench "waiting for the next time," is quietly menacing. *A Polite Invitation* reads now as Pace's whole method — he never takes, he holds doors, "Come with me" — and the series title *With a Long Spoon* hums against all that feeding: he who sups with the devil needs one, and I don't yet know who the devil is here. What I want next: Vivienne, obviously — and I dread the bench's "for you" turning out to be elastic. Guess from here: the game hasn't started yet on-page, and Randi's "not ever" will not hold, because the jacket says she's falling too.

## Carry-forward state

**Principals**

- **Vivienne Thorne** (jacket only; has not appeared or been named on any page): the girl the jacket says Randi and Pace picked as a third and told nothing.

**Relationship ledger**
- **Randi & Pace:** Enormously moved — by intimacy on his side, by asymmetry on hers. First bench scene; he dismantled her "wanted-position" script; she begged in a voice that was *hers*; orgasm, tears, tender aftercare, "Stay tonight" / "Yes." He feels triumph and tenderness (an unspoken word "stayed behind his face"); she feels terror at having been found, flinches at "You're beautiful," and re-seals herself while he watches, fooled. Watching them, I felt the scene's beauty and then grief — I know the evening's true ending and he doesn't.
- **Randi alone with herself:** The mirror is the wound: being seen reached *her* for the first time and it was ecstatic and unaffordable; she re-armored and chose the face. Her unnoticed bottle-cap-under-the-nail tells me the re-entry costs her pain.
- **Pace alone with himself:** Satisfaction, patience, a managed erection, plans to "answer her hunger" again. No self-doubt on the page.
- **Brooke & anyone:** no change — a name so far.

**What I know that they don't**
- Pace doesn't know: she locked the bathroom (a first), cried again in there, and decided *not ever* — the woman he "found" is going back behind the face; her returning smile was a lie; her "yes" to staying coils around an unresolved plan. He thinks: remarkably good evening, resting woman.
- Randi doesn't know: the bench was begun **seven months ago**, long before they met — his "For you. Tonight" elided that. Nor the full depth of his study of her (apple slices, her food-policing, the missing furniture inference he watches her not make).
- I know (jacket only, on no page yet): these two are meant to pick Vivienne as a third and tell her nothing. Watching this chapter's tenderness with that loaded in my hand is the book's first irony — the attentiveness that undid Randi is the same faculty that picked a girl across a quad.

**Motifs (running shape)**
- **Locks, deadbolts, keys:** she locks his front door behind her "first thing, before anything else"; the bathroom lock is a first in his house; her retreat arrives "quiet as a deadbolt going over"; the hum in her ribs is "the size of a key turning in a lock." Sealing = safety.
- **The face vs. her:** the face takes all looking/wanting/touching so nothing reaches further; the smile that lies; "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." Being *seen* is the danger and the ecstasy.
- **Food, portioning, feeding:** thin slices, cut sides up; apples permitted; a girl "portioning herself for years"; he feeds her / she feeds him one bite. Hunger answered is the chapter's love language (and rings the series title).
- **Watching, reading, missed inferences:** he catalogs everything; she can't read the price of his furniture because she can't imagine making; scripts fail on the bench.
- **Hidden-purpose objects:** bench-as-table, top leaning on the wall; tomorrow it's "a low table of odd proportions again" — full of what it is, waiting. People too.
- **"Hi" ritual** (door; upside-down at the bench; mid-edging "Pace." "Hi."; the bathroom return) and **her small breath-laugh** — their re-finding gesture and her tell, the laugh's meaning darkening each time.
- **Gloss vs. warmth:** buffed, polished, expensive surface vs. sweat, salt, flush, the hairs the razor missed — the real body under the finish, which is what he holds.
- **Blue:** the startling eyes; the toenail polish that favors them — "the one small place she let herself step out of line."

**Symbolism noticed**
The bench as being-seen, disguised as furniture and waiting; the deadbolt as the sealed self; the mirror as the recognition chamber; the bottle-cap in the nail bed as the private cost of re-entry.
