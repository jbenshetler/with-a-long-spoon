## Reader reaction

I did not expect the book to open *here* — inside the established couple, three weeks in, no Vivienne anywhere. The jacket told me Randi is the predator, half of a "they" who pick a girl out across a quad. The page hands me Randi straddling her boyfriend in his kitchen, eating apple slices he cut thin because she's trained herself to only eat thin slices, and by midnight she is the one who has been taken apart. So the first thing I feel is a recalibration: whatever game the jacket promised, this chapter is about the method, and the method is being demonstrated *on one of the conspirators*. "He had begun building it seven months ago. He had met her three weeks ago." That sentence pair is the coldest thing in the chapter, dropped without emphasis. The bench wasn't built for Randi. It was built for *someone*, the way you'd build a trap before you'd found the animal. And when she asks "For me?" he answers "For you. Tonight." — which is not the same thing, and she doesn't catch it, and I did.

**Pace.** I am drawn to him and I don't trust him, in roughly equal measure, and the text earns both. The attentiveness is real — the apple cut-sides-up, the water bottle left in advance, "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset," the way he takes her shoes off mid-scene. But there's a running condescension he keeps off his face (the text tells me he keeps it off his face, repeatedly — a man whose interior is itself a locked room): the furniture inference she never makes, the food policing he watches "run through whatever calculation it was running." He *assesses* her. "He had been right that she was showing him only a fraction of who she was" — that's a hypothesis confirmed, not a lover surprised. And the Brooke moment is where my stomach turned: he decides, unilaterally, mid-scene, to bring her sorority into the room — "let her feel which was stronger" — and the narration itself flags the result as "a woman in a position she had not consented to even though she had consented to the position." That's the tagline, arriving on schedule. Every yes was hers. The devastation was not in the yes.

**Randi.** She deepens more in one chapter than most characters get in a book. The polish is a survival structure — the mother with the knife in her voice, the sorority that "took only girls built to the one scale," the face that was "not her" so that what was given away "cost nothing." The bottle-cap pressed under her thumbnail, "she did not seem to know she was doing it," landed like a bruise the text declines to explain. By the bathroom scene — the first time the narration lets me *inside* her — I'm entirely hers. The face that had begged. The smile that "sat on top of the face that had begged." And then the vow: "not ever." The woman who always locks the door behind her, locking the door on herself, "the size of a key turning in a lock." I noticed that rhyme with the first page and it quietly wrecked me.

**The divergence** is the chapter's real ending: he sits there "quietly happy about her," composing a tender little theory about feeding her hunger, while she lies on his shoulder deciding never to be seen again. He thinks he found her. He did — and finding her is exactly what lost her. He doesn't know that. I do.

**Erotic charge:** working, strongly, and fused to dread by the end. The kitchen straddle and the button-by-button undressing are the best of it — patience as foreplay. The bench sequence sustains through its denial loop; the peak for me isn't the orgasm but the *please* breaking — "the please came from below" — followed immediately by the cost. It goes slightly clinical in places ("the edging," "the mechanisms had been his own design") but that flatness is his head, his engineer's register, and it characterizes rather than deflates. The one wobble: "years of cheer written into the muscle of it" — a wink that doesn't belong in a scene this solemn.

**Friction as a reader:** almost none. The mid-scene strike-and-soothe cycles blur a little — I started tallying the "he soothed the place" beats — but the escalation structure carries it. The chapter is long for a single continuous scene and earns it.

**Titles.** "The Bench" tells nothing going in and everything coming out: the ordinary object with a removable top, a polite surface over an engineered purpose — which is also Randi's face, and also, I suspect, the book's whole method. *A Polite Invitation*: the evening is nothing but polite invitations — "Come with me," "Show me," "Stay tonight," "Yes" — and the trap is that they're all genuine. *With a Long Spoon* I read as the old warning about supping with the devil: Randi thinks her persona is a long spoon, a safe distance. The bench is inside the distance. And the tagline now reads less like marketing and more like a diagnosis.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the morning after — the walk past the re-tabled bench the final paragraph promises she won't look at. I dread Vivienne's arrival, because I've now seen the method from inside and the jacket swears she'll never know it's a method. Guesses, marked as guesses: the bench predating Randi means a plan predating Randi, and I wonder whether Randi is his project as much as his partner; I wonder what happened seven months ago; I expect her vow to hold about as well as a smile on a face that begged.

## Carry-forward state

**Principals**

- **Pace** — he/him. Lives alone in a house (jacket: a young mathematician at the end of a long drive; the page itself does not say mathematician — it says middle-class income, a house with an office, kept deliberately warm). Builds his own furniture: kitchen table, oak cabinet, and the bench. Methodical, patient, watchful; keeps his interior off his face as a matter of habit. Reads Randi with a precision that is half tenderness, half assessment, and knows it. In a three-week relationship with Randi. Consent-forward in practice: safewords, check-ins, aftercare, water; sets his own arousal aside. Unilaterally escalated the scene into humiliation-adjacent territory (naming Brooke) without prior negotiation, believing he was freeing her. Ends the chapter believing the night was a beautiful success.
- **Miranda "Randi"** — she/her; 21, sorority member, black hair, startlingly blue eyes, resting expression of slight surprise; dancer's body; expensive quiet things. Raised to register cost by appearance and to police her own appetite (mother's egg whites and tofu; sorority's one scale). Her polished "face" is armor built since thirteen: what isn't her can be given away and cost nothing. Has a barely-acknowledged self-harm tell (bottle-cap pressed under the nail bed, unconscious). Three weeks into her first relationship where anyone has looked *past* the face. Tonight she was seen, begged in a voice she hadn't used since childhood, was called beautiful and meant-it — and responded by vowing, wordlessly and permanently, to put the real self back behind the face: "not ever." Lying to Pace with her best smile by chapter's end, while staying the night at his invitation.
- **Vivienne Thorne** — jacket only; not yet on the page. Per the jacket: a junior, picked by Randi and Pace to be their third, told nothing of the game.

**Relationship ledger**

- **Pace ↔ Randi:** advanced by intimacy on his side, by asymmetry on hers. He took her further than she'd ever been taken, got the real voice, the real begging, the real orgasm — and received, in return, a performed reunion and a locked door (literal, in his bathroom; figurative, everywhere else). He feels tender triumph: she "met more of herself," and "he would answer it again, as often as she wanted to bring it to him." She feels terror at having been found, grief, a nameless interior "hum," and resolve. I watched him misread her stillness as peace; the gap between what he thinks happened and what she has decided is the book's first great irony, and it is entirely mine to hold.
- **Randi alone with herself:** the mirror verdict — the eyes "had crossed something. She could not uncross it." Aliveness came from being seen, and being seen is the one thing she cannot allow again. Watching her reassemble the smile while I knew what it covered was the saddest beat in the chapter.
- **Pace alone with himself:** no crack; composed, satisfied, mildly moved by his own feeding-her-hunger metaphor. I felt warier of him than he has any idea a reader could be.

**What I know that they don't**

- Randi was told the bench was "For you. Tonight." **I know he began building it seven months ago and met her three weeks ago.** She doesn't.
- **Pace does not know Randi's bathroom interior** — the vow, the hum, the lock, the decision that he can have the face but never again the one he found. He thinks it was a remarkably good evening.
- Randi doesn't know he catalogues her failures of perception (the handmade furniture she prices wrong, the food policing) with faint, concealed superiority.
- Randi doesn't know he stayed hard all night and deliberately set it aside as "not what this night was for."
- (Jacket-level, carried loosely: Randi and Pace will together choose Vivienne as a third and tell her nothing; Vivienne's story hasn't started on the page.)

**Motifs** (running shapes, charged instances)

- **Locks and keys:** she always locks his front door behind her, first thing — safety or secrecy, unsettled; the bathroom lock she'd never used before, turned over mid-crisis; her vow arrives "quiet as a deadbolt going over"; the unresolved hum ends the chapter "the size of a key turning in a lock." The lock is where Randi keeps what she won't give.
- **Surfaces and faces:** the face that lies for a living and is "good at lying"; the smile placed on top of the face that begged; the bench that is a table when its top is on; gloss, polish, waxing, lacquer — versus salt, sweat, slick, and the "warmth under it that was his."
- **Appetite and feeding:** apple sliced thin, cut sides up; her permitted foods; the mother's knife-voiced austerity; water she drinks more of than she expects; his concluding theory of her as "a girl who had been portioning herself for years," hunger found and answered.
- **Being seen:** lights kept up since night one; his looking as the act itself; "There you are, Randi"; the mirror where her own eyes are the ones she can't face; the one blue-polished toe-line of permitted deviation.
- **"Hi." / "Hi."** — their greeting ritual, repeated at the door, mid-scene upside-down from the face cradle, and after the bathroom: the token of the unperformed relationship, which by the last use is being performed.

**Symbolism noticed**

The bench/table as the double life of things and people — "full of what it was... waiting for the next time," which reads as a promise or a threat depending on whose next time it is. The deadbolt as self-custody turned self-imprisonment. The apple as appetite under training, fed by hand.

**Open questions**

- Who is Pace, exactly — and what happened seven months ago that started the bench?
- How did he and Randi meet three weeks ago, and did he choose her the way the jacket says they'll choose Vivienne?
- When and how does Vivienne enter? Is the jacket's "game" already underway offstage?
- Will Randi's vow hold — and what will it cost, either way?
- The bottle-cap press: history of self-harm? Will the book return to it?
- Will Brooke and the sorority ever learn his name — and does the secrecy protect her or him?
- Does Pace have any idea what he actually did tonight?

**How I feel**

Toward Pace: attracted, impressed, and steadily uneasy — his tenderness is real and his patience is a tool, and I can't yet tell whether he knows the difference. Toward Randi: strong sympathy and grief; she is a liar by training and tonight she lied to the person who finally wasn't lying to her, and I understand exactly why. Overall mood: a warm room with something cold running under it — the afterglow that isn't shared. I trust the book's surface and distrust everyone in it, which I think is where it wants me.
