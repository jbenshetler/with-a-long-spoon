## Reader reaction

This chapter got me completely — and the fact that it got me is almost the whole point. The froyo date is a swoon: the flavor architecture ("you put chocolate on tart and it's an argument the whole way down"), the grandfather-questions with no wrong answer, the listening that waits out her sentences to the end. "She wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on" — that line landed like a bruise pressed gently. And then her embarrassing confession, and his answer, which is the best thing he's done yet: not "you deserve it," not a speech — "Your tart's holding up." He lets the true thing have its space and then proves, concretely, that what she built can stand. I melted.

But here's what undid me: "Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." The cup leaning, too much, and she finds she doesn't care. That's Vee's cardigan coming off in miniature — one hour of being listened to and the installed voice just... didn't show up. I was genuinely moved, and then I remembered whose voice *also* gets silenced around appetite on this page: Randi's, who polices hers. The book is running the same experiment on both women with opposite conditions.

And Randi — God. "He didn't look like a man who needs to be desperate... He looked like a man who wants you. He wants to see you naked." She took the phone out of Vee's hand to *check*, and then delivered her lover's desire back to Vee as a gift, grinning. "The rule's for boys you're not sure about. You're not unsure." She's right, and it's kind, and it's also stage management. I can't tell anymore whether Randi is generous, performing generosity, or both at full sincerity — and I think the book wants me exactly there. Her certainty unsettles me more than anything Pace does.

Cassie's healer line made me laugh out loud — a whole semester of Vee keeping someone alive in a video game is the most Vee fact imaginable (she manages everyone, at cost, at 2 a.m.) — and it quietly frames the question: is this another person she's going to pour herself into keeping alive?

Trust vs. suspicion: the text earns both. Nothing on the page proves the text-message timing was orchestrated, but twenty minutes after he left, with Randi right there having texted someone earlier? My suspicion is live but the warmth is real. The erotic charge is low but simmering — "He wants to see you naked" said by the woman Vee is actually attracted to, about the man she's falling for, is a three-way current running under a conversation about frozen yogurt.

The title, "The Long Way": they "walked the long way, not toward anything" — the date literally named. But it also reads as the design's method: the slow, indirect route to a destination nobody's announced. Both fit. That pleases and worries me.

What I want next: the second date, and the reveal I keep dreading. My guess from here: when Vee learns whose Pace is, "your tart's holding up" will either save them or be the cruelest line in the book.
