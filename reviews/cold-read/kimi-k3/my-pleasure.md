## Reader reaction

I needed this chapter and I hated how much I needed it. I've known since chapter one that Randi and Pace are lovers, known since chapter three that Vee was chosen between them — but knowing it and sitting inside it are different things. This is the first time I've watched the two of them use Vee out loud, mid-act, as fuel between them, and it landed in my stomach like a stone wrapped in silk.

Because it's beautiful. That's the awful part. The sex itself is gorgeous — tender, playful, mutually fluent, two people who know each other's bodies like a language they invented. "Showing off." "Now it's mutual." The cleanup that turns into something else, the deadpan "Elegant." / "Thank you. I value your professional opinion." I laughed. I was turned on. And all the while they are trading intelligence about Vee like handlers comparing notes: *She kisses me first now.* *She kissed my neck again.* *I nearly turned around.* The thing Vee told Randi across a white cloth on Thursday — the kiss on the neck, the ticklish deflection — is here, in Pace's bed, being converted into arousal. I watched Vee deliver that material believing it was sealed. The harvesting is no longer something I infer; it's the scene's engine.

And yet — God — the chapter keeps refusing to let me file Randi under villain. "I miss her during the day. More at night." That's not strategy; that's a woman confessing in the only room where she can. "I want her to know exactly what she does to me" — and it *breaks Pace's control*, which tells me how real it is to him too. And then the line that stopped my breath: "She's not the only one in love." No smile, no rescue from the sentence. That's the closest anyone in this book has come to saying the word out loud, and it's Randi saying it *for* Vee and *about herself* in the same breath, to the one man who can't say it at all. The triangle's unspoken center, spoken — and still not to the person it's about.

The detail that gutted me quietly: Randi kisses his palm, and says "You know she loves you, right?" and his face softens *before he can do anything about it*. She sees it. She stays on it. She is more intimate with his unwatched face than Vee has ever been allowed to be — and she just spent Thursday night teaching Vee that tasting herself off a lover's mouth is "powerful," and here she is, doing exactly that, theatrically, twice. She was rehearsing Vee's education on herself. Or demonstrating the curriculum. I can't decide which, and the chapter doesn't let me.

The title — "My Pleasure" — does triple duty and all three are loaded. It's the service phrase, the pleasure-slave/princess game Vee invented Saturday morning, here played for real between the two people who invented *her*. It's Randi's answer to everything: her pleasure, claimed without apology, "arrives without apology" — the exact thing she's trying to teach Vee to have. And it's what neither of them says to Vee: the pleasure is theirs, taken at her expense, in her name, with her name "staying in the room between their breathing" like a third body. Against the volume title — *A Warm Reception* — this is the warmest reception in the book so far, and the most chilling.

What I dread next: Vee "arriving at it on her own," per Randi's plan — and now I've seen the arrival being choreographed from this bed. What I want, guiltily: more of this room. Randi and Pace are the book's dark hearth and I can't look away.
