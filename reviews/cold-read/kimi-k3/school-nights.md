# Cold read (grounded) — School Nights

## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This one landed quiet and low and exactly right — a cooling breath after the run of heat, and I needed it more than I knew. The whole chapter is two girls in a dorm room past eleven, one lit up, one dry as toast, and it held me completely.

The tomato burn undid me a little. Not the burn itself — the way Vee's hand went to the side of her breast "without her knowing it moved," and Cassie's flat "There's only one way *that* burn happens. Not enough on." I laughed, and then it ached: Vee is carrying his house around in her body now, marks and stories both, and she hands Cassie the sweet version, the one where the wound is a tomato. She doesn't mention the spank, the bet, the heels, any of it — and I noticed, because the checkpoint of my own reading is full of how much she *does* tell Randi. The two friendships have sorted themselves by what they can hold, and it hurts that the trustworthy one gets the smaller share.

"You don't come home school nights" is the hinge, and it's a gentle devastation. Not a question. Cassie just waits while Vee counts back her own week and finds her bed empty more often than not. There's no scolding in it — "Mm" and "whatever you say" — but the observation sits there doing its work. She is being absorbed. Her clothes are loose, her drawer is assigned, her nights are elsewhere, and the person watching the shape of it from outside is the one with the cracked window and the book against her knees. I trust Cassie completely and I wanted to shake Vee, kindly: *this is the one you should be telling everything to.* Cassie never says a word against Pace. She just keeps the record.

And then "Do you love him," said the same flat way as everything else — and Vee, who has not stopped talking since the door, goes small. "Maybe. I think so." Here's the thing that got me: I was *just* in her chest in "Above Him," where she set the word down plainly and let it be true — *she loved him*, full stop, counting all the ways he said it without saying it. So the "maybe" is a lie she tells out of modesty, or out of not wanting to be seen mid-fall, and the truer sentence is the one that follows: "It's all so fast." That's the real confession. Not doubt — velocity. And Cassie lets the maybe stand, doesn't say *I know*, just goes back to her book. The restraint of that scene is beautiful.

No suspicion moved in me this chapter — nobody did anything — but the quiet itself has a weight I couldn't shake. The reader knows what "it's all so fast" can't yet know: the speed isn't hers alone. She thinks she's falling at the pace of her own heart, and I know there's a grade on the path. Nobody on this page earned unease; the unease is entirely what I carried in.

The title, "School Nights," is doing two things at once and I like both: the literal — the nights she's supposed to be home, the ordinary student life quietly emptying out of her week — and the tonal. There's something almost parental in the phrase, a curfew word, and it makes her new life read as both liberation and truancy. Against the volume title — *With a Long Spoon · Book One: A Polite Invitation* — the spoon keeps being the food (now tomatoes, breadcrumbs, eaten greedily to the point of a burn) and the invitation keeps widening: from a dinner, to a drawer, to all the nights that aren't school nights. The cover's politeness is exactly what Cassie is too kind to name.

Next I want — and dread — the moment Cassie's record-keeping becomes a question she can't not ask. And I want Vee to say "I love him" to *Pace*, or hear it, because the unsaid word now has real mass. (Guesses from here, both.)
