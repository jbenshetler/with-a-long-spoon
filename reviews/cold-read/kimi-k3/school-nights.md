## Reader reaction

This is the quietest chapter in the book and it landed like a hand on my shoulder. One room, two girls, past eleven — and the first time Vee has ever told anyone about Pace who isn't Randi. I didn't realize how much I'd been bracing during every prior telling until this one came with no lever in it. Cassie listens "with no face on at all," asks two flat questions, and lets both answers stand. I trusted her completely, and I noticed myself trusting her, because the book has trained me to watch the listener.

The telling itself undid me a little. Not any one night but "the shape her weeks had taken, which was the truer thing now." The soup story — him getting a cold cloth on the burn "before she'd got the first swear out," still asking about it two days later. Him watching her eat "like that was the show." And the line I keep turning over: the house "warm as a held breath now, warm enough that she never once thought about how much she had on." I believe the tenderness and I heard the acclimation in the same sentence. A held breath belongs to a body that's waiting. I don't know if the page wanted me to feel that, but I felt it.

The two wordlessnesses are the chapter's heart. Vee, who "never" runs out of words, hits the end of *fine* and finds nothing after it, just both hands up at the size of him. And then "Do you love him" — flat, no question mark, asked like the others — and she goes small: "Maybe. I think so." Last chapter she admitted it whole in her own chest and breathed "Peter" at a sleeping man. Awake, to the safest person she has, she can only spend a *maybe*. That gap between what her chest holds and what her mouth can pay out is the most honest thing the book has done with her. And "It's all so fast" is the truest sentence she's said aloud all novel.

Cassie deepened into my favorite person in the book. "You don't come home school nights" — not a question — and Vee counts back and finds her own bed empty more nights than not. Cassie has been keeping count without saying so. And then the line that killed me: "She didn't say a thing against it, and she didn't say I know." Randi would have said *I know* — said it first, said it better, made it hers. Cassie just goes back to her book. "Go to sleep, streetlight." The streetlight bit becoming roommate shorthand — "You're a public utility" — is exactly how a friendship sounds, and it's the only friendship in this book I don't suspect of anything.

No suspicion earned on this page by anyone. All my standing dreads stand, compounded by absence — that's two chapters now with no Randi, and the unextracted store keeps growing: the reunion, the drawer, "Peter," and now a spoken love-hedge that exists outside her file. The love is *portable* now. It's been said aloud once. I dread who hears it second.

The title names Cassie's evidence — the migration is visible from outside, priced in empty weeknights. First title that's come from the world's side of the door, not the house's. And the tagline keeps rhyming: nobody has asked her for a single thing, and she's rearranged her bed, her meals, her weight, her week. Every yes freely given. *With a Long Spoon* — she's supping nightly now, devouring, and the spoon gets shorter every chapter.
