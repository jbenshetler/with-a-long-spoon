## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction — "broken-in"**

The title did it to me before I'd read a word, and then the chapter went and earned it twice. "Broken-in" — like shoes, like a horse, like a lead you stop fighting. And this whole chapter is Vee being gentled: washed, walked, dressed, shod, knotted, kissed, and sent back down the white-columned steps "already planning." What unsettles me most is how good it felt. I was happy for her. I watched her climb out of the sad-clown sink, bite the apple, find her stride, and I felt the lift of it — a woman with a plan and a friend who opens the door in a robe and says "Oh, honey. Come up." The warmth is real on the page, and I let it warm me, and that is exactly the mechanism the book has been building since the pointing game. I keep catching myself purring while being stroked, same as her.

Randi. God. This is the fullest we've ever seen her work, and the most I've ever liked her, which is its own alarm bell. The reframe — "You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about," "You were so far ahead you couldn't see the race" — is genuinely kind and genuinely true, and it also happens to be the exact thing that re-loads Vee and aims her back at Pace on a *delay*, on *Randi's* timing: "Not today." That stopped my breath a little. The instruction is dressed as wisdom, and maybe it is wisdom, but I know what Vee doesn't — that Randi is calibrating the delivery of a thing she and Pace own together. And then "He won't text you, by the way... If he's the man you've just described to me" — the *casualness* of it. She doesn't have to guess what that man does after a fight. She's slept in his bed; she knows he sets the drinks down and leaves. The sentence performs deduction while stating knowledge, and it slid past Vee entirely. Also Brooke: "Oh — you're the stats friend." The house has a folder on her. Brooke smiling wider, giving back "nothing but more warmth." Small chill, real, earned.

The shoe scene is the hottest thing in the book that involves no sex at all, and I'm not sure it's close. The skirt falling "the whole length of her over the work" so Vee can only *feel* — cool hands past the hem, the hand up under the skirt to her thigh holding her tipped on one foot, the thumb pressing once into the hollow behind her knee — I read that with my shoulders up around my ears. And Vee's hope that her breath catching "wasn't out loud." The architecture of the shoe that "took her foot and meant it," being given no say making her *surer* — the book is not being subtle about what this is teaching her body to love, and I don't want it to be subtler. It's Randi's mouth forming the word Pace never got to say: tending, positioning, holding. First the lead on the dance pad, now the hands under the hem. "Broken-in." And Vee noticing, a half-beat late in the foyer, that she kissed Randi back *without bracing* — "It was only what they did" — that line landed on me like the chapter's true last sentence even though it isn't. Something crossed in her and she filed it under friendship. The scarf lesson, cool hands over warm, "that's the whole difference between dorm mixer and dinner in Paris" — I would have walked into traffic too.

Pace is entirely absent from this chapter and more present than in half the ones he's in. Everything Randi selects — the neckline down, the cognac heels that hold, the scarf tied snug "so it'll hold its shape by the second drink" — is an outfit being assembled for *his* door, and Vee is letting herself be dressed for him by the woman who shares his bed. The dramatic irony has never been denser or less winking. And the detail that gutted me quietly: Randi's sparse cocoa freckles she "plainly never gave a thought to," next to Vee's ten minutes burying hers. The whole book's wound in one small unremarked contrast — shame as labor one woman does and the other was never billed for.

Friction, honestly, very little. The bartender's "wasn't him they were jealous of" retold secondhand lost a touch of its sting in the retelling but gained Randi's soft "jealous of *them*," which was better. If anything I'm registering that Vee's recovery moved fast — one sleep, one apple, one reframe — but the text owns that: the plan is the drug, and Randi is the pharmacist. No tonal miss. The Edna Mode laugh was perfectly placed, pressure-release right before the heat.

The book's title and volume play against this one beautifully: *With a Long Spoon* — you feed someone with a long spoon when you don't want to get close, and everything here is about how *close* the feeding has gotten. *A Polite Invitation* — and here is the invitation being accepted by a woman who still doesn't know one was extended. "Broken-in" answers to both.

What I want next: tomorrow night, the door, the scarf tied by her own hands. What I dread: that it goes perfectly, and that the perfection is the trap closing. And I keep thinking about what Randi gets out of this morning — not the errand, the *hour*: Vee in her bra in her room, bare feet on her carpet. (A guess from here: Randi's Pole is tipping, and the threesome she's engineering may be the one thing she's honest about wanting.)
