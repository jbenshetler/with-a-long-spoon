## Reader reaction

This one landed squarely in my chest. The sex is warm and unhurried and real — the worked soreness she's "glad of," the hair like a red curtain, the kisses wandering up her breast that "rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end." Pace keeps doing the thing this book does best: tenderness at full explicitness. And then the photograph. God, the photograph. He arranges her like a woman in an old calendar, kisses each place his hands set her, tilts the lampshade, picks the shot, grins "like a boy who'd gotten away with something." I melted. And then Vee's own cold sane thought arrived right on schedule, and mine arrived with it, doubled: she makes him promise he'll never share *her*, and he answers — precisely — "I'd never share your photograph." She hears one thing; the sentence technically says another. The page doesn't underline it. But I've read this book long enough to feel the seam in that vow, and watching the bar cross to *Delivered* hurt a little, because believing him "was the brave part" and I cannot tell yet whether her bravery is safe.

Then the parents. "I don't go back." Four words, each set down clean, no door left open — the first wall I've ever hit in Pace. The scar was never asked about; now this. The man is warm at every scale she can reach and locked at exactly the ones she can't, and she *takes her hand off it* and lets the quiet be kind, which is loving and also exactly what the design would want her trained to do. I don't know which I'm watching. That's the chord of the whole book now.

Sheri at Thanksgiving — the flicker of jealousy, gone in a breath, "she's alone." It read kind to me. Genuinely kind. And that's almost worse: I can't find the cruelty anywhere on the page, only in the spaces between pages.

The cooking scene is the book's signature at full strength — the lemon zest in "fine bright rain," the wine poured into the pan "to make her genuinely sad," the butter turning the sauce "from bright to silk." "She had used to just eat. Now she tasted... and she knew exactly whose doing that was." Yes. That's the seduction rendered as education, and it's gorgeous and it is also the mechanism.

Title check: "In His Hands" — the posed body, the phone, the picture, the cooking, all of it. She's literally arranged by his hands and happy to be. It doesn't feel like a spoiler; it feels like the book saying the quiet part on the cover. What I dread next: Thanksgiving apart, her family seeing the glow, and whatever Pace's "I don't go back" is sitting on top of.
