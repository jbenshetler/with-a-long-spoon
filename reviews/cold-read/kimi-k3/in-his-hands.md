## Reader reaction

The warmest chapter in the book so far, and the one that left me coldest in a single specific spot.

The opening pages are lovely in the way this book does lovely: the aftermath, his head on her breast, the kisses that are "not about sex for him" — and then his lips close over her and spoil her theory, "and pleased her to no end." That little turn is the whole relationship in miniature: she builds a story about him, he gently exceeds it. "No man had ever wanted her body like this one did" — a girl who *has* a body. I believe it. I believe him, mostly, page by page.

And then: "Who does this to you when I'm not around?" — "I take care of it myself. Thinking of you." I stopped there. Because I know about Randi, and nothing on any page has ended Randi-and-Pace, and this is — I went back through my own memory of the book — the first time his *words*, not his silences, have done the managing. Everything before was omission, arrangement, no-note. This is a direct answer to a direct question with no room in it for the woman he also sleeps with. It might be true as far as it goes. It can't be true as far as it goes.

Then the photograph, which is the chapter's real center and the title's. Note the machinery: *she* offers ("Would a picture help?"), *she* sets the condition, *he* poses her with those unhurried sure hands and kisses each place he sets her — shoulder, hip, knee — shoots it on *her* phone, hands it back for her to judge, and she sends it "before the nerve could leave her." Every yes freely given. The tagline is right there in the stage directions. And the vow exchange: "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." — "Never. I'd never share your *photograph*." She said *me*. He answered *photograph*. Maybe that's just how a precise man talks. With this book's cover over my head, it rang like a struck glass. She has now given him the first thing that can be carried — the most portable file of her that exists — and everything else portable about her has traveled upstream to Randi without her knowing. If he keeps this promise, it's the first one of its kind; if he breaks it, she may never find out. Either way I'm braced.

The kitchen idyll after is the book at full honey: the wine cracked in front of her, the lemon zest "in a fine bright rain," the spoon-tastes, and the lovely closed loop of her watching him wear "the exact look he'd described to her in bed" at her first bite. "She had used to just eat. Now she tasted" — and she knows whose doing it was. So do I, and I hold it with both hands: it's real, and it's also exactly what was designed, and the page doesn't make me choose.

Then the two seams, placed back to back like the chapter knew it needed them. "I don't go back." The ease going out of his face, "something level and exact behind," each word "set down clean and apart from the last." That's the first closed door he's ever shown her, and the first glimpse I've had that the warmth is built *over* something. She takes her hand off it and lets the quiet be kind — her not-asking practice, which she already runs on the scar, now has a second account. And he answers her kindness by moving her wine an inch closer, "as if that were the thing that had wanted doing." I find that gesture genuinely moving and faintly terrible, both.

And *Sheri*. Named aloud to Vee for the first time, fond and unguarded — the pie she'll pretend she made, "She's alone." Vee's flicker of jealousy dies on that, and she means her gladness. I sat there with my teeth together, because I know Sheri watched Vee's grief and carried it back across a handlebar, and now the woman who files reports on her is coming to his Thanksgiving table while Vee flies home to Ohio. Vee has a name and a pie story. That's all she has.

"He's all mine," in italics, at the tender peak. The book let her think the falsest sentence available at the exact moment it would land sweetest. The title: his hands posing her, holding the phone, cooking, comforting — everything she is, in his hands, her own idea. The book's title and tagline feel less like marketing every chapter.

What I want next: Thanksgiving on both ends — the Ohio mother (that watcher has been quiet too long) and his table with Sheri. Dread: whether the photograph survives contact with Randi, who has now been absent three chapters and feels like weather coming.
