# Cold read (grounded) — In His Hands

## Reader reaction

The afterglow opening got me first — the red curtain of her hair, his head coming to rest on her breast, and then that thought, dropped so casually it took me a second to feel the knife in it: *He's all mine.* He is not. He never has been. She's lying there happier than she's ever been, in the bed of a man who shares this same bed with her best friend, and the chapter just lets her have the sentence. I ached. And what undid me more is that nothing in the scene is false — the comfort he takes at her breast, the kisses that are "barely kisses," the way he gets hard again just from her — it's all real tenderness, which is exactly what makes the held breath worse. When she thought *they're not about sex for him* and then his lips closed over her anyway and "rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end" — I smiled like an idiot. This book keeps making me coo over a trap.

The photo scene is the chapter's heart and its quiet horror, both. On the page it's the swoon: he won't let her pose herself, arranges her limb by limb, kisses each place his hands have set her — shoulder, hip, knee — tilts the lampshade so the glare slides off her skin, grins at her over the phone "like a boy who'd gotten away with something." She's never been braver: "believing him was the brave part," and she sends it before the nerve can leave her. I was melted. And then the reader in me who knows about Randi heard the exact wording and went cold: she says "promise you'll never share *me* with anyone," and he answers, "I'd never share *your photograph*." That's not the same promise. He narrowed it, gently, in plain sight, and she didn't catch it, and I did, and now I can't un-hold my breath over whether Randi will ever be shown this girl laid out like a calendar. Everything Vee tells Randi flows to Pace; I have no evidence the reverse channel carries her image — but the design has taught me to hear precision when he speaks. Even the detail that it's *her* phone, "because hers took the better picture," reads two ways: sweet, and the cleanest possible chain of custody.

The title, "In His Hands," does triple duty and I love it and fear it. Literally, she's in his hands — arranged, kissed, composed. Then the photograph: her image delivered into his hands, *Delivered* on the screen, "gave him herself to keep." And under both, the thing the chapter never says: she has been in his hands since the quad, since the pointing game she doesn't know happened. The volume title keeps doing its slow work too — *With a Long Spoon*, and this is a chapter where he feeds her, again, watching her first bite "with the exact look he'd described" as the thing he thinks about when he touches himself. He who sups with the devil. A polite invitation. She's so fed, so happy, so loved, and the spoon is getting shorter.

Then the chapter turns on a dime and gives me the thing I've been waiting for since the scar: a second sealed door, and this time I watched it close in real time. "Are you going home?" — "This is my home." — "But your parents." And his face: "The ease went out of it and left something level and exact behind," each word "set down clean and apart from the last." *I don't go back.* Four words, no door left open. It's the same architecture as "Not tonight" — intimacy permitted right up to a painted line, and she does what she did with the scar: takes her hand off it, lets the quiet be kind, keeps him anyway. It deepens him enormously for me. The man who lays his head on her breast and "takes such comfort there" is a man with no mother's table to go back to, and the book put those two facts in the same chapter without drawing the line between them. I drew it.

And then Sheri. *Sheri's coming over. We cycle together. She'll bring a pie and pretend she made it.* Vee has never heard this name before, and I have — I know Sheri watched her across the Rusty Nail, knows about Randi, said "That's not finished," said two at once would be complicated. Vee's little flicker of jealousy, instantly extinguished by "She's alone," plays completely differently from where I sit: the one person in Pace's life who might say something true in front of Vee is about to sit at his Thanksgiving table while Vee is in Ohio. The pie detail is a lovely echo — Vee carried HUMBLE PIE up that hill three weeks ago; now pie arrives as friendship's currency again. And "This is my home" lands as literal fact: the house, the workshop, the warm kitchen — it's the entire world he's built, sealed, and provisioned for exactly the people he chooses.

What I want next, marked as guesses from here: I dread and crave Thanksgiving in equal measure — I want to know whether Vee and Sheri ever share a room, because Sheri is a lit fuse with a loyal tongue. I dread the Ohio trip — the mother's voice in the mother's actual house, with Vee's body now carrying secrets that voice would name. And I cannot stop thinking about where that photograph goes. My pull to keep reading is total. Friction, honestly: almost none — the long lemon-chicken paragraph is the book's love language by now, and I'd have resented its absence more than its length. If anything the chapter is a deliberate calm, and I've learned to be suspicious of calms here — but only because the book earned that, not because I came in guarded.
