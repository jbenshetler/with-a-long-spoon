## Reader reaction

I read this whole brunch with my hand half over my eyes and I could not stop reading. From the first line — "she's already here. Back porch, come on" — I was in two seats at once: Vee's, dazzled and warming herself at "There's my girl," and mine, where I know every sentence she says is being collected. The mimosa detail gutted me before anything even happened — "a mimosa was the cheapest yes on the card, and no one needed to know that was why" — Vee performing ease she has to budget for, in a glassed-in room where nothing is hidden from the light. That little flinch of class under all that sunshine set the whole chapter humming.

And then the telling. I don't think I've ever read a scene where the *narration* of sex is itself the sex act, but that's what this is — Vee laying each piece on the table between them, "the laying-down was its own hand on her," her body keeping faith with every word while Randi draws it out of her one fond, relentless question at a time. "He checked." "He did *not*." I was aroused, genuinely, and I was also watching Randi work, and the two didn't cancel each other — they compounded, which is this book's whole trick on me. The moment that floored me was Vee's face doing "something she couldn't call back" and Randi watching it do the thing "with frank, open pleasure." Randi is not pretending to enjoy this. That's what makes her so dangerous and so — I have to say it — honest, somehow, at the exact center of her dishonesty.

The bathroom undid me. Vee finally *going* — and then Randi's face arriving behind her eyes, "clear, and fond, and near," and her hand stopping. I've spent thirty chapters watching her mother's voice be the thing that polices her body, and here, alone in a stall, the policing voice has a new face: "What would Randi think." That is a transfer of ownership and nobody announced it. And it's worse — *she stopped because being looked at by Randi was too much to bear while touching herself.* Her refusal of herself is now tangled up in wanting Randi in a way she still will not name. I ached for her at the cold tap, wrists under the water like a faint, in that blue light "that found everything and forgave none of it."

Then "Sticky fingers?" and the floor left the room for me too. I was certain — *certain* — Randi knew, had sent her in there with "Do what you need to" like a woman setting a place at a table. And then the tilt toward the pastries, and Vee standing there unable to tell "whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." That's the design in one sentence. I still can't tell, and I think I'm not meant to. Randi took exactly one bite of pastry, said "Delicious" in a way that closed the subject, never touched it again — the discipline of it, next to Vee's enormous unmanaged appetite, is a whole character study in a bite.

The ending made me put my hand over my mouth. "You're my best friend in the whole world" — and Vee, who has defenses against jealousy and shame and her mother, has "no guard up against at all" this, and the cold morning goes "briefly luminous," and I *believed* her joy while knowing it's being spent. Then the kiss, the double press she's stopped bracing for — and the last line: the glaze of a pastry Vee never touched, on her own lip, via Randi's mouth. She was fed a bite she never took. She has Randi's taste in her mouth and will go home thinking it was only a goodbye. That is the most quietly obscene image this book has given me and there was no sex in it. It's tender and it's a violation and it is exactly the ch-1 apple, sliced thin because she'll eat it that way, come back around — except this time she doesn't know she's eating.

Titles: "One Bite" played innocuous all chapter — brunch, a pastry — and then detonated on the last line; it recolors Randi's whole one-bite performance at the table as foreplay she conducted in public. And the volume title, *With a Long Spoon — Book One: A Polite Invitation*: the old proverb about supping with the devil is sitting over this chapter like a lampshade — a polite invitation to a meal where the food gets into you by a route you never agreed to. What I want next: I want someone — anyone, Cassie, please Cassie — to see this. I dread the day Vee learns Randi and Pace share a bed, and I now dread almost as much the day Vee realizes what her own body has been doing across brunch tables. *Guess from here:* Randi will report the bathroom to Pace — and I think some part of her will lie about whose face was in it.
