# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: kimi-k3 · memory: raw ch001..ch008 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read:**

This chapter undid me, and it undid me *knowing what I know* — which is the whole strange pleasure of it. On the page, read straight, it's one of the swooniest first-date chapters I've been given: the hidden street she'd never turned down in two years, Christine's face easing at the sight of him, the dog-eared menu she lets defeat her, "May I choose?" and the relief of handing the choice across. The food writing is where I melted — the bread torn against the heat, the steam, the sharp green thing and then the smoke-dark thing, "one to wake you and one to fold you under." And the hollow. "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the book's whole subject arriving as dinner: a girl who has been portioning herself (like Randi with her apple slices, like Vee at the froyo shop waiting for her mother's voice that didn't come) finally, *finally* fed, and not watching herself eat for once. I wanted this for her with my whole chest.

And I couldn't stop holding the other book over it. I know things Vee doesn't. I know Pace goes home to a woman who picked Vee out on a quad, who asked "Do you think she'll like the bench?", who watched him court her in a coffee shop and reported it back over wine with "Tell me." Every grace note of this evening — "She was already learning she could trust where he was taking her," the hand that "felt less like something beginning than something resolving" — lands double: genuinely tender, and also *proceeding according to plan*. The menu she can't read is the whole situation: "each soft sound a closed door with something behind it… content for once not to know." She has no map. She says so, laughing. I have the map, or part of one, and it made the sweetness ache instead of curdle — because I've been inside Pace's head and he isn't faking the tenderness. He noticed her appetite like he noticed Randi's. He meant it. That's what's so destabilizing: the scheme and the sincerity are the same gesture.

The small observations gutted me one by one. The damp hair and razor-blush jaw, read as *recently, for this* — care running "down to the part of him she'd only get close enough to smell." The right-hand rule she notices without knowing why, "one more thing he knew that she didn't" — that line chilled me gently, because it's the entire relationship in miniature. Him licking the drip off his hand mid-sentence, and her losing the thread, and then — the beat I loved most — *letting it stay lost*. "She found she didn't need to follow the words exactly to be inside the warmth of them, and the not-needing-to was the most restful thing she had felt in as long as she could remember." A woman whose whole life is monitoring, granted an evening off. The tea surfacing her out of it like the pool chapter in reverse — the mint waking her by degrees the way the green water had put her down.

And the ending is perfect because it's hers. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — the old reflex coming back at the worst moment, the rule already in her mouth — and he doesn't push, doesn't lean, just smiles and lets it close and starts walking. And *she* stops. Her feet stay. The hand pulls taut. "But — I'm making an exception." After a chapter where every good thing arrived by her handing choices to him, the kiss is the one thing she reaches back and takes. I noticed that. The page made sure I noticed that. It matters to me that the exception was her move.

Cassie's single beat is doing quiet work: "That's not a boy. That's a man" — said without teasing, which from Cassie is almost an omen. I keep remembering "Goodbye, Vee." Cassie reads rooms flatter and truer than Vee does, and her steadiness makes me trust the evening less, or trust it differently — she's the only person in this book who isn't inside some performance or design, and she's standing at the edge of this one watching.

The title does beautiful, quiet damage. "May I Choose" is three words about hummus, and it's also the question the whole book is asking without Vee hearing it: who is choosing whom? Randi chose her on the quad. Pace chose her across a coffee shop. Tonight he asks — exquisitely, sincerely — for permission to choose her dinner, and she says yes, please, and means it, and the smallness of the ask is exactly what makes the largeness of what's already been decided *for* her land so hard. The book title — With a Long Spoon, Book One: A Polite Invitation — now reads to me as the whole mechanism: an invitation so polite, so warm, so well-set that you sit down at the table before you've read the menu. I don't think Vee is in danger exactly — I've seen Pace's consent practice with Randi, "tell me your word," and I trust his patience — but I think she is being *carried*, and the dread I feel is not that they'll hurt her but that the night she finds out how much of this was arranged, the sweetness retroactively changes shape. What I want next: the two threads to meet — Randi and Vee in a room with the truth closer to the surface. What I dread: that scene going wrong, and the hum in someone's ribs.
