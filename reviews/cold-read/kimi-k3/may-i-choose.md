# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

I didn't expect a chapter this soft to work on me this hard. After the bench, after the pointing game, after watching Randi weaponize a two-minute lawn collision, I came into "May I Choose" braced for the machinery — and instead Pace took Vee to a little Lebanese place and fed her, and I just… melted. The whole dinner is rendered as relief: the dog-eared menu, "May I choose?", the food arriving as soft promises turned solid, the hollow that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line got me. Vee's hunger — literal, maternal, the mother-voice that says *that's plenty* — is the thread this book keeps pulling, and here it gets *answered*, simply, with bread and smoke and no one watching her eat. She stops monitoring herself and doesn't notice she's stopped. That's the swoon. That's the whole chapter, and it worked.

The kiss under the tree is one of the best things the book has done with consent so far. She announces her rule, half meaning it; he accepts it instantly, no lean, no charm — "just smiled, like she'd told him something true and fine about herself" — and then *she doesn't go*. The feet staying, the hand pulling taut, "I'm making an exception." And then he leaves the last inch to her, twice. I felt that in my chest. The mint in both their mouths, him smiling inside the kiss. Peak tenderness, and earned at every step.

And underneath it — because I know things Vee doesn't — the hum of unease. I watched Pace build the bench for Randi; I've read what his attention does, how thoroughly he reads a woman and hands her back her own wanting. So when he keeps the street side without making anything of it, when he doesn't tell her where they're going, when his hand-take feels "less like something beginning than something resolving" — I can't tell whether I'm reading devotion or craftsmanship, and I think the book *wants* me unable to tell. That's not a complaint. That's the design of my unease, and it's delicious. Also: Randi pointed Vee out. Randi said "you should go out with him." Every sweetness in this chapter is happening inside a frame Vee can't see, and I keep holding both facts at once.

Cassie's "That's not a boy. That's a man" landed warm and also slightly ominous to me — Cassie, who gets the flat great-aunt look from everyone, is the only person in this book whose readings I fully trust, and here even she seems charmed by proxy. Small friction: the chapter is almost *too* seamless in its softness — the elevator-doors beat is the only stumble Vee's allowed, and it's adorable rather than costly. But I wasn't bored for a second.

The title: "May I Choose" is Pace's line at the menu, but it inverts on a second look — the chapter is about Vee choosing, again and again: to say yes, to break her rule, to close the distance. He offers; she takes. Whether that freedom is real or the most sophisticated enclosure yet is exactly what I can't resolve, and don't want resolved yet. The cover board — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — keeps recoloring: the long spoon is for supping with someone dangerous, and "a polite invitation" now reads as Pace's entire method. May I. May I choose. The manners are the mechanism.

What I want next: I dread, warmly, the moment Vee's world and Randi's world touch again with this kiss in it. Randi has been told the girl is being courted — how does that play at the next coffee? And *guess from here*: the exception Vee just made is the first of many, and Pace knew it would be.
