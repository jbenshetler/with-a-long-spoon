## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurt. The warmest scene in the book so far and the one that scared me most, and both of those are the same scene, and that's the whole point, isn't it.

**Randi, first — she's back, and I watched her work.** After three chapters of silence she has Vee by the wrist before Cassie is even up the aisle. And the first thing she does is fumble the man's name — "Paul?" — and my stomach dropped, because I know whose lover Pace is, and there is no world in which this woman doesn't know his name cold. "'Pace.' She said it back, already past it." That's a mask going on, and the page plays it as a cute beat. Then the catastrophe story — the French-restaurant date who talked about himself all night — which is just a little too perfectly the anti-Pace, a foil built to make Vee's story come out shaped the right way. "Tell me he is nothing like that." I can't prove the date never happened. I also can't unsee that Randi's cover requires her to be single and looking, and that this story does that job too.

And then the hinge of the chapter, the thing I'll carry: Vee starts to give the kiss the way she gave it to Cassie — *I made an exception*, the dignified version, the version where it's **hers** — and Randi says, soft and near tender, "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." And Vee "felt it leave her instead as the smaller, truer thing. '…Yeah,' she said. 'Because of him.'" One chapter ago her testimony was "I did it. Me." Twenty-four hours later, in a brighter room, under a warmer gaze, the authorship of her own desire gets reassigned — freely, gladly, as the price of being understood. Nobody took it from her. She handed it over, the way she handed over the menu. Every yes freely given. I wanted to shake her and I also completely understood why it felt *good* — Randi makes surrender feel like intimacy, and that's the scariest skill in the book.

**The Cassie beats gutted me.** The folded-away look at the top — Cassie's eyes going to Randi lit up and reaching for Vee, and "whatever had been in them was folded away" — and then "I'll see you at the dorm," easy, already going. The sentinel saw the claim being made and yielded the field, and Vee *did not watch her go*. And then, worse: Vee, who could have stopped at "she's got practice," adds that the smoothie place "isn't really her thing" — the leggings, the matched hair — dismissing the girl who blessed her twelve hours ago, to smooth the road for Randi. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That small turning thing is her conscience, and she files it. Between that and the oat milk she ordered because Randi was standing there — "let herself not look at that either" — this is a chapter of tiny self-betrayals, each one trivial, each one freely chosen, each one costing her a piece of the self she was so proud of founding last chapter.

**The claiming.** When Brooke — finished, cool, deep-water Brooke, with her silent friend doing a slow appraisal-circuit of Vee — asks "who's your new friend," Vee shrinks herself to nothing: "We have stats together." And Randi overrides her: "Vee's my dear friend," taking her hand and holding it on the white tabletop in plain view, "in no hurry to let go." There's the title. *Dear.* Beloved — and, the page insists, costly: "She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it in front of them. She knew only… that it had cost something, that Randi had spent it on her." Vee reads the spend as generosity. I read it as an investment — and, God help me, maybe also as the first real thing Randi has spent, the first hairline in her. And there's a hum under it I don't think Vee hears: the held hand, the hug she leans into "like it was already a thing they did," "Love you, girl." The jacket promised me these two would close in, and this is what the first inch of that looks like — "a warmth that came with weight on it."

**Pace** exists here only as a story told in a bright room, and notably the story Vee chooses for Randi is the surrender-cut: "May I choose?", "I just handed it over," "I stopped keeping track of myself." She gave Cassie facts and agency; she gives Randi texture and yielding. Same evening, two selves, each one shaped to the listener. The heat of the tree stays unspoken to both — "Not six years old when I kissed him" gestures at an adult current she still can't put in any room.

**The class-wound cluster** — the pilled cardigan "covering her like an apology," the wrong-size feeling, the high window table where she's "a thing the room could turn and look at" — is laid on thick, almost too thick, but it lands, because the cure for the exposure is the claim: she ends the chapter not minding being seen because she's been *picked*. "She felt picked. She carried it the whole way home" rhymes deliberately with last chapter's "warm and did not feel it," and the rhyme itself frightens me — the book keeps ending on her happiness, and I keep turning the page with both hands.

**Titles.** "Dear" gives nothing away until the moment it lands, and then it does double duty — dear friend, and dear as in expensive. The book's title gets a shadow-appearance: Brooke's "We should all get dinner sometime" — "lovely, and meant nothing" — a polite invitation that costs nothing, set against the one this book is actually about, the one that's costing everything. And the tagline has a new voice now: not testimony but revision — the yes that quietly edits the self that said it.

**What I want / dread next:** the hike, the gravel road, the house — whose shoes? — and, with real dread, the scene where this harvest gets delivered. Somewhere there's a man who will be told "because of him," and I don't want to watch him receive it. I also want to know what was in Cassie's folded look. Guesses from here: the hike within a chapter or two; a Randi-and-Pace scene I am not ready for; and the Brooke thread, which smelled dormant, now has Vee's name and a witnessed hand-hold inside it.
