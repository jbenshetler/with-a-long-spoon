## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter got me twice — once straight, and once in the underside. Straight: I watched a girl get chosen out loud in a roomful of finished women, and it *worked* on me. When Randi took Vee's hand on the white tabletop, "in no hurry to let go," and said "Vee's my dear friend" after Vee had just made herself small — "We have stats together," offering Brooke nothing to hold against Randi — I felt it exactly as Vee did: warmth "with weight on it." Someone picked her. In public. The wrong-size feeling didn't go away, "it only stopped mattering next to the other thing." That's a true thing about how belonging lands, and I didn't want to be immune to it. And then the double exposure: I know what that claiming cost and what it was. Brooke, who probed Randi about *Pace* over coffee in chapter two, standing there watching Randi hold this girl's hand. Brooke's smile "did not change," and that non-change is scarier than a reaction. The taller woman's slow circuit of Vee — "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — is the pointing game's gaze wearing a sorority face, and I hated it.

The center of the chapter is the debrief, and it's ch7 turned inside out — only now Randi gets the confession from Vee's own mouth instead of Pace's, and it's *gentler* and therefore worse. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Held up to the light. And then the moment that genuinely chilled me: Vee has her dignified version ready — *I made an exception*, the thing she was proud of in ch10, "I did it on purpose" — and Randi reframes it, soft, "near tender": "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." And Vee lets the smaller version replace her own. Her one act of sovereign wanting, re-narrated as surrender, two days later, by a friend she's handing everything to. Randi doesn't even have to push. That's the horror of it — it's all done in the key of delight.

And yet — I keep having to hold this — Randi's want keeps leaking real. "Ask and then ravish" comes "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." She dropped into the fantasy *with* Vee before she caught herself. The disaster-date story — which I read as fabricated, "a story she had told before and would tell again," built to preach that men who don't listen won't take care of you, i.e., built to make Pace shine — even that has real hunger underneath the construction. My feeling for Randi hasn't soured; it's gotten sadder and more dangerous at once. She's grooming her friend and meaning the friendship, both, in the same gestures.

Cassie's three lines are doing heavy work. The look that goes past Vee to Randi "turned in her seat and lit up," then gets "folded away" — she saw it, again, whatever *it* is. And then Vee, for the first time, edges her out: "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one," and walks off without watching her go. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie" — that's the second time this book has shown me Vee's not-seeing as an *act*, and this one cost Cassie. It hurt. Cassie is the only clean pair of eyes in this book and Vee is quietly demoting her.

The food stuff snuck up on me: Vee ordering oat milk "because Randi was standing right there," Randi's bruise-colored sentence of a drink ordered "as though it were her usual" — egg white, her mother's egg whites — the armor eating with them at the table. And the title: "Dear." The dear friend. Also *dear* as in costly — "she knew only that it had cost something, that Randi had spent it on her." Also, maybe, dear as in what you call someone at the start of a letter they haven't read yet. It recolors the whole chapter on second thought: the chapter's warmest moment is the title, and the title is the con and the real thing at once.

The ending wrecked me quietly. "She did not feel, now, like a thing the room could turn and look at. She felt picked." She *was* picked — on the quad, by a man pointing and a woman saying a bare *yes* while her stride dropped out. The book lets Vee carry the exact word home and lets me carry what it actually means. Next: the hike, Sunday, a mountain she's never climbed with "her man" — and I want it for her and I'm braced for it. My guess from here: the hike is where Pace moves this forward physically, and somewhere behind it, Cassie's folded-away look is going to have to unfold.
