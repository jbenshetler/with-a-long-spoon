## Reader reaction

Six chapters dark, and Randi comes back in a green convertible and immediately does the most frightening thing she's done all book: she's *gentler*. "The performance gone out of it." And Vee — my Vee, my careful curator who held out against Cassie last chapter — notices, and talks herself out of trusting it, and then the smoothie-place memory finishes the job. I wanted to shake her and I also understood completely, because *I felt the warmth too.* That's the horror of this chapter. The jacket has been telling me for six chapters that Randi is the trap, and the trap arrived looking like the best lunch of Vee's life, and I would have climbed into that booth too.

The extraction scene is the most gripping thing in the book so far, and almost nothing happens in it. Watch the ladder: "Nobody measures over a bra" — and there's the title landing, *How It's Done*, the whole chapter in one gentle authority, normalization as a lockpick — then the tit-goddess compliment, then "challenges," then Vee's flat panicked lie, "**It was cold in the house**," and my stomach actually dropped, she is a *terrible* liar, and Randi just sets the word on the table — "Cold… Hmm" — and Vee collapses inside a breath: "actually it was really *hot*." Then the yeahs. "Which hip?" "The left one." I was gripping the pages. And then Randi says "**the little platform. The pedestal**" — and I stopped. Did Vee say there was a platform? I went back through what we actually heard of her telling, and I can't find it. Maybe it came out in the compressed parts. Maybe. But with the jacket in my pocket, that detail arriving out of *Randi's* mouth, in *Randi's* grammar — "They put you up on a literal pedestal" — reads like a woman describing furniture she's stood on. And Vee never registers it. Not a flicker. That silence is the scariest beat in the book.

And then the door. After six chapters of doors left open — Pace's whole grammar of invitation — Randi walks Vee to the threshold of the one secret she has never given anyone, not Cassie, not anyone, the kneeling, *what did he see*, Vee's mouth already opening, the wanting finally winning — and Randi says "**You don't have to tell me**" and sits back and asks for the check. I felt *physically robbed*. I had been leaning in too. The book edged me exactly as it edged Vee, and left me standing there with the charge stranded and nowhere to put it. "A door closing very softly." That's the inversion of everything Pace built, and I don't know yet whether it was cruelty, pacing, or — the worst option, the jacket's option — craft.

The tickle story: funny, filthy, hot, and I don't believe a word of it. Or rather — I don't know what it is. Saturday. The *same* Saturday. Tickling, which is this book's private signature since Vee found his ticklish spot. And the man is "sweet, pretty, completely in love with himself," which is so perfectly *not* Pace that it feels constructed to be deniable. This is a suspicion, marked as one; on the page alone it's just Randi being Randi. But the jacket won't let me leave it there.

The kiss worked on me. Both stages. "See you soon, gorgeous." I wanted it and I got it and then the book immediately made me pay: Vee's instant "*French thing*" explanation, and then the window-scan, counting the dark squares, the cold thing spreading. The same chapter where Randi told her "*Oh, you* love *being looked at*" and handed it back as a gift — the jacket's exact promise, fulfilled on the page at last — ends with Vee terrified of being seen. The gift and the shame in the same afternoon. That's the trap wearing its own face.

The money hum hurt too: reading the right side of the menu "the way you read a road in fog," the Rombauer number, the debit card "speaking to" a number "that would be smaller tonight in a way she would feel on Thursday" — and Randi watching her pay, warm and easy, not reaching for the folder again.

Titles: the chapter's is the thesis — normalization, tutorial, method; how bras and dresses and seductions are done. The book's — the lunch, the kiss, every invitation exquisitely polite. The series title: she's in the booth now, supping, and no spoon is long enough. The tagline: "Yeah." "The left one." "Yeah." Every one freely given.

Next: I want the call and the fitting; I dread the induction dinner with the mother in the room; I expect the kiss to escalate; and I am now *desperate* and terrified for the first page Randi and Pace share, because six chapters in they still haven't, and I can feel it coming.
