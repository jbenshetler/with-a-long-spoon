# Cold read (grounded) — How It's Done

*scene: scenes/how-its-done.md · model: kimi-k3 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch016 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

I read this whole chapter with my stomach in a knot and a smile on my face at the same time, which is, I think, exactly where the book wants me. From the first beat — the Mercedes at the curb, the *different* smile, "gentler, the performance gone out of it" — I was doing what Vee does: wanting to believe it and checking the want. Vee even does it herself ("Maybe she was inventing the difference because she wanted it that badly") and then reaches for the smoothie-place evidence, "choosing Vee at a cost, for a reason Vee had never once been able to locate." That line *hurt*, because I can locate the reason, and she can't, and the whole chapter runs in that gap.

The lunch scene is the most quietly masterful — and I mean masterful the way you'd say it about a con — extraction yet. Randi's Saturday story set off every alarm I own: "this beautiful idiot," the scarf, the tickling, "he switches. To somewhere else *entirely*" — after the fabricated French-restaurant "catastrophe" that I already suspected covered a bench night, I hear this as Pace re-narrated and disguised, again. And then Randi uses it as a teaching text: "That's the whole point, baby. The letting go." She's *grooming the concept* — handing Vee the vocabulary of surrender over tomato soup — and it's wrapped in such genuine warmth that I can't tell where the lesson ends and the seduction begins. Then the fitting story: Randi's tightness while Vee glows about Pace (jealousy? possession? both — the thumb working the glass edge is a beautiful tell), and then "Nobody measures over a bra... it's just how it's done." I physically reacted to that line. It's a lie delivered as kindness — or at least a convenient half-truth — and its function is to *retrospectively launder Vee's consent*, to tell her the thing she already did was ordinary, nothing to be ashamed of, everyone knows. Which is exactly what you'd say to keep someone from ever re-examining it. And it works — "the clench in Vee's chest let go." God.

And then the cruelest, most interesting move in the chapter: Randi leads her down and down — which hip, the pedestal, "And what did he *see*?" — Vee's mouth is literally opening around the wetness secret, the thing she's told no one — and Randi closes the door. "You don't have to tell me." Gentle. Kind. I don't think it's kindness. I think Randi brought herself to the edge of hearing the detail her own body most wants and least survives — the woman her body answered for, aroused, on a pedestal, in daylight — and couldn't take it. The "clean silence" after, where the laugh used to live, is the loudest moment in the chapter. Vee feels it as rejection ("her secret still half out of her with nowhere to go") and I felt it as Randi flinching from her own want. It aches from both sides at once.

The kiss undid me, honestly. Randi's hand at the hinge of Vee's jaw, "soft, and in no hurry," and then — the *second* press, "a little more" — that's the move of someone who has kissed a lot of people and means this one, or means to be remembered by it. Probably both. It's the first physical flag between the women and it lands *hot*, precisely because it's small and deliberate and deniable. And then the ending turned the heat over into dread so fast: Vee explaining it away ("French... that's just how women like her say goodbye") and then the colder thing — *had anyone seen?* — counting lit windows. That scan chilled me. She's right to scan, and she doesn't know why she's right. Someone in this book is always watching; the reader is one of them.

My feelings have moved: Randi is now the most dangerous person in the book to me and also the one I ache for most — her "real laugh" was in this chapter twice, and both times it was wrapped around an extraction. Pace is absent and I miss him, which itself feels like information about where my trust lives. Vee — I want to shake her and hold her. She tips well so she won't be "the broke girl who tipped badly," against a single glass of Rombauer that outcost her whole meal, and that detail gutted me more than the kiss.

The title lands hard and double: "How It's Done" is Randi's line about measuring over a bra — the authoritative fiction that dissolves shame — and it's also the chapter's whole subject: *this is how it's done.* How you extract a confession, how you teach surrender, how you kiss someone goodbye so it can't be named. The volume title, WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION, keeps sharpening for me: supping with someone requires a long spoon, and the invitation gets more polite and less refuseable every chapter. I dread — *guess from here* — that the dress fitting's next session, or the induction itself, becomes the stage where the threesome invitation finally reaches her, and that Randi's composure is the real fuse.
