## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

I am undone by this chapter, and I don't trust how undone I am. The kiss at the door first — she takes it, finally, the one the mountain and the cop's lights stole, and it "went deep," and then *he* stops it. "No boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs." That line landed in my chest. It is the most romantic and the most quietly alarming thing he has done yet, because I can feel what it does to her — want "still lit in her with nowhere to put it" — and I can also feel it as design, whether or not he means it as design. He banks her fire and calls it nothing. Every restraint of his makes her want more, and she is starting to bring the want to him pre-loaded: she wore satin under everything, on purpose, hoping he'd open the bedroom door. He didn't. "My bedroom," flat as the kitchen. And I know what's behind that door. The bench is in there, re-topped and waiting, and her leaning toward it while he declines is the whole book in one gesture — she is reaching for a door whose other side she cannot imagine.

The house tour melted me anyway, completely. The shoes squared in the closet, one pair per thing he does; the KitchenAid worn to a shine; the papers in a language she can't read and his refusal to explain it down to her size; the middle-schooler she conjures flat on the floor with the dragon paperback; the workshop with every tool inside its painted ghost. And then the two-tier reveal: he *made the shirt* — the flannel she'd turned inside out hunting for a tag, filing the absurd answer away — and he has bought silk to make her the dress. Not the brick-red of the magazine. Burgundy, with purple under it, because he held it to her face and matched *her*, not the picture. I cried a little when she understood that. "No one had ever—" and the sentence has no end. It is the most cherished she has ever been, and it is real on the page — and I watched him choose her in the pointing game, so every atom of this tenderness sits on a foundation she doesn't know about. That's the splinter that won't come out: nothing here is fake, and all of it is framed.

And the measuring. "Down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it'll fall." Reader, I felt my eyebrows go up. In good light — the man who leaves the lights up, who likes to look. He has built an invitation inside the ordinary, a reason for her to undress for him that arrives as generosity, and she says yes "warm and immediate, no hedge in it," because of course she does; the yes is freely given and the yes is also the yield of weeks of careful banking. I don't think it's calculated in the cruel sense — I think it's just how he moves through the world, doors and patience and right angles — but the effect is the same either way, and the book knows it.

The title did its work on me late. Two towels on the rod, in the plain clean bathroom, one line, unremarked. She files the too-warm house under "the small mysteries of him" — "the way you walk past a wrapped box on the counter without asking who it's for" — and never once asks who the second towel is for. I know. Randi has kept a cashmere blanket at that house since week two. Randi doesn't appear in this chapter at all, not even in Vee's thoughts — and her absence is loud, because her things are in that house even when she isn't. Vee drove past the driveway the first time; she is missing every cut in the hill.

Also: "parents" made something close in him, "a door easing shut two rooms away" — a new splinter in Pace himself, the first real shadow on his own history since Daphne, and I noticed the patent answer came fast and light right after. And the error-correcting codes speech — "say it more than once so the noise can't kill it" — sat down next to the latent-variables lecture and the MRI patent, signal pulled out of noise, and I felt the book quietly describing itself: the real thing buried in the ordinary, inferable but never measured. She can't read his papers. She can't read his house, either. I can, a little, and it hurts.

What I want next: the measuring, obviously, with dread braided straight through the want — her in satin under good light, him with a tape, the bedroom ten feet away. And I'm bracing for when Randi re-enters this frame, because she will, and Vee is now emotionally past the point where learning the truth will be survivable-clean. Guessing from here: the dress gets made, the induction happens in burgundy, and somewhere in those two weeks the polite invitation gets a lot less hypothetical.
