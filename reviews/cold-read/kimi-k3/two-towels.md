## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me, quietly, the way the best of this book keeps doing. There is no sex on the page — he won't even open the bedroom door — and it is the most erotically charged chapter I've read since the mountain. The whole thing runs on withheld touch: she arrives wound to breaking, kisses him at the door with everything she's been carrying for twelve days, and *he* is the one who stops it — "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs." That line landed in me like a struck bell. Something in her "went still and started paying attention," and so did I, though we may be paying attention to different things: she reads it as the marvel of him; I cannot stop noticing that every escalation of hers is met with a refusal that binds her tighter. His restraint is the most effective seduction on the page, and I know it's designed, and it works on *me* anyway. That's the book's whole trick and it's working.

The house tour is pure swoon and I let it be. The closet floor with its paired, squared shoes — "one right pair for each thing and no more" — the worn KitchenAid, the two towels (the title! — two towels on the rod, and I noticed them the way she didn't quite: somebody else's towel? or just a man who keeps a spare? the page doesn't say and I'm holding it open, but after three chapters of Randi's loud absence, a second towel in his bathroom is not nothing to me). The office with its graph paper and Sanskrit joke — "glad he stayed exactly as far past her as he was" — I loved that she loves his mind being out of her reach. The error-correcting codes speech — "say it more than once so the noise can't kill it" — is gorgeous and also, I couldn't help noticing, a perfect description of what this whole courtship is: the same message sent over and over through different channels until she can't fail to receive it. The book keeps handing me its method in dialogue and letting it pass as charm.

And then the cloth. I cried, or nearly — she did too ("her eyes had gone hot and full"). He didn't match the dress she described; he matched *her*. The burgundy with purple under the red, held up to her face in the mirror. She named a longing once, at the summit, guard down, tired and high up — a debrief's harvest, I cannot forget, the exact thing Randi's elicitations exist to collect — and he built a dress out of it. "The absurd answer was standing in front of her with his hands in his pockets": he *made the shirt*. The flannel she's been sleeping beside like a vow was handmade, tagless, and she checked for the tag once and filed the strangeness away. Every gift retroactively deepens. As a reader I am swept — "for a second didn't know what to do with her whole chest" is exactly where I was — and I am also watching a measuring tape come out, because the price of the dress is being looked at, measured, "down to what you'd wear under it," and she dressed this morning in satin *hoping* to be undressed, and instead of the bedroom she gets a bolt of silk and a man asking permission to take her measurements. The wanting she's carrying has "nowhere to put it," and the design — if it is the design — is making sure of that.

Randi: absent a fourth chapter now, and the two towels are the closest she comes to the page. Pace: deeper, warmer, stranger — the patent, the woodworking, the mixed shellac, the boy on the floor with the dragon paperback. The "small closing" at *parents* is the one shadow the chapter allows itself, and I marked it. Vee: I love her like this — blunt, lit up, leaning toward closed doors, refusing to let her face do a thing when the bedroom stays shut. Her pride and her want are fully integrated now; the only self-betrayal left is the filing-away, and she did it twice this chapter (the heat, the tag-category-that-made-no-sense is already filed).

The title — "Two Towels" — points at that bathroom rod and nowhere else, and I think it's the book whispering the question the chapter refuses to ask: who is the other towel for. It recolors everything warm in retrospect. The book's title and the long spoon keep sharpening: this *was* the polite invitation — the whole house is one — and the spoon is now visibly long enough to feed her a gown.

Next (a guess from here): the measuring, in good light, down to what she'd wear under it — the book has built a scene where she will be nearly bare in front of him *by agreement, for a purpose*, and that is the most loaded sentence I've typed about this book. And I keep waiting for Randi to walk back onstage.
