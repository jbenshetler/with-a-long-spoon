# Cold read (grounded) — Burn

## Reader reaction

This one took me by the throat early and never really let go — though the way in was a lifted plate and a snapped neck muscle, not the usual door.

The opening line did something sneaky: "I won't get my brunch this weekend. Randi's got some sorority thing Saturday." After the last chapter ended on Cassie's "Is this what you want?" going unanswered, Vee hands Pace this light little excuse and we both know — reader knows — that Randi is the live wire. And then the chapter simply… doesn't go to Randi. It goes to the gym with Pace, and the Randi hum drops under the floorboards while the literal burn starts building. That pivot is the whole architecture of this chapter: Vee's Pace-engine running at full throttle while her Randi-problem sits banked. And I bought it completely, because the Pace heat here is real enough to crowd anything out. I also can't help noticing that her neck — the exact place Randi's mouth found last chapter, "the exact place she'd put her own mouth twice" — is the thing that gets injured and then gets the balm. The text doesn't press it, but I noticed it.

The gym is a gift of a set piece. That grungy warehouse room with no music — and the text tying Pace's character to that void ("Pace did not seal himself off from a room. He read it.") — is such a clean inversion of the book's "music always already playing" motif. He doesn't need a soundtrack; he needs to read rooms. And the collar line — "Everything gets a collar" — sparked for me before I knew the payoff was coming: the flinching accident happens *because* two careless boys don't collar, and Vee gets hurt by exactly the kind of thoughtlessness Pace would never commit. The parable lands without him ever saying it. "You hurt her. Your carelessness hurt her." — quiet, cold, ordered. I have not seen Pace in cold protective fury before, and it's convincing; it's not anger, it's correction, delivered at a volume that made two college boys fold. And the earlier beat — him setting her up with tiny plates "as though it mattered" and refusing to lift more than he could re-rack alone so she'd never be responsible for what she couldn't hold — that's the cherishing that keeps pulling me toward Pace even holding everything I know. The chapter keeps restocking that account.

Then the injury→tending sequence. The practical details are exquisite and unhurried — ibuprofen, shower angled hot, towel drawn forward off her neck, the squat glass jar of camphor balm — and all of it is eroticized only insofar as Vee can't stop eroticizing his care. That's the trick the chapter plays: the care is genuinely aseptic ("He was only tending her, and asked nothing of her by it") and her body refuses to file it that way. "The trouble was only that being handled by those hands was the exact thing her body had spent the whole drive wanting." I felt that low pull running under the neck-wire the whole drive-home scene, which is a strange dual sensation the chapter manages without strain.

And then the consent choreography — I have to stop here and respect it, because this book knows exactly what it's doing and does it on the page. She presses her breast into his hand; he withdraws — "Keep still." She pulls his wrist back and places his hand on her breast herself. She says "Yes" before he's asked anything; he stops anyway, goes still, and asks, flat and careful: "Do you want me to keep going?" — "all of him waiting on the word and not on her." That's the cleanest written yes I can remember in this book, and it matters, because the thing she's saying yes to is pain — hot-burning balm on her nipples with full knowledge of what it will do ("The neck was what the breast would be. The breast was what the tip would be."). Even her self-audit is honest: she catches "asked" and corrects to "Demanded," twice out loud, in the one word. The chapter refuses to let consent be implied; it performs it slowly while still being scorching. That is rare air, and I breathed it gratefully.

The burn itself: escalating, topographic, almost musical — the warmth waking "from above" at the neck, the lying-warmth trick, the fear and pleasure fused until "she gave up telling them apart." The "I know," as he rolls the burning nipple — that undid me. His hard-on kept still against her spine is the cruelest tenderness in the chapter: he wants, unmistakably, and will not spend a drop of it where it could cost her.

And then the refusal. My god. She's climbing, her neck won't allow the finish, and he says "Easy. Not tonight. We need to wait." — and then he *brings her down by hand*, holding the heat banked, giving her "no finish, no service, no way in the world to spend it." The "we." The text hangs a lantern on it — "We. / The word went through her almost worse than the burn." For a man who cannot say *I love you*, the "we" is huge, and Vee hears it as enormous, and I did too. Is it progress toward the word he can't say, or just his native inclusive grammar? The page holds it open and so will I. Ending on "She carried home the places he had not finished touching" — that's the ache as souvenir, and it lands like a thesis sentence for the whole Randi situation too, though the chapter never says so.

Friction: honestly, very little. The burn scene is long and luxuriant in this book's established mode; I never once wanted it shorter, because the escalation-denial arc is structural, not decorative. If anything the gym's first half risks feeling like setup, but the accident pays it off and the collar parable retroactively dignifies every detail. The only beat that pinged as slightly convenient was how perfectly an accident arrives to create a tending scenario — but it's earned, physically plausible, and Pace's behavior in it reveals something real, so I forgive it.

The title: "Burn." Plain, direct, triple-referent — the balm's burn, her own smoldering want, and under both the banked Randi fire the chapter refuses to touch. It prepared me accurately and then cut deeper than "prepare" implies. The cover-board series title "WITH A LONG SPOON" — my packet gives no tagline, just that line — still reads to me like a half-remembered proverb about dining with dangerous company, and now, two books in, that promise feels fulfilled and tightening: everything in this story is eaten with the appropriate long spoon, and the spoon is always loaded. "BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION" — warmth as welcome, warmth as the house turned up for her, warmth as balm that becomes fire; "warm" has steadily shaded from comfort into burn. The reception is warm enough to hurt now.

What I want next: I want to know whether the balm game repeats and whether Pace ever lets her finish that loop; I want — with dread and pull equally — the sorority thing Randi is at; and I'm watching the "we." Guess from here (marked as such): the denial is training sets of restraint that will eventually be cashed in a scene he *does* finish; and the Randi banked-fire has to come due, probably when Vee least has Pace's hands to pour it into.
