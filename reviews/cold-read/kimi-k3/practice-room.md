## Reader reaction

I thought I was walking into the Cassie reckoning. Instead I got a piano closet and the hottest scene this book has written without anyone touching anyone.

The lecture first: two kinds of outlier — the one you lift out and the line goes on, and the kind that "never quite gets to zero... It's in everything that comes after." Vee writes *never all the way gone* because he says it twice, and then her pen stops because Randi's knee has come to rest against hers. The page knew exactly what it was doing there and so did I: this weekend is the second kind. It's in everything after. I felt the truth of that in my own chest, and I also laughed a little at the professor winking that hard.

Then Vee does the extracting — the reversal stopped me: "For weeks it had gone the other way... Now it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist." She tows Randi off to confess, proud, and that's genuinely moving. And it was Randi who thought of the practice rooms. I noticed that. The soundproof cells nobody uses at lunch, the place people "used to... you know." Randi chose the room.

The scene itself: I was gone. Swooning. "Tell me about the one he gave back... His." — and then Randi reconstructing the wet hand out of a detail Vee hadn't known she'd given, and Vee's voice dropping "into something lower and slower that she didn't entirely recognize," and the hot slow drop of "she wasn't only telling it. She was doing it again. Here. Now." That line is the book's whole engine in one sentence. And then Randi *answers* — "Oh, baby... Sometimes it's the mouse that roars" — and gives back a real confession of her own: over his knee, counting out loud, "and God, it made me buck," the hand that slipped, "by then it's welcome. Believe me. Very welcome." That's the warmest, truest thing Randi has ever said to Vee, and it's also — I have to hold both — Randi "enjoying it, watching Vee's face take each piece." The cat-stillness. "Did you deserve it?" is a *leading* question, and Vee answers it and realizes a moment late what she agreed to, and Randi's smile brightens. I cannot tell whether this scene was communion or harvest, and the page's last move is to say Vee can't either: "as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And Vee couldn't tell whether she had." That's my unease, and it is fully earned.

And the bathroom undid me in the tender direction. Soaked through, "made by nothing but a woman talking in a chair," and then the hand that "knew the way. It moved before she did" — and she catches it. "To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." Her body has been the honest one all book, and this is the first time she's closed a gate on it, and I can't decide if that's dignity or the mother's grammar firing under the hard white light. Probably both. The mirror — "pass for collected" — is her old shame-register, and it costs her.

Cassie, meanwhile: saved the seat, and Vee answers her "without hearing herself," and "see you in the room" lands "to the side of Vee's head." Ouch. The reunion I was dreading didn't even get to happen.

The ending wrecked me quietly: the goodbye kiss "so worn-in now that Vee barely clocked it," and then her arms slow coming down, holding on past the song — and she puts it down to being full of him. She is misattributing. The proximate voice was Randi's. I can see it; she can't; nobody in the scene can or will say it.

No real friction. The professor's double-tap on "never all the way gone" is the one place I felt the book's thumb, and I forgave it on the spot.

The title is perfect in hindsight — a room for rehearsal. They're practicing something. Saying desire out loud between women, in a licensed form, without naming it. And knowing what I know about where this is all going, "practice" lands with a second meaning I won't pretend I didn't feel. *A Polite Invitation* keeps renaming itself — this chapter is nothing but invitations ("Walk me out?", "Tell me everything," *welcome*), every one of them polite and none of them innocent. *With a Long Spoon* feels more pointed than it ever has around Randi: Vee was fed a story and drank it down. I don't have the cover tagline in front of me anymore, so I'll leave that be.

What I want next: the actual dorm reunion, now doubly loaded. Whether Randi meant it. Whether the wet-hand detail travels back to Pace — a guess from here: something about Vee's confession to Randi becomes somebody else's instrument, and it will hurt because this scene was also genuinely good. And the word Vee doesn't own yet — "Have you ever—" hung there and I wanted to snatch it out of the air. When does she get the word?
