## Reader reaction

This chapter scared me more than anything explicit could have, and it did it with a woman talking in a chair.

The lecture first, because the book knew exactly what it was handing me: *additive* outliers you mark and lift out, the line going on "like it was never there" — that's the script's theory of the blonde fight, apologize and excise — versus the *innovational* kind that "comes in through the part that carries forward… It's in everything that comes after." Vee writes *never all the way gone* because he said it twice, and then the page says, flat as the professor's cereal-box voice, "She did not think about herself at all." I did the thinking for her. She's the second kind. Whatever this is — the wanting, the conditioning, Randi — it's in the part that carries forward now.

And then the reversal, small enough to miss: "For weeks it had gone the other way… Now it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist." She's towing Randi. She's learned the gesture by immersion and doesn't know she's speaking it. "To Randi. Only to Randi," while Cassie says *see you in the room* to the side of her head. That one hurt in a minor key — the saved seat, the granola bar, the wrong textbook, the answer Vee gives "without hearing herself."

The practice room itself is where my stomach dropped. Soundproof. "The building went away. Not muffled. Gone." And Randi — God, Randi is *good*, and I mean that as indictment. She hears Vee skid past the middle and names the skid: "A game of slap-and-tickle." She reconstructs the crime scene from evidence — "His hand was wet. You said he was doing dishes." She asks "Did you deserve it?" and gets Vee to laugh "Probably I deserved it" — and Vee realizes "a moment late… what she'd just agreed to." That's the whole book in one beat. A yes, freely given, understood only after. Then Vee re-lives the spank aloud, voice gone "lower and slower," body answering the *retelling*, and Randi says "I bet he did" and pointedly "didn't say what she herself could see."

Then the mouse-that-roars story, and I went cold in a hot scene. A slight soft-spoken man you'd never pick out of a room, who gets her home and puts her over his knee, makes her count, his hand slipping "right between" — "by then it's welcome. Very welcome." Is that Pace? The temperament rhymes; the build doesn't. Is it an ex? Invented? The page refuses me, and the refusal is the point, because what the story *does* is independent of who it's about: it tells Vee that her body's answer is normal, shared, enjoyable — that women like Randi want exactly this. It's a priming payment gone sexual. "She was enjoying it, watching Vee's face take each piece." That sentence. Randi is running the play *and* getting off on it, and the two together are worse than either alone.

The bathroom is the chapter's bravest page: "made by nothing but a woman talking in a chair," her hand moving before she does, and then — the first real counter-move this book has given her — she catches her own hand and holds it flat against her knee. "To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." I wanted to cheer and couldn't, because the refusal was private and the architect walked away warm and blameless: "as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And Vee couldn't tell whether she had." The doubt itself is the design. And "All better?" — Randi knew. Of course Randi knew.

The goodbye undid me: the hug, the kiss, the second touch, "so worn-in now that Vee barely clocked it." Escalation become texture. Her arms slow coming down, attributed to sleeplessness and being "full of him." She's falling for Randi and filing it under Pace.

The erotic charge is real and I'm implicated by it — the story is genuinely hot and the chapter makes Vee's body prove it works on me too. Heat peaks at "by then it's welcome" and again, inversely, at the caught hand.

"The Practice Room" — rehearsal space, soundproof, the place freshmen used for hookups. Practice for the bed the jacket promised. *With a Long Spoon* — Randi never touched her; the payload arrived by voice alone, contact at a distance. That's the long spoon. The tagline has stopped being ominous and started being diagnostic.

Next I want: Cassie to be given eyes on something. Randi's report to Pace — she's holding the spank file now. And I dread Vee almost-naming what she feels for Randi.
