## Reader reaction

This chapter got under my skin in a way I did not see coming, and I've been sitting with why. Nothing happens in it. Two women sit in a piano closet and talk, and one of them nearly comes apart in a bathroom afterward, and the one who caused it walks off "warm as anything, as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it." And I believe both readings at once — that Randi meant everything by it, and that she also simply, genuinely, enjoys Vee — and holding those together is exactly the ache of this book.

The first thing that moved me: Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist. "Walk me out?" For weeks it's been Randi cutting her out of the herd, and now Vee is the one towing "the most interesting person she knew" toward the door, with Cassie saying see-you-in-the-room to the side of her head. I felt that flip in my stomach. Vee thinks she's pulling her best friend aside to gush. I watched her deliver herself, hand-first, into the one room on campus where no one can hear her — a room whose other reputation she blushes to name. The book never has to say the word "grooming." It just lets me watch Randi turn a chair around and put her attention on like a lamp.

And then the extraction itself, which is the most skillful and the most unsettling thing Randi has done yet — worse, to me, than the coffee-shop fishing, because this time Vee *knows* she's exposing something. "Did you deserve it?" is the whole chapter in four words. Vee laughs and says probably, and "a moment late, she realized what she'd just agreed to" — and Randi's smile brightens and it goes down through her "like a swallow of something warm." I had to stop there. That's Randi drinking it. The wet-hand detail she catches and feeds back — "So his hand was wet" — the cat-stillness, the "attention so complete it was nearly a pressure, nearly a hand." Randi is doing two things at once and I can't separate them: she is priming Vee to want the thing Pace already does (and I remember the bench, I remember who taught whom this language), and she is, unmistakably, aroused by the priming. Her own story — the mouse that roars, over his knee, counting out loud, the hand that "slipped" — I don't believe it's true and I don't believe it's false. It's a lesson wearing a confession's clothes, built to land on the word *welcome*, and it lands exactly where she aimed it.

The bathroom scene is where the heat peaked for me, and it's all refusal. Vee soaked through from "nothing but a woman talking in a chair," her hand moving before she does, and then catching it — "she would not say yes to it." That restraint burned hotter than any consummation in the book so far. The shame-arousal wire the book has been laying since "floozie" is fully live now, and Randi has found the switch without ever touching her. And Vee files it all under Pace. That's the cruelty of the ending: her arms slow coming down from the goodbye hug, and "she put it down to that" — to him, to the weekend, to anything but the woman whose mouth she'd just kissed without clocking it. The one thing she's never once examined is the one thing the chapter is actually about.

Cassie keeps shrinking at the edges of these scenes — saving the seat, asking about the problem set, saying goodbye to the side of Vee's head — and I notice the book noticing it. Nobody here feels "off" in a way the text hasn't earned; Randi feels off in a way the text has *built*, line by line, and I trust it.

The title: "The Practice Room." Literal — the soundproof cell. But it recolors on the way out: Vee is practicing a desire she won't name, Randi is practicing the craft of eliciting it, and a practice room is where you rehearse something you're not ready to perform. The volume title — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — keeps earning itself: Randi feeds Vee everything at a distance, through stories, never her own hand; and the invitation was never extended to the person it's about. What I want next: the induction, the dress, the shoes — but what I dread is Randi reporting this hour back to Pace, because I no longer doubt that she will.
