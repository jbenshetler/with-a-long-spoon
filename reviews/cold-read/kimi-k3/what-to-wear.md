## Reader reaction

New room, new table, new witnesses — and the first thing I notice is how *happy* this chapter is. The athletic dining hall going "gold and forgiving," Vee laughing "with her whole body, loud and easy," stealing fries. Meg and Kayla arrive fully formed in about four strokes each: Meg, dark-haired and level, whose setting down her fork is "an event," who follows the Pace situation "in installments like her shows"; Kayla, all curls and motion, whose sentences you climb onto rather than follow. I liked them both immediately. And Theo lifting a hand across the room — the boy who asked her to the fall thing in September and "took it like a gentleman" — gave me a real pang. He's the sunlit road not taken, waving from across the cafeteria, age-appropriate and nice, and she has already forgotten him mid-fry.

But here's what the chapter actually did to me: I watched Vee *edit in real time*, and because I've been at every table, I could measure the cuts. The shirt story she tells whole — the dry shirt he'd packed for himself, his back to the truck in the rain — and it's true, every word. Then the cab. And the cab she *launders*. The display is gone. "On purpose. Me." — the thing she gave Cassie with her whole face open — becomes "I was about to. I turned and he was right there," twice, an almost-kiss thwarted by his hypothermia and then by the siren. Her wanting survives only as romance, never as lust. "She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care." That's the third version of her I've watched her wear: surrender for the first bright-room debrief, the whole truth for Cassie, and now the rom-com cut for the light table. The narration even lets the menu moment flip valence — what was once "handed it over, stopped keeping track" is now "He asks me first... and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it." Same event. Different girl per listener. I don't think she's lying; I think she's tailoring. But I noticed that the only person who holds the true cut is Cassie, and Cassie wasn't in this chapter — and now Meg and Kayla hold the single biggest fact going, and Cassie doesn't.

Because: the invitation. "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." I actually sat up. The house — the long drive, past where it goes to gravel, the place I've been waiting for this whole book — is suddenly a door with a day behind it. And the message is *nothing*: no purpose, no meal, no what-to-wear. Kayla calls it a ransom note, then reads the hour like a body: "There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat... He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes." The table screams; I went cold. Because I know what's on the jacket and she doesn't, and the politeness of it — the asking-first, the question-shaped summons — is exactly his established pattern and exactly the tagline's mechanism at once. "Every yes was freely given." He's asking. She'll say yes. Freely.

And Randi: three chapters absent now, unnamed, and her silence has stopped feeling like a pause and started feeling like weather. The house is, per everything I picked this book up knowing, *her* room as much as his. A house-invitation with no stated purpose, while the woman who picked Vee out across the quad stays off the page — I can't tell whether I'm dreading Randi's arrival at Saturday or dreading her prior knowledge of it. Both, in different organs.

The erotic charge is all anticipation and it works on me: "It's making me lose my mind... climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." Lust is socially out now, if sanitized. Meg's gentle counterweight — "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny... Sometimes they even are" — is the only caution anyone offers, folk-wisdom strength, and "Vee let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." That line did something to me. Nothing catches on her anymore.

Friction: honestly, little. The ensemble comedy flirts with stock ("scandalized at a professional level") but earns its keep, and the one explicit flag — the story "getting better in the telling" — is a pattern the book has already built with its hands, not its mouth. The "Twelve" beat (she knows the days since the kiss without counting) is lovely and quietly alarming.

The title: "What to Wear" is Kayla's unanswered question — "He didn't say" — and it's the chapter's empty center: no instruction, no script, or a script so well-fitted it's invisible. It also names what the chapter shows: she wears a different self per table. And it points straight at the ghost I can't stop seeing — a russet dress set back down once, waiting for an occasion. *A Polite Invitation* just stopped being a mood and became an object on the page: this *is* the polite invitation, minimal and question-shaped. The long spoon keeps looking like his manners — ask first, stand in the rain, don't touch — and the tagline keeps humming under every courtesy.

What I want next is Saturday, and I dread it in exact proportion. Guesses from here: the dress returns, from Randi's hand; Randi is at the house or engineered not to be; Vee tells Cassie before Saturday and Cassie says nothing again. And someone — Kayla, Randi, or a box — answers the title's question for her.
