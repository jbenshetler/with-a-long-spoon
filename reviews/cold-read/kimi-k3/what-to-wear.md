# Cold read (grounded) — What to Wear

## Reader reaction

This chapter landed on me like sunlight after two heavy ones — and I don't trust how good that felt, which is exactly where this book keeps putting me.

First, the pure pleasure of it: Vee at a gold-lit corner table between Meg and Kayla, "laughing with her whole body, loud and easy, the kind that made the next table look over and then want in." That's the girl Pace pointed at in the dining hall, the laugh with her head thrown back — and here she is again, unguarded, inside a friendship that has no design on her at all. Meg with her fork and her patience, Kayla who "let nothing finish," both of them following the Pace situation "in installments like her shows." It's the first time I've seen Vee's ordinary life have this much room in it — not Randi's high white window table where she felt "set up on a shelf," not Cassie's wary dorm room, just girls and fries and too much cheese. I didn't realize how starved the book had made me for that until the scene gave it back.

And the content under the comedy is Vee's desire, finally said out loud in her own register: "It's making me lose my mind, is what it is... He's not pushing. It's the opposite. He asks me first... And I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." That's the heat of the chapter for me — not anything shown, but the wanting confessed to people she trusts, Meg going "quiet in the good way." I felt the swoon of the shirt story all over again in the retelling, and I noticed she edits it — the striptease becomes "I was about to" kiss him. That's not a lie, exactly; that's a girl deciding which parts of herself belong to her friends and which belong to the man. I liked her for it. Also: "Twelve." She hadn't even had to count. The book keeps showing me how far gone she is by what she can't help knowing.

Then the text arrives — "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock. That's it" — and the chapter tilts. Vee reads it as pure excitement, "a door, and Saturday behind it, and not one word about what it was for," and Kayla reads it as a ransom note, and I read it with the book's whole weight behind me: I know what that house is. I know what's in the bedroom. I know who locks that door behind her every visit. "There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat." Kayla means it as a joke about sex and lands, without knowing, somewhere much more specific — "He wants you for the afternoon" is almost exactly the shape of the plan I've been watching get built since the pointing game. My stomach dropped and my heart lifted at the same time. Is this it? Is this the invitation the volume title has been promising? Or is it just a man asking a woman to his home, cooked for, looked at? Both readings are fully alive, and that doubleness is the book working perfectly on me.

Trust vs. suspicion, plainly: nothing *new* to suspect Pace of in this chapter — the message itself is bare, unmanipulative, almost austere. If anything his "no dress code, no agenda stated" reads as more of his exact courtesy. But I can't unsee the frame: Vee telling her friends "He's different. I know how that sounds," and Meg's gentle "Everybody's different at the start... Sometimes they even are." The book lets that hang there, kind and devastating. I also registered, quietly, that Randi is nowhere in this chapter — Vee's big news is being broken to Kayla and Meg, not to her "dear friend," and I can't tell if that's scheduling or a slow drift the book is letting me notice before Vee does. And Cassie, who got the whole shirt story, is absent from this retelling too. Vee's life is splitting into audiences.

New people: Kayla is instantly alive (the curls that move when she does, scandalized "at a professional level"), Meg confirmed as the calm dark-haired girl from Vee's floor, and Theo — mentioned only — gives Vee a history of being wanted and saying no kindly, which quietly fills in who she was before Pace. The new facts about Pace delighted me: powerlifter, lifts at dawn, writes six hours and stops, "My writing's no good after that." Vee's "I think there's a whole situation under there" from last chapter now has a confirmed body under it, and the fact that he lifts — that his strength is trained, deliberate, a discipline — sits very interestingly next to everything I know about him.

The title, "What to Wear": Kayla asks it twice and the message doesn't answer it, and it's the question of Vee's whole life — the deliberate cardigan, the russet dress she set down on the rock, the flannel she's still keeping. What do you wear to a door you've never been through, for a purpose no one will name? I love that the chapter ends without answering it, on "Bring a change of clothes" — which as a reader with my knowledge I find genuinely hard to sit with. The volume line — BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION — now feels like it's pointing *here*, at this bare two-line text: the politest invitation imaginable. And WITH A LONG SPOON keeps its cold little proverb grinning under all this warmth. What I want next: Saturday. What I dread next: Saturday. My guess from here — marked as a guess — is that the house will be immaculate, tender, and that I will spend the whole chapter listening for a second woman's key in that door.
