## Reader reaction

Oh, this one landed — and it landed clean. After the practice room's braided communion-and-harvest, here is a chapter with no design in it at all except the dress and the woman inside it, and I felt the difference in my shoulders. Vee alone in a high warm room, her name on a card taped to a folding chair, holding a seam shut with one low hand — and then: "She took a breath, and made the second step long." I actually caught my own breath. That's the whole book in one stride — the girl who'd been pointed at and picked and dressed choosing, on her own, in front of somebody's parents, to give the room her leg and keep her face level. The murmur, the scroll "absurdly light for the weight of the walk," the heat coming up "a half-second before her head was sure of it" — body ahead of mind again, but this time she doesn't veto it. She rides it.

And Cassie. God, Cassie. "You look—" and not finishing it, "which from Cassie was a standing ovation." Taking the phone out of her hand to hold it. Eyes bright at the corners "which she'd deny to the grave." The glancing blow of last chapter is repaired without a word about it — Cassie just shows up, holds the phone, takes the picture, buys the terrible sandwich. This is the reunion I was dreading and it turned out to be the warmest thing in the chapter. I may have been braced for the wrong door.

The photo is the chapter's closed circuit: Cassie catches the frame, Vee "knew to the heartbeat what second it was," sends it uncropped to Pace in his Berkeley hotel — "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." There's something piercing in it: the dress he made for exactly this, worn bare the way it was built to be worn, the wearing he'd never see, documented by the one friend with no design. Three people who each hold a different true piece of the same second.

Pace's absence is handled beautifully — sorry "in the quiet way he was sorry about things," the conference that won't move against the ceremony that won't move. And the memory that he was "up the mountain with her" when she talked the induction up, cherries in her hand — so he knew what it meant before the dress existed. The dress is the most anyone has ever made for her and they both know it. I believe that completely.

Suspicion: almost none this chapter, and I want to say so plainly. The only cold note is structural — he made the dress for her name read out in a good room, and I know the induction is also where "she gets there," whatever the design's destination is. But on the page, this is hers. The last line says it exactly: pride or heat, "by now, the two came to the same thing."

Friction: none worth naming. The title, "The Induction," is doing double work and I know it — it's the honors ceremony on the page, but given everything I carry about the sorority and the plan, I half-expected this chapter to be the *other* induction, and the ceremony is almost a feint. Or both are the same event and the dress walk IS the induction, performed before an audience that doesn't know what it's watching. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, and *With a Long Spoon* still sit oblique — the walk itself felt like a woman supping with something and holding the spoon herself, for once.

What I want next: whatever the induction's other half is — the sorority side, Brooke's folder, the roped rows of honorees (is Vee being inducted into something more than the honor society?). And I want Pace to open that photo.
