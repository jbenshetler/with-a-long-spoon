## Reader reaction

Nobody touches anybody in this chapter, and it's one of the most charged things I've read in this book. The charge lives in the long build of carefulness — the managed steps, "a hand kept low at her hip," "the small choreography of a woman with a secret keeping it" — and then the release she authors: "She took a breath, and made the second step long." I actually thrilled for her. The murmur, "the small still beat where a warm dull room worked out that there was nothing between it and her but the silk" — I felt the room wake up. And then the book's signature arrives, in her own service this time: "her body agreeing, low and certain… catching up to a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it." For three chapters that body has answered *to* people — Randi's thumb, Pace's hand, Randi's voice. This time she aimed it. It reads like freedom. It reads like graduation.

And then the undertow, because I can't unknow what I know. The bareness her bravest public act required was the dress's built-in condition — she "found it out in his mirror," and I've been carrying for chapters that the mirror and pedestal were staged. The heels on her ankles were found by Randi, who "knelt and done the little ankle strap with two fingers and sat back to look" — the fitting thumb, transposed. The frosted plum on her fingernails is the polish she hid for Pace and Randi found, read, and thumbed; now it "woke and threw the light" in public. She walks into her own triumph wearing both custodians, and her one secret — "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers" — is the chapter's knife, because he *built* the slit. I strongly suspect he can read every inch of that frame. Her private pocket of ownership is, I fear, pre-read. The last line says it plainer than she can: she can't tell "whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing." That's the conditioning's end-state, spoken as a feeling of power. Both things are true and the book makes me hold both.

Feelings moved: **Cassie** owns this chapter's heart. "You look—" unfinished as "a standing ovation"; taking the phone "to hold" and then revealing she *shot* it — "Somebody had to document it" — eyes bright "which she'd deny to the grave"; "Come on, streetlight." She read Vee "fast and whole" and is the only person in this book who reads her without an angle. **Pace** stays swoon-at-a-distance; the sorry with "no performance in it" landed sincere, and "the most anyone had ever made for her and they both knew it" genuinely moved me. **Randi**, absent, keeps giving gifts that are *exactly right*, which helps nothing about my structural suspicion. No new suspicion earned on the page — nobody worked her this chapter. That's almost the scary part: the machine ran unattended.

Friction: Cassie says "Go make your mother cry again," so the mother I've been dreading is *in that room* — and Vee never once thinks of her during the bare walk, and the page never gives the mother a line. That silence is either a deferral (the dinner still coming?) or a deliberate routing-around, and I noticed the seam.

The title: the ceremony, obviously — but *induction* is also initiation, and, given whose lover built the dress, it's a proof method: establish the first step, then one step implies every step after. First step small. Second step long. And the jacket's tagline has never rung truer: nobody asked her to open the slit; every yes was hers; that's exactly the problem. The long spoon now reaches from Berkeley and from a practice room — full presence, zero contact.

What I want next: Pace in his hotel opening that photo; whether it travels to Randi; the dinner and the mother; the mouse-that-roars question still burning unanswered.
