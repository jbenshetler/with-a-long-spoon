# Cold read (grounded) — The Induction

## Reader reaction

**The Induction — felt read:**

Oh, this one landed. It's a short chapter and it does exactly one thing, and it does it all the way down: the daydream from the fitting — the room turning, being looked at and costing nothing — finally happens, and it happens *nothing* like the fantasy, which is why it's better. Nobody turns on their own. The room is "warm and dull and kind," the navy girls cross and come back and no one watches. Vee has to *make* the room turn. "She took a breath, and made the second step long." I actually felt my own breath go with hers. The whole chapter hinges on that one decision, and the book trusts it — no one can ever prove anything, "and not a soul in it could have said so out loud," which is exactly what makes it hers. The murmur, the leg given away "a step at a time," the face giving away nothing — I was swept. This is the furthest the shame-loop has ever been from winning: she is bare under silk in a room full of somebody's parents, and instead of the floozy-verdict arriving, her body just *agrees*, "low and certain," the way it did in his mirror.

And then the chapter quietly breaks my heart in the margins. Pace isn't there. He built the dress for precisely this room, and he's in Berkeley, and she can't even say "it's only a dress" or "it's only a ceremony" without it coming out thin, because the mountain remembers. Her mother isn't there either — Cassie's "Go make your mother cry *again*" tells me the crying happened at the letter, from a distance, and that the person actually standing at the door holding Vee's phone is Cassie. The mother who policed egg whites and invented "floozy" does not show up to watch her daughter be honored in a dress a man sewed to her body. Cassie does. Cassie with her unfinished "You look—" which is "a standing ovation," Cassie with bright eyes she'd "deny to the grave," Cassie buying the terrible sandwich. I love Cassie so much it aches, and I ache that Vee's real family in that room is a roommate with a deadpan, while the two people who made this moment possible are a secret from each other in her life.

The photo undid me a little. Cassie catches the exact stride — the leg out to the hip, the face that has "every part of it handled" — and Vee looks at it "and knew to the heartbeat what second it was. None of that was in the picture. Or all of it was, and only she would ever see it there." And she sends it to Pace, uncropped, whole. That is such a tender, such a *trusting* act — and here's where my double-vision as reader kicks in hard. She thinks: "He would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." But he *built the dress*. He knows that slit "came open to the hip and the edge of anything showed" — she found it out in *his* mirror. There is a real chance he opens that photo in his hotel and knows exactly what's under the silk, because he cut the silk. The chapter lets her believe she has a secret from him, and I couldn't tell whether I was watching a woman keep something gloriously private or a woman whose privacy is thinner than she knows — and that not-knowing is this book's whole weather system, and I'm completely inside it.

Trust and suspicion: honestly, in this chapter, nobody does anything to earn suspicion. It's the cleanest, most Vee-owned chapter in a long time — maybe ever. Randi appears only in a memory, kneeling with the ankle strap "with two fingers," and even that I read as tender rather than staged, though the kneeling echoes are piling up. The unease is all structural, all mine: the induction was the thing the game was *pointed at* — the dress debut, the shoes, the room turning — and both of its architects are absent for it. Which means the moment they engineered got away from them and became hers alone. I find that wonderful and I don't trust it to last.

The heat is real and unusual — it's heat with no man in the room, no touch, just a body in silk and a decision. "Whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing" — that's the thesis of the whole chapter and maybe of Vee, and the book just lets it stand there unanswered. Erotic charge peaked on the walk itself and I wouldn't change a word of it. No friction for me anywhere; even the scroll being "absurdly light for the weight of the walk" felt exactly right.

The title: "The Induction" is doing double duty now — the literal honors ceremony, and an initiation she performs on herself. She wasn't inducted by the department; she inducted herself, one long step in, into being a woman who gives a room her leg and doesn't make herself small. And there's a third edge for the reader who knows what I know: induction is recruitment language, and she is being recruited — except today the recruit walked her own walk. The cover-board line, WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION, keeps darkening for me: you sup with the devil using a long spoon, and "A Polite Invitation" is exactly what this whole courtship has been. Today Vee took a bite that nobody served her, and I loved it, and I'm braced for the bill.

What I want next: I want Pace's hotel room — I want to see him open that photo, because whatever crosses his face will tell me something true. And I dread, gently, the moment Vee finds out how much of her "that part was hers" was ever private at all. (Both guesses from here.)
