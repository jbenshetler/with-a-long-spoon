## Reader reaction

This is the chapter the book has been building toward, and it got me. Fully. I was aroused and moved and quietly sick with the knowledge I carry, all at once, which I think is exactly the experience this novel wants me to have.

The first half delighted me before it undid me. She texts him *first* — "she had never once been the one to reach first" — and arrives an hour early, and catches him off-guard in gym shorts with a shake, "the salt of the gym still on him," and his face doing "the gladness he never quite managed to hide." And then the poking, and the flinch — "He had been a wall since the day she met him… and she had just watched it flinch." I loved that. She's never landed on him before. And the chase — her discovery that she's *faster* than him on open floor, "all that mass of him had to gather and come around like a ship," the stolen red shorts waved "like a flag over a taken field," and then the flash — "She had never in her life done such a thing." I was grinning. She is brave and playful and free, and the text names her freedom's sources as "the house… the game… the week of wanting" — and I sat there with my blood cold, because *the game* is not the game she thinks it is.

Then the bedroom. The bet is the whole book in miniature: "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." Both branches end in her surrender; both branches are technically her choice. "Slow enough that she could have stepped out of it the whole way and didn't." Every yes freely given. The finger held up "gleaming in the lamplight," "the old shame doubled, the wet and then the shameful plenty of the wet" — and his look, "the way he had looked at her that first evening, under the tree… Like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." I believed him. That's the thing. I believe his tenderness completely, and I have heard him say "I want her to get there on her own," and both are true, and I cannot reconcile them and neither can the book, and that's the engine.

The spanking itself is the hottest passage so far — "No. Slow. That's part of the bet too" (it wasn't), the turned-down bed she watches him prepare, "the place where the debt would be paid, and she the payment," the tap that is "almost nothing… a question," her hips lifting into his hand while her mouth says "It's not. I don't—" until "she could not have said which one of them she was lying to." And mid-scene, Randi's story rises in her "like something she'd swallowed" — she braces for theatre, counting, a show — and "what came was nothing like the story." That detail wrecked me, because whether or not Randi designed it, the false script made his quietness land as revelation. Her installed material did its work in a room she wasn't in, again.

And then the line I will carry: "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness, anywhere." But there was a witness. Me. And there will be another one, downstream, if the pattern holds — "no Randi to be told" is a sentence that can only be true for the length of an afternoon. The aftercare — the juice held to her mouth, "wonderful, wonderful," "for the first time she could remember, treasured" — reads unplanned and I believe it's unplanned, and I also know care is his native method. Both. The scrunchies with no note, "more of them than a person could go through quickly," the plenty saying "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet" — duration claimed in green (the green again, the satin's color). And the curry that "looked like a dozen gravy-covered dishes from her childhood. It tasted like none of them." Ohio remade by his hands. She's going to bring a bag. Randi said "Move in. I mean it." She is walking toward the cage and furnishing it herself.

Vee: prouder of her than ever — the text, the early arrival, the flash — and grieving the denials. Her mirror-honesty afterward ("My Victorian LARPing") is real growth and real surrender at once; the last defense has become a joke she tells instead of a wall she keeps. Pace: most charmed and most alarmed I've been. "There it is" is the coldest line in the chapter — recognition, not discovery. The flinch and the juice warm him; the bet's architecture is consent wearing a trap's clothes. Randi: absent two chapters running, twice as present. Friction: almost none — the aphorisms ("Shame wants a witness") do thesis-work out loud, but they land, so I don't mind. The chapter is one long scene and earns every page; the curry's decompression is right.

The title: "We Find Out" — the bet's own words. They find out about her; she finds out about herself; and I find out that the reader is the witness the room pretends not to have. *A Polite Invitation* is enacted here more nakedly than anywhere yet — an invitation she could refuse at every step, ending exactly where it was always going. What I want next: the retelling question, burning now — does she carry this to Randi, does a version of "the plenty" become an anecdote-gem, and does Randi's face do the thing Vee won't catch? What I dread: the bag becoming a move-in, and the dinner with her mother still out there somewhere.
