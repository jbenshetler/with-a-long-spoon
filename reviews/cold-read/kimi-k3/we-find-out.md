# Cold read (grounded) — We Find Out

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. This is the chapter the whole book has been quietly building a road to — the spank from the kitchen, the "Please" attached to nothing, Randi's over-the-knee story — and it pays off with such warmth and such patience that I was swept completely. The play-fight first: I loved her here. Stealing his shorts, waving them "like a flag over a taken field," discovering she's faster than him in the cuts and reverses, flashing him — "she had never in her life done such a thing" — this is a Vee I've never seen, drunk on her own speed, and the fact that *she* texted first, *she* came an hour early, *she* started the war, makes the whole scene hers before he ever touches her. And that tiny flinch when she hits his thigh — the first thing of hers that has ever landed on him, "he had been a wall since the day she met him" — I noticed it and I don't know what it means, and I'm holding it open.

The bet is the hinge and it's beautifully done. "Don't" — and the hand leaves *instantly*, palm up, empty, and her body's answer to its absence tells her the truth her mouth won't. "Yes." "Yes?" "We find out." The title sitting right there as her own words. And then the finger, and the terrible ease — "her body had decided without her… every night this week in the dark and hadn't thought to tell her, and now it was telling him" — the shame of the *plenty* of the wet, and him looking at it the way he looked at her under the tree, "like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." That's the swoon of the whole book for me, the shame-to-gift conversion done at full voltage, and then "Slow. For me" — handing her back the giving of it — and I melted. The spanking itself refusing to be Randi's story — no counting, no theatre, just a tap, a question, soothing, a kiss — was exactly right, and her hips lifting while her mouth says "It's not" broke my heart a little. The line that undid me most: "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness." After a book full of eyes — the riser, the courtyard windows, the induction stage — she finally gets a room with none.

And then, because I am apparently incapable of just being happy here: I know how he knew. Or I suspect. He felt her go still in the kitchen himself — that's real, that's his own — but Randi extracted the spank story in the practice room and Randi carries everything to his bed, and he walked into this afternoon with a bet already shaped in his pocket, "spanks from a lost bet come on a bare bottom," with the confidence of a man holding intelligence. Vee never once wonders how he knew to offer exactly this game on exactly this day; the wondering is left entirely to me, and it's the same old vertigo — everything he did was tender, everything she gave was given, and the path was graded. "No Sunday, no Randi to be told" — that clause stopped me cold. For the first time she lets there be a thing that isn't for Randi. Except Randi will be told, or Randi already authored the frame. Also: "the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep," her palm on the scar, not asking — I've decided to keep him too, and I remember Daphne, and I don't know what I'm keeping.

The aftercare wrecked me in the good way. The juice — "sweet and cold and faintly ridiculous" — held to her mouth first, before anything. "Wonderful, wonderful," quieter each time, until the word and his hand in her hair were the same thing, and "for the first time she could remember, treasured." And then the scrunchies — a whole form of them, deep green, more than a person could go through quickly, no note, "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet" — that's a vow made in bulk, and it's the most romantic object in the book so far. Her mirror monologue — "My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky," "I'm going to start bringing a bag" — made me laugh; the pretense is over and she knows it and she's fine. And of course he feeds her: the curry that looks like her mother's casseroles and tastes like none of them. That is this whole relationship in one bowl — the familiar shape, the new content, the heat arriving late.

As a title, "We Find Out" did not give anything away beforehand — it could have pointed anywhere — and inside the chapter it turns out to be the terms of the bet and also the chapter's whole action: they find out what her body already knew, and quietly, so do we. The volume title "With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation" keeps recoloring: this was the politest possible invitation into a thing she was too ashamed to ask for, and the long spoon — supping with the devil — hums underneath, because I still don't know whether the devil is in the room or only in my knowledge of it. Next I expect the telling: this goes to Randi, and I want and dread that scene in equal measure — whether Vee frames it as her discovery, whether Randi's face does anything, whether "no Randi to be told" survives contact with the practice room. My guess from here: the scrunchies' "long while yet" is the book making a promise it intends to test.
