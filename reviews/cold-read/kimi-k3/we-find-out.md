This chapter swept me up completely, and I want to say that plainly before anything else. The whole first movement — her texting first for the first time, coming at three when he said four, the poke that actually *landed* on the wall — was pure delight. "She had just watched it flinch." This is the freest I've ever seen her, and it's in *his* house, in the middle of the design — and I didn't care, because the page made it feel earned and hers.

The bet itself is where the heat peaked for me. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out" — the slow hand up the thigh, the whole time she could have stepped away and didn't, the nod she didn't decide to give. And then the finger coming out coated, "more of her than she would ever have admitted she had in her" — the old shame of the wet, doubled, and his answer to it being that look, "like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." That undid me as much as it undid her. And the line "shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness" is the thesis of her whole arc arriving in one sentence.

The aftercare is what broke me open, though. The juice. "Sweet and cold and faintly ridiculous." He hadn't planned it, "and that was the thing that undid her." The hair stroking, "wonderful, wonderful," "for the first time she could remember, treasured." Then the scrunchies — a whole form of them, more than she could go through quickly, "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." And then he *cooks her a curry*. I melted. If this is the design, it is wearing the most convincing human face imaginable, and I'm finding it harder and harder to hold the harvest-ledger in my head against the evidence of this chapter.

Where my wariness lives now: it's quieter than it's ever been, but not gone. The bet's terms grew under her — "No. Slow. That's part of the bet too" when it hadn't been — and she noticed and let it go, "too far down into the warm dim place." The title, "We Find Out," is his wager made literal — and it cuts both ways: *she* finds out too, what her body has been deciding all week without telling her. She says it herself in the mirror: "Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together."

I want to know if Randi gets told. That's my dread now — this is the chapter's silence: "no Randi to be told" inside the bed, but outside it, Randi exists, and Pace is Randi's.
