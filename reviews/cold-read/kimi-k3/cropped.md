## Reader reaction

This one got me quietly, and then kept getting me after I finished it. On its face it's the smallest chapter in a long while — two girls, a cafeteria, trays — but it's the first chapter where the person doing the concealing is Vee, and the concealment is entirely hers. Nobody steered this conversation. Randi isn't in it, Pace isn't in it, and still Vee cannot produce a whole version of her life for anyone. She crops the photo for her mother. She tells her father "the parts that fit." And when Cassie finally asks the only real question — "So what is he. Not to your dad. To you." — she has nothing. "Both. Probably both." Can't say and don't want to, and she can't even tell which is which. I felt that in my chest. Because I know something Vee doesn't: even her most uncropped answer would still be a crop. The full frame of her own relationship contains Randi, and Vee doesn't have the negative. The title does all of that work — the photo cropped at the waist for Mom, the story cropped for Dad, the self cropped for Cassie — and underneath it, for me alone, the girl who sent Pace the *uncropped* frame in chapter 31 now living inside someone else's crop without knowing it. It didn't give anything away; it recolored everything.

The parents are rendered with such economy it almost hurt. Mom crying twice with pride and then touching her own collarbone at the shoulders of the dress — the shame-voice transmitted without a single word, "You know how she does," and Cassie does. That's the "floozie" voice in its native habitat: not spoken, gestured. And Dad burning the rolls on purpose, pricing the dress in silence, picturing a grey-templed doctorate and getting a boy barely older than his daughter. Vee's line — "my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would" — made me laugh out loud with Cassie, and then her follow-through, "weird is worse than wrong... wrong is something he understands and rejects," is the sharpest thing Vee has ever thought on the page. She is *not* stupid about her own life. That's what's moving: "I'm not ashamed of it. The opposite, if anything." I believe her. The shame is losing. What's left isn't shame — it's that the truth has no frame it fits in.

Cassie deepens again without asking for anything. Her parents still treating her as a high schooler, the dork brother-in-law, and then that careful, exact, not-prying question — the closest she has ever come to pushing — and when Vee gives her "both," Cassie nods and goes back to her plate. And the last line is the one I'll carry: "Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not." She half-wanted to be pressed. Some part of her wants a question big enough to force the answer out of her, because she can't get it out alone. I ache for both of them — Cassie watching her oldest friend recede an hour at a time, Vee standing at the edge of a confession she doesn't have the pieces of.

No heat on the page, and none needed — though the ghost of it hangs over everything, because we know what the uncropped photo shows and what the dress was worn with nothing under. Trust-wise, nothing moved: no new suspicion of anyone, no reason to doubt Cassie ever. If anything the chapter *lowered* my guard, which is its own kind of dread — the architecture was invisible for a whole chapter, and I noticed its absence like a draft.

What I want next: I want to know what Thanksgiving at Pace's with Sheri was actually like (we only got Vee's side). I want, with real dread, the day Vee's father meets Pace and prices him in person. And I want Cassie's patience to run out — except I don't, because when it does, something breaks.
