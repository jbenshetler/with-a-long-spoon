That undid me — it's the playlists-already-playing again, the dough set going before she arrives: care as the shape his attention takes. And then the chapter turns that tenderness into the one unspeakable thing. Her father does silent math on the dress's cost and pictures grey temples; the truth — a twenty-two-year-old who makes his girl's ceremonial dress by hand — would read to him as *weird*, and "weird is worse than wrong." "Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's the line I'm keeping.

No new suspicion earned on the page — Cassie is exactly, carefully kind. My unease is the one I walked in with, and I notice Randi is never even *named* here, though she's the reason "So what is he… to you" has no clean answer. Her absence is the loudest thing in the cafeteria. And I realized with a small jolt that I only ever get Pace through Vee's crops too.
