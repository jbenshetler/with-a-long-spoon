# Cold read (grounded) — Believe Me

## Reader reaction

I finished this chapter sitting very still. This is the one the last three chapters were loading — "Seconds," "Under the Rug," "Bare" — and it breaks exactly where it had to break, in the doorway, over the word.

Vee first, because she's magnificent in it. She drives out with her jaw set and a taste like a coin under her tongue, coat buttoned to her throat — the cardigan again, the armor back on, and I noticed she carries nothing in: "The bag was in the car." The whole chapter is in that detail; she came to a house where she has a *drawer* and she brought nothing to put in it. And then her speech. "You touch me with such care, treat me like this precious thing... Like my body comes with me attached. Nobody ever made me feel that." She lays out the entire liturgy of his goodness and then turns it: "All of that is in here... Out there, what are we?" Her voice doesn't shake. Mine did. I've watched this girl apologize for her own appetite across a whole fall, and here she stands asking the plainest question a person can ask — *who am I to you* — and refusing every soft thing offered in place of an answer. When she flings back "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me," I actually made a sound. It's the truest line in the book so far, because his entire love language is expenditure — heat raised hours ahead, wine already poured, two glasses standing on the counter — everything spent except the coin she asked for.

And Pace. God. From inside her eyes this reads as a man who won't commit, and she is *right* to feel it that way — but I know things she doesn't, and the chapter becomes almost unbearable to read with both sets of eyes. "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?" is such a perfectly *him* answer — a mathematician's answer, a proof by comparison — and it is also the answer of a man who cannot say the word because the word is already spoken for. He has Randi. Whatever "girlfriend" or "love" would obligate, it would obligate him toward a truth that would end the experiment he and Randi built around this girl. When he says "Words are cheap. Believe what I do" — the title's line — I heard it as both his whole philosophy and his whole confession: *I cannot tell you. I can only show you.* And the awful thing is, the showing is real. I still believe the warmth is real. But "Something went across his face and shut," arms folding, "the still of a man holding a line at cost" — that is the first time in the entire book I've watched him choose the concealment over her, at a visible price, and let her walk out rather than pay. He reaches to settle her with the hands that settle everything, and the narration lands the knife: "Not the hand in place of the word." And then — the detail that nearly did me in — he calls after her, once, "Vee," and the text says *her name was not the word she'd come for*. He literally gives her a word, one word, the wrong one.

The echo I can't put down: this is the man who keeps the error-correcting-codes book — *say it more than once so the noise can't kill it* — whose whole stated theory of communication is redundancy against noise. And he will not say the sentence even once. That's not a man who thinks words are cheap; that's a man who knows exactly what words cost, and what this one would cost him.

Any swoon? Only inverted — the swoon of the first five lines, the heat already up, the wine poured, the piano moving like water, all the architecture of devotion standing ready, and this time it made me feel sick instead of held, because for the first time Vee is standing *outside* the spell looking at it as architecture. The book has been training me to melt at "he would have brought the heat up hours ago," and this chapter weaponizes that training: "It went through her now, and she hated that it did." Trust vs. suspicion: my suspicion is fully confirmed in shape if not in content — whatever his reason for withholding the word, he has one, and it isn't shyness. What I don't know is whether the withholding protects Randi, protects the plan, or protects something in himself I haven't seen. No heat in this chapter — the wanting is named as grief, not charge, and the sex is entirely in the past tense of her speech. It didn't need any; the tension is all in the unsaid.

Titles: "Believe Me" is his line, and it recolors on the way out — first it sounds like *trust my actions*, and by the end it sounds like a plea from a man who knows his actions are all he can ever offer, and maybe not even honestly those. The volume title — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — lands harder every chapter: supping with something dangerous requires a long spoon, and this is a book where every intimacy is served at a table. She just pushed her chair back from it. And "A Polite Invitation" still hasn't been issued — which means, my guess from here, it will be, and now I dread it arriving into *this* wreckage.

What I expect next: she drives away into the cold with nowhere warm to go — and the other warm place in her life is Randi. I think she goes to Randi, like she did after the Rusty Nail, and tells Randi all of it, and Randi will hold her and comfort her and carry every word of it back to the man she just left. The thought of that scene makes me genuinely queasy, and I cannot wait to read it.
