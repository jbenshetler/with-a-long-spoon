## Reader reaction

She went back. After the bare night, after the heap of shirt and the chosen cold — she got in the car and drove *to* his warm house and said it out loud. "What am I to you?" And I sat there with my shoulders up around my ears right along with her, because I knew — I have known for chapters — exactly how he would answer it, and he did: "You're the most important person in my life," and then, when she wouldn't take the proxy, "Words are cheap. Believe what I do." And her answer is the best line in the book so far: **"Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me."** I actually stopped. That is the whole closed loop named out loud at last — cheap, and still too expensive for him.

What gutted me is that the chapter doesn't let me have a villain. The heat turned up hours early "so she would never once be cold under his roof" — worked out by her, never announced by him. Two glasses poured, one for her. His face opening "the way it opened every time." His offer when she asks what they're doing is the whole good evening, plainly laid: the chicken, the porch, "it's clear tonight, you can see everything." He is doing everything he knows how to do, at full cost, and it is not the thing, and *he cannot find the thing in himself* — "reach for the thing she was asking for and not find it anywhere in himself, come back with the truest thing he had." That sentence broke me a little for both of them. His counter-question — "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?" — is genuinely true and genuinely not the point, and the text knows it: "she hated that he'd made the true thing pull against the point."

And his stillness when she lands the blow: "Something went across his face and shut... the still of a man holding a line at cost." So much for "nothing grinding in him." She couldn't see it at 3 a.m.; I can see it now, and it doesn't fix anything — it makes it worse, because the grinding is real *and* the line holds anyway.

Her inventory speech is the chapter's heart: "Like my body comes with me attached. Like it's just fine as it is. Nobody ever made me feel that." She says every cherished thing out loud, on the record, before she indicts the frame — "All of that is in here. Inside this house, with the door shut. Out there, what are we?" And then the devastating proof of her own thesis: "What do I tell my mother, that he feeds me and we have a lot of sex?" — with no name on it, the best thing in her life leaves her mouth sounding ugly. That's the title working: **"Believe Me"** is his creed — believe what I *do* — and this chapter is the stress test it fails, because a word is not a garnish on the deed; it's the thing that lets the deed exist out there, in the world with other people in it.

The end wrecked me cleanly: she cries, hates that he gets the crying too, turns her face — and his arms come unfolded, the cold going out of him, the hand lifting "to settle her with the hands that settled everything... Not the hand in place of the word." That sentence. She sees the comfort coming and knows it's the closed loop again, and she leaves rather than take it. "Her name was not the word she'd come for. She went out into the cold, and it was worse, and she did not go back in." Two chapters in a row now ending in chosen cold — but this one is a door closing, not a window going grey.

No erotic charge at all and the chapter doesn't want any — the sex is spoken about, beautifully and gratefully, as evidence. Friction: almost none. The scene is one room, one question, and it earns every line. If anything, the coin-taste and shoulder-climbing at the top slightly announce the confrontation before it starts, but I'd have been braced anyway.

The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, reads colder to me every chapter — the warmth of that house *is* the invitation, polite, standing, and nameless. *With a Long Spoon* keeps whispering: you sup here, but at a distance, and no one says what you're eating.

What I want next: I dread and need the aftermath — Cassie and the drive home, Randi in the path of this, and whether "did not go back in" holds. (Guess from here: the pressure this just built has to vent toward Randi.)
