## Reader reaction

She drove to his house with her jaw set and said the whole thing to his face, and I am still holding my breath. This is the chapter the last three were compressing toward, and it did not blink.

The opening hurt before anyone spoke: the heat he'd raised hours early "so she would never once be cold under his roof," and "It went through her now, and she hated that it did." His care landing as pain — that's the whole book in one sentence now. Then the ritual map drawn by violation: the bag left in the car, the chair by the door, the floor she doesn't cross, the mouth she doesn't turn up. I knew what was coming from the mat, and so did his face, eventually — "she watched him not understand," and that watching is itself part of the evidence she's gathering.

Vee — I've never been prouder of her. Her voice doesn't shake and it surprises her; it surprised me too. The inventory speech is the "Bare" litany finally aimed at its author, and she insists on the fairness of it — "I am not complaining, I need you to hear that" — before the turn: "All of that is in here. Inside this house, with the door shut." And then the ugliest, truest line: "What do I tell my mother, that he feeds me and we have a lot of sex?" She lets it come out ugly "because that was the whole of it" — with no name on it, the best thing in her life sounds like that. That's the argument the whole book has been building, and she made it clean.

Pace — my trust took its first real wound, and complicatedly. "You're the most important person in my life" — said "simply, with certainty" — I believe him, and she does too, and it's not what she asked. His counter-question ("Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?") is genuinely good, and the narration gives it to him — she checks, and no, she wouldn't trade — "and it wasn't the point, and she hated that he'd made the true thing pull against the point." Then the creed: "Words are cheap. Believe what I do." And her answer, the best line in the book so far: "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me." I felt that like a struck bell. Because he's right that words are cheap — and that's exactly why withholding them costs him nothing but her. The narration's phrase "reach for the thing she was asking for and not find it anywhere in himself" cut me — it reads as absence, not refusal — but then "Something went across his face and shut" reads as an act, a closing, and the folded arms and "the still of a man holding a line at cost" reads as policy. I cannot tell can't from won't, and neither can she, and that's the engine of the whole scene.

The ending undid me. The hand lifting — "the hands that settled everything" — and her refusal: "Not the hand in place of the word." First time she's ever refused his touch. Then the single call: "Vee," once, "and her name was not the word she'd come for." Then the cold, "and it was worse, and she did not go back in" — rhyming deliberately with the shirt-night's "it was worse... and she did not put it back on." Twice now she's chosen the true cold over the false warm; this time the false warm was his whole house.

Erotic charge: none, and rightly — the sex life appears only as testimony in her argument ("We have sex, so much of it, and it's the best of my life"). The heat engine is fully offline and it feels correct, even necessary.

Friction: almost none. The counter-question is so neatly argued it flirted with staged-debate, but the narration undercut it instantly and kept me inside her. The abandoned evening — two poured glasses, chicken low on the stove — left standing in the house with him after she's gone is the image I'm carrying out.

Title: "Believe Me" is his line — "Believe what I do" — quoted back as the indictment. Belief was never the missing currency; she believes what he does, that's the tragedy. It recolors everything: his philosophy on trial, verdict delivered in one sentence. And the tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — this is its bill coming due: nothing was ever coerced, so there's nothing to blame but the shape itself.

What I want/dread next: Does he chase? The bet established he chases — but he called her name once and let the door close. I dread his next move being an object, a made thing, a gesture — the wrong move, made perfectly. And I dread the alternative: that the word comes now, spent under duress, discounted before it arrives. The price has gone up. (Guesses from here, all of it.)
