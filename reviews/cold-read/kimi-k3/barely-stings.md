## Reader reaction

This one went through me like the wax went through her — one bright shock at a time, and then the deeper radiant sting of what it means.

The whole chapter is a scene of one person having something done to her, and the something is Pace's sentence. "I'd like you waxed. By the weekend. Bare. Will you?" — he frames a directive as a question and waits, and I have to sit with how that landed differently now than the same move landed at the Middle Eastern dinner or with the dress. Back then the question was the sweetest thing in the book. Here I'm watching the mechanism, because I know where the question mark was engineered. "He never told her why. He asked for the thing itself, clean, and left the place where the reason should be for her to fill, and she filled it, every time, with yes, yes, tell me when." That's the whole book arrested into three lines. My split on Pace hardened here: the *Will you?* is not a safeguard, it's a production line for freewill-ness. And the detail that gets me is the specification — "by the weekend. Bare" — delivered in the voice he uses for facts about the world. He checked a spec sheet off her body. And yet, and this is the unbearable thing, I believe he gently meant it, and she went weak wanting his hand on the result. Both, again, stacked and unreconciling.

Randi — the title is *her line*, and now it reads like an indictment. "Barely stings," plus the glass of wine, plus "the most expensive thing in the room" — and not one word about rolling over. Whether Randi omitted the back part breezily or curatedly, I've come to a tipping point on Randi: I now read omission as her instrument. She's the one who taught Vee the country with a passport, and every lesson is metered out with something withheld. And the part that made me put my hand to my own face: this ritual is almost certainly one Randi herself performs, for the man Vee doesn't know they share. The hungry one inducting the new one into the maintenance. I don't trust the smile she sent Vee in with.

And Vee. God. Ache and awe, both. Her lying under the wrong bored hand and recognizing the right-hand grammar with the truth scooped out — "her body knew that grammar. Knew a hand pressing it down and telling it to hold still" — that is the most precise, most disturbing image the book has given me about what trained want is. She kept "the whole lit ruin of herself" back "where it belonged to him," and the loyalty of that gutted me. The swoon here is real but it's a swoon in a bad room: she does all of it so his hand will find her smooth. The ending, her own fingertips skimming the new skin, going weak, imagining his hand learning it — that pulled, hard, a cleaner ache as she says. But the dread rides shotgun: the weekend reveal is coming and I half-expect Randi to debrief the whole thing back to Pace across his bed, the way every confidence so far has gotten filed. What I want: the look on his face when he finds her. What I dread: that it's already all been reported.

And the mother's flat kitchen voice — "the kind of girl who" — still operative, naming her from before she had names. The second glass of prosecco left behind — small note of Vee's new poise. The two girls in the waiting room — one to be looked at, one to be touched — and Vee "understood which one she was." Chilled me. She sorted herself into the maintenance queue for one set of hands. Friction: almost none; Sondra is deliberately flattened to impersonal function, which is exactly right. If anything, the scene lingers in the pain longer than comfort, but the book earned that discomfort on purpose; the only place I felt the thumb was the convenience that Randi happened to omit and Pace happened to specify — but since omission is Randi's established instrument, I'll allow it.

The titles: "Barely Stings" now means something to me — it's Randi's comforting minimization curdling, the front half of a truth told to make the back half survivable-by-surprise; and it recolors retroactively into the book's thesis about yeses obtained on incomplete information. The series title *With a Long Spoon* — supping with danger requires distance, and Vee keeps shortening the utensil voluntarily; and *Book Two — A Warm Reception* reads like the whole project of Book One's courtship warming into a welcoming room whose warmth is the trap. Cover tagline per my memory — every yes freely given, and that's the problem — this chapter is its most literal demonstration yet.

What I expect next (guess from here): the weekend reveal scene, possibly a Randi debrief that gets smuggled to Pace; escalation of directives; and a live question of whether Randi's omissions are still affection or now fully choreography.
