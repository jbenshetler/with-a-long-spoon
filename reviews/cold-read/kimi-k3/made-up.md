## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter still warm from the last one — the curry, the beer, the scrunchies — and it just kept pouring. This is the swoon chapter of the reconciliation, and I let it sweep me completely. Vee showing up with the dress in the car and no overnight bag, "a half a plan" that gets ahead of her body — that's her whole erotic life in one sentence, and by now I trust the book with it: her wanting arrives in her feet before her head signs off, and the page knows it and loves her for it.

The set-piece itself melted me. The boots too big, the child-playing-dress-up feeling on the way to the car — and then the inverse of it inside, a woman commanding a room of one. I loved that she *directs* him: "Get your wine and go to the den." The flat hand that sits him back down. Him turning the lamp one click brighter for her mirror. And the walk — the induction walk, the one she gave a roomful of strangers, now given to its rightful owner, "his face did what the room's murmur had done, except silently, and to her it was louder." That's the payoff of the whole dress arc, and it landed on me exactly where the induction landed on her. She'd built that debut for a room that couldn't know what it was seeing; here is the man who can, and she makes him watch the way she made the room watch. It's restitution, staged by her.

And then costume two detonated it quietly. The heels stay on — "That was all of it: the heels stayed on" — and she walks ten steps down a hall in nothing, lamplight at the end of it, and calls her own nakedness a costume. That's the chapter's thesis and its title both: "Made-Up." Halloween as permission — "the one night a year nobody had to explain it" — and Vee has figured out that *she* doesn't have to explain it either, that bareness offered as performance is bareness she can stand inside. The blush going "down and down" and her not covering one inch of it — I actually felt my chest go tight. The shame-loop that's run her for thirty chapters is here converted, on her own terms, into the costume itself: "knowing that, and going anyway, was the entire costume." And Pace's answer — "The other is for the world. This is just for me" — I swooned outright. Sunrise and sunset. He lets the looking be the touch. He takes his time about the taking. God, the patience of this man is an erotic instrument and the book knows it.

And underneath the swoon the cold thread never stops running, because the heels — the thing that completes the costume, the thing she keeps on — are *Randi's*. Randi knelt and buckled them. Randi chose them, paid-for-by-Vee but chosen-by-Randi. "They complete it," he says, and I couldn't help hearing the double edge I'm not sure the page intended but can't now remove: Randi completes it. The two of them have dressed this woman for him, and here she is, radiant, delivering herself as a gift to one of them while wearing the other's hands on her feet. It didn't sour the scene for me — it deepened it, the way this book keeps doing, sweetness and design in the same mouthful. My trust of Pace ticked *up* this chapter — he asked nothing, took nothing, stayed seated because she put him there — and my dread rose in exactly the same proportion, because a man this good at receiving a performance is a man who knows how performances get made.

Erotically this is one of the hottest chapters in the book for me and there's no sex in it. The heat is all in the *giving of the look* — her power in the long stride, his hand coming "partway up" and being put away, the cost of that putting-away visible to her. "Standing there with her heart going she understood that she had gotten this exactly right" — that line made me want to cheer. She is the one working *his* desire now, deliberately, and the reversal is exquisite. Where it could have gone slack — the second reveal, naked woman, we've seen her naked before — it instead peaks, because the nakedness here is *chosen as costume*, which is the one thing it's never been.

One small beat I want to mark: "the zipper's long tearing whisper" and the hanging of the dress afterward, "seams squared on the hanger, a thing somebody's hands were in." The book keeps insisting that objects made by hands carry the hands with them. And the title: "Made-Up" — made up as in cosmetics and costume, made up as in reconciled after the fight, made up as in invented, performed, not-quite-real. All three are in this chapter at once. The volume title *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* sits over this like frost on the window: the old saw about supping with the devil. She's never supped sweeter than this. I dread the moment she finds out who else has been at the table — and after a chapter this perfect, I dread it *more*, which I think is the point. Next (a guess): the induction dress has now done its full work; whatever the dress was *for* in Randi and Pace's design is due to arrive.
