## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. This is the loveliest chapter in the book so far and it hurt the most, and those two facts are the same fact.

Vee first: I've never liked her more. She *authors* this one. Two "No"s, "Get your wine and go to the den," the flat hand that sits a man twice her width back into his chair — and she knows exactly what she's built: "standing there with her heart going she understood that she had gotten this exactly right." The walk given twice, the dress and then what was under it, and then that final image — the blush going "down and down" and her not covering one inch of it. After the bet chapter's "could not have said which one of them she was lying to," watching her body and her will finally point the same direction felt like a sunrise. I was proud of her. Genuinely.

And Pace — he plays it perfectly, which is the problem. The one click brighter on the lamp, unasked. "Take your time." The eyes that stop at her feet: "They complete it." The hand that comes up and gets put away, "and the putting-away cost him something she could see." I believe the cost. I also remember "I want her to get there on her own," and I watched a man let himself be directed for exactly one evening, inside his own house, his own lamp circle, his own dress. Then the line: "The other is for the world. This is just for me." She hears devotion. I hear a claim that I happen to know is temporary — because nothing in that house has ever stayed just his; everything gets carried to Randi. The text even hands me the tell: "a man being handed something and taking his time about the taking." The taking. There it is.

Trust vs. suspicion: my suspicion is total now, and this chapter doesn't sharpen it so much as weaponize the tenderness against me. Nothing he does is wrong. That's what's wrong. The one thing I keep turning over: she sent him the uncropped photo — so "You missed the debut" isn't quite true, and his silence about that frame (whatever he read in it, in whatever hotel) sits inside her gift like a stone.

Erotic charge: peak of the book for me, with zero contact. "He stayed where he was and let the looking be the touch" — that's the sentence of the chapter, and the nude ten-step walk in the heels is the scene of the book so far. Nothing clinical, nothing slack; the restraint *is* the sex.

Friction: almost none. "Choose between sunrise and sunset" is a shade too polished out of him — but she gives him an eyebrow for it, and he recovers into the real answer, so the book knows its own man. My only other itch is Randi's third straight absence — I feel it as a held breath now, not a gap.

Titles: "Made-Up" is doing four jobs. Halloween. Makeup she isn't wearing — the blush is "the entire costume." A made-up story: "Tonight they're costumes" is a fib she needs, the same way she needed the bet — she still can't want without a fiction to want inside. And *made up*: assembled. By his hands, her buckling hands. The bravest self Vee has ever shown arrives dressed as a game, inside the game she doesn't know about. As for *A Polite Invitation* — this chapter she issues the invitation herself, and the tagline grins at me: the freest yes yet, and the trap's exact shape. *With a Long Spoon* still means nothing glossed on the page, but it feels like his whole method: long reach, slow supping.

What I want/dread next: for the night to keep going, and for it never to be narrated. Randi's return is coming, and I now dread the extraction of *this* — the one thing Vee is proud of rather than ashamed of. Different meat for the same grinder.
