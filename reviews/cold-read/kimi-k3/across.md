## Reader reaction

This chapter is a warm bath, and I let it be one — mostly. After three chapters of bedroom and kitchen intimacy, the book puts Vee back among her own people, in sunlight, in a loud glass room that smells of butter, and the pleasure of it is real. Kayla grieving the waffles at a locked door like a woman at a deathbed, then being restored entirely by orange juice arriving in a flute — I was grinning. Meg's maple-bacon-and-Matrix apology story is a perfect little counterweight: *sometimes you have to make it right in their language.* Everybody at this table understands the grammar of making amends through a gesture. It's the friendliest chapter in a long time, and the ease is earned — Vee rescued the morning, paid attention to whether the expense was okay before springing it, split the check three ways with nobody covering anybody. Her friendships run on fairness the way Pace's house runs on quiet provision, and the chapter lets me feel both systems at once without lecturing about it.

The swoon, for me, is Vee's comfort in the telling. She performs the porch scene for Kayla and Meg with a slow sip of mimosa and a dropped punchline — "I flashed him on his front porch" — and she is *good* at it, savoring the shriek she knew she'd get. The girl who died by inches in front of Kayla's laundry basket three days ago now narrates the same night as a caper. That transformation is delicious. And the last line — "Vee had the middle of it all the whole time" — lands as pure earned happiness: her people on one side, Pace on the other, Wednesday coming to join them.

And here's where my unease lives, and it is entirely earned by the page: the story she tells is not the story that happened. "She'd decided she wasn't doing that anymore" — she didn't decide; Randi designed it, coat, heels, nothing underneath, down to the drive. Randi has been edited out of her own authorship, and Vee doesn't even know she's doing it — she genuinely experiences the plan as hers now, which is exactly what Randi intended ("let her arrive at it on her own"). I watched Vee do the same thing with Cassie in "unpacking" — the sweater-shrinking lie, "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." With Cassie she lied knowingly; here she lies innocently, and I'm not sure which one scares me more. Also the tiny tell: "she never had to say one word about the drive." The near-caught-by-a-cruiser drive, the self-touch at the wheel — curated out even from the audience who'd have loved it most. There's a layer of the experience Vee keeps for no one, or for Pace only. Or — I realize with a small chill — for Randi, come Tuesday, anthro, the little table, "nearly everything."

The Wednesday dinner is the engine now, and I notice Pace built it. "I want to meet your people" was his move, his venue, his treat — and Vee announces it as "he's handling it" with audible pride. But Kayla's question — "I need to know what this dinner *is*" — is the right one, because I know things about the guest list Vee doesn't: Pace and Randi will be at the same table for the first time in front of her, pretending to be near-strangers who said nine words about math once. Two people who have shared a bed the entire length of Vee's courtship, sitting across a dinner table performing their first real meeting. And Theo may come — the boy who asked her out, invited by Pace without a flicker. And Cassie, who sees more than she says, will be watching Randi with those flat eyes. I am braced for Wednesday the way Kayla is, except with dread under the delight instead of hair plans.

The title: "across." Across the table, most plainly — flutes meeting over the middle, Vee telling the story across to her friends, the worlds about to sit across from each other on Wednesday. But it also catches Vee's position: she is the thing *across which* all these people will finally be connected, the middle of it, and she doesn't know two of the legs are already joined underneath her. It also quietly echoes "Back," where the dinner was proposed across a sink. Oblique but it clicks once the chapter's over.

No erotic content at all, and the chapter doesn't miss it — the charge here is social warmth, Vee's glow still carrying the weekend on her like sun. Heat is at the edges only, in the retold flash and Kayla's "And did he...?"

What I want next: Tuesday with Randi, obviously — the debrief, what Vee chooses to tell her (the kiss-taste thing was teed up in "missed-a-spot" and then this chapter skipped past the anthro dinner entirely — I notice we never got that scene; either it's coming or the book withheld it on purpose). And Wednesday, Wednesday, Wednesday.
