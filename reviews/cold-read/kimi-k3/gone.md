## Reader reaction

The first thing I have to say is that this chapter got me. After *Portion* — the untouched stew, the line drawn through the sauce, the hard fast sex with her eyes shut, the back turned in the bed — I came into this one braced for more of that cold. And instead the whole chapter is a thaw, and it undid me a little, because I remembered exactly what it was thawing *from*. "Is there anything to eat in this house?" She has *never asked him that*. This is the woman of the beet smoothie, the egg whites, the bowl of stew she drew one line through and left. And here she is in his undershirt, feet tucked up, eating half a chicken breast and every stalk of asparagus "to a line he could have drawn with a ruler," and he "did not say so." I nearly cried at a plate of couscous. That's the book's whole language — food as the thing you let yourself want — spoken in Randi's mouth for the first time, and it landed like a confession.

And the shopping story did something to me too, because I'd just read that afternoon from Vee's side — the canoe shoe, the bigfoot walk, the older couple — and now I get Randi's retelling, and what's inside it: "She was *so* embarrassed." A beat, fond. "That was kind of hot." She says it *to Pace*. Out loud. The woman who resolved in chapter one never to let him past the face again is sitting in his kitchen in his shirt telling him that Vee's blush aroused her, and she doesn't catch it or take it back. "She got past me before I could help it." That's the vow cracking, or at least bending, and the page lets me see it while Pace only half-sees it — he notices the staying down "the way he noticed the lamp."

But then the moment that made my chest actually hurt: Pace at the sink, not looking at anything, telling her what Vee does to a room. "Like somebody found the lamp. And she has no idea she does it." And Randi goes still — "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding" — something tightening in her shoulders under his shirt, and then, a beat too late, "Yeah. She is." I read that and something in me just ached. Because nobody has ever said that about Randi. She's the one who walks into rooms and reorganizes them, and it's all *performance*, all the face, and here's the man who once saw past the face describing this other girl's unselfconscious aliveness like it's the most precious thing in the world. The page doesn't tell me whether it's jealousy, or grief, or longing to be that unguarded herself — it holds it open, and I'm holding it open. What she does with it is convert it into desire: gets up, touches him, drops her voice half a register — "God. What she keeps under there. It's *criminal*, Pace." She hands him Vee's body as a picture, but the text is careful: "not the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi... wanting something out loud in his kitchen and not able to stop it." Her wanting is the thing on the table. And he takes *her*, not the picture — "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold."

The erotic charge is real but strange — it's arousal routed through a third person who isn't in the room, and I felt both the heat of it (the slow ride, "coming apart by degrees, the blue on her toes the last composed thing left of her") and a faint unease I couldn't shake. Her blue toes against Vee's frosted plum. The color thread runs through both of them now, and they're both, in different ways, being painted for this household. And "She's got it *bad*. It's a little pathetic. I love it" — *pathetic* is a word with teeth in it, affectionate and condescending at once, and I don't think Randi fully knows which.

The kiss on his lower lip — soft, brief — and "That's why she's so gone over you" is the closest to pure tenderness Randi and Pace have had since the bench. But I noticed what wasn't there too: she never gives him the full picture of the lunches, the mouth kisses, "See you soon, gorgeous." Vee in this telling is a sweet enthusiastic girl who embarrasses easily and listens well — the *pathetique* version. The intimacy Randi is actually building with her stays off this table. So the thaw is real and the withholding is real, in the same woman, in the same hour.

The title, "Gone," is doing quiet work: Vee gone over Pace, Randi going for Vee despite herself, maybe the guarded Randi of chapter one going — and, reading it against the book's geometry, a little foreboding too, like something about to be lost. It fits the run of one-word titles and darkens them. What I want next — and dread — is the induction, the dress and shoes arriving in public, and the first moment these two currents I'm watching from above have to occupy the same room with Vee in it. I also want, selfishly, more of this Randi: the one who asks for food.
