## Reader reaction

This chapter turned me cold in the middle of a warm bed. I've been waiting — dreading, really — to be back inside the Pace–Randi room, and here it is: afterglow, Sade low under the door, her hair across his chest, and the two of them talking about Vee the way you'd talk about a crop that's coming in well. And it's tender. That's the worst of it. "She got past me before I could help it" — Randi confessing a genuine attachment, loose as she only goes afterward — and the confession is itself part of the management. I felt the pull of their intimacy and the trap of it at the same time, and the book clearly intends me to hold both.

The retelling is where my stomach dropped. I *just* read "Sorority." I know what happened in that store: Vee set the giant shoe down without a word; it was **Randi** who came down the aisle in the rhinestone platforms doing the walk, and the older couple's glance barely flushed Vee. But in Randi's version for Pace, Vee is the one who stomps and growls and goes "so red I thought she'd go up" — and then, softer, "That was kind of hot." She hands Pace a Vee who is endearingly, sexily humiliated, and erases her own clowning entirely — the real laugh, "the one that costs her something to give," stays hers. After the bench chapter I can read exactly what that is: even post-sex, even "loose," Randi does not hand Pace a single image of herself uncomposed. She gives him Vee's embarrassment instead. The ch 1 resolution is holding, and it's hiding inside a fond anecdote. That's the most chilling craft in the chapter, and it earned every bit of my unease.

And then the steering: "She's not ready, though. She'll get there. You'll want to take your time." I cannot settle what that is — genuine protectiveness, or Randi slowing the convergence so Vee stays *hers* longer, or a file note from the collaborator. The page won't tell me and I'm holding it open. When Pace says "I want her to get there on her own" and "something crossed her face and was gone" — I felt that crossing. Randi checking the temperature of his heart and not liking, or liking too much, what she found. "God, you two" covers a multitude.

Pace undoes me here as usual. "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" — he *sees* Vee, truly, names her wound in one sentence. And "she comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp" — that's the most beautiful description of Vee anyone's given, and I swooned at it, and then remembered who he's saying it to and what it's for: intelligence passed across a pillow. The swoon curdled without ever fully dying. That's this book's signature move and it worked on me again.

Randi's own movement is the saddest thread. She goes quiet at "somebody taught her to be ashamed" — the egg-whites-and-tofu daughter hearing shame named — and then *uses* it, "that's why she's so gone over you." And her desire for Vee keeps surfacing: "What she keeps under there. It's *criminal*, Pace," showing him with her own body, calling Vee's crush "a little pathetic. I love it." Contempt and hunger in the same breath. I believe her wanting is real; I also watched her convert it into a show for him — and then take him, riding him slow, "the blue on her toes the last composed thing left of her." Even her orgasm is tracked against her composure.

The title, "Gone," does quiet triple duty for me: Vee is "so gone over you," said clinically between the two people engineering her; Randi is going — past boredom, past her own defenses, "she got past me"; and something is simply *gone* — Vee's unarranged life, maybe, or the version of this where anyone's desire is only theirs. The volume title — *With a Long Spoon: A Polite Invitation* — has never fit a chapter better: this is the devils' supper, Vee the topic of the table, the invitation still being written in a hand she'll never see. The old proverb ("he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon") hangs over every warm thing in this room.

What I dread next: Vee in the dress, in the shoes bought on the emergency card, walking into the induction glowing — while these two lie in the lamplight having already priced her. I want the Saturday shoe date's warmth back and I know now what it was costing. My guess from here: Randi's "take your time" is not kindness, and the question of what Randi wants *from* Vee — not for Pace — is the bomb under the rest of the book.
