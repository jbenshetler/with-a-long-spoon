## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt in a very quiet way. I felt immediate tenderness for Vee, and frustration with her too—not for cancelling, which is sensible, but for how completely she has stranded herself inside a question she cannot ask. She types and deletes, highlights a line she cannot remember, holds her thumb over an empty message field: all these small actions made her loneliness physical before the chapter ever reached the bed. Pace’s *“Of course. Study hard. I’ll be here”* is kind on its face. Nothing in it is cold or punitive. That is precisely what makes it unbearable for her; it offers no resistance and no new information. I did not read fresh cruelty into it, but I wanted badly for him to give her something she could hold.

Randi’s cancellation added a second, softer abandonment. Neither woman has done anything wrong, and I have no new suspicion earned by this chapter—the finals and travel are entirely plausible—but *“Last Saturday had been the last one, then, and she hadn't known it while she was sitting in it”* landed hard. Their brunches have become one of Vee’s weekly sources of color and being chosen, and now that warmth is retrospectively altered. January suddenly feels very far away for her, even if objectively it is not.

The flannel sequence devastated me. Her wearing it over another shirt so Cassie will see only *“a girl too far gone in a boy,”* then stripping both off so the cloth can touch her directly, shows how much of her longing has to be concealed even while she is trying to comfort herself with it. For a moment the smell works: the sawdust, salt, cooking, warmth, all the evidence of his care. Then comes the terrible recognition that the shirt is warm only because she has warmed it. That felt truer and sadder than any argument they could have had. She is surrounded by proof of what he does and unable to turn it into proof of what she is.

There was no erotic pull for me in her nakedness. The cold coming onto her bare skin felt exposed and almost self-punishing, not sensual. I ached when she left the shirt *“in a heap where a dropped thing lands”* instead of folding or cherishing it. That small loss of care frightened me more than the sleeplessness. Cassie breathing easily below her sharpened the isolation: she is physically near someone steady and truthful, but the person she needs an answer from is across town, and she has already decided no wording can safely obtain it. My existing knowledge of Pace and Randi’s concealed arrangement keeps the larger situation uneasy, but this chapter itself is not about new deception; it is about how much room silence leaves for Vee to suffer alone.

**Bare** means exactly what she does—removing her shirt, his shirt, and finally the comfort the shirt pretends to provide—but also being left without the usual protections of routine, sex, food, brunch, or a name for the relationship. It did not give anything away; it became clearer and bleaker as the scene went on. The cover titles **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** still make me think of feeding, intimacy, and care offered across some distance. Here that warmth is painfully inverted: the only heat in the bed is Vee’s own, returned to her by cloth. No cover tagline was supplied. Going forward, I dread how much this lonely interval may intensify her need for reassurance, and how vulnerable that leaves her when attention returns. My guess from here is that the unsaid question will become harder to contain after finals, but I do not know whether she will finally voice it or find another way to bury it.
