## Reader reaction

This chapter got me physically and then turned the heat cold in my hands. The sex is vivid and unruly in a way I found genuinely exciting: Randi arriving in that ivory slip with nothing underneath, demanding, “Hard. Like you’re leaving,” and Pace discovering that she likes him “unpolished.” The bed he built creaking for the first time was such a good detail—his careful construction giving way under appetite. But the charge never became simple release. “Tell me” now means something different after the preceding chapters, and I could feel Saturday’s Vee being carried into that bed whether anyone said her name or not.

Randi fascinated me and frightened me more here than she ever has. Her deliberately terrible shirt-rolling and “I know where I am” are funny, possessive, intimate; I believed her pleasure in provoking him. Then “I plan everything” landed like the chapter quietly opening its other eye. She goes still when he considers honest disclosure and comes alive at “give it to her,” using her body to steer the decision while pretending the seduction and the argument are separate things. And yet her desire does not feel counterfeit. “Me,” with Kentucky all through it, and “She’ll remember me,” felt like naked longing and naked acquisitiveness arriving together. Her softness afterward—the loose hair, the watered eyes, staying on his chest—moved me. So did the return of her composed face. The teeth at his shoulder felt like an impulse to mark him before Friday, though I don’t think the page settles exactly what she meant by it.

Pace lost something with me. He initially protects Vee again: “It was not his to hand across,” and I was glad he kept the fitting private. That made his later surrender worse. He can imagine telling her plainly, can even hear how decent his own wording would be, and then lets Randi make decency look small and clumsy. His rhetoric about not explaining or pricing a gift is beautiful self-deception; Vee cannot freely receive an invitation whose central fact has been deliberately withheld. “He felt how right she was, and liked it” chilled me because the page lets him enjoy the corruption of his own standard. The brief stillness after “And she would not know, until she did” was the moment I most wanted him to stop—and he lets it go.

The erotic peak for me was Randi confessing her wish to be Vee’s unforgettable first woman while Pace drives into her. It is hot because her want escapes her polish, and disturbing because Vee is being turned into a future memory before she knows there is a choice to make. I also felt real tenderness between Randi and Pace afterward: the blanket, her staying this time, his quiet “there.” None of that cancels the manipulation; it makes it sadder. Even his final satisfaction that she came “for him” feels precarious when she has also come to settle Friday.

“Remembrance” is devastatingly direct: Randi wants to be the woman Vee remembers at eighty, while Pace is already being governed by his own remembrance of Saturday. It turns memory into something they are competing to author inside Vee. The cover titles, **WITH A LONG SPOON** and **BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now suggest courtesy stretched over something dangerous: dinner, a dress, “may I,” all the polished forms of care surrounding an invitation whose terms remain hidden. No cover tagline was supplied. The covered burgundy panels at the end made the whole book feel like that dress—something beautiful being assembled in secrecy around a woman who has only said yes to dinner.

I want Friday immediately, and I dread it. Vee’s simple “Yes” hurt more than any elaborate cliffhanger could have. My guess from here is that Pace and Randi will attempt to bring her directly into an erotic situation rather than disclose their relationship first; I hope Vee’s own boldness—the woman who pressed her breast to his cheek and laughed—survives the discovery of their design. I also want Randi’s first kiss with Vee to be something she asks for plainly, because her longing deserves better than becoming another trap.
