## Reader reaction

This chapter made my stomach drop. After the extremity and tenderness of the bench, I wanted Randi to get even one private minute of safety; instead she walks into a common room where warmth itself functions like a net. Brooke’s “Oh — *hey*, you” is friendly, and I believe she enjoys being friendly, but by “The not-standing was the move” I was completely on Randi’s side of the door. Brooke questions the paper, the study location, Pace’s name, his field, and whether he teaches her, each inquiry arriving “without a seam.” Sloane and Kinsey soften the room only superficially: their talk about trucks and “short-cute” gives Randi ways to diminish him that she can accept without defending him. “Most of it had been smiling” landed harder than open cruelty would have.

Randi breaks my heart here without asking me to feel sorry for her. Her late smile, her answers arriving a fraction behind the questions, and then the small, purposeless movements around her single room all feel exactly continuous with the woman rebuilding her face in Pace’s bathroom. She can place the clutch where it belongs but cannot remember why she opened the closet. She gets into bed with her cardigan buttoned and her shoes on, under a duvet that is “light and synthetic and adequate.” That flat adequacy hurt because I had just seen the cashmere blanket she brought to his house and the water he had waiting for her. Pace is absent, but his care is everywhere in the contrast. I still feel tenderness toward him; this chapter does not give me evidence that he mishandled her. It does make me afraid that the very completeness of being seen by him has become something she experiences as danger.

The line that changed temperature for me was “*He’s hard to say no to.*” First it is the perfect social alibi, the version in which dating him required no admission of desire. Heard again “in a different key,” it opens onto everything she cannot yet think. I appreciated that the chapter stops there rather than translating the key for me. I do not know whether she is revisiting her wanting, her surrender, her inability to remain composed, or some mixture; what I know bodily is that the joke now frightens her. The not-crying is even more devastating than tears: “It felt like there being no door.” After the previous chapter’s deadbolts and locks, that absence feels terrifyingly total. She has not firmly decided to reject him or what happened. She has simply reached a place where neither wanting nor grief can presently move.

There is almost no swoon for me here, and I did not miss it. The erotic charge is residual—a body coming home in last night’s silk, the unspoken memory under one repeated sentence—but the dominant sensation is exposure without intimacy. Brooke’s warmth earns my suspicion without making her a cartoon predator; she may be curious, protective, status-conscious, or all three, but she enjoys controlling the release. Randi’s matched laugh with Kinsey, pitched before she can choose it, was the moment I felt her self-possession most thoroughly slipping. My only friction was a brief impatience with how exquisitely controlled the interrogation remained, but the control is the experience: no one raises her voice, so there is nothing Randi can legitimately resist.

“Standards” is immediately the board that earns Randi her single, but by the end it also means the standards by which Brooke measures a boyfriend, the house measures a suitable girl, and Randi measures every appetite before allowing it. Her private standard—being real enough to beg and be held afterward—is the one she cannot bring upstairs. The cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now makes “polite” feel especially double-edged: an invitation can be tender, as Pace’s waiting was, or socially impossible to refuse, as Brooke’s questions are. The series title still makes me think of feeding and of his handmade wooden things. No separate cover tagline was supplied. From here I want Randi alone with Pace again, though I dread how much of this she will hide; as a guess from here, Brooke will keep gathering small facts while Randi tries to turn what happened back into a manageable dating story.
