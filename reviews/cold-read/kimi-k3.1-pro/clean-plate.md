## Reader reaction

I finished this flushed, fond, and a little achy. Vee dressing for Randi “with the same keyed-up gladness she saved for getting dressed for Pace,” skipping dinner so she can arrive pleasantly hungry, and then polishing her plate while Randi eats neatly and leaves the polenta untouched—that contrast got to me before any of the explicit storytelling did. Vee’s appetite is so expansive now: food, praise, sex, being looked at, being loved publicly. Her repeated joy over *boyfriend* still feels earned rather than naïve. Pace remains offstage, but the man in her account—the wrist he “parked” at, the individual kisses for her toes, the unembarrassed *Okay* when she calls his wet kiss gross—makes me trust him more, not less. He doesn’t argue with her recoil or punish her for it; he also doesn’t pretend not to like what he likes.

The dinner itself was seriously hot. Not because the stories were explicit, though they were, but because Randi keeps refusing to let Vee file them down into amusing anecdotes. “Did you take it all?” and “Don’t you *dare*… Give me the real one—the harder one” turn remembering into something happening live between them. Vee is squirming, unable to say aloud what she proudly did, and Randi is completely attentive. Then Vee sets up her little comic closer—“Who would ever actually *want* something that gross?”—and Randi answers, “I do.” That was the peak for me. Quiet, unsmiling, no escape hatch. “It was powerful. It turned me on” made Vee clench in the booth, and I felt it too. Her subsequent “But it’s gross,” much too loud, is pure shame trying to recover territory her body has already surrendered.

Randi’s “How does he act?” is such a good question, and such a tender piece of guidance. She moves Vee away from abstract decency and toward the actual man’s actual pleasure. Vee’s unvetted answer—“He likes it. Actually—he’s enthusiastic”—becoming the sentence that follows her home felt like the real conclusion of the chapter. Nothing is announced or resolved; she simply lets it keep walking with her. I loved that. And yet I don’t read Randi here as only a wise, selfless guide. She is delighted and generous, but she also wants these details with an intensity “without a scrap of mercy.” Knowing what I know—that she loves Vee, that she is also Pace’s lover, that she helped arrange Vee’s selection—I felt both sympathy and unease. She gets to feast on intimacy she cannot openly claim.

“You picked well” especially landed with a little sting. Vee notices only the small technicality: Pace approached her, so she didn’t exactly pick him. I know the much larger concealment underneath the compliment. That doesn’t make Randi’s affection feel false; it makes the affection crowded. Her hunger, pride, restraint, and pleasure in Vee are all sitting at the same table. I also couldn’t stop thinking about her untouched polenta while Vee scraped her plate clean. I don’t want to make that too neat, but the page kept putting their appetites beside each other.

The goodbye undid me in a quieter way. Vee kisses Randi’s neck again “before any of her had convened on the question,” and this time Randi’s body gives “a catch and then a rise,” the rise lasting a beat too long before *ticklish* arrives to cover it. Vee is pleased with herself and lets the explanation stand; I very much did not. I’m not ready to declare what either woman will ultimately call this, but the charge between them is no longer something I can regard as incidental. Vee’s strange after-feeling—“she’d handed the morning over and was still holding it”—was just as telling. The story has been given away, but whatever it woke up has not.

**A Clean Plate** works literally and then keeps working: Vee’s cleaned plate, the “clean” version of the story that can cross a white cloth, and the impossibility of leaving the evening emotionally spotless. There is still an aftertaste. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK THREE — A PLACE AT THE TABLE** now feels strongly centered on who gets fed, who gets a seat, and whether intimacy can remain safe when everyone is concealing a different hunger; the long spoon suggests closeness with a protective distance still built into it.
