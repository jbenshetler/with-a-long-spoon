## Reader reaction

The opening went straight into my body: the sour, careful stillness of the hangover, and then the almost embarrassing tenderness of being looked after. The water placed where she would find it first, down to the folded paper towel protecting the wood; the melted ice; the warm cloth; the two pills; his undershirt swallowed around her; above all, “his mouth, once, on her forehead, in the dark, after he thought she was already gone.” I melted. Nothing about his care needed an audience, and nothing sexual was taken from her helplessness. My trust in Pace deepened here. Even the loaf cooling on the rack undid me a little — “You made *bread*” is exactly the right incredulous response. This was domestic swoon rather than erotic swoon, and it worked completely.

I also loved Vee’s helpless, sober repetition of “I like her so much,” followed by the more startled “She *liked* me.” Sheri’s appreciative inspection remains warmly charged in memory, and Vee can now enjoy having been enjoyed without immediately turning it into shame or rivalry. That felt like movement. Her triumphant “I don’t have to be jealous” holding “from every side” made me happy for her, though I remember how quickly the heat survived the death of her imaginary blonde, so I’m not going to nail down her feelings more firmly than she does. Randi is absent but vividly present as the person Vee wants to tell — she is already anticipating Randi being insufferable about having been right, and gladly handing her the win. That affection made me smile. I have no suspicion of Sheri, and none of Pace’s behavior during the night.

Then “My one friend. There isn’t anyone else” landed with a genuine thud. It recolored the roadhouse for me immediately: all that apparently effortless social fluency had never meant belonging. Pace can give Danny, Theo, Meg, Kayla, and Cassie a better evening without having anywhere ordinary to put his own Christmas, his own family, his own heartbreak. I did slightly resist Vee’s instantaneous embellishment of “Women misunderstand it” into proof that women necessarily fall for his extraordinary attention; that is her flattering completion, not something he actually spells out. Likewise, “Men make friends by competing” reads to me as Pace’s long-handled explanation, not a universal law. But the desolation underneath both explanations convinced me. “Either weren’t safe or weren’t welcoming,” laid beside “not home since he was sixteen,” gave me real unease — not suspicion of him now, but dread about the child he had been.

The Daphne passage hurt most. Vee trying to hold Pace’s “best two years” beside “the wrong shape it had actually been,” without forcing either truth to erase the other, felt emotionally honest. And the realization that when Daphne left there was no one “to take the heartbreak to” was the detail that finally broke me. Vee’s abundance of people — fries taken by Kayla, clothes borrowed from Meg, Cassie hearing the unfinished sentence, Randi doing triage — makes his deprivation legible without turning it into a simple comparison of who has more. The hug was beautiful: she crosses the counter she had wanted between them, gives him the same whole-weight embrace Sheri had given him, and does not ask him to receive pity. His delayed arms and his being “somewhere else with it” kept the moment open. He is not suddenly healed, and I’m glad the chapter doesn’t make her embrace do that.

My one friction is that the middle explains its ache longer than I needed. By “Not one. Not ever” I was already there, and Vee’s reconstruction of entire empty semesters and summers sometimes felt like her laying the loneliness out very thoroughly for herself. I didn’t disbelieve it; I just felt the repetition. “Hangover,” though, is exactly right: literal first, then emotional, the recognition arriving “late, all at once, and more than she’d counted,” just like the cider. **WITH A LONG SPOON** still suggests to me the dangerous distance involved in sharing appetite and intimacy; **BOOK THREE — A PLACE AT THE TABLE** became painfully literal here. Pace can prepare the table and make everyone else comfortable at it while lacking an ordinary place of his own. No cover tagline was supplied.

Next I want Tuesday’s retelling to Randi, including the dangerous, delicious little fact of Sheri’s look, and I want the Valentine’s dancing. My dread — a guess from here — is that Vee may try to become the answer to every empty place in Pace’s life. And because I have just watched Randi return to his bed without Vee knowing, this chapter’s complete trust carries an extra ache for me: the closer and safer she feels, the worse that concealed knowledge sits with me.
