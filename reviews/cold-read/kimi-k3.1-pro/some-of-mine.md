## Reader reaction

This tiny chapter landed harder than I expected. After the dancer floor, the frantic sex with Pace, and Vee spending days outrunning her own wanting, everything narrows to two girls still wearing their coats and a question “like a third person nobody had offered a chair.” I felt immediate tenderness for Vee, but also a flash of frustration: when she cannot answer Cassie, she reaches for “You don’t like her,” an unfair little attack designed to make the room about Cassie’s supposed prejudice instead of Vee’s desire. It did not make me think less of her, exactly. It made her panic painfully recognizable. She would rather start an old, unwinnable argument than remain for one more second inside the new question.

Cassie completely won me here. “Is this what you want?” is such a different question from *what happened* or *what is Randi to you*—no demand for a label, no prosecution, just whether Vee can locate her own wanting. And Vee actually tries. That was the saddest part: “she actually went and looked,” found the edge of something large, and retreated. Her defensiveness is not a performance for Cassie alone; she cannot yet bear to know the answer privately. My feeling for Cassie deepened from steady affection into real trust. She steps over the bait, keeps whatever judgment she has to herself, and notices the practical truth: “You didn’t have dinner.”

The offer of Cocoa Pebbles genuinely melted me. There is no sexual swoon in this room, but there is a powerful emotional pull in being given a question small enough to answer. Vee’s “Yeah” comes “fast and whole,” and I felt the relief of it in my own chest. Cassie does not force the confession or punish the evasion; she feeds her. The erotic charge from Randi remains only as the live place on Vee’s neck and the devastating “Her neck said yes,” before the scene deliberately lets that charge recede beneath care and hunger. It did not go slack to me—it changed register.

I have no suspicion of Cassie. Her worry reads as concern, and her restraint is entirely consistent with the person who has watched Vee closely without demanding repayment for it. Randi is offstage, so this chapter gives me no new evidence about her motives; all the uncertainty remains inside Vee rather than becoming a fresh accusation against Randi. Pace is likewise untouched here. I had no real friction with the prose or the length. The density of thresholds, floors, burns, and stains suits Vee’s inability to get solid footing, and the cereal ending feels earned rather than cute. She can accept nourishment without yet being able to accept knowledge.

The title **Some of Mine** becomes literal with “Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles?”, but it also gently recolors the whole chapter: Cassie offers something of hers without trying to take possession of Vee. Against Randi’s recent language of getting to *have* her, that matters to me. The title did not give anything away beforehand; it clicked into place at the end. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK THREE — A PLACE AT THE TABLE** continues to promise appetite, feeding, sharing, and belonging, and here a place at the table is created with nothing grander than a cereal box. No cover tagline was supplied.

Next I both want and dread Saturday brunch with Randi. My guess from here is that Vee will keep constructing small, manageable permissions for herself—brunch, friendship, another harmless goodbye—while refusing the larger answer Cassie asked for. I also hope Cassie remains exactly this steady: not silent forever, but not willing to rip the answer out of her.
