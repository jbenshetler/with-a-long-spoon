## Reader reaction

This chapter felt like coming up for air, even though its most beautiful passage takes Vee down under the surface. After *Tannin*, where I had to watch her body become material for Randi and Pace’s shared fantasy, having Vee returned to her own senses was an enormous relief. The bleacher comments didn’t arouse me; they made me brace. Her controlled walk, the old suit with “nowhere to hide,” and then that green silence below the water all landed physically. “Nobody down here wanted anything” is the line that ached most. Of course she stays a beat past her lungs asking. Underwater she isn’t Randi’s discovery, Pace’s prospective date, or a body being graded from the bleachers. She is simply capable, weightless, and at home—and the small grief in that belonging made me much more tender toward her.

Cassie completely won me here. Her flatness in the earlier chapters had already made me trust her perceptions, but this showed the active devotion underneath it. She keeps “Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman” for Vee rather than performing outrage at the men, and she answers Vee’s sadness without demanding that Vee explain or enlarge it. The precisely engineered splash, followed by Cassie taking the return wave full in the chest without changing expression, was the chapter’s great swoon for me—not romantic, but deeply loving. Vee’s “ugly” shriek-laugh felt precious. It recalled the whole-body laugh that first caught Pace and Randi on the quad, except this time her unguardedness belongs to her and her best friend, not to anyone hunting her.

Pace becomes more attractive through Vee’s memories without becoming entirely safe to me. I understand the pull exactly: he helped “gently, and just enough,” listened until her research sounded real to her, and held the froyo door with his back. Even her need to insist that he isn’t gorgeous feels truthful; what draws her is his oddness, his economy, his attention. “Don’t answer tonight” leaves the yes alive in her mouth, and I felt that suspense with her. But I also know what she doesn’t: she has already been discussed as someone “for us.” I don’t read malice into this chapter, and nothing here makes me think Cassie is right or wrong about him—Cassie doesn’t appear in his remembered courtship. The unease is simply that Vee may be consenting to a lovely dinner while knowing only part of the invitation.

The erotic charge is slight but real, and it arrives exactly where it should: not with her body exposed to strangers, but with Vee alone in a towel remembering “His hands, the knuckles thick on them” and wondering again what he does with them besides math. Her getting into her sweats “pretty fast after that” made me smile. The water itself is sensuous—cold, green, pressureless—but its pleasure is release rather than desire. I had no real reader friction.
