## Reader reaction

This hurt in the clean, breath-stopping way I’d been bracing for without knowing the exact shape it would take. From the first sentence, all the things that usually melt me—the truck in the drive, the low lamplight, the heat raised hours before her arrival—had turned into evidence against the life Vee wants. “It went through her now, and she hated that it did” caught the whole chapter for me: his care still reaches her body before her resolve can stop it. I felt proud of her for making herself stay on the mat, hands in her pockets, coat buttoned, bag still in the car. She isn’t rejecting the goodness of what happens inside the house. She says, explicitly, “I am not complaining about one minute of it.” That made her demand much harder to dismiss than a simple accusation would have been. She loves it. That is precisely why the absence of a name has become unbearable.

My feelings about Pace moved, but not into dislike. When his face opens and he comes for the customary kiss, I still feel the familiar tenderness in him; when he offers chicken, warmth, the clear porch sky, he is sincerely offering her the best evening he knows how to make. And “You’re the most important person in my life” landed on me with real force before Vee’s immediate “But what am I?” exposed its limits. Importance is a feeling; she is asking for a place that can exist in speech, among other people. His answer—“Do any of your friends have a relationship you’d rather have than this one?”—made me genuinely angry. It is another lovingly prepared dish set down beside the hunger she actually described. I believe his stillness and folded arms cost him something, and I felt sorry for the man reaching for something he cannot find “anywhere in himself.” But sympathy did not make me want Vee to accept less.

The title, **“Believe Me,”** cuts both ways. Pace says, “Words are cheap. Believe what I do,” and the terrible thing is that Vee already does believe his actions. She has believed the warmed house, the fitted dress, the drawer, the food, the patience. Belief is not what she lacks. “Words are cheap, and you still won’t spend them on me” is the line that broke the chapter open: if words cost so little, their continued withholding becomes impossible to excuse as mere humility about language. Then the final reach of his hand—”Not the hand in place of the word”—was devastating. His care is real, and in that instant it is also the wrong answer.

There was no swoon for me here, only the ache of wanting Vee to receive the one small thing that would let her keep everything else. The erotic charge went completely cold, appropriately; even her reference to “the best of my life” functions as testimony to what she stands to lose, not as titillation. I didn’t feel bored or manipulated by the writing. The repeated question—“What am I to you?”—could have become circular, but each return strips away another possible refuge: plans, comparisons, deeds, silence. His “Vee” at the end especially hurt because her name is tender in his mouth and still not the name she came for.

The cover titles **WITH A LONG SPOON** and **BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** feel newly bitter to me. This chapter is literally a warm reception prepared with exquisite care, yet Vee leaves colder than when she arrived. Feeding, measuring, warming, and giving in carefully chosen portions have been the language of their intimacy; now she is asking whether that language can cross the threshold into the rest of her life. No cover tagline was supplied. From here, I dread her getting on the morning flight with nothing settled and having to face her family while this relationship still sounds ugly when forced into ordinary speech. My guess is that she will not simply pretend this confrontation never happened. Whether Pace can follow her with words, rather than another act of care, is what I most need to read next.
