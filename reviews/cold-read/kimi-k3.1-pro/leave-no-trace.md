## Reader reaction

This chapter melted me. Not just the cab — though the cab certainly got me — but the whole slow accumulation of being cared for without being managed. Pace walking “at the speed of her not knowing how,” fixing the boot and then apologizing for not having asked about it sooner, watching her face rather than the view and then giving her back the view: I felt myself trusting him more with each of those choices. And Vee getting up the mountain on her own legs mattered as much to me as anything sexual. Her body isn’t only something being looked at here; it carries her somewhere astonishing. The summit’s indifference — “it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it” — gave her a brief release from the constant social scrutiny she lives under, and I felt that spaciousness with her.

The food scenes continue to be disproportionately intimate for me. The sandwich, the shared bottle, the cherries she wants before she’s finished the first one: appetite is becoming the cleanest language Vee has for wanting without immediately punishing herself for it. Her mortification over spitting the pit hurt a little, especially the swift internal word *common*, but Pace setting his own pit beside hers without watching her learn was exquisitely gentle. “He hadn’t made her small to give it to her” could describe nearly everything he does in this chapter. Even “That sounds like a beautiful dress” lands because he neither rescues her with an offer nor lectures her about the black dress. He lets the wanting exist.

And then the cab. God, the cab. Her wanting him inside with her turning into the slower, more dangerous admission — “I want him to see me” — was the erotic peak for me, well before the buttons. The stripping works because she is doing it: calling him back, holding his eyes, choosing to look down so his eyes will follow, offering him what her mother’s voice insists must disgrace her. “The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice” is exactly right; the shame doesn’t sit outside the desire as a moral warning, it runs through it and intensifies it. I found that genuinely hot, not distressing, because the page keeps her want unmistakable. His glad stillness is the answer she needs. He doesn’t pounce, praise her bravery, or turn her boldness into his entitlement. He simply receives her. Then her discovery that his hand is shaking with cold turns the whole thing tender again: the seductress becomes the woman warming him against her belly. That reversal may be the loveliest moment in the chapter.

I have no new suspicion of Pace from this chapter itself. His restraint feels consistent and materially demonstrated, not merely announced. The wider situation with Randi remains an ache in the background — especially because *ravish* arrives in Vee’s imagination through Randi, and Randi has her own designs on this same unfolding intimacy — but this chapter gave me no reason to contaminate Vee and Pace’s day with fresh distrust. Cassie, meanwhile, remains such a steady pleasure. “I had your blood type ready” made me laugh, and her quiet “Vee,” “like setting something down where it wouldn’t break,” told me she understands that this is more than a funny dating story. I loved that she hears the shirt-off account without shaming her friend.

My only small friction was the campus cop’s arrival. The siren snatching away Vee’s sentence is effective — I felt the abrupt bodily flight after all that stillness — but it also flirts with romantic-comedy interruption just when I wanted one more breath in the cab’s aftermath. Still, the image it leaves is strong: Vee under the overhang in his flannel, moleskin “holding,” both garments and small acts of care staying with her after he’s gone. The chapter title begins literally, with “We take nothing and leave nothing” and the cherry pits, then turns quietly ironic. He leaves no trash on the mountain, but the day leaves traces everywhere: foam on her heel, his shirt on her body, a new knowledge of her own courage and wanting. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** feels, from here, like a promise about appetite and the manners used to approach it: who is invited to want, who serves, who chooses, and what civility can conceal. What I want next is simply more of Vee discovering that she can act on desire rather than only be desired — and I dread how exposed that discovery may leave her.
