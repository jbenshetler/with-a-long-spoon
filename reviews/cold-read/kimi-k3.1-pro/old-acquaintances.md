## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt in a quiet, almost airless way. I wanted to climb into that too-small bed and put my arms around Vee. The room loving “Vivienne” while having “no idea who was in the bed” caught me immediately: she has outgrown not just the house but the girl everyone there knows how to love. Her mother’s food is love and still not what she hungered for; her father’s daily question about her “young man” strikes the very place she cannot answer. Nothing here is cruel, and that makes the suffocation sadder. She is safe, cared for, and completely unseen.

The cruellest part was knowing what Randi’s fairytale actually contained. Vee imagines a white-tie crush, a room chanting Randi’s name, perhaps a devastating billionaire, while I have just left Randi repairing her face beside a sleeping man who never found her. When Vee thinks Randi took time out of something wonderful to answer her, the tenderness is real and the picture is utterly wrong. I ached for both of them. Vee’s message—“i’m here if you want to talk. or not.”—is such an open hand, especially because she is still protecting Randi over the loan discovery without asking for anything back. And Randi’s *miss your face most though* is the one true line inside her performance. Their attachment feels more mutually sustaining than anything either has elsewhere, even with everything Vee does not know.

My frustration with Pace remains, but this chapter did not turn him into a villain for me. Vee’s jealousy has no particular evidence behind it: the blonde is explicitly someone she builds “from spite,” and the Christmas cooking she pictures is speculation. Yet her underlying panic is horribly understandable. He left her the room, as he always does, and now that respect feels like abandonment. The sentence “He could be anyone’s, anywhere, and she had no standing to so much as ask” carried extra dread because I know how much of his intimate life is genuinely hidden from her, even though her chosen suspect is imaginary. I do not suspect him of spending Christmas with some dance-hall girl; I do fear that his silence and insistence on leaving room may let this rupture harden.

The sensual memory of the first night briefly glowed—the top sheet slipping, the garlic and pepper blooming, nothing needing to be said—and then it was stolen from inside her own mind. That was more painful to me than arousing. Food, warmth, sex, and being chosen had all been one seamless experience; now she cannot remember it without installing another woman at the table. The final imagined embrace was the chapter’s strongest pull: his arms arrive vividly enough to comfort her and immediately become arms around somebody else. Wanting and fear share one body here.

“Old Acquaintances” landed through the New Year echo of old selves and absent people: school friends who no longer text, trophies, the clarinet, the Vivienne the room remembers. Nobody literally walks back into her life; the old acquaintance is the girl she used to be, no longer recognizable to the woman in the bed. **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** now feels pointedly ironic beside all these cold bedrooms and messages that do not come, while “warm reception” also evokes the warmth Pace prepares and Randi cannot generate. The cover supplies no tagline. Next I want Vee and Pace actually to speak, not simply be fed or touched back into temporary ease. I dread her returning to Randi as the one certain person and confiding this wound without knowing Randi’s place in it.

One small friction: the abrupt slides into “you”—“you burrowed under the blankets,” “He left you the room”—pulled me out of the otherwise close narration. The midnight spiral is long, and the blonde fantasy repeats itself, but the repetition mostly felt true to sleepless rumination rather than padding.
