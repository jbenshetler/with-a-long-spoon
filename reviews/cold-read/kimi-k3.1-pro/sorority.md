## Reader reaction

Vee’s happiness carried me straight through this one. I loved her arriving underslept and “overfull,” wanting both her girls in one room, and I especially loved the distinction between how Cassie and Randi receive her. Cassie doesn’t care about the dress; she watches Vee because “the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see.” Her quiet “Was it a good night?” and the way something eases at Vee’s second, fuller “Oh, yes” felt profoundly tender. I trust Cassie completely. She isn’t competing for a share of the story or trying to extract the juicy middle. She just needs to know her friend is happy.

Randi is much harder for me to hold steadily, because parts of this chapter genuinely delighted me. The ruined-shoe contest was wonderful: the pink rhinestone platforms, the canoe-sized shoe, the disintegrating flat “one brisk walk from becoming a sandal.” Vee feeling like “the funniest woman in Virginia” while Randi gives her the laugh that costs something was one of the warmest moments they’ve had. And “He didn’t make this *for* you. He made it *to* you” is exactly right; Randi sometimes understands Vee’s experience with uncanny precision. I can see why Vee loves her. I don’t think the affection is simply counterfeit.

But Vee’s interpretation of Randi’s silence made my chest hurt. She decides Randi might be jealous *of Pace*—of having a man who stays up all night paying attention—and wants that devotion for her. From what I already know, Randi’s stillness reads much more like exclusion, hunger, and jealousy of what Vee and Pace have without her. Vee is being generous in precisely the place where she is most exposed. The boutique continues that imbalance: Vee cannot admit she cannot afford to stand inside the door, and Randi answers with “A dress your *lover* made you?” and a pair of shoes whose rightness makes refusal feel like betraying Pace’s work. I can’t tell how deliberately Randi is steering the money shame, and I don’t need to settle that to feel uneasy. The result is plain: Vee puts the debit card away and uses credit she knows she cannot cover.

The erotic charge is quieter than in the last several chapters, but very much alive. The good soreness in Vee’s thighs and nipples gives “You lucky slut” an extra jolt, and Randi looking at her in the dress has more than friendly admiration in it. The peak for me was Randi holding her foot, noticing the frosted plum, and moving her thumb once across the toenail: “Mm.” Vee grabbing Randi’s shoulder because the room seems to swing was beautifully bodily. Then Randi kneels, buckles her in, and the shoe’s arch supports her “firm as a hand”—an unmistakable echo of Pace’s fitting, with the roles of adoring observer and kneeling attendant sliding between them. I found it intimate, exciting, and faintly alarming all at once.

The two-store structure is long, but I think it earns its length: the laughter and nearly chosen tan heel are necessary for the boutique’s seduction to hurt. I did notice the familiar rhythm of Vee reading prices and doing private arithmetic, but this time it culminates in a consequential choice rather than merely repeating her discomfort. The chapter title, **“Sorority,”** means much more than Randi’s actual house now: this is an initiation into Randi’s rites of dresses, shoes, shared sizes, confidential laughter, and “practically a marriage,” while Cassie offers a different, unceremonious kind of sisterhood. Simpson’s paradox also lingered—each woman’s attention means one thing separately, and something else when placed beside what I already know.

**WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** increasingly promises exquisite manners around something hazardous: care, gifts, and invitations drawing Vee toward appetites and obligations she cannot yet price. No cover tagline was supplied. Next I want the induction itself; I desperately want Vee to have her room-stopping entrance. I also dread the credit-card bill, Randi’s next escalation, and Cassie being slowly repositioned as the less glamorous friend. My guess from here is that the shoes and dress will become part of how Randi draws Vee closer to the encounter she and Pace originally planned, though I hope Vee reaches any such choice with her eyes much more open than they are now.
