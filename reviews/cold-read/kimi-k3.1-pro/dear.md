## Reader reaction

This chapter left me glowing and aching at once. Vee’s joy is so physically convincing—the words spilling out before her coat is off, the spin that nearly takes out the closet door—that I felt it with her. The kiss becomes even more moving in her retelling: “I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.” After watching her spend so much of her life monitoring her body, her appetite, and other people’s responses, that sentence felt like a small emancipation. Pace remains attractive to me through her account: his restraint gave her room to discover her own initiative rather than merely respond to his. But the person I most trusted here was Cassie. Her question about alcohol briefly broke the enchanted mood, yet her immediate acceptance of Vee’s answer, her practical teasing, and “Look at you” all felt like steadfast love rather than suspicion for its own sake.

That made the classroom handoff hurt. Cassie sees Randi already reaching for Vee, folds something away, and leaves; Vee does not watch her go. Then Vee supplies the exclusion herself—The Smoothie Bird “isn’t really her place”—and calls having separate people healthy. It is reasonable on its surface, which is exactly why it hurt. She is beginning to do Randi’s dividing for her. I do not know precisely what Cassie suspects, and I do not need to: her care and her hurt both read clearly. I wanted Vee to turn around, even while understanding completely why she does not. Being chosen by Randi feels, to her, like entering a life with color in it after two years of being “fine.”

My feeling about Randi moved sharply toward unease, though she remains magnetic. Knowing what I know, her pretending to grope for Pace’s name—“Paul?”—and her polished catastrophe about another date are almost painful to watch. Vee offers her something unguarded, and Randi keeps subtly revising it. Vee’s “I made an exception” becomes “Because of him”; her relief at choosing becomes “You let him choose for you… And you liked it.” Then comes “your man,” the ravishing fantasy, and Randi’s low, belated “Ask and then ravish.” That last beat genuinely charged the room for me: Vee is halfway up an imaginary trail, and Randi joins her there. I do not think Randi’s desire is counterfeit. The danger is that real desire and careful management seem able to occupy exactly the same warm smile.

The Brooke encounter was exquisite social dread. On the tall chair with her feet dangling and her pink drink sweating, Vee becomes a child on display just as Brooke’s warmth takes her measure. “We missed you Thursday” opens an entire world behind her, and Vee shrinks herself to “a girl from a stats class” so that nothing about her can reflect badly on Randi. Then Randi takes her hand and says, “Vee’s my dear friend.” I understood the rush completely: she is chosen aloud, in front of the people whose judgment has just made her feel wrong-sized. But I also felt the possessive weight of it, and the fact that Vee notices the charge without understanding its source. Her final “I felt picked” is lovely and devastating because both meanings are available.

“Dear” therefore lands first as tenderness and then as expenditure, possession, perhaps even price: Randi spends status on Vee, and Vee receives the gesture as sanctuary. The volume title, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now feels suspended between sweetness and caution. The recurring drinks, stirring, feeding, and being chosen for make the spoon feel tangible; “A Polite Invitation” recalls Pace’s exquisite manners while making Randi’s friendliness feel like another invitation whose full terms Vee cannot see. No cover tagline was supplied. I want the hike badly—I want Vee’s happiness to remain hers—but I dread her being carried between two people who understand one another while she understands only how wonderful each makes her feel. That is a dread based on what I have already seen, not a prediction about what they will do.
