## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt in the softest, most insidious way: I was completely swept up in Vee being chosen while knowing exactly how deliberately Randi had chosen her. “*There* you are” landed on me almost as warmly as it lands on Vee. So did the squeezed forearm, the lavender latte ordered without fuss, the stool at the high table—the whole experience of a door opening into a brighter life. Walking home “in no hurry at all, warm the whole way there” was the swoon for me. It is such an ordinary, precious happiness that I wanted to protect it immediately, even while enjoying Vee’s enjoyment.

Vee became a person to me here rather than the lavish red-haired answer to Randi’s *yes*. I adore her mixture of longing, perceptiveness, vanity, and self-concealment. She reads every room exquisitely and then refuses the plainest reading of herself. Her mother’s *floozies* arrives just as she looks a beat too long at the barely dressed girls; she knows she has “more than they had, and better than any of them,” yet covers herself to the collarbone and calls it preference. That contradiction felt painfully alive, not hypocritical. And her attraction to Randi is all the more affecting because she files it under admission, friendship, glamour—anything except wanting a woman. “Flat helpless want” told me what she will not yet tell herself.

Randi remains magnetic, and I do not think her pleasure in Vee is necessarily counterfeit. That is what makes this so uneasy. I remember her glowing after the staged collision, her happy footwork, her breathy *yes*. But now I also watch the warmth swing over people “total, undimmed,” watch her turn a coffee invitation into “I’m not asking, I’ve decided,” and watch Vee mistake fluency for intimacy. Vee cannot remember the conversation afterward, only how chosen she felt; that blank transcript frightened me more than overt villainy would have. Her imagining Switzerland or Paris to explain Randi’s voice made me smile and wince, because I have heard the Kentucky underneath. She is falling in love with an atmosphere and supplying a biography to fill it.

Cassie I liked immediately. Her flat “Cassie,” her refusal to perform the expected return volley, and her genuinely unbothered “You go” all felt clean beside Randi’s abundance. “Goodbye, Vee” did catch me, especially against the chapter title, but I do not know what Cassie sensed—or whether she sensed anything beyond an odd social current. Vee smooths the wrongness almost before it registers. I trust Cassie’s directness; I am not turning her into someone who secretly knows the plot.

The erotic charge is quiet but persistent: warm breath during the whispered midterm question, shoulders touching, citrus, the hand between Vee’s shoulder blades, and her looking at other women’s bodies before Randi makes the whole room go quiet. Its peak is not a touch but “the want had nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her*.” I felt both the pull and the deprivation built into it. Pace’s absence intensified rather than cooled things for me; I carry his “For us” into every kindness Vee receives, and that knowledge makes me complicit in a way the previous chapter did not.

“See You Later” did not give anything away, but it beautifully separates the two farewells: Cassie’s finished *goodbye* and Randi’s promised future. To Vee, “see you later” means the queen has already scheduled her into another day; to me, it also means this approach is only beginning. The cover-board title, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now has the café’s spoons ringing through it, everyone “stirring up the sweetness at the bottom.” “A Polite Invitation” feels especially pointed beside an invitation that expressly is not a question. No separate cover tagline was supplied. Next, I expect—only a guess from here—that Randi will keep making each escalation feel like belonging before Vee has time to examine it. I want Vee to discover and enjoy her desire; I dread her being handed a script for it before she knows she has one.
