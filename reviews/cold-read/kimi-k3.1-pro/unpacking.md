## Reader reaction

This chapter felt like coming back into a warm room after all the intensity: not diminished, just able to breathe. Cassie turning “the whole chair” around to give Vee her full attention got me immediately, and the room becoming “a thing switched back on at the wall” was the real swoon for me. Their rhythm is so good—Vee helplessly effusive, Cassie repeating “He salts the persimmons” and “He burned the toast” with just enough flatness to let the joy be ridiculous without mocking it. Her snowshoeing brother-in-law and the Play-Doh cookies made me laugh. I trust Cassie more completely than anyone else around Vee, and this chapter deepened that rather than disturbing it.

But Vee’s account of the weekend gave me a distinct little pang. She did not leave the bag in the car; she deliberately carried nothing. There was no sweater catastrophe behind her nakedness or the burned toast. Watching her invent the shrunken cranberry sweater for Cassie—the person who sees her most cleanly—hurt more than a sexy omission would have. “The record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody” is such a quiet, exposed sentence. It repeats what she did with the induction photograph for her mother: she crops out precisely the part that made her feel most herself. I don’t think that makes her deceitful in some newly sinister way; it makes her loneliness visible. Even in her safest friendship, she still believes some portions of herself need editing before they can be served.

The moment about Cassie’s mother undid me. “My mother says thank you for the card” and “She wants to know when she gets to see you again” arrive in exactly the same dry register as the snowshoes, and Vee’s hand going to her robe collar tells me how enormous it is before she says the tiny, inadequate “Tell her I said hi.” Cassie hearing the size of it and then recapping the highlighter, ending the subject just before it could demand anything, is such tender friendship. I felt more moved there than by any of the food. Vee has somewhere she is wanted that she did not have to seduce, impress, or shrink herself to earn—and she can barely look at it.

Pace remains enormously attractive to me in Vee’s telling, but the chapter does not let me pretend everything was solved by that magnificent reunion. Her “It’s — we’re—” still has no noun to land on. She is happy, secure enough to sleep because “Friday would come,” but the public name she needed in December has not appeared. That kept the warmth from becoming uncomplicated reassurance. There was almost no erotic charge for me here—an afterglow in the invented t-shirt detail and the flannel going faint against her skin, nothing that wanted to ignite—and that felt right. I was warmed rather than aroused.

“Unpacking” works beautifully: Cassie’s duffel is literally half-unpacked, while Vee unpacks the weekend selectively and leaves its most revealing objects at the bottom. The cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK THREE — A PLACE AT THE TABLE**, now seems to promise not just feeding and erotic generosity but the harder question of where Vee can belong with her whole self. Cassie’s mother wanting her back gives “a place at the table” a second, quieter meaning beyond Pace’s table. No cover tagline was supplied. My only slight friction was that the mushrooms, oranges, and persimmons rerun a breakfast I had just read at length, though Cassie’s deadpan responses mostly make the retelling earn itself.

Next I want Tuesday, Anthro, Randi, and the proposed gathering of Vee’s people. I also dread Vee carrying this newly edited version of herself into more rooms. My guess from here is that the reconciliation’s glow will meet everything it postponed: Randi’s concealed investment, Pace’s unfinished “Vee, I l—,” and Vee’s continuing inability to say what they are. For this one night, though, I believe her happiness completely.
