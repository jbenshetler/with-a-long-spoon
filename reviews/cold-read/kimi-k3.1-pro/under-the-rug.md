## Reader reaction

This chapter left me aching more than swooning, though the sex genuinely worked on me. The pain is that Vee is not simply being neglected or deceived in this scene; she is also actively preventing herself from knowing what she knows. Her need is entirely legitimate — she wants to be loved in daylight, named, taken somewhere the world can see them — but the need comes out as an accusation, and then she has to keep accusing because stopping would mean hearing herself. I felt enormous sympathy for her and also wanted to shake her when she said, “You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail,” while some back room of her remembers the humble pie. The cruelest detail is her crossing the room and deciding “that she had forgiven him.” It gives her a warm, generous role to inhabit and lets her avoid the much more frightening possibility that there was nothing to forgive.

Pace deepened considerably for me at Chili’s. The slipping eyes, the fork being squared and squared again, the irritation he tries to hide, and then his shoulders dropping in the truck made his experience painfully legible to me while Vee reads every piece of it backward. I believed him when he said, “In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can’t hear you.” It also recontextualizes the quiet house, the music already playing, the television she has never seen on, and the extraordinary concentration he brings to cooking and sex. I do not read this dinner as evidence that he is ashamed to be seen with her. At the same time, “Where do you want to go tonight?” solves the outing without answering “what we are now.” His care is real; his care is not the whole question. I trust him more here, not less, without feeling that Vee’s larger uncertainty has been resolved.

The erotic charge is strongest when her wish for his control to break is actually answered. The long attention to her breasts and mouth between her legs is familiar Pace, intimate and effective, but “the patience went out of him” is the live wire: his urgency catches hers, and her second orgasm pulls him after her. It feels like mutual wanting rather than one more expert service performed on her body. That makes the aftermath devastating. “She came with his name in her mouth and not the other thing” is the whole chapter in one sentence. Sex can drown the question for a few minutes; it cannot answer it. By the time she lies there feeling every place he has been and thinking, “He’d given her all of it and not the name,” the physical fullness has become almost unbearably lonely.

I did not find new reason to suspect Pace of manipulating this particular evening. The older concealment with Randi still means his private, attentive world is not ethically clean, but this chapter’s immediate tragedy is narrower and sadder: two people can be sincere and still not be telling each other the thing that matters. Vee’s fear that his attention might be “showing her the truth or keeping her from it” feels earned, not paranoid. So does her inability to call him her boyfriend without hearing the word go wrong. My only mild friction was the nacho meditation — “a billboard is to a painting” is a little polished in the middle of such raw confusion — but the dead chip in her mouth landed. His cooking has not merely spoiled chain-restaurant food for her; it has made the old life where “fine had been plenty” impossible to reenter.

“Under the Rug” did not give the chapter away so much as quietly name its mechanism. The love word goes under the rug, the question of public status goes there, the truth about who went cold after the dance goes there, and “I have forgiven him” is another handful of dirt swept under it. Nothing is dealt with; it is only made comfortable enough to walk over. The cover line **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** now feels poised between nourishment and self-protective distance. “A Warm Reception” is exactly what Vee receives bodily and exactly what fails her relationally: his house and arms are warm, while the public restaurant gives her no reception she can use. The series title’s spoon fits all this feeding and awakening, but also the distance Vee senses between herself and the word she wants. No cover tagline was supplied.

I badly want her to ask the plain question once in daylight, without sharpening it first and without letting orgasm carry it away: *Are we together? Do you love me?* I dread that she will instead keep accepting bodily certainty as a substitute and carry this hurt to Randi, whose affirmation feels so good and whose interest is so compromised. That last part is only a guess from here. The pull to continue is strong because the chapter does not make me choose between “he obviously loves her” and “she is obviously being fooled”; it leaves both the tenderness and the not-knowing intact.
