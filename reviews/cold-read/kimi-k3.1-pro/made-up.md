I melted at this one. After the induction gave the dress to a whole room, Vee brings its finished self home to the one person who hadn’t seen it—and then gives him something the room never got. The second walk down the hall, in nothing but the heels, had me holding my breath. What made it so hot wasn’t simply her nakedness; it was that she had conceived the entire scene, ordered him into the recliner, refused his help, and chosen exactly when he could look. “She had something he deserved to see. And something else she wanted him to see” beautifully separates gratitude from desire. Neither is an obligation. She wants this viewing, wants him, and wants to be the author of his seeing.

Vee feels wonderfully changed here without feeling magically cured. She still brings no overnight bag, still refuses to plan openly for staying, but she brings the dress and dumps out her books to make room for makeup. Those little evasions and preparations are so recognizably her. Then she walks into the lamplight and doesn’t cover “one inch” of her blush. I kept waiting for her mother’s *floozie* to arrive, and it didn’t. That silence mattered. The induction walk was a daring performance she could keep secret from the audience; this is more exposed because Pace can see not only her body but her intention. Her hip cocking “on its own” was perfect—the body no longer merely betraying a want she must hide, but joining the performance she has chosen.

Pace continues to work on me through his attention. He turns the lamp brighter, lets her alone behind the bathroom door, sits back down when she raises one hand, and puts his own reaching hand away. That restraint never reads as indifference: she can see exactly what it costs him. “He stayed where he was and let the looking be the touch” was the chapter’s hottest line for me. His sunrise-and-sunset answer skates close to cornball, but her eyebrow saves it, and then he lands the much better distinction: “The other is for the world. This is just for me.” I read that as intimacy, not possession—he understands the difference between a public triumph and a private gift.

Randi is absent but tenderly present in the buckled heels and in everything Vee has learned about standing inside being looked at. He looks astonished, grateful, and safely under her direction.

“Made-Up” first means the careful makeup, then the Halloween invention of two “costumes,” and finally something more poignant: everyone in town is out “being somebody else,” while Vee’s most elaborate costume turns out to be wearing almost nothing and not hiding. There’s also a faint echo of reconciliation in the title, two people made up after a rupture, though the chapter itself is all celebration rather than repair.
