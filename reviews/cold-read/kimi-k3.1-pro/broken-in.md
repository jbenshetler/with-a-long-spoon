## Reader reaction

After the misery of *Lesson*, this chapter felt like being taken in out of the cold. I was relieved almost immediately: Randi sees Vee’s repaired makeup and buttoned coat, says, “Oh, honey. Come up,” and simply makes room for her. Their laughter over Edna Mode released something in me too. I still feel tender toward Pace and angry on his behalf, but Vee’s account—“I took the best thing about him and threw it back at him like it was something to be ashamed of”—made me trust that she understands what she did. Even worse and more honest: “some piece of me was glad.” I loved that she admitted that. Her shame isn’t making her noble; it’s making her look.

Then the shoe fitting turned the whole chapter warm under the skin. The hidden hands beneath the long skirt, the steadying grip on her thigh, the thumb behind her knee, and Vee hoping her breath wasn’t audible—it was exquisitely charged precisely because neither woman names it. And the shoes themselves become erotic: “Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less.” That reaches straight back to Pace’s hand leading her through the dance. Vee responds to being held, directed, relieved of guessing. Here it is Randi providing that safety, and my body understood before Vee did. The final kiss landed just as strongly: she realizes only afterward that she hadn’t braced. “It was only what they did” is not nothing; it is a door quietly becoming ordinary.

My unease lives alongside the swoon, not over it. Randi’s affection feels entirely real to me here, and so does her desire to repair Vee’s romance. But Vee surrenders more than her outfit: “the thing Vee had been holding clenched since last night let go of her, all at once—because Randi had said so.” Randi decides when she goes, what sorrow should look like, what Pace’s silence means, and how she should arrive—“not sorry… *sure*.” Her intimate knowledge of Pace slips through under “If he’s the man you’ve just described,” and I felt the imbalance even while Vee felt rescued. “He doesn’t stand a chance” also turns an apology into a campaign. I don’t suspect Randi of trying to break them up; I suspect Vee is becoming wonderfully, dangerously willing to let Randi author her choices. Brooke’s “you’re the stats friend” and her all-seeing warmth added a smaller chill—the house has a folder on her indeed.

Cassie’s opening *Hrm* hurt more than her absence should have. She sees the shirt, the pretended sleep, the damage, and leaves without forcing the question. Meanwhile Vee folds the flannel into a “totem,” cleans herself up, and takes her trouble to Randi instead. I don’t think Vee is cruel; she is dazzled and relieved, and her little embellishments in the retelling—the bartender setting down water, her insult to Pace becoming still harsher—read as shame polishing the story. But Cassie remains the person watching Vee rather than the production, and I missed her.

The title *broken-in* works beautifully: the supple boots, the shoes that hold rather than hurt, and Vee herself being gradually accustomed to touch, instruction, display, and Randi’s kisses. It makes the chapter feel pleasurable and faintly ominous without deciding which will matter more. The cover’s **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** now feels doubly apt: this is literally a warm reception at Randi’s house, while food, clothing, hospitality, and care keep becoming ways into people. No cover tagline was supplied. I want tomorrow night’s reconciliation badly; I dread Vee arriving as Randi’s composition rather than simply herself. My guess from here is that every successful step with Pace also binds her more tightly to Randi, and that the eventual truth will hurt not because the love was counterfeit, but because so much of it was real.
