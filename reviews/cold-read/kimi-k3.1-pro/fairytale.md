## Reader reaction

This chapter hurt in a very quiet way. For a while I let myself have the fairytale anyway: the invented wife and children in the lift queue, John matching Randi’s skiing without needing to beat her, the blue dress waking up her eyes, his hands crossing over his heart when she comes downstairs. The waltz and the sleigh genuinely swept me up. I understood why she thought, *this is the one.* Then the “good sandwich eaten fast” comparison landed like a tray hitting a counter, and I felt suddenly sad for everyone in that beautiful room. John gives her pleasure, spectacle, ease, even a plausible future, and she still leaves the experience untouched.

Randi breaks my heart here without becoming easier to trust. Her parents’ breakfast is awful in the most polished way: her father’s hug inspires revulsion, her mother is “all edges and points,” and Randi manufactures harmless romantic conquests for their amusement. Her own severely specified breakfast and her mother’s egg-white omelette made the family inheritance feel very clear without anyone announcing it. I also believed her pleasure with John. She is not faking the skiing, the laughter, the dancing, or her choice to go upstairs; she explicitly likes not being asked and lets him take her. I did not feel that John had coerced her. His assumption is part of the seduction for her, which makes the later emptiness more disturbing than if he had simply done something wrong.

The comparison with Pace is wonderfully uncomfortable. Randi decides John’s firm, unmistakable lead is better because she is “done with the work,” then discovers that being carried effortlessly through every step does not mean being found. *Pace on his worst night reached further* is a devastating sentence, especially coming immediately after Vee has walked out because Pace’s reaching still would not become words. It deepens my tenderness toward what Pace can perceive, but it does not erase what he withheld from Vee. Meanwhile Randi prefers John’s assumptions to Pace’s repeated verbal invitations, while Vee is starving for precisely the words Randi experiences as work. Nobody’s want lines up cleanly with anybody else’s.

The mirror nearly undid me. The face coming back “even, exact,” the smile that “meant nothing,” and especially *Nothing had happened to her eyes* brought me straight back to Randi repairing herself after the bench and deciding never to be seen that way again. This time there are no tears to hide because nothing has penetrated far enough to produce them. Her conclusion—*She had put nothing on the table and had won exactly that*—is the bleakest victory I can imagine for her. John does not feel sinister to me; he feels limited, and the text earns that through his sleeping ease and her detachment, not through some concealed flaw. The real unease is Randi’s decision about Vee: *Let Vee arrive at it on her own* sounds like patience, but I know how carefully Randi arranges the conditions under which other people “arrive.” Keeping the word’s cover protects the friendship and preserves the deception at once.

Vee’s text was the warmest thing in the chapter: *i’m here if you want to talk. or not.* No demand, no performance, just a door left open. Randi answering with the fairytale and then adding the one sincere line, *miss your face most though, gorgeous*, made me ache more than any speech could have. I want her to tell Vee plainly, and I completely believe her terror that even Vee’s kindness would permanently change the brunches. I also dread exactly what she chooses: more brunches, more double meanings, more waiting for Vee to cross a distance Randi will not admit she is measuring. My guess from here is that she returns determined to preserve everything as it was, which now seems like one of the most dangerous resolutions she could make.

“Fairytale” did prime me to distrust the glitter, but it did not spoil the fall; it made me notice how deliberately Randi authors the story, down to the imaginary daughter she will someday tell it to. By the end, the title is the cover she sends Vee, not the life she is actually having. The cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**, keeps gathering appetite and hospitality around these emotional hungers; “A Warm Reception” feels especially ironic after this faultless reception leaves her cold and only able to borrow warmth. Pace and Vee gain dimension through contrast without the chapter absolving Pace or reducing Vee to an ideal.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The long, glittering courtship earns its length because I needed to feel its seduction before the emotional drop. The breakfast is efficiently chilly, the ball luxurious without dragging, and the movement from sexual afterglow to emptiness to Vee’s text is beautifully controlled.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A powerful shift outward after Vee’s painful confrontation and walkout, but not a loss of momentum: it reframes warmth, words, consent, and being known while sharpening my dread about what waits when Randi and Vee resume their ordinary rituals.
