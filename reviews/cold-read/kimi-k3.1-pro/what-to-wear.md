## Reader reaction

This chapter made me grin, and then ache while I was still grinning. Vee is so easy here—laughing “with her whole body,” stealing Kayla’s fry, showing off Pace’s doctorate and powerlifting without bothering to hide her pride. After watching her monitor herself so fiercely, seeing her loud and silly between Meg and Kayla felt like being allowed into another part of her life. The “Twelve” absolutely got me: she knows precisely how many days since that kiss without having to count. Her wanting is giddy, physical, slightly embarrassing to her, and completely winning. I melted again at the dry-shirt story, especially because she can hear herself turning it into a legend and does not care.

But I know the story running alongside hers now, and that made the swoon hurt. Vee is truthfully describing a man who asks, waits, notices, and leaves her the decisive movement. She is also building an increasingly exclusive slow-burn romance out of those facts, while I know Pace drove home from that mountain and had sex with Randi—sex fed by details of Vee’s scent and body—and was then left standing hurt in his own doorway. That doesn’t make his tenderness toward Vee read as counterfeit. It makes her “He’s different” both true and dangerously incomplete. Randi never enters this chapter, but she is everywhere in my awareness. I don’t simply hate her; her flight after “I want us” looked like someone overwhelmed by her own desire. Still, I feel much more protective of Vee now. I don’t want this happiness converted into her eventual humiliation.

Meg and Kayla are delightful, and I trust them. Kayla’s unstoppable excitement could easily have become generic meddling-best-friend noise, but her professional outrage over the missing outfit instructions is too specific and funny for that. Meg’s dry “From Meg, that was an event” restraint and her gentle warning—“Everybody’s different at the start”—give the table some ballast without poisoning the mood. Vee lets that warning go by: “It didn’t catch on anything.” That line tightened my stomach more than any overtly sinister detail could. Even Theo’s brief wave adds something valuable: Vee has declined a man kindly before and remained his friend. She is not simply a girl with no romantic history or judgment, waiting to be swept away.

The erotic charge is all anticipation, and it works on me. Its peak is not the kiss recap but Kayla’s remorseless arithmetic: “There’s no meal at two, Vee. He’s not asking you over to eat… He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes.” Suddenly the bare little message has an entire afternoon inside it. Vee pressing her palms along her thighs, rereading the text, letting her heart do “something quick and light and stupid”—that is heat generated by not knowing what will be asked of her and discovering how much she wants to find out. The fact that I know what his house contains, including the bench beneath its ordinary table, charges the invitation further without the chapter having to mention any of it. I have no new suspicion of Pace from the message itself; its brevity is not proof of anything. My unease comes entirely from the information the book has already earned: Vee still does not know that Randi and Pace know each other, let alone that they selected her together.

“What to Wear” is a lovely, deceptively light title. It is literally Kayla’s question, but it also catches Vee at another threshold of self-presentation, after the cardigan over the better shirt, the borrowed date shoes, Meg’s wrong boots, and Pace’s flannel. What version of herself belongs inside his house, when he has given her no instructions? The title didn’t give anything away; it made the final exchange recolor everything before it. The cover board reads **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, with no separate tagline supplied. “A Polite Invitation” now feels almost literal: Pace’s courtly *would you come* opens a door while withholding what lies behind it. “With a Long Spoon” still feels oblique to me, but increasingly like a warning about approaching something desirable without getting as close as desire demands. Next I want Saturday, badly. My guess from here—only a guess—is that the invitation connects to Randi and Pace’s larger plan, but I do not know whether Randi will be there or whether Pace wants Vee privately first. I dread Vee being managed across a threshold she cannot see; I want her to enter wanting, with a real choice still hers.
