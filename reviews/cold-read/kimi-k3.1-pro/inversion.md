This hurt in the best, most infuriating way. The lime candy did it before the hearing ever began: Vee tasting the mixer, holding her leg still against Randi’s, then standing in that hug and feeling guilty for wanting more. Learning that Randi left with “the shape of the girl still on her” turned the whole scene inside out for me. Vee is gratefully telling herself that Randi mercifully refused to let one drunken kiss damage their friendship, while Randi is privately calling *I almost believed it* her worst cowardice: “She had believed every second of it.” I have been wary of Randi for so long that this almost physically changed my posture toward her. I trust the longing. I ache for her. I also don’t feel she has been absolved of anything.

The Standards hearing made me feel cornered on her behalf, and then deliberately refused me the comfort of making her a spotless victim. Brooke’s concern is dreadful because it is so procedurally impeccable: the handwritten folder, the fossil bylaw, *instructor* made to carry an implication everyone knows is false, and finally the offer to make everything “soft” again if Randi will publicly set down “The friend. The gentleman.” Sloane’s scrupulous fairness is nearly as bad as Kinsey’s visibly thrilled inventions. But *It was the room they had built around Lauren Pike, and she had helped* keeps Randi’s suffering from becoming simple martyrdom. She knows this machine because she operated it. Her “No. Thank you. But no.” was still deeply satisfying—not a public declaration, not even an impulsive sacrifice, but a clear-eyed refusal to renounce either person she loves. She pays with Thursdays and keeps her face intact. That is exactly Randi.

Pace surprised me by becoming softer in memory. His walking into Randi’s ring without selling himself, letting the girls land their own jokes, and offering her a kiss that made no claim explains something real about her original attachment to him. I want Randi to tell her; I also cannot forget that Vee still does not know about Randi and Pace, or how deliberately she was chosen. A confession of love would not make that other truth disappear.

My suspicion of Randi has changed shape: I no longer wonder whether her feeling for Vee is only an appetite or a game for Pace, but I still do not trust her instinct to manage everyone rather than risk the truth.
