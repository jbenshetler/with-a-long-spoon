## Reader reaction

This chapter swept me up—and then kept making me aware of exactly *how* it was sweeping me up. The opening sex is gorgeous, especially Randi coming simply because Pace asks whether she would like to taste another woman. Her body answers before she can make the desire respectable, and I felt both the heat and the terror of that. What made me trust Pace most was the explicit distinction: “He knew her body’s answer. He did not take it for her answer.” He waits until they are quiet and she is gathered before asking again. And Randi’s recovery—“Only in a threesome… With you”—is painfully intelligent. She turns an exposed want into a gift she can safely offer him. I don’t think she is simply lying, either; her excitement about doing it with him feels real. The want and the camouflage coexist.

The pointing game itself worked on me much more than I expected. There is something delicious about Randi beginning with offended propriety and quick verdicts, then scanning ahead, wanting him to point, discovering that “I point. You pick” means the taste is hers. Her rejections deepen her: not the expected mirror-image girl, not the inseparable pair, definitely not the commanding blonde whose ease is performed *at* people. Then Vee appears, laughing with her head thrown back, and all of Randi’s discrimination collapses into that bare “Yes.” That was my swoon. Not just Vee’s copper hair and lavish, unapologetic body—though the description is intensely alive—but Randi being unable to stop looking. Her subsequent grin, happy footwork, and daylight version of her bedroom “Yes” made me want her to have this badly.

Pace is harder for me to settle. His patience, tenderness, and pleasure in being surprised by her are attractive. I loved that he follows her “maybe” toward someone warmer and more open rather than merely choosing the woman he most wants. But the whole afternoon is also an exquisitely controlled extraction of something Randi cannot safely admit, and he knows it. The engineered collision is dazzling and unsettling in the same moment: Randi makes Vee apologize for an accident Randi created. That is social competence as predation, however playful the scene feels. Nobody has done anything sexual to Vee, and I have no reason to think she will be coerced—but she is currently outside the private understanding Randi and Pace share. “Do you think she’ll like the bench?” gave me a real chill alongside its charge. Vee has become a person with a name and a stats class to them; she is still an image and an opportunity to us.

Vee herself instantly appealed to me: the whole-body laugh, the physical balance, the clothes that refuse to supply a grading system, the way she is stricken and apologetic after the collision. But I’m aware I know almost none of her yet. Most of what glows here is Randi’s looking and Pace’s fantasy. I want the book to let Vee exceed that, because right now her uncurated freedom is precisely what makes her useful to them. That tension is part of the pull, not an objection I want resolved prematurely.

“The Pointing Game” is exactly right: a childish-sounding name for selection, exposure, and the careful distribution of agency. Pace points; Randi picks; Vee does not yet know there is a game. The volume title, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now feels connected to Pace’s repeated feeding of Randi—the apple last time, the refused cheeseburger, and finally “Let’s go feed you”—while “A Polite Invitation” seems to hover over the approaching invitation to Vee, with politeness itself doing complicated work. No cover tagline was supplied. Next I want an actual conversation with Vee, away from Pace’s interpretive gaze. My guess from here is that the stats class will be Randi’s bridge. I dread Vee being treated as the solution to Randi’s self-division, but I also badly want the possibility of uncomplicated mutual recognition between the women.
