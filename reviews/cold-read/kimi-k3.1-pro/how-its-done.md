The Cassie opening gave me such a clean, affectionate exhale after the intensity of the fitting. Their laughter is easy without being empty: Cassie catches the meaning of the warmed house before Vee can quite say why it matters, and “Oh, Vee” lands like someone genuinely rejoicing in her being cared for. I loved Vee’s indignant “I was on a box,” and then the private reversal beneath all the comedy—“Like I was supposed to be there.” That sentence stayed with me. Cassie’s hand on her knee, given and withdrawn without demanding anything, made their room feel safer than any of Pace’s carefully arranged warmth. My feeling for Cassie deepened considerably: she is teasing, curious, even a little incredulous, but she never makes Vee pay for telling her the truth.

Then Randi’s softer smile actually got me. I wanted it to be real—the slower attention, “*Look* at you,” the sense that Vee is the first good thing in her day—and I still think some real hunger is in it. Vee studying her wrist, jaw, hair, and collarbone is openly sensuous even while she keeps translating it into admiration. The restaurant initially sweeps her up in exactly the way I understand: velvet, bread, leather, being seen beside someone dazzling. But Vee reading prices “the way you read a road in fog,” tucking back her scuffed shoe, and paying with money she will feel missing on Thursday kept the glamour physically uncomfortable. Randi’s abundance is not merely background; it lets her set the terms while Vee works desperately not to seem purchasable or ungrateful.

Randi’s tied-to-the-headboard story did work on me, precisely as the picture works on Vee: not the anonymous man, but immaculate Randi laughing, helpless, undone. The erotic peak for me was that image followed by Vee’s frightened little “Weren’t you scared, though?” and Randi’s answer about finally putting down the reins. It is seductive because it offers Vee something she has just discovered she wants. But the interrogation afterward made me increasingly protective of her. Randi warms her, names her desire before she can name it, praises her body, and turns the bra removal into ordinary sophistication: “it’s just how it’s done.” Then she feeds Vee the next detail each time—“I bet he started at your hip”—so confession feels like agreement rather than disclosure. When Vee blurts “It was cold in the house,” contradicting her own rhapsody about the heat, I felt both the comedy and the trap.

The silence after “You don’t have to tell me” was the chilliest moment in the chapter. It sounds permissive, but after all that coaxing it leaves Vee half-exposed and suddenly alone; Randi sits back, bites her knuckle, and asks for the check. I cannot tell cleanly whether she is jealous of Pace, aroused past her own control, angry that Vee’s experience exceeds what she was meant to have, or some mixture—and I want to leave that unresolved. Her thumb driving beneath her nail at the water glass recalled Randi pressing the bottle cap into her nail bed after Pace truly saw her: again, intensity gets converted into a small hidden injury. I trust her desire for Vee more than I trust her care.

The kiss is brief and devastatingly effective. Randi’s hand at Vee’s jaw gives it just enough deliberateness that I cannot file it under casual continental manners, however hard Vee tries. “See you soon, gorgeous,” the scent caught at her jaw, and Vee touching her own mouth all glow—and then the lit and unlit dorm windows turn that glow instantly into exposure. Her fear of being seen is not proof she did not want the kiss; it is exactly what makes the moment so painful. She has just spent an afternoon learning that being looked at need not mean being found wanting, and Randi’s kiss returns her to scanning for witnesses.

“How It’s Done” is wickedly apt: it names the technical normalization of undressing for measurements, but also Randi’s method of extracting a secret while making Vee feel sophisticated, desired, and safe. The cover’s **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** now feels like a warning about approaching something dangerous through exquisite hospitality: meals, warmth, silk, attention, and invitations whose real cost remains unstated. No separate cover tagline was supplied. From here I want Vee to finish the dress and have her induction, but I dread the next “polite” invitation and what happens if she ever tells Randi no. My guess is that the kiss will make her seek Randi out while insisting nothing changed.
