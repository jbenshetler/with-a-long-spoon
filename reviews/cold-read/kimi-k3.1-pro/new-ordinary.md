This chapter went straight through me. After Randi’s grief in “Four” and the awful stillness around MIRA in “The Outlier,” being alone with Vee and Pace felt like stepping into the warm house with her. I love Vee’s contradiction here: she genuinely needs sleep and study time, yet some “unreasonable corner” wants Pace to fight her for the nights. He simply says, “Take the time you need… I’ll be here,” and his grace hurts more than possessiveness would. That feels exactly like her—learning she can want without being punished, but not yet knowing how to trust wanting that isn’t enforced. Pace remains almost dangerously close to her ideal, but the brief hurt when he thinks she is returning his flannel makes him more human. He is vulnerable to losing her tokens too; he just doesn’t interrogate her about it.

The erotic opening worked completely. Her urgency against his slowness—the jeans stuck around one thigh, the socks, the bra caught so she has to unwind herself out of it—was funny, physical, and hot without becoming choreographed. “There was no dignity anywhere in any of it and she’d have burned the house down for him” made me laugh and feel the wanting at once. The sex then eases deliberately into food rather than going cold; the whole chapter remains sensual, just at a lower flame.

The pizza section could have been merely another display of Pace’s competence, but it held me because Vee understands something important while watching him: “He’d have made the pizza exactly like this for an empty house.” His care is not a seduction performance. And her awakening to separate tastes—“I used to just *eat*”—moves me as much as the sex does. Still, the flannel is the chapter’s romantic peak. Her mortified admission that she wore it every night until “it didn’t smell like anything at all,” and his immediate acceptance—“All weekend… You’ll have it back Sunday”—nearly brought the same foolish tears to me that it brings to her. He puts it on over his existing shirt, standing in his own kitchen in two shirts because she asked. No speech could improve on that. Their new ritual means the nights apart can now have a renewable token instead of a slowly dying one.

Nothing in this chapter gives me a fresh reason to distrust Pace. I still know what Vee does not—that she was selected inside his and Randi’s concealed design—and the green thong landing unnoticed on the long, peculiar table gave that knowledge one quiet, bodily flicker. But the chapter itself earns its warmth. I refuse to pretend the flannel scene is sinister just because I know more than she does. My strongest feeling toward Vee right now is protective tenderness; toward Pace, affection complicated by the secrecy rather than newly aroused suspicion. Randi and Cassie are absent, and their absence lets this feel unusually sealed-off and safe.

“The New Ordinary” fits beautifully without giving anything away beforehand. The ordinary is a drawer that stays stocked, sex followed by dinner, a bag that gets lighter, and a shirt carried back and forth to be refilled with his smell. From here, I want more of this happiness, and I dread the old selection secret and Randi’s newly exposed family wound entering it.
