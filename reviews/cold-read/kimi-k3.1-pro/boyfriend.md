## Reader reaction

This chapter left me aroused, wrung out, and unexpectedly sad. Vee arrives at Pace’s door so urgently that she rings twice, and his sleepy, glad “Vee” before he has even woken enough to wonder why nearly undid me. But she is not simply flying to the man she desires; she is trying to use “the plain male fact of him” to drive Randi out of her head. That made the first sex difficult to enjoy uncomplicatedly. She turns her face into his neck so he cannot see her, comes, finds the picture still there, and starts again. I felt enormous sympathy for her without believing her explanations. “An offering with the wrong name on it” was the line that caught the whole misery: the wax was done for Pace, but bare beneath Randi’s skirt, her body refuses to keep its assigned loyalties.

Then the confession game became fiercely, almost unbearably hot. Pace holding her just off him and making each truer admission buy back an inch was explicit in exactly the way that works on me: the sex is not adjacent to the emotional exposure; it is the instrument of it. He hears every tidy little exit—“We danced bigger,” “I don’t know”—and simply waits until she returns with the thing underneath. I did not read Vee as coerced. She tests the rule, laughs “That’s *cheating*” while “nowhere near offended,” and something in her leans its whole weight on it and finds that it holds. Still, introducing that bargain inside the act gives it a dangerous edge rather than a clean, negotiated feeling. The peak for me was “You finish when you’ve finished,” followed by her finally admitting not merely what Randi did, but “I wanted it.” That is the confession the entire night has been circling, and her orgasm feels like being allowed, at last, to stop lying for several seconds.

My feeling for Pace moved: more attracted to his perception and patience, less able to rest inside them. “That wasn’t you wound up—I know that one. That was angry” is breathtaking attention. He does not punish her for kissing someone else, and his water, blanket, hand in her hair, and lack of jealousy are genuinely tender. Yet “another game, running just as he liked” landed hard because I remember that he and Randi originally chose Vee together and that he has long imagined the three of them. His hunger here is not detached generosity. When she accuses him—“you want Randi”—something flickers, and for once he defends himself instead of leaving her the whole room. Vee calls it “the sweetest thing she had ever seen him do”; I could not join her there. I do not know exactly what he is concealing or intending, and I do not want to settle it prematurely, but the page earned my unease.

Vee’s first outright lie to him hurt more than the kiss. She has just poured out an astonishing amount of truth, then answers “Anything else you want to tell me? About Randi” with “No. Nothing,” burying the goodbye kisses and her private fantasy. “It wasn’t the doing that made you a cheat. It was the keeping of it” felt like her discovering a moral fact she is not ready to live by. Her scramble through *sex brain*, drinks, best friend, *I’m not gay*, and *however much we get, I just want more* was painfully recognizable without becoming tidy self-diagnosis. And Randi, though physically absent, is everywhere: salt and lime still in Vee’s mouth after everything. “She’d gotten away with it” gave me no relief at all, especially followed by Tuesday sitting at the end of the dark.

The title **“Boyfriend”** is exact and quietly ironic. It names the public word Pace finally claimed among Vee’s friends, but here it becomes “the rail on a stair,” a “plain straight fact” she grips to keep from falling into a less nameable want. She has a boyfriend; the word is true. It simply cannot do all the work she demands of it. The series title **With a Long Spoon** and volume title **A Place at the Table** continue to suggest appetite, feeding, intimacy, and the danger of what one agrees to partake in; after so much nourishment and belonging, I now hear the possible question of whether there is a place for all three of them—and who would be setting that table. No cover tagline was supplied.

Next I both want and dread Tuesday. Vee has two nights to invent “a face for it,” and I do not believe any version she constructs will survive sitting close enough to smell Randi’s perfume. As a guess from here, Pace may encourage rather than resist this current between the women, and Vee may mistake that encouragement for proof that she is safe and normal. What I dread is not her wanting Randi; it is her confession becoming another means by which other people get to arrange her wanting for her.
