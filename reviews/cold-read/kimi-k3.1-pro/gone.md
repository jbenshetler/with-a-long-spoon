## Reader reaction

This chapter undid some of the ache of “Portion” without pretending it had never happened. Randi stretched out on Pace, warm and loose and talking too freely to be managing every word, felt like being allowed back into a room she had locked. Her delight in Vee is what opens her: the bigfoot story, Vee seeing the older couple and nearly combusting, and then that unguarded little admission, “That was kind of hot.” I believed her when she said, “She got past me before I could help it.” It made me soften toward her enormously. She is not merely collecting confessions or steering Vee toward Pace; she is saving up stupid things to tell her just to hear her laugh. That is such an ordinary, helpless kind of tenderness, and coming from Randi it feels enormous.

Pace made me ache too, especially when he answered Randi’s “You do nice work” with a simple “Thank you.” There is something lovely about them being able to admire what he has helped Vee become without immediately turning it into strategy. His certainty that “Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body” lands as care, not conquest. But the chapter does not let me settle into uncomplicated warmth. When he says Vee makes a whole room brighter while believing herself too much, Randi goes still in “a different one, the kind that takes holding.” I felt that tiny tightening all the way down. Is she hearing how completely he sees Vee, or noticing that he has never spoken about her that way? The page leaves it ajar, and then she finds him hard and smiles. The sex follows so smoothly that I cannot tell whether it carries the hurt away or covers it. I do not think her affection is false; I just do not trust the stillness to be nothing.

The erotic charge is strong because the bodies keep doubling in Pace’s mind: Randi’s breasts dragging over his chest become Randi over Vee, black hair falling into red, and then abruptly Vee alone in his kitchen, wrapped in his sheet and watching him cook. That last image is almost more intimate than the threesome fantasy. The actual sex is brief but hot—Randi setting the pace, coming apart above him, “the blue on her toes the last composed thing left of her.” That detail got me. Composure is always the last thing Randi surrenders, and here even her pedicure outlasts her face.

The real romantic peak, though, is the kitchen. Randi asks for food, puts on his undershirt, curls herself into the chair, listens to him talk about Chicago, touches his wrist, and eats everything. After the untouched bowl in “Portion,” the clean plate felt almost shocking. I caught myself smiling when Pace did. Her “No. Thank you” to a second helping keeps this from becoming a miraculous cure, and I appreciated that; she has eaten one full plate, not renounced a lifetime of rationing herself. Then she goes back to bed with him and sleeps. After ch18’s turned back, this is a genuine turn toward him, though not necessarily a resolution. “Gone” first points to Vee being “so gone over” Pace, but by the end it also describes Randi: gone past her own defenses, at least for one night.

There was no real friction for me. The chapter earns its quiet length because the food, clothing, wine, and sleep are the emotional action. The cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now feels increasingly pointed: feeding and being fed are everywhere, while “invitation” sits uneasily beside how much Vee still does not know. This chapter’s invitation is simply “Will you make me something?”—and Randi accepting the answer feels as intimate as any sexual offer. No cover tagline was supplied. From here I want Vee and Randi in the same room again, and I dread the moment Vee’s happiness forces the concealed arrangement into the open. I also want Randi to say plainly what that stillness was, though I do not expect her to.
