## Reader reaction

This chapter melted me. Not cautiously, not with an asterisk — I felt Vee’s grin at the phone, the ridiculous happiness of being met in the lobby, and the almost painful pleasure of watching a man’s care reveal itself in shaved skin, polished old boots, and the fact that he is *already there*. Pace’s restraint is deeply attractive to me here: “May I choose?” is such a small question, and yet the whole date seems organized around giving things back to Vee rather than taking them from her. He chooses only after she hands him the choice; he answers her first kiss with no more than she offers; he leaves the last inch. When she says, “But…I’m making an exception,” I wanted her to have him. The slow second kiss, with both of them smiling inside it, is where the chapter landed sweetest and hottest on me.

The meal is genuinely erotic for me, not merely atmospheric. Vee watching his right hand, losing what he is saying when he licks the drip from it, tearing bread as he does, tasting things she cannot name — her attention has become sensual before she has had to label it that way. And the hunger writing is devastating: the hollow that “had been empty a lot longer than since noon” gathers up her mother’s food policing, her constant self-surveillance, and every other appetite she keeps covered. What undid me most was that she stops watching herself eat and nothing happens. Nobody grades her. Pace does not even turn her freedom into a compliment. She simply eats, listens, drifts, and rests. After the froyo cup that got away from her and Pace’s thin apple slices for Randi, feeding and being permitted an appetite are becoming a powerful current through the book.

My feelings are necessarily divided by what Vee does not know. Inside this scene, I have no reason to doubt Pace’s tenderness; it feels patient, specific, and real. But I remember him and Randi selecting her, Randi’s fantasies of offering her body to him, and his own picture of Vee’s mouth at Randi’s breast. So Cassie’s “That’s not a boy. That’s a man” made me smile and ache at once: Vee is not wrong about the man in front of her, and still she is walking into a much fuller design than she can see. My unease is not that the date is secretly cold or that his consent practices are performative — the page does not show me that. It is that Vee’s honest first love-story feelings are forming inside an omission. I feel fiercely protective of her. Pace remains compelling, and more lovable here than in his calculating moments, but I cannot clean him up into simply the perfect boyfriend.

Vee herself deepens beautifully. Her Carole King answer, her “beautiful nonsense” at the menu, and her half-meant rule about not kissing a man she has known for days are all so alive: she is funny, principled, inexperienced with this kind of ease, and repeatedly braver than her reflexes. Cassie continues to be a person I trust completely — delighted for her, attentive, and never making Vee’s vulnerability smaller. Christine’s brief recognition of Pace also matters to me; his kindness has a life outside Vee’s enchanted perception. My only slight friction is that the prose insists several times over that Vee has stopped performing and finds listening restful. The experience itself is so well rendered that “It was the most restful thing she had felt in as long as she could remember” barely needs the reinforcement, though the repetition did not break my spell.

The chapter title now feels like the hinge of the whole evening: “May I choose?” is Pace asking to order, but also Vee discovering that choosing can be offered, accepted, withheld, revised, and reclaimed. She lets him choose dinner; she chooses the exception; she chooses both kisses. *WITH A LONG SPOON* still has no definite meaning for me, though the shared food and the mixture of intimacy with distance make it feel suggestive rather than arbitrary. *BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* now resonates strongly with Pace’s recurrent “May I,” but I also hear the larger invitation Vee cannot yet see. No cover tagline was supplied. Next I want more of this happiness, almost greedily, and I dread the moment Randi steps out from behind it. My guess from here is only that Vee’s feelings for Pace are going to make the eventual three-person reality much harder to sort than either Pace or Randi currently imagines.
