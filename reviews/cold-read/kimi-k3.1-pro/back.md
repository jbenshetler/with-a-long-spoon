## Reader reaction

This chapter loosened something in me that even the glorious reunion sex hadn’t quite reached. “I want to meet your people” is such a simple offer, and it landed like gold. Vee had asked what she was to him *out there*, beyond his warmed house and shut door; now Pace is voluntarily stepping toward her friends, her ordinary life, the people who knew her before him. He still has not said *love* or supplied a neat name for them, and I don’t think this erases that wound. But it is a substantial answer in his own language, not merely more private tenderness standing in for one.

The remembering was what really made me melt. Meg’s boots were a half-sentence from a difficult October hike, yet Pace retained not only their owner but “Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off.” Then he returns Vee’s own description of Kayla to her: “talk the paint off a wall.” All those evenings she spent talking at his back really had been heard. And “Bring Theo,” with no jealous interrogation after she nervously overexplains, settled me as much as it settled her. I trust Pace more as a partner after this. His kindness is not only food, heat, and sexual patience; it is attention with a memory attached.

I also loved Vee’s attempt to design an evening where nobody has to “be a good sport.” Her inability to imagine Cassie at Randi’s real-napkins restaurant, or Randi comfortably at Cassie’s kind of place, felt observant rather than snobbish. She loves both women and sees the constraints each would politely endure. Pace’s “Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one” opens her little fantasy dinner into a gathering where everyone can belong. Their banter about the lavender sweater and his deadpan “Let me handle it” made the reconciliation feel lived-in rather than merely sexually repaired.

The ache, of course, is that Vee pictures Randi and Pace “finally getting to find out about each other” when I know how much is already hidden between them. That gave her happiness a precarious edge without making Pace’s offer itself feel false to me. He could have asked only for Randi; instead he adds Cassie, Meg, Kayla, Danny, and Theo, and thinks about everyone’s comfort. I have no fresh suspicion of him here. Randi’s returns-counter story does show her familiar gift for making manipulation feel like delight—“She let him feel like it was his idea”—but Vee tells it with uncomplicated admiration, and the chapter leaves that admiration intact.

Erotically, this ran as a pleasant afterglow rather than a fresh burn: Vee naked on the lacquered chair Pace made, wine in hand, entirely at home talking while he washes up. The nakedness now signifies ease and belonging more than display. Nothing dragged, though after the length and intensity of the reunion chapters this felt deliberately compact—a breath, and a consequential one.

“Back” works on several gentle levels: Vee is back in the house and their domestic rhythm; she talks to Pace’s back, which makes confession easy; and he finally turns around to promise, “Let me handle it.” The cover board gives *With a Long Spoon · Book Three — A Place at the Table*, with no tagline supplied. *A Place at the Table* feels almost literal now—Vee trying to make a table for all her people—but also emotional: no one seated at the end, no one merely tolerated, everyone offered a place. Next I want to see what venue Pace chooses, especially after Vee quietly remembers how overwhelmed he became at Chili’s. My guess from here is that he will find somewhere unusually well suited to all of them. I’m eager for the gathering, and a little apprehensive about having Vee, Randi, and Pace visibly together among her friends.
