## Reader reaction

This chapter wrapped me up before it started hurting. Pace lifting Vee off the ground at the door, the clothes abandoned inside it, his slowness even after a week apart — I felt the relief and greed of their reunion. But the domestic part swooned me hardest: salmon, warm tomatoes, Vee eating everything, and then discovering “there could be an *after*,” that he might want her again while dinner is still settling. Her private admission — “She loved him. There.” — landed as a real romantic peak, and the barely breathed “Peter” against her sleeping ear made me ache. I believe her happiness. I also felt the exposure of it: she has to assemble his love from “the everything-he-did” because he gives her no words.

The dinner conversation about Randi changed the temperature for me without making the tenderness retroactively false. Vee innocently explains the collision, the friendship, even the supposedly French goodbye kisses, while I know Pace already has the other half of every story. His mild “Sure” when she asks whether he remembers Randi is a direct concealment, and “I’ll pretend it was that kind of French” is playful and arousing on the surface, queasy underneath. He is receiving her confidences while letting her build an innocent version of him. My mistrust of Pace has moved upward: not because his care seems counterfeit, but because genuine care and this sustained performance are occupying the same gestures. Randi, though offstage, remains the person I trust least; Vee’s account of her disappointing boys and European manners is poignant in how thoroughly it misses the woman I have seen.

Vee herself deepened beautifully here. She can say “I do” about liking Randi and “maybe” about loving Pace when the opposite certainties seem to be sitting plainly inside her. Cassie’s flat “Do you love him” catches that evasion without turning it into a lecture, and “You don’t come home school nights” makes Vee count a week she had not noticed passing. I felt real relief that Cassie remains steady and wry rather than jealous or moralizing — “Go to sleep, streetlight” is affection with excellent aim. Vee is not simply being stupid or blind. She is repeatedly converting uncertainties into livable answers: Pace must love her because of how he behaves; Randi’s kisses must be European; her increasing absence from her own room is just the shape of happy weeks.

The bathroom-counter moment was small and wonderfully uncomfortable. Her cosmetics thrown across his immaculate order, his eyes holding on the mess, and then no response at all: I could feel her waiting for permission to exist untidily in his house. His smile may simply mean that he does not mind, and I do not want to decide otherwise for him. But “Had he minded? She couldn’t tell. He hadn’t said. He never said” now sits beside all the larger things he has not said. The long swim then takes those unsaid things into her body. I loved the pool returning as the one place where her body has no opinions and only works. The chapter’s most frighteningly lucid thought is not Cassie’s question but Vee’s own four-stroke wonder: “whether he was that good or she had made him that good.” The water does not answer her, and I appreciated that the chapter does not either.

The erotic charge is strong early on, more from anticipation and being prioritized than from description: her hands already at his shirt, his hard answer to the Randi story, his hand over her breast while the pan hisses. It cools naturally into love, unease, and the meditative swim; I did not want the latter half to force the heat back up. Nothing seriously dragged, though the repeated turns and counts nearly become hypnotic to the point of stasis — which is also exactly their use.

“Not in Your Bed” immediately reads to me as Vee’s empty dorm bed and the quiet speed with which Pace’s bed has become her default. It also makes Cassie’s gentle observation feel larger than sleeping arrangements: Vee is leaving familiar places without consciously choosing to leave them. The cover-board titles, **WITH A LONG SPOON** and **BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**, now sit in productive tension. This is literally the warmest reception imaginable — an open door, food, warmth, attention — while “a long spoon” keeps suggesting that warmth at a table is not necessarily safety. No cover tagline was supplied. From here, I want Vee’s love to be met with truth rather than merely more evidence she must interpret. I dread her eventual discovery of what Pace and Randi have concealed, especially after this chapter lets her confide in him so trustingly. My guess from here is only that Cassie’s observations and Vee’s own unanswered questions will become harder to swim away.
