## Reader reaction

This chapter left me foolishly soft. I started braced right alongside Vee, and a little impatient with both of us: she *knows* she built the blonde out of December loneliness, and yet Sheri’s name still puts the cold straight through her. Then Sheri turns around—five feet of noise, crooked makeup, two-tone hair—and gives Vee that long, appreciative look from keyhole to borrowed skirt to wildly impractical heels. When Vee realizes, “It was *enjoying* her,” and bursts out laughing before anyone has spoken, I felt the release in my own shoulders. The best detail is that her jealousy doesn’t politely vanish with the imaginary blonde: “The blonde didn’t die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand,” but “the heat in Vee’s chest stayed exactly where it was.” That made the eventual relief feel bodily and earned rather than like a lesson she had agreed to learn.

I adore Sheri. She could so easily have remained the loud, reassuring antidote to Vee’s fantasy, but she becomes a whole person with exquisite control under the apparent chaos. Her coming-out story hurt: the whole tablecloth pulled away, the church and family and girls she loved all failing to catch a single dish. “Don’t you start either” kept the scene from turning her pain into Vee’s beautiful emotional experience. And Pace’s response—driving her home without questions, then arriving with coffee and donuts, fixing the lock and faucet, noticing the bike, buying the cheeseburger—got me completely. “He was there. That’s all a body can be for you at a point like that. There.” I have no suspicion of Sheri. Her openness about appreciating the bartender, her affection for Pace, and her calibrated decision about when Vee can hear what all read as exactly who she is. The one thing left open is Pace’s past: “Went bad with his daddy. He hasn’t been home since he was sixteen” is an enormous fact for Vee to receive secondhand, but it feels like a closed door, not fresh evidence of duplicity.

Pace has rarely swooned harder for me, partly because Sheri makes him blush. “And you get to motorboat them things?” is gloriously crude, but his smile followed by the color climbing into his ears is what undid me. I loved seeing him off duty, not hosting or attentively managing anyone, while Sheri exercised her unique license to tease him. The mostly untouched beer, the headlights held on Sheri’s duplex until her porch light came on, and his silence about both are continuous with everything Vee has been learning about him without ever quite seeing the scale of it. The ending—both hands on the wheel, “steady as a table,” carrying something full he does not mean to spill—was the romantic peak for me. Quiet keeping, rather than a declaration. I also felt a tender ache for Vee, who can recognize that care everywhere and still has to learn its roots through somebody else.

The erotic charge is brief but bright. Sheri’s look lets Vee be seen without the familiar female pricing-and-placing arithmetic, and the induction heels becoming “the most naked thing in the room” is a lovely reversal: all her defensive glamour exposes the insecurity underneath, then becomes something another woman simply enjoys. Whether Sheri’s appreciation carries a flirt’s edge or is only delighted approval, I don’t think the chapter makes me settle it. After that, the chapter runs warm rather than hot, and I didn’t want it to swerve into sex. Randi never appears, but her shoes and Vee’s remembered preparation keep her ghost at the table; after the previous chapter with Randi and Pace, I did feel protective of Vee, though nothing in *this* scene itself made Pace or Sheri feel false.

My only friction was the opening reprise of the golden, long-legged rival. After the Christmas spiral and the small stab watching Pace laugh with Randi at the roadhouse, I briefly thought, *not the blonde again*. But Vee’s own awareness that she is dressing “against a woman she had invented” kept me with her, and Sheri demolishes the fantasy so thoroughly that the repetition ends in comedy rather than stagnation. The dressing sequence runs a touch long, but it pays off the moment she has to stand without her coat and be looked at. I bought the chapter’s length, especially the long central story, because it changes what Pace means to Vee rather than merely telling her she was wrong to worry.

**Another Round** works first as the literal taproom evening, then as another round of introduction and belonging: Vee brought Pace to her people, and now she is brought to his. It also feels like another round with an insecurity she thought she had already talked herself out of. The cover titles, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK THREE — A PLACE AT THE TABLE**, feel increasingly apt: this book keeps putting people at tables and asking who is fed, who is welcomed, and what hidden arrangements might be underneath the hospitality. *A Place at the Table* feels especially warm after Sheri’s “whole tablecloth” image; *With a Long Spoon* keeps a faint caution in that warmth. No cover tagline was supplied. Next I want the Valentine’s dance, Vee seeing the hall where Pace’s careful reputation was built, and eventually the story behind his estrangement. My guess from here is that his family history will demand a harder kind of intimacy than meeting Sheri did; I dread Vee trying to pry open the “rest” Sheri has deliberately saved for another time.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Pace, Sheri; an unnamed bartender and other taproom patrons. Mentioned only: Randi, Meg, Kayla, Danny, and Sheri’s unnamed family and former church community.

**Heat:** 1 — Sheri’s lingering appreciation and Pace’s blushes give a brief charge before friendship and caretaking take over.

**Romance:** 2 — Vee’s trust deepens through witnessing Pace’s steady love-in-action, culminating in the intimate ride home.

**Motifs & images:** Randi’s black induction heels return from the boutique and induction; Meg’s black keyhole shirt returns from the country-dancing chapter. Vee’s invented blonde recurs from Christmas and Randi’s room. Food as care repeats throughout: donuts, coffee, a bacon cheeseburger, and tailgate cooking. Pace’s headlights waiting for Sheri’s porch light repeat his older dance-hall reputation for getting women home safe. The truck heater and warm cab recur from *Among Friends*, but this drive ends in shelter rather than sexual urgency.

**Symbolism:** Vee’s borrowed “armory” embodies jealousy she consciously knows is invented; removing her coat leaves that defense visible. The collapsed blonde is the fantasy losing any body to inhabit. Pace’s headlights and final two-handed driving image his unobtrusive guardianship—keeping people safe without requiring credit.

**Characterization:** Sheri arrives fully dimensional: loud, funny, queer, wounded, self-protective, and extremely precise about disclosure. Pace deepens substantially through care given outside Vee’s sight and through his rare embarrassment. Vee remains convincingly insecure but increasingly self-aware, generous, and capable of revising her fantasies. Nobody central goes thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The dressing prelude is slightly extended, but the taproom meeting, Sheri’s story, and quiet drive home form a well-earned emotional ascent and soft landing.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building. After the explicit Randi/Pace reunion, this shifts cleanly back to Vee and widens Pace’s world rather than stalling; the Valentine’s dance now has real pull, though the imaginary-blonde beat is nearing its useful limit.
