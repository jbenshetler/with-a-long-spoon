## Reader reaction

This chapter made me exhale. After the cold, the reconciliation, and the overwhelming naked weekend, Vee gets something different: a sunlit table, food she chose, and friends on either side of her. The strongest swoon for me was not sexual at all. It was “Get in my car… first breakfast back,” her asking whether expensive is okay before taking them there, Kayla’s orange juice arriving in a flute so she is included in the celebration, and the three glasses meeting over the table. Vee is not hidden in Pace’s house or apologizing for her appetite here. She has “the middle of it all the whole time,” and I loved that for her.

Kayla and Meg both became more real to me. Kayla’s grief over the locked dining hall—“It’s the last morning of my life and it’s going to be peanut butter crackers”—could have been mere comic noise, but her delight is so open that I found her adorable. Her horror at having discussed socks and underwear with a woman en route to flashing somebody was the chapter’s best laugh. Meg’s steadiness now has more history and humor underneath it: Danny’s heating pad, “I told him the cord was stupid,” and reparations by maple bacon and the Matrix movies. I trust both of them. Nothing in their teasing felt like extraction or judgment; they let Vee disclose exactly as much as she wants, and nobody lets her cover the whole check merely because she suggested the place.

Pace himself stays offstage, but Wednesday’s dinner makes me warmer toward him. Meeting her people is an action directed precisely at her “out there” complaint, and her pleasure in saying “He’s handling it” is palpable. Still, there is a small ache under the cheerful retelling. “We talked all night… We’re better than we were” is, as the page carefully says, “the size of it she’d brought.” Her unanswered question has not actually been answered. She also gives Kayla and Meg a courteous, complete doorway line—“It’s late. Is everything alright?”—where I remember Pace beginning, “Vee, I l—,” before she opened the coat. I cannot turn that interruption into the declaration she wanted; he might have been about to say several things. But I notice that her public version is neater than the night I read. I do not think she is cynically lying. She is making the reconciliation into a story she can happily hold. The slight unease is that flashing him becomes “the apology” and “it worked,” while the word she needed remains unspoken.

The erotic charge is mostly retrospective and comic. The coat and heels still carry Friday’s heat, and Kayla’s shriek briefly brings the audacity of it back, but this scene is about friendship and belonging rather than wanting. I was glad of that. It lets Vee be funny, generous, socially confident, and loved without every warm thing immediately passing through sex.

“Across” reads to me as reaching across a table and across compartments of a life: friends to friends, Vee’s private relationship toward her public world, one side of her to another. It did not give the chapter away; it gained meaning from those flutes meeting over the middle and from Vee sitting between her people. The cover board gives **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK THREE — A PLACE AT THE TABLE**, with no tagline supplied. **A PLACE AT THE TABLE** feels almost literal here: Vee has made a place for herself and her friends rather than waiting to be fed behind a shut door. **WITH A LONG SPOON** still suggests sharing food and intimacy while some distance remains—which fits, because the coming table will include Pace and Randi and everything Vee does not know about them.

I very much want Wednesday now. I want to see Pace choose a place he can tolerate, Cassie and Randi negotiate the same room, and Kayla try not to detonate with excitement. My guess from here is that the dinner may be genuinely warm while also being socially dangerous: Pace and Randi will have to preserve their apparent unfamiliarity, with Cassie and Meg watching closely. I also dread Randi having to absorb another glowing account of Vee and Pace’s reunion after Brooke’s warning. But I am not bracing for inevitable catastrophe. This breakfast earned its happiness, and I want some of it to survive contact with the larger table.
