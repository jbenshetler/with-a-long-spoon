## Reader reaction

Ouch. After the heat and raw emotional crack-open of *The Bench*, walking into the sorority house in *Standards* feels like stepping out of a warm bath into an ice storm. My sympathy for Randi just shot through the roof, and it is an aching, claustrophobic kind of sympathy. Watching her get lightly, smilingly filleted by Brooke, Sloane, and Kinsey in the common room made my stomach knot up. I hate Brooke immediately—not as a cartoon villain, because she’s way too real for that, but for the seamless, velvety cruelty of her authority. The way Brooke asks about the paper she already knows was a lie, then pivots "without a seam" to "*Pace.* That's actually his name?", and then dismisses her with "Anyway, I'm sure you want to shower" before Randi can even use the defenses she’s spent forty seconds assembling—it’s surgical. And Sloane ("I love that you don't care about cars") and Kinsey ("Like — short-cute?") are the perfect chorus, shrinking Pace down into an embarrassing charity project so Randi knows exactly what it costs her in house currency to be with him.

What hurts most is watching Randi’s armor fail her by tiny, fatal fractions of a second, and then watching her shut down completely once she’s alone. In the last chapter, I felt a cold dread when she locked the bathroom door and decided the girl Pace had found was never coming out again. Here, we see the other side of her trap: she can't be the girl on the bench with Pace because being seen and held terrifies her, and she can't be the effortless Standards girl in the house anymore because last night broke her timing and exposed her to Brooke's radar. When she gets to her room and can’t remember why she opened the closet, and then lies down on top of her made bed in last night's wrinkled cashmere with her shoes still on—the same heels Pace unbuckled so tenderly!—pulling a cold, light synthetic duvet over herself that isn't his blanket ("It was not — it was a duvet"), my heart really ached for her.

There is no erotic charge on the page here, only the hangover of it—and the way the social world weaponizes it. The erotic memory of Pace’s hands on the planes of her body, of him taking her completely apart, gets compressed into Kinsey's hideous little "short-cute" and Randi hearing herself match Kinsey's pitch and say, "He's cute. Yeah." That betrayal of what actually happened to her body last night—reducing the man who made her beg and weep to "cute" to survive a four-minute ambushing—is brutal. And then the memory of her old joke to the girls—*He's hard to say no to*—coming back to her "in a different key. It was not a key she could sing in." Last night she found out what it actually meant not to be able to say no to what he drew out of her, and it scares her to death.

Do I suspect anyone of something hidden? With Brooke, there’s nothing "off" to guess at—her power play is right there on the surface under the bright smile, and it’s completely earned. She obviously knows Randi wasn't at a study group on Wilson; she knows about the white pickup parked behind Hahn; she's putting Randi in her place for slipping and for dating below the house's register. As for Pace, he isn't here, and the contrast between his warm house, his patience, and his real cashmere blanket versus this cold house and these smiling vipers only makes him look more like a refuge—except I still carry the chill from the cover board (*WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*). I still don't have a single concrete reason from the text to distrust Pace—he has been nothing but attentive, consensual, and gentle—yet that proverb about supping with the devil sits in the back of my head while I watch Randi get squeezed between two worlds.

I had zero reader friction here. Every beat of the four-minute interrogation and the silent, numb aftermath in her single room felt horribly true. The title, **Standards**, works on at least three levels at once: it's Randi's actual position on the sorority board (the reason she has a single room with a door she can close); it's the vicious, shallow social standards Brooke and the house enforce (trucks, height, majors, never being caught slipping); and it's the rigid personal standard of perfection and emotional untouchability Randi has kept for twenty-one years, which is now cracking down the middle.

Where this leaves me: I'm dreading and dying to see what those two unread notifications on her facedown phone are—almost certainly Pace checking in on her—and what she does with them. My guess from here is that Brooke's little humiliation ritual is going to push Randi in one of two bad directions: either she tries to cut Pace off or compartmentalize him harder to protect her standing in the house, or the coldness of the house drives her right back to his warm foyer, even more divided against herself than before.
