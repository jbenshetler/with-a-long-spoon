## Reader reaction

After the warmth and afterglow of "Famished" and "Fed," this chapter lands like a sudden cold draft, and my stomach was knotted through almost all of it. Coming right out of Vee's glowing Sunday morning brunch — where Randi was so gentle, calling Vee "lucky" and kissing her twice on the mouth at the curb — walking into Pace's kitchen that evening and watching Randi's armor clank shut is devastating. I feel a fierce, complicated ache for both of them here, and a creeping dread for Vee caught in the middle of a geometry she still doesn't know exists.

My feelings about Randi moved sharply here — not away from sympathy, but deeper into a kind of tragic alarm. I've known she restricts food (the thin apple slices, the mother on egg whites and tofu), so the moment Pace ladled a rich cream-and-potato stew into her "small one, the one he sliced her apples into," I winced before she even sat down. Watching her draw the spoon once across the top of the sauce, set it down untouched, and give him her polite, restaurant-voice *"It's wonderful"* hurt to read. And then her thumb pressing the rim of the wineglass under her nail — the exact self-punishing gesture she did at lunch with Vee! She is starving in every sense: sitting across from a bowl she won't eat, having spent the morning listening to Vee glow about sleeping with Pace, and watching Pace's face light up ("glad all the way down") at the news. When she drops the performance and goes flat — *"You've got her all weekend, every weekend, and you're in no hurry, and I can't think why you would be. ... It's selfish, Pace"* — she isn't wrong about how unequal their portions are right now, even if she's also impatient to push Vee faster than Vee is ready to go.

And Pace — this chapter showed me his blind spot more clearly than anything since Chapter 1. With Vee, he is almost miraculously attuned; with Randi, he keeps missing what is right in front of him. He notices the bag on the chair (she brought the overnight bag again after leaving in "Swim Lanes"), notices the polite *"wonderful,"* notices her pouring her own wine for the first time, notices the untouched bowl — and yet at the end he stands in the kitchen and thinks, *"He did not know what he had done. He looked for it, back through the evening from the gravel forward, and it was not there."* On one level, I respect his line in the sand: *"She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything."* That is the Pace I trust with Vee. But he also genuinely cannot see that he has fallen into a real, tender, private romance with Vee while Randi is left holding the logistics and the scraps, nor can he see what the cream stew meant to a girl who only eats thin apple slices. Covering that congealed bowl with a plate and putting it in the fridge "for her" broke my heart with how well-meant and how completely, hopelessly wrong it was.

There is no swoon in this chapter — it's the anti-swoon after the highs of the last three chapters. The erotic charge here is dark, fast, and explicitly disconnected. When Randi takes him to bed, she skips "the inch and the stop and the eyes," shuts her eyes, doesn't ask *"tell me,"* and uses his body hard and fast to get off before rolling into the cashmere blanket with her back to him. Pace recognizes that "whatever this was, it was not for him, and he gave it to her anyway." It has a raw, angry heat to it — a 2 rather than a 1 because the physical intensity and the ache of what's missing are so palpable — but it's sex as release and wall-building, the exact inverse of the open, eye-locked intimacy he just had with Vee.

I had zero reader friction; every beat felt earned and razor-sharp. The title, **"Portion,"** works on every level at once: the small bowl Pace sets out for Randi's rationed eating, the salad leaves she actually consumes, and the unequal share each of them has of Vee right now (Pace getting Friday-to-Sunday intimacy while Randi gets a Sunday brunch and a secret kiss). Against the cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — the spoon Randi draws across the top of the cream sauce without eating feels electric. You sup with the devil with a long spoon, and here Randi barely touches the spoon to the dish before setting it down. What I dread and want next is the collision: Randi has stopped waiting for Pace to pour her wine or set the schedule. She already kissed Vee twice on the mouth at the curb today (which she did *not* tell Pace!), and my guess from here is that Randi is going to start moving on Vee on her own timetable.
