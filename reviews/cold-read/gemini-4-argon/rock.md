## Reader reaction

This chapter broke my heart open in the best, quietest, most devastating way. After Chapter 21 ("Gone") gave us Randi and Pace finding their tender equilibrium again—with Randi actually eating a full plate of food and sleeping in his arms—this chapter gives us Vee and Pace in the deep, quiet aftermath of their own night. My feelings for Pace shifted dramatically here. Until now, despite his patience and care, he has always seemed so older-than-his-years, composed, the steady architect of everything happening in this house. Finding out he is twenty-two—just one year older than Vee—and that he went to college at *fifteen* after a seventh-grade teacher put his desk in the hallway for a year because he taught himself trigonometry knocked the wind out of me. And then the revelation about Daphne. Nineteen and fifteen. When Vee does the arithmetic and holds down "the clean ugly grown-up word" under her tongue, realizing "that he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck," I felt such a wave of protective ache for him. And yet—Wait. I also remember from Chapter 1 that Pace thought *he* had crossed a door Daphne built, and that it cost him more than he knew. So the version he gives Vee here ("Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong... So she went on. Which was only sensible") isn't even the whole wound; it's the neat, protective story he tells to keep Daphne safe from judgment, or to keep the worst of the pain contained. 

The swoon in this chapter isn't about heat; it's about the sheer, intoxicating romance of being *listened to* and met all the way. The way Pace responds to Vee's stories about Ohio church-basement casseroles not by mocking them, but by saying, *"And affectionate"*—going "first to the hand underneath the thing"—made me melt. And when he opens up about why math is beautiful to him (Gabriel's horn, the coastline paradox, Newton's three laws binding the ball, the leaf, and the stars into one poem), you can feel why Vee is falling so completely in love with him. The physical intimacy is warm and domestic rather than hungry: his palm resting over her bare breast under the open robe "as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm," and the extraordinary moment when her hand finds the long scar high on the inside of his thigh. When he says, *"Not tonight,"* and instead of moving her hand away, presses his own hand gently over hers on top of the scar so she can turn her palm up to meet his—and she whispers *"Peter"* into the dark "the way you say a word to keep it"—that is top-tier romantic intimacy.

And then the ending comes along and turns the knife in my chest. Vee lying awake, wrapped around him, thinking back over her semester: how she "stumbled into" Randi outside the dining hall, and how ten days later she complained about an integral and Pace crossed a coffee shop for it. *"One piece, he'd said, about the leaf and the stars. It felt like that. It had come to her all of a piece, the one and then the other, in six weeks, for no reason she had done anything to deserve. She had never been so fortunate, or so happy."* The dramatic irony here is almost unbearable. She is right that it is "all one piece," governed by the same hidden law—because Pace and Randi *arranged* both meetings in the pointing game! Yet Pace's feelings for her here are not a game at all; he just gave her his real name, his childhood, and his hand over his deepest scar (things we haven't even seen him give Randi). I had zero reader friction in this chapter—every line earned its weight.

As for the titles: **Rock** feels like what Pace has become to Vee, and what they are building here—bedrock, something solid and ancient like the dark ridge outside the glass, or the gravitational pull holding the stars and the rock together. Against the book's cover line, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, the dread is sharper than ever. "With a long spoon" comes from the proverb about supping with the devil, and here Pace has literally been feeding both women with such tenderness while the foundational deception sits underneath everything. I want—and dread—what happens next: we are heading toward the induction ceremony, the dress, and the inevitable collision when the two halves of Vee's "fortunate" life meet in the same room, or when she learns that the two people she loves didn't find her by accident.

**Characterization:**

**Pace — within the chapter:** Unhurried, meditative, and completely earned. The long fireside conversation moves organically from cumin and Ohio casseroles to names, age, math, Daphne, lifting, and the scar without ever feeling like an info-dump.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building beautifully by pairing two consecutive afterglow chapters ("Gone" with Randi, "Rock" with Vee). Instead of feeling repetitive, the parallel structure creates a tight, aching tension: both women are now deeper in love with him (and each other) than ever, while the secret between the two halves of his week grows heavier by the page.
