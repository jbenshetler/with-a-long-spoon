## Reader reaction

This chapter gave me the strangest, most intoxicating double-vision I’ve had in the book so far. On the surface, it’s two sunlit morning-after scenes — first in the dorm with Cassie, then at a bright glass-porch bistro with Randi — and Vee is so brimming with happiness ("She had never in her life been so happy") that I couldn't help beaming right along with her. Yet underneath that sunlight, every single beat between Vee and Randi is charged with what I know and Vee doesn't, and paradoxically, what Randi did for Vee here still moved me.

My heart went out to Vee in both scenes, though the contrast between what she gives Cassie and what she gives Randi made me ache a little. With Cassie — her truest, safest friend, who clocks her painted October toenails in two seconds flat — Vee edits out the middle of the night ("the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole") and literally has to zip her skirt back up because she drove home bare underneath after trading Pace her green satin panties. That beat where she realizes she has nothing on under her skirt three feet from Cassie was funny, hot, and tenderly protective of her own secret all at once. Cassie doesn't pry; she just celebrates that a man cooked for her. And yet, with Randi, Vee *wants* to lay the truth on the table. Reading Vee think that Randi is "the friend she'd wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting to get. Who liked her, some days, better than she managed to like herself" twisted my stomach, because I know how Randi engineered this friendship.

And yet — here is where the chapter really got me — Randi's guidance at brunch *worked* on me anyway. When Vee describes sitting at Pace's table at midnight wrapped only in his warm top sheet watching him cook, and feels a flicker of her old shame ("the other version of it was right there, a girl in a man's sheet with nothing on underneath at midnight"), Randi steps in before the shame can land: *"That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one."* And then, instead of letting Vee frame the night passively ("He was patient, he was gentle"), Randi walks her right back through her own choices — the frosted-plum toenails ("You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands"), the new underwear ("That's not nothing, that's a *campaign*") — until Vee says out loud, *"I wanted it... And I took it. For once."* Even knowing Randi is cultivating Vee for her and Pace's bed, watching Randi hand Vee back her own desire without shame felt genuinely generous and deeply satisfying.

At the same time, my suspicion of Randi is fully awake, and the text keeps feeding it while making Randi herself more complicated. Randi opens with another invented cover story (an "old boyfriend on campus... in such a hurry, like the building's on fire") to bait Vee into talking: *"Give me something to live on. Be cruel. Tell me everything."* Notice how in Chapter 14 Randi refused to stay the night or let Pace cook for her, and now she has to listen to Vee glow about sitting wrapped in Pace's sheet at midnight while he cooked for her — and about how frosted plum matches the burgundy silk dress Randi already knows Pace is making for her. I waited for Randi's jaw to tighten or her thumbnail to find her glass rim like last time, but instead, when Vee blushes over how far gone she is, Randi covers her hand and says, *"That's the best thing there is, being that gone on somebody. Do you know how many people never get within a mile of it?"* Knowing Randi locked her own real self behind a deadbolt in Chapter 1 because she couldn't survive being loved like that, that line felt devastatingly honest — Randi talking about a country she exiled herself from.

And the erotic charge in the bistro is unmistakable, peaking right when Randi draws out Vee's confession of premeditated wanting. Vee notices Randi's voice changing — "the esses running long, the a's lifting, more of whatever it was than there had been when they sat down — and her eyes had gone liquid at the edges." Vee innocently thinks Randi is tipsy, checks her nearly untouched mimosa, and drops the question! I loved that detail: Randi isn't drunk; she's visibly aroused (or emotionally undone, or both) by Vee's wanting, her native inflection slipping through her polish. And then at the curb, when Randi kisses her on the mouth twice again ("the hand light at the hinge of her jaw, the mouth, soft, the small drawing-back and then the second press"), Vee *meets* it this time and doesn't even look around to see who's watching. I had zero reader friction in this chapter — every line earned its keep.

The chapter title, **"Fed,"** lands on so many levels at once. Literally, Pace fed Vee dinner at midnight (and Cassie eats her cereal while noting, *"He cooked"*). Emotionally, Vee's lifelong hunger to be cherished and to have a girlfriend she can tell everything to is being fed. Erotically and darkly, Randi says she is *"running on fumes"* and asks Vee to *"Give me something to live on"* — she is feeding on Vee's secondhand intimacy with Pace and on Vee's waking desire, even as Randi's own mimosa sits mostly untouched. Together with the cover line, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, that image of a ladle lifting the middle out of the soup pot and Randi feeding on Vee's confession over a polite weekend brunch makes me shiver. What I both want and dread next is finding out whether Pace has told Randi they slept together, what happens when Randi and Pace next compare notes, and how much further Vee is going to lean into Randi's mouth before she realizes what that kiss actually is.
