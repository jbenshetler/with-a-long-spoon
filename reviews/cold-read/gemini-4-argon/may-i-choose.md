## Reader reaction

On its own terms, from inside Vee's shoes, this chapter is an absolute swoon. Watching her stand in the elevator, forgetting to step out because she's drinking in the sight of Pace freshly shaved and dressed in well-kept boots *for her*, made my chest ache for her. And that whole dinner at Christine's — the relief of handing over the menu when he asks *"May I choose?"*, the way she stops policing her own appetite and stops doing "the small constant work of keeping up her end," sinking into the restfulness of letting him carry the evening — is so deeply romantic and seductive. By the time they're under the shadow of the tree, and she blurts out her rule (*"I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"*), and he accepts it with zero pushback, just starting them gently down the path until her own hand pulls taut to stop him — God, if I didn't know what I know from the earlier chapters, I would be melting into a puddle right alongside her. Her smiling inside the second kiss feels completely earned from her side.

And that's what makes this chapter so deliciously, horribly complicated to read: I *do* know how they got here. I was on the quad when Pace pointed at Vee and told Randi to *"Go get her. For us."* I was in the coffee shop when they staged the math-problem meet-cute, and in Pace's living room in *Tannin* when Randi straddled him with a glass of red wine, demanding *"Tell me"* about his froyo walk with Vee. Yet inside *this* chapter, Pace doesn't feel like a cold puppeteer running a play. His history with Christine, his wry admission about placing third in powerlifting, the care with which he feeds Vee and leaves her room to choose — it all feels completely genuine. Even the kiss under the tree is his signature move, the exact thing he did with Randi at his front door in *The Bench* ("that was what he did, he backed off and let her decide"), and it works because his patience and attentiveness are real. My feelings about Pace are caught right in that tension: I am intensely drawn to his steadiness and the way he sees and rests a woman, and at the same time my stomach is knotted knowing he and Randi have set a trap around Vee that she has zero inkling of.

My heart is completely with Vee. She is so warm, so hungry in ways she hasn't let herself feel ("It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon"), and so disarmingly honest with herself when she steps past her own defenses. And Cassie continues to be a quiet anchor I love — her immediate read on Pace telling Vee not to answer the dinner invite until the next day (*"That's not a boy. That's a man."*) is spot-on about his composure, which makes it sting all the more that even perceptive Cassie only sees this as a one-on-one romance for her friend.

The erotic charge here isn't explicit, but it simmers steadily underneath everything and peaks twice: first at the dinner table when a drip of smoky baba ghanoush catches the side of Pace's hand and he licks it off without breaking his sentence — the way Vee loses the thread of what he's saying and just drifts in his voice and hands is deeply sensual — and then under the tree when she steps into that last inch of space, smelling the soap, the shave, the smoke in his shirt, and the unnamed scent of him, and goes back for the real kiss with mint in both their mouths. I had zero reader friction here; every beat of the date breathed and took its time.

The chapter title, **May I Choose**, works on three levels at once. On the surface, it's Pace's polite offer with the Arabic menu, giving Vee the sweet relief of surrender. Underneath that, by the end of the walk, it turns into what he hands *back* to her under the tree: he chooses the dinner, the pace, the path, and then leaves the kiss entirely to her choice. And darkened by the book's cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** (the old proverb about needing a long spoon to sup with the devil) — "May I Choose" echoes darkly back to *The Pointing Game* ("I point. You pick."). Everything Pace offers is framed as a polite, unpressured invitation where the woman chooses every step, even when the table itself was set long before she walked in.

What I want and dread next: I'm holding my breath for what happens when Monday comes, or when Randi hears about Saturday night. Vee thinks she's falling into a private beginning with a man who only has eyes for her, while Randi is waiting in the wings imagining Vee's breasts in her hands on Pace's bench. My guess from here is that Pace will tell Randi enough to stoke her hunger, or Vee herself will confide in her new friend Randi about the date — and watching Vee walk deeper into both of their orbits without knowing they're connected is going to be excruciating and unputdownable.
