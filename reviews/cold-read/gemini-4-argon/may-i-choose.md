## Reader reaction

Oh, I am completely, helplessly swept up, and I have that exquisite, aching knot in my stomach because I know everything Vee doesn't know, and yet this date worked on me so deeply that I was melting right alongside her.

My heart is so wide open for Vee right now. Watching her let her guard down — watching that constant, exhausting self-monitoring finally go quiet over warm bread and smoky eggplant — made me love her even more than I did in the pool chapter. When she realizes, "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped," after we've seen her mother's voice policing her food and her body, and after we've seen Randi calculate every thin slice of apple, it felt like watching someone finally exhale after holding their breath for years. And Cassie! Cassie continues to be the absolute rock of this book. Her deadpan "That's a whole face" and then her quiet, serious assessment when Vee explains how Pace left the invitation with her without demanding an immediate answer: *"That's not a boy," she said. "That's a man."* Cassie sees so clearly, and the tragic irony is that here, Pace's patience genuinely *does* read like rare, grounded manhood to Cassie, because Cassie doesn't know about the pointing game on the quad or what Randi and Pace are building toward.

And that brings me to Pace, and how complicated my feelings are. Because on the page, in this chapter, Pace is *devastatingly* appealing. I felt every ounce of the swoon Vee felt. The way he shows up in the lobby freshly shaved ("that color that meant *recently*, that meant *for this*") in polished boots he actually takes care of; the way Christine at the restaurant clearly adores him; the way he says *"May I choose?"* and takes the burden of the unfamiliar menu off her shoulders without making her feel ignorant; the way he lets her stop working so hard to hold up her end of the conversation ("Following him took nothing out of her. It was the most restful thing she had felt in as long as she could remember"). And then the kiss! When she blurts out her rule — *"I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"* — and he doesn't wheedle or sulk or do a "patient little wait for her to change her mind," he just accepts it warmly and starts walking again, leaving the space completely hers until *she* stops him, *she* says *"But I'm making an exception,"* and *she* leans in twice. And remember the first chapter, with Randi at the door? "He started to back off — that was what he did, he backed off and let her decide — and she did what she had started doing, which was to lean in and bring her mouth back to his..." He does the exact same move with Vee under the tree! He answers her small kiss, eases back a breath of space, and lets her choose to come back in for the real one. It's his signature move, and it works so well because it *is* genuinely attentive and consensual — he really doesn't push, he really does let the woman choose to cross the last inch.

That's where the tension between swoon and unease lives for me right now. Nothing Pace does in this chapter feels cruel or fake in the moment — his attentiveness, his calm, his delight in feeding a woman who usually polices her appetite, all of that feels totally consistent with who he actually is. Yet I cannot forget that he and Randi picked Vee out on the quad *for a threesome*, that he went straight from froyo with Vee to Randi straddling him on the couch in *Tannin* saying *"Tell me,"* and that Vee has no idea Randi and Pace even know each other before the coffee shop! Also, it hit me like a bolt of lightning in the restaurant: when Vee lost the thread of what Pace was saying and just sank into the sound of his voice and how restful it felt, it echoed how Vee couldn't remember a single actual word Randi said at Chi Latte, only the warm, chosen feeling of it! Both Pace and Randi have that effect on her, though in totally different registers — Randi is high-wattage dazzle, Pace is deep, quiet rest.

The erotic charge here is a slow, sensual simmer that peaks in two places for me: first at the table when a drip of smoky baba ghanoush slips onto the side of his hand "and he brought it to his mouth without a pause in what he was saying — quick, unthinking," and Vee literally loses the thread of his words watching his mouth and hands (and we finally learn why his forearms and knuckles are thick — he's a powerlifter!). Second, under the tree at the end, smelling the soap and razor-blush and smoke on him, standing in that last inch of space letting herself want it before she kisses him with mint still in both their mouths. I had zero friction as a reader; every detail, from the little worked-brass tea cages to the cultural etiquette of eating only with the right hand, felt lived-in and effortless.

The chapter title, **"May I Choose,"** works on two gorgeous levels: it's Pace's polite question over the menu that lets Vee surrender control and be taken care of, and by the end of the chapter it flips silently into Vee discovering that *she* is allowed to choose — to break her own rule, to stop him on the path, to take the kiss she wants. And against the book's cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — those polite words (*"May I call you?"*, *"May I choose?"*, *"Don't answer tonight"*) ARE the polite invitation. The proverb behind "With a Long Spoon" (*he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon*) hangs over this literal supper where Pace is feeding her dish after dish and she's eating right from his lead with no long spoon at all. I am dying to see what happens next — whether Vee tells Randi about her amazing Saturday date with "the coffee shop guy," and how long this delicate, secretive triangle can hold before the next turn.
