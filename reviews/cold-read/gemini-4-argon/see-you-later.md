## Reader reaction

Oh, my stomach dropped the second we shifted into Vee’s head and I realized how completely, effortlessly she had been hooked. Up until this chapter, Vee was the warm,copper-haired vision on the quad who laughed with her whole body and forgot she could be seen. Being inside her skin is so different, and so immediately endearing and aching. She is a math-smart girl who covers her lush body up to the collarbone because her mother’s voice (*"Floozies"*, *"not the kind of attention a nice girl"*) lives inside her head, even while a hidden, proud part of her knows she has *"more than they had, and better than any of them,"* and lingers a beat too long looking at another girl's bare stomach. And more than anything, she is hungry to be let inside the bright, effortless world she feels shut out of. Watching her try not to look at the lecture hall door broke my heart a little, because we just watched Randi engineer that collision on the lawn with cold-blooded, three-second sorority precision and then ask Pace, *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"*

My feelings about Randi shifted into something much more chilling here, even as I couldn't take my eyes off her. In *The Bench* and *Standards*, I ached for Randi—the armor of "the face," the girl portioning out thin apple slices and lying under a synthetic duvet in her shoes so she wouldn't cry or be truly seen. Even in *The Pointing Game*, her sudden, helpless orgasm at the thought of tasting a woman felt like a crack in her armor. Now I see what Randi does to keep that armor sealed: she takes the exact social machinery Brooke used on her—and the patient, attentive "choosing" Pace used on her—and turns it on Vee at full wattage. From the outside, through Vee's eyes, Randi looks like the one person in Chi Latte who isn't performing (*"Because Randi wasn't in it... Randi was... Randi"*). The irony is breathtaking and awful: Randi is performing harder than anyone in that room, down to her canned name-joke bit (*"Yes, like the adjective"*) and the way her flattery leaves Vee feeling warm and chosen while *"the transcript was blank."*

And yet—the swoon and the pull of this chapter are completely real, and they worked on me right alongside the dread. That's what makes it so potent. I felt the exact rush of a girl-crush when Randi slid into the row smelling of cold citrus, squeezing Vee’s forearm, whispering with her shoulder pressed to Vee’s so that Vee couldn't even hear the lecture on residuals. When Vee looks at Randi in the coffee shop with *"a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance, except the distance was three feet now,"* that is pure, heady infatuation—the line between wanting to *be* the golden girl and wanting *her* blurred into one warm ache in Vee's chest. Even knowing the setup, I felt the high of Vee being lifted onto that tall stool in the window, knees nearly touching Randi's, drinking the lavender latte and walking home spinning on *"Love you, girl"* and *"See you later."*

The suspicion here isn't a guess; the text earned every ounce of it both from the prior chapter and right on this page. Cassie saw it immediately. I loved Cassie instantly—in her ball cap and yesterday's shirt, refusing to play Randi's name-bit, putting her one-word name down flat, and then looking at Vee with an unnamed expression and saying, *"Goodbye, Vee."* Not *see you later*. *Goodbye*, like Vee is already stepping onto a train taking her somewhere Cassie can't follow. And the blank transcript! The fact that Vee can't remember a single actual word Randi said—even when Cassie asks the next day—only that something went in *"like a key in a lock"* (the exact phrase from *The Bench* for the secret hum in Randi's ribs!), is deeply unsettling. Randi gave nothing real of herself away while making Vee feel she'd been handed the kingdom. I didn't have a single moment of reader friction here; the pacing, the voices, and the dramatic irony were razor-sharp.

The titles all lock together now in a way that gives me chills. **See You Later** is Randi's breezy, possessive sign-off—the promise that has Vee floating home because it treats a next time as a *"flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see"* (and we know what calendar that is!). Paired against Cassie's flat *"Goodbye, Vee,"* the chapter title breaks your heart. And the book's cover board—**WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**—suddenly rang right inside the prose when Vee walked into Chi Latte and heard *"spoons going against the insides of cups, all over the room, an irregular ringing, everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom of their drinks."* "Who sups with the devil must have a long spoon," and here is Book One's "Polite Invitation": a lavender oat-milk latte at the high table in a butter-yellow room, stirred with a little spoon.

What I expect and dread next—as a guess from here—is the next step of the invitation: Randi bringing Vee into Pace's orbit, or to his house. I want to see what happens when Vee's buried, unexamined physical confidence and that "flat helpless want" meet the reality of what Randi and Pace are actually doing—and I'm terrified of how much it's going to hurt Vee when she finds out she wasn't chosen the way she thinks she was.
