## Reader reaction

What a dizzying, complicated, deeply unsettling and simultaneously swoony chapter this is. Because I know what Vee doesn't know — that Randi and Pace are together, that they played "The Pointing Game" on the quad and picked Vee out together — watching this coffee shop ambush happen from inside Vee's naive, hopeful perspective gave me the wildest split sensation in my stomach. 

On one level, I am completely swept up for Vee, and I hate how vulnerable that makes her. The swoon of how Pace approaches her is so real on the page that even knowing it's a setup, *I* felt it work on me. The way he doesn't rewrite her math problem, just leaves two short strokes in the margin and hands the pencil back at the exact moment she can solve it herself; the way he rolls his cuffs back over forearms that aren't a math tutor's; the quiet, unhurried *“May I call you?”*; and then at the froyo shop, when Vee lets her cup get too full without her mother’s voice stopping her, and she confesses *"This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo."* My heart just broke open for her right there. And when he doesn't make it huge or brush it off, just says *"Your tart's holding up,"* it is so warm and grounding that I genuinely felt the pull between them. Even if they came here by arrangement, Pace's attention on Vee — listening to her talk about econ for five minutes, laughing warmly, really seeing her mind — doesn't feel fake in the moment. 

And yet the suspicion — or rather, the awful dramatic irony — is screaming through the whole scene. Randi suggested moving to "the place on Wilson" (the exact 24-hour spot she lied to Brooke about studying at in *Standards*!). Randi is texting right before Pace walks in. They staged a whole fake meet-cute where Pace pretends to be a stranger in line, and Randi introduces herself to her own boyfriend with a flat, bare *"Randi"*! And what is going on with Randi at that table? Twice she lifts her coffee cup to her mouth and sets it down without taking a sip, her other hand in her lap. And why did she ask Pace that random question about Bessel's correction (*n*-minus-one) right in the middle of him charming Vee? Was she pulling his attention back to remind him she's there, or testing the cover, or feeling shut out as Pace focuses entirely on Vee? Cassie clocking it with *"He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt"* made me gasp out loud. Cassie sees *everything* — she was watching Randi text right before he walked in! — even if she doesn't know the whole picture yet. And then Randi pushing Vee toward him afterward (*"He looked like a man who wants you... He wants to see you naked"*): she's wingmanning her own boyfriend onto the girl they picked out for a threesome, except Vee thinks she's just being asked out on a normal date by a guy who forgot his coffee for her!

That's where the title, **Substitution**, hits like a ton of bricks on three different levels at once. On the surface, it's the calculus trick: *u*-substitution or Euler's substitution, turning an impossible integral into something you already know how to do. Second, it's the bait-and-switch of the setup — instead of Randi seducing Vee or bringing her to "us," Pace has been substituted in as the lone suitor while Randi steps back into the "friend" role. Third, and most chillingly, I can't help wondering if a real substitution is starting to happen right in front of Randi's eyes. In *The Bench*, Randi locked her real self behind the door and decided Pace could only ever have "the face." Now here is Vee, unguarded, building a leaning tower of froyo and admitting how rarely anyone is nice to her. When Pace says of Euler's formula, *"On their own these two are a struggle — you push and they won't give, and neither one quite looks like what it is. Put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain,"* it gave me chills — is he talking about math, or about putting these two women together? 

And against the book's cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — the whole chapter hums with quiet menace. "With a long spoon" comes from the proverb about supping with the devil, and here they are eating froyo with little plastic spoons after the politest invitation imaginable (*"May I call you?"*). I had zero reader friction here; every line earned its weight, and watching Vee borrow Randi's name-introduction bit (*"Vivienne Thorne. V.T. Yes, like the university"*) only for Randi to drop her own bit right after was excruciatingly good.

What I want and dread next: I'm terrified for Vee when she finds out Pace and Randi already know each other — or worse, how long they're going to keep this charade running before they close the trap. My guess from here is that Pace and Vee are going to build a real one-on-one connection first, and Randi is either orchestrating this from behind the curtain to keep herself safe from Pace's tenderness, or she's about to find herself on the outside of something she set in motion.
