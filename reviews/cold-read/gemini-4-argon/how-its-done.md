## Reader reaction

This chapter broke my heart in two completely different ways—first by making me love Vee and Cassie's friendship so much I wanted to wrap them in a blanket, and then by making my stomach drop through the floor during that velvet-booth lunch with Randi.

The opening half in the dorm room with Cassie is pure gold. After the breathless, dizzying intimacy of the dress-fitting in *A Round*, watching Vee debrief with Cassie feels like coming into warm air. I love how Cassie loves her: *"Vee felt the attention like sun on her face,"* and when Vee tells her how it felt to stand on the box with Pace on his knees looking up (*"Like I was supposed to be there"*), Cassie just reaches over and puts her hand on Vee's knee once and takes it back. And then the comedy of Cassie working the puzzle and asking, *"Vee. Are you sure he's not gay?"* followed by Vee briskly patting her own ass like a diagram and confessing with wicked delight, *"*I* was grinding on *him*"*—I laughed out loud. You can see how safe Cassie makes her: Vee tells Cassie the real, vulnerable emotional truth (*"It was like his eyes asked, May I look"*), and keeps the deepest bodily secret (the soaked satin panties) privately to herself without ever being pressed for it.

Which makes the contrast with Randi in the second half almost unbearable to watch, even while it's wildly hot and hypnotic. My feelings about Randi are so tangled right now. In the car, her smile is *"gentler, the performance gone out of it, the edges soft,"* and I believe her attraction to Vee is completely real. Yet everything about this lunch is a masterclass in extraction and power. First there's the class ache—Vee reading the right side of the menu *"the way you read a road in fog,"* ordering tomato soup and water while Randi orders poached salmon and a glass of Rombauer that costs more than Vee's whole meal, and then Randi only making a token *"oh, let me"* reach before watching Vee pay and tip well on a debit card she'll feel on Thursday. That hurt.

Then comes the seduction at the table. Randi's story about the "beautiful idiot" tying her wrists to the headboard with an Hermès scarf, tickling her until she's helpless and crying-laughing, and then switching *"to somewhere else entirely"* is another brazen fabrication—just like the Friday French-restaurant date at Smoothie Bird—and she is clearly repackaging her own experience of being broken open on Pace's bench into a breezy, glamorous anecdote to plant the idea of bondage and surrender in Vee's body: *"That's the whole point, baby... The letting go... A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right."* And it works! The erotic charge for Vee is immediate and unexamined: *"It was the picture that did it — not the man, the man was nobody, but Randi, Randi naked and helpless and laughing, her wrists tied over her head and her control gone... and the heat the picture put low in Vee was a heat she had no shelf for."*

What chilled me to the bone was watching what happened when Vee told her story. Earlier in the chapter, Pace's protection of Vee in *Swim Lanes*—how he never told Randi about the truck or the fitting, only *"Her stomach is soft"*—turns out to be the exact reason Randi is fishing so hard here. And when Vee says Pace turned the heat up for her and *"he looked at me and I was fine,"* Randi freezes: *"something had gone tight behind it, some muscle she was holding, and her thumb had found the rim of her water glass and set the edge of it in under the nail, bearing down."* That's the exact self-harm tell from Chapter 1 with the water-bottle cap! Hearing that Vee stood bare before Pace and felt *fine*—no shame, no terror, when Randi had to lock herself in the bathroom and vow never to be seen behind "the face" again—stabs straight into Randi's deepest wound. And how does Randi retaliate? She lures Vee step by step into confessing her deepest bodily shame. She catches Vee in the lie about the house being *"cold"* when Vee already said he'd warmed it up; she walks her down the platform, hip to front, until Vee is dreamy and on the verge of confessing the soaked satin she didn't even tell Cassie—and then Randi cuts her off: *"You don't have to tell me."* A door closing softly. Leaving Vee's secret *"half out of her with nowhere to go"* before snapping into daylight voice and asking for the check! It's Pace's signature move—edging someone to the brink and stopping—Except where Pace used his look on the platform to free Vee from shame, Randi draws the secret to Vee's lips just to prove she can, and leaves her dangling and turned inside out over tomato soup.

And then that ending! Randi stops in the exact same dorm fire lane where the campus cop barked his siren at Pace, says *"Come here,"* holds Vee's jaw, and kisses her on the mouth—and then does the *exact* Pace double-kiss: *"a small drawing-back, the barest pause, and then back again with a little more, a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't."* My heart was pounding. The swoon of that kiss against Vee's instant, frantic rationalizing (*"It's a French thing"*) and then the cold terror of scanning the dorm windows to see if anyone saw her lean across the seat to kiss a woman—it captures her internalized shame so sharply.

The chapter title, **"How It's Done,"** works on three levels at once: Randi's worldly line normalizing being topless for a fitting (*"Nobody measures over a bra... it's just how it's done"*), her rationalization afterwards (*"that's just how women like her say goodbye"*), and the devastating demonstration of how Randi conducts a seduction—edging Vee, closing the door, washing the heaviness away with pop music in a convertible, and claiming her mouth in the fire lane. Against **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, this expensive Sunday lunch and that *"Come here"* kiss feel like the polite invitation tightening into a claim. I can't wait (and half-dread) to see whether Randi takes Vee's fitting confession back to Pace—who deliberately kept the fitting secret from her—or keeps it as her own leverage.
