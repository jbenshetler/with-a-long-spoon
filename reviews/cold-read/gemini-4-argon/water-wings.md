## Reader reaction

I love Vee and Cassie in this chapter so much it almost hurts, especially knowing what is circling Vee out of sight. This quiet, breathing-space chapter belongs entirely to Vee and her body—and to Cassie, who is rapidly becoming my favorite person in the room. Watching Vee navigate the rec pool in her old synchronized-swimming suit, carrying her rolled towel down at her side instead of holding it up as a shield, walking the gauntlet of the bleachers with her chin level while guys lob *"You won't need floaties with those!"* at her—my heart went straight out to her. We saw her from the outside in *The Pointing Game* when Pace and Randi spotted her ("built on a lavish scale, lush in a way that had nothing curated about it"), and we saw her mother's voice inside her head at Chi Latte (*"floozies"*), and now we feel what it actually costs her to walk fifty yards in a swimsuit. When she goes under the water—*"She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken"*—that landed right in my chest. And then Cassie! Cassie keeping her joke low just for Vee instead of pitching it to the bleachers so fifty yards of held breath can go out at once; Cassie refusing to ask what's wrong when Vee surfaces sad because *"asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger,"* and instead shooting a sheet of water ten feet across the pool so Vee can shriek-laugh and splash her back. That is pure, unsentimental best-friend tenderness, and it made me ache for Vee knowing Cassie said *"Goodbye, Vee"* two chapters ago when Randi took her to coffee.

The swoon in the second half is quiet, solitary, and bittersweet. Vee sitting on the locker-room bench in her towel, still warm from the hot shower, winding a wet curl around her finger and thinking about Pace—it works on me even though I know how she was chosen. Becausefrom her side, everything she remembers about him is genuinely lovely: *"every boy she'd ever gone out with sat down like a dropped backpack, and he'd folded into that chair at the coffee shop like he'd measured it first."* And the detail that he asked her to dinner for Saturday and then told her *"Don't answer tonight"*—on one level it echoes his ethos from *The Pointing Game*, wanting a woman to answer when she's gathered back into herself and free to choose, and on another level it hooks her thoughts completely. The erotic charge here is a gentle, private simmer: her body warm under the towel, cold air on her shoulders, remembering the thick knuckles of his hands and wondering *"what he did with them, besides math. She was wondering it again now, in a towel, on a wet bench, which was really not the place."* It's sweet and real—she's a young woman feeling a quiet pull toward a man who treated her with care, while we know what his hands actually build and do.

As for trust and suspicion: there is zero suspicion inside this chapter's walls—Cassie is rock-solid, and Vee is completely guileless—which only makes the dramatic irony we're carrying from the earlier chapters sharper. Vee thinks she is deciding whether to text back an odd, attentive grad student; she has no idea that Randi is already in his bed drinking red wine and asking about her froyo cup, or that Randi asked *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* I had no reader friction at all here—every beat of the pool and locker room felt lived-in and exact.

The chapter title, **Water Wings**, hits twice once you've read the page. Literally, it's the gross bleacher catcall (*"You won't need floaties with those!"*) reducing her breasts to a joke while she walks past. Emotional and deeper down, it's the contrast between that ugliness up in the air and what she actually is in the water: a former synchronized swimmer whose hands automatically feather a figure-eight scull to hold her weightless at the bottom of the deep end. She doesn't need water wings; the deep water is the one place her body belongs to her without being a problem. Set against the volume title on the cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, Pace's *"Don't answer tonight"* feels like the literal "polite invitation" hanging in the air at the crosswalk—mannerly, unhurried, giving her all the room in the world to say yes on her own terms, while the proverb in *With a Long Spoon* reminds me that she has no idea what table she's actually being invited to sit at.

What I want and dread next: I'm holding my breath with Vee's thumb hovering over the keys while the crosswalk light goes to WALK and back. My guess from here is that she texts him yes for Saturday dinner—and I both want to see how Pace is with her one-on-one at dinner and dread the moment when the warm, attentive man she's picturing in the locker room converges with the three-way arrangement Randi and Pace have already set in motion.
