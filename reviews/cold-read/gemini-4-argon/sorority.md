## Reader reaction

This chapter was such a delicious, breathless tightrope walk between genuine girlish joy and the quiet, gathering storm underneath it all. Coming off the absolute sensual heights of *Peekaboo*, seeing Vee walk into Friday morning stats "underslept and overfull," glowing and sore in all the best ways, was an utter delight. And I have to start with Cassie, because the contrast between Vee's two friends in that lecture hall broke my heart a little in the best way. When Vee comes up for air from Simpson's paradox and realizes "Cassie had never gone down into them at all"—that Cassie doesn't care a whit about dresses or shoes, only about "what the dress had done to her friend"—and just asks, *"Was it a good night?"* and eases when Vee says *"Oh, yes,"* I felt such a wave of pure affection for her. Cassie is the true ground here.

And then there is Randi, who is so dazzling, so intoxicating, and so complicated in this chapter that I couldn't take my eyes off her. My feelings about Randi are in a constant tug-of-war: I know she's running a game, I remember her cold fury at Pace in *Portion* ("How long are you going to take?"), and yet when she is in the room with Vee, her desire for Vee and her delight in her feel completely real. When Vee walks out of the dorm bathroom in the burgundy silk and Randi goes still—*"Oh, baby. Turn for me — slow"*—and steps up to run her fingers up the bias seam to Vee's bare shoulder, my breath caught right along with Vee's. And when Randi gives Vee the exact word Vee had been crying for—*"He didn't make this for you. He made it to you"*—and then goes quiet for a long beat when Vee says *"He was up all night finishing it... I watched him sleep,"* I felt the sting in Randi's chest. Vee misreads it so generously, thinking Randi is lonely for a man who pays attention, never dreaming that Randi is picturing *Pace*—her Pace, who has never made her a dress, who sat in a dark kitchen putting her untouched stew away while she turned her back on him.

The swoon and the erotic charge of this chapter peak in two places, and both of them are between the women. First, that dorm-room fitting where Randi's hand lingers on Vee's warm shoulder under the cool charmeuse; second, and even hotter, in the shoe boutique. When Randi waves the saleswoman off (*"I've got her"*), drops to one knee on the floor in her expensive clothes—echoing Pace on his knees at the fitting!—and takes Vee's bare foot in both hands, I nearly melted. That beat where Randi sees the frosted plum toenails they talked about at brunch, says only *"Mm,"* and brushes her thumb once across Vee's big toe: *"Vee felt it go all the way up the length of her, felt the floor of her tip, a small swing of the room though she was sitting down, and her hand shot out and found Randi's shoulder to stop it."* That was electric. And what's so rich about the characterization is how Randi uses Vee's own secret logic to steer her: at the bypass store they have that genuinely hilarious, goofy moment with the yeti shoe and the pink rhinestone platforms (which made me love their chemistry even more), and when they discover they're both a size seven (*"We can share. God help us both, it's practically a marriage"*), Randi uses Vee's exact private daydream—walking in late and stopping the room cold—to pull her out of the sensible tan shoes and into the luxury boutique.

Yet that boutique scene also gave me a real knot of dread in my stomach. The financial mismatch between Vee and Randi has been simmering through the restaurant checks, and here it bites hard. Watching Vee put her debit card back and pull out her emergency credit card for four-inch strapped heels she has no way to pay off next month—rationalizing that *"The shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed"*—while Randi casually drops several times that amount on everyday loafers without even looking at the total just to keep her company at the register (or just because she felt like it!) made me wince. Randi doesn't force her, and even offers the "we can share" joke earlier, yet she walks Vee straight into a room where Vee's pride won't let her back down or admit she can't afford to be inside the door. I felt no friction with the writing at all—every beat, from Simpson's paradox to the credit card slip, felt sharp and earned.

The chapter title, **Sorority**, lands with a wonderful double edge. On the surface, Vee is finally tasting the sisterhood she's always been outside of: sitting between "both her girls" in stats, giggling in the shoe aisle, having a glamorous friend clasp a heel on her ankle and walk down the golden sidewalk with her (*"she had never once in her life had a friend like this"*). Underneath, it points to the Greek-letter world of status, performance, and Initiation/Induction that Randi rules—and the very expensive price of admission Vee just put on a credit card with nothing behind it. Against **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** (the cover board is all the packet shows, with no tagline or jacket), every "date" Randi invites Vee on feels like another spoonful at a table where Vee still doesn't know who else is dining. And Simpson's paradox in the lecture—two separate trends that point one way individually, and reverse completely when you combine them—felt like a quiet shiver of what happens when Vee's two separate worlds (Pace and Randi) finally get put together.

Next, I am dying for the induction night and for Vee to walk into a room in that dress and those heels—and I'm half-dreading, half-aching for the moment the two columns of her life combine.
