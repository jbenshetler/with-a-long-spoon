## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurt in the best/worst way — not because anything went wrong on the page, but because I watched Vee do the thing I feared she'd do: take the whole night and quietly edit it down to a story she could hand Cassie. "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness." That line is devastating precisely because it's *true* — it is a kindness, to herself and to Cassie both — and also a first crack of something I don't have a name for yet. This is the first time I've seen Vee curate her own desire for someone else's consumption, and it lands differently than the mother's-voice shame from last chapter, which was involuntary. This is chosen.

Cassie is exactly as warm as I remember her — "That good?", the cereal, the teasing about the toenails — and I love her for reading Vee's face instantly and matching the grin before asking a single question. But there's something quietly sad in "Cassie listened... and did not pull at the seam. Whether she felt it there, Vee couldn't have said" — the closeness that let Cassie draw out the shirt-thread earlier now runs up against a wall Vee's built on purpose, for the first time. I feel the friendship's warmth completely intact, but I also feel a door easing shut that wasn't shut before.

The zipper moment nearly stopped my heart — the beat where Vee almost undresses in front of Cassie by habit and catches herself, realizing there is no story left to tell past that point, only the fact of her own bareness. That's the whole chapter in one gesture: how much of intimacy is what you choose not to say. And then the ending — "the green was given away, and the smile would not come down. She had never in her life been so happy" — that's a swoon, unguarded and total, and I believed every word of it even while some part of me is bracing for what she's not looking at.

Erotic charge here is zero on the page — this is entirely the morning-after, interior and social — but it's saturated with the *memory* of heat, which does its own work.

Trust: no suspicion of anyone new. Cassie reads as pure warmth, slightly misreading ("get your brain back from your boyfriend" — wrong, but tenderly wrong). My unease is still all displaced forward, onto Randi and the unaddressed kiss — which this chapter, again, never mentions. That absence is getting loud.

**Titles:** "Toenails" is such a disarming, tiny title for a chapter about withholding — it points at exactly the detail Cassie notices and jokes about, the frosted-plum polish, and lets the real weight (what Vee keeps out of the telling) hide underneath something domestic and funny. It recolors nothing ominous — it's just precise, the way this book's titles tend to be. *A Polite Invitation* still feels apt — this whole book is about the gap between what's offered on the surface and what's actually being extended underneath.

**Want next:** I want Randi back on the page badly — I need to know if she knows, and how Vee will hold that when she does. And I'm newly curious what it will cost Vee to keep curating her own story, to Cassie and to herself.
