## Reader reaction

Oh, this one's quieter but it landed somewhere I didn't expect — not in Pace's kitchen at all, but in a cold dorm room with cereal gone soft in milk. I love Cassie immediately again, the "half a second" of something crossing her face before she reads Vee's grin and matches it — that's such an exact, generous piece of friendship-writing, the instant recalibration to be glad *with* someone rather than at them.

What actually got me was watching Vee lie by omission to the one person she's supposed to be unguarded with. "What she kept was the middle. She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole, and told herself it was a kindness." That's a gorgeous, slightly chilling sentence — chilling not because anything sinister happened but because I've spent chapters trusting Cassie as the place Vee doesn't perform. And here she is performing, giving Cassie "the old easy words she'd always handed Cassie about a man" — *sweet*, *good kisser* — the exact currency of a story that isn't true enough. I don't distrust Cassie for a second; if anything I want to know whether Cassie feels the missing seam ("Whether she felt it there, Vee couldn't have said") — that ambiguity is honestly held, not resolved for me.

The half-undressing moment is the chapter's real charge for me. Zipper halfway down, and she stops — not from modesty exactly, but because "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." That's such a smart, physical dramatization of the same withholding — the body almost outing what the mouth just spent five minutes concealing. And then the reveal, backdated: she gave her underwear to Pace, "Trade," and drove home bare. So the "Trade" from last chapter's carry-forward — his something for her panties — now literalizes, though what he traded still isn't named on this page. I like that it stays a little unaccounted for.

The floozie motif doesn't erupt here the way it did in the sex scene, but it's present in miniature — "frosted-plum toenails her mother would have had a word for. The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." That not-minding feels earned rather than triumphant — a small, credible aftertaste of last chapter's shame-that-didn't-close-her, now metabolized into something closer to peace.

Pace himself is entirely offstage but present in every choice Vee makes about what to tell and what to keep — I don't get new information about him, but I notice how much of my trust in him is riding on Vee's own unedited memory ("His hands. The white sheets. The thing her body had done before she could decide to let it") rather than on anything she says aloud. The gap between what she felt and what she narrates is the whole chapter, and it's doing quiet, careful work.

Erotic charge is low and that's right — this is a comedown chapter, the pleasure metabolizing into ordinary life, a bowl of cereal, a friend late for a game. The one line that still hums is the zipper stopping halfway; it's not explicit at all but it's the most charged image on the page precisely because of what it's not showing.

Titles: "Toenails" is such a small, domestic word to hang a chapter on, and it works exactly because it's the thing Cassie notices first — evidence before confession, the observable fact standing in for the whole unspoken night. It doesn't give anything away in advance; it recolors *after*, the way the best small-object titles do. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* still feel like they're pointing at something more clinical and calculated than this chapter's warmth — I keep expecting the "long spoon" to mean distance, deal-making, care taken with something dangerous, and this chapter is the opposite of that: a girl who can't quite tell her best friend the truth because the truth is too big, not too dangerous. That gap between title-promise and chapter-warmth is its own small unease, low-grade, worth keeping an eye on rather than acting on.

I want to know if Cassie ever gets the real story, and I'm newly curious about what Vee traded her underwear *for* — what's in Pace's palm now, so to speak, that we haven't seen. Mostly I just want to stay a while longer inside how happy she is, because the chapter earns that plainly: "She had never in her life been so happy."
