# Cold read (grounded) — across

*scene: scenes/across.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch050 + raw ch051..ch053 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Coming right off "Forbidden Kiss," this whole run is a warm bath, and I mean that as both compliment and slight worry. Let me take it in order.

**Forbidden Kiss / Back** land first in my memory before "Across" even starts, so my feeling walking into this chapter is already glow-soaked — the farmers-market breakfast, the "pleasure princess" reframing, the astonishing cunnilingus scene, the small unsettled thread of "why does he like it so much" that Vee tucks away instead of examining. Then "Back" gives me Pace wanting to meet her whole world — Randi, Cassie, Meg, Kayla, even Theo — which read to me as one of the most purely likeable things he's done in the book: he's been *listening*, keeping her people on a shelf (the boots, the wall-talking-paint line), and the impulse to fold them all into one room felt like real, uncomplicated care, not a technique. I noted my own relief at that — after fifty chapters of dramatic-irony unease about him, a scene where his attentiveness serves nothing but Vee's happiness landed clean.

So I come into "Across" already fond of Vee, trusting Pace more than usual, glad for her and Cassie's ordinary, easy night (the shrunk sweater bit, "Cassie. Shrank your sweater." — that's just good, warm comedy, and Cassie's small "Goodbye"-adjacent tenderness about her mother asking after Vee was a lovely little ache).

**"Across" itself** is the lightest, sunniest chapter I can remember reading in this book — closed dining hall, Kayla's operatic hunger, a sun porch, mimosas, and Vee holding a happy secret she gets to dole out on her own terms. My felt experience: pure pleasure, low stakes, real friendship texture. I like Kayla enormously here — her comic overflow ("we're going to have to eat granola bars over the sink like animals") did actual work, giving Vee cover to tell the porch story without narrating the drive itself, which the text is explicit about: "and never had to say one word about the drive." That's a nice small craft moment I noticed on the page, not one I'm inferring — Vee visibly manages what gets told and what doesn't, even in a scene that's otherwise total transparency and joy.

Meg is quietly wonderful too — "That's a big apology" / "It was a big fight," and her own maple-bacon-and-Matrix confession, is exactly the kind of unglamorous, real reciprocity that Cassie represents and that I trust completely in this book. It's a nice structural echo: Meg's story of repairing things "in their language" rhymes with what Vee just did with the coat, without either woman knowing it.

**Where I felt something move**: my read on Pace continues to soften in these three chapters. The dinner-for-everyone plan, arriving here as "he wants to meet you," reads as generous rather than performative — there's no checkpoint-knowledge reason yet to suspect this gesture of being another "repeatable method," and I want to be honest that I don't feel that suspicion on the page itself, even though my memory knows to hold it in reserve given the Randi material. I'm choosingly *not* importing dread into a scene that earned none.

**Erotic charge**: essentially zero in "Across" itself — it's a hangout chapter, warm not hot — but it's coasting on the afterglow of "Forbidden Kiss," which was the peak of the whole book so far for me: patient, drawn-out, genuinely overwhelming, and then that startling swerve — her taste-of-herself flinch, the "no — not that," his wordless retreat and return — which is the most honest moment of consent-in-the-middle-of-heat I've read in this book: a real no, immediately respected, immediately revised by her own clearer next word, no punishment either way. That whole beat earned my trust rather than my suspicion. The minor-key aftertaste — her tongue hunting for "salt" in his mouth, wondering how many times, and letting it go — is the one thread in "Forbidden Kiss" that quietly unsettled me; the checkpoint has trained me to hear an echo of Randi in any unexplained taste or scent on Pace, and the text plants it and then lets Vee talk herself past it with the "he forgot to wipe" excuse. I don't know if that's real dramatic irony or my own trained paranoia — the page itself keeps it genuinely ambiguous, "found nothing it could be sure of," and I want to hold that open rather than decide it for the text.

**Friction**: none, really, in "Across" — it's slight by design, a palate-cleanser chapter, and it didn't overstay. If anything I noticed the mimosa/checking/tapping-phones realism (splitting the bill three ways, "deliberating the tip buttons out loud") as slightly over-rendered compared to how spare the emotional beats are elsewhere, but it's charming rather than draggy.

**Titles**: "Across" — I don't feel it click into anything pointed. It could gesture at the porch table, at the drive out to Pace's, at crossing back into the semester, at Vee finally being able to say things "across" a table to friends instead of holding them in — all plausible, none insisted on. It reads more like a quiet, functional title than a loaded one; if it's meant to recolor on reread I can't feel it yet. "Forbidden Kiss," by contrast, gave itself away a little in advance — I knew something at the edge of a kiss would be transgressed, and the chapter delivered almost exactly that, tenderly. "Back" felt aptly plain — his back at the sink, the return from break, both literal.

The book/series titles (*A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*) still sit in the back of my mind as the frame I'm reading everything through — courtesy over appetite, and something about needing distance from what you're dining with. This stretch of chapters, especially the dinner-with-everyone plan and the sunny brunch, feels like it's building toward exactly that kind of long-spoon supper — all of Vee's worlds seated together, Pace hosting, everyone polite — and given what I know and Vee doesn't, I'm bracing pleasurably for that scene as the next real test of the whole architecture, not because anything here signaled danger, but because I can't help holding the dramatic irony in reserve.

**What I want next**: I want that Wednesday dinner. I want to see Randi in the same room as Cassie and Kayla and Pace, all performing their versions of themselves at once, and I'm curious whether the narrative lets anything slip in front of that many witnesses. Pure guess from here, no signal in the text itself yet — but the anticipation is real and it's the main engine pulling me forward after this quiet, happy interlude.
