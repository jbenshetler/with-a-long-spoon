**The felt read**

I came into this chapter already braced, and it didn't disappoint — it delivered the hangover from the mixer with real precision. What lands hardest is how *physical* the dread is: Vee lying rigid in the top bunk with her hand flat on her stomach like she's holding a lid down, and the line "as long as she didn't give it the thing that would make it true, it could stay only wanting" — that's the whole chapter's thesis in one sentence, and it's devastating because I've watched her do this exact maneuver with shame before (the floozie voice), except this time the thing she's containing isn't shame about wanting Pace, it's wanting Randi, and she has no vocabulary and no permission structure for it at all.

**Randi** — my feeling about her has curdled further, and it's earned. "Having you at the dance was so great. I've wanted to do that for the longest time" is a genuinely chilling line to read with the reader's knowledge that Randi has been in love with Vee since at least ch46 and has been engineering exposure to her body and confessions for months. And then the neck kiss — "See? How do you like it?" — is almost cruel in its confidence; she's not fumbling toward something, she's *testing a working theory* on a live subject. The walk-her-home offer, closing off Vee's escape route, reads as pure velvet-gloved control. I don't trust one word of Randi's warmth anymore, even though I still believe the warmth is also real — that's the trap the book keeps setting and this chapter tightens it.

**Pace** — barely on the page (only in memory, from Sunday), but the detail that stuck with me: Vee twice initiated sex over the weekend, which she "almost never" does, and each time "something in her let go... and then, after, took itself back up." That's not really about him at all — it's her using his body to try to outrun something, exactly as she did in "Boyfriend" with the county-road drive. I feel for him without him being present; he's become the wall she keeps running herself into.

**Cassie** — the chapter's gut-punch is the last line. "What was that with you and Randi?" is the plainest, most loaded question in the whole book so far, and Vee genuinely cannot answer it — not won't, *can't*. Cassie's "thread of disbelief she didn't trouble to hide" tells me Cassie sees more than she's saying, as always. This felt like the chapter where the widening gap between Vee and Cassie finally has a concrete hinge-moment instead of just atmosphere.

**Swoon vs. suspicion** — there's no swoon here at all, and I don't think the chapter wants one; it's the anxious comedown after several chapters of escalating heat. What's aching instead of hot is the wintergreen Lifesaver moment — Randi's finger grazing Vee's wrist as "static" she can't quite believe is static — small, exact, and it does more work than a bigger set piece would.

**Friction** — none, really. The prose stays inside Vee's avoidance so tightly that even I as reader don't get more clarity than she has, which feels right rather than withholding.

**Title** — "Coming Due" is unambiguous and lands hard: whatever Vee has been putting off examining (about Randi, about her own wanting, about what she told Pace was "nothing") is being called in. It doesn't recolor anything so much as confirm what the chapter was already doing — a bill arriving. Series/volume title "With a Long Spoon · A Warm Reception" — I don't have a fresh read on that here since this chapter is all interior and avoidance rather than the courtship/hospitality material the tagline evokes; it feels like we're in the underside of "reception" now.

**What I want next** — I want the Saturday brunch Vee just impulsively scheduled with Randi, and I'm dreading it exactly as much as Vee is. Pure guess from here: I think Randi is going to use that brunch to either escalate or finally say something, and I don't think Vee has anywhere left to hide by then.
