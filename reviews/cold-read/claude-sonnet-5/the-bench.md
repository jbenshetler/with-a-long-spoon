## Reader reaction

Okay — first thing I have to say: I spent the first two-thirds of this chapter assuming I was reading about Vee. The jacket built her up as *the* girl, and here's a man building a bench in secret for an unnamed "she," and I filled in the blank with Vee's name without the text ever giving it to me. That's on me as a reader, but it also means the reveal — "There you are, Randi" — landed as a genuine jolt. I had to go back and recheck: no, nothing before that line named her. That's a real structural surprise for an opening chapter, and it recalibrated the whole book for me in real time. The jacket's Vee hasn't appeared at all. Instead I've spent a whole chapter inside Pace and Randi's relationship, and it's clearly not new — the ritual of the door, the "Hi"/"Hi," the sliced apple he already knows how she likes it, the blanket she's already left there. This is an established, ongoing thing.

**How I feel about the characters.** Pace is meticulous, patient, attentive to a degree that reads as devotion rather than control-freakery — he notices the marks her bra straps leave, remembers she gets cold after, leaves water out in advance. I like him, genuinely, and I trust his tenderness as real on the page — no smirking, no cruelty for its own sake. But there's also something faintly unnerving in how *complete* his read of her is, how much he's already cataloguing and anticipating her before she can name anything to herself. The paragraph about Daphne stopped me — a past hurt, a lesson about not forcing a closed door — gives him real interiority and history, and makes his restraint at the end (not knocking on the bathroom door) feel earned rather than performed.

Randi is the chapter's real center, and she got to me hard by the end. The apple-eating, image-conscious, "the face" she's built her whole life around — the chapter makes plain, gently and then devastatingly, that her beauty has functioned as armor, a thing she could spend without it costing her anything. Watching that armor get stripped on the bench, watching her actually beg in a voice she hadn't used since childhood — that's where the chapter's heat *and* its ache both peak at once.

**Swoon / pull.** The bench scene itself is where I felt swept along — the slow-build spanking, the edging, the "you are doing so well," the moment he calls her Randi for the first time and it reads like a christening. It's tender and hot in exactly the way I want erotica to be: nothing coy, nothing rushed, consent checked ("tell me your word," "Red") without breaking the mood.

**And then the turn, which is the chapter's real gut-punch.** The moment she's called beautiful on the bed and *flinches* — that's the line that unsettled me most, more than anything in the scene itself: "She flinched. Involuntary, the kind of flinch a body makes when something lands somewhere it was not braced for." And then the bathroom scene, watching her actively, deliberately decide to bury what just happened and go back out wearing the smile — "Not tonight — and this arrived without words, quiet as a deadbolt going over — not ever." That's not a happy ending to the scene, even though the scene itself was full of real pleasure and real tenderness from him. It's a woman choosing self-erasure in the very moment she was most alive. I ache for her and I'm also a little frightened for what that choice costs her going forward, especially set against Pace lying there "quietly happy," having no idea she's just decided to keep herself from him permanently.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I don't distrust Pace — nothing on the page reads as manipulative; the Daphne paragraph if anything argues for his good faith. My unease is entirely about Randi's interior resolution, and it's earned by the text itself, not manufactured by me.

**Erotic charge.** Very high, and it's not clinical — the writing stays in her body (the wetness, the sweat, the trembling) without losing the psychological thread underneath it. It peaks with the "please, please, please" breaking into her real voice.

**Titles.** "The Bench" — plain, almost deadpan, and it undersells what's coming, which I like; by the end the bench itself becomes a small monument, "waiting for the next time," faintly ominous in its patience. As for the book's title and tagline — the closing blurb line "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." This chapter is a direct illustration of that line, except transposed onto Randi instead of Vee — her yes was total, freely given, and it's already becoming "the problem," at least for her.

**What I want next.** I want Vee. The jacket promised her and she hasn't shown up — I'm curious whether this chapter is prologue-adjacent (establishing Pace and Randi before Vee enters their orbit) or whether the book will keep cutting between the two women. Guess from here: given the jacket's talk of a "secret choice" and a "game," I suspect this Pace/Randi relationship — and Randi's newly resolved self-concealment — is going to matter enormously to whatever happens to Vee. But that's a guess, not something the page told me yet.

Tonight, on his bench, she came undone into a real, unguarded self for the first time — then privately decided, in his bathroom mirror, to never let him see that self again.

No wariness toward Pace himself; my unease is entirely for what Randi is going to do to herself, and possibly to him, by keeping this secret. Eager to meet Vee.
