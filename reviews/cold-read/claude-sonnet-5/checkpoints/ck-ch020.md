### Symbolism

- **The bench** reads as more than furniture — a built structure that strips away performance and forces total exposure/surrender; what it does to Randi in ch1 foreshadows what fittings and beds later do to Vee.
- **Measuring/fitting** (ch15) functions as a metaphor for being truly seen and known, rather than merely admired — echoed by Pace's patient, attentive "reading" of people generally (the calculus tutoring scene, the codes book explanation).
- **The pointing game and the "point/choose" structure** symbolically enact consent and complicity at once — Randi is given the choosing, but the whole exercise originates with Pace's question, mirroring the larger structure of the novel's dramatic irony (an invitation extended before the invitee knows there's a game at all).
- **Doors** function throughout as a symbol of consent and privacy — respected doors (Pace's rule, his own bedroom held back until offered) versus violated or self-locked ones (Randi's bathroom, the "Daphne" backstory) — mapping who is safe with whom.
- **Fire/hunger imagery** ("Famished," appetite, being "fed") ties directly to the book's larger controlling metaphor of appetite and consumption promised by the jacket copy.

### Story so far

Chapters 1–2 open with an unnamed woman (revealed at the end of ch1 to be Randi) visiting Pace's house for an established, ongoing intimate relationship. Pace has built an elaborate bench/apparatus; over an extended scene he spanks, edges, and finally allows her to climax after she breaks through her practiced composure and genuinely begs — the first time she has ever been fully unguarded with anyone. Afterward she is shaken, cries alone in the bathroom, and privately resolves never to let herself be that exposed again — she will give him "the face" from now on, not herself. Chapter 2 shows her returning to her sorority the next morning; chapter president Brooke and sisters Sloane/Kinsey needle her lightly about the new boyfriend ("Pace," his program, whether it's appropriate). Randi manages the interrogation smoothly in public, then privately collapses into a numb, unweeping stillness in her room.

Chapter 3 reveals Pace and Randi's private "pointing game": in bed, Pace asks Randi if she'd like to taste another woman; her body answers with an involuntary orgasm before her mind can manage the question. Afterward he asks again plainly; she reframes her "yes" as a gift-offer for a threesome with him, making the desire survivable by making it his. He proposes "a walk on campus" and the two invent, on the spot, a game: he points at women, she rates them, revealing (through her genuine reactions) her real type, working past performed pickiness. He finally points at a striking, unselfconscious redheaded girl laughing outside the dining hall; Randi's reaction is immediate and unguarded — a bare "Yes." Pace tells her to go meet her; Randi engineers a "collision" and charms the girl into a friendship. The girl is Vee. Randi returns euphoric and tells Pace her name and that they share a stats class.

Chapters 4–9 shift toward Vee's point of view. She meets Randi (an engineered "chance" encounter) and is swept into her orbit — a stats class, a coffee date, effusive warmth, being publicly claimed as "dear friend." Meanwhile, at a coffee shop while Vee struggles with a calculus problem, Pace approaches, solves the tutoring moment with restraint and generosity (Socratic, not showing off), and asks to call her. Their courtship proceeds through a froyo outing, a formal dinner date (his choosing the menu, "May I choose?"), Vee's first kiss (initiated by her, against her own rule), and a difficult, revelatory hike to a mountain summit where Pace tends her blistered heel, feeds her, and quietly registers — without acting on it yet — her longing for a burgundy/red dress she can't afford for her honor-society induction. A sudden storm on the descent leads to a charged, unconsummated moment in his truck: Vee, cold and moved, calls him back into the cab and undresses to her bra in front of him; he is deeply affected but doesn't touch her, and gives her his dry shirt.

Chapters 10, 13 show Vee's world (Cassie, and dorm friends Meg/Kayla) reacting to the unfolding romance, plus a first meeting between Vee and Randi's sorority-world (Brooke) at a smoothie shop, where Randi publicly and warmly claims Vee as "dear friend" in front of her sisters.

Chapters 14–16 cover Vee's first visit to Pace's house: he shows her his workshop, his patent (an MRI signal-processing improvement), his handmade furniture — and it's revealed the "mystery" flannel shirt he gave her on the mountain was handmade by him. He reveals he has secretly acquired burgundy silk (deeper/more specific than the magazine dress she described) to make her the dress she wanted. A long, intensely charged fitting scene follows: he measures her nearly nude, insists the bra come off "it changes the line," and Vee, mortified by visible arousal, is met not with mockery but delight; she ultimately begins reciprocating, he nearly breaks composure, and the fitting ends with Vee feeling that being seen at her most exposed doesn't cost her anything.

Chapter 17 gives an extended lunch between Randi and Vee: Randi tells a story about being tied up (with a scarf) and made helpless by a recent date, framing surrendered control as a gift to herself; she then draws out Vee's account of the fitting, using leading questions to extract sexual detail Vee hadn't planned to share, before gently, deliberately closing the door ("you don't have to tell me"). Vee pays for the expensive lunch despite Randi's wealth. Afterward, in the car, Randi kisses Vee on the mouth in a way that's more than a peck.

Chapter 18 ("Famished") is Vee and Pace's first time having sex: a home-cooked dinner, Vee initiating, both undressing in his bedroom, her shame-voice running throughout but never closing her off, Pace's tenderness and real-time consent, and full consummation — the first time she's ever stopped narrating/managing herself during sex. Afterward they eat again at the kitchen table (her wrapped in a bedsheet), and she trades him her worn panties for his earlier gift of the shirt.

Chapter 19 shows Vee returning to the dorm the next morning glowing, giving Cassie an edited version, and nearly undressing in front of Cassie before catching herself, since she has nothing on underneath.

Chapter 20 ("Fed") is another lunch between Randi and Vee. Randi tells a self-deprecating dating story, then draws out a fuller account of Vee's night with Pace, including the frosted-plum toenail polish (chosen to echo the burgundy silk) and the new underwear Vee bought anticipating sex. Randi frames Vee's willing, planned desire as something to be proud of ("You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky") and gets Vee to admit, "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." The lunch ends with a second, more comfortable goodbye kiss on the mouth, which Vee meets without anxiety this time. The chapter ends with Vee driving home happy, unaware of anything beneath the surface of either relationship.

### Impression

**Pace** reads, on the surface, as extraordinarily attentive, patient, and generous — his craft all in service of noticing and answering what people actually want before they can ask for it. But the reader now knows his courtship of Vee began inside a deliberate "game" conducted with Randi, and that he has an unexplained past (the scar, "Daphne," a door he once violated) that hasn't been unpacked. My trust in him is high on the level of craft and care, but there's a structural unease underneath — he is *very* good at reading and opening people, which is exactly what makes the engineered nature of Vee's "chance" meeting with him land uneasily in hindsight.

**Randi** is the most compelling and most unsettling figure so far — genuinely warm in the moment, generous in ways that cost her something, and also visibly performing at nearly all times, mining Vee's disclosures with real skill and (it seems) real desire she can't fully name even to herself. The reader has access to her private collapse (ch1–2) that no one else in the story has, which makes her public warmth toward Vee land as both sincere and calculated at once — I don't fully trust her intentions toward Vee, even though I believe her feelings are real.

**Vee** is easy to be fully on the side of — hungry, guileless, self-doubting in a way the narrative treats gently, and now visibly happy in a way that's earned on the page even if its origins weren't. The dramatic irony (that both people closest to her arranged this) creates real tension between rooting for her happiness and dreading what she doesn't know.

**Cassie** remains the most straightforwardly trustworthy person in the book so far — dry, loyal, protective, and rightly wary of Randi without any ulterior motive visible. She reads as safe.

Overall: warm, sensuous, unsettling by design — the reader is meant to feel both drawn into and complicit in the seduction, per the jacket's framing, and that's exactly how it's landing.
