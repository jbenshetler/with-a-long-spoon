### Story so far

Vivienne "Vee" Thorne, a financially strained, food- and body-shamed college junior in Blacksburg, is drawn — she believes by chance — into friendship with Randi, a glamorous, wealthy sorority senior, after a staged "accidental" collision on campus. Unknown to Vee, this meeting was the outcome of a private game Randi played with her established (secret) partner, Pace: he pointed out women on campus and Randi chose, ultimately picking Vee out of unplanned, instinctive desire. The book opens, before any of this is explained, on Randi and Pace's established BDSM relationship — a scene (the bench) in which Randi is taken further than ever before, begs and breaks down, and afterward locks herself away from him emotionally, resolving never to let him (or anyone) see that far into her again; she maintains a flawless public "face" at her sorority the next morning (Standards board, chapter president Brooke's needling scrutiny about "Pace").

Randi engineers Vee's path to Pace: at a coffee shop, Pace "happens" to help Vee with a calculus problem, they exchange numbers, and a courtly, deliberate courtship begins — frozen yogurt, a home-cooked Middle Eastern dinner, a mountain hike (where Pace tends a blister, gives her his dry shirt in a rainstorm, and she undresses for him on impulse in his truck), and eventually his house, a hand-built, book-filled home that reveals him as a powerlifter, expert woodworker, and a math PhD (22 years old, real name Peter) who holds a patent on faster MRI signal processing. He learns, offhand, that Vee once wanted a burgundy silk dress for her honor-society induction and can't afford it; he measures her (an extended, charged fitting scene) and hand-sews it for her. They have sex for the first time (Ch18, "Famished"); the relationship deepens rapidly into an intense, consent-forward, increasingly kink-inflected sexual relationship (spanking, edging, photography, "bets").

In parallel, Vee's friendship with Randi intensifies into near-romantic territory: recurring lunches/brunches where Randi extracts increasingly explicit detail about Vee's sex life with Pace while showing her own visible arousal; escalating goodbye kisses on the mouth ("See you soon, gorgeous"); a shoe-shopping day that turns intimate (Randi kneeling to dress Vee's feet); and one incident where, alone in a bathroom stall after a particularly charged story, Vee begins to masturbate and stops herself specifically upon imagining Randi watching — an attraction she does not name even to herself. Vee learns Pace's own painful history: schoolboy punishment for precocity, entry to college at 15, a formative relationship at 15–17 with an older tutor, Daphne, that ended when she turned 21; and that he does not visit his own parents, for reasons undisclosed. He carries an unexplained surgical scar on his inner thigh.

A single serious conflict occurs: at a country bar, another woman implies to Vee that Pace "gets all the girls home safe," triggering Vee's jealousy and insecurity; she lashes out at him in public, he leaves quietly, and she spends a bad night before going to Randi (not Cassie) for help repairing things — Randi coaches her on how to apologize ("sure, not sorry") and outfits her (borrowed heels, scarf). The apology succeeds; the relationship deepens further, with Vee essentially cohabiting on weekends, gaining her own drawer in Pace's bathroom, and settling into "the new ordinary."

Structurally underneath all of this: Pace and Randi's own relationship continues in secret (seen directly in Ch24, "Gone," and referenced by his cycling friend Sheri in Ch28, who notes it "is not finished" and warns that juggling both women will get complicated). A class project (Ch39, "The Outlier") accidentally exposes that Randi's family wealth is tied to a company, Miranda Holdings LLC, that appears to have misused COVID-era PPP loans (took the money, cut jobs anyway) — Randi's uncharacteristic loss of composure is witnessed only by Vee, who says nothing to Cassie or anyone else, protecting her. The book ends this span (Ch40) on a tender, settled domestic beat between Vee and Pace — the flannel shirt washed and handed back to him to be "recharged" with his smell — with the larger structural truths (the game, the secret couple, the depth of feeling all three now hold for one another) still entirely unknown to Vee.

### Impression

**Vee** — deeply sympathetic: a young woman starved of attention and permission her whole life, blooming almost unbearably under two people's focused care, entirely unaware she's inside a plan. Her joy is real and earned on the page even though the reader can see the frame around it; the effect is tenderness shot through with real unease on her behalf.

**Pace** — reads as genuinely tender, patient, and attentive — every individual scene with him is warm, consent-centered, and generous — but the reader's trust in him is necessarily qualified by what we know he's concealing (the game, Randi, unexplained past pain, the scar). Attraction and unease sit side by side; nothing he's done to Vee directly reads as cruel, but the architecture around him is not innocent.

**Randi** — the most complicated figure emotionally: capable of real cruelty-by-omission (staging a friendship as bait) and real, apparently helpless tenderness (falling for Vee against her own plan, protecting her composure at cost to herself, visible cracks around her family's money). Sympathy and wariness are both earned and both fully warranted — she is drawn as someone trapped by her own performance as much as anyone else is trapped by it.

**Cassie** — the one relationship in the book with no discernible hidden agenda; pure, plain-spoken loyalty. Trust here is uncomplicated, though her own quiet, unstated feelings (if that's what they are) add a small ache of their own.

Overall: this is dramatic irony working at full strength — the reader is asked to feel the same warmth and desire Vee feels while carrying knowledge that recontextualizes nearly everything as engineered, which produces a steady, low-grade unease under even the most purely pleasurable scenes.
