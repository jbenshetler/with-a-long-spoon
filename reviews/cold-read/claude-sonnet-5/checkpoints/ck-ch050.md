### What I know that they don't

*Using/withheld side:*
- The entire Vee/Pace relationship began as a literal selection game ("The Pointing Game," ch3) that Randi and Pace played together — Randi chose Vee out for him. Vee has never learned this and has no reason to suspect it.
- Pace and Randi have an ongoing secret sexual relationship for the entire span covered so far, including nights overlapping Vee's own involvement with him (ch1, ch24–25). Vee has no idea.
- Randi relays intimate details of Vee's body and reactions back to Pace in bed (ch24: "What she keeps under there... It's criminal, Pace. But you already know") — implying he already had firsthand knowledge by then. Vee never learns this.
- Randi's vivid "personal" sexual anecdotes (the tied-up/tickled story, the over-the-knee spanking-with-counting story) closely prefigure things that then happen to Vee with Pace — strongly suggesting either they are about Pace in disguise, or deliberate coaching. Vee never connects this.
- Randi is, by her own interior admission (ch46), in love with/sexually fixated on Vee and has deliberately chosen never to tell her, to protect the friendship. Vee senses odd moments but always explains them away.
- Reader (and Vee, briefly, alone) saw Randi's shock at the PPP-data reveal (ch39: Miranda Holdings is the single worst outlier); Cassie never learns the company is her own groupmate's family, so her data-driven argument was validated by evidence she doesn't know is personal.
- Pace's courtship "signature" moves (pre-warming the house, wine poured in advance, elaborate cooking, tailoring) read, structurally, as a repeatable method rather than something unique to Vee — left ambiguous by the text but visible to the reader across both his Vee and Randi scenes.

*Caring/protective side:*
- Pace's attentiveness is nonetheless sincere moment-to-moment: safe-word checks, blister care, warming the house, tracking her food preferences down to apple-slice thickness — real tenderness runs alongside the concealment.
- Cassie is a steady, non-manipulative source of real friendship and protection even as Vee drifts from her; her odd, weighted "Goodbye, Vee" (ch4) reads in hindsight as more prescient than either character registered.
- Sheri genuinely challenges Pace on Vee's behalf, an outside, unglamorous voice of conscience (ch48).
- Randi's manipulations coexist with what reads as real, if compromised, love for Vee — she calls her "the best friend I've ever had" and means at least the friendship part of it.

### Symbolism
- **The bench** — an apparatus built explicitly to strip performance and reach what's "real" underneath a woman; echoes the whole book's design (the pointing game, the courtship) of engineered vulnerability presented to its subject as organic discovery.
- **The dress** — a literal garment built to her exact body, gorgeous and freely chosen by her to wear bare underneath — but entirely authored, cut, and controlled by him; mirrors the blend of genuine gift and quiet authorship running through the whole relationship.
- **Warm houses vs. cold dorm rooms** — Pace's unnaturally warmed house signals care but also insulation (he struggles in loud public spaces with her); Cassie's cracked window and cold room are associated with honesty and rawness rather than comfort.
- **Randi's curated closet** vs. her interior chaos (breakdown after ch1, stillness at ch39's reveal) — surface control masking real disorder, a pattern that also describes her whole persona.

### Impression
**Pace** reads as genuinely tender, almost supernaturally attentive to Vee's body and needs — and yet the accumulating pattern (identical rituals with Randi, the "pointing game" origin, his flat refusal to ever name anything, "words are cheap") makes him increasingly hard to fully trust, even though nothing he does to either woman in the room is ever anything but generous. The dramatic irony is doing a lot of work: as a reader I like him less than Vee does, and worry for her more than she worries for herself.

**Randi** is magnetic and the most complicated figure in the book so far — her warmth toward Vee reads as real, her manipulation of Vee's confessions also reads as real, and by ch46 her love for Vee reads as real too, all at once, without resolving into simple villainy. I feel sympathy for her (the family money, the parents, the self-imposed loneliness of never being fully known even by "safe" perfect men) and real unease about what she's doing to Vee without permission.

**Vee** is easy to root for — her hunger, her shame, her growing willingness to want things out loud is the heart of the book, and her blindness to what's actually happening around her (engineered by two people she trusts completely) creates steady, aching suspense rather than frustration with her as a character.

**Cassie** is the one person in the book I have no reason to distrust at all — a genuine, low-key, protective presence, increasingly and sadly sidelined.

Overall: warm, explicit, and unsettling in roughly equal measure — the tenderness on the page is real and the withheld truths under it are substantial; I'm pulled toward the relationships and wary of nearly everyone in them except Cassie.
