# Cold read (grounded) — Water Wings

*scene: scenes/water-wings.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch006 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Well, that was a whiplash of a chapter to land on, and I mean that as praise. I came off "The Long Way" — froyo, "Your tart's holding up," the sweetest and most unguarded I've felt watching Vee and Pace — and then I get dropped straight into a locker room with Cassie fighting a swimsuit and two guys heckling from the bleachers. No sex, no Pace, no Randi. Just Vee and Cassie at a rec pool. It reoriented me hard, and in a good way — this book has a wide lens and it just used it.

**How I feel about each character right now.** Cassie is the one who moved on me this chapter, and she moved a lot. In the recent chapters she's been a flat, dry presence at the edge of Vee and Randi's scenes — "Cassie," one word, "goodbye, Vee" landing wrong, the look Vee "would not have been able to name and did not try to." Here she gets a whole interior register: fighting the ugly suit "like it had insulted her family," not caring that it doesn't flatter her, clocking the catcalling guys with flat contempt ("Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman") pitched low just for Vee, and then — this is the moment that got me — choosing not to drown the boys in her rec-season lungs but to keep the joke small and private, *for Vee*. And then the water-shooting trick that isn't about getting her wet so much as knowing exactly how to end a grief she's not going to ask about. That's real, unshowy love, and it reads as more trustworthy than anything Randi's warmth has shown me so far. I like Cassie enormously right now, and I trust her completely — she is the one person in this book who has asked nothing of Vee and given her a lot.

Vee herself broke my heart a little in the middle of this — the green-and-white suit from sixteen, "when there'd been less of her to fill it," the towel carried "not up against her," the deliberate chin-level walk designed to survive a gauntlet she knows is coming. The catcalls landing and her holding her face together, and then going underwater and having something in her "quietly sit down" — the swim as the one place her body was never a problem, the "grieve, a little" — that's exactly the shame-under-the-wanting texture I read this book for, and it's not attached to Pace or sex at all here, which surprised me. It's just her, alone, with a body other people keep narrating for her.

**Swoon / pull.** The froyo walk that closed out the previous chapter is still doing work on me — "may I call you," the tart holding up, him letting her embarrassing confession "have its space" instead of laughing it off — that's the tenderest, least engineered Pace has felt yet, genuinely lovely, no visible machinery. This chapter's own pull is different: not romantic, but the ferocious tenderness of the splash fight, "you can't grieve and fight your best friend for a mouthful of pool at the same time; the splashing won." That line got me. It's a real friendship rescue, quietly and completely earned.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No suspicion of Cassie at all — she reads as the one un-agenda'd person in the book. My unease is retrospective and about Randi, sharpened by rereading "See You Later" and "Substitution" now that I've had the water chapter to breathe in — the cup that keeps coming up and not being sipped, the busy hand while Pace talks to Vee, the "he wants to see you naked" delivered with a grin that's doing something I can't fully read yet. I don't distrust Randi outright — the text hasn't forced my hand — but I notice I'm holding her more warily than Vee is, and this chapter's total absence of her, its insistence on a totally other relationship, sharpened that gap rather than closing it.

**Erotic charge.** None in this chapter, and it doesn't need any — this is a cooling-off, a palate cleanse, a chapter about a body that isn't for anyone's gaze for once. That absence is doing thematic work against everything I've just read (the bench, the pointing game, the froyo date), where Vee's body has been variously assessed, catalogued, offered, or admired. Here it's just hers, briefly, underwater.

**Friction as a reader.** None really — it's a tight, controlled scene. If I'm picking at anything, it's that the chapter arrived with zero transition or context after "The Long Way," which is presumably intentional (contrast is the whole point), but for a moment I wondered if I'd missed a scene break inside the chapter rather than a hard cut between chapters.

**Titles.** "Water Wings" — I initially expected something twee or babyish, maybe ironic distance from the childhood swim aid. But by the end it reads more as the thing that used to hold Vee up and doesn't anymore — she doesn't need floaties, doesn't need Cassie beside her past the shallow end, she goes under and finds her own old competence again. It also plays off the leering "you won't need floaties with those" line early on, turning an ugly joke about her body into a quiet reclaiming of an actual, private, competent self underwater. Nicely oblique — didn't give anything away, recolored gently on reflection. The jacket's "the shame she has carried since girlhood" line lands squarely on this chapter; this is the shame chapter, and Cassie is positioned, so far, as its antidote rather than its exploiter.

**What I want next.** I want more Cassie — she's become the character I trust most and want to see more of, especially given how oddly she's been positioned at the margins of Vee's growing orbit with Randi and Pace. I'm curious whether this chapter is a breath before something rougher, or whether Cassie is being set up as a counterweight the book will eventually ask Vee to choose between. Guess only: given the jacket's warning about "a secret choice made about her," I find myself hoping — not predicting — that Cassie is simply outside whatever game Randi and Pace are running, and stays that way.
