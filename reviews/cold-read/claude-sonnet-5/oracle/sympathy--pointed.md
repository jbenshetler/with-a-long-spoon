# Oracle (grounded) — sympathy · pointed

*model: claude-sonnet-5 · probe: sympathy · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Rate your sympathy for each — Vivienne, Randi, Pace — 0-5 (0 = none or active dislike; 5 = deep sympathy, rooting for them). One line each on what earned it; if any is active antipathy rather than just distance, say so.

---

Vivienne: 5 — I was fully on her side from "See You Later" straight through to the end. Her hunger, her shame-voice, her small acts of self-assertion (the mirror walk at the induction, outbutting her mother's voice in the truck), and especially her late-book grief over Pace's silence ("Bare," "Believe Me") had me aching for her constantly. No antipathy at all — even her cruelty at the dance hall or her jealous spiral at New Year's read as forgivable, human wounds, not flaws that cost her my rooting interest.

Randi: 4 — genuine, deep sympathy that kept complicating rather than curdling into dislike. The bathroom-mirror scene in "The Bench," the PPP-loan reveal in "The Outlier," and above all "Fairytale" (the hollow night with John, "she was not brave enough to lose her") gave me real, unshadowed ache for her. What keeps her from a 5 is that I never stopped clocking the technique underneath the tenderness — the extraction at lunches, the scripted "sure, not sorry," staging Vee's reconciliation sex down to the shoes — so my sympathy always comes paired with unresolved unease rather than pure rooting. Not antipathy, though; by the end I wanted good things for her as much as for Vee.

Pace: 3 — real sympathy, especially after "Rock" (Peter, the hallway punishment, Daphne) and "The Usual" (Sheri's blunt read that his withholding might be armor, not virtue), which made him legible as a wounded person rather than an operator. But I never fully let go of the discomfort seeded by "The Pointing Game" and "hills-and-valleys" (his flat "that's not finished" about Randi while gushing about Vee) — that felt like real, page-earned reason to keep him at arm's length. Not dislike, but the most guarded of the three sympathies; I trusted individual scenes with him far more than I trusted the whole of him.
