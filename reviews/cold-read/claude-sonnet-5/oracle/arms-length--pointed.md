**Question asked (verbatim):** Specifically Pace and Randi — were you able to hold them at arm's length as you read, or did they get past your guard and pull you in the way they pull Vee in? 0-5 for each (0 = I stayed fully at arm's length, an observer; 5 = they completely got past me, I was taken in alongside Vee), with what did or didn't let them reach you.

---

**Pace: 4/5** — He got past my guard more than I expected, and more than I was comfortable with in retrospect. From The Bench onward I trusted his hands, his pacing, his consent practice completely, and that trust kept renewing itself even after I *knew* about the pointing game — Leave No Trace's shivering-in-the-rain gesture, A Round's genuine unplanned laugh at "I've never thought about it," Rock's Peter/Daphne disclosure, the dress in Two Towers/A Round, the "May I" register throughout — all of it worked on me as sincere, not just as technique, chapter after chapter. I kept naming my own swoon honestly rather than pretending to stay clinical about it: "I felt the swoon completely," "I wanted them to have that," "I trust him more this chapter than I have all book." What kept me from a full 5 was that the checkpoint knowledge never actually left me — I was always reading his tenderness in stereo, once as felt and once as method, especially after "Gone" (hearing him describe Vee to Randi in bed) and "hills-and-valleys" ("that's not finished," flat and administrative). Later chapters (Seconds, Under the Rug, Believe Me, The Usual) earned real on-page suspicion of him too — the withheld word, "words are cheap," Sheri's flat diagnosis — so by the end I wasn't simply swept up, I was holding both the swoon and the indictment at once, the way the book kept insisting I do. But the swoon was never fake or merely observed — it landed in my body every time, which is why this sits high rather than in the middle.

**Randi: 3/5** — She pulled me in less completely and less durably than Pace, and for a long stretch I was actively catching her technique in real time rather than being taken by it — Standards, How It's Done, One Bite, broken-in, The Practice Room all have me narrating her extraction moves almost clinically ("that's a lock-pick," "that's an inventory being taken, dressed as girl-talk," "I caught her doing it"). I stayed more of an observer with her through most of the book, sympathetic but wary, never fully forgetting the bathroom-mirror vow from chapter one. What changed that was "Fairytale" — getting fully inside her head for the first time with no audience at all cracked me open ("real, uncomplicated sympathy for Randi rather than sympathy-shadowed-by-suspicion," "I ached for her") and retroactively softened how I read her staging afterward (My Friend Randi, Nothing Underneath) even while I kept clocking that she was still directing the scene. So I did get pulled in — genuinely, by the end — but later and less completely than with Pace; for most of the book my guard against her held because the text kept handing me the mechanism in plain sight, and only in the last stretch did her loneliness override my wariness the way it's meant to override Vee's.</text>
