# Oracle (grounded) — pov-reliability · pointed

*model: claude-sonnet-5 · probe: pov-reliability · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** 0-5: how much do you read each POV as feeling-colored rather than objective — a character who loves or wants someone seeing them more flatteringly, or more forgivingly, than they might deserve? (0 = I take each narrator's read of others as straight truth; 5 = I constantly adjust for who's looking and what they feel.) Point to a place you caught yourself doing it.

---

4 — I spent most of this book actively discounting and re-weighting what each POV told me about the other characters, because the narration kept giving me the tools to do it and then rewarding me for using them.

The clearest, earliest lesson was Pace. Read through Vee's eyes in the courtship chapters (the froyo date, the hike, the fitting, "Peekaboo"), his attentiveness reads as pure devotion — and I felt the swoon completely, for real. But I was carrying the earlier chapters ("The Bench," "The Pointing Game") where I'd watched the exact same attentiveness get built and deployed as a method with Randi first. So by the time Vee is marveling at "May I choose" or the hand-sewn dress, I wasn't taking her wonder as objective information about him — I was reading it as Vee's love making his calculation look like grace, and holding my own separate, colder read alongside hers without letting her feeling overwrite mine. I said this explicitly, chapter after chapter: "I don't distrust his tenderness as felt, in the moment, and I still can't fully trust the shape of it."

Randi is where I adjusted hardest and most self-consciously. Every warm gesture toward Vee (the "dear friend" moment, the toe-stroke in the boutique, "you're not greedy, you're finally hungry out loud") I read as two true things running on the same wire — real affection and calculated extraction — because I'd watched her operate (the pointing game, the Brooke interrogation, the practice-room "coaching") with enough craft that I stopped trusting her own account of her motives, or Vee's grateful acceptance of them, as reliable narration of what was actually happening. I caught myself doing this most explicitly in "Fed" and "One Bite," where I noted that Vee's telling of a scene and my own reading of the same beat (Randi's "cost her nothing" aside, the timed "mouse that roars" story) diverged sharply — I was reading Randi's technique in real time while Vee, inside her own gratitude, couldn't see it at all.

The place I'd flag as the sharpest, most deliberate catch, though, is "Believe Me" and the run around it — I noticed my sympathy for Pace actually softening after "The Usual" gave me Sheri's outside, unflattering, unfeeling-for-him read of his silence ("not saying it matters more to you than having her"), and I registered explicitly that this was a different, more reliable data point than either Vee's grief-colored read or Pace's own self-narration, precisely because Sheri has no stake in flattering him. I was consciously triangulating between three colored accounts (Vee's hurt hope, Pace's self-protective logic, Sheri's flat outside judgment) rather than taking any one as truth.

I didn't score this a 5 because there were real stretches — Cassie's chapters, mostly — where I did just take the read as straight truth, because the text never gave me a reason not to; her plainness was the one place I let my guard down completely and trusted a character's perception of others at face value.
