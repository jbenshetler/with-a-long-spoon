# Oracle (grounded) — sympathy · neutral

**Question asked (verbatim):** Whose corner are you in? Who do you feel for, and who leaves you cold or wary — Vee, Randi, Pace?

---

Vee, no contest. From "The Pointing Game" on, I've known she's being chosen rather than met, and every chapter since has just deepened how much I'm rooting for her — the froyo date where she says "this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time," the pool scene with Cassie where her body finally gets to be hers, the induction walk where for once nobody drew the desire out of her, she just reached in and took it. I trust her completely and I ache for her constantly, and by the end — watching her cry alone in a rental car, invent a rival to torture herself with, dress herself in Randi's borrowed courage and Randi's borrowed shoes to go apologize for a fight that wasn't really her fault — I was fully, protectively in her corner. She's earned every bit of that; she's smart, funny, generous to Cassie, and starving in a way that was never her fault.

Randi is the one who moved me the most, and my feelings about her never settled into one place, which I think is the point. The bathroom mirror scene in "The Bench" ("the face had been lying for years") and the Standards interrogation gave me real, deep sympathy for her early — genuine crisis, genuine cost. But "The Pointing Game," "How It's Done," "Sorority," and especially the "sticky fingers" scene in "One Bite" showed me, on the page, a woman who extracts, stages, and scripts the people she loves, sometimes in the same motion as loving them. I stopped being able to call her warmth and her control separable. Then "Fairytale" cracked me back open toward her almost completely — watching her alone with "John," feeling nothing, admitting to herself she's "not brave enough to lose" Vee — that's the chapter that made me stop being just wary of her and start grieving for her too. So: I feel for Randi, genuinely, but I don't fully trust her, and I don't think the book ever let me choose one over the other. She's the character I'm most conflicted about and probably the best-written one in the book for it.

Pace is the one who leaves me coldest, though even that took the whole book to settle. Early on ("The Bench," the hike, "Rock") I trusted his tenderness and technique completely — the apple slices, the boot fix, standing in the rain so Randi/Vee wouldn't be watched. But "The Pointing Game" showed me he runs an experiment on desire and calls it love, "hills-and-valleys" showed me him casually telling Sheri "that's not finished" about Randi in the same breath as rhapsodizing about Vee, and the whole "Seconds"/"Under the Rug"/"Bare"/"Believe Me" run showed me, on the page and without needing my prior suspicion, that "believe what I do, not what I say" is at minimum self-serving and possibly a wall dressed as virtue — Sheri calling that out flat in "The Usual" ("not saying it matters more to you than having her... that feels pretty shitty") did more to crystallize my distrust than any of the engineered-meeting material had. I don't hate him, and the Daphne/Peter chapter ("Rock") gave me real sympathy for where the control came from — but of the three, he's the one I'd least want dating a friend of mine. Cassie, for what it's worth, is the only person in the whole book I trusted without a single reservation from her first line to her last.</text>
