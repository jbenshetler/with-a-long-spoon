**Question asked (verbatim):** The statistics professor and his lectures — the paradox, the error-correcting codes, the outliers. How did he land the FIRST time, and had that changed by his third or fourth lecture? A straight teaching device, or a joke/wink shared with you — and if a joke, did you enjoy him or did he wear thin? 0-5 on how much you enjoyed him by the end (0 = groaned, too on-the-nose; 5 = delighted, wanted more), with what changed your read.

---

Score: 4/5 — delighted, and more so by the end than at the start.

The first time this device showed up was in "All Told," with the Simpson's paradox lecture (parts vs. whole, "short stretches can lie against the whole"). I remember reacting to it as "a lovely bit of structural mischief," delivered deadpan by a professor who "couldn't care less" — and I clocked it as a possible wink from the page rather than pure campus texture, but I held that read loosely, explicitly noting I had no idea if it was meant as commentary or just color. So the first landing worked, but tentatively — I liked it, I didn't fully trust it yet.

It recurred in "The Practice Room" as "outliers... never all the way gone," and there I remember being more guarded about it — I noted I "clocked it as possibly meaningful but didn't want to overweight" it. That's the moment closest to wearing thin for me: a second invocation of the same device, still oblique, and I was starting to brace for it to become a tic — thematic wallpaper standing in for actual plot movement.

Then "The Outlier" cashed it in completely, and that's what moved my rating up rather than down. The lecture stopped being a wink and became load-bearing: the outlier concept isn't just resonant with Randi's situation, it's the literal mechanism by which the reveal (her father's name in the foreclosure data) happens. I specifically remember thinking the chapter "makes you feel the mechanism... before it hands you the label," and that the professor's own throwaway line about arguing "with data, not with analogies" read to me as "almost a joke at the book's own expense" — the book acknowledging its own device with a wink, right as it stopped being decorative and started doing real work. I noted explicitly that this didn't feel cheap despite being "almost too on the nose," because the chapter earned the label before handing it to me.

So the arc for me was: charming-but-uncommitted → slightly wary of repetition → fully won over once the third use paid off structurally rather than just thematically. I don't have any memory of an "error-correcting codes" lecture specifically — that's not something that registered in my reading record, so I can't speak to it. But the paradox/outlier throughline, taken as a whole, is one of the more satisfying small-scale motifs in the book for me: it never became a tic because each appearance did more work than the last, ending as plot rather than metaphor.
