**Question asked (verbatim):** Put yourself in each of their shoes. Do you get why they did what they did — could you see yourself doing the same in their situation?

---

Putting myself in each of their shoes, honestly:

**Vee** — completely, yes. Every choice she makes tracks from a hunger I recognize: a girl trained by her mother's voice to distrust her own wanting, finally handed total, specific attention, and unable to stop reaching for it even when some quieter part of her clocks the wrongness (Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee," the "goodbye kiss" she stops bracing for, the practice-room bathroom moment where she catches her own hand). I could absolutely see myself doing what she does — narrating myself into the best possible version of the story to my friends, keeping "the middle" for the listener who won't flinch, editing a cropped photo for my parents. None of that reads as culpable to me. It reads as ordinary self-protection dressed up as ordinary joy, and I never once lost sympathy for her, even at her most willfully blind (the "I did it because I wanted to" defense in "Off Six-Fourteen," which she's saying about a scene I watched get built for her thirty feet away).

**Randi** — I get her, and it disturbed me more than Vee's choices did, because I could see the logic of it working even while hating where it led. A woman who's spent her life being seen and never known, who found in Pace the one place safety and exposure coexisted, and who then can't let herself simply have that safely — she has to turn her own wanting into a gift for someone else before she'll admit it's hers (the "Yes... Yeah, I like her" moment on the quad is the whole mechanism in miniature). Could I see myself doing what she does — engineering someone's happiness while also mining her for material, redirecting real guilt into stagecraft, coaching a friend's reconciliation sex down to the shoes? Honestly, some of it, yes — the impulse to manage a friend's pain into something fixable, the inability to just say "I'm falling for you" instead of building elaborate architecture around it. But the deliberate extraction (the "nobody measures over a bra" lock-pick, the "sticky fingers" ambiguity she never resolves) is a level of calculation I don't think I'd let myself do, or at least I hope not — and "Fairytale" made me believe she pays for it, that the control is also a cage she built for herself.

**Pace** — the hardest of the three for me, and where my empathy kept getting interrupted by real judgment. The Rock chapter gave me an origin I understood completely — a boy whose love of math got turned into a punishment, whose first relationship at seventeen he still narrates as the best two years of his life because the alternative is unbearable — and I could see, structurally, how a person built like that ends up believing "asking before taking" and "leaving the room to choose" is the whole of ethics, full stop. But running the pointing game, keeping Randi "not finished" while building Vee a life in parallel, and above all the "words are cheap, believe what I do" wall — I don't think I could do that and still think of myself as the tender man he clearly believes he is. I understand the mechanism (defer feeling rather than spend it, per "he had long practice at deciding a thing could keep"), but understanding it isn't the same as forgiving it, and by "Believe Me" I'd stopped extending him the benefit of the doubt I'd been carrying since the bench.
