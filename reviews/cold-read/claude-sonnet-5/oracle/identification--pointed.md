# Oracle (grounded) — identification · pointed

**Question asked (verbatim):** For each — Vivienne, Randi, Pace — 0-5: how much could you see yourself making the same choices in their circumstances? (0 = alien to me, I'd never; 5 = honestly, I'd probably do the same.) Name the one choice of theirs that most tests your answer.

---

**Vivienne — 3.** A lot of her choices are ones I recognize completely: reaching for warmth she's been starved of, editing her story for an audience, letting shame and desire duel and desire win once in a while, choosing not to press a painful question rather than break something good. I could see myself doing most of that. The choice that tests it hardest is the very end of the run I read — after the "Believe Me" wall and a month of silence, she lets Randi dress her, script her, and hand-deliver her back into Pace's arms, and the second he's tender again she drops the actual demand ("nothing needed saying now"). I'd like to think I'd hold the line on the thing I said I needed. I'm honestly not sure I would either, which is why it's a 3 and not higher — I recognize the impulse to let relief overrule the unresolved wound, I just don't love it in myself as much as the book lets me love it in her.

**Randi — 1.** Most of what she does toward Vee I find myself unable to imagine choosing, even generously interpreted — not the wanting (that I understand), but the machinery around it: coaching Vee's every crisis into a costume and a script, extracting confessions at exactly the moment someone's most open, narrating Vee's body to Pace in bed. The one choice that most tests the rating is the earliest and the most originating: standing on that quad, hearing Pace ask "her?" and answering "Yes" before she'd let herself feel it as her own want, then orchestrating the "accidental" collision with Vee within minutes. That's the choice I keep coming back to — picking a stranger out like produce and staging a friendship as a delivery mechanism. "Fairytale" and the outlier chapter made me love and grieve for her, and even so I can't get past that founding choice; it's the one thing in the whole book I felt was actually alien to me, not just uncomfortable.

**Pace — 2.** His hands-on tenderness, his consent practice, his patience — I can imagine wanting to be that person, and some of his restraint (the closed bedroom door, "not tonight" about the scar, walking away from the bar fight instead of defending himself) reads as choices I'd hope to make. What tests the rating hard is the ride with Sheri: asked point-blank whether "two of them at once" is complicated, he just confirms it, flatly, present tense — "Randi. That's not finished" — in the same breath as rhapsodizing about Vee's hands on his furniture. That casual, administrative doubleness, sustained across months while he's building Vee a dress by hand and teaching her to trust him completely, is the choice I can't extend myself into. I understand withholding the word out of old damage (Daphne, the hallway, the parents he won't go back to) — that I could imagine doing. Running two women in parallel without either knowing, and treating it as a manageable open item rather than a crisis, is where I part ways with him.
