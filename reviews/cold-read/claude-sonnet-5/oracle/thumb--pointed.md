# Oracle (grounded) — thumb · pointed

*model: claude-sonnet-5 · probe: thumb · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Did you ever feel the author's thumb on the scale — telegraphing, over-explaining? 0-5, quote it.

---

1 — barely, and only in small, forgivable moments. Chapter after chapter I logged "Friction: none" almost as a refrain; this book mostly trusted me to do the work (the dramatic irony, the withheld motive, the unresolved ambiguity of Randi) without pointing at it. The handful of places I actually caught the prose leaning on itself were minor. In "The Induction," right at the close of Vee's stage-walk, the narration says: "whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing" — I flagged that in the moment as the one spot where the book told me what to feel about an image (her private thrill at being seen) instead of trusting the walk itself to say it; small, didn't break the chapter. In "A Recognized Method," I noted the apology chapter resolved Vee's guilt a little too cleanly in Pace's favor — the blonde cast as pure villain, the bartender's vindication doing the same work twice — so I wasn't given much room to wonder if Pace's chivalry was itself uncomplicated; I went along with it but registered the one-sidedness. And in "Lesson," Meg's line "Meg, who followed the Pace situation in installments like her shows" struck me as a slightly cute, winking compression rather than a natural thought. Beyond that — nothing. Even big engineered turns (the bench reveal, the pointing game, Randi's whole extraction technique) landed as earned plot machinery I could watch happen, not as the author nudging me toward a verdict; several times I explicitly noted the book let ambiguous moments (Randi's "cost her nothing" aside, the "sticky fingers" line, the "It doesn't" flinch) sit completely unresolved rather than glossing them for me. So: real but faint — a 1, not a 0, because I did catch it those few times, but nowhere near a heavy hand.
