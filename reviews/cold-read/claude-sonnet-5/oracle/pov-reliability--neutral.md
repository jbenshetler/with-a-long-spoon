# Oracle (grounded) — pov-reliability · neutral

*model: claude-sonnet-5 · probe: pov-reliability · tier: neutral · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** When you're inside a character's head and they're describing someone they love or want — Vee on Pace, or on Randi — do you take that as how the person really is, or as colored by what the narrator feels? Walk me through how much you trust each viewpoint's read of the others.

---

Honestly, by the end I trusted almost nobody's read of anybody straight — every viewpoint in this book turned out to be a lens, not a window, and the book trained me to feel that gap chapter by chapter rather than telling me about it.

Vee's descriptions of Pace I learned to read as sincere but radically incomplete — not because she's an unreliable narrator in the unreliable-narrator-twist sense, but because she's working with a fraction of the information the reader has. When she calls his tenderness "unhurried, courtly, exact," I believe that's exactly how it feels in her body, in the moment — the book never once undercuts the sensory truth of what she's experiencing. But her read of *what that tenderness means* — that it's for her, singular, that "no man had ever wanted her body like this one did" — I couldn't hold as fact, because I'd watched (in The Pointing Game, in Gone, in hills-and-valleys) the same instrument deployed on Randi, sometimes in the same week. So with Vee-on-Pace I ended up trusting the texture completely and distrusting the interpretation almost entirely — I'd feel the swoon she felt and then immediately hold a second, colder reading underneath it that she had no access to.

Vee's read of Randi went through the most dramatic arc of trust for me. Early on I mostly took her adoration at face value because I had nothing to contradict it with directly (just the dramatic irony from the earlier Pace/Randi chapters). But once the book started letting me into the mechanics on the page — "nobody measures over a bra," the toe-stroke in the boutique, the practice-room "does he know she's here" — I started reading Vee's "Randi loves me, Randi is the safest person I've told everything to" as true-and-incomplete the same way I read her feelings about Pace: real affection on both sides, but Vee mistaking control for intimacy because she has no other model for being that thoroughly attended to. I never stopped believing Vee's feelings were genuine. I stopped believing her diagnosis of what she was feeling from.

Where I actually trusted a character's read of another person without qualification: Cassie's, always. Cassie has no stake, no performance, no third-party audience — "he looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt," "that's not a boy, that's a man," "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to" — every one of those landed as flatly true and none of them were ever contradicted by later chapters. Cassie became my calibration instrument: when her reaction to something matched my unease, I trusted my unease; when she wasn't worried, I let myself relax a beat.

I trusted Randi's and Pace's reads of each other more than either's read of Vee, oddly — in "Gone" and "hills-and-valleys," when they talk about each other in private, unwatched, that felt like the one place in the book where I was getting something close to unvarnished truth, because neither of them had an audience to perform for in that moment. It's when either of them talks about Vee — to each other, to Vee herself, or in Randi's case to Vee's face across a lunch table — that I felt the performance-layer thickest, precisely because Vee (or the idea of Vee) was always the third party being managed.

So the short version: sensory/emotional texture, I trusted at whatever level of POV access I was given, always. Interpretation — what a feeling *means* about the other person, or about the relationship's shape — I trusted in direct proportion to how little audience the speaker had at that moment. Cassie's reads I trusted outright. Vee's reads of both Pace and Randi I trusted as true feeling, false frame. And Randi's and Pace's reads of each other, spoken to Vee, I trusted least of all — by the end I assumed anything either of them told Vee about themselves or each other had already been shaped for what it would do to her.
