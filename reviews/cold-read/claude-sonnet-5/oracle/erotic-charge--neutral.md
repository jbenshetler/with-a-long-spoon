**Question asked (verbatim):** Is the book working on you — where does it run hot, where does it go slack?

---

Yes — overwhelmingly, for most of the book. It's one of the most sustained "working on me" experiences I can point to, and I can name exactly where the engine runs hottest and where it idles.

**Where it runs hottest:**

The dramatic-irony machinery is the real motor, and it never once stopped working on me from "The Bench" through at least the mid-book. Knowing Randi and Pace built the "chance" meeting with Vee before she existed to them — watching "The Pointing Game" happen and then getting to rewatch its consequences in "See You Later," "Substitution," "The Long Way" — gave every subsequent tender gesture a double charge I couldn't turn off. That doubling is where the book is at its best: chapters like "A Round," "Leave No Trace," "Two Towels," "Peekaboo" hit me as pure swoon *and* pure unease simultaneously, and the friction between those two readings is what kept me leaning in rather than settling into one register. The heat scenes themselves (the bench, "Famished," "We Find Out") are genuinely, physically effective — explicit without going slack into choreography, because the interiority (shame, permission, her mother's voice) never drops out even at the most graphic moments.

The other place it ran hot for me, unexpectedly, was Randi's interiority — the bathroom mirror scene in ch1, "Standards," "The Outlier," and especially "Fairytale," where I finally got her alone with no audience. Those are the chapters where the book stopped being clever-mechanism and started actually hurting, and I trusted it most there.

The slow accumulation of the unsaid "I love you" across "Seconds," "Under the Rug," "Bare," "Believe Me" also worked on me hard — that's the book converting structural dread into an ordinary, universal ache (loving someone who withholds one specific word) that didn't even need my outside knowledge to land. It's the one long stretch where the suspense wasn't "what does Vee not know" but just plain heartbreak, and it hit harder for being plain.

**Where it went slack:**

The middle-book run of Vee/Pace domestic-warmth chapters — roughly "Two Towels" through "Turned Up," and again the "All the Time"/"The New Ordinary"/"In His Hands" cluster — is where I felt myself coasting rather than gripped. Individually each chapter is well-made and I said so at the time, but stacked together they're mostly the same beat (tender gesture, her shame dissolves a little more, repeat), and I noted more than once wanting the next complication to arrive. The book seemed to sense this too — it kept cutting back to Randi or dropping a new revelation (the scar, "Peter," Sheri's bike ride) right when I was starting to get restless, which mostly worked, but I did feel real fatigue in a couple of those stretches, waiting for momentum rather than riding it.

The other place I went cooler rather than hotter was anywhere the book told me the theme instead of showing it — a few closing lines ("whether that was pride or only the same heat still running..." in "The Induction," the slightly over-explained "no witness" framing in "We Find Out") tipped from image into statement. Minor, but noticeable against a book that's otherwise so good at letting me do the work.

Net: it worked on me continuously and at high intensity for the majority of the read, cooled into pleasant-but-repetitive warmth for stretches in the middle, and then re-ignited hard in the last third once the unsaid word became the engine and Randi's own ache got full daylight in "Fairytale."
