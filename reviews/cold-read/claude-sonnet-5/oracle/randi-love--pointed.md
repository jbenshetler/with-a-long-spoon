# Oracle (grounded) — randi-love · pointed

*model: claude-sonnet-5 · probe: randi-love · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** How strongly do you sense Randi's feelings for Vee run past friendship — into love, or wanting her? 0-5, with what in your memory puts it there (or the absence of any).

---

5 — by the end I don't think this is ambiguous anymore; the book moved me from suspicion to certainty.

For most of the middle of the book I was holding it as a genuine open question, the way the text seemed to want me to: the toe-stroke in "Sorority" ("felt it go all the way up the length of her"), the unnamed stillness in "Gone" when Pace calls Vee beautiful ("Not the loose stillness of after... the kind that takes holding"), the goodbye kiss escalating chapter by chapter until it's "only what they did" in "broken-in," the bathroom scene in "One Bite" where Vee can't finish because she imagines being looked at by Randi specifically, with fondness. All of that read as real but deniable — desire that could still be read as intense friendship, performance, or appetite-by-proxy, and I kept it open on purpose because the text kept it open.

"Fairytale" is what closed the question for me. That's the chapter where I finally got Randi alone, with no audience, no Vee, no Pace to perform for — and her own unguarded interior voice says it plainly: watching a genuinely generous, "faultless" man fail to touch her the way an ordinary Tuesday with Vee does, and then, explicitly, "she was not brave enough to lose her." That's not a friend's fondness. That's a person naming, to herself, in privacy, that she's in love and afraid of what naming it out loud would cost her. It also recontextualized everything before it backward — the practice-room coaching, the shoe-fitting, the endless engineered intimacy — as also, underneath the manipulation, genuine longing.

So: a 5. Not "runs past friendship into something" — by the book's own most private evidence, it already is love, freely admitted by Randi to no one but herself, and withheld from Vee out of fear rather than indifference.
