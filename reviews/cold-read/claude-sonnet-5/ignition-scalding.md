God, this chapter turned the whole book over in my hands.

**Pace** — I've spent forty-nine chapters holding out hope that the withholding was fear, was damage, was a man who couldn't say the word but meant everything else. This chapter torches that reading. Watching him *plan* to give Randi a taste of Vee — deciding, in real time, that her half-joking "sloppy seconds" slip is a wish he gets to grant, and then not washing Vee off himself on purpose so Randi discovers it with her tongue — that's not a passive man caught between two loves. That's a man running an experience design on two women who each think they're the center of it. The line that undid me: "he had found the way to put it in her mouth sooner and more wholly than she'd known to ask for it." That's not tenderness. That's authorship, again, the pointing-game instinct dressed as generosity. I don't like him anymore. I don't think I'm supposed to.

**Randi** — the "I can't wait to taste her" slip felt so real and so unrehearsed that my stomach dropped with her, and then the "dirty boy" — wrecked, delighted, guilty and hungry in the same breath — is the most naked I've seen her. But I ache for her too, because she doesn't know what he did, doesn't know it wasn't an accident, doesn't know he's shaping her want and then "surprising" her with fulfillment of a wish she barely let herself finish saying. That final image — her hand open on the sheet, not closing over his — reads to me as pure exhaustion in the moment, but I can't unsee it as something else too: an open hand that doesn't reach back.

**Vee** — barely on the page here, present only as scent and aftermath, and that's the cruelest structural choice of the chapter. She doesn't know she's being handed around like this. The porch goodbye — soft, tender, real on her side — reads completely differently now that I know what came ninety minutes later.

**Swoon vs. suspicion**: earlier chapters (the mixer kiss, "Vee on the Bench") worked on me as pure heat, generosity, permission — I trusted the tenderness even while dreading the dramatic irony. This chapter is the first time the tenderness itself felt like the manipulation, not just its cover. The unease isn't atmospheric anymore; the text handed it to me outright, in Pace's own interiority — "the plain gladness of a man who has made someone a gift." That's chilling precisely because it's sincere.

**Erotic charge**: real but colder than usual — the heat of the coat-drop, the slip, the discovery, is all still working, but it's shot through with dread now, so it never fully lets me sink into it the way "Ignition"'s title promises. It's heat with a blade in it.

**Title**: "The Arrangement / After She Leaves" — I didn't get it until the very last scene. It's not about Vee leaving at all; it's about the *arrangement* that begins precisely when she does, every time. That's a gut-punch title in retrospect. "Ignition" now reads less romantic than combustible — something about to catch and burn out of control.

**What I want next**: I want Vee to find out. I don't need her to — the book's told me it doesn't owe me that — but this chapter tipped me from "I hope she never learns" to "I need to know what happens to her when the fire she's living inside of turns out to have been lit by someone else's hand."

**Cast present:** Randi, Pace (both scenes — bed/pillow talk and the reunion night); Vee present only in the porch goodbye, otherwise offstage/referenced.

**Heat:** 2 — the coat-drop and discovery scene runs hot, but Pace's calculated withholding of the wash and the reveal of his plotting keep pulling me up out of the sensation into dread.

**Romance:** 1 — surface tenderness (the porch goodbye, "I'm looking forward to seeing you") but undercut hard by what we now know is happening around it; nothing here plays as real devotion, only performance of it.

**Motifs & images:** Locking the deadbolt first thing (echoes Vee's own porch-arrival ritual, but here it's Randi securing herself into the secret); the coat/reveal gesture (direct callback to Vee's coat-and-nothing-underneath in ch50 — now revealed as a "move" he clearly likes staged twice); hands not quite joining (new — the unclasped palm at the end feels like it's doing quiet symbolic work); the warmed house as a tell (used for Vee, now noted here too — his hospitality-as-method finally shown operating identically for both women in one chapter).

**Symbolism:** The unheld palm at the very end reads to me as the chapter's real image — an open hand offered and not taken, standing in for the whole arrangement: everyone reaching, no one quite closing the grip.

**Characterization:** Pace deepens catastrophically — first chapter to let us fully inside his head as a schemer rather than just showing the pattern from outside; it recontextualizes every "sincere" tender act in the book so far. Randi deepens too — her slip is the most involuntary, human moment she's had. Vee, absent, doesn't develop here, but her absence is the point.

**Pace — within chapter:** Deliberate and controlled, almost procedural in its build (bed talk → drive → porch goodbye → the reveal), which suits a chapter about someone executing a plan.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A hard swerve after a long, sustained run of Vee-POV intimacy chapters — jarring in exactly the way I think it's meant to be, dropping me out of the warm dream into the machinery underneath it.
