# Cold read — Lesson

## Reader reaction

God, this one hurt in the good way — the specific, particular way a jealousy spiral hurts when you're inside a POV that's narrating its own worst behavior and can't stop.

**How I feel about each character right now.** Pace stays likable here, maybe more so — patient teacher on the concrete pad, funny ("There's no sorry unless you take off a foot"), and then genuinely wounded by her line at the bar — "The ease went out of his face. Not anger — the good mood just draining off him." He answers her cruelty straight, doesn't retaliate, and leaves quietly rather than making a scene. The bartender's testimony (he gets drunk girls home, doesn't take advantage, "wasn't him they were jealous of, honey") reads as trustworthy — she's not selling anything, she's annoyed at Vee and says so. So my read on Pace actually firms up toward *good* here, even while the previous chapters' unease about his method with Vee (Randi coaching him, the tutor history) sits unresolved underneath. Vee I feel for hard — I was inside her spiral (the blonde's dig, the "floozies," "she'd spread her legs like a slut") and it's ugly and recognizable and the text doesn't soften it or excuse it. She knows she's doing it — "She knew what she was doing, and she did it anyway" — and that self-awareness without the power to stop is exactly the kind of interior mess I read this book for.

**Swoon / pull.** The dance lesson itself is the swoon — unhurried, exacting, funny, physical without being sexual, which somehow makes it more intimate. "Give me something to push back against... Being jelly doesn't work" lands as both dance instruction and about forty other things at once, and the chapter lets that resonance sit there without underlining it. The moment she stops guessing and the lead just arrives — "The pattern came through his body before it arrived in her feet" — is genuinely thrilling, a small physical epiphany rendered with real precision.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** The blonde at the bar is a plant, deliberately — designed to detonate Vee, and it works exactly as intended, which made me suspicious of *her* motives even as I understood Vee couldn't see it in the moment. Pace himself reads as trustworthy this chapter — the bartender's unprompted testimony is the strongest external validation he's gotten in the book. My carried suspicion from prior chapters (his coordination with Randi, the tutor pattern) doesn't get addressed at all here, which is its own kind of honest — the chapter isn't trying to resolve it, just letting a different truth about him play out in a different arena.

**Erotic charge.** Almost none, and that's fine — this is a relationship chapter, not a heat chapter. The closest thing to charge is retrospective and painful: her lying in his shirt at the end, holding both the shame and "his hand at her shoulder blade... the moment the dance took her" at once.

**Friction as a reader.** None that felt like a thumb on the scale — the escalation from public embarrassment to private cruelty to regret felt earned beat by beat. If anything I wanted one more beat of *why* the blonde's comment landed so hard — but the text gives me that: "She'd let herself feel like an exception. She was looking at the rule." That's plenty.

**Titles.** "Lesson" is doing careful double work — the dance lesson literally, but by the end it's what Vee has to learn about herself and about jealousy, and possibly what she's about to go ask Randi to teach her. Doesn't give anything away up front; recolors nicely by the end. *A Polite Invitation* — the dance-hall crowd's warmth toward Pace, the "hey Pace," the door held, register as invitations extended and, this time, badly received by Vee's own hand. Feels apt without being pointed at me.

**What I want next.** I want to see what "go to Randi's" means — apology, confession, something else entirely. Guess from here: this might be Vee going to Randi not just to process the fight but because some part of her now associates Randi with knowing how to be wanted correctly, given the established coaching dynamic — but that's speculation, the text only gives me "she was going to fix it."
