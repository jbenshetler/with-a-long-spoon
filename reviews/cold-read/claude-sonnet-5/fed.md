## Reader reaction

There's a real pull to this chapter, and it's the double-vision of Randi. On one hand: the warmth on the page is genuine and it *works* on me — "Good," Randi says, and reaches over and holds Vee's hand, and I felt that land as real tenderness. Randi's whole affect — "There's my girl," the delight, "you're lucky" — reads as generous, attentive, exactly the friend Vee's been starved for. And yet I can't unsee what the earlier chapters taught me: this is coaxed confession again, dressed as intimacy. "Which is very pretty, and you are not getting away with it" — that's Randi steering, closing the loop, refusing to let Vee's edit stand. She catches the ladle-trick in real time (the "and then, well" wave of the hand) and pries at it — gently, warmly, expertly. I don't know if that's love or appetite or both, and the not-knowing is exactly what's unsettling me. It never tips into cold; it stays plausibly, maybe truly warm. But I notice the *pattern*: nail polish, underwear, "you knew how that night was going to end" — Randi builds Vee's confession move by move the way an interviewer builds a witness, and each step is disguised as being seen.

Vee herself: I love her more here than almost anywhere so far — "I wanted it, and I took it, for once" is the most naked, unguarded thing she's said on the page, and it's beautiful, earned, hard-won against a lifetime of not letting herself want. That she gives Randi *more* than she gave Cassie, that she "went further than she'd gone with anyone" — I feel the intimacy of that as real, even while I clock that Randi is the one person actively pulling for it. Both things are true at once and I'm not going to resolve them.

The kiss at the curb — "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them, the way she had the first time" — got me. Quiet, devastating little detail: she's stopped hiding it from herself, or from the world, or both. That's a turn, and I don't know yet what it's a turn *toward*.

Erotic charge: mostly retrospective and verbal here — Vee's retelling of the sheet, the kitchen, "hungry for me... singular" — has real heat in the telling, but the chapter's charge is really about the *extraction* of the story, not the story itself. The peak for me isn't sex, it's "That face isn't about a color, is it" — that's the most charged line in the chapter, and it's not sexual at all, it's exposure.

The mimosa-pricing beat is a small lovely continuity touch — Vee doing arithmetic under a brunch menu, then the second mimosa she "did not price at all." That's a real, earned marker of how far she's loosened.

Title "Fed" — lands on multiple things at once: Pace literally cooking for her (again, retold), Randi feeding her the questions that draw the confession out, Vee feeding Randi the story she wants, and maybe Vee herself being fed — attention, appetite, permission — in a way she's clearly starved for. It doesn't give anything away, it just sits under the whole chapter once I'm done, quietly apt.

I want to know what Randi is really after, and I both dread and want to see it play out — I'm rooting for Vee's happiness while braced for what all this careful harvesting might be for.
