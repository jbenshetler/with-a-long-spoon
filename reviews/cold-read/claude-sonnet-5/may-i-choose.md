# Cold read — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: water-wings*

## Reader reaction

His hands, again — I noticed it before I could stop myself: "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it, one more thing he knew that she didn't." I flinched slightly at that line landing right after the pattern of his hands from the froyo chapter — the deliberateness of him is still there, quietly, under everything. But I want to be honest about the rest of this chapter: it worked on me. It worked on me hard. The text, the "may I choose," the ordering in a language she can't read, the bread torn and folded and handed to her — this is seduction as generosity, and it's beautifully rendered as generosity even while I'm carrying forward the knowledge that this whole courtship began as an engineered "collision." I'm split right down the middle and the book knows it — that's the ache of reading Pace now: every tender, exact thing he does is genuinely tender *and* exact, both at once, no contradiction resolved.

Vee is the whole chapter, really, and I love her more with each one. The specificity of her hunger — literal (she skipped lunch, the hollow that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon") and figurative (wanting things with no names for them) — is the best writing in the book so far for me. "Content for once not to know" is such a precise description of relief-through-surrender that I felt it in my own chest. And then the thread-dropping: "she didn't need to follow the words exactly to be inside the warmth of them" — that's the whole book's argument about her, I think, laid bare without being told to me. She is starving for a place to stop being vigilant, and he is, without meaning any harm that she could name, the person supplying that place while also being the person who built the collision that put her there. I don't know if he knows that's what he's supplying, or if it's just what he does with everyone.

Cassie is lovely here too, brief but exactly right — "That's not a boy. That's a man" landing with real weight because Cassie doesn't tease about things that matter. I trust Cassie completely and she remains the only unclouded relationship in the book.

**Swoon / pull** — God, yes, this chapter swooned me. The hand-holding passage undid me a little: "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly, and was only now where it belonged." That's a genuinely gorgeous sentence and I felt the swoon and let myself have it, while also hearing, underneath, the machinery I already know is there — the fact that a hand feeling like it's "finally where it belongs" is exactly the kind of feeling Pace is expert at manufacturing. Both readings sit in me at once, neither canceling the other.

The kiss reversal at the end is the erotic peak — not explicit, not even a described kiss, but the turn itself: her own rule breaking against her own will, "I'm making an exception," her feet not moving, the taut hand between them. That's heat that comes entirely from restraint and reversal, and it landed clean.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — My suspicion of Pace hasn't grown this chapter exactly, but it hasn't relaxed either — it's just present, quietly, under real tenderness that the text is not undercutting. The hand-pattern detail is the one thing that felt like a small deliberate wink at me, the reader who's been tracking it since the froyo scene. Christine's warmth toward him ("something in her eased at who'd walked in") reads as one more unclouded data point in his favor — people who know him longer than Vee has clearly like him — which complicates rather than resolves my wariness.

**Erotic charge** — Working beautifully, and almost entirely non-explicit — heat carried through food, hands, language she can't parse, the taut arm at the end. Peaks at the hand-holding and the closing line.

**Friction** — None really. If anything the "always the right hand" detail felt almost too neat as a callback, a little bit of the author's thumb on the scale reminding me to keep suspecting him — but it's subtle enough that I'll allow it; it reads like an observation Vee herself might make if she were paying that kind of attention, which she almost is.

**Titles** — "May I Choose" is his line, offered as courtesy over a menu, and then it recolors backward over the whole evening — he's choosing the food, the pace, the restraint at the tree, and she's the one who ultimately chooses to break her own rule at the end. It's a lovely double edge: his choosing and her choosing meet right at the climax of the chapter. The book title *A Polite Invitation* fits this chapter almost too well — literally an invitation to dinner, polite to the point of never asking her to answer right away — and now I feel the tagline's shape (whatever it is) pointing at exactly this: politeness as the vehicle for something much less neutral.

**What I want next** — I want to see this consummated, and I want to see whether the encounter's tenderness holds up once desire gets more explicit — and I'm bracing, not dreading exactly, for the moment Vee's trust in "nothing bad will happen" gets tested. Guess from here: the "may I choose" dynamic will recur as a template for consent between them, and I suspect the book is setting up to ask what it means that her surrender feels like relief when the surrender was, in some sense, engineered from the start.
