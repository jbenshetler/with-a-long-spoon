# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter already carrying something the earlier chapters gave me and Vee doesn't have: I know Pace picked her off a sidewalk with Randi at his side, laughing about it, testing Randi's wanting against a lineup of strangers. I know "her" was a game before it was a girl. So every gentle, exact thing Pace does in "May I Choose" — the "may I choose" at the menu, the not-asking her to answer that first text right away, the walking-her-home-the-long-way, the hand taken like it was "already settled" — lands on me twice. Once as pure, unguarded swoon, because it is genuinely lovely writing about being courted well. And once as a small cold finger down my spine, because I watched this same attentiveness — the noticing of what side a hand favors, the deliberateness about what's offered and when, the letting someone else believe the choosing is theirs — get built, piece by piece, on Randi first. Pace ordering for Vee ("was there anything she wouldn't eat," building the meal "as he went") is almost a direct echo of the honeycrisp apple sliced thin with the cut sides up because he'd noticed she ate them faster that way. That's the same man. I don't distrust him exactly — nothing on the page here is anything but generous — but I can't unknow how he watches, catalogs, and gives back exactly what will land, and how much of that is also control dressed as care.

Vee, though — I just love her in this chapter. The internal furniture of her (the mother's voice that polices what she eats and wears, the "rule" about not kissing a man she's known a couple of days, the reflexive smoothing-over of anything that feels wrong, like Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee" a few chapters back) is so specific and lived-in that I trust every beat of her hunger here — literal and otherwise. The dinner scene is the loveliest thing in the chapter: "the hollow only opened wider, asked for more before she'd swallowed... something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's not really about hummus. It's about a girl who's been rationing herself her whole life discovering someone who will just feed her, without commentary, without a bill coming due — "she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong... and she let it not be there." I ached for her there, cleanly, no irony needed — that line does exactly what it wants to do to me.

Cassie remains the person I trust most without reservation. "That's not a boy. That's a man" lands with real weight because Cassie doesn't do bits, doesn't flatter, doesn't perform warmth the way Randi does — so when she says something kind, it's load-bearing. I keep collecting small evidence that Cassie sees more than she says (the earlier "Goodbye, Vee," the flat look at Randi's texting) and I want to know what she's not saying about Randi specifically, because I have information Cassie doesn't and I badly want her radar to catch up.

Randi isn't physically in this chapter, but she's present as a felt absence — I kept waiting for her, the way you wait for a hand you know is about to come down. Her not being there let me watch Vee fall for Pace uncomplicated by her, for one chapter, and that itself felt engineered, almost merciful of the author toward me as a reader — a breath before whatever's coming.

Swoon / pull: The elevator scene got me — Pace already there, freshly showered "for this," the doors closing on her before she notices, him having "the grace not to show it" that he clocked her staring. And the closing kiss is beautifully built: her stated rule, the old reflex "the part of her that always caught the thread before it could drop," breaking anyway — "I'm making an exception." I felt the swoon completely; it's earned, unhurried, exactly Pace's register (asking, never taking, "he didn't do anything she'd braced for"). I wanted her to have this. I also can't fully let go of the game on the quad three chapters back while I want it.

Trust vs. suspicion: My suspicion isn't about Pace being cruel — nothing here suggests cruelty, and the text is warm and consensual throughout. It's about the asymmetry of knowledge, which the page has fully earned by showing me the pointing game in explicit, deliberate detail before letting me watch its result play out as a "spontaneous" romance. The jacket told me this outright ("she doesn't know there's a plan") but it's the prose itself — the apple-slice echo, the ordering-for-her echo — that makes the suspicion feel structural rather than imported.

Erotic charge: Almost none, deliberately — this chapter is nearly heat-0, and it works, because the hunger is displaced into food and hands and the ache of being fully attended to. The one moment with real charge is small and non-sexual on its face: him licking a drip of dip off his own hand mid-sentence, "unthinking," and her losing the thread of what he was saying. That's more erotic to me than a lot of overt content would be, because it's about her attention snagging on his body without permission.

Friction: None that felt like a thumb on the scale — if anything I noticed how patiently the chapter withholds explanation (we never learn where the restaurant is, what they talked about, what the "beautiful nonsense" on the menu is), matching Vee's own dissolving attention. That restraint felt intentional and good.

Titles: "May I Choose" does real work — it's literally his line at the table, but it's also the whole book's argument in miniature, matching the jacket's "he asks before he takes and leaves her the room to choose." The ache is that the title's surface reading (his courtesy) and its darker reading (whether any of this was ever really hers to choose, given the pointing game) sit on top of each other without the text tipping its hand. I don't think it gave anything away I didn't already have from the jacket and from Standards — but it sharpened the question rather than answering it. The series title, With a Long Spoon, keeps nagging at me now in a way it didn't during The Bench — a long spoon is for supping with the devil, and I keep turning that against Pace's careful, patient hands.

What I want next: I want to see Randi and Vee in the same room again now that I've watched Vee fall this hard, and I want to watch Randi watch it. I have no idea yet whether Randi feels anything like guilt, and the book has given me no reason to assume she does — I'm holding that open. Guess from here, clearly marked as a guess: I suspect the dinner-table intimacy Vee is heading toward with Randi (per the jacket, "lunches too intimate to name") is going to complicate this romance in ways Vee can't see coming, but I don't know the shape of it yet.
