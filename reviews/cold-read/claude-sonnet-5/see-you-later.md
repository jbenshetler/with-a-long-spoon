# Cold read — See You Later

*scene: scenes/see-you-later.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: the-pointing-game*

## Reader reaction

This chapter pulled a full switch on me and I didn't see it coming — we're inside someone brand new, Vee, and for the length of the chapter I forgot Pace and his pointing game existed. That's not a complaint; it's the chapter doing its job. But I read every warm thing Randi does here with a second track running underneath, because I know something Vee doesn't: that the "accidental" collision on the lawn was choreographed by Randi with, as I remember it, trained precision, and that Pace steered the whole afternoon that produced Vee as a name in the first place. So when Randi says "*Oh my god, we should sit together*" and later "Love you, girl. See you later, okay?" — lines that land on Vee like sunlight, like being chosen — I can't take them at face value the way she does. That gap is the whole chapter for me.

**How I feel about each character right now.** Vee is instantly, achingly sympathetic — her interiority is rendered exactly the way I want it: the not-looking-at-the-door she's decided three times not to do, the running of "we should sit together" both ways "enough times to wear a groove," the mother's voice that arrives pre-formed in her own mouth calling other women "floozies" and then immediately complicating itself with a longer, hungrier beat of looking she won't examine. I like her enormously and I ache for her a little already, because I can see the shape of what she wants (Randi, plainly, though she'd never let herself say it that flatly) and I can see that she's being handled, even if gently, even if lovingly on Randi's side too.

Randi, seen from outside for once, is genuinely lovely on the surface — warm to Cassie too, not just performing for Vee, generous with her attention — and I want to believe that. But knowing what I know from inside her own head last chapter (the recovery reflex, the practiced face), I can't fully relax into it. The hug, the "love you, girl," the "I've decided" — is this Randi's real self finally let loose with someone who isn't Pace, or is this the same performance machinery, aimed somewhere new? I genuinely don't know, and the not-knowing is uncomfortable in a productive way.

Cassie (new to me, but I'm told she and Cassandra are one person) is the chapter's quietest, sharpest note. Four words a day, declines the invitation without sulk, and then: "*Goodbye, Vee.*" Not "see you," not "bye" — the whole word. The text itself flags it ("the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday") and Vee's own body catches the wrongness before her mind does — "her face did the thing it did with wrongness, which was to smooth it." I trust this as an earned unease, not manufactured; the prose does the work.

**Swoon / pull.** The Chi Latte scene is genuinely swoony — the pun on the sign resolving itself in Vee's mouth, the high table, the "flat helpless want... the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance, except the distance was three feet now." That's good, specific, unshowy longing. I felt it. And the detail that Vee can't reconstruct a single word Randi actually said — "she had been keeping the feeling... and the transcript was blank" — is a lovely, true thing about being infatuated, and it also quietly tells me the content of Randi's charm doesn't matter, only its aim does. That unsettles me exactly as much as it moves me.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I have no reason to distrust Vee — she reads as wholly herself, unguarded on the page in a way Randi never is. My suspicion is aimed entirely at Randi (earned last chapter, not manufactured here) and, offstage, at Pace, whose fingerprints I can feel on this whole sequence even though he never appears. Cassie earns a flicker of suspicion too, but of a different kind — not that she's dangerous, but that she sees something coming that Vee doesn't, and said goodbye instead of bye because she meant it.

**Erotic charge.** Basically zero in the conventional sense — no bodies together this way — but the *social* eroticism, the charge of being chosen and touched and warmed by someone glamorous, is doing real work and doing it well. It's a different register of wanting and it lands.

**Friction.** None that felt like a thumb on the scale. If anything the chapter trusted me — it let "latent variables" sit there underlined and unexplained, let me do the work of connecting "the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" to everything else going on beneath this scene's warm surface.

**Titles.** "See You Later" — I felt this one land twice: once as the throwaway thing people say and don't mean, which Vee explicitly clocks as usually empty, and then as Randi says it and means it as "flat fact, a thing already scheduled." Given what I already know about how deliberately Randi and Pace stage things, the title now reads to me as faintly ominous rather than casual — a promise that's real, but real in whose interest? It didn't spoil anything for me going in; it recolors on the way out. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — the "invitation" is very literally happening here (into the sorority-coded warmth, into Chi Latte, into "later"), and "long spoon" I still can't place concretely, but this chapter, with its spoons "ringing all around" the room and its careful choreography of who invites whom into what, feels like it's circling the phrase without naming it yet.

**What I want next.** I want inside Randi's head again to see whether this warmth toward Vee is anything like the bare "Yes" from before, or fully manufactured. I want to know why Cassie said goodbye like that. And I'm newly worried for Vee in a way I wasn't expecting to be this early — I like her too much to want to watch her be someone's answer to a question she doesn't know was asked.
