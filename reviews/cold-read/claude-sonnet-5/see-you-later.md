# Cold read — See You Later

## Reader reaction

This chapter is a gut-punch delivered in slow motion, and it's the first time the book has let me sit fully inside Vee, and it changes everything about how I read what happened last chapter.

I liked Vee before I met her — the "warm/unguarded/laughing openly in public" sketch from the collision — but this chapter gives me the whole architecture of her, and it's devastating in the specific way that recognizable things are devastating. The waiting for the door, the "third minute of not looking at the door," the pencil-moving-so-her-hand-has-somewhere-to-be — I know this person. And then the interiority around her own body and desire: the "floozies" moment that arrives in her mother's voice, followed immediately by "a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for" — that is one of the best-executed passages of self-deception I've read in this book so far. She judges the girls, catalogues exactly what her body is doing while she judges them, decides she's better than them anyway, and then reburies the whole thing under her mother's second voice about what "a nice girl" doesn't invite. That's not authorial commentary, that's just watching a person build and inhabit her own trap in real time. I trust this book more for how precisely it rendered that without naming it.

My feeling about Cassie moved hardest this chapter, from "minor sorority sister, not present" to a real ache. Cassie is not sorority — she's Vee's actual person, low-key, four words a day, declining the invitation with "no sulk, no edge, just... a person declining a thing that wasn't hers." And then "Goodbye, Vee." Not "see you later." The chapter does the thing where it makes you feel the wrongness in your own body a half-beat before Vee smooths it over — "her face did the thing it did with wrongness, which was to smooth it" — and then hands you, at the very end, Randi's actual "See you later, okay?" as the payoff of that discarded chord. Cassie knew something. Cassie's goodbye was final in a way Vee wouldn't let herself hear. I am worried for Cassie and worried for Vee on Cassie's behalf, and I don't yet know why, which is exactly the right kind of dread — earned, not manufactured, because the text built the asymmetry itself (flat "Goodbye, Vee" vs. warm "see you later") and let me do the subtraction.

Randi, meanwhile — I'm carrying real unease into this chapter because of what I know from the last one: that this "chance" meeting was engineered, that Vee was pointed at and selected. Reading Randi's performance here — the bit about her name, the total warmth "swung over" onto Cassie and then "returning" to Vee, the "I've decided," the hug, "love you, girl" — I can't unsee the machinery under it anymore. It's beautifully done as performance (I believe every beat of it as something that would work on a person, because it worked on me too, a little), but I now read Randi's fluency as exactly what ch.2 established: a face she puts on. The line "she'd picked the seat beside" — Vee reads this as being chosen for herself; I read it as target acquisition completed. That gap between what Vee is feeling and what I know is happening is the chapter's whole engine, and it hurts to read, in the good/bad way this book has been training me to expect.

Swoon and pull: genuinely swept up by Vee's whole sensory experience of being chosen — the high table, the gold Greek sign resolving into a joke she's suddenly inside, the citrus smell, "the words were gone... she had been keeping the feeling." That's real, un-ironic swoon, rendered gorgeously, and I don't think the book wants me to disbelieve the euphoria — I think it wants me to feel exactly how good it feels to be selected by someone who is very good at selecting people, which is the most efficient possible way to make me dread what's coming.

Erotic charge: this chapter has none of the explicit heat of the last one — it's a courtship of attention, not bodies, and the "want" is diffuse ("a flat helpless want... the want sat there in Vee's chest, warm, unexamined") rather than sexual on its face. But it's charged all the same, because I know (and the reader-state confirms) this is being staged as the front end of a desire Pace is engineering Randi toward, with Vee as the object — so the warmth here is doing double duty as seduction, even though Vee experiences it as friendship. That's an unusual and effective kind of erotic tension: heat by anticipation and dramatic irony rather than by scene.

Friction: minimal. The one place I felt the author's hand slightly is "*Χ ΛΑΤΕ*" / "Chi Latte" bit — a little cute, a little long, but it earns itself by paying off Vee's outsider status ("she had stood outside this exact joke") so I'll allow it.

Titles: "See You Later" is doing quiet, precise work — it's Randi's actual closing line, but set against Cassie's "Goodbye," it reads now as a title about which parting is honest. I don't think it's giving anything away so much as it's already, on finishing the chapter, recoloring itself — it sounds breezy and turns out to be the least true thing said in the whole chapter. As for the book/series titles — "A Polite Invitation" fits disturbingly well onto this chapter specifically: Vee has just received the most polite, most flattering invitation of her life, and I don't yet trust it.

What I want next: I want to know if Vee ever gets any information about what actually happened to her — whether the dramatic irony resolves into her knowing, or stays permanently one-sided. I want more Cassie; that goodbye needs answering. And I'm bracing for the next Pace/Randi chapter to show me how this "collision" gets used — guess from here, not knowledge.
