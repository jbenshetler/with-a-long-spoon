# Cold read (grounded) — See You Later

*scene: scenes/see-you-later.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch003 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Okay, this one lands very differently than the two before it, and I felt the gears shift the moment the chapter opened on a stats lecture instead of a bedroom.

**How I feel about each character right now.** Vee I like instantly and completely — she's the first POV I've been given who isn't already inside the game. She's just a girl who got noticed by someone dazzling and can't stop turning it over. The detail of deciding "not to look at the door" on the walk, on the stairs, and then narrating her own failure to keep that decision — that's such exact, generous interiority. I trust her narration completely; there's no performance in it, which is a real change of register after two chapters of watching Randi perform for rooms. My stomach dropped a little for her, protectively, because I already know — and she doesn't — that this "chance" collision at the dining hall doors was Pace pointing and Randi choosing. She thinks she was seen and chosen for herself. I know she was pointed at. That gap is unbearable in the good, aching way.

Randi is the one my feelings really moved on. In "The Bench" and "Standards" I had aching sympathy for her — the crying she couldn't let Pace see, the locked door, the deadbolt-quiet decision that he could have the face but not her. Watching her turn that same practiced brilliance on an innocent, susceptible college kid — the warmth "swinging over" to Cassie and then "returning" to Vee, the "right pen" line delivered with the same calibrated skill I watched her use on Brooke in the sorority common room — recolors her. It's not that she's become a villain to me. It's that I now see the mechanism running underneath the warmth in real time, on someone who has no idea it's a mechanism, and it's chilling exactly because Randi is so good at it, and because I know from "The Pointing Game" what this "want" actually is for her — a live coal she can't hold barehanded, so she's handing it to Pace instead, wrapped as a gift for him. Vee is the wrapping paper's next stop and doesn't know that either.

Pace barely appears (a few paragraphs, offstage-remembered), but "Go meet her" and his watching from the light pole are right behind this chapter's surface the whole time — I kept feeling him in the room even when he wasn't in the room.

Cassie is the chapter's quiet gut-punch. Four words a day, and the four words she chooses — "I've got a thing," "I'm good," and then, devastatingly, "Goodbye, Vee" instead of "see you later" — land like someone who can already see the shape of what's coming and is saying so in the only register she has. Vee feels the wrongness and *smooths it over*, which is exactly her tragic flaw on display in miniature: she notices the stair that isn't where her foot expected and steps past it anyway because the better thing is moving.

**Swoon / pull.** Genuinely swept up by Vee's hour at Chi Latte — the high table, the oat-milk latte, "the want had nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her*." That's real longing, not manufactured, and I felt my own want-to-be-chosen ache right alongside her. The Greek sign resolving into a joke she's suddenly let in on is a lovely small image of belonging. And "love you, girl" / "see you later" as a closing beat is a perfectly pitched gut-twist — pure social warmth from Randi's side, and from Vee's, something closer to a covenant. I wanted to reach into the page and warn her, which is the sign the chapter did its job.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I don't suspect Vee of anything — she's exactly what she appears to be. I do now actively distrust Randi's warmth on the page, but the text has fully earned that: I watched her perform an identical warmth-and-recovery routine on Brooke under interrogation two chapters ago, and I watched Pace literally point her at Vee across a lawn. This isn't paranoia; it's continuity.

**Erotic charge.** Zero explicit content here, and it doesn't need any — the charge is entirely displaced into social hunger: being noticed, being touched on the forearm, being pulled into a hug that smells like cold citrus. It's warm rather than hot, which is exactly right after two chapters that ran very hot; the tonal cooling itself functions almost as dramatic irony, letting the reader catch their breath while dread quietly accumulates underneath.

**Friction.** None really — the only thing that nagged slightly was how thoroughly Vee's interiority forgives every odd note (Randi's "we should sit together" ambiguity, Cassie's "goodbye") within a sentence of noticing it. But that's clearly the point, not a miss — it's characterization, the specific shape of a person who smooths wrongness because she needs the good thing more than she needs to be right.

**Titles.** "See You Later" is quietly devastating once you reach the end and see it echo against Cassie's flat "Goodbye" — the chapter is built around the gap between those two partings, one that promises continuation and one that (maybe) doesn't. It didn't spoil anything for me going in; it recolored beautifully on the way out. As for the book/series titles — "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" — this chapter is the first time I've actually watched the invitation being extended to its addressee rather than watched it being *planned* by the two people running it. It confirms the jacket's promise precisely: "she doesn't know there's a plan," and now I've seen the exact moment the plan touches her, dressed as an accident, a coffee, a hug on a sidewalk.

**What I want next.** I want to know what "see you later" turns out to mean in practice — whether Randi's attention on Vee sustains or was a one-afternoon performance now that Pace has what he wanted (the answer to his question). I'm dreading, mildly, watching Vee fold Cassie further out of the frame as this "brighter life" takes hold. And I want — selfishly, as a reader who's been given more knowledge than Vee has — to see the moment, if it ever comes, when Vee reads a room the way I'm reading this one.
