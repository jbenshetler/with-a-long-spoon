# Cold read (grounded) — broken-in

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

Okay, this one moved something in me and it wasn't only the erotic charge (which is real but almost secondary here) — it was watching Vee's shame do exactly what it always does: flip target. She spends the whole night furious at Pace for a sin she invented, and then she wakes up and instead of sitting with that alone, or bringing it to Cassie — who is right there, dry and safe and would tell her something true, if unwelcome — she goes to Randi. And Randi is so good at this. That's the thing that lands hardest for me. "Not today... Tomorrow night... you don't go in there sorry — sorry's easy. You go in there sure." It's warm, it's competent, it even sounds wise. But notice what it actually is: it's Randi taking Vee's raw, unprocessed guilt and repackaging it into a *performance* — an outfit, a scarf-tying tutorial, a script ("you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about"). Randi never once asks Vee what she actually did wrong, or whether Pace deserves an apology on his own terms rather than a triumphant one. She turns Vee's contrition into styling. I felt the same unease I've had since the checkpoint, just sharper now, because this chapter is basically Randi's playbook laid bare on the page: draw the story out, hold the hand, then redirect the whole thing toward *Randi's* aesthetic control of Vee.

And then the dressing scene. God, that scene. It's tender and funny — the Edna Mode bit made me laugh out loud, it's such a real, silly, girls-being-girls moment — and it's also completely soaked in something more. "Standing in her bra in front of Randi was no event... it was nothing." Except it so clearly isn't nothing to Randi — the "something moved in her face, there and gone" at Vee's bare feet, the thumb pressed into the hollow of Vee's knee, the hand bracing high on her thigh under the skirt each time she has to lift a foot, the breath Vee "hoped wasn't out loud." This is written as seduction disguised as a shoe-shopping favor, and the reader (per the checkpoint's frame, which I'm carrying forward, not manufacturing new here) can see Randi's hand doing more work than the scene needs it to do for its stated purpose. Vee registers the sensation but not the meaning — "something Vee didn't have a name for and was too happy to go looking for." That's the tragic little hinge of the whole book so far, distilled into one clause.

The kiss at the end got me too — not because it's shocking, we've had two before, but because of the specific beat: "she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." That's an escalation dressed as ordinariness, and it's quietly devastating because it means the wanting has stopped announcing itself to Vee at all. It's just become weather.

Pace, interestingly, barely appears — he's structurally present as the absent center of the whole chapter, the person being fixed-toward, but we never get his interior here. That's fine, it's not his chapter, but it does throw the full erotic and manipulative weight onto Randi, and I noticed how much more I distrust her scripting of "sure, not sorry" than I distrusted anything in the previous chapters. It's the first time Randi's guidance felt less like generosity and more like direction of a scene she's staging.

Cassie gets one beat — the "Hrm" in the dark, not pushing — and it stung a little, because Vee is now visibly choosing not to go to her, twice: not calling her for the cab, and going straight to Randi's door instead of home in the morning. The gap between the two friendships keeps widening and Vee doesn't seem to notice it's a choice.

The Rusty Nail material (recent chapters, not this chapter, but still live in me) continues to work on me too — that whole "he's so good at getting a girl home safe" reveal, and the bartender's "wasn't him they were jealous of, honey," is one of the best-earned reversals in the book so far. I bought Vee's jealousy completely, and I bought the shame-spiral after.

Swoon / pull: the shoe-fitting is genuinely erotic and tender at once — I felt the pull of being *tended to*, the specific vertigo of "being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less," which rhymes so precisely with how Vee has responded to Pace's control that it reads almost like a thesis statement smuggled into a shoe store. That symmetry is doing real work on me as a reader — I felt swept along and slightly alarmed by how well it worked.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't trust Randi's motives in this chapter more than I did before — the text itself earns this, not just my memory of the checkpoint. The "not today, tomorrow night" instruction reads less like friendship and more like control dressed as care; she's literally scripting how Vee will present herself to another man, timing the apology for maximum effect, choosing the neckline. I have no new suspicion of Pace — he isn't on the page enough to generate one — and Cassie remains fully trustworthy, quietly declining to be used the way Randi is used.

Erotic charge: peaks in the shoe-fitting (the thigh, the hollow of the knee, "her skin came up singing") — clothed, intimate, charged rather than explicit, and it works precisely because it's disguised as errand-running. Goes warm-but-not-hot in the hug/kiss at the end; there's no explicit sex in this chapter at all, which is fine, the chapter isn't reaching for that.

Friction: none that felt like the author's thumb on the scale — if anything the chapter trusts the reader to catch what Vee doesn't, which I appreciate. The only thing that nagged slightly was how tidy Randi's read of the whole night is ("you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about") — it's persuasive but also very convenient for keeping Vee moving toward Pace on Randi's schedule, and I noticed the convenience.

Title: "broken-in" — I keep turning it over. New boots that need breaking in (Vee's tan lace-ups, actually not — those are already soft; the new heels from Randi are what get broken in, physically and otherwise). But it also reads on Vee herself — a shame cycle worn smooth by repetition, a girl being fitted, literally and figuratively, into shapes someone else has already chosen for her. It's a quiet title, not a spoiler, but it recolors the shoe-fitting scene retroactively as the chapter's real center once I sit with it.

Series/book titles: "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" both feel like they're tightening their grip this chapter — the "sup with the devil" resonance of the spoon title lands hard on the shoe-fitting scene, where being handled with total tenderness is indistinguishable from being handled, period.

What I want next: I want to see the actual reconciliation with Pace — whether Randi's script performs the way she promised, and whether Vee, standing in someone else's shoes and someone else's dress-code, notices the strangeness of walking into an apology that isn't hers in design. Guess from here: I suspect the "sure, not sorry" apology will work exactly as Randi predicted, and that unsettles me more than if it failed.
