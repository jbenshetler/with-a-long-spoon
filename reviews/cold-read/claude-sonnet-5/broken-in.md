## Reader reaction

Okay, this chapter did something to me I want to name carefully, because it's not simple. It starts in shame — the good, earned kind, Vee facing the mirror and calling herself a "sad clown," washing off both the makeup and the funk of the night before. That's a person taking responsibility. I liked her for it. I liked the apple, the bare legs in October, the little private ceremony of deciding to fix things. That's a Vee I can root for.

Then she gets to Randi's, and the whole register shifts, and I have to be honest: I was swept up. The confession scene is genuinely moving — Randi taking "the worst minute of Vee's life and turning it over in her hands and showing her the back of it," reframing the jealousy as proof of how much Vee's in it, is exactly the kind of friendship-alchemy that makes you want to cry a little. "You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about." That's true, and it's kind, and it works on me the reader the same way it worked on Vee — the relief was contagious.

But then the dress-up sequence starts, and something in me went quietest and most alert at once. It's staged as girls-being-girls, "the oldest game there is" — Vee even tells herself that, explicitly, defensively almost: "Standing in her bra in front of Randi was no event." But the prose doesn't let it stay that. The shoe-fitting is written with unmistakable erotic charge — "her skin came up singing," the thumb pressed into the hollow behind her knee, the hand bracing high on her thigh, Vee's breath doing "something she hoped wasn't out loud." This is not neutral dress-up prose. It's seduction prose wearing dress-up's clothes, and I don't think that's my imagination — the text gave me the singing skin and the caught breath on the page, not just implied.

What makes it complicated rather than simply hot: Randi is doing this the morning after coaching Vee toward Pace, in the same conversation where she overrides Vee's own instinct ("not today," "tomorrow night") and script-writes exactly what she'll wear and how she'll walk in. The tenderness is real — I don't doubt Randi likes Vee, wants good things for her — but there's an authorship happening here that Vee can't see and I can. Randi is dressing Vee for Pace while touching her like this is for Randi too. "He doesn't stand a chance," Randi says, watching Vee's body, and it goes through Vee "warm and a little ridiculous, because it was Randi who'd said it" — Vee registers the strangeness of that even as she doesn't examine it.

The kiss at the door lands hardest for me: "she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." That casualness — an established, unremarked-upon thing between them — recontextualizes everything before it. This isn't new; it's a rhythm they're already in. I don't know what to call it and the text doesn't make me. I just know I can't file it as innocent, and I also can't file it as predatory. It's genuinely ajar.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't distrust Randi's affection for Vee — it reads as real. But I distrust the frictionlessness of it, the way Vee's own agency (going today, out of urgency) gets overridden and replaced with a script, timed and costumed, and Vee just... complies, happily. That's not new information exactly — I already knew Randi was coordinating with Pace — but seeing her direct Vee's body this intimately, this specifically for Pace's benefit, sharpens the unease considerably.

Erotic charge: real and specific, entirely within the dress-up scene — the shoe fitting is the hottest thing in the chapter by a wide margin, more than any actual named desire. It goes slack a little in the exposition-y wardrobe talk ("classic's classic for a reason") but comes roaring back for the shoes and the scarf-tying, where Randi's hands walk Vee's hands through the knot — tactile, controlled, intimate.

Title: "Broken In" — I keep turning it over. Shoes broken in, obviously (the perfect shoes that "held" her foot and made her surer). But it's also Vee herself — broken in like a horse, like leather, made pliable, told where to be and finding she likes it: "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." That line is doing a lot of work and I don't think it's accidental that it sits right next to the title's meaning. It doesn't feel like a giveaway so much as a quiet magnifying glass held over what's already there.

What I want next: I want the apology scene with Pace, obviously — does Vee walk in "sure" the way Randi scripted, or does something more honest happen? I also want, badly, to understand what's actually between Randi and Vee — whether this is affection with a private agenda, or something Randi herself doesn't fully examine either. I don't dread it exactly, but I'm wary of how neatly Vee is being assembled for delivery.
