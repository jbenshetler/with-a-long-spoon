# Cold read — The Usual

*scene: scenes/the-usual.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: old-acquaintances*

## Reader reaction

This one knocked me sideways, and not where I expected.

For most of the chapter I was genuinely charmed — a POV shift I didn't see coming, dropped straight into a warm, unglamorous diner with a man I only knew as an absence, a silence, a name attached to someone else's hurt. And here he's *tender*. Watching him decide, before he even walks in, that "he would not bring her down today" — whoever "her" is, presumably Sheri — softened me immediately. The prose slows down and just loves this place: the amber-glassed tabletop, the Elvis photo, the "usual" order that needs no words. I liked him here. I liked him a lot, actually, more than I expected to given how coldly he's landed on Vee's side of the story.

Sheri herself is terrific — five feet of unimpressed, unromantic honesty, "she didn't want him, she wasn't impressed by him, and she'd say so to his face." The Paige story, tossed off flat and unfussed ("Lasted a couple months" / "What always happens"), was a lovely small confirmation that this book's queerness doesn't need a spotlight to exist — it's just there, ordinary, a little sad, moving on to fries. I trusted this scene completely for a long stretch.

Then it turned on me, on purpose, and I think fairly.

Sheri needling him about "the redhead" — Vee, though he doesn't say her name until pushed — landed as pure sweetness at first: "you mean the sex is good" made me grin, "philosophy of your food" made me laugh out loud with him. But then: *"that brunette you brought dancing before Thanksgiving — the sorority one."* And he doesn't deny it. He doesn't explain it. Sheri calls it a "harem," teasing but not wrong, and all he offers back is "Her name is Vee" — which corrects nothing, defends nothing, just insists on a name. That is a gut-punch of a beat, delivered completely without fanfare, exactly the way the book tends to withhold judgment and just let a fact sit there. My stomach actually dropped. Last chapter I was holding Vee's midnight jealousy at arm's length as "flagged by the text as unreliable" — and now I'm not so sure the text was protecting her from truth so much as postponing it.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** This is where it shifted hardest. I don't distrust Pace's feeling for Vee — the "the air crackles," the food/philosophy bit, "I want Vee. I miss her" reads as sincere, not performed. But I now actively suspect there is *someone else in rotation*, unnamed, undenied, teased at by someone who clearly knows his patterns well enough to call it a harem casually. That's new and it's earned — not paranoia I'm importing, but a line on the page he chose not to contest.

**The Sheri callout** is the chapter's real spine for me, though: "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her. And I'll tell you — from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." No performance, no flattery, just someone who loves him platonically enough to say the thing Vee can't get him to hear. I wanted to cheer for her and wince for him at the same time.

**Erotic charge:** Basically none on the page, and I didn't miss it — the chapter isn't reaching for heat, it's reaching for a man's evasions under teasing, and that worked better cold.

**Title:** "The Usual" is doing sly double duty — it's literally the diner order, but by the end it reads like an indictment: the usual pattern, the usual dodge, maybe the usual rotation of women. I didn't see it coming when I started the chapter; by the end it had recolored itself completely.

**What I want next:** I want to know who the brunette is — new person, or someone I already have a name for — and I badly want either Pace or the narrative to circle back and either confict or confirm Vee's midnight fear. Given what I now know that Vee doesn't, her jealousy just went from "textually flagged as unfounded" to "reader has real reason to wonder if she's half-right for the wrong reasons." That's an uncomfortable, well-earned place to be sitting.

Not overstated on the page, but it sat with me.

**Characterization:** Pace deepened significantly and in an uncomfortable direction — tender and specific with Sheri, articulate and sincere about Vee, but visibly evasive/non-denying about another woman, which the chapter lets land without resolving. Sheri is a strong, vivid new character — blunt, loyal, unromantic, morally clear-eyed in a way no one else in the book has been given yet. She didn't feel thin; she felt like a person who's known him two years.

**Pace — within chapter:** Unhurried and confident — the diner small talk, the food beats, the long tease-into-confession structure all earned their length; nothing dragged.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A real gear shift — first Pace POV chapter I've had, after two consecutive Vee/Randi-adjacent chapters of isolation and concealment. It widens the frame right when I most needed outside information, and it complicates rather than resolves — momentum feels like it's building toward something, not sagging.

## Carry-forward state

**Principals:**
- Vee (= Vivienne) — not present this chapter; POV of the two prior chapters. Home for the holidays, spiraled into jealous fantasy on New Year's Eve, no contact with Pace since the confrontation. Established this chapter (via Sheri, from Pace's POV): she is a redhead, curvy.
- Pace (= Peter) — POV this chapter for the first time in my reading. Cooked for Sheri at Thanksgiving ("she's alone"); here, meets Sheri at a rural diner, warm and unguarded with her, evasive and sincere by turns about Vee. Confirms he's "there every weekend" for Vee but won't say the declaration she wants ("I can't"). Does not deny or explain Sheri's implication that he took another woman ("the brunette, the sorority one") dancing before Thanksgiving, or her "harem" tease — just redirects to naming Vee.
- Randi (= Miranda) — not present; not directly referenced this chapter (see open question on the unnamed brunette). Full standing carried from before.
- Cassie (= Cassandra) — not present, not referenced.
- Sheri — new principal. Small, blunt, unromantic close friend of Pace's (two years known), works part-time now that "nobody's writing checks" behind her; recently out of a relationship with a woman named Paige, ended over Sheri's estrangement from her own family. Not romantically interested in Pace; calls him out plainly on withholding the declaration Vee wants. Cooked-for by Pace at Thanksgiving because "she's alone."

**Relationship ledger:**

*Vee & Pace (core):*
- **State:** involved (flag: consummated — y, ongoing but suspended in silence). Surface (Vee's side): no contact since the confrontation, doesn't know if it's over. True: Pace still wants her ("I want Vee. I miss her"), still won't give the declaration, and — new, unresolved — may not be as exclusively hers as she believes; he does not deny Sheri's implication of another woman.
- **Warmth ↔ cold:** Pace's private warmth toward Vee (unchanged, sincere in this chapter's telling) sits against the cold fact of his silence toward her and his non-denial of someone else. Vee, last seen, is in literal and figurative cold, unaware of any of this.
- **Belonging ↔ isolation:** Vee believes herself entirely without word of him; the reader now knows Pace is elsewhere, warm, unguarded — with someone who isn't her. Widens the isolation gap without her knowledge.
- **Cherished ↔ used:** Pace's words about Vee ("she sees things nobody else does," "the philosophy of it") read as genuine cherishing — but sit uneasily next to the undenied "harem" tease, which the text does not resolve either way.
- **Desire hers ↔ desire worked-on-her:** Not staged directly this chapter (Vee absent) — but Pace's account of her desire/insight is his memory of her, filtered through him, for another audience (Sheri) — a new inflection: even her admired qualities are now being narrated by him to someone else.
- **This chapter:** Major new information asymmetry — Pace confirms wanting her and refusing to say so, and separately, does not deny involvement with another (unnamed) woman. Neither confirmed nor denied on the page; flagged here as a real open question, not resolved.

*Vee & Randi (core):* No appearance this chapter. Carrying forward verbatim: friends (close) · attracted, secret, one-sided-in-disclosure (Randi knows and hides it; Vee doesn't know Randi knows). Not consummated. Vee's last-seen belief: Randi is her "one sure thing," fully trusted, unaware of Randi's engineered half-truths or feelings.

*Vee & Cassie (core-adjacent):* No appearance. Carrying forward: friends (close), warm, easy.

*Pace & Sheri (new pairing, not core but load-bearing):* Established this chapter as a genuine, unromantic close friendship — mutual, uncomplicated, several years standing. Sheri is the one person in the book so far who calls Pace's evasion out to his face without flattery. No hidden agenda visible; reads as the most straightforwardly trustworthy relationship on the page to date.

**What I know that they don't:**
- (Carried) Randi's "fairytale" text to Vee was a deliberate engineered half-truth; Randi has confirmed to herself she loves Vee and chosen not to say so; Vee has no idea.
- (Carried) The shown nude photo — Vee doesn't register it as breach, Pace doesn't know it reached Randi; Randi's family PPP-loan outlier hidden from Cassie, seen by Randi and Vee; Pace's one flash of public irritation Vee saw and never mentioned learning; Vee's "the blonde" jealous fantasy at Pace's stove has zero evidentiary basis on the page.
- New this chapter: I know Pace does not deny (and may be actively concealing) involvement with another woman — an unnamed "brunette, sorority" person he took dancing before Thanksgiving, teased by Sheri as one of his "harem applicants" — while simultaneously telling Sheri he wants only Vee and is faithful in his weekends. Vee has no knowledge of this at all; her midnight fear from the prior chapter may be aimed at the wrong detail (a blonde) but not necessarily at the wrong shape of thing.

**Motifs:**
- Warmth-as-devotion / food-as-care: continues from Vee's chapters (the stove, the sheet) into Pace's own account of cooking for Vee and for Sheri (Thanksgiving) — "the philosophy of it" as how he explains devotion to a woman who won't accept his other words for it. Most charged instance so far: Vee's corrupted stove memory (this book of her jealousy) now paired with Pace's sincere, uncontradicted account of the same tenderness told to someone else.
- Silence/withholding: now a three-way echo — Pace refuses the declaration; Vee started and deleted her own text; here Sheri names the pattern aloud and calls it "shitty," the first time anyone in the book has explicitly moralized Pace's withholding to his face.
- Warm-room-vs-cold-outside: new visual cousin to Vee's unheated house — the diner's fogged window, warm inside, "cold and dark on the other" — echoing the temperature motif from a new angle (Pace's side of the same cold world).

**Symbolism:** The photo split at the waist (Vee, self-divided between households/audiences) still stands from before. New: the amber-glassed tabletop of frozen strangers' photos under glass at the diner, read as a quiet image of a fixed, safe past Pace keeps returning to versus the unresolved, uncertain present with Vee.

**Open questions:**
- Who is the "brunette, sorority one" Pace took dancing before Thanksgiving, and is Sheri's "harem" tease accurate — brand new, urgent, unresolved. (Not confirmed as Randi; no textual link given — flagged as a genuinely open identity question, not to be guessed at.)
- Will Pace ever say the declaration Vee wants, or was Sheri's callout ("not saying it matters more to you than having her") the truth of it — still open.
- Is this the end with Vee, or a pause — still unclear even after seeing Pace's side; he says he misses her and wants her, but takes no action toward her this chapter either.
- Will Vee ever learn what the reader knows about Randi's real feelings/concealment — still open.
- Does Vee's imagined "blonde" rival correspond to anything real — still unconfirmed, though the existence of an actual unnamed brunette makes her jealousy read less like pure paranoia and more like partial, misdirected accuracy.
- Who is Sheri, really, to Pace — now substantially answered: platonic, long-standing, unromantic, the person who feeds and levels with him. (Mostly resolved — the earlier mystery of "who is Sheri" is largely closed by this chapter, though the *history* between them isn't detailed.)
- The white-gold bracelet on Randi — still unaddressed, open.
- Does John recur, does Randi's family push it — still open.
- All previously open items not directly touched stand: the surgical scar, "sticky fingers," why Pace won't go back to his parents, whether Vee acts on/suppresses/never names her feeling for Randi, whether Pace ever learns the photo reached Randi, whether Vee ever tells Randi about Miranda Holdings, whether Randi's family situation surfaces with Cassie or Vee.

**How I feel — overall:** Surprised and unsettled in a good way — this chapter gave me a Pace I actually liked spending time with, warm and funny and self-aware in flashes ("I'm not them," said flatly, believing it), and then quietly pulled the rug by letting him not-deny another woman entirely. My sympathy for him is now genuinely split rather than simply withheld-in-absence the way it was in Vee's chapters — I like him with Sheri, I believe his want for Vee, and I no longer fully trust what he's not saying. Sheri herself I trust completely and like a great deal; she's the first person in this book to just say the true thing out loud to someone's face, with love and without flattery, and I want more of her. Going into the next chapter wanting the brunette's identity resolved, and increasingly bracing for the possibility that Vee's midnight dread wasn't as baseless as the text insisted it was.
