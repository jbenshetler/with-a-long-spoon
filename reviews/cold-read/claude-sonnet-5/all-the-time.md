# Cold read — All the Time

*scene: scenes/all-the-time.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: in-his-hands*

## Reader reaction

This one knocked the wind out of me a little — in a good way, and then in a way I'm not sure is good.

The first half is just glorious. Vee finally gets to *say* it — all of it, the sex, the food, the sleep, the wanting that doesn't run out — and it comes pouring out of her in that stammering, blushing, can't-stop-once-she's-started way that felt completely earned after all the chapters of her hedging with everyone else. "Is that — is something wrong with me?" gutted me a little, and Randi's answer — "You're just finally hungry out loud… You've been starving your whole life and calling it fine" — is one of the best lines in the book so far. I believed every word of Vee's monologue. The egg yolk running everywhere, the piano that's playing before she even walks in the door, the TV nobody's ever turned on — all of it lands as real, unforced happiness. I was rooting hard for her, glad for her, a little envious of her even.

And then the photo. That's where the chapter turned a corner I didn't expect. Vee showing Randi the cropped shot first is understandable, sweet even — showing off a little, proud of her own daring. But Randi's "you only gave him half?" needling her into pulling up the full uncropped nude — that's the moment I felt something shift under me. Vee's stated reason is pride, refusing to be "that girl," but what I actually watched was someone else's opinion of her nerve overriding her own judgment about a private image that was, as far as I know, meant for Pace alone. It's consensual — nobody's forcing her — but it's also clearly *performed for Randi's approval*, and that's a different transaction than the one she had with Pace when she sent it. I don't think Vee sees that gap. I do.

Then the goodbye kiss. Vee leaning in for the extra half-inch, "before she'd thought," and deciding after the fact that this was what she wanted — and Randi holding the eye contact "a beat past what a goodbye needed." I felt the charge of it completely — my body registered it as sexy and unresolved, exactly like the bathroom scene from before — but I also can't tell you what it means to Randi. Is this flirtation as sport? Genuine desire she's letting surface carefully? I don't know, and the text doesn't tell me, and I appreciate that it doesn't pretend to.

The line that stopped me hardest was Randi's "It doesn't," said "not quite even," with her breath catching — right after Vee says *he wants me, with the body attached, not the other way around*. That reads to me like something in Randi actually got hit, hard, by hearing that someone else has what she apparently doesn't, or hasn't had. It's the first crack I've seen in Randi rather than in Vee's perception of her — a private ache surfacing for one unguarded second before she smooths it back into performance ("Miss November," the soundtrack question). I don't know what that ache is *for* — envy of Vee's happiness, longing for Vee herself, something about her own past — but I felt it as real, not manufactured.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No suspicion of malice from Randi — nothing here reads as calculated cruelty. But I do trust her less as a *safe* audience than I did — the "you only gave him half?" line has a needling, competitive edge to it that I hadn't clocked before, dressed as encouragement. It got Vee to do something with a private, trust-laden image that I don't think Vee would have done unprompted.

**Erotic charge:** Very high in the monologue — Vee's account of sex with Pace is the most sustained heat the book's given me, delivered secondhand and somehow more intense for being spoken aloud, blushing, at a brunch table. The photo-showing scene has its own charge, different in kind — exposure charge, performance charge, not tenderness. The goodbye kiss carries the low simmering Randi-charge from before.

**Friction:** None that felt like the author's thumb on the scale — every beat here felt psychologically motivated, even the uncomfortable ones.

**Titles:** "All the Time" — after this chapter I read it as pointing at Vee's own line, "we have so much sex... more than I knew people did," the sheer volume and constancy of what she has with Pace now. But it might also gesture at the Randi thread running "all the time" underneath the Pace relationship, never fully surfacing, always there. I'm not sure which the title means yet — maybe both.

**What I want next:** I want to know what Pace would feel if he knew that photo went further than his own eyes — and whether Vee will ever tell him, or Randi, anything true about what almost happened in that goodbye kiss. I'm also newly curious about what exactly moved in Randi during "It doesn't" — that's the loudest open question the chapter left me with.

## Carry-forward state

**Principals:**
- Vee (= Vivienne) — protagonist. This chapter: gave Randi, for the first time, the full unguarded truth of her sex life and happiness with Pace, in vivid, blushing detail; showed Randi first a cropped, then (pushed by Randi's needling) the full uncropped nude photo she'd sent Pace; initiated extending their goodbye kiss past its usual bounds, noticed herself doing it, and let it stand.
- Pace (= Peter) — absent this chapter, entirely offstage, present only through Vee's account of him: devoted, attentive, unhurried in sex and in cooking, plays music before she arrives, no TV, no phone-scrolling, gives her his whole attention. (Standing note from before: has one guarded no-go zone, his parents/"I don't go back," untouched this chapter.)
- Randi (= Miranda) — present, primary scene partner. Warm, generous listener through most of the brunch; needled Vee into showing the full nude photo ("you only gave him half?"); had one unguarded moment — eyes bright, breath catching, saying "It doesn't" low and uneven after Vee's line about being wanted "with the body attached" — quickly smoothed back into playful banter. Extended and held the goodbye kiss/eye contact a beat past ordinary.
- Cassie (= Cassandra) — mentioned only, not present: already completed her humanities elective with someone named "Wardy," so won't be in the new class Vee and Randi are signing up for together.

**Relationship ledger:**

*Vee & Pace (core):* No appearance — carrying forward verbatim.
- **State:** involved (flag: consummated — y, ongoing). Surface: warm, playful, deeply intimate; a first guarded edge appeared last chapter around his parents ("I don't go back"), otherwise fully open in action if not always in words.
- Cherished ↔ used — cherished, powerfully and consistently, per Vee's own account this chapter: unhurried attention, "there's not a part of me he doesn't want," his focus on cooking "all of that focus is pointed at making me something good." No used-current from him; quiet on this axis this chapter since he's offstage.
- Desire hers ↔ desire worked-on-her — both live in her account: her own wanting is vivid and growing ("the more we do it the more I want it"), but she also frames some of it as produced in her, beyond her control ("I've got sex brain… I don't know how one person is both"). Genuinely mixed, not resolved.
- Warmth ↔ cold — warmth total in her account of him; the single cold note (his parents) from the prior chapter is not revisited here.
- Belonging ↔ isolation — belonging, deeply: sleeping whole nights at his house, the quiet that doesn't need filling, feeling entirely inside his attention.
- **This chapter:** no direct scene, but Vee's account to Randi is the fullest, most vulnerable articulation yet of what she has with him — and simultaneously the first time something private between her and Pace (the nude photo) has been shared with a third party without his knowledge, a new asymmetry worth watching.

*Vee & Randi (core):* 
- **State:** friends (close) · attracted, **secret** (at least on Vee's side — she still hasn't named it to herself outright) · possibly one-sided, possibly mutual — genuinely unresolved. Not consummated in any form, but escalated this chapter: an extended, deliberately-lingered goodbye kiss, initiated by Vee, met by Randi holding eye contact "a beat past what a goodbye needed."
- Cherished ↔ used — mixed and newly complicated: Randi's listening is generous and total ("the best friend she'd ever had… she could take any of it"), genuinely cherishing; but her needling ("you only gave him half?") that pushes Vee into showing the full nude reads as using Vee's want-to-please to get more than Vee had offered — the clearest used-adjacent beat between them yet, though gentle and framed as banter.
- Desire hers ↔ desire worked-on-her — desire hers, more visibly than ever: Vee leans into the kiss "before she'd thought," notices it, and *decides* she wanted it rather than explaining it away — the most self-aware instance of her own desire toward Randi so far, though she still doesn't name what it is.
- Warmth ↔ cold — mostly warmth (mimosas, the food, the laughing-together over the photo), but Randi's "It doesn't," low and uneven with a catch in her breath, opens a brief, unexplained cold/vulnerable pocket under the polish — not sourced, not resolved.
- Belonging ↔ isolation — belonging dominant: "the one friend she trusted with all of it," a class signed up for together, unhesitating. No isolation beat this chapter.
- **This chapter:** the charge between them escalated concretely for the first time — a real photo shown, a real kiss extended by Vee's own initiative, and one unguarded crack in Randi's composure that neither woman addresses.

*Vee & Cassie:* No appearance — carrying forward verbatim. Established as genuinely close, low-drama friendship; Cassie already finished her humanities elective (with someone named "Wardy," new mention this chapter, otherwise unexplained), so she's out of the new class Vee and Randi are taking together.

*Vee alone:* This chapter shows her twice choosing to go further than her own instinct first suggested — pulling up the full nude after Randi's nudge, leaning into the extra half-inch of the kiss — both framed by her as her own decisions after the fact ("she noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted"), though the reader can see the first of the two was clearly shaped by Randi's provocation.

**What I know that they don't:** Genuine uncertainty, unchanged, about whether Randi's warmth toward Vee is simple friendship, romantic/erotic attraction, or something Randi herself hasn't sorted out — this chapter adds real evidence for the last two (the needling, the unguarded "It doesn't," the held kiss) without resolving it. New: I know that the nude photo Vee sent Pace in an act of private trust has now been shown to Randi — Vee doesn't seem to register this as any kind of breach or complication, but I do; Pace doesn't know.

**Motifs:**
- Made-by-his-hands/quiet devotion (Pace) — dress, table, chair, cooking, the drawer, tending her when hurt, the unhurried posing-and-kissing during the photo (most vivid instance, prior chapter). Dormant on the page this chapter but reaffirmed through Vee's account of him.
- Taste/food as intimacy — reinforced yet again, now doubled: the egg yolk running everywhere at brunch ("this is the best part, the middle, when it goes everywhere") and Vee's own retelling of Pace's cooking ("like my mouth had been asleep since I was born"). Strongest recurring motif in the book.
- The photograph as trust-token — established last chapter (sent to Pace past her own fear, "believing him was the brave part"); this chapter complicates it — the same photo is now shown to Randi, first cropped then (pushed by Randi) fully uncropped, turning a private trust-object into something shown off, performed. Worth tracking closely — does Pace ever learn this, and does it matter to him if he does?
- The extended/lingering kiss — first hinted in an earlier Vee/Randi chapter (bathroom scene, ambiguous charge); recurs here concretely as a real physical beat, initiated by Vee this time, held a beat too long by Randi.
- Desire hers vs. worked-on-her (Vee, re: Pace) — both explicitly voiced this chapter in her own words to Randi, unresolved which dominates.

**Symbolism:** The photo now reads as symbolizing two different transactions at once — trust given to Pace, performance given to Randi — and Vee doesn't seem to notice the difference, which is the part that unsettles me as a reader. Randi's "It doesn't," said with a breath-catch right after hearing she's wanted "with the body attached, not the other way around," reads as the first glimpse of something unmet or unresolved in Randi herself — not yet legible as more than that.

**Open questions:**
- What happened in "Randi's story" (an earlier, still-unshown reference) — still never shown.
- Whether Vee ever tells Randi or Cassie anything true about the charge between her and Randi specifically — still open, and now more urgent given the extended kiss.
- The unexplained surgical scar on Pace's inner thigh — still unaddressed.
- What did "sticky fingers?" actually mean (earlier chapter) — unresolved.
- Is Vee's attraction to Randi something she'll act on, suppress, or never fully name — still open, now visibly escalating (she's begun noticing and half-owning her own moves, even if she won't name them).
- Will Vee ever say "I love you" to Pace while he's awake, and will he ever say it back — still open.
- Why won't Pace "go back" to his parents — still nothing beyond the refusal itself (not touched this chapter).
- Who is Sheri, really, to Pace — still only a name mentioned in a prior chapter, not seen on the page.
- New: what does Randi's "It doesn't" mean — envy, longing, something about her own past or her own relationship to being wanted? The text gives nothing beyond the moment itself.
- New: does Pace ever learn the nude photo was shown to Randi, and does it register to him (or to Vee, eventually) as a breach?

**How I feel — overall:** Full and warm from the first half — Vee's happiness poured out loud is one of the most purely joyful stretches the book's given me, and Randi's response to it ("finally hungry out loud") is generous and true. But I leave the chapter carrying a new, real unease that isn't manufactured — it's sitting right there on the page: the way Randi's approval visibly outweighs Vee's own instinct in the photo scene, and the charge of that extended kiss that neither of them will name. I don't distrust Randi's affection for Vee, but I no longer read her as simply safe — there's appetite in her too, and I don't yet know what it wants.
