## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter warm and a little dreamy, still carrying the run of scenes just before it — the induction walk, the pantsing-turned-spanking, the drawer he clears out for her — and this one plays as the soft landing after all that plot: a brunch where nothing "happens" except Vee finally saying the true, shapeless thing out loud. "There's not — it's not a thing that happened... It's just how it *is* now." That line got me. It's the first time she's tried to describe a *state* instead of a *scene*, and the chapter is built entirely around her fumbling for the vocabulary of contentment, which she's never needed before. I liked her for it — the honesty of "I used to be a person with thoughts," the confession about sex brain, the fear underneath ("Am I turning into some kind of — I don't know. Sex pervert?"). That's exactly the shame-voice the checkpoint told me to expect, and here it surfaces almost gently, almost defanged, because Randi is right there to catch it.

And Randi's catch is the best moment in the chapter for me: "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud... You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*." That's a genuinely loving, generous thing to say to someone, and it lands as loving on the page — I felt the relief in Vee's body when she says it, "her throat did something under it too." I want to trust that fully. I can't, quite, because I know what the checkpoint knows about the "pointing game" and the bed-conversations about getting Vee "there on her own" — so even this kindness reads to me as also, structurally, *management*: Randi giving Vee permission to want more, which keeps Vee opening further, telling her everything, handing over the photo. But I want to be fair to the text — nothing here is manipulative on its face. It plays as real solidarity between women, real delight, and I don't want to manufacture menace where the page hasn't shown me the mechanism this time. It's the accumulated dramatic irony doing the work, not this scene's own evidence.

The one moment that actually unsettled me on the page itself — not from outside knowledge — was Randi's reaction to the "even with my clothes on... he wants *me*. With the body attached" line. Randi's eyes go "bright and a little unfixed," she shakes her head "as if to clear it," her breath catches, and she says, low and uneven, "It doesn't." Vee reads this as a story landing hard, evidence of her own power to move a woman like Randi. But *I* read that beat completely differently — that sounded to me like grief, like something in Randi cracking open for a second at hearing what Vee has and Randi, by her own established architecture (D/s with Pace, the mask she rebuilt after the bench), maybe doesn't let herself have in the same unguarded way. It's a lovely piece of writing because Vee's interpretation and mine diverge sharply, and the gap between them is where the book's ache lives right now.

The photo exchange is where the erotic charge and the unease braid together tightest. Randi's "you only gave him *half*?" pushing Vee to send the fuller, more exposed picture — and Vee complying, specifically so Randi won't think her a girl who flinches — is a small, exact moment of Randi's influence reaching past Pace and into Vee's own self-image, dressed as banter. It's hot (I felt the "thrill and the shame running together" she names), but it's also the clearest structural "off" note in the chapter: Randi isn't just enjoying the story, she's actively steering what Vee will risk and reveal, and Vee mistakes the steering for closeness.

**Swoon / pull.** Genuinely swooned at the eggs Benedict paragraph — "the yolk broke and ran, thick and gold... this is the best part. The middle, when it goes everywhere" — because it's doing the appetite-motif work so plainly and so joyfully, no shame attached, food-as-pleasure fully unlocked. And the closing image, Vee driving back toward Pace "already, thirty minutes out, beginning to want" him again, is a lovely, simple note of a woman who finally believes she's allowed this much wanting. I rooted for her happiness in this chapter more purely than I have in a while — it felt earned, not ironic, even while the irony sat under it.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new reason to distrust Pace here — he's offstage, described only through Vee's love-drunk report, all of it consistent with the tenderness established earlier. My suspicion is entirely reserved for Randi, and it's the checkpoint's inheritance more than this chapter's evidence — except for that one unguarded "It doesn't," which the text gave me fresh, unprompted, and I trust my read of it as real.

**Erotic charge.** Mostly at a low simmer — this is a talking-about-sex chapter, not a sex scene, and it works as such: the retelling itself becomes an act of intimacy and mild exhibitionism between the two women, peaking at the photo. It never goes slack; even the parts about music and quiet in his house carry warmth without needing nudity to do it.

**Friction.** None really — the chapter's shapelessness (Vee's own line, "no edges, no place it began") is clearly intentional and well-executed, a deliberate contrast to the scene-driven chapters around it. If anything the two goodbye-kiss beats (Vee leaning in for an extra half-second, then Randi holding the eye contact "a beat past what a goodbye needed") are laid on a little symmetrically — but they track the checkpoint's established escalation motif rather than feeling like new authorial thumb-on-scale.

**Titles.** "All the Time" — I first read it as Vee's own line about the volume of sex ("we have so much sex... all the time"), which the chapter delivers on the surface. But by the end it also seems to gesture at the whole *texture* of the relationship becoming continuous rather than occasional — "it's just how it *is* now" — and, uneasily, at Randi being present in that "all the time" too, in a way Vee doesn't examine. It didn't give anything away going in; it recolors mildly in hindsight. The book title and tagline ("Every yes was freely given. That was the problem") loom over this chapter specifically because every yes here — the elective, the photo, the extra kiss — is Vee's own, happily given, and the chapter doesn't argue with that; it just lets me watch the "problem" quietly accumulating underneath the yeses.

**What I want next.** I want to see Randi alone, or with Pace, to find out what that unguarded "It doesn't" was actually about — grief, jealousy, longing, all three. And I'm curious what "just us" in the new anthro class will do, now that it's explicitly a plan for time without Cassie or Pace. Guess from here: that class becomes another private space where whatever's building between Vee and Randi gets room to keep building.
