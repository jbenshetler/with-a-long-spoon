# Cold read (grounded) — Old Acquaintances

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one gutted me a little, in the quiet way rather than the loud one. We just watched "Fairytale" from Randi's side — the ski slopes, John/the tailcoat, the sleigh, the hotel room where she lay against a perfect stranger and felt nothing, then sent Vee that glittering, evasive text — and now "Old Acquaintances" hands us the same night from underneath, from Vee's childhood bed, and it recontextualizes everything. We already knew Randi's "fairytale out here" was a performance built over an ache. Vee doesn't know that. She reads it and believes every word, and it wrecks her a little more than it would have if she'd never seen the man asleep behind it. That gap — what I know, what she doesn't — is doing exactly the ache-work this book keeps doing to me, but this time it isn't about the game or the secret couple. It's smaller and sadder: two women lying awake in different beds on the same New Year's, each one certain the other has it better.

**Vee** — I ache for her here more than almost anywhere in the run so far. The detail of her old room "knowing Vivienne" and not knowing who's actually in the bed now is devastating and exact — "It had known Vivienne — knew her still, in high resolution, and loved her," while she herself no longer fits it. The bunting, the swimming trophies, the "gold-fringed best-in-show," Wheel of Fortune, the lavender plug-ins — all rendered with such specific tenderness that I felt her whole outgrown-legs, small-in-the-bed physical discomfort. And the cruelty she does to herself — inventing a blonde rival, then hijacking her own best memory (the fra diavolo dinner, the sheet slipping) and re-populating it with that invented girl, "the one at the table in the falling sheet stopped being her" — is such a precise, awful thing minds do at 11pm alone. I wanted to reach into the page and stop her.

**Randi**, seen only through the single incoming text here, reads differently to me now than she would have cold, because I just watched her compose it from inside a stranger's hotel room, performing "fairytale" while genuinely wretched. Vee takes it completely straight — "She was fine. Better than fine... a devastatingly handsome billionaire" — and I sat there wanting to shout through the page that it's the opposite, that Randi is also lying awake essentially alone, just somewhere colder and better-lit. Dramatic irony doing exactly what it's been doing all book, but here it's not about the game or the threesome-seed, it's just two lonely people failing to reach each other on the one night reaching would have helped most.

**Pace** doesn't appear at all except as absence and invented-rival material, and that absence is the whole engine of the chapter. I don't feel new suspicion of him from this chapter specifically — his silence over the break reads consistent with a man who, per the "Fairytale" fight, genuinely doesn't have the word she needs and won't manufacture one, rather than a man withdrawing cruelly. But I do feel the accumulating weight of "words are cheap, believe what I do" starting to cost something real now that there's distance and no proof in front of her.

**Swoon / pull** — less swoon here, more ache-pull; I wanted to keep reading specifically to see if either of them breaks the silence. The one moment that did something warm to me was almost involuntary and small: "She lay still with her feet at the footboard and let herself have the thing everyone else was having somewhere tonight — arms around her, his arms" — and then the immediate collapse of that comfort into "whose he had them around instead." That's a real, felt swing between tenderness and dread, done in one breath, and it worked on me.

**Erotic charge** — essentially zero, deliberately. This is a cooling-off chapter, all longing and no touch, and it's stronger for it — the one sensory flash (fra diavolo, the sheet slipping) exists specifically to be corrupted by her own jealousy rather than to titillate.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — I don't distrust Pace or Randi any more from this chapter itself; if anything my sympathy for Randi widened, since I have information Vee lacks that makes Randi's silence look like self-protection rather than indifference. My unease is structural, not new: I already know things Vee doesn't, and this chapter just tightens the screw on that knowledge rather than adding fresh grounds for doubt.

**Friction as a reader** — none really; the invented-rival passage risked feeling like a device, but it's grounded so specifically in Vee's psychology (spite-built, blonde, "a stripper's" versus "a fashion model's body," her own self-loathing vocabulary surfacing) that it read true rather than mechanical.

**Titles** — "Old Acquaintances" is doing real work for me: New Year's Eve, "should auld acquaintance be forgot," and Vee is surrounded by exactly that — the girls on the corkboard who don't text anymore, the room that remembers a Vivienne who's gone, the clarinet retired in its case. But it also glances sideways at Randi and Pace themselves — old acquaintances now become something else, something she can't name to her parents or even to herself. It didn't give anything away going in; it recolored gently once I was inside it. As for the book/series titles and the tagline I was given — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter doesn't touch consent directly, but it does show me the cost side of the same architecture: nothing was taken from Vee, and yet she's lying alone in the dark, constructing her own suffering out of silence and a text that lied to her without technically lying. That tracks with "the problem" the tagline names — not force, but the slow damage of things freely entered and left unnamed.

**What I want next** — I want the break to end and want to see whether Pace reaches out first, or whether Vee has to. I'm also newly curious whether Randi's side of this — coming home from a hollow "fairytale" to a friend she's still hiding her real feelings from — starts to crack under its own weight. Guess from here: I suspect the return to campus will force some kind of reckoning between Vee and Pace before it forces one between Vee and Randi, since Pace's silence has a harder deadline (the relationship itself) than Randi's does.
