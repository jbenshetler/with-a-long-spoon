## Reader reaction

This one hurt in the good way — the specific, physical way. I'm back with Vee for the first time in a while, and the chapter drops me straight into her body: the childhood bedroom too small now, the lavender, the bent knees from a bed she's outgrown. That opening image — "The room did not know her. It had known Vivienne" — did something to me immediately. The split between the girl the trophies remember and the woman in the bed is the whole chapter in one line, and it's devastating without being showy about it.

**Vee** — my sympathy for her deepens hard here. The cropped photo detail lands as confirmed now, from her own side: she scissored her own leg off the picture she sent home, self-censoring the exact thing that made her feel like herself, because her parents can't know what the dress *did*. That's such a precise, quiet cruelty she does to herself. And then the spiral — inventing a rival out of spite, watching her own best memory get hijacked by an imagined blonde mid-fantasy — "She could not keep her own night" gutted me. That's desire and grief and self-erasure all braided together and I felt every turn of it.

**Randi**, seen only through her text and Vee's projection, is dramatically ironic now in a way that's almost unbearable given what I know from the last chapter — Vee reads "never coming home... miss your face most though, gorgeous" as evidence of Randi's charmed, easy, enviable life, when I know it's a curated lie sitting on top of a hollow, cold night and a private, concealed love for Vee herself. Vee envies exactly the performance Randi built to hide her loneliness. The dramatic irony is the engine of the whole chapter for me.

**Pace** stays offstage but is everywhere — the stove, the garlic, the building-then-giving mirrored in his hands in the dark. Vee's helpless spiral around "who is he cooking for" felt true to how absence works: not knowing becomes its own kind of knowing, worse than the truth.

**Trust/suspicion**: nothing off here — this reads as pure, earned interiority. No manipulation, no plot machinery, just a woman alone at midnight losing an argument with her own mind.

**Erotic charge**: low and correctly so — this is a grief chapter, not a heat chapter. The one image with charge (the sheet slipping, the wine, "nothing that needed saying") is deliberately *un*-charged by the end, corrupted by jealousy into something that can't be kept.

**Friction**: none. The prose earns its length — the accumulation of small deadening domestic details (Wheel of Fortune, the casserole, "the news muttering low, always something dire") does real work building the beige weight she's escaping.

**Title** — "Old Acquaintances": on the nose in the best way once I finish — it's Auld Lang Syne, New Year's, and also literally about who she used to be (Vivienne) versus who she is now, and the ache of holding both at midnight. Lands well, doesn't give anything away early.

I want the rupture to break — I want a message, I want Pace to say something, anything. The chapter ends with her "still reaching" and nothing coming after "and what about Pace," and I feel that unresolved reach too.
