## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction**

Oh, this one has teeth. I felt for Vee hard in the opening — the red-eye, the empty room, lying in her plane clothes through "the whole drab, colorless day" — that's such an accurate rendering of grief-adjacent heartbreak, and I ached for her walking into Randi's not trusting herself to look in the mirror. And then Randi catches her, and for the first page or two it's pure relief: the hug that goes "past the end of it," the smell of her neck steadying Vee, Brooke's unbidden kindness downstairs. I wanted this comfort for Vee as much as she wanted it.

But watching Randi work her through it, I felt the ground shift under me. This is the most naked instance yet of what I've only been told about before — Randi taking Vee's raw material and shaping it into exactly the outcome Randi wants, while never once appearing to want anything herself. "Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do" — reframing Pace's evasiveness as tenderness, which, fine, might even be *true*, but it's suspicious how neatly every question walks Vee toward "you did this to him" and away from any anger of her own. And then the coat. Randi doesn't tell her to do it — she leaves the sentence hanging and lets Vee complete it, "And nothing under it," so it can feel like Vee's own idea, her own daring, when the shape of it was built and placed by Randi word by word. That's the chapter's whole architecture in miniature: Randi's hand on the string, and Vee's belief that she chose.

And it lands on the reader-knowledge I'm carrying like a gut-punch: Vee doesn't know Sheri is real but *not a rival* — short, round-faced, ordinary, grieving her own lost girlfriend, cooked for out of loneliness, not lust. Vee's invented "blonde, tan, built like a model" is wrong in every particular, and Randi — who has no way to know the truth either, to be fair — waves the whole fear away as three-a.m. invention rather than engaging with the one true thing buried in it (there *is* another woman, just not this one, not like this). I don't think Randi is lying here so much as she's confidently wrong, but the effect on Vee is the same: comfort built on sand.

The ski story is its own small ache — Randi narrating a fairy tale and then going quiet exactly at "and then it got very late," the one door she doesn't open even for Vee. Vee reads it as discretion; I read it, with everything I already know about Randi's self-concealment, as one more instance of the curated version — she gives Vee warmth and story, keeps the interior locked.

Erotic charge is low-key but present — mostly anticipatory, in the coat, the shame-then-want turning-over Vee feels when she says "slutty" out loud and Randi reframes it as bravery. That whole exchange is charged precisely because it's coercive-by-consent — Randi doesn't force anything, she just keeps talking until Vee's own resistance collapses into agreement, and I felt the vertigo of that as a reader, not just approval of the heat.

**Title:** "My Friend Randi" — it reads almost naive in its warmth, the kind of title a much younger Vee might put on this chapter in her own head, and that plainness is exactly what unsettled me on second look. It doesn't gesture at anything sinister — it's sincere, it's what Vee would call this chapter — and that sincerity is the point: this is *how Vee experiences it*, friendship, safety, being seen. The reader knows more.

**What I want next:** dread, mostly. I want to see Vee go to Pace's door — and I'm braced for it to be either devastating (if the "brunette" thread surfaces at exactly the wrong moment) or for it to work, in which case I'll have to sit with how much of that "win" was actually authored by Randi. Guess from here: this coat-and-nothing-under-it plan is heading straight into collision with whatever the sorority brunette actually means to Pace.
