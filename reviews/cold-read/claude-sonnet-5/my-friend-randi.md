# Cold read — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

Back to Vee, and back to the sentence that opens with her crying most of the way in from the airport — that landed on me instantly, no runway needed. I know things she doesn't (Sheri is a friend, not a rival; Pace confirmed he's "there every weekend," misses her, wants her) and reading her build the wrong fear brick by brick was genuinely painful. Not painful because I doubt her — because I trust the ache is real and misdirected, and I can't stop it.

Randi is the whole chapter, and she is doing something I want to sit with rather than rush past. On the surface this is the most tender scene we've had between them — the wordless hug at the door, held past where a hug stops, Vee breathing in Randi's smell to steady herself, Randi noticing the wrecked girl in her doorway before her own half-unpacked room. That's real and it worked on me completely; I believed the love in it. But underneath the comfort, Randi is doing something I don't think Vee can see happening to her: walking her, question by question, away from her own instinct — "Do you actually believe he was using you? Or is that the fear talking?" — toward a conclusion that happens to serve Randi (send Vee to Pace, tonight, in a coat and nothing under it) rather than toward Vee actually finding out anything true. The line that got me was "You backed him into a corner in his own house... How do you think that felt, from where he was standing?" — reasonable-sounding, and also completely reframing Vee's grief as Vee's fault, with zero acknowledgment that Pace withheld the one thing she asked for. I don't know if Randi believes what she's saying or is managing Vee toward an outcome. Both readings sit on the page at once and I'm not going to force it to one.

The coat plan is where my stomach actually turned, in the good/bad complicated way this book has trained me to expect. "You just show up already being the answer" — and Vee finishing the sentence herself, "And nothing under it" — is staged as Vee's own desire surfacing through shame, and I do buy that the wanting is hers, lit by the transgression rather than manufactured whole-cloth by Randi. But Randi orchestrated the conditions for it so precisely — leaving the sentence hanging, handing her the shoes, deciding "Randi always knew the shoes" — that I can't tell anymore where Vee's agency ends and Randi's engineering begins. That ambiguity is the charge of the whole book so far and this chapter turns the dial up hard on it.

The John story is its own small ache — Randi narrating a fairy tale and then going quiet exactly at "we went up," the one thing she keeps behind a door even from Vee, even now knowing what I know about Randi's feelings for Vee (the concealment, the "fairytale" text as engineered half-truth from an earlier chapter). Vee reading the silence as "discretion" is dramatic irony I feel in my chest — I know there's more going on with Randi than modesty, though I don't know what.

Erotic charge: mostly anticipatory and psychological rather than physical on the page — the "nothing under it" moment is the peak, charged entirely through shame-into-want rather than any actual sex scene, and it worked, it's a slow burn that lands.

Friction: none really — the writing trusts me to sit with the ambiguity rather than explaining it, which I appreciated (nobody moralizes at Randi the way Sheri moralized at Pace last chapter).

Title — "My Friend Randi" reads plainly warm on first glance and gets a little more loaded the longer I sit with it, given what I know and Vee doesn't: is "friend" doing quiet, bittersweet work, marking the limit of what Vee thinks this is versus what I suspect it might not be for Randi? It doesn't feel like a trap door so much as a soft ache placed under an ordinary phrase.

Going forward I want to know if Vee actually goes to Pace's door tonight, what happens when she does, and whether the coat plan reads as triumphant or awful once it plays out — and I'm bracing a little, because Randi built this plan and I don't fully trust the hand that built it.
