## Reader reaction

This chapter knocked the wind out of me a little, in a good way. After the unease of the last one — Randi's extraction, the kiss, my worry for Vee — the book pivots hard back to Pace, and it's such a relief and such a swoon at once that I have to sit with why. The buildup is gorgeous: the flannel shirt gone thin with wear, the green satin bought "for the color," the toenail polish painted early so it'll be perfect, the agonizing over shoes that "said out loud the thing she was trying not to." All of it is Vee performing casualness while every gesture betrays how much this matters to her, and I felt that ache of a girl getting ready for a night she's told herself means nothing.

*Floozie* — the mother's voice — is doing serious, precise work here. It arrives right as her body responds and keeps running underneath the whole scene, "the same wire" as the heat, no seam between them. That's the chapter's real achievement for me: it doesn't resolve the shame into pleasure, or let pleasure erase the shame — it holds both, running simultaneously, and the line "the shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her" landed as the emotional peak of the whole scene, more than the orgasm itself. That's writing about desire with real interiority, exactly what I read for.

Pace is enormously trustworthy on the page here — the question "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?", the patience going down her legs "like a man learning ground he meant to come back to," staying in the wet of her rather than moving past her shame, the care with his own weight until she pulls him in. Every one of these beats reads as real attentiveness, not performance of attentiveness. My earlier ledger-suspicion about his motives doesn't surface once here — this chapter argues, hard, for his tenderness being simply true, at least in the room.

The ending — "Trade" — got me. The folded panties handed over like something formal and total, "something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private," is such an odd, specific, moving gesture. It reads like a vow dressed as a joke, and I don't fully know what it means yet, which I like.

New detail: a large scar high on his inner thigh, her hand stopping on it, then moving past it unremarked. That's a flag I'm holding — not dread, just a genuinely new fact about his body and history that the text pointedly doesn't explain.

Erotic charge: this is the most explicit and most successful the book has been sexually — heat and tenderness both fully rendered, nothing coy, nothing clinical. It peaks in the standing orgasm scene and never goes slack.

I want to know what "Trade" meant to him, whether Randi will surface again and complicate this fresh tenderness, and what that scar is. No dread yet toward Pace — just toward everything outside this room.
