## Reader reaction

This one landed differently than I expected — quieter, more unsettled, less pure-joy than last chapter, even though on its surface it's another "recounting the date" scene like the one with Cassie.

The opening beat with Cassie got me first. That look — Cassie's eyes going past Vee to Randi, "lit up, already reaching for her," then folding away by the time they came back to Vee — I don't know what to make of that yet, and I like that I don't. Is it hurt? Jealousy? Just Cassie clocking that class is over and Randi's already got Vee? It's small, but it's the kind of small the book has taught me to watch. Then Vee's own quick self-talk — "it was fine to have your own people," settling herself, while "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie" — that's Vee doing the thing this book keeps showing her doing: noticing a wrongness and choosing not to look at it. I trust the text enough now to flag that as deliberate, not incidental.

Randi is warm here, genuinely funny (the "beet and wheatgrass" order "like a sentence handed down" made me laugh), and her public claiming of Vee — "dear friend," the hand on the table in front of Brooke and the silent taller girl — is a real, moving beat. Vee's relief and gratitude at being *picked*, at "the place was here, next to Randi," is affecting and I believed it completely as a feeling. But I can't un-know what I know: Randi is the person who engineered Vee's meeting with Pace, and here she is again shaping how Vee narrates her own desire — "You let him choose for you. And you liked it," landing not as a question but as something Randi is confirming, almost testing. It reads as friendship on the surface and, to me, faintly as continued handling underneath. I'm not certain of that reading; I'm holding it.

The smoothie shop itself is the sharpest writing in the chapter for me — Vee going from lit-up to "underdressed before she had looked at a single person," feeling like "a thing the room could turn and look at." That's the body-as-public-property motif again, and it's working. Brooke is fine, glossy and forgettable-friendly. But the unnamed taller woman — coffee in hand, "a slow, easy circuit of the table. Of Vee" and then, leaving, "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — that gave me a small chill. Appraising, almost predatory in its detachment. I don't know who she is. I'm not going to guess she's Sloane or Kinsey just because those names exist; the page didn't say so.

Erotic charge is low this chapter — no scene with Pace at all — but there's a real current of intimacy in the Randi/Vee physical contact (the cool damp hand, the hug, "love you, girl") that reads as tender rather than sexual, and I take it at that register.

The title, "Dear" — has to be the "dear friend" line, and now that I've read the chapter it recolors that whole beat: it's the word that costs Randi something to say and buys Vee her sense of belonging in one stroke. Simple, well-earned title, no giveaway.

I want to know who the silent taller woman is, whether Cassie's folded-away look means anything, and whether Randi's gentle validation of "you let him choose" is friendship or something else. Not dreading, just watching closer than last chapter.
