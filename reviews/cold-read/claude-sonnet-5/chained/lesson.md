## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction**

This one hurt in a good way — the kind of chapter that makes you want to reach through the page and grab Vee by the shoulders. The dancing lesson at the top is just lovely: patient, unshowy, specific ("give me something to push back against... being jelly doesn't work"), and I felt her body figuring it out before her mind did — "she stopped guessing... the pressure of her hand on his shoulder became readable, actual information." That's a real seduction, and it's not sexual at all, which makes it land harder. I was fully swooning through the pad scene and the first song at the bar.

Then the turn. A stranger's poisoned little compliment — "knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to... he's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night" — and I watched Vee's whole interior architecture collapse in real time. The line that got me was "*The girls.* All the bright smiles and shiny eyes and big hugs for Pace. She'd let herself feel like an exception. She was looking at the rule." I understood exactly why she said what she said to him, and I still winced — "spread her legs like a slut," "Floozies" — that's her mother's voice (the "Floozy" motif from before, dormant, suddenly loud) doing violence to her own self-image, redirected outward at other women and then at herself. This is ugly and true and I don't think the book is asking me to like her for it — it's asking me to recognize it.

Pace's exit is the chapter's quiet gut-punch: no scene, no raised voice, just "the good mood just draining off him, all at once" and him walking out into the cold with his beer untouched. I trust him more after this than before — he answered her straight, didn't perform hurt, didn't punish her back.

The bartender is a small but perfect corrective voice — "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey" — and I believed her completely; nothing about her reads unreliable or convenient, she's just a person who's watched this room for years.

The ending devastated me a little: Vee in his shirt, unable to call Cassie because she can't say the words yet, holding both the shame and the sensation of the lead at once, deciding by morning to go fix it at Randi's — not Cassie's, not Pace's door. That choice landed strange on me, a flicker of unease I can't yet name, given everything I already know about Randi's steering hand that Vee doesn't.

**Trust/suspicion:** none newly earned against Pace — if anything this chapter is the strongest evidence yet that he's decent under pressure. My unease is entirely about Vee's shame-spiral and her choice to go to Randi rather than Cassie.

**Erotic charge:** none explicit — this chapter runs on tension of a different kind, the charge of bodies moving in sync, then the charge of jealousy and self-disgust. It worked on me anyway.

**Title:** "Lesson" is doing double work by the end — the dance lesson, obviously, but also what Vee has to learn about herself and what she does to good things she's afraid to trust. Feels earned, not cute.

I want the next chapter badly — I want to see what she says to Randi, and whether she goes to Pace at all, and I'm holding my breath a little on that "not how... but what" ambiguity.
