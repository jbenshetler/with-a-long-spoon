## Reader reaction

This one knocked the wind out of me a little, and not from the sex — from what got said in the pillow talk before and after it.

**Pace and Randi together, finally on the page** — first time I've seen them in a scene rather than heard about them, and it recalibrates a few things at once. He's tender here in a way that matches everything Vee's felt from him secondhand — "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body," said "low, certain, without heat," and "I want her to get there on her own." That's not a predator's patience, or it doesn't read like one to me. It reads like real, careful want. My guard around Pace, which has been mild but present since the scar and the "beautiful idiot" business, eased a notch here.

Randi is the gut-punch. Her retelling of the shoe-store scene matches what I already loved from her own chapter, but hearing her say "She got past me before I could help it" — with the implication that she *tried* not to like Vee, that liking her was a failure of some resolve — recontextualizes the whole friendship. That's not the voice of someone running an extraction op with a clear head. That sounds like someone losing a fight she meant to win. And then: "I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come." That "you always get bored" is a small, cold window into Pace's pattern that neither he nor Randi elaborates — and I clocked it hard.

And then the moment I keep turning over: Pace, praising Vee's effect on a room, unprompted, unguarded — and Randi goes *still*, "not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding." Then, a beat too long, "Yeah. She is." That's not jealousy exactly — it's something more like grief or recognition, and I can't fully name it, which I think is the point. It sits right next to her sliding into sex with him a moment later, which reads almost like a redirection, a thing she does with her body because she can't sit inside that feeling.

**Erotic charge** — real but not the center of gravity for me. The physical writing is good (Sade under the door, her toes still polished blue, the drag of her body along his) but I was more riveted by the conversation than the act; the sex felt almost like punctuation on the feeling that preceded it, which given "he let her, and there was nothing quiet left in either of them now" — feels earned rather than tacked on.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — this chapter complicates my suspicion of Randi rather than resolving it. It's genuinely harder to read her now as a cold operator when I've just watched her almost-confess something like real, resisted love for Vee. But "you'll want to take your time," "she's not ready" — Randi is still coaching him, still fluent in managing Vee's arrival into whatever this is. So the extraction/steering read from last chapter doesn't go away; it gets a second, warmer engine sitting right alongside it.

**Title** — "Gone." Randi says Vee's "gone over" Pace, but the word lands harder on Randi herself by the end — gone still, gone quiet, maybe gone for Vee in a way she can't afford to say. Nicely ambiguous, earned rather than telegraphed.

**Pull forward** — I want to know what Randi's stillness *means*, and whether Vee ever gets to see this side of either of them.
