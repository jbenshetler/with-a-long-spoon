## Reader reaction

This chapter got its hooks in me and I loved almost every minute of it, even while a low hum of unease kept threading underneath.

**Vee** — radiant, and I felt for her hardest at the counter, that half-second holding the debit card before she puts it back and takes out the credit card she can't actually afford to use. "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it." That's the moment the chapter earned real weight for me — not a swoon, an ache. She's spending money she doesn't have to be worthy of a gift, and nobody made her do it, which is somehow worse.

**Randi** — she is *so good* at this scene, and that's exactly what unsettles me. The pratfall shoe comedy is genuinely delightful, real, unforced — the yeti boot, the rhinestone platforms, Randi folded over the bench laughing "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give." I believed that. But then the second store: "It goes on these," the thumb across Vee's toenail, "Mm" — and Randi effortlessly signing for a multiple of what she just watched Vee agonize over, never looking at the number. The generosity reads as real and the carelessness of easy money reads as real, but the effect on Vee — steering her past the sensible shoe toward the one that "the dress was owed" — is Randi's taste and Randi's money quietly overriding Vee's own judgment, dressed as warmth. I don't think Randi is being cruel. I'm not sure she's being fully honest with herself either.

**Cassie** — absent, only glimpsed in the made bed's "nobody home," which felt like a small callback rather than a scene.

**Pace** — absent but present as the engine of the whole afternoon; "he made it *to* you" lands as beautifully as it's meant to.

**Swoon / pull:** the shoe-store comedy is the best pure-joy stretch the book's given me — I was laughing with them. And "you did not, you *could* not, set a thing like that down onto shoes that were an apology" is seductive logic I felt seduce *me*, even as I clocked it as seduction.

**Erotic charge:** low and precise — the toenail-touch is the whole charge of the chapter, one thumb-stroke doing more than a page of description would.

**Friction:** none really — no over-explaining, it trusted the reader.

**Titles:** "Sorority" is oblique to me — nothing overtly sorority-related happened (no induction, no other girls beyond a glimpsed older couple). Maybe it's pointing at the bond between Vee and Randi itself as its own private sorority — or foreshadowing the dress's actual debut. Held open.

**Want next:** I want to see the induction/party where the dress finally gets shown to more than "one living soul," and I'm bracing a little for what it costs Vee financially and emotionally that this chapter just quietly loaded onto her.
