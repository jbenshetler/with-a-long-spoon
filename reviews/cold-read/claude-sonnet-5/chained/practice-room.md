## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction**

I need to sit with this one for a second before I can write anything coherent about it. My body's still in that practice room.

Vee — I keep loving her, and this chapter cracks her open further than the last one did. Her impulse to run and tell Randi *first*, before she's even fully awake, before Cassie, before anyone — that's not new, but seeing her physically tow Randi out of a room by two fingers on the wrist, reversing weeks of the other direction, landed on me as real growth in confidence and also as something else I can't name yet, a hunger to be *seen* by Randi specifically. And then the confession itself turns into something she can't control the shape of — she starts out proud, telling "the good part," and Randi's questions ("Did you deserve it?") walk her, almost surgically, into re-experiencing it in her body, right there, in front of her. The line "she wasn't only telling it. She was doing it again" — that's the whole chapter for me. It's gorgeous and it's a little frightening, the way Randi does that to her with nothing but a voice and patience.

Randi — this is the sharpest, most unsettling I've found her. Her own confession (the husband, the spanking, "it made me buck") reads at first like generous mirroring, offering vulnerability for vulnerability — but it's also, unmistakably, a tool, deployed at the exact moment to push Vee over an edge Randi can watch her go over. "Sometimes it's the mouse that roars," "Oh, baby" — so warm, so knowing, and I can't tell if I'm supposed to trust the warmth or watch it as technique. Probably both. That's the discomfort the book has earned honestly — I've watched Randi extract and steer for chapters now, and this is the first time it's shaded into something that felt, to me, almost cruel in its precision, even though nothing she does is unkind on its face.

**Swoon / pull:** The erotic charge here is the most intense the book's given me — not through action but through *telling*, the physiological betrayal of Vee's body responding to a story the way it responded to the thing itself. The bathroom scene, her hand moving before she decides anything, catching it and holding it flat against her knee — "Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it" — that refusal is doing enormous work. It's not modesty, it's something more like self-protection, a boundary she's drawing without being able to say against what.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** My suspicion of Randi sharpens here — not that she's malicious, but that her intimacy always seems to be doing two things at once, comfort and extraction, and this chapter is the first time I felt the extraction win out over the comfort, at least for a few minutes. Randi's ease at the end — bag on shoulder, breezy goodbye — after what just happened in that room unsettles me. Does she know exactly what she did? The text won't say, and that ambiguity is its own kind of pressure.

**Friction:** None that snagged me — this felt controlled and earned throughout, even the outlier lecture doing quiet thematic work ("never all the way gone") without hammering it.

**Title:** "The Practice Room" — on the surface, literal, the tiny soundproof cell. But it lands differently now: a room for rehearsing something, for going through the motions of a thing until your body knows it without your mind's permission. Given what happens in there, it feels exactly, quietly right — not a giveaway, but perfectly apt once read.

**Book title / series title:** Nothing new to add — "A Polite Invitation" still hovers over Randi's whole mode of operating; this chapter is one more invitation, and Vee keeps accepting them without knowing quite what she's agreeing to.

**What I want next:** I want to know if Vee tells Cassie any of this — I doubt it. I want to see whether Randi's precision here gets addressed, ever, by anyone, or whether it just keeps accumulating unspoken. And I'm newly worried, in a way I wasn't before, about what happens when Randi decides to do more than talk.
