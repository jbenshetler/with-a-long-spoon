## Reader reaction

Oh, that landed like a small, quiet gut-punch. I was fully inside Vee for this one — the whole slow architecture of the sentence she'd been building for weeks, the "crackers... a meal" line she'd polished and polished, and I felt the risk in it before she even opened her mouth. The prose earns every second of that silence after she says it: "his stillness for one beat that was a beat longer than his stillness had been the moment before," him taking two breaths where usually there's one — that's Pace, careful and controlled, actually rattled for once, and it's the most legible he's ever been to me.

And then "I love how good we are together." I felt the exact same drop Vee felt. It's warm, it's not nothing — the pulling her closer, the long kiss on her head, "I've never" — but it answers a different question than the one she asked. She said something about being *fed*, about being *known*; he answered about performance, about "what your body does." I don't think he's lying or performing cruelty — I think he genuinely doesn't, or won't, meet her at the level she offered. That's the ache. It's not betrayal, it's a mismatch, and the book lets it just sit there uninterpreted, which I respect completely — Vee herself can't resolve it ("the one person who could resolve it was the one person she could not ask"), so neither do I.

**Trust/suspicion:** nothing sinister here — Pace's response reads as real tenderness, just tenderness of a different shape than what she needed. I don't distrust him; I ache for the gap.

**Erotic charge:** low and that's right — this is intimacy-charge, not heat: the quilt, his hand on her shin, the deliberateness of being held. It works entirely on tenderness, not desire.

**Friction:** none — the writing trusts the reader completely, no over-explaining. The laundry-room coda, replaying the sentence two days later, is exactly the kind of interior return I want from this book.

**Title — "Seconds":** lovely and exact once I finish the chapter — the second cup of coffee, the second breath he takes, her sentence and his as two unmatched "seconds" to each other, maybe even "seconds" as in a lesser helping — she gave him the whole meal-sentence and got something smaller back. Didn't give anything away going in.

**Pull forward:** I want to see whether this small unresolved hurt festers or gets absorbed the way everything else has ("she had had to do small repair work to keep functioning" — that phrase alarms me a little, retroactively, about how many of these she's already banked).
