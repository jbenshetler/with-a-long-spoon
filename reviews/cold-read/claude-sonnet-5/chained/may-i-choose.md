## Reader reaction

Cassie's line — "That's not a boy. That's a man." — landed exactly where I distrust things most: a warm friend, confirming a feeling I'm not sure I trust either. I want to like this chapter simply, and mostly I do, and that's what unsettles me about it. This is the most seductive Pace has been on the page yet, and I felt it working on me the way it's working on Vee — the "May I choose?" line did something to my chest too, and I'm aware enough to name that as the swoon rather than pretend I'm above it.

**Pace** — the attentiveness here is staggering in its precision: the right hand always the right hand, the drip of dip caught and eaten off his own skin without ceremony, ordering food she can't read as a kind of intimacy by proxy, weighing the menu "not because he needed it but to weigh what he was choosing for her." Every gesture reads as genuine care and I have no textual reason to call it performance — except that I already know, from the carry-forward, that this whole courtship began as an engineered exercise in choosing a target. That knowledge is doing all the unease-work here; the page itself offers nothing but tenderness. That gap — between what I'm shown and what I already know — is the chapter's whole charge for me.

**Vee** — devastating to watch her self-vigilance dissolve course by course: losing the thread of his sentence and not scrambling to catch it, not noticing she'd stopped watching herself eat, that "low background readiness... carried everywhere" just absent. The line "she let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could" is the saddest and sweetest sentence in the chapter — sad because of what it implies she's always had to carry alone, sweet because of how completely he seems to be offering to take it. Her rule-breaking kiss is earned and warm and I wanted it for her, wholly, even while some colder part of me flinched at how fast the "exception" arrived.

**Swoon / pull** — the food, the hand-holding as "something resolving," the whole restaurant sequence: yes, absolutely, this worked on me at full strength. I was there in the softness with her.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — nothing new textually indicts him; my suspicion is entirely imported from what I know and Vee doesn't. That's an honest split to hold, not a contradiction.

**Erotic charge** — 1, maybe pushing toward 2 emotionally — the food-as-seduction (feeding her literally, choosing for her, the "opening at the ends" foreign words she can't read) is more sensual than the kiss itself, which stays almost chaste on the page.

**Title** — "May I Choose" is the line and also, chillingly, the whole book's engine given back to me plainly: he has been choosing her since before she knew there was a choice to make. It didn't give the plot away so much as it let me feel, retroactively, how loaded a request that ought to sound.

**What I want next** — I want to see this collide with what I know. I'm dreading, a little, the moment Vee finds out — and dreading more that this chapter has made me want to protect the softness she's found even though I know its foundation is compromised.
