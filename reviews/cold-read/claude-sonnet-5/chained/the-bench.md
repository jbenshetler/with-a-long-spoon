## Reader reaction

I went into this cold — no packet memory of characters beyond the jacket blurb — and the chapter plays a real trick on me: for the entire scene I have no name for "she." I default, because of the jacket copy, to assuming this must be Vee — young woman, glamorous, a man who "asks before he takes and leaves her room to choose," courtly and exact. The details build a very specific person: sorority girl, monogrammed overnight bag, black hair, startling blue eyes, mother who lives on egg whites, apple-portioning, the practiced beautiful face. It reads like Vee. And then — the sentence that turns the whole chapter over: *"There you are, Randi."* I actually had to stop and reread it. This is not Vee. This is Randi — the "glamorous new friend" from the jacket who is supposed to be steering Vee toward Pace, not in bed with him herself, called by name in the most intimate, undone moment of the night. That's a real jolt, and a good one — it recalibrates everything I'd been building. So Pace and Randi are already involved, already deep in something built and practiced (the bench "he had built... two winters ago," a returning rhythm, "the first night," "the second visit") — this isn't a beginning, it's already established, ongoing.

**Pace** — I find him magnetic and unsettling in the same breath. He's attentive to an extraordinary, almost engineered degree (the apple slices cut thin because she eats them faster that way, the water bottle left in advance, the blanket she likes) — and that attentiveness reads as real tenderness *and* as a kind of expertise, a man who has studied her the way you'd study an instrument. The scene where he refuses to let her come, waiting for her "please" to become a real please rather than a performed one, is where I feel both swooned and faintly cold — it's the most controlled, most devastating thing in the chapter. It works on me as heat and as something more clinical: he is *taking her apart on purpose*, and he knows exactly what he's doing and why, and he's pleased with himself for it ("the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go"). The backstory fragment about Daphne — a door he "had not respected," a lesson learned — gives him depth and history, and makes his current restraint (not following her into the bathroom) read as genuine growth, not performance. I don't distrust him exactly, but I clock how much he is *managing* her, gently, expertly, always for what reads as her good — and I'm watching that closely.

**Randi** — she's the gut-punch of the chapter. The bathroom mirror scene is devastating: she looks at her own face and decides, in real time, to bury the person who just came out from behind it. "He could have the face... The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." That's a genuinely sad, specific piece of self-betrayal, rendered without a false note. I ache for her. And knowing from the jacket that this is the same Randi who is supposed to be "steering" Vee toward Pace — I don't yet know how that fits, but I already feel a weird vertigo about it: is this the same relationship the jacket describes, already secretly present, or does the jacket's Randi/Pace/Vee triangle happen *after* whatever this is? I genuinely don't know, and I'm holding that open rather than guessing.

**Swoon vs. unease** — both are strong and not opposed. The physical writing (the honeycrisp, the cashmere blanket, "there you are") lands as real tenderness that I believe. But the engineered quality of it — a bench built specifically for her, a script he clearly recognizes and is dismantling ("There was a script for it... The bench was not there") — gives me the same unease the jacket promised: "psychologically merciless in its tenderness." I feel that phrase justified already.

**Erotic charge** — very high, sustained, and it earns its length; the pacing of denial and return kept me genuinely there rather than skimming.

**Titles** — "The Bench" is plain and literal until it isn't; by the end it's clearly the chapter's central object and metaphor — furniture that reveals its true purpose only when you're already inside the room. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* both feel newly loaded: "polite" now reads ironic against how unpolite/exposing this scene actually is, and "long spoon" (an idiom about dining with the devil) makes me wary of Pace and of whatever arrangement put Randi on this bench in the first place.

**What I want next** — I want to know how this connects to Vee. Is Randi already involved with Pace before Vee is "steered" toward him? Is this chronologically earlier, or is something else going on? I'm hooked and slightly braced.
