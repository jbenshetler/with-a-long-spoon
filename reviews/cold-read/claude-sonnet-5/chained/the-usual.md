## Reader reaction

Well — that's a door I didn't expect to walk through, and I'm glad I did. This is the first time Pace has been the one in the room, not a memory of him, and it changes the temperature of everything I've been carrying. He is *tender* here, genuinely, unguardedly — with Sheri, who isn't performing for him and doesn't want anything off him, which he says himself is rare and precious to him. I like him more for how he is with her: he lets her rib him, he laughs the real laugh at the end, he sits with her grief about her family without trying to fix it ("People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice"). That's a good man in that booth. And I ache for Sheri too — new person, fully drawn in one sitting: the girlfriend lost to the unfixable wound of Sheri's estrangement from her own family, the irony she names and won't let him soften.

But then the gut-punch, delivered so casually I almost missed how big it was: **there is a real brunette.** "That brunette you brought dancing before Thanksgiving — the sorority one." Vee, alone in her cold childhood room, invented a blonde dance-hall rival out of nothing — and meanwhile there actually was someone, unnamed, undefined, and Vee doesn't know. Pace's own answer to "She find out about the brunette, then?" is "No. Not like that" — which is not a denial, it's a dodge, and I felt the floor tilt reading it. I don't yet know who this is or what it means (it could be entirely innocent — Sheri clearly reads it as flirtation-adjacent gossip, not betrayal), but it's the first hard evidence that Vee's fear, wrong in its particulars, may not be wrong in its shape.

The other gut-punch is hearing Pace's side of the rupture at last. He *did* say the words — "the most important thing in my life," out loud — and it wasn't enough for Vee, and he can't understand why, laying "brick on brick" of true things that don't add up to what she needs. Sheri won't let him off: "not saying it matters more to you than having her... that feels pretty shitty." I trust Sheri completely here — she's not invested in either of them romantically, she just sees clean, and her verdict lands with real weight because it isn't performed for anyone.

Erotic charge: none, and rightly so — this is a warm, funny, aching two-hander about friendship and evasion, not desire. It works entirely on tenderness and unease rather than heat.

The title, **"The Usual"** — I love this one. On the surface it's just the diner order, the running joke of a place with no menu, everyone getting what they always get. But it's really about Pace's whole pattern: giving Sheri "the usual" easy affection because it costs him nothing, giving Vee "the usual" true-but-insufficient reasons instead of the one true thing she wants, refusing to depart from what's safe and known even when he can see it isn't working. It recolors the chapter nicely on reflection.

I want to know who the brunette is and whether it matters. I want Pace to break — and I'm less sure now than I was that his silence toward Vee is purely grief or confusion; it might be something closer to what Sheri names it as. Guess from here: the brunette is likely unrelated to any real betrayal, but Vee finding out about her — even innocently — could detonate everything Vee already fears.
