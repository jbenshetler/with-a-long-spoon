## Reader reaction

Half a beat of confusion opening this — new name, new setting, bikes and cold mornings, no Vee, no Randi in the room. Took me a sentence or two to place it as Pace's own page, first time we've been inside anything like his interior since he walked out last chapter. That alone is worth something: I've been aching to see him on his own terms, and here he is, quieter and more legible than I expected.

Sheri lands as instantly vivid — sharp, funny, proprietary in the easy way old friends are, unbothered about naming a woman's "backside" the way she'd name the weather. I like her immediately and don't distrust her; she reads as exactly what Pace needs, someone who'll rib him and also actually watch out for him ("I kept an eye on her").

What she reports about Vee corroborates everything Vee told Randi and everything I already believed: alone, glaring, danced with nobody. It's a small, quietly devastating confirmation from outside Vee's own account — she wasn't performing her misery for anyone, she really was sitting in it. That steadies my trust in Vee's remorse.

But the real jolt is Pace himself — "he was past the name before he'd decided to be," going on about the walnut chair and the math book and not stopping, Sheri watching him sideways and saying "Listen to you." This is unguarded Pace, gone soft and voluble over a woman for once, and it's the most romantic beat we've gotten from his side yet — not staged, not careful, just spilling. It moved me. I want this Pace and the Rusty Nail Pace to be the same man Vee gets to see.

Then the gut-punch line, tossed off sideways: "Randi. That's not finished." I sat with that. It doesn't explain itself — no gloss, no flashback — but it lands as exactly the kind of thing this book has trained me to catch and hold rather than resolve. Whatever "not finished" means, it complicates every warm thing I've felt about Randi dressing Vee for a reconciliation with this very man. Sheri's flat arithmetic — "Two of them at once… If that's not already complicated, it will be" — says the quiet part loud, and I appreciate that it comes from someone outside the triangle, with nothing at stake in flattering anyone.

Erotic charge here is near zero — this is a cold, physical, companionable chapter, bikes and breath and hills — but the emotional charge around Pace's unedited enthusiasm for Vee, and the unresolved "not finished" with Randi, is high. Friction: none, this reads clean and controlled, no thumb on the scale, just information delivered through a friend's teasing.

Title "Hills and Valleys" — on the surface it's just the ride, climbs and descents, breath given and taken. But it also sits neatly over the shape of this whole triangle right now: Pace's climb of feeling for Vee, the valley of "not finished" with Randi, the up-and-down of what's said and unsaid. Doesn't feel heavy-handed, feels apt after the fact.

I want the reconciliation chapter now more than ever — and I want to see whether Pace, unguarded like this, will actually get to be this version of himself in front of Vee, or whether Randi's staging swallows it.
