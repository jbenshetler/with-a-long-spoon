## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurts differently than the last chapter. No Pace at all — just Randi, alone, running the aftermath through a gauntlet of surveillance disguised as sisterly warmth. I felt my stomach tighten reading Brooke's interrogation, because it's so precisely calibrated: "friendly," "warm," and absolutely a cross-examination. "The not-standing was the move" — that line landed hard, the way Randi herself reads power in real time even while she's the one being worked over. I believed every beat of it: the recalibration when "math" lands below "engineering," the relief-exhale when Randi isn't in his section, Kinsey's "short-cute" reducing him to a punchline. It's a masterclass in how a group of women can dismantle someone's private life while smiling the whole time, and Randi has to perform gratitude for the smiling.

What moved me most is watching the chapter's title, motif, and Randi's own board seat collide: she's Standards. She literally polices this exact kind of scrutiny for the chapter, and now she's the one being quietly graded by it, and passing — barely, unconvincingly to herself even if not to them. That's a gutting irony that the text lets you find rather than announcing.

The second half, alone in her room, is where the real ache lives. No dialogue, just her body refusing every gesture it starts — moving to the vanity, not sitting; the closet, hand half-raised, then nothing; running water she doesn't touch. It reads like dissociation rendered as blocking, and it's devastating without a single overt statement of feeling. "The not-going did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." That's the line I'll carry — it recolors the last chapter's door motif brutally: last chapter she learned to lock a door against him; this chapter reveals she doesn't even have one against herself anymore, hasn't for years ("twenty-one years... it no longer registered as an act").

Erotic charge: none, deliberately, and that's correct — this is the cost chapter, the comedown, and it works because of what it withholds after what the previous chapter gave.

Trust/suspicion: nothing new about Pace here since he's offstage; if anything my sympathy for Randi deepens and darkens — I'm starting to wonder how much of her polish, even before Pace, was already this same practiced self-erasure, which reframes the "cultivated" blue eyes and portioned apple as older, sadder machinery than just protection from one man.

Title "Standards" — lands two ways at once: literally her sorority board position, and cuttingly as the standard she's being held to (and holding herself to) in that common room, plus the standards she can no longer meet with herself alone. Good title, does real work.
