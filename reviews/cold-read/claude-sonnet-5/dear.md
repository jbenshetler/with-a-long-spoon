## Reader reaction

Okay, this one got under my skin in a different way than the last chapter, and it took me a minute to name why. It's not the smoothie shop, not even Brooke's cool appraising friend — it's watching Vee hand her whole story to *Randi*.

Because I know what the last chapter's carry-forward told me: that Randi and Pace engineered the "collision" together, that Vee was selected and delivered. And here's Vee, glowing, retelling the "May I choose?" line, saying "I stopped keeping track of myself. I wasn't watching myself the way I always am" — to the one person in the scene who already knows exactly why that happened. Randi's response — "You let him choose for you… And you liked it," said slow, "holding it up to the light" — read to me like someone checking a result against a hypothesis. I don't think the text tells me Randi is coldly gloating; her warmth toward Vee feels real on the page, the hug, "dear friend," the hand held out in front of Brooke. But I can't unhear the double meaning under "You little minx," under the whole indulgent, delighted listening. That's a genuinely unsettling effect, and I think the chapter earned it — it didn't spell it out, it just let me sit in a room where I know something Vee doesn't and watch her offer up the evidence with her whole heart.

The Brooke scene is its own small gut-punch, differently. That instant of being sized up — "her feet not reaching the floor," the cardigan suddenly reading as an apology, the room "remembering she was sitting in it" — is such a precise, humiliating physical sensation, and I felt for Vee hard there. And then Randi's rescue, the hand on the table, "dear friend" said in front of the two finished women, genuinely moved me — "she had spent it on her without appearing to count it." I want that gesture to be uncomplicated kindness, and on its own terms it plays as one. But I'm aware I'm holding two readings of Randi at once now: real fondness for Vee, and calculated deployment of Vee. The chapter doesn't force a resolution and I'm glad it doesn't.

The tiny Cassie beat at the top stuck with me too — she looks at Randi across the room, "lit up," and something is in her eyes that "folded away" before she turns back to Vee. I don't know what that was. Irritation? A pang? Recognition of something about Randi that Vee doesn't see? I'm noting it, not solving it.

Erotic charge is low this chapter — mostly retold, secondhand ("May I choose?" repeated, the kiss described rather than shown), which is fine; this reads as a social/interior chapter, not a heat chapter. The charge that *does* work is the discomfort-charge: the sizing-up at the smoothie shop, and the queasy double-register of Randi's listening.

One line that snagged me with unease I can't fully place yet: "It was still in her somewhere, the cool finished force of the woman, the way you stay aware of deep water for a while after you are back in the boat." That's about the unnamed taller friend of Brooke's, who never speaks and is never named — she just watches Vee, top to bottom, and leaves. I don't know who she is or if she matters again. Flagging her as a genuine open unknown, not folding her into anyone.

Title "Dear" — I assume it's pointing at "dear friend," the phrase Randi uses to claim Vee in front of Brooke, the thing that cost something to say and landed as a real gift. It's a small, warm word made to carry a lot of weight in one scene, and now that I've read the chapter, I think that's exactly what it's there to flag — watch what "dear" does in this room, who spends it and what it buys.

I want to keep reading with real hope for Vee's happiness intact, but I'm carrying the Randi-as-listener discomfort forward now in a way I wasn't before this chapter.
