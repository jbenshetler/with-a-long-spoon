# Cold read (grounded) — forbidden-kiss

## Reader reaction

This chapter is almost pure aftermath-glow, and for a long stretch it just lets me have it — I sank into it happily. Vee waking alone in that enormous bed, reaching for cotton instead of heat, the little lurch of December fear before his voice from the night before resolves it — that's a lovely, economical way to remind me how far they've come from the rupture without belaboring it. And then the whole extended morning: the bread timed to wake her into its smell, the sweats set out and pointedly *not worn*, the "pleasure princess, not pleasure slave" reversal she works out for herself, padding the hall naked and cackling at her own cleverness — I was smiling through most of it. It's funny in a way this book doesn't always let itself be; Vee's giddy, silly, entirely herself, and I liked her enormously here. The burnt toast bit made me laugh out loud — the idea of Pace, of all people, standing there elsewhere while his own kitchen smokes, undone by a woman deliberately unspooling his composure with a raised knee and an inspected toenail. That's a genuinely fun, mutual, playful scene of desire, and it's rare in this book for Vee to hold this much of the wheel this comfortably.

The breakfast itself — the oranges, the persimmons with their pinch of salt, the mushrooms with their "almost masculine" taste — is Pace at his most Pace: sensory, patient, controlled, a whole paragraph of care poured into food nobody asked him to make this beautiful. It's seductive exactly the way his cooking scenes always are, and I still feel the tug of it even knowing, as this reader does and Vee doesn't, that this exact choreography (warmed house, food prepared in advance, wine/tea appearing unasked) is also his choreography with Randi. I don't distrust the tenderness in the room — it reads as real, moment to moment, same as the checkpoint told me to expect — but I can't switch off the awareness that none of this is unique to her, and that awareness is doing quiet, steady work under the sweetness the whole chapter through.

The extended oral sex sequence is where the chapter's real charge lives — slow, patient, deliberately paced almost to a fault (the "tiling," wave after wave of kisses down each limb) but it earns the patience: I felt the ache of the delay build in her body along with her, and the two-orgasm structure (her coming once before he's even inside her, again after) reads as generous rather than performative. The "pointing" game as a wordless command language is a nice extension of established motifs — her finger standing in for words the way Pace's own diction ("May I—") always has been about consent phrased obliquely.

Then the kiss-after-oral moment lands with real force, and it's the chapter's genuine spike of unease rather than manufactured shadow — her body answers before her mind does ("her tongue went to it… before any part of her had ruled on it"), then the mother's voice slams the door ("that's — no. Wrong"), and he goes instantly, wordlessly still and starts to withdraw — earning back, hard, all the trust the book has built around his consent-literacy. That's beautifully handled: no argument, no sulk, just "Okay." I trusted him fully in that beat.

But then the chapter does something I sat with for a while: the "faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning" in his mouth, later in the same encounter — and her calm explaining-away of it ("he must have forgotten to wipe") that the prose itself doesn't let me fully believe, because it deliberately leaves the door "not all the way" shut — "it settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting." That's the chapter's real needle, and it's the first time I've felt this specific, physical kind of dramatic-irony dread in the book: not "is he lying about feelings" but a genuinely bodily, almost forensic little wrongness that Vee herself notices and then talks herself past. I don't know what it means — the chapter doesn't resolve it and neither do I — but it's the first moment where Pace's body itself, not just his silences, felt like it might be withholding something from her literally in the room that morning. I'm holding that open exactly as the page holds it open; I won't guess whose salt that was or whether it means anything at all, but I noticed the chapter wanted me to notice.

Her decision to tell Randi "nearly everything" and maybe even this — "Randi would know what to make of it" — closes the chapter on a small, cold irony only I can feel: she's about to hand her one true flicker of doubt about Pace straight to the one person least likely to be neutral about it.

**Swoon/pull:** the whole morning through breakfast, unreserved — funny, tender, mutual, some of the lightest and happiest Vee has been on the page. The oral sequence too, though its patience occasionally tipped toward inventory for me (see below).

**Trust vs. suspicion:** no new suspicion of Pace's *care* — that reads sincere as always — but the salt detail planted a small, specific, bodily unease I hadn't had before, earned by the text itself, not manufactured by me.

**Erotic charge:** high, peaking in the extended cunnilingus sequence, though a couple of the "tiling" repetitions (the wave-motif down each limb, described almost identically twice) slackened the charge slightly through sheer accumulation before the finger-point mechanic picked it back up.

**Friction:** minor — the repeated "tiling"/wave imagery is lovely once, slightly less so on its third invocation; and "her whole body doing the arithmetic of it and getting happiness for an answer every time" felt like one metaphor past its welcome in an otherwise restrained chapter.

**Title:** "Forbidden Kiss" pointed exactly where the chapter went once I got there — not the courtship's early kisses but this new, specific, taboo mouth-to-mouth-after moment. It didn't spoil anything going in (I assumed the obvious reading and the chapter delivered something more precise and stranger than I expected), and on reflection it recolors the whole cheerful morning slightly, in hindsight, toward that one unresolved thread.

**Book/series titles:** *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* still feel exactly as promised by the jacket — courtly surface, real appetite, something withheld underneath that the reader can see and Vee can't quite. This chapter is squarely inside that promise: warmth and heat delivered in full, plus one small, cold thread (the salt) that I suspect — guess only — is the spoon getting a little longer.

**What I want next:** to see whether that salt detail ever surfaces again, and whether Vee actually tells Randi about it — and if so, how Randi's face does or doesn't give anything away.
