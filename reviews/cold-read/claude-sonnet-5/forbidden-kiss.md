# Cold read (grounded) — forbidden-kiss

## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter still glowing a little from the coat scene, and for most of it that glow just deepens — this is the morning after, the two of them alone in his warmed, bread-smelling house, and it reads like the reward the last book earned. I love Vee here. Waking up reaching for the ghost of him, the flash-panic of December before his voice from the night before catches up to her — that's such a true small terror, and I felt the relief land with her when it resolves. And then the sheer delight of "she couldn't dress. Poor thing" — the girl who spent the whole first volume buttoned to the collar over her own body deciding, alone in a warm house, to just *be* naked, on purpose, for the pleasure of it — that's the arc paying off in real time and it made me grin. The "pleasure slave" bit that flips itself into "pleasure princess" mid-hallway ("Not the kind of woman who is kept for a man's pleasure. The kind a man keeps *in* pleasure") is genuinely charming, funny, and earned — she's reasoning her way to her own dignity using evidence (he comes first, he cooks, he burns the toast watching her toes) rather than being handed it. I was rooting hard for her the whole first two-thirds.

Pace is, on the surface, at his absolute best in this chapter — the timed breadmaker, the cream in the fridge, the sweats folded and left unworn, "Come here first," the coat opened around her in the cold. The doorway moment where he loses every word he has ("Whoa—") and has to visibly reassemble his composure "piece by piece like a man picking up dropped change" is lovely and made me like him more, not less — it's the one crack in the courtly performance I actually trust, because it's involuntary. But I'm carrying the checkpoint's dramamtic irony into this scene whether I want to or not: every one of these signature gestures — the pre-warmed house, the food built like a case being made, the wordless gifts — is, per what I already know, a repeatable method he's used with someone else too. The chapter doesn't reference Randi in the room, but I couldn't fully unknow her while I read the bread and the blood oranges.

And then the ending. I want to be honest about exactly what happened to me there rather than what I expected to happen. The kiss-after-oral moment itself — her tongue going to the taste of herself before her mind can rule on it, then the installed voice ("no, that's wrong, that's not a thing you do") slamming down over something her body had already said yes to — that's the chapter's real center, and it's a gorgeous, precise piece of writing about shame arriving a half-second late. His response (drawing back at once, not sulking, not explaining, just "Okay") is exactly the kind of consent-literate tenderness this book does so well, and her legs locking around him — "No — not that... Don't you dare stop" — is a good, clean, hot correction, not a coerced walkback.

But then the second taste — "a faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning" — is where the chapter turned a knife in me that I don't think Vee felt turning at all. Given everything the checkpoint told me — an ongoing, secret, still-active Pace/Randi relationship, "identical rituals," his morning spent alone at a farmers market — I could not read "he must have forgotten to wipe" as the safe landing the text hands her. The line right under her relief — "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been — it slid away when she reached for it... and it did not go all the way; it settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting" — felt like the book looking straight at me over her shoulder. I want to be careful here: the text itself keeps this genuinely ambiguous, offers the innocent explanation with real tenderness and doesn't confirm anything sinister, so I'm holding it open rather than deciding he's been unfaithful that very morning. But my suspicion of him, dormant since the checkpoint, woke straight up on that page, and I don't think that's me manufacturing a shadow — the specific phrase "not there this morning" is doing real work, planting a *timing* question the innocent explanation doesn't fully cover.

**Swoon / pull:** Enormous, right up until the very end. The whole extended lovemaking scene — the pointing game she invents (finger to wrist, to nipple, to "lower"), him obeying to the letter, the deliberate detour down her legs and back up when she's already begging — is patient, controlled, genuinely hot writing that never once loses the thread of *her* pleasure being the point. "He tiled her belly with kisses" and "he was opening her as he had opened the orange" — that image lands the whole chapter's structure in one line: this is the same attentiveness whether it's aimed at fruit or at her, and for most of the chapter that reads as devotion, not menace.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** Real movement here, and it's earned by memory, not manufactured. I trust Vee completely — her hunger, her humor, her small self-corrections are all consistent and true. My trust in Pace, which the checkpoint already had me holding cautiously, took a fresh dent at the very end — not from anything definitively wrong on the page, but from a detail the text chose to leave open rather than resolve, immediately after establishing (again) how identical his rituals are across women. I don't distrust the *scene* — every beat of the sex itself reads sincere — but I distrust the morning that isn't shown, the gap between the door and the groceries.

**Erotic charge:** Working on me the whole way, and it peaks hard in the oral sex sequence — the breath-without-touch beat, the "he was drinking it" line about her shame-and-arousal fused, the "her whole body clenching, pulsing around his fingers, her thighs shut around his ears" — genuinely one of the hottest passages in the book so far, unhurried and generous rather than mechanical. It never goes slack; if anything it's almost too sustained, one long uninterrupted rise, which is a minor structural note more than a complaint.

**Friction as a reader:** Very little. The breakfast sequence is long — a full page-plus of orange segmentation and mushroom searing before anyone touches anyone — but it's purposeful long, doing the same "food as love language" work the book has always done, and the burnt-toast beat earns its laugh. If I have one small snag it's that "Randi always did" as a closing line felt slightly on-the-nose after the taste ambiguity — it points a little too neatly at where my suspicion should land, though I can't say for certain that's the intent versus just Vee's genuine, uncomplicated trust in her friend.

**The titles:** "Forbidden Kiss" is doing exactly the work a good title should — it named nothing until I got there, then the whole chapter re-sorted around it. On first read it's simply the taboo of tasting herself in his mouth, the "ugh, gross" that becomes "don't you dare stop" — a private, small forbidden thing between two people, sweet in its resolution. But by the chapter's last page it's picked up a second, colder meaning I can't unsee: a kiss that might be forbidden for a reason she doesn't know to ask about. I don't think the chapter forces that second reading, but it offered it to me, and once offered I couldn't put it back down. As for the book title — *With a Long Spoon* has always implied you need distance from something dangerous you're sharing a meal with, and this chapter, of all the ones so far, is the first time that proverb felt literally, physically alive on the page rather than just structurally true of the plot: a meal, a spoon's length of doubt, supped with anyway. *A Warm Reception* fits this chapter almost too perfectly — groceries, a warmed house, a welcome home — which makes me wonder if the volume is going to keep handing me rooms this warm right up until it doesn't.

**What I want next:** I want to see Randi in person again — the checkpoint left me deeply unsettled about her feelings for Vee, and this chapter's closing line ("Randi would know what to make of it") makes me dread and want in equal measure watching Vee hand her this exact detail across a restaurant table. I'm guessing — purely a guess from here — that the "salt and sharp" thread doesn't resolve cleanly either way for a while, and that the book is going to keep letting Vee suppress it while I can't. I want to know if this new volume finally gives Pace a scene where his guard drops all the way, or whether "Okay," said low and unexplained, is as far as he ever goes.
