## Reader reaction

Okay. That one got me — properly, in the body, before I had any critical thought at all. I read the whole fitting scene with my shoulders up around my ears.

**How I feel about each of them right now**

Pace keeps not doing the thing I brace for him to do, and that keeps being the whole engine of my feeling about him. Every single beat where I expect the ick — he says "the bra too, it changes the line," and I flinched on Vee's behalf, thinking *here it is, here's where the tenderness turns instrumental* — and instead what happens is he laughs, genuinely, at "pre-aroused," and gets stumped by her question, and I watched a "young math nerd" flicker up out of the "courtly powerlifter" for a second and it undid me a little. And then the scene's real hinge: he goes still and silent at the base of her, and she braces for the grin she's spent her whole life bracing for, and instead he's craned his neck all the way up to catch her eyes the second she opens them, having deliberately not looked down. That's the moment I trust him completely — not because he's perfect, but because the text shows me the *effort* of it (he chooses the dress over "the easy yes she was all but handing him, and the choosing cost him"). I still carry the checkpoint's dramatic irony — I know this courtship started as a game — and that hasn't left me, it just sits under the surface now rather than souring the scene, because what's happening between them in this room reads as real regardless of its origin.

Vee is unbearably legible to me here — the mother's-voice vocabulary doing exactly what it's done every chapter (floozy, stripper, common), the counting of maybes, the bargaining with her own body. I ached for her hardest at "she'd stood up on a box in the light of day and been found out completely" — that's the oldest shame there is, made literal and staged, and then the chapter refuses to let the shame land where she expects it to. I loved watching her discover "a kind of power she'd never been issued" and start, tentatively, to spend it — the deliberate breast-press, the "Oops. Sorry." That's a woman waking up inside her own wanting, not being talked into anything.

**Swoon / pull**

The whole tape-measure sequence is doing something I haven't quite felt this book do before: eroticizing *withdrawal* rather than contact — "closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil" — so that the ache builds in the gaps, not the touches. That's a genuinely inventive piece of craft-as-felt-experience and it worked on me completely. And the shirt reveal earlier (in the recent chapters) — finding out the flannel she's been wearing into the ground has no tag because he made it — landed as a small gut-punch of tenderness before this chapter even started; it primed me to trust the dress the way she does.

**Trust vs. suspicion**

None that the page itself earns in this chapter. I still hold the checkpoint's knowledge that this all started as a game, and I notice myself watching for the calculation under the care — but nothing here plays as manipulation; if anything this chapter is the strongest argument yet that whatever began as a plan has become something he's fully inside of, costing him something to hold to his own rules.

**Erotic charge**

This is close to the hottest the book has run for me, and almost none of it is explicit — it's a fully clothed man and a half-dressed woman and a tape measure, and it is scorching because of what's withheld. It peaks for me at "Keep still" — four words carrying an entire negotiated, consensual, costly restraint — and again at the wet-satin thread, which is mortifying and arousing in exactly the ratio the character herself is feeling it, never played for cheap titillation, always tethered to her interior shame-spiral.

**Friction**

Very little. If I'm honest, "Muse came to her, ridiculous, embarrassing, and she set it down quickly" is a hair too on-the-nose about naming its own mechanism — I felt the authorial hand for a half-second there, though it recovers fast into the "particular her" idea, which is the good version of the same thought.

**Titles**

"A Round" — I don't have it yet. No round of anything obvious happens on the page (no drinks, no boxing, no circling — well, actually, he does knee-walk a full circuit around her, "the geography of it," which retroactively might be the round: literally circling her body with the tape and the pins. If that's it, it's a quiet, exact title, the kind that only clicks after the fact rather than announcing itself. The book title and tagline still feel exactly where they said they'd take me — "every open door is a temptation and every step is hers" — this chapter is that promise made almost unbearably literal, a closed bedroom door beside an open, willing undressing in a different room, and every inch of Vee's exposure a step she takes herself even as it's engineered by a plan she doesn't know about.

**What I want next**

I want to see whether this fitting becomes the hinge where Vee's desire for Randi (per the jacket) starts to surface more concretely, and I'm bracing — pleasantly — for the induction dinner itself, dress finished, to be a real set-piece. Guess from here only: I suspect the dress becomes an object charged enough to matter again later, the way the flannel already has.
