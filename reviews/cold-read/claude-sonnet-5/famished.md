## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one landed with real heat on me — the whole chapter is a slow-burn approach that finally arrives, and it earns the arrival. I felt the week of waiting the way Vee feels it: the flannel gone thin of his smell, the "floozie" popping up at the least provocation, the deliberate shopping trip for the green set she buys "for the color" and pretends isn't for this. The book has been building this exact groove for several chapters now — desire arriving braided with shame, never one without the other — and here it finally gets a full unbroken run at it. I liked that the text didn't let her "win" against the shame; it just stopped being the thing that stops her. "The shame was still running and she was burning through it" is doing real work — that's not resolution, that's something more interesting: she goes through the wanting *with* the shame intact, not by defeating it.

Pace is still the tender, exacting, watchful man I've trusted since the trail and the fitting — I felt the full swoon of the hike chapters again in miniature here: garlic and pepper flakes measured and waiting, the wine breathing, him stopping mid-prep because he wanted to greet her, not finish cooking. "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" is such a Pace question — it reads as genuine consent-checking even mid-arousal, and it's also, as the text itself flags, "something else as well": him wanting proof, wanting to see her body answer for her. I don't distrust him exactly, but I noticed the text noticing that his tenderness and his want-to-witness-her-wanting are two things running on the same wire, same as her shame and heat. That's consistent with what the checkpoint already told me about him — his care is real and it's also calibrated, and I'm watching both at once without the book asking me to pick.

Where the chapter really got me: the scar. "Her hand stopped — a scar, large, high on the inside of his thigh." One sentence, no explanation, and then straight on. It's the first genuinely mysterious, slightly ominous detail attached to Pace's actual body rather than his behavior, and it landed like a held breath — I want to know what that is and the text isn't telling me, which is exactly right. It doesn't moralize or pause the scene; it just sits there as a fact she notices and keeps going. I trust the book more for not explaining it immediately.

The final image — her folding the soiled panties deliberately and handing them to him, "Trade," in exchange for the shirt she never got to thank him for properly — is the whole chapter in one gesture: her offering the very evidence she's spent the scene ashamed of, on purpose, as a gift, and something in his shoulders going "private" in response. That felt like real vulnerability from him too, quiet and unglossed, which I appreciated — the chapter doesn't only strip her.

Randi is entirely offstage here except as an echo in Vee's inner voice/shame-loop indirectly (not directly — actually she's not present at all this chapter, but the previous "How It's Done" chapter's kiss and Vee's paranoid scan of the dorm windows are still sitting hot in my mind going into this one). I notice I'm carrying that unresolved kiss forward into how I read Vee's hunger here — there's an unspoken sense that Vee is now chasing/consuming warmth from two directions and hasn't metabolized either.

Swoon/pull: high. The domestic detail (measured pepper flakes, the pan clean and dry, the wine breathing) does more seductive work for me than most explicit writing manages — it's foreplay made of care, and I felt myself soften right alongside her at the door. The sex itself, once it arrives, is graphic and sustained but never loses the psychological throughline; it doesn't go slack into mechanics.

Trust vs. suspicion: no new alarm from the text itself, but the scar is a genuine, earned note of "I don't know something here" — not dread exactly, just an open door I want to walk through later. Everything else about Pace in this chapter reconfirms rather than complicates the established trust.

Erotic charge: peaks hard at "he was in the wet of her and tasted her and was glad of it, his hands coming up the backs of her thighs to hold her open" — full-body, present-tense, unflinching, and it stays inside her interior experience (the shame-loop) rather than becoming pure choreography, which is what makes it work on me rather than just describe at me. It never goes clinical.

Friction: very little. The line "she heard it a beat after he did" (both saying "Famished") is a small cute beat that verges on cute-coincidence, but it's light enough not to bother me. The transition from "will you show me your bedroom" to full undress happens fast, but the chapter has spent four chapters earning that speed, so it didn't feel unearned to me.

Titles: "Famished" is doing exactly what it should — it's the word she says at the door, almost a joke, and then the chapter proves it true on every register: food hunger, sex hunger, hunger for being fully seen without flinching. It doesn't give anything away beforehand — it reads as a light, almost throwaway word until the chapter fills it up. Nice, unforced double meaning. The book's title, A Polite Invitation, and the tagline about "every yes was freely given" sit uneasily over this chapter in a good way — everything here is her initiating, her choosing (the kiss, the bedroom, the panties-as-gift), which makes the jacket's ominous undertone ("that was the problem") hover at the edge without landing yet. With a Long Spoon as a series title still reads as pointing toward something more dangerous than what's on the page so far — I keep expecting the appetite motif to curdle, and this chapter, for all its heat, doesn't curdle it yet. It deepens the appetite instead.

What I want next: I want to know what that scar is, eventually, on the book's own schedule. I also want to see how Vee metabolizes having two people (Pace, and the still-unprocessed kiss from Randi) both pulling at this same hunger in her — whether she'll ever let herself look at that collision, or keep filing it away the way she filed away the tag-less shirt. Guess from here: the panties-trade and "something private" in his shoulders suggests Pace is more emotionally exposed by this than the narration usually lets him be, and I'd like the book to open that up rather than keep it locked behind his composure.
