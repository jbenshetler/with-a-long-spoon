## Reader reaction

I felt something quietly shift the ground under me in this chapter — not with Pace, who's offstage entirely, but with Vee herself, watching her hold a version of her life up against her family's frame and watch it not fit anywhere. The dinner-table scene with her parents, relayed secondhand, is the whole engine here: "I cropped it before I sent it. The version they have stops at the waist." That line did something to me before I even got the rest of the chapter — it's such a small, ordinary act of editing, and it recolors everything she says after about what else she's editing. Not just the photo. The whole account of Pace. She tells her dad the parts that "fit" — the PhD, the hiking, the dancing on concrete — and withholds the parts that don't: that he cooks, that he owns his house, that he made the dress himself on a sewing machine. That last one lands hardest. "Honestly, my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's such a sharp, funny, sad sentence — it tells me exactly what her father's world can and can't hold, and it tells me something about Vee too: that she has become fluent in triage, in knowing exactly which true things are sayable and which aren't.

Cassie is lovely here — warm, unhurried, not performing curiosity, letting Vee's silences be silences. "So what is he. Not to your dad. To you." is the sharpest question anyone's put to Vee in a while, precisely because it isn't really about Pace. And Vee's answer — "Both. Probably both." — when Cassie asks if she can't say or doesn't want to — is the chapter's real center for me. It's honest in a way that surprised me; she's not performing modesty or coyness, she genuinely doesn't have the shape for what this is. That tracks with everything I've been carrying about her — the shirt confession that came out "small," "shoved out all at once" — but here it's not shyness about need, it's something closer to not having language at all for what she and Pace have made. And the last line — "She was grateful. She was also, a little, not." — is exactly the kind of double-valence honesty I trust this book to give me. She wanted Cassie to ask more, and she's relieved Cassie didn't.

Attraction/swoon: none in the conventional sense — no heat here at all — but there's a real ache and warmth in watching Vee try to describe Pace to people who can't receive him. It made me love him more by proxy, oddly, hearing his devotion translated into things a father could recognize (a man who dances with you on concrete) versus the things he can't (a man who sews). And it made me tender toward Vee's parents too, briefly, sketched with real specificity — the dad burning rolls on purpose, the mom touching her own collarbone while she looks at the dress's shoulders. Neither cruel nor obtuse, just people whose frame is smaller than what's actually happening to their daughter.

Trust/suspicion: nothing off. I trust Cassie completely, still. No new worry about Pace — if anything this deepened him, secondhand, through what Vee has to leave out because it's too good, too strange, too much his own thing (sewing a dress on a sun porch).

Friction: none really — this is tight, controlled, and every line is doing double work. The only thing I'd flag as slightly odd is how compressed the parents' whole visit is, collapsed almost entirely into this one photo/dress anecdote — but that's clearly the point, that this is the only thing she actually wanted to talk about.

Title: "Cropped" is doing a lot of quiet work — the literal crop of the photo (stops at the waist) as the governing image for everything else she's cropping in this account: the sewing, the money, the fact of who Pace really is to her. It illuminates the chapter cleanly rather than spoiling it — I only understood the full reach of the title by the end.

The book's title and tagline don't feel newly activated here — this read more like a still, interior beat between the big engines. I want to keep reading to see whether Vee ever finds the language Cassie asked for, or whether "both" stands as her answer for a while yet.
