## Reader reaction

This one landed differently than I expected — quieter, and it snuck up on me. It's just Vee and Cassie at dinner, but the whole thing is Vee circling the shape of a secret without ever quite saying it, and I found that more moving than a lot of declared-feeling scenes would have been.

The cropped photo is the thing that got me. "I cropped it before I sent it. The version they have stops at the waist" — that's such a precise, small act of self-editing, and it rhymes with everything I already knew about Vee curating what she shows people. But here it's aimed at her own parents, about her own body, about a dress her boyfriend *made* her. There's something quietly sad in that detail once she explains why: not shame exactly, but the sheer unspeakability of what she actually has. "Sex he understands. The sewing he never would" is a wonderful, funny, true line — and underneath the humor it's actually kind of devastating, because it says her father's whole moral vocabulary doesn't have a slot for the realest, tenderest thing about Pace.

Cassie is lovely here — blunt as ever, but gentle-blunt. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" is exactly the kind of question a genuinely good friend asks: not letting Vee off the hook, but not forcing her either. And Vee's "Both. Probably both" is honest in a way I trust completely — she's not performing vagueness, she really doesn't have the word.

That last line — "She was also, a little, not [grateful]" — is the one that stays with me. It suggests Vee actually wanted to be pushed, wanted someone to make her name it, and Cassie's restraint, however kind, left something unfinished in her. That's a real, earned ambivalence, not manufactured.

Erotic charge: none, and it doesn't need any — this is a relief chapter for interiority, and it works entirely on the strength of what's implied and withheld.

**Title:** "Cropped" — perfect, does double duty. Literally the photo, but also Vee's whole self-presentation to her parents, and by extension the parts of her life with Pace she keeps out of frame for everyone, maybe even for herself.

I want to know what "the version they have stops at the waist" implies about the uncropped photo — was it revealing, was it just the dress's cut, or something more (echo of the nude photo shown to Randi)? That's an itch I can't scratch yet, marked as open.
