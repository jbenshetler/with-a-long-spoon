## Reader reaction

Okay, felt read for "Some of Mine."

---

I sat with this one for a minute before writing anything, because it's so small — one scene, maybe five minutes of clock time, in a doorway — and yet it's carrying the whole book's weight on its back.

**Vee** — my heart genuinely hurts for her here. She comes in still lit up (or scorched) from that goodnight with Randi, and the first thing she does is lie, badly, to the one person in the book who's never lied to her. "It's a sorority thing. She's like that with everyone" — and the prose tells us straight out: "There were too many words in her mouth and not one of them the size of an answer." I believe her when she says she "went looking for the answer" to Cassie's question and actually couldn't find it — not wouldn't, couldn't. That distinction matters to me as a reader; this isn't evasion so much as a genuine blank where self-knowledge should be. And then the ugliest little turn: she reaches for the old, agreed-off-limits weapon — "You don't like her, you've never liked her" — deploys it, and it doesn't even land, because Cassie just... doesn't pick it up. That's Vee at her most cornered, and it's the least sympathetic I've found her in a while, and yet I don't dislike her for it. I recognize it. It's exactly what a person does when a friend asks the one question with no safe answer.

**Cassie** — still the person I trust without reservation, and this chapter is a small masterclass in why. "Is this what you want?" — not "what happened," not "who is she to you" — she goes straight past the facts to the only question that actually matters, and she asks it flat, the way she asks everything true. And then, when Vee swings the old antagonism plank at her, she just steps over it. "The eyebrow that went up and stayed and said nothing at all" — that's Cassie's whole method in one image, and it's devastating precisely because it refuses to escalate. And then the cereal. God, the cereal. "Do you want some of my Cocoa Pebbles?" is one of the most quietly loving gestures in the whole book so far — she doesn't get an answer, doesn't push for one, just gives Vee something small enough to hold. I love her more after this chapter, not less.

**Swoon / pull** — not much swoon here, but a huge amount of ache-pull. The whole chapter is Vee standing in a doorway unable to cross the distance into her own feelings, and I wanted to reach in and answer the question for her, which is exactly the trap the checkpoint warned me about — I'm holding it open because the text holds it open. The pull for me was toward Cassie, actually — toward that offered box of cereal as the only honest thing in the room.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — no suspicion of anyone new. This chapter doesn't cast doubt so much as it makes visible how far Vee has drifted from being able to tell Cassie the truth, which the recent chapters have been building toward chapter by chapter (the withheld thong detail, the "no, nothing" to Pace). This is the first time I've seen her lie to Cassie specifically, and it landed harder than any of the lies to Pace, because Cassie's the one person who's never asked for anything but honesty.

**Erotic charge** — none, and it doesn't need any. This is the cooldown chapter, the ash after the fire of "Boyfriend." The only heat-adjacent note is Vee's awareness of her own neck "still live where it had been" — a single, precise physical echo of the scene before, not eroticized here so much as incriminating.

**Friction as a reader** — none. If anything this chapter is doing exactly what I wanted after the last two: slowing down, refusing to resolve, letting someone just ask the plain question and get silence back.

**Titles** — "Some of Mine" lands as the whole chapter in three words once I finished it: Cassie's cereal, offered without condition, "some of my Cocoa Pebbles" — the only thing Vee can take tonight, because it's the only question with an answer she has. It recolors on second thought too: this is a chapter about a girl who has been giving pieces of herself away all book — to Pace's questions, to Randi's confessions — and the one person who just quietly offers her *some of hers*, no toll, no game, is Cassie. It's almost unbearably tender for how plain it is. I don't have the volume or series title in front of me for this packet, so I can't react to those — my sense of where this is going is built entirely off this scene and what came before it.

**What I want next** — I want to know what happens to that open question. Not resolved — I don't need Vee to suddenly know what she wants — but I want to watch her sit inside not-knowing instead of running from it to Randi or to Pace. And I'm braced for Thursday, for brunch Saturday, for whatever Randi does with the fact that Vee dodged the goodnight kiss. Guess from here: I don't think Cassie lets this go entirely quiet, even if she never asks again outright — that "you didn't have dinner" felt like someone quietly keeping watch.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Cassie. (Randi mentioned/recently offstage — her goodnight and departure are referenced but she is not physically present in this chapter's scene.)

**Heat:** 0 — no charge in the room itself; the one live wire (Vee's neck) is aftermath, not present-tense wanting.

**Romance:** 1 — faint, but real: Cassie's cereal offer is a small act of platonic devotion, not romance in the book's usual register, though it's the tenderest beat on the page.

**Motifs & images:** the doorway/threshold as a held, unresolved space (echoes "Coming Due"'s doorway ending — this chapter opens exactly where that one stopped, unbroken); Cassie's steady flat questions ("Is this what you want?" mirrors "Do you love him?" from the checkpoint, and "What was that with you and Randi?" from the previous chapter) — a repeat pattern, not a first sighting; the "reaching for the nearest thing that would take her weight" — an explicit callback to the identical phrase used in "Boyfriend" when Vee swung the conversation onto Pace instead of answering him; the offered food-as-care (bread, mushrooms, Pace's whole cooking motif) now handed to Cassie's register — cereal instead of a home-cooked breakfast, but the same shape of love.

**Symbolism:** the cereal box reads as the anti-dress, the anti-induction-gown — nothing tailored, nothing engineered, no author behind it but Cassie's own hand, offered with zero expectation of anything back. It's the cheapest object in the whole book and it might be the most honest one.

**Characterization:** Cassie deepens again — this is maybe her best chapter yet, saying almost nothing and revealing the most. Vee doesn't flatten, but she's shown at her least admirable and most human — the reflex to attack rather than answer is new information about her, consistent with (not contradicting) the shame-avoidance arc from the checkpoint.

**Pace — within the chapter:** perfectly judged length — it's a held breath, not a scene, and it doesn't overstay. Ends on the exact right small gesture rather than pushing for a confession that wouldn't be earned yet.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** a deliberate exhale after "Boyfriend" and "Coming Due," which were both wound tight. This one doesn't advance plot so much as let the reader (and Vee) sit in the wreckage for a second before the next thing happens — good pacing, a necessary rest beat, not a lull.
