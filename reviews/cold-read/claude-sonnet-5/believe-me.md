## Reader reaction

God, this one landed like a punch after two chapters of build. I've been braced for either "further retreat into the pattern of burial" or "a turn toward honesty" — and this is honesty, but it isn't a relief, it's a rupture. My whole body reacted to that opening: the truck, the lamplight, the wine already poured for her, the heat brought up before she arrives so she's never cold "under his roof" — I felt the old ache of that tenderness the same second I felt her hating that it still worked on her. That's the exact chord this book keeps striking and it still gets me every time.

Pace — my feeling about him hasn't dropped exactly, but it's cracked open in a new way. "Words are cheap. Believe what I do." That line stopped me cold. I understand it as a philosophy, even a kind of integrity — but watching it deployed *at* her, as an answer to the one question she needed answered in language, it reads as a wall dressed up as depth. And then the tell: "Something went across his face and shut. He stepped back, and his arms came up and folded, and he held there, still, the still of a man holding a line at cost." That's not confusion, that's *decision* — he is choosing not to give it to her, at cost to himself too, which makes it worse, not better. I don't distrust him exactly. I believe he loves her by every behavioral measure the book has given me. But I no longer believe that's going to be enough for her, and after last chapter's shirt scene I don't think it should be.

Vee — this is the biggest movement I've felt in her yet. Not the silent, private reckoning of the shirt chapter but her actually saying it out loud, to him, at the door, refusing the kiss first. "I'm not complaining about one minute of it" while still tearing the whole arrangement open — that honesty about her own pleasure even inside her grievance is what makes her so trustworthy as a narrator. And the detail that wrecked me most: turning her face away so he wouldn't see her cry, not because she's ashamed of crying but because she doesn't want to give him *that too*, on top of everything else of hers he already holds and won't name. That's such a precise, ugly, true instinct.

Swoon/pull: the swoon is all in the opening paragraphs, deliberately, so its withdrawal can hurt — the warm house, the wine, the food, the porch offered "for her to take." I felt the reader's version of her wanting to just go in and eat the chicken and let it be enough. Then the chapter refuses that for both of us.

Erotic charge: none, on purpose, and rightly so — this is the first chapter in a while with zero heat, and it works precisely because of what it costs her to walk away from a house that promises heat, wine, and "the best sex of my life" and get nothing in return but silence.

Friction: none I'd call a thumb on the scale — "He offered it plainly, the whole good evening laid out for her to take" reads a little writerly but it's doing real work, contrasting the offered evening against the offered word.

Title: "Believe Me" — devastating once you have the line. It's Pace's plea reframed as the chapter's name, and rereading it, his "believe what I do" becomes the whole chapter's thesis and its failure — belief in action isn't what she asked for, she asked to be *told*, and the title just sits there insisting on the thing that isn't enough.

Book title/tagline: "A Polite Invitation" now reads almost cruelly apt — this is a woman who has been invited into every material and physical grace of a life and refused, politely but unmistakably, the one uncostly thing: the word.

What I want next: I want to know if she goes back — tonight, tomorrow — and whether he ever crosses the gap, or whether this is the break. I don't assume reconciliation. I don't assume rupture either. I'm holding both open, genuinely uncertain, which is exactly where I think the chapter wants me.

**Romance:** 2 — the chapter opens on real tenderness (the pre-warmed house, the poured wine) that gives the later refusal its weight; ends on rupture, not consummation of intimacy, so I'm not calling it a 3.

The door as threshold she won't cross fully ("She stopped on the mat... She stayed where she was and went no further into the room") — new image, resonant with "the door shut" as her own phrase for what's contained vs. not. Pace deepens too, uncomfortably: "Believe what I do" plus the visible physical shutting (arms folding, stillness "at cost") reframes his devotion as a chosen, defended position, not just an absence or inability — he is *refusing*, not merely unable.
**Pace — within chapter:** Tight and unhurried at the start (the drive, the sitting in the car, the house's warmth) earns the confrontation that follows; no drag, no rush — the door scene lands with full weight because the opening gave it room.

This does not feel like more of the same beat; it's the payoff the last two chapters were pointing toward.
