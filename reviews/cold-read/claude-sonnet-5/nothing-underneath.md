## Reader reaction

God, that whole first stretch — the shower, the mirror, the mascara done "as she did for the things that mattered" — I was completely inside her nerve. I felt the drawer-slam moment ("Pace was getting neither... Pace was getting the one thing no bra in that drawer could beat") as genuinely funny and genuinely poignant at once, that's a hard note to hit and it landed. And then the mirror scene — I wasn't expecting a full masturbation sequence *before* she even leaves the room, and it worked on me completely, not gratuitous, it's doing real work: she's arguing herself into her own body, using her own hands the way she imagines his, learning by touch what she's about to offer. Stopping herself — "It was not hers to finish" — is the single best beat in the chapter for me. That's desire that's hers, deliberately withheld *for* someone else, which is a different thing than every withholding we've watched Pace do to her all book. I felt that reversal.

The traffic-stop scene is the chapter's best piece of tension-management — genuinely scared for her, genuinely embarrassed with her, and the "caught, just like this" spiral rang completely true to how shame actually ambushes desire. Very well earned adrenaline before the door scene.

The door itself — I was swooning, no other word. "He looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look and she could not have pulled them apart" — that's the payoff of everything Randi engineered and everything Vee felt in the mirror, and it's rendered with real heat, not clinical at all: the porch sex, the couch, the graceless collapse into each other. I believed all of it as bodies, not choreography.

Where I sat up and felt something colder: "Vee. You sure know how to make an entrance," followed by him saying her name — and the text tells me explicitly this is *not* the word she needed in December, and it still isn't the declaration. He gives her tenderness, pancakes, "I missed you so much" — real, I don't doubt it's real — but he still hasn't said the thing Vee actually asked for back then. The chapter lets her be fully happy and fully fed and I felt that happiness genuinely, but I'm carrying the reader's-advantage knowledge that this wasn't resolution, it was reunion. Randi's plan worked exactly as engineered, and Vee experienced it as her own triumph, and I don't think the text is asking me to distrust that — I think it's asking me to hold both at once, the way the whole book has been doing.

Cassie's "Hmm" at the very end is a nice small cold water moment — someone outside the two-person warmth registering the whole story at a glance and not being fooled or impressed, just amused. I liked that texture, a world outside the bubble still there.

No suspicion of Pace here beyond what I already carry (the undenied brunette) — nothing on the page contradicts or confirms it. No suspicion of Randi surfaces on the page this chapter either; she's offstage entirely, but I can't stop feeling the shape of her hand in every choice Vee makes tonight, down to the shoes she's still wearing when Pace tells her to leave them on.

Title: "Nothing Underneath" is doing exactly what it says — literal (the coat), and also the emotional register of the whole chapter, a woman finally showing up with nothing held back, nothing performed except the choice to be unguarded. It doesn't feel like it gave anything away so much as it named the chapter's whole argument cleanly, in retrospect satisfying rather than spoiling.
