## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still braced from the run before it — the Chili's fight, the "words are cheap" wall, the whole cold month of Vee driving home from break in tears. So the first thing I felt reading "Nothing Underneath" was relief, almost against my will, the same relief Vee is chasing. And then a kind of vertigo, because I could hear Randi's voice narrating the whole plan from inside Vee's own head the entire time she's dressing — "take this into your own hands, you've got the goods, gorgeous girl" — literally intercut into Vee's private, most vulnerable moment in front of the mirror. That's the thing that unsettled me most, more than the (considerable) heat: this isn't really Vee's plan. It's Randi's script, performed by Vee's body, on Pace. Vee has to talk herself into it in Randi's cadence before she can find her own conviction — "she said it first in Randi's warm certainty and then in her own less certain voice, to see if it would bear weight." I don't think the book wants me to be comfortable with how thoroughly Randi is now directing Vee's most intimate choices, down to which shoes go on her feet during sex with another man. And it lands with extra weight after reading "Fairytale," where I got Randi's own interior for the first time at real length — the ski trip, "John," the perfect empty man, and Randi's flat, devastating verdict on herself: "This man had taken a beautiful woman to bed... and never found her underneath." That chapter cracked Randi open for me in a way nothing before it had — I felt real grief for her, not just wariness. She is lonely inside her own competence, and she knows it, and she's using Vee (even now, even lovingly) as the one true thing she gets to keep behind a locked door. Reading that right before watching her literally hand-script Vee's reconciliation sex with Pace gave the chapter a charge I don't think I'd have felt without it.

**Vee** — my heart genuinely went out to her here. The shower, the eyeliner-as-argument-with-herself, the bra she almost puts on and then doesn't, the giggle at "last thing, only thing" about the heels — all of it is so tender and so specific to a person, not a type, that I was fully with her even while worrying about her. The masturbation scenes (mirror, then the car) are gorgeous and genuinely arousing to me as a reader — unhurried, physically exact, and never coy — and then the cop car is a real jolt, a moment of pure adrenaline that earns its place; I actually held my breath.

**Pace** — the doorstep reveal and everything after is a real swoon for me. "He looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — that's the sentence that got me. Going to his knees on cold boards without hesitation, the couch, then getting up unprompted to shut the door and cover her with a blanket, then making pancakes at midnight and going down on one knee with his voice cracking on "I missed you so much" — I believed every bit of his relief and tenderness. And yet: the actual thing Vee needed from him — the word, the name for what they are — is never given here. It's just outrun by the reunion's intensity. I noticed that and I don't think I'm wrong to notice it; the chapter is honest that "she said nothing, because nothing needed saying now" is Vee choosing, again, not to need the thing she said she needed a month ago. That's not resolution, that's another wave washing over the same rock.

**Randi** — not physically on the page this chapter, but everywhere in it, which is almost more unsettling than if she'd shown up. The heels are hers. The plan is hers. "Those stay on," Pace says of the heels at the very end — a small detail that made me feel Randi's fingerprints on the whole encounter, unbeknownst to either of them.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — I don't distrust Pace's feeling in this chapter; every gesture reads as real. My suspicion is structural and has been earned by everything the checkpoint carries: I know things Vee doesn't, and watching her literally get dressed in Randi's borrowed courage and Randi's borrowed shoes to go fix a wound Randi helped set up in the first place (however unknowingly, on Randi's end, this specific fight) put a low hum of dread under even the sweetest lines.

**Erotic charge** — very high, and it's not gratuitous to me; it tracks Vee's actual state (denied twice, then given), and the doorstep scene — the open door banging in the wind, neither of them stopping to close it — has real abandon in it that I felt. It never goes slack; if anything the quieter coda (pancakes, his knee, the crack in his voice) is where the heat resolves into something that felt like the emotional payload rather than an afterthought.

**Friction** — none that felt like the author's thumb on the scale. The Kayla-in-the-hallway beat is a lovely bit of comic tension that never breaks register. The one thing I clocked as possibly too neat is how completely the pancakes-and-kneeling erase the actual unresolved question — but I think that's the point being made about Vee, not a crack in the writing.

**Titles** — "Nothing Underneath" is doing exactly what it says on the surface (the coat, the plan) and then a second, colder thing once I sit with it: there is a lot going on underneath everything in this book that Vee still can't see — Randi's engineering, Pace's withholding, the whole game. The title gave me the surface pleasure and then, on second thought, needled me with the deeper meaning. That's a good title. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the tagline about "every open door" and "every step is hers" feel newly loaded after this chapter — Vee walked through an actual open door tonight, on her own two (borrowed) feet, believing it was entirely her choice.

**What I want next** — I want to see whether Vee ever finds her own voice separate from Randi's script, and I'm dreading, a little, the moment (if it ever comes) when she realizes how much of her own desire has been staged for her by someone she trusts completely. I also want more of Randi's interior like "Fairytale" gave me — it's the strongest writing in this stretch for me.
