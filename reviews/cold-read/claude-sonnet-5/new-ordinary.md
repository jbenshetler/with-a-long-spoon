## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one just about melted me, and then it did something quieter and sadder underneath the melting that I didn't expect from a chapter this cozy.

Pace, first: he's still the steadiest, most legible person in the book, and this chapter leans hard into what I already trust about him — no argument when Vee needs distance for school, no cold shoulder, no toll exacted. "He hadn't argued and he hadn't gone cold and he hadn't made her pay a cent for it, and the plain grace of that had cost her more than a fight would have, because some unreasonable corner of her had wanted him to fight." That line is so good and so true to how insecure love actually works — she wants proof of need, and he gives her something better and harder to trust, which is total ease. I love him in this chapter and I also can't stop hearing, underneath every bit of that ease, the earlier scenes where the reader knows this same unhurried, generous, "I'll be here" mode is not unique to Vee — it's simply how he loves, full stop, and he's loving someone else the same way at the same time. That's not new information this chapter, but it colors every gesture of his patience here in a way I can't switch off anymore.

Vee is where all my sympathy lives right now, more than ever. The five-nights-apart guilt, the running tally her body keeps that "her mouth would never once have read out," the shirt she washes and immediately regrets washing and has to explain herself into — all of it reads like a woman who still can't quite believe she's allowed to want this much, and is negotiating with her own hunger in real time even as she's stopped denying it exists. That's consistent and moving. The pizza scene, where she can suddenly taste every separate ingredient instead of "one flavor pretending all on its own to be a meal" — that's the food motif doing exactly the work it's been doing all along (her appetite waking up, permission arriving through her senses before her mind catches up), and it still works on me every time.

Swoon / pull: the whole reunion — the door opening before she reaches it, the coat sliding off unnoticed, the comic ungainly striptease down the hall (the jeans that "had been sensible in the car and were the enemy now," her hopping out of them, kicking the door) — is genuinely hot and genuinely funny at once, which is a hard combination to land and it lands. And then the shirt at the end got me the most, more than the sex did: her giving it back not to relinquish it but to have it refilled, "so it smells like you again," and him hearing "exactly how many of her nights had been spent breathing a dying shirt" and not once making her defend it. That's the chapter's real climax for me — small, domestic, wordless — and it's the kind of tenderness this book has earned honestly, scene after scene.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new happens on the page to make me distrust Pace here — if anything this chapter is pure, undiluted proof of his gentleness. My unease is entirely inherited, not earned fresh by this chapter; it's the dramatic irony sitting quietly under an otherwise flawless scene, and I notice I'm carrying it into a chapter that gave me no new reason to.

Erotic charge: strong through the hallway sequence — playful, mutual, unhurried even in its urgency, her body "publishing the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private" when she comes almost too fast. It cools into something warmer and more domestic with the pizza-making, which is the right choice; I didn't want two straight scenes of heat back to back, and the shift to him "gone down into the work" of the dough felt like breathing room, not slackness.

Friction: very little. If I'm picking at anything, the striptease choreography runs a beat long — jeans, socks, bra, thong, each item gets its own little paragraph — but it's charming rather than tedious, and it earns its comedy ("there was no dignity anywhere in any of it and she'd have burned the house down for him").

Titles: "The New Ordinary" is doing real work once I sit with it. It names exactly what this chapter is — not a first, not a rupture, just the routine now: her drawer, her side of the counter, the running tally of nights apart, the shirt-refilling ritual. It's a title about domestication, about how fast and how completely an affair this intense has become a life. Read against the jacket's "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one" and the series title With a Long Spoon, it lands with a chill I don't think the chapter itself intends me to feel yet — the more ordinary this gets for Vee, the more thoroughly she's inside something she still doesn't know the shape of. The tagline's "every open door is a temptation and every step is hers" isn't visible anywhere in this particular chapter — there's no Randi here at all — which made the chapter feel like a held breath, a purely Pace-and-Vee interlude right after the gut-punch of the previous one.

What I want next: I want back to Randi, urgently — the "Outlier" chapter left her exposed in a way I haven't seen before (the flat, ragged "cheating bastards," the withdrawal Vee watched and said nothing about), and this chapter's total silence on that thread feels less like forgetting and more like Vee tucking it away exactly the way she tucked away Randi's shame, protective and unspoken. I want to know if it resurfaces, whether Vee ever tells her she saw it, and whether Cassie's flat unease about Randi ever gets a data point to work with. Guess from here: I suspect the PPP scene is going to matter more than a single chapter's worth — it's the first time Randi's polish cracked in front of Vee specifically, not just the reader, and that feels load-bearing.
