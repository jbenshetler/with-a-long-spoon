## Reader reaction

This one landed hard, and differently than what came before. Vee's outburst — "Is this what we are now?" — felt so true to how a held-in question actually detonates: too loud, wrong-sized, immediately regretted but unstoppable. I felt for her completely in that dark room, and then I felt the whole evening curdle. The Chili's scene is one of the best pieces of writing yet — his eyes sliding to the TV, the fork-squaring, the flicker of irritation he hides from her — because it's rendered entirely through Vee's anxious surveillance of him, and I don't know if I'm watching a man genuinely uncomfortable in public with her or a man who just doesn't like loud restaurants. The text won't tell me, and neither will she resolve it — that not-knowing is now the book's whole texture.

What unsettled me most wasn't the restaurant, it was the fight in the front room. "You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail" — and she knows this isn't true even as she says it, "some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold that night." She's rewriting history in real time to keep a fight alive because she needs him to prove something to her. That's not villainy, that's someone starving. And then his answer — "I still can't hear you... Somewhere quieter, I can. And I do. I'm listening now." — is genuinely moving, and I felt the fight go out of both of us at once. But I registered, the way she almost registers and then doesn't, that "you know what happened after the dance" is Pace declining to relitigate rather than actually explaining himself, and that his restaurant explanation, while true-sounding, conveniently never answers the actual question she asked: are they public? Are they anything with a name?

The sex scene is gorgeous and full — his mouth at her breasts, "as though he were taking something there for himself," his control finally breaking — and I felt swept up in it completely, wanted it for her, wanted the loss of control to mean what she needed it to mean. But the ending gutted me: "she came with his name in her mouth and not the other thing." Her own body censors her. And then lying there afterward, marked all over with him, still nameless — "he'd given her his control and all of his body and every last thing but the one" — that's the whole chapter in a sentence. This is the "pile" from the prior chapter made explicit and enormous: she can't ask her mother, can't tell her father, doesn't have a word for the best thing in her life. The mismatch from last chapter (crackers/meal vs. "how good we are together") has now fully bloomed into something she can't manage silently anymore, though she's still trying to.

I don't distrust Pace exactly — his explanation about the restaurant is plausible and kind — but I no longer trust that plausible-and-kind is the same as true, or that it's the whole story. That's new. The "not finished" with Randi sits under all of this like a stone I keep bumping.

**Titles:** "Under the Rug" — brutal once you finish it. Everything in this chapter gets swept under something: her outburst gets answered but not resolved, the fight gets "forgiven" before it's actually settled, the real question (are we anything, publicly, with a name) never gets answered, it just gets soothed. Even her orgasm sweeps the word back down. The title doesn't just describe the chapter, it indicts the whole coping mechanism she's using — burying, not resolving.
