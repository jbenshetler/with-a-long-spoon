## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. From the very first paragraph — the pie with its label, "the good scissors," the buckles done up "by feel" — I was fully back inside Vee's anxious, over-engineered contrition, and I felt for her instantly. That heel-height moment at the door is such a small, perfect humiliation: she built the whole apparatus of the apology (heels, scarf, pie, script) and the first thing it does is accidentally make her *taller* than him, and she reads it as one more disrespect. My chest actually clenched at "Sorry, these are, I didn't mean to, I can take them…" — and then his line, the true extravagant thing said plainly, undoes her completely. I believed every second of it.

The confession itself is the best writing about Vee's inner life this book has given me — "I knew what she was doing and it got into me anyway" is exactly the kind of self-honesty this reader wants, the shame she can't quite look at squarely finally spoken aloud. And Pace's response — not absolution, but "Thank you for telling me what she said" — is the single warmest, most earned beat between them so far. He doesn't let her off with a clean apology; he wants the actual truth, and when he gets it he receives it rather than fixes it.

**Pace himself** is landing beautifully now across two chapters — courtly without performance, genuinely curious about her rather than merely forgiving, and domestic in a way that disarmed me: him at the sink, the folded T-shirt left for her to find, "You're always beautiful" instead of "you're beautiful." That distinction is the kind of precise tenderness I read this genre for.

**Swoon / pull:** the whole reconciliation and the morning-after sequence is pure warmth — I wanted them to have this, and the book gave it to me without irony. The dishwashing scene is genuinely charming, funny, alive — "Soaking is a recognized method" made me smile before anything else happened.

**Erotic charge:** the spank is where it flares — unplanned, mutual (her swat, then his), and her reaction is rendered with real honesty: "a soundless *oh*... in the middle of his kitchen." I felt the charge; it's not clinical, it's disorienting *for her*, which is exactly right. It reads as new information her body is giving her that her conscious mind hasn't caught up to — which is the kind of interior complexity I want from this book.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** nothing here reads as off. This chapter is almost entirely inside a real, unguarded intimacy — I have no fresh suspicion of Pace from the page itself. My unease is carried over, not renewed: "not finished" still sits in my head from last chapter, unaddressed here, and its absence from this scene is itself slightly eerie — this is the tenderest he's been, and I know something Vee doesn't.

**Titles:** "A Recognized Method" — I love this. It's Vee's own joke-line about soaking dishes, deployed with total unseriousness, but it retroactively reads as commentary on the whole chapter: her scripted apology (a method!) failing and something truer replacing it; even the spank landing as an unplanned "method" of arousal neither of them scheduled. Playful, not spoiler-heavy, but it rewards a second look.

**What I want next:** I want Randi back on the page — I'm dying to know if she knows about "not finished," and how she'll react to Vee glowing from this weekend. I also want to know whether Vee will tell Cassie or Randi any of this, and whether Pace's tenderness here is fully what it appears, or complicated by whatever's unresolved with Randi.
