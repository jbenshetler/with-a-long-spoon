## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still vibrating from the mixer kiss, and the opening paragraphs land exactly where they should — her body "warm clean through, lush and alive, strumming with the evening," refusing to settle. What got me first wasn't the drive to Pace's house, it was the small compulsive detail before it: she starts to touch herself, catches her own hand "like something she'd caught stealing," reaches for the word *boyfriend* "the way you reach for the rail on a stair," and then — instead of taking the rest of the way off — puts Randi's skirt back on. That's a gut-level, wordless self-betrayal dressed up as a decision to be good. I noticed it and believed it completely; nobody told me what it meant, I just felt the wrongness of the logic (I'll go be with my boyfriend so I don't have to think about her — while putting her friend's clothes back on her body first).

**Vee** — my sympathy for her doesn't just hold here, it deepens into something almost unbearable, because this whole chapter is her lying to herself in real time and I get to watch the seams. The 1 a.m. arrival, the "I need you" that's really "I need a wall to hide behind," the insistence to herself that this is just her being wound-up and horny like other nights — and then Pace naming it in one line ("That wasn't you wound up... That was angry, Vee") before she's even ready to know it herself. I ached for her. And then the confession scene, which is the emotional and erotic center of the chapter, is agonizing in the best way: she wants to tell the truth so badly ("some part of her was already reaching for this, dying to set the whole weight of it down") and can't tell the one person she'd normally tell (Randi, obviously, is the wound), so it all comes out sideways, to Pace, under duress, traded for pleasure inch by inch. By the end, lying to him about the masturbation ("No... Nothing," "level as a countertop") right after all that raw honesty was the single worst moment for me as a reader — worse than anything she confessed about Randi — because it's the first outright lie I've caught her in, not an omission she's arguing herself into but a flat, practiced denial. And then the last lines: *What is wrong with me* — no answer, mind stuck on Tuesday, body finally quiet and mind refusing to follow. That's the whole book's engine in miniature and it got me.

**Pace** — this is the chapter where my trust in him actually moved, and not in the direction of more trust. Everything he does in the room reads, on its own terms, as generous: he clocks her anger immediately and doesn't punish her for it; he builds a game that lets her tell the truth without being interrogated head-on; he never once turns jealous or ugly about Randi; his final answer to her accusation ("You want Randi") — "You keep saying her name, Vee. Not me. It's all right. I don't mind." — is written to melt, and on the page, unqualified, it does melt, it's tender, it's secure. But I am carrying more than the page is carrying right now. I know — the text doesn't, Vee doesn't — that he's been sleeping with Randi the entire time, that Randi feeds him Vee's reactions in bed, that the whole relationship began with him asking Randi, mid-sex, "would you want to taste another woman" and pointing her out on a quad. So when he calmly asks, almost as an afterthought clearing the table, "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" — I felt a chill under the tenderness that I don't think the prose is asking me to feel yet, but that I can't not feel, because it's the exact shape of the fantasy that started all of this. I want to be careful here: the text itself never confirms he's fishing, or performing generosity, or executing anything — it plays entirely straight as devotion, and I have to hold that open rather than decide it for the page. But my suspicion of him, which had been more diffuse before, sharpened into something almost specific in this chapter. The truth-for-inches game itself is the clearest image yet of what unsettles me structurally about him: pleasure rationed out as currency for confession, offered as intimacy, indistinguishable on the page from control.

**Randi** doesn't appear on the page at all this chapter and is somehow the most present person in the room the entire time — in Vee's mouth, in Vee's body, in the fantasy that interrupts her own hand, in the dread/anticipation of Tuesday. That's a good trick and it worked on me; her absence from the scene makes her hold more weight than her presence usually does.

**Cassie** gets one quiet, devastating beat: asleep, "long down and gone," never stirring while Vee dresses and slips out at 1 a.m. to go confess to someone else the thing she'd never bring to Cassie. It's brief but it lands — the steady friend sidelined again, this time literally unconscious to it.

**Swoon / pull** — the interrogation-through-sex device is genuinely, viscerally hot: the rationing of inches for truths, the way each true confession is rewarded and each softened one is punished with stillness, the way her own arousal becomes the thing forcing her honesty rather than the reverse. That's where the chapter peaks for me — "You finish when you've finished" is a wonderful, cruel, generous line all at once. I was swept along with it exactly as intended.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — Cassie remains the only person I trust without qualification (and she's barely awake for it). My suspicion of Pace, previously more atmospheric ("something about the pattern"), got a concrete hook in this chapter: his casual, unshaken calm at the exact question that started the whole engineered relationship. I want to be honest that the text doesn't earn a verdict here — it earns a chill, not a conviction.

**Erotic charge** — very high, sustained across most of the chapter, peaking at "You finish when you've finished" and the final "He drove her over. All of him at once, nothing kept back." It never goes clinical; if anything it gets more emotionally freighted the longer it runs, because the stakes (what she'll admit) keep escalating alongside the physical stakes.

**Friction** — none that bothered me. The device (truth traded for penetration depth) risks feeling gimmicky on a plot-summary level but on the page it's controlled and escalates cleanly; I never felt the author's thumb on the scale.

**Titles** — "Boyfriend" is quietly devastating once you've read the chapter: it's the word she reaches for as a lifeline ("a plain straight fact of a word... the rail on a stair"), and the chapter closes on the same word, deployed with visible irony — "Her boyfriend. She had just had sex with her boyfriend, twice... and her mind gone straight down the county road and back to campus." The title names the anchor and then shows it not holding. It didn't give anything away going in; it recolors retroactively, which is the best kind. The tagline — *Every yes was freely given. That was the problem* — feels like it was written for this exact chapter: every yes here (to the kiss, to Pace, to the confession) is hers, freely made, and still she ends up somewhere she didn't choose and can't undo.

**What I want next** — I want Tuesday. I want to know what happens in that classroom, side by side with Randi, after this. I'm bracing for the possibility that Pace's calm at "do you want to sleep with a woman" wasn't idle — guess from here, not knowledge — and dreading, a little, what it would mean if Randi and Pace already know what's happening to her before she's said a word to either of them separately.
