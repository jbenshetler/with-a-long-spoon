## Reader reaction

Well. That escalated fast, and I mean that as both body and heart response.

The opening — the "I miss you" text she's never sent before, the fifteen minutes of raw exposure while it sits there — that's the chapter's real hinge for me, more than the sex that follows. This is Vee reaching first, for the first time on the page. It lands as a genuine escalation of intimacy, not just plot. And the horseplay that follows — stealing his shorts, flashing him, discovering she's faster than him — is some of the most purely *fun* she's been allowed to be. I loved it. It's playful in a way this book hasn't given her before, and it makes the turn into the bet-spanking feel earned rather than dropped-in.

The spanking scene itself: I have to name the swoon and the unease honestly, side by side, because the text asked me to hold both. The tenderness is real — "he gentled her," the kiss after, the soothing, "wonderful, wonderful" until it's indistinguishable from his hand in her hair. That's devotion on the page, not performance. But I can't unhear Randi's story sitting right there in Vee's head mid-scene — "would she have to count out loud? Was that the theatre of it" — and the deliberate contrast the chapter draws: "What came was nothing like the story. Behind her he was quiet." That's the book doing careful, pointed work, not me projecting it. It's asking me to compare this man to whoever taught Randi to count, and finding him gentler here. Which either reassures me about Pace, or makes me more uneasy about what happened to Randi — possibly both.

The consent architecture of the scene is the thing I trust completely even as the content gets darker: "Either you admit you lost, or we find out" is a real, playable dare with a real escape hatch, and her body's honesty ("she nodded. She didn't decide to") reads as her truth outrunning her pride, not coercion. The narration keeps insisting on this — "Shame wants a witness... there was no such thing as a witness" — and I believe it, because the whole scene is staged as safety, not exposure-for-its-own-sake.

The scar again — she touches it, doesn't ask, "the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep" — still totally unexplained, still an itch.

The coda with the curry and the juice and the scrunchies undid me a little. "I meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet" — that's the tenderest line in the chapter, quietly devastating. And her talking to her own reflection, self-aware and funny and a little rueful ("my Victorian LARPing... does me no good at all") — I like her so much in this small domestic aftermath. It's the most relaxed, most herself she's been on the page.

Title: "We Find Out" — direct lift of his line in the bet, and it does double work: she finds out what her body wants regardless of pride, and I as reader find out something about what kind of "spanking" this actually is versus Randi's story. Sits well, doesn't overreach.

I want to know now whether she'll ever tell him about Randi's story, whether the parallel she drew silently in her own head will ever surface between them, and whether "Peter" is ever going to arrive.
