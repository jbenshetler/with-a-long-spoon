# Cold read — Sorority

## Reader reaction

There's so much pleasure in this chapter and it worked on me almost completely straight — and then that ending line landed with a small, cold undertow I wasn't braced for.

The dress reveal at the top is a delight. I felt Vee's private, sudden wanting — "the wanting got there before the plan" — and Randi's response is one of the loveliest things she's done on the page: the stillness before speaking, the fingers reading the seam like a sentence, and then "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." That line does something real to me too — it's exactly right, better than Vee's own aborted "it's the most—", and I felt the generosity of Randi handing Vee the precise word for what she'd cried about without being able to name. My trust in Randi went up here, genuinely — that's not steering, that's a friend seeing something true and giving it back better than you had it.

Then the shopping trip is just pure fun — the yeti shoe, the rhinestone platforms, "do these go with burgundy," the two of them collapsing into each other on a bolted bench. This is the least loaded, most simply happy Vee and Randi have been together on the page, and it worked on me completely; I was smiling. The older couple watching them, "just two girls being silly over shoes," is a lovely small human detail — it made me feel how visible their affection is, how unguarded.

But then the register shifts, cleanly and on purpose, at the boutique. The touch — Randi holding Vee's bare foot, the thumb crossing the nail once, "Mm," Vee's hand shooting out to steady herself against a small swing of the room — that's charged, and I felt it as charged: a current under the shoe-fitting that's not just friendship, though nothing is named. It's the clearest erotic beat of the chapter for me, precisely because it's so restrained — a single touch, a single syllable, and Vee physically destabilized by it. I don't fully trust my own read here — is that lust, or just intimacy so total it registers in the body the same way? The text holds it open and I want to hold it open too.

And then the money. This is where the chapter turned on me a little. Vee putting the debit card away and taking out the emergency credit card, knowing she can't actually afford it and doing it anyway — "she did not have the next part, the paying-it-back part" — landed as genuinely painful, not swoony. And right beside it, Randi's careless multiple-of-Vee's-cost purchase, not even glancing at the total, buying loafers to replace loafers she'll "wear out" — the asymmetry is stark and the text doesn't soften it. It's not cruel, exactly; Randi isn't doing anything to Vee. But it makes visible, wordlessly, a gulf between them that the whole warm day had let me forget. "You can put it in the trunk, you know... It's not going to *spoil*" — I felt that line as unintentionally cutting, Randi reading Vee's careful hold on the box as pure sentiment when some of it, I think, is also that Vee is holding onto the only expensive thing she's ever owned like it could still somehow be taken back. The chapter doesn't editorialize this; it just lets me see it and lets Vee laugh it off, "unrehearsed," genuinely happy — which is its own kind of ache, because I'm not sure she's let herself see what I just saw.

Cassie is barely on the page (one line: her bunk "made tight and square... nobody home") but even that glancing detail lands as characterization — orderly, private, absent by design from a scene about spending and being seen.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't suspect Randi of anything calculated here — this doesn't feel like manipulation, it feels like class difference rendered honestly and without villainy. But my eyes are open in a new way to how much of what "friendship" costs is being paid unevenly, and how little either of them seems fully able to say so.

Erotic charge: low overall, one hot little needle-point at the shoe fitting — Heat 1, all in that touch.

Titles: "Sorority" — no sorority appears on the page (unless I'm missing a house/context I don't have), so it reads to me as pointing at the *kind* of intimacy on display — the sisterly, performative, generous-to-a-fault love between women, its rituals (showing the dress, the shopping trip, the laughing collapse) — and maybe, given the ending, at what that intimacy can paper over. It doesn't give anything away outright, but it recolors the chapter faintly toward "sisterhood has its own economies" once I finish it. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — the "long spoon" idea (which I only have as a title, no gloss) keeps nudging me toward reading generosity and its costs as a live thread, and this chapter, with Randi's genuine warmth sitting right next to an economic gulf neither of them names, feels like it's quietly serving that.

What I want next: I want to see whether Vee ever reckons with the money on the page, even just to herself — right now she's laughing it off, and I want to know if that's real or performed. And I'm newly curious, in a wanting-not-dreading way, about that foot-touch — whether it recurs, whether it's named, whether it's just Randi being Randi.
