# Cold read (grounded) — Sorority

## Reader reaction

Felt read

This one softened me toward Randi and then quietly unnerved me, in the same breath, which is exactly the trick this book keeps pulling on me.

Vee is as easy to love as ever — I laughed out loud at the yeti shoe and the "brisk walk from becoming a sandal" flat, and that shoe-store slapstick is genuinely funny on the page, unforced, two young women being ridiculous together. I felt the pure sweetness of it. And her private arithmetic at the end — swapping the debit card for the emergency credit card, "the last careful thing in her give way" — landed hard on me. That's not romance-novel spending, that's a twenty-one-year-old on a real budget deciding a pair of shoes she can't afford is what the dress is "owed." I wanted to grab her by the shoulders. It's not that Randi forces her — nobody puts a hand on the card — but the whole afternoon is engineered, gently, inexorably, toward that number on the sole. "The word was more than Vee had ever let herself call him... Pace rearranged in her own chest into something adult and lit" — Randi drops "lover" like a key in a lock and it works instantly. That's masterful and it's also exactly the kind of skill I no longer trust in her.

Randi herself: my feeling toward her keeps splitting down the same fault line the checkpoint already opened. The joy in the shoe store felt completely real to me — "the real laugh, the one that costs her something to give" — I believe that. But then the boutique scene turns it, deliberately: she reads the dress like an appraiser ("Charmeuse... And a good weight"), she names the exact shoe before Vee can protest, she kneels and touches Vee's foot and the frosted-plum polish with a single thumb-stroke that visibly destabilizes Vee ("felt the floor of her tip... her hand shot out and found Randi's shoulder to stop it"), and Randi's only response is to smile and say nothing and bend back to the box. That's not accidental. That's Randi fully aware of the effect she's having and choosing not to acknowledge it, which reads to me as something closer to control than to innocence — even inside real tenderness. The reveal that they're the same shoe size, "practically a marriage," delivered as a joke that Vee can't answer, is doing the same double duty — genuine delight and a line dropped exactly where it will land.

Pace is offstage this chapter but present in every sentence about the dress and the shoes — I still trust the craft and the care completely, though the checkpoint's unease about the "pointing game" origin sits under all of it like a stain I can't unsee, even in a chapter this warm.

Cassie barely appears (a made bed, a flat "nobody home" glance from Randi) but her absence from this outing is its own small note — she's the one person who might have said "don't buy the shoes."

Swoon / pull: the peekaboo-dress reveal to Randi at the very top — "He didn't make this for you... He made it to you" — got me. That line does real work; it names what Vee herself couldn't find words for in the previous chapter, and Randi handing her the exact phrase she needed is one of the most seductive things in the whole book so far, seductive in the literal sense of drawing someone toward you by giving them what they're starving for. And the shoe-store comedy is just plain fun — I was smiling the whole way through it, no unease at all in that stretch.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't think Randi is being cruel, and I don't think she's lying about her affection. But I no longer read her warmth as separable from her deliberateness — the two are fused in her, and this chapter shows that fusion at its most functional: she reads Vee's body (the toe stroke), reads her wallet (steering her off the tan heel), and reads her heart ("lover") all in one afternoon, and gets exactly the outcome she was angling for each time, wrapped in genuine-feeling delight. That's earned suspicion, not manufactured — the text gave me the mechanism in plain sight ("Which is exactly why you're not buying that").

Erotic charge: low-key but present, and its peak is the toe-stroke — "A thumb moved once across the nail of the big toe, light — and Vee felt it go all the way up the length of her." One touch, more charged than most full scenes manage. The rest of the chapter is warm rather than hot — the dress reveal in the dorm room has some heat in how Randi handles the fabric and Vee's shoulder, but it's more sensuous than sexual.

Friction: none that bothered me — the comic shoe-store sequence runs a little long relative to what it's doing, but it earns its length by being genuinely funny and by setting up the tonal turn into the boutique. No thumb-on-the-scale moments; the credit-card swap is shown, not narrated at me with a moral.

Titles: "Sorority" is almost misleadingly plain — I expected sisters, a house, Standards-board politics, and instead I got a dorm room, a bypass shoe store, and a Randi boutique. On reflection it might be pointing at the two of them as their own sorority-of-two, a private sisterhood built entirely around Randi's terms and taste — "practically a marriage," she says of the matching shoe size — which recolors the title nicely after the fact rather than giving anything away up front. The book title and tagline aren't quoted to me here, but the jacket's line about Vee "growing to want Randi, too" and never noticing it feels like exactly what's dramatized in the toe-stroke moment — the wanting is right there on the page and Vee names it only as dizziness.

What I want next: I want to see whether Vee ever notices what her own body just told her in that boutique, and I'm dreading, mildly, the credit card bill and what it will cost her later — financially and otherwise. Pure guess from here: I suspect Randi will find some way to "help" with that bill too, and that help won't be free either.
