# Cold read — Rock

*scene: scenes/rock.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: gone*

## Reader reaction

God, this one knocked the wind out of me a little, and not the way I expected going in. After the last chapter — after sitting in that room with Randi and Pace talking about Vee like she was a subject to be paced — I came into this one braced for more of that management, more of that coldness under the tenderness. Instead I got Vee and Pace alone, actually alone, the two of them under a quilt with cold tea and a fire, and it's the most unguarded either of them has been on the page. My whole read of Pace moved tonight.

The food/family exchange at the top is just lovely — warm, funny, real. "It was made with love. It just never tried to surprise anybody" — that's such a precise, aching way to describe a childhood, and Pace's "And affectionate" answer, going straight to the tenderness under her joke instead of laughing at the casseroles, is exactly the kind of listening that's made me trust him in earlier chapters even while I've had reasons to doubt him. I believed every second of it.

Then the real name. "My real name is Peter." That landed with real weight — the first crack in "Pace" as armor, something he's built and something he's choosing, just once, to set down for her. And then the whole backstory: skipping ahead at fifteen, the math teacher exiling him to the hall for asking questions she couldn't answer, doing long division as punishment for loving the thing too much. I felt real grief for him there — "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment," Vee says, and I felt that in my chest.

But it's the tutor story that really got me, and got me uneasy in a way I can't shake. A nineteen-year-old woman, assigned to a fifteen-year-old boy, for two years, ending when he turned seventeen and she turned twenty-one — "the best two years I'd had," he says, "plainly," like a man handing someone a wound and calling it luck. Vee does the arithmetic and holds the ugly word under her tongue, and so did I. The text doesn't let either of us say it, and that restraint is doing real work — it's not naming it as abuse, but it's showing us a boy who was groomed (there, I did think the word, even though Vee wouldn't say it) into believing that arrangement was the best thing that ever happened to him, and now, as a grown man, he replicates something of that shape — an asymmetric, curated, "showing her she doesn't have to be ashamed" tutelage — with Vee. I don't think the chapter is accusing him. I think it's quietly, carefully letting me see where his whole idiom of care — controlled, patient, "I want her to get there on her own" — actually comes from. That's devastating in a way no cruelty could be.

The math/lifting monologue is gorgeous and genuinely moving on its own terms — the coastline paradox, the paint-filling shape, Newton's three sentences as "the language the world is written in." I felt the swoon here completely unguarded; this is a man who has been starving to be understood and is finally being listened to by someone who's actually looking at him. And then Vee's insight — "That's how you are with me... it's the lift. It's the same face" — ties his whole interior life to how he's present with her, and it's tender and true and I wanted to cry a little.

The scar: finally touched, finally almost-named, and refused. "Not tonight." That's not evasive in a bad way to me — it reads like an honest door with a real hinge, not a stonewall — and the gesture of pressing her hand down over it and holding it there is one of the most intimate things in the book so far. I don't feel manipulated by that withholding; I feel like I'm being asked to wait exactly as long as Vee is.

**Erotic charge:** low and it doesn't need to be higher — this is a heat-0/1 chapter that runs entirely on intimacy, and it's some of the most charged non-sexual writing in the book. The hand on her breast at the opening, unhurried, "as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm" — that's warmer to me than a lot of actual sex scenes.

**Friction:** none that felt like a thumb on the scale. If anything the chapter trusts me more than most — it never says the word "grooming," never says "control," lets Vee's held-back sentence do all the work. That restraint is the opposite of friction; it's the chapter's whole method.

**Titles:** "Rock" — I don't have a strong lock on this one yet. Could be the ridge outside the window, could be something steadying (the quilt, the fire, Pace himself as something she can lean her whole weight against), could point toward the scar — something old and hard under the skin that doesn't move. I'm holding it loosely; it doesn't feel like it gave anything away, just sits there waiting for me to understand it in hindsight.

**What I want next:** I want to go back to Randi now with everything I know from this chapter in hand — does she know this history? Did Pace tell her the tutor story too, or is tonight the first time he's said "Peter" out loud to anyone in this book? I want the scar, eventually. And I'm newly, warmly, worriedly invested in Vee learning to hold both truths at once — that Pace's tenderness is real, and that its shape was formed somewhere that wasn't safe for him.
