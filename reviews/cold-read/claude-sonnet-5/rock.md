## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. This is the chapter I didn't know I was waiting for — Vee and Pace alone, quiet, no Randi, no performance, just the two of them under a quilt with tea going cold, and it's the most intimate thing in the book so far without a stitch of sex actually happening on the page (well — implied earlier, "before the rest of it," but the chapter itself is all aftermath and talk). I feel like I finally got let into the room I've been reading around for chapters.

**Pace** — this is the chapter that either confirms or dismantles everything I've been suspicious of, and it does both at once, which is the honest answer. His real name is Peter — and it lands exactly as heavy as it should: "The name sat there between them, plainer than Pace, with no forward motion in it." The hallway story wrecked me a little — a math teacher moving a gifted kid's desk into the hallway as punishment for asking questions she couldn't answer, "and I did long-division worksheets" for a year. Vee's line back — "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" — is exactly right and exactly what I wanted said. Then the math-as-beauty monologue (the paint-a-shape-you-can't-paint-the-outside-of thing, the coastline paradox, Newton's three sentences) is genuinely swoony, a real seduction that isn't a seduction — it's a boy handing a girl the actual center of himself and having her get it.

And then Daphne. This is the big one. A tutor assigned to a fifteen-year-old, "she was nineteen," two years, ending when the "distance that had been nothing at nineteen and fifteen was, all at once, the entire thing." I felt the exact cold drop Vee describes — "the clean ugly grown-up word" she holds under her tongue and doesn't say. The text does not let Pace hear what he's telling her; he "believed every part of what he'd told her," calls it "the best two years I'd had," and Vee's read on it — "he was defending it... setting himself between the memory and the word she hadn't said" — is devastating and exactly earned by the flat, over-careful cadence of his telling ("each word set down separately and precisely in its place, as though the placing of them were what held the sentence up off the floor"). This recontextualizes everything about how he is with women — "you always get bored," his patience with Vee, the age gap he and Randi and now Vee are all inside of, whether he's aware of the pattern he's repeating. I don't think the text tells me to condemn him. It tells me Vee sees it and swallows it, out of tenderness, and that's its own quiet unease.

**Vee** — I love her here. Casseroles and unopened saffron and "Vivienne" vs. "Vee" (the name that means love in one mouth and trouble in the other — Pace's line, and it's a keeper) are warm, specific, funny, lived-in. And her restraint with the Daphne story — not saying sorry, not saying the word, pressing her shoulder against his arm "there and gone" instead — is the most grown-up thing I've seen her do. She's listening past what he says to what he needs her not to say back.

**Swoon/pull**: enormous. The whole domestic frame — robe smelling like him, tea going cold because talking mattered more, her palm over his heart where "the careful voice had come out of" — this is tenderness I trust completely, on her side and largely on his. The scar moment at the end ("Not tonight" — not a wall, "a door with a time on it that wasn't now") is a perfect close: consent to withhold, honored, and she accepts it without pushing. That's the same patience he's shown her, mirrored back.

**Erotic charge**: low-key, almost none directly (no sex on page), but the tactile warmth — his hand flat and easy on her breast at the opening, unhesitating and unasking — is intimate in a way that reads as more erotic than explicit heat would have here.

**Unease**: Daphne. Genuinely earned, not manufactured — the text gave me the arithmetic and let me do it myself before naming anything, which is exactly the kind of trust I want from a book like this. I don't know what to do with it yet and I don't think I'm supposed to.

**Title "Rock"**: I don't see an obvious anchor for it on the page — no rock literally named. Could be the lifting/squat-bar steel, could be Pace himself as ballast/immovable, could be the scar (rock-hard old wound). Left genuinely puzzling to me; nothing forces a reading.

**Want next**: I want to know what the scar is. I want to see whether Vee carries the Daphne story forward into how she reads Pace, or lets it go the way she let it go on the page. I want Randi and Vee back in a room together now that I know what Randi knows and feels.
