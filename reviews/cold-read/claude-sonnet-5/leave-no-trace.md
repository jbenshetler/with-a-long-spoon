## Reader reaction

The chapter opens on pure body-fear — the wall of trees, her chest tight, hand halfway to her throat — and I felt that with her, the specific vertigo of committing to something with your mouth before your legs have agreed to it. And then Pace is just... good here. Genuinely good, in the unglamorous, competent way: noticing her face and not making anything of it, setting a pace that doesn't shame her, the boot scene — unlacing her boot, finding the hot spot, apologizing for not asking about boots beforehand. I want to trust this. Some large part of me does, in the moment, the way she does. The cherry-pit moment got me especially — "We take nothing and leave nothing," gentle, no scolding, and then he eats his own cherry and lays his own pit down without looking at her, so she isn't singled out in her not-knowing. That's real tenderness technique, whether or not it's also technique-technique. I can't unknow what I know about him from earlier chapters — the froyo system, the dinner built as he went — so even this lovely, ordinary kindness lands with a double register I didn't ask for and can't turn off.

The summit is the best writing in the chapter for me — "the world did not care that she was standing on the edge of it... and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it." That's a real image, earned, not decorative. And him looking at her face instead of the view, letting her catch him, then turning away to give her the seeing alone — that's a considerate, secure kind of wanting, the opposite of grasping. I liked him there without reservation.

The dress moment is where the chapter quietly breaks my heart a little — she wants the russet silk dress, catches herself wanting it, puts it back ("You put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking"), and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and doesn't push on the bruise. That restraint reads as real respect. I trust that beat completely, no undertow.

Then the rain, the shirt — his only dry shirt, given to her, and then him getting back out into the storm rather than sit in the cab with her. I felt the chivalry and I felt, simultaneously, exactly what she felt: the ache of it being wrong-shaped, a kindness that leaves her alone in the cab with her body doing something urgent. And what she does next is the chapter's real center for me — the deliberate unbuttoning, eyes locked on his, giving him her body to look at while the internalized mother-voice runs commentary the whole time ("floozy," "what kind of girl"). This is exactly the "desire she can't fully account for versus desire that is unmistakably hers" tension the whole book has been circling, and here it's her own hands doing it, her own choice to knock on the glass and crook a finger. I don't feel manipulated *by Pace* in this scene at all — if anything the reversal (she calls him in, she controls the pace of the buttons, she's the one performing) reads as her claiming agency after a chapter of small physical vulnerabilities. That's a genuine swoon for me, heat that's actually hers, wanted, hot on the page and not evasive about being hot.

And then the gut-punch tenderness: she reaches for his hand to pull it to her mouth for "the next thing," and it's shaking — he's freezing, has been the whole time, silent about it, because he was giving her the moment. She stops everything to warm his hand against her belly and says "Pace. You're freezing." That reversed the whole dynamic in one beat — suddenly she's caring for him, not the other way around, and it felt like the truest intimacy in the chapter, better than the striptease itself.

The ending is a gut-drop I didn't see coming — the cop lights, the siren eating her sentence, him pulled away before either of them can say the thing that mattered. It's abrupt and a little cruel structurally (to the reader, not by anyone's design on the page), and it leaves her alone under the overhang in his flannel with nothing resolved. That felt like a real narrative choice, not sloppiness — the intimacy gets interrupted by the world exactly at the point of confession.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't distrust anything Pace does *on this page* — every individual act (boot, cherry pit, dress comment, flannel, standing in the rain, the shaking hand) reads as considerate almost to a fault. My suspicion is entirely imported from what I already know about him, and the chapter doesn't add new evidence either way — it just makes the tenderness harder to dismiss, which if anything increases my unease rather than resolving it, because it's working on me the same way it's working on her.

Erotic charge: strong, and it peaks exactly where I wanted it to — the buttons, the deliberate held eye contact, "she gave it to him to see." It doesn't go slack; if anything it's cut short by the siren before it can, which feels right rather than frustrating.

No friction to speak of — the sensory writing (mustard, horseradish, cherries, rain, cold) is doing real work and I bought all of it.

Title: "Leave No Trace" — on the surface it's the hiking ethic, literally spoken ("We take nothing and leave nothing"), but it sits oblique over the whole chapter in a way that unsettles me a little on reflection — nothing that happens here leaves a trace either: no photo, no witness, no dress bought, the truck pulling away before her sentence lands, even the flannel eventually goes back. Given the reader-side knowledge I'm carrying about how deliberately Pace builds things, "leave no trace" as a hiking virtue and as a description of an affair conducted with total discretion sit uncomfortably close together, though the chapter itself never draws that line — I'm drawing it.

I want to keep reading badly — I want to know what she was going to say before the siren cut her off, and I'm newly worried about that shaking hand and what it might mean beyond cold.
