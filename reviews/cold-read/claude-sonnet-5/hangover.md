## Reader reaction

I feel about each character:

Vee — my warmth toward her only grew. The chapter opens on that gorgeous, easy hungover-morning intimacy, and I loved watching her body settle into being *cared for* rather than performing for once — the water glass with the paper towel folded under it, the loaf of bread, being dressed by him while unconscious. That's the most naked kind of trust in the book so far, more than any of the sex has been. And then she turns around and does something I really respected: she doesn't flinch from what he tells her, she does the arithmetic in real time, sitting with the ugly word about Daphne she "kept pressing back down" instead of letting herself off the hook with his own sanitized framing. That's real interior work, not just swooning.

Pace — this chapter cracked him open more than any before it, and what's inside is sadder than I expected. "There isn't anyone else" landed on me like a small, quiet gut-punch. All the courtly attentiveness, the method-like repeatability I've been suspicious of — this chapter gives it a genuine, devastating origin instead of explaining it away: a boy with no peer group at any age, ever, who built his entire capacity for closeness around one person and then lost her with nobody to grieve to. I don't think this excuses the Randi situation the reader knows about — if anything it makes his walled-off refusal to *name* things with Vee read differently now, less like withholding and more like a man who was never taught the muscle exists. But I notice I'm being asked to feel sorrier for him than the facts I hold as a reader (Randi, the pointing game) fully license. I'm resisting that pull on purpose.

Sheri — not on the page this chapter except in memory, but she looms large and lovely, "the only kind the world had ever quite let him keep." I trust her completely; she's earned it in every scene she's had.

Swoon / pull: The whole hungover opening is the tenderest domestic writing in the book — genuinely swept me up. Then the friendship confession swept me up differently, toward ache rather than heat. Vee crossing the kitchen to hold him, not offering comfort-as-fix but just weight against weight — "she didn't say she was sorry" — is one of the most moving beats in the book so far.

Trust vs. suspicion: No new suspicion of Pace here — if anything this chapter argues, convincingly, for sincerity under the technique. My suspicion remains exactly where the checkpoint already has it (Randi, the pointing game), untouched by this chapter, which is honest of the text — it doesn't try to launder anything, it just adds grief where I'd been reading calculation.

Erotic charge: Almost none, and rightly so — this is a recovery-and-confession chapter, and it earns its quiet.

Friction: None that felt like a thumb on the scale — the "men make friends by competing" speech is a touch tidy/essayistic for how he normally talks, but he's established as someone who's turned his pain into "good order," so the over-explaining reads as characterization, not authorial cheating.

Titles: "Hangover" is doing double work — literal (the ciders) and emotional: the ache/reckoning that follows a night of feeling good, both hers (physical) and the reader's dawning sense of what it costs to be Pace (emotional). Volume title "A Warm Reception" continues to feel apt and slightly ironic — warmth is everywhere on the page, but its cost keeps surfacing underneath.

What I want next: I want to see Randi and Pace's dynamic through this new lens — does she know this about him? I also can't shake wanting Vee to eventually turn this same unflinching arithmetic on Randi.
