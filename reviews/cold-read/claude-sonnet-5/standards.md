# Cold read (grounded) — Standards

*scene: scenes/standards.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch001 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Okay, I need to sit with this one a minute, because "Standards" landed very differently on me than "The Bench" did, and I think that's the point.

**How I feel about each character right now**

Pace, coming out of The Bench, is a strange mix for me. The craft of him is undeniable — the attentiveness, the water bottle left in advance, the "tell me your word," the not-knocking on the bathroom door because of Daphne, whoever she was to him, taught him that a closed door is hers not his. That's a man who has clearly done real work on himself around consent and restraint, and I believed every beat of it while I was in the scene. But I came out of that chapter with a small itch I didn't examine at the time and this chapter made me go back and look at it: he called her Randi — "There you are, Randi" — at the exact moment she broke, and it read to me in the moment as tender, as him seeing the real her. Now, reading her stand at her own bathroom mirror deciding "he could have the face... not ever," it reads differently — like he got something from her that she had never once agreed to give him, however sweetly he asked for it in the middle of the scene. I don't think the text is telling me he did anything wrong, exactly — everything was consensual, checked-in, aftercared. But my sympathy for him has gone from uncomplicated to interested-but-wary. He "was aware... that he was still hard" and set it aside — that's presented as evidence of his generosity, and I bought it as generosity, but it also quietly told me his own want was very much alive under all that care, which colors "I would answer it again, as often as she wanted to bring it to him" with something a little more proprietary than I noticed on first pass.

Randi is the person this chapter belongs to, and she's the one who moved me. In The Bench she was already interesting — the composed woman coming briefly apart, the "please" that stops being performance — but "Standards" is where I actually fell for her as a person rather than as an object of Pace's attention. Watching her manage Brooke's interrogation is a small masterclass in a kind of exhausting competence — the recalibrated lies arriving half a beat late, the laugh matched to Kinsey's pitch before she'd chosen it, "he's not anywhere near mine" landing exactly the deflection that was needed. And then the second half of the chapter, alone in her room, not sitting at the vanity, not sitting on the bed, opening the closet and forgetting why, running the water without putting her hands in it — that restlessness she can't name or complete is devastating in a very quiet way. "She had gotten very good, over twenty-one years, at the not-going; she had gotten so good at it that it no longer registered as an act, the way a held breath stops feeling held." That line got me. I trust her interiority completely here, and I ache for her in a way I did not expect to from the checkpoint's promise of a woman "steering" someone else into a plan.

Brooke is new to me this chapter and she is genuinely unsettling in the most social, mundane way — not a villain, just someone running a soft, precise interrogation dressed as friendliness, using her seatedness as authority ("the not-standing was the move"), landing "engineering, isn't it" as a status demotion and then walking it back as if by accident. I don't trust her an inch, though nothing she does is remotely a crime. It's the horror of a house where everyone is performing warmth at each other constantly and everyone knows it.

**Swoon / pull**

The swoon in The Bench was real and considerable — the slow undressing, the bench itself as object and metaphor, the edging, "good girl," the shudder — it worked on me physically and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But this chapter doesn't swoon at all, and I don't think it's supposed to; it's the comedown, and it's the better chapter for what this book is trying to be, in my read. The pull here is almost dread-adjacent — I wanted her to get up those stairs, I wanted Brooke to let her go, and when Brooke finally did release her ("Anyway, I'm sure you want to shower... Sorry to grill you. Go.") I felt the same relief Randi must have felt, which is a good sign the scene worked on me.

**Trust vs. suspicion**

The checkpoint told me this began "as a game" between two people about a girl who doesn't know there's a plan — that's marketing/jacket knowledge, not scene knowledge, and I'm holding it loosely as instructed. But I notice this chapter has made me suspicious of Randi's stability in a way the jacket didn't need to manufacture — the "hum in her ribs... the size of a key turning in a lock" from The Bench and now the flat affect of "it did not feel like a decision... it felt like there being no door" are the text itself earning my unease, not me importing it. Something in Randi is closing rather than opening, and I don't yet know if that's dread or self-protection or both.

**Erotic charge**

Zero in this chapter, deliberately — and that's fine, it's not what the chapter is for. It's the exhale after The Bench's peak, and it needed the quiet.

**Friction as a reader**

None that felt like a thumb on the scale. The social choreography of the common-room scene is very tightly written — I never once lost track of who was performing what for whom, which is impressive given four speaking women in a fast exchange.

**Titles**

"Standards" is doing a lot of quiet work — it's literally the name of Randi's board position (the perquisite that got her a single room), but it's also obviously pointing at the standard she's being held to by Brooke, the standard she holds herself to with the face in the mirror, the standard of performance that "worked on everyone... for years." It didn't give anything away before I read it, but it recolors nicely in hindsight — a title that's a pun I didn't catch until the chapter delivered it.

The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, and "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" feel newly loaded after this chapter — Randi said yes on that bench, wholly, and it's already curdling into something she can't hold. I'm watching for how that maps onto Vee, who the jacket says hasn't even received her invitation yet.

**What I want / expect / dread next**

I want to know what Randi does with this — whether she confides in anyone, whether "the plan" (whatever it is) starts to cost her before it costs Vee. Guess from here: I suspect the "game" mentioned in the jacket is between Randi and someone else regarding Vee, and this chapter is showing me the collateral damage on the person who thought she was running the game. I'm pulled forward mostly by wanting to see Randi's face when it isn't performing — which this chapter promised me is rare and possibly running out.
