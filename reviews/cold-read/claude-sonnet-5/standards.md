## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I'm still coming down off "The Bench" when this chapter opens, and that whiplash is doing a lot of work on me. That whole scene — the apple, the slow undressing, the bench itself, the spanking, the edging, the collapse — built and built with a patience that had me leaning in the whole time, and then it turned out the woman on it was Randi, not Vee. "There you are, Randi." That line landed on me like cold water, in a good way — it recast everything I'd been reading. I'd assumed (because of the jacket) that Pace and Vee were the couple being built toward, and instead I get this fully-realized, already-intimate, already-ritualized thing between Pace and Randi, complete with a safeword, a water bottle left in advance, a "tell me your word" — routine, tender, expert. That's a big reorientation and I liked being wrong-footed by it.

So "Standards" arrives as the morning-after, and it's a gut-punch of a different register entirely — quiet, social, claustrophobic. My feeling about Randi has moved enormously. In "The Bench" she was gorgeous and controlled and then finally, devastatingly, not controlled — begging in a voice she didn't recognize, crying without knowing she was crying, digging her thumbnail into the underside of a water bottle cap. That was already aching. But this chapter is where I really fell for her as a person and started to hurt for her, because now I get to watch what happens to a woman like that when she has to put the face back on and walk into a room full of people who love the face. The Brooke interrogation is beautifully awful — friendly, unhurried, absolutely surgical. "The not-standing was the move; Brooke was the chapter president and did not need to stand to stop someone in her own common room." I felt my stomach tighten reading that, the way you feel it watching someone corner an animal gently. Randi has to invent a study-group cover story on the fly, has to manage the reveal of Pace's name, his field, whether he's her professor — all while Sloane and Kinsey volley in with "short-cute" and "I love that you don't care about cars," oblivious, and Brooke clocks every hesitation without ever raising her voice.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't distrust Brooke exactly — nothing here reads as malicious, and the text is careful to keep her warm-voiced, genuinely curious-sounding. But the chapter earns my unease about her anyway, because it explicitly narrates the tactics under the warmth ("the guess landing him a notch down without seeming to reach for it," "a recalibration, barely there, the smile absorbing it without conceding the notch"). That's not me projecting a shadow onto a scene that worked fine straight — the prose itself is telling me this is a performance being read as a performance, by someone (Randi) who's fluent in the same language and is losing the exchange anyway. I don't suspect Pace of anything; if anything he deepened for me in the last chapter — patient, attentive, morally careful (the Daphne memory, the closed bathroom door he wouldn't open), genuinely moved by her. He's offstage here but his line about her, "he's hard to say no to," gets thrown back at Randi in her own head in "a different key," and that's a haunting little callback.

The real ache of this chapter is the ending — Randi alone in her room, unable to complete a single action (walks to the vanity, doesn't sit; walks to the closet, can't remember why she's there; runs water, doesn't put her hands in it), lying on her bed in last night's clothes with her shoes still on, unable to cry. "She had gotten very good, over twenty-one years, at the not-going; she had gotten so good at it that it no longer registered as an act, the way a held breath stops feeling held." That sentence wrecked me a little. It's not melodrama, it's just precise, and it made the whole chapter feel less like plot and more like watching someone's nervous system fail to process an event it doesn't have a category for.

Erotic charge: zero in this chapter, deliberately — it's all the comedown, the surveillance, the numbness. That's not a complaint; it's the right beat after the previous chapter's heat, and it makes the heat retroactively costlier.

Friction: none, really — I believed every beat of the Brooke scene, and the pacing (four minutes, mostly smiling) felt exactly true to how those interrogations actually go.

Titles: "Standards" is doing real double duty and I like it a lot — Randi sits on the sorority's actual Standards board, which the chapter tells me flatly ("she sat on the board herself, Standards"), so there's dry irony in her being the one enforcing appearances while faking her way through a cover story. But it's also just what she's been living by her whole life — the standard of the face, the standard of "perfect is safe," the standard that just got blown open on a bench in someone's bedroom. It didn't give anything away going in; it recolored retroactively, in a good way. As for the book/series titles — "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" — this chapter is making me feel the sinister edge of "polite" much more sharply than heat alone could: the politeness in that common room is the whole machinery of the trap, smiling and unhurried. I don't yet know what the "long spoon" is reaching toward, but "you need a long spoon to sup with the devil" is starting to hum under this.

What I want next: I want to know what Randi is going to do with this — whether she goes back to Pace, whether she tells anyone, whether Vee (mentioned in the jacket but not on the page yet in what I've read) enters this picture and what she'll mean once she does, now that I know Pace and Randi already have this whole private world. I'm worried for Randi and I'm rooting for her in a complicated way — not for a rescue, just to see her let herself have something without having to pay this exact toll for it every time.
