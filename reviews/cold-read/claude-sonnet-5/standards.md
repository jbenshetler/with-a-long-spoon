## Reader reaction

This is a gut-punch of a chapter, and it works entirely through what it doesn't show. I felt Randi's whole body-language of concealment before I understood why — "Randi closed it behind her quietly, which was already wrong. Randi did not close doors quietly" — and that one line told me everything about how tightly performed her normal self is, which recontextualizes the "face" from the previous chapter as not just a private defense against Pace but her entire operating system, all day, every day, in front of everyone.

Brooke is a fantastic and slightly frightening new addition — I don't trust her exactly, though nothing she does is villainous. The chapter earns real suspicion here, not manufactured: "the not-standing was the move," the casual social-class dig disguised as a compliment ("Engineering, isn't it," landing him "a notch down without seeming to reach for it"), the fishing about whether Pace teaches her section done under cover of friendliness. I felt Randi's math ("she sat on the board herself, Standards — and she knew exactly how much authority it took") as a woman clocking a superior performer of the exact game she herself plays, and losing. That's a cold, well-observed social scene — sorority interrogation as velvet-gloved surveillance — and it lands the anxiety physically: the widening fractions of a second, the laugh matched to Kinsey's pitch "before she had time to choose it."

Then the second half, alone in her room, is where the chapter really got me. It's almost unbearable in its restraint — no crying, just the itemized non-actions: doesn't sit at the vanity, doesn't sit on the bed, opens the closet and doesn't know what she came for, runs water and doesn't touch it. "It did not feel like a decision... It felt like there being no door." That line recasts everything from last chapter's bathroom-mirror resolve — this isn't willpower, it's dissociation so practiced it doesn't register as effort anymore. I ached for her here in a way I didn't expect to so soon after being unsettled by her retreat; the chapter turns her self-concealment from something I was wary of into something I grieve for her.

**Swoon/pull:** none romantic here — this chapter is Randi alone (and performing for her sisters), no Pace on the page. But there's a different kind of pull: I'm fully hooked into her interiority now, rooting for her almost parentally, wanting someone to actually reach her.

**Erotic charge:** zero, and rightly so — this is the comedown, the cost chapter.

**Friction:** none that bothered me — the sorority dialogue is sharp and precise, not overwritten. The one thing I noted without objecting to it: we get a lot of exact detail (eight steps, seven steps, palette bone-to-camel-to-oatmeal) that I read as characterizing Randi's own controlled attentiveness to surfaces, mirroring Pace's meticulousness from the last chapter — an interesting rhyme, maybe not accidental to me as a reader even though I can't know intent.

**Title:** "Standards" is doing double work I really like — it's literally her sorority board position (Standards board), but obviously also the whole architecture of her self-presentation, the standards she holds herself to and is now failing to meet even in private. Didn't give anything away going in; it recolors retroactively, which I liked a lot.

**Series/book titles:** Still resonant from before — "A Polite Invitation" and the SATC-adjacent world of manicured house dynamics feels newly relevant here, the whole sorority scene is one long "polite invitation" to confess dressed as friendly chat.

**What I want next:** I want to know if Vee is about to enter — this feels like it could be building toward her, maybe as another Standards sister, maybe adjacent to this house. I'm worried for Randi and want to see if anyone gets past the performance, including possibly a woman rather than Pace.

**Cast present (in person):** Randi, Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey (all physically on page, in the sorority common room and then Randi alone in her room). Pace mentioned only, offstage.

**Heat:** 0 — no erotic content, entirely aftermath/social-anxiety register.

**Romance:** 0 — this chapter is about Randi's isolation, not the bond with Pace, though it's shadowed by last chapter's intimacy.

**Motifs & images:** Doors/locks — recurs powerfully: the quiet click of the front door (wrong, first time), the loud click of her own door (right, normal), the vanity where the clutch "lives," the closet door opened and closed without action — all continuing the doors-as-control motif from the previous chapter, now generalized beyond Pace to her whole life. New image: water run but not touched, in the bathroom sink — an echo/inversion of Pace's water-bottle-left-out attentiveness, but here self-directed and empty, a ritual gesture drained of function.

**Symbolism:** The closet's disciplined bone-to-camel-to-oatmeal palette "with the one disciplined oxblood at the end" — reads like the same single-permitted-deviation logic as the one blue toenail from last chapter; her whole wardrobe is the face, externalized. The duvet described almost dismissively ("It was not — it was a duvet") feels like her own self-narration flatlining, refusing comfort even in language.

**Characterization:** Randi deepens significantly — we now see the performance is total, not just Pace-specific, and that she is skilled enough at it to nearly fool herself. Brooke is a strong new sketch: socially expert, faintly predatory in a status sense, warm on the surface. Sloane and Kinsey read as lighter, believable sorority texture — not deep yet but not flat either, just doing their function well.

**Pace within chapter:** The sorority scene is paced beautifully tight, real-time, four minutes that feel like an interrogation. The solitary room scene slows deliberately and earns every beat of stillness — nothing rushed.

**Pace chapter-to-chapter:** A hard tonal pivot from last chapter's heat and tenderness into pure aftermath and dread — momentum shifts from swoon to ache, which feels earned rather than deflating.
