## Reader reaction

Well, this one landed very differently from the bench chapter — no Pace at all, and the whole heat of last chapter has cooled into something almost forensic. I spent this entire chapter inside Randi's skull watching her perform, and it's brutal in a very quiet way.

**Randi** — my sympathy for her deepened hard here, and my unease about *her*, not about Pace, sharpened. The interrogation scene in the common room is agonizing to read because it's so recognizable — the "friendly" grilling from Brooke that isn't friendly at all, dressed as warmth ("I was about to be scandalized," "I don't even know why I went there. Sorry"). Every one of Randi's answers is a controlled release of exactly as much truth as necessary, timed to Brooke's rhythm, not her own. "Math," Randi said. "PhD." — clipped, defensive, giving nothing extra. I felt for her the way you feel for someone watching them navigate a minefield they've clearly walked before. And then the second half of the chapter — alone in her room, unable to sit at the vanity, unable to open the closet, running water she doesn't touch, lying down in last night's clothes with her shoes still on — that's the carry-forward from the bench chapter paying off exactly as promised: she decided never to let anyone see this, and now I'm watching the machinery of that decision operating on herself, in solitude, with no one to perform for. It's devastating precisely because it's so undramatic. "It did not feel like a decision... It felt like there being no door." That line got me — that's not repression as an act, it's repression so practiced it's stopped being an act at all.

**Brooke** — new to me this chapter, and she's rendered with real skill: a soft, warm surface that is actually surveillance. "Brooke had more of it than she did" (authority). I don't trust Brooke's warmth for a second, and I don't think I'm supposed to — the text earns that through the pacing of her questions (name, car, school, section — each one landing a small demotion or a small trap) far more than through anything stated. This isn't paranoia on my part; it's built into the scene structure.

**Pace** — absent, but present as the subject under interrogation, and interestingly, his absence lets me see how *contained* Randi is keeping him. He doesn't get to exist as himself here — he gets flattened into "Pace," "math," "not near my section," defended and minimized in the same breath.

**Swoon / pull** — none here, by design, I think — this chapter's pull is dread and ache, not heat. The pull is wanting Randi to have one place, one person, where she doesn't have to do this. Which of course throws real doubt back onto whether Pace's house — for all its tenderness in the last chapter — is actually that place, or just a different room to perform in.

**Erotic charge** — zero, correctly. This is the cold after the room's warmth, exactly the axis flip the last chapter's ledger anticipated.

**Friction** — none I can point to; the writing trusts silence and repetition (the walking to the vanity, not sitting; the closet, not opening it) rather than explaining Randi's state, which is exactly the discipline I want from this kind of book.

**Titles** — "Standards" is doing a lot of quiet work: it's literally her seat on the sorority board (Standards), but it's obviously also about the standard she's holding herself to, the standard of composure she can't let slip, and maybe the standards by which Brooke is silently grading Pace (and her). A title that gave something away — cleanly, precisely — once I got to the closet scene. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — I still don't have a strong read on these, but "polite" now resonates uncomfortably with Brooke's interrogation-as-hospitality.

**What I want next** — I want to know if Vee ever shows up, per the packet, and I'm newly worried about Randi rather than curious about her — I want to see whether anyone in this world actually gets to see her, or whether that's foreclosed now.
