## Reader reaction

Well, this one just quietly took my chest and squeezed it. No Pace at all this chapter — no heat, no scene of intimacy — but it's the direct, devastating aftermath of the last one, and it lands harder for the distance.

Watching Randi walk into that common room is like watching someone perform a very small, very high-stakes play with an audience that doesn't know it's watching a performance. Brooke is terrifying in the most banal way — that "can I ask" crease, the not-standing that is itself the exercise of authority, the "Pace? Engineering, isn't it" needle dressed as friendliness, the "I was about to be scandalized" line that's really a test disguised as relief. I don't trust Brooke exactly, but I don't think she's a villain either — she reads like someone who runs a small kingdom and is doing routine maintenance on it, sniffing for anything that doesn't fit the house's version of Randi. The line that got me was "Sloane was watching the exchange without watching it" — the whole room is a surveillance apparatus that everyone in it has agreed to pretend isn't one.

And Randi performing under that surveillance, after last night, is brutal to read precisely because I've just seen what she's protecting — or hiding. "Math," Randi said. "PhD." lands like a small refusal to let Brooke's condescension stick, the one flash of something like loyalty to him breaking through the mask — and then she immediately smooths it back over ("He's not — it's a different course. He's not anywhere near mine"). That gap between the flash of real feeling and the retreat into management is the whole chapter's engine.

Then the second half — alone in her room — is where the real ache is. This is some of the most exact writing about dissociation-as-habit I've read in this book so far. "It did not feel like a decision... It felt like there being no door." That line recontextualizes everything from the last chapter: the locked bathroom door wasn't new caution, it was one more layer over an already-doorless place. The controlled inventory of the room — closet, phone, sink water she doesn't touch, the duvet she pulls up while still wearing her shoes — reads as someone rehearsing self-soothing motions without being able to access the thing underneath them. I don't feel manipulated by this; I feel like the book has earned every beat of it, especially by contrast with how alive she was on Pace's page.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** I don't suspect Randi of anything toward Pace — my earlier unease wasn't about her intentions, it was about what she's doing to herself, and this chapter confirms that reading rather than complicating it. Brooke I hold at low simmering wariness — not hostile, but I don't trust what she'll do with "Pace... PhD... math" once she's finished filing it. That distrust is text-earned: the whole scene was staged as an interrogation with a smile.

**Erotic charge:** None, deliberately — heat 0. This is the cold-shower chapter, structurally and emotionally, and it works as a rest beat that's actually a gut-punch instead.

**Friction:** None that snagged me — the sorority-house social choreography (who sits, who doesn't stand, who laughs at what pitch) is rendered with real precision, not shorthand.

**Titles:** "Standards" is doing quiet double work — it's literally the name of Randi's board seat (which is why she has a private room at all — a detail that now reads sadder, since we've just watched what she does alone in it), and it's also, achingly, about the standard she holds herself to: composure, the "no door" she's built. A title that meant nothing going in and means a lot coming out — that's the good kind.

**What I want next:** I want Vee. She still hasn't appeared and the jacket says she's central — I'm getting impatient in the good way. I also want to know whether Brooke's questions go anywhere, or whether this was just atmosphere establishing the world Randi has to perform inside. Guess from here: Brooke becomes a pressure point later, maybe the person who eventually forces the secret into the open.
