## Reader reaction

Pace doesn't appear on the page here at all — this is Randi's chapter, alone, and it's a completely different register than the last one. Where "Standards" left me was somewhere quieter and more devastating than the bench scene, honestly. That chapter had heat and then a private cold underneath it; this chapter has no heat at all and is entirely about watching the machinery of the "face" running in real time, in daylight, among other women, and then breaking down once she's alone.

The Brooke interrogation is the best-written thing in the chapter for me — it's a masterclass in social violence disguised as warmth. "Brooke's voice was always warm. She had a voice the way other people had a perfume." That line told me everything about how dangerous Brooke is before she'd even asked a real question. And then the actual content of the questions — is he grad school, does he teach, is he in your section, math not engineering — each one a tiny status recalibration performed as friendly curiosity, and Randi tracking every micro-beat of it ("the fraction-of-a-second was longer," "Randi felt the room hold it") while performing fluency. I felt genuinely tense reading it, the specific tension of watching someone lie by omission under low-grade surveillance from people who love her. Sloane and Kinsey piling on with "he's cute" and "I love that you don't care about cars" — the room performing supportiveness while quietly building a file — was queasy in exactly the way it should be.

Then the second half, alone in her room, is almost unbearable in its stillness. The catalog of things she starts to do and doesn't finish — vanity, bed, closet, running water she doesn't touch — reads as a body trying to reenter its own routines and failing, over and over, in tiny increments. "She had come to the closet to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what." That's dissociation rendered physically, no narrator telling me the word for it, which I appreciated — the prose never diagnoses her, it just shows the stall.

The line that landed hardest: "*He's hard to say no to,* she had said, in the kitchen... *He's hard to say no to,* she heard, in her room, in a different key. / It was not a key she could sing in." That's the whole chapter's thesis in two sentences — the same words serving two entirely different functions, performance versus confession, and she can only access the performance register out loud.

And the ending — "the not-going did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door" — ties directly back into last chapter's actual locked door (the bathroom deadbolt) and makes it worse: last chapter she could still choose to lock something out; this chapter there's nothing to lock, because the door was never there to begin with, she trained herself out of even having one. That's a genuinely chilling escalation and it's earned by everything shown, not asserted.

Swoon/pull: none, and that's clearly correct for this chapter — it's the cost side of the ledger after last chapter's heat. I ache for her rather than being swept up.

Trust/suspicion: no new suspicion of Pace (he's absent), but my suspicion about whether Randi can actually sustain the "never again" resolve deepens — this chapter shows how total and practiced her self-concealment already is, which makes me wonder how she survives being genuinely wanted by anyone, Pace or otherwise.

Friction: none really — the pacing of the stalled actions in her room could have felt repetitive but instead accumulated correctly; I didn't feel an authorial thumb anywhere.

Titles: "Standards" is doing real work — it's literally her board seat/title (gives her the private single room), but it's also clearly about the standard of self-presentation she's enforcing on herself in real time, and possibly a dark echo of "standards" as in the bar she's set for what she'll allow herself to feel or show. Didn't give anything away, but recolors nicely in hindsight. The book/series titles still feel distant from this particular chapter — no showing up here of "invitation" or "long spoon" imagery that I caught.

What I want next: to see Vee finally, and to understand whether this concealment is sustainable or whether the book is setting up its collapse.
