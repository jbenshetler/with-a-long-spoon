# Cold read (grounded) — School Nights

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one's a comedown, and a welcome one — after the heat of "We Find Out" and "One Bite" and "Above Him," a short, quiet chapter that's just Vee and Cassie in a dorm room at eleven at night, and it lands like a held breath let out. I noticed my own body relax reading it. No Pace on the page, no Randi — just the one relationship in this book that has never once made me suspicious.

Cassie is, again, the person I trust completely, and this chapter reconfirms exactly why. "You're doing the streetlight thing again... You're a public utility." That's such a specific, funny, loving insult — she's needling Vee for glowing, and it's affectionate needling, the kind you can only do with someone you've loved a long time. And then the turn: "You don't come home school nights." Not a question. She's not accusing, she's not warning, she's just naming a fact Vee hadn't counted herself. I felt that little drop in my stomach along with Vee — "found her own bed had sat empty more nights than it hadn't" — because I know things Cassie doesn't (the whole architecture the checkpoint gave me), and watching someone so clear-eyed and grounded gently press on the edges of something she can only half-see is unbearably poignant. Cassie doesn't push. "I don't get it," she says — "Kindly, though" — and that kindness is doing a lot of work; she's not going to make Vee defend her happiness, but she's also not pretending not to notice.

"Do you love him" — asked "the same flat way she'd said the rest of it" — is the chapter's real center for me, and Vee's answer undoes me a little: "Maybe... I think so... It's all so fast." The girl who has been narrating explicit, escalating sex to Randi over mimosas, who told her body's whole truth in a bright glass room, goes small and inarticulate in front of her actual best friend. That gap — what she'll say to Randi versus what she can barely get out to Cassie — is the checkpoint's central worry made visible in miniature, without anyone having to point at it. Cassie doesn't say "I know" and doesn't say anything against it. She "let the maybe stand." That restraint from Cassie is its own kind of love, and it's the chapter's quiet ache: she sees something, she won't crowd it, and she goes back to her book.

I liked how the domestic detail (the tomato burn, the cold cloth, the fussing "like the small hurt was a big thing") gets relayed secondhand, filtered through Vee's telling — it's warmth reported rather than warmth witnessed, and that secondhandness itself made me feel the distance opening between Vee's real life (with Pace, at his house) and her nominal life (this dorm room, this roommate). Her hand going "to the spot as she told it... without her knowing it moved" is a lovely small physical tell — her body still narrating even in a totally unsexual retelling.

**Swoon/pull:** minimal by design — this chapter isn't reaching for swoon, it's reaching for something quieter and sadder, the ordinary erosion of a friendship's daily intimacy by a relationship taking over someone's nights. It worked on me that way. I didn't swoon; I ached a little, for Cassie mostly, watching her be the last honest mirror Vee has and get less and less of her.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** none of Cassie — full trust, as always. None newly of Pace or Randi here either, since they're offstage; my existing unease (the reader's foreknowledge of the game) sits in the background but isn't activated by this chapter's content. If anything the chapter deepens my worry *for* Vee rather than *about* Pace directly — I'm watching her drift out of the one unconditionally safe relationship she has.

**Erotic charge:** essentially none, and rightly so — the one physical beat (the tomato/burn) is played for tenderness and a little comedy, not heat. This is a rest chapter, structurally and tonally, and it reads as one on purpose.

**Friction:** none really — it's tight and does exactly what it sets out to do in under a page. If anything I wanted slightly more of Cassie's interiority (we only get her outside, her lines, never her thoughts), but that restraint is consistent with how she's been rendered all along — we know her only from what she says and how she looks, never from inside, and that's part of why she reads as solid ground.

**Title:** "School Nights" is quietly devastating once you sit with it — it sounds so ordinary, a phrase about homework and bedtimes, and then the chapter turns it into an absence: the nights Vee *isn't* home for school, sleeping instead at Pace's. It's a title that gives nothing away on its own and then, having read the chapter, feels exactly right — small, plain, and sad in a way that doesn't announce itself. Very much the innocuous-surface thing this book keeps doing with its titles.

The book's title/tagline aren't activated much by this chapter specifically, but "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" sits in my head anyway while I watch Vee not quite manage to say she loves him — her yeses keep outrunning her words.

**What I want next:** I want to know if Cassie ever gets more than "maybe" out of her — whether this quiet scene is the last uncomplicated real-talk they get before Vee edits her further, or whether it cracks something open. I have no plot information beyond this page; this is a guess from here, but the flatness of Cassie's "Do you love him" reads to me like a woman filing away a data point, not closing a conversation.
