# Cold read (grounded) — Another Round

*scene: scenes/another-round.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch050 + raw ch051..ch051 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I walked into this chapter still warm from the roadhouse — that ending swept me hard, the parking-lot kiss and the truck, all that unguarded desire — and then Pace says one name, "Sheri," and I felt exactly what Vee felt: the cold arriving unscheduled, "a train she'd been told was cancelled." I'd forgotten Sheri was a real, waiting landmine (my checkpoint had her filed as "the seed of Vee's later jealous fantasy" — I knew this was coming, structurally, but the chapter still made me flinch along with Vee) and I loved that the text let me feel the dread build with her rather than immediately correcting it. The invented blonde rival — "tall, gold, built on a fashion model's chassis" — was such a good bit of dramatic irony, because I *know* from the checkpoint that Vee built this woman out of her own insecurity in a scene I wasn't shown directly but was told about ("I built her on the nights I felt stupid"), and watching that fiction collapse the second the real Sheri stands up off the stool was one of the loveliest reversals in the book so far. Five foot nothing, two-toned hair, "a round face under too much makeup, a little off, worn like she'd dare you to mention it" — I laughed with Vee, that same helpless unvoted laugh, because the joke really is on her and it's a kind joke, not a cruel one.

Sheri herself is instantly one of my favorite people in the book. Blunt, loud, generous, calibrated under the noise ("there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when") — and her coming-out story landed like a gut-punch, unadorned and devastating: "Whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish." That she trusts Vee with it within one evening, unprompted, while pointedly not decorating the pain, made me trust *her* completely and immediately. And it recontextualizes Pace beautifully — not through anything he says, but through what she reports of him: driving her home without asking a question, showing up with donuts, fixing a faucet, never handing her "one false word." This is the same tenderness-in-action I've watched him give Vee for fifty chapters, but seeing it refracted through someone else's testimony, someone with nothing to gain from flattering him, made it land more convincingly than his own behavior sometimes does on me as a suspicious reader. I liked him more in this chapter than I have in a while — precisely because the checkpoint has me primed to distrust his "method," and here the method is aimed at someone who isn't being courted, isn't being seduced, just being *taken care of*. It reads as genuinely, structurally kind rather than technique.

And then his blush. "Pace, embarrassed" — I actually smiled at that line, because the checkpoint has trained me to see him as someone who never fully opens, and here's a crack that costs him nothing to have and everything for Vee to witness: "the single most disarming thing she'd watched him do." That's a real swoon beat, small and earned.

**Swoon/pull:** The whole taproom stretch — Sheri's roast of him ("what'd I tell you back in December, bet those legs feel like Christmas"), the pretzel line, the truck full of three people and a filthy sing-along — is just pure warmth, unguarded, and it worked on me completely. I wanted to be at that table. I wanted Sheri's friendship for Vee as much as Vee did.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion of Pace here — if anything, this chapter is a rare data point *against* the "method" reading I've been carrying, since Sheri's account of him has no erotic charge in it at all, just plain decency. My suspicion of him stays exactly where the checkpoint left it (the Randi secret, the refusal to name things) — nothing here touches that, and nothing here resolves it either. I have zero suspicion of Sheri; she reads as completely, refreshingly honest.

**Erotic charge:** Almost none in this chapter, and that's fine — it's a chapter about friendship and misplaced jealousy, not heat, and it doesn't force any. The only charge is the low hum of Vee's jealousy curdling into affection, which isn't erotic so much as emotionally intimate.

**Friction.** None that bothered me. The rhythm of Sheri "opening a folder and shutting it with no seam" when Pace returns is maybe a touch writerly-neat, doing it twice (once with the two-step, again at the goodnight), but it felt like characterization, not a thumb on the scale — that really is what a practiced, protective friend does.

**The titles.** "Another Round" — I read it first as literal (drinks at a bar), but it's clearly also "another round" of the same pattern from "Among Friends": another gathering where Pace is introduced to a piece of Vee's world (or here, Vee to a piece of his), another test of jealousy survived, another loop of the courtship ritual. It sits comfortably, not cleverly hidden — it does its job without straining. The book's title and tagline aren't touched directly by this chapter, but the closing image — "like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling" — rhymes uncomfortably with everything I already know he's carrying and not spilling (Randi). That line felt aimed at me specifically, the reader who knows more than Vee does.

**What I want next.** I want more Sheri — she feels like the book's one wholly safe adult, alongside Cassie, and I trust her instincts about people. I'm bracing (a guess from here) for the promised Saturday dance-hall scene, and mildly worried the text will use Sheri's warmth as more raw material for Vee's insecurity rather than resolution of it — though this chapter argued hard against that outcome. Mostly I just want to keep watching Pace be plainly good to someone with no agenda, because it's the version of him I like best and trust least to last.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Pace, Sheri. (Mentioned only: Randi, Cassie, Kayla, Meg, Danny, Theo, Sheri's family/church — offstage backstory.)

**Heat:** 0 — this chapter has no sexual content; it's entirely social/emotional (the previous chapter, "Among Friends," carried the heat).

**Romance:** 2 — not romantic in the Vee/Pace sense so much as relational tenderness: Pace's care for Sheri, the truck ride home, the arm around Vee's waist ("With him, she was coming to understand, those had never been two different things") reads as steady intimacy rather than a peak.

**Motifs & images:** His back at the sink as a listening device (repeat, from "Among Friends" and earlier chapters — "his back was a good listener"); the truck/county-road setting (repeat, site of the previous chapter's road scene and the September storm night); the headlights held on a door until the porch light comes on (new — small gesture, but rhymes with the established pattern of quiet, unannounced care — pre-warmed houses, stocked scrunchies); Pace's blush (first sighting — new information about him); the invented rival collapsing on contact with reality (echoes "the blonde" unresolved thread from the Rusty Nail, ch26 — this time explicitly acknowledged as invented by Vee herself).

**Symbolism:** The imagined blonde vs. the real, unglamorous Sheri reads as a direct rebuke to the "pricing look" Vee describes women giving each other — the chapter seems to be arguing (through Vee's own realization) that her jealousy machinery runs on a script that has nothing to do with actual people.

**Characterization:** Sheri deepens beautifully — moves from a name/rumor into a fully vivid, trustworthy person in one sitting. Pace deepens too, in a good direction — his blush and his history with Sheri complicate the "method" reading positively, showing capacity for plain, non-romantic devotion. Vee stays consistent — her jealousy-then-shame-then-affection arc tracks her established pattern (the Rusty Nail jealousy, the coat gesture) without contradiction.

**Pace — within chapter:** Comfortable, unhurried; the middle stretch (drinks accumulating, Sheri's stories) runs a little long but earns it — it's doing real character work, not padding.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate cooldown after "Among Friends'" high heat — holding rather than building momentum, but productively: it widens the emotional world right after narrowing hard into one truck-cab scene. Feels like a breath before whatever comes next.
