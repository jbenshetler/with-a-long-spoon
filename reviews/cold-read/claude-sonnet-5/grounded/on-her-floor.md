## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

God, I need a minute with this one.

I came in already primed for something charged — the checkpoint told me this kiss has been building for forty-some chapters, "the mouth that had been finding hers at the end of every brunch," months of goodbye-kisses that lingered a beat too long — but the way the chapter walks up to it is what got me. It doesn't open with tension. It opens with the *skirt*. Randi handing Vee a scrap of fabric — "It'll look better on you than it does on me anyway" — and Vee taking it because "being included was worth the risk." That line did something to me immediately: it's the whole book's engine in one clause. She knows the risk. She takes it anyway, because the belonging is worth more to her than the exposure costs her. I felt for her there — not pity exactly, more a kind of wince of recognition, watching someone hand over a little more of themselves for the warmth of being chosen.

Cassie is, again, the one clear-eyed voice in the room — "That hem's not going to stay put" — and again she's right, and again nobody listens to her, and I love her a little more for being consistently, unglamorously correct and getting nothing for it but a raised eyebrow from Vee. She's still the person in this book I trust without qualification.

The bracelet exchange stopped me cold — "the bracelet is called Love... you don't [take it off]. It's a real commitment. Taking it off requires tools." I don't know what to do with that yet. It's the first time the bracelet's been explained at all, and the explanation is somehow *more* unsettling than the mystery was — a commitment device, permanent, literally requiring tools to undo. I don't know if it's about Pace, about her family, about something else entirely. I'm just marking that it landed strange, deliberately strange.

Then the dance floor. I have to be honest about the charge of it, because it's enormous — Randi's hands opening Vee's arms, working her hips wider than she'd give herself, then the "your panties are showing," then the whole engineered "let's put on a show for Harrison" pretext collapsing into something neither of them is performing anymore. The prose here is doing exactly what the earlier chapters trained me to feel — the slow-motion suspension before the kiss ("the held inch became its own long event"), the taste of her ("salt and lime and under it Randi, particular"), Vee's own hand going into Randi's hair "without asking her." It's genuinely swoony and genuinely hot — heat 3 territory, sustained, not a single beat rushed — and I felt swept up in real time, the way Vee is swept up, wanting the kiss to keep going even as I also felt the undertow of everything the checkpoint told me Randi has been doing to her for months.

That undertow is the thing I can't set down. Randi's post-kiss line — "You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it" — is one of the cruelest small sentences in the book so far, not because it's aggressive but because of exactly how gentle and light it is. She hands Vee a lie to live inside immediately after taking something real from her, and does it with a "swat on the arm, light, fond." I know from the checkpoint that Randi is, by her own admission, in love with Vee and has decided never to tell her. Watching her get the kiss and then immediately, skillfully, retract the meaning of it — reframe genuine desire as a bit for a boy neither of them will remember — is the most quietly devastating thing in the chapter. Vee's body isn't fooled ("her mouth still had the taste of Randi on it, and her body had not been told") but her voice goes along with the fiction instantly, "in a voice that knew how to do this even now." That "even now" — even after that — is doing a lot of work. It suggests Vee has a whole practiced register for performing unbotheredness that predates tonight, maybe predates Randi entirely, going back to whatever taught her to manage her own wanting in public.

I don't feel angry at Randi exactly — I feel the ache the checkpoint promised me I'd feel, watching someone I like manipulate someone I like more, for reasons that are at least partly loving and partly self-protective and partly cruel, all three true at once. I don't trust the "show" framing for one second, and neither, underneath everything, does Vee's body.

**Swoon / pull:** Enormous, right at the kiss — the suspended-inch paragraph, the taste, the "I could not have found the part of herself that would have minded." I was there with her.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion of Randi's *intentions* — I already had it, in full, from the checkpoint. What's new is watching Randi actively deploy the "just a show" excuse on Vee in real time, which sharpens the ache from anticipated to witnessed. I trust Cassie completely, as ever.

**Erotic charge:** Peaks hard at the kiss — one of the most charged sustained passages I've read in this book, and it never goes clinical. The waxing chapter (recent, not this one) was more visceral/bodily-detailed; this one is more suspended and emotional, and both work.

**Friction:** Very little. Maybe the tiniest wobble at "she'd have drunk it down and never known" — a slightly over-explained beat about Randi's class-fluency vs. Vee's — but it's minor and consistent with established characterization, not a new tic.

**Titles:** *On Her Floor* — I don't fully land it. Could be literal (Vee's dorm floor, where this all starts), could gesture at being on someone's turf/territory, could be about the borrowed skirt "climbing" and things ending up on the floor of a party — it stays a little oblique to me even after finishing, not a giveaway, just quietly apt in several directions at once. The tagline's promise ("a woman waking to a man and, without quite letting herself know it, wanting the woman beside her") — this chapter is the moment that promise stops being subtext and becomes text. It feels like the hinge the whole jacket copy was pointing at.

**What I want next:** I want to know what Vee does with this — whether she lets herself examine it in daylight, whether she tells Cassie (I doubt it), whether she tells Pace (unlikely, but the dramatic irony there would be brutal since he already knows everything about Randi and Vee that Vee doesn't). Guess from here: I suspect Vee will do what she's done with every other unexamined want in this book — carry it forward without naming it, folding it into the general glow of being chosen, until it can't be folded anymore.
