FELT READ

Okay, this one landed on me differently than the others, and I want to be honest about exactly how. The chapter opens sweet and a little funny — Vee dazed with happiness, Randi's knee against hers under the desk, the professor's outlier lecture ("never all the way gone") ticking away in the background like a little bell I clocked but didn't want to lean on too hard. Then Vee does something new: for the first time in this whole run, *she's* the one reaching for Randi — two fingers on the wrist, "Walk me out? To Randi. Only to Randi" — and I felt a small thrill at that, watching the gravity finally pull the other direction, watching Vee want something and go get it instead of being drawn.

And then the practice room. God, that scene. I want to name what it did to me plainly: it worked on me erotically, immediately and completely, and it also made my stomach drop at the same time, in real time, sentence by sentence, in a way none of the earlier lunch-interrogation scenes quite managed. Because this time Randi doesn't just extract detail — she reframes it as she goes. "Did you deserve it?" is such a small, quiet, surgical question, and Vee answers it before she's caught up to what she's agreeing to — "Yeah. Probably I deserved it" — and I felt the floor tilt under both of us at once, reader and character. That's the moment the chapter stopped being charming and became something else.

Then Randi's own disclosure — the "mouse that roars" story, the over-the-knee, the counting, the hand that "slips" — is the most nakedly instructive thing she's done yet. She isn't just drawing Vee out anymore; she's handing her a script, in real time, for what just happened to her in Pace's kitchen, telling her what it means and what it's for, and doing it while watching Vee's body respond to the telling with an attention the text calls "nearly a pressure, nearly a hand." That line stopped me cold. It's the clearest moment yet of Randi doing something *to* Vee under cover of doing something *with* her, and I don't think it's incidental that it happens right after Vee finally reached first — like the pull got answered by being pulled somewhere Vee didn't fully choose.

And then Vee in the bathroom, catching her own hand before it can go where her body wants to go — "Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it" — that's such a precise, aching moment of a woman drawing a line she knows is already half-crossed. I ached for her there. It's the first time I've seen her name, even privately, that this is a yes she's refusing to give, which means she knows there's a yes to withhold.

How I feel about everyone: Vee — I love her more here than ever, actually, because she finally acted instead of just being acted upon, and then immediately got outmaneuvered by someone better at this than she is, and she half-knows it ("she couldn't tell whether she had"). Pace — this chapter barely features him directly except through Vee's retelling, but the retelling itself (the kitchen scene from the previous pages) reads warmer and more spontaneous than his usual deliberate courtliness — the tickling, the swat, him going still and watching her face — it felt like the first unplanned, unchoreographed erotic beat between them, and I liked him more for it, even as Randi immediately annexes it into her own narrative. Randi — my suspicion of her, which had been soft and diffuse before, sharpened into something almost clinical this chapter. "Sometimes it's the mouse that roars" as a pivot line, deployed exactly when Vee is most open, reads like technique. I don't fully trust that her arousal at watching Vee is separate from her intent to shape her — both can be true, and the text seems to want me holding both.

Swoon and unease are genuinely tangled together here rather than taking turns, which I think is new. The earlier lunches had a rhythm of warmth-then-a-cool-withdrawal; this one just kept building, warmth and manipulation as the same motion.

The titles: "The Practice Room" is quietly perfect and only lands fully once you're inside it — a literal room where music students rehearse alone, repurposed by Randi into somewhere private enough for exactly this kind of rehearsal, and the double meaning (Vee practicing being wanted, being told what she is, before she's ready to perform it for real) only clicked for me after the "those are where everybody used to..." line. It didn't spoil anything going in — it read as a throwaway campus detail until the room did what it did. Good title, felt earned rather than clever-for-its-own-sake.

I want to keep reading with a real edge of worry now, more than at any point since the checkpoint's Chapter 1 bench scene. I don't know what I expect next — a guess, nothing more: I think Randi's hold on Vee is about to become more explicit, that the "does he know she's here" doubling might collide with the "he" Randi describes belonging to someone else's story, and I'm bracing for Vee to find out that the man in Randi's confession isn't a stranger.
