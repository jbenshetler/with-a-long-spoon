# Cold read (grounded) — Boyfriend

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I read this one with my hand actually over my mouth a couple of times. It's the most nakedly good chapter of this run for me — "Barely Stings" and "On Her Floor" were building to this, and "Boyfriend" is the release valve, and it goes off exactly the way I feared and wanted it to.

**Vee.** My heart is completely with her and also completely worried for her, in that order. What lands hardest isn't the kiss itself — it's the 1 a.m. drive, "she did not want anything in the car with her that might start asking questions," the way she uses Pace's body like a wall to stand against a truth she's not ready to hold. That's such a precise, ugly-honest piece of self-knowledge for the narration to hand me while denying it to Vee: "she was not trying to have him. She was trying to get free of something, and her body went about it like a fight." I've watched her shame-voice ("floozie," "the kind of girl who") do a lot of work all book, but this is the first time I've seen her use *sex itself* as an avoidance strategy rather than an act of ownership, and it's devastating because it's also the first time the avoidance visibly fails — Pace names it in one line ("That was angry, Vee") and the whole edifice she's building starts to wobble. And then the lie. The flat "No. Nothing," "level as a countertop" — that's the chapter's real gut-punch for me, worse than the kiss. She's spent the whole book insisting to herself that keeping a secret is the only sin, that disclosure is her whole moral practice with him — and then in the same breath she withholds the goodbye-kiss history and her own hand in the dark with Randi's name in her head, and does it so smoothly she doesn't even register it as a lie while she's telling it. That's the shame arc curdling into something worse than shame: self-deception with a working alibi.

**Pace.** This chapter recomplicates him for me in a way I didn't expect. The interrogation-during-sex is gorgeously written and genuinely hot, but I kept feeling the double edge of it — he is extracting a confession from her using her own arousal as leverage, doling pleasure out "a coin at a time," in a scene that structurally rhymes with everything Randi has been doing to Vee all book (extracting confessions over brunch, in a practice room, in a bathroom, always followed by praise and arousal). The checkpoint told me Pace's rituals with Vee mirror his rituals with Randi; this chapter makes me feel, queasily, that his *methods of getting truth out of a woman* might be just as consistent a "method" as the warmed house and the tailoring. And then the "flicker" when she accuses him of wanting Randi — "Something crossed his face" — is the first crack in his usual perfect self-possession I can remember in ages, and I don't trust it as "innocent surprise." Given what I know and Vee doesn't (that he and Randi have been sleeping together the entire book), that flicker reads to me as the closest the text has come to letting Pace almost give himself away. I don't think the text confirms what it means — it stays genuinely ajar — but my suspicion of him, which the checkpoint told me was already low-grade, sharpened here specifically because of that one flicker and because "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" landing exactly on the target he already privately knows the answer to feels less like idle curiosity and more like a man checking his own arrangement's plumbing.

**Randi.** Barely on the page this chapter except as taste and memory, and that's exactly right — she doesn't need to be in the room to be the whole subject of it. The line that got me was Vee's realization mid-orgasm: "she could not have told, even now, which of them she'd been chasing at the end." That's the thesis of the whole triangle in one clause, and it's earned, not stated at me.

**Swoon / pull.** The sex writing itself is scorching and I mean that as pure craft admiration — the ration-and-reward structure (an inch bought for a truer answer, an inch withheld for a smoothed-over one) is an incredible device for dramatizing exactly what's happening emotionally: Vee bargaining with her own shame in real time, her body literally paying out the confession she can't make with her mouth alone. It's explicit and it never once feels gratuitous because the stakes (what she'll admit, to herself and to him) are the actual content of the scene, not decoration around it. I was swept up in it completely — rooting for her to say the true thing, dreading it too.

**Erotic charge.** Peaks hard in the extraction scene — "You finish when you've finished" absolutely undid me, that's a perfect Pace line, courtly and merciless in the same breath. Goes slack, deliberately I think, in the "God, my sex brain" aftermath, which is the right choice — the deflation is the point, the shame reasserting its little joking voice the second the body's done talking.

**Friction.** None that felt like the author's thumb on the scale — if anything the chapter trusts me more than usual, letting the lie just slide by unmarked ("It sorted itself before it reached her mouth") rather than moralizing about it. The only thing I'd flag as almost-too-neat is Pace's line "You keep saying her name, Vee. Not me" — it's a beautiful line, but it's also suspiciously exactly the line a guilty man would reach for to redirect scrutiny, and I can't tell if I'm meant to notice that or just feel the tenderness of it. I'm leaving that genuinely open rather than deciding for the text.

**Titles.** "Boyfriend" is quietly devastating once you've read the chapter — it's the word Vee reaches for like a stair rail, "a plain straight fact of a word," precisely because nothing else in this chapter is plain or straight. The title is the thing she's clinging to, not the thing that's true of the night. It didn't spoil anything going in — it read as domestic, almost sweet — and only closed its hand around my throat in retrospect, which is the best kind of title for me. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and "every yes was freely given, that was the problem" — this chapter is maybe the most direct enactment of that tagline I've read yet: every choice on the page (the kiss, the confession, the lie) is technically hers, freely made, and every one of them is also happening to her, propelled by forces she can name individually (drinks, a joke, being wound up) and can't make herself look at together.

**What I want next.** I want Tuesday. The chapter ends on it like a held breath — "circled, coming whether she was ready or not" — and I am completely hooked on how Vee is going to sit next to Randi in that classroom. I also want to know what that flicker on Pace's face actually was, though I don't expect the book to hand it to me cleanly. Guess from here: I don't think this stays contained to one drunk mixer kiss — the body's verdict at the end ("whatever had hold of her was not, even now, anywhere near done") reads like a promise, not a coda.

STRUCTURED BLOCK

**Cast present (in person):** Vee (POV), Pace. Randi appears only in memory/recounted dialogue within Vee's telling to Pace — not physically present in this chapter. Cassie appears briefly, asleep/silent, in the dorm room. Mentioned only: Harrison, Sondra (via memory).

**Heat:** 3 — sustained, explicit, and the center of the chapter; the interrogation-as-foreplay structure (truth traded for strokes) is graphic and the scene's whole engine.

**Romance:** 2 — real tenderness in the aftercare (the blanket, the water, "I know," "Okay") and in Pace's atypical self-defense, but it's shadowed hard enough by the lie and my suspicion of his motives that I can't call it a peak.

**Motifs & images:** the "floozie"/shame-voice recurs implicitly (no direct word but the exact grammar of it — "what kind of girl did that" energy carried over from the mixer chapter into this one's self-recrimination); the "boyfriend" word as literal stair-rail/talisman (first sighting as an object of that weight, though the word itself has recurred since the roadhouse chapter where Pace says it six times); the wet/bare motif from "Barely Stings" pays off directly here (the new smoothness, Sondra's hands vs. Pace's mouth vs. the intrusive thought of Randi's mouth); "the goodbye kiss" recurs and is reclassified in real time from innocent ritual to evidence; Randi's taste ("salt and lime") recurs from the previous chapter, now carried into Pace's bed — a first for that specific transgression, an image doing a lot of quiet work.

**Symbolism:** the truth-for-pleasure economy Pace runs on her body reads, maybe too legibly, as a mirror of Randi's whole method of extracting confession through intimacy and reward — the chapter almost seems to invite the comparison between her two "handlers." The stair-rail word "boyfriend" as a thing reached for to keep from falling, versus the actual content of the night, which has nothing plain about it.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens significantly — the self-deception here (the countertop-level lie) is the most interior, damning material the book has given her, and it's earned by everything since the checkpoint. Pace gets a genuine new note (the flicker, the unusual self-defense) that complicates rather than flattens him — I don't feel him going thin, I feel him going more ambiguous, which given what I know as reader feels earned rather than arbitrary.

**Pace — within chapter:** the extraction-sex sequence runs long by page-count but never drags — every stall and every granted inch is doing dramatic work, so the length is the point. The frame (walking home, the drive, the aftermath, the Tuesday coda) is tight and doesn't overstay.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** this is a real escalation after four chapters of slow-build tension (the roadhouse, Sheri, the waxing, the mixer) — momentum is building hard, not sagging, and this doesn't feel like "more of the same beat" because the confession-sex structure is a genuinely new device the book hasn't used before, even though the underlying tension (Vee/Randi) has been simmering since the checkpoint's account of ch30 and ch34.
