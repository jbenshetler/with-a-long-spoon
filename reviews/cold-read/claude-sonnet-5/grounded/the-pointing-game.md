# Cold read (grounded) — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this chapter still holding my breath from The Bench — Randi crying into the leather, locking a bathroom door against a man who'd only ever been gentle with her, deciding *not ever* he'll know the rest of her — and then Standards, that horrible bright four-minute interrogation dressed as sisterly chatter, Randi lying fluently and matching Kinsey's laugh a half-second before she'd chosen to. So I opened The Pointing Game already primed to watch her go on lying, to herself most of all. And that's exactly what I got, except the lying got recruited into something new and much colder-blooded: a game.

Pace opens the chapter making her come with his mouth, sliding into her still-pulsing and undefended, and then — mid-fuck — asking her if she'd like to taste another woman. That's a genuinely unsettling piece of craft-as-seduction: he's not asking her a question, he's *timing* a question to land on a body that can't yet defend itself. "The question reached her body before it reached her, and it took her all at once." I felt the manipulation before I could name it as manipulation — my stomach did something uneasy even as the prose kept insisting how much he *wanted this for her*, how he'd wait for her to be "gathered back into herself," how it mattered more to him that she get to choose than what she chose. I don't fully believe that generosity is separable from the control. He decided the timing. He decided the second, sober version of the question would come only once she'd already answered with her body twice.

Then the walk. This is where the chapter earned its title and its dread, in equal measure — the *pointing game* itself, Pace narrating strangers on a quad like items on a menu, Randi's whole prim social self ("the bald social wrongness of it, the exposure of being two people who pointed at a stranger") dissolving in real time into hunger. I felt her arousal as legible and — this is the thing — mine too, a little, guiltily: the writing makes the escalating "no... no... too expected... maybe..." into an actual seduction rhythm, and it worked on me the way it was clearly built to. The kiss with his own mouth still tasting of her, mid-game, "You dirty boy" landing as costume over real heat — that's erotic and it's also chilling, because I can see her building herself a persona to survive being caught wanting this at all.

And then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her head thrown back, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — and I felt the floor drop the way Randi's stride dropped. I already knew from the jacket that Randi steers Vee toward Pace "as if by accident." I did not expect to watch the actual originating moment: Pace pointing, and Randi saying *"Yes"* — bare, helpless, ahead of her own permission to want it — and then immediately reaching for the recovery, "Yeah. I like her," turning her own appetite into a gift for him before she's let herself feel it as hers. That's the same maneuver from The Bench, replayed at street level: she cannot let a want be simply hers, it has to become something she's offering someone else. Watching her orchestrate the "collision" with Vee — thirty feet of pure trained social choreography, made to look like chance, made to look like Vee bumped into *her* — is horrifying and admiring at once. I trust that Pace sees exactly what he's watching ("felt a deep warmth in his body at the sight of it") and I don't trust *him* an inch more for it.

**Character feelings, and what moved**

Pace has slid further from "generous, patient lover" toward something I'm now actively wary of. The Bench let me read his patience as tenderness with maybe an edge. This chapter reframes the patience as method — he is running an experiment on Randi's desire and calling the running of it love. "He had not known what the idea might stir in her" reads almost like a researcher's honesty, and it disturbed me more than it reassured me, because the affection is real *and* he is still, unmistakably, engineering her.

Randi I ache for and distrust in the same breath. The person who locked a bathroom door against being fully seen is the same person who, four minutes into a hallway conversation with her sorority sisters, performed fluent deflection without missing a beat. Now she performs desire itself as a gift so she never has to own it as appetite. I feel real tenderness for her — the "there being no door" image from the last chapter is still with me — and real unease about what she's about to do to Vee, sight unseen, before Vee even has a name.

Vee, appearing only in the last two pages, is dazzling and completely unsuspecting — the laugh, the freckles, "forgotten... that she could be seen." Knowing what I know from the jacket, that unselfconsciousness is unbearably poignant. I like her instantly and I'm afraid for her instantly, which the chapter clearly wants.

**Swoon / pull**

The walk itself pulled at me hard — the maple burning orange while everything else hasn't decided to change, the wind in Randi's hair, the whole valley-in-fall texture. It's gorgeous and it's doing real work, letting a genuinely dark game happen inside genuinely lovely weather so the horror stays soft-edged. And yes, I melted a little at "Go meet her" / "he let her see it" — the tenderness Pace shows her in the moment of permission is not fake, and that's exactly what makes the whole chapter so hard to simply condemn.

**Trust vs. suspicion**

Real suspicion now, earned by the page, not manufactured: I don't trust the frame Pace puts around his own motives ("that mattered more to him than the answer did"). I don't trust that "the game" stays a game. And I have a queasy new awareness that Vee — who doesn't appear in either of the recent chapters until she's laughing by the dining hall doors — is being selected, not met.

**Erotic charge**

Working hard early (the mouth-still-wet kiss, the edging-by-question) and working differently on the quad — a slower, tenser charge built from confession-by-flinch rather than touch. It goes almost unbearably taut right at "Her," and "Yes." That's the peak of the chapter for me, more than any of the bedroom heat.

**Titles**

"The Pointing Game" — exact and merciless once I've read it: I now know precisely what it names, and in hindsight it gave nothing away, it just waited to be filled. It reframes "A Polite Invitation" for me too — the invitation wasn't polite at all in its origin, it was pointed, literally, at a stranger's back.

**What I want next**

I want to see Vee's own chapter now — I've had two chapters of watching her be chosen from the outside and I want her interior at last, before this machinery closes around her.
