# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this chapter already braced — the run leading up to it (the porch confession he half-answered, the Chili's fight, the "what am I to you" scene, the sleepless nights with his shirt) had me genuinely worried for Vee, and this chapter is the emotional floor of all that: a girl crying alone in a rental car for forty minutes because a month of silence has convinced her she's been discarded. I felt for her immediately and completely. The image of her lying on top of her own made bed still in plane clothes, unable to look in the mirror, walking across campus because she "knew where she went when it got like this" — that's such an honest, specific picture of a person managing her own collapse. No performance in it.

**Randi** is the whole chapter, though, and my feelings about her did move — not toward more trust, toward more clarity about what I already half-suspected. Everything she does here is, on the surface, exactly what a devoted best friend should do: she drops her own unpacking, sweeps her new things off the bed without a glance, holds the hug a beat longer than it needs to be held, talks Vee down from her invented rival with real, careful logic ("That doesn't make her true. It means you can't stand how much you'd lose"). It's genuinely moving, and I don't think it's insincere — I think, per the checkpoint, her feelings for Vee are real. But the checkpoint also told me Randi's own relationship with Pace is a secret, established, ongoing thing — so when she walks Vee through reading Pace's silence ("That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him") with total, warm confidence, I felt the floor tilt a little under me as a reader. She knows things about that man's interior that a best friend giving generic comfort shouldn't know that precisely. And then the coat plan. "You take this into your own hands. You don't do it with words." She is engineering Vee's next move on Pace, staging her again, the way she staged the shoe-fitting and the dress and the apology after the bar fight — every single crisis in this relationship gets solved by Randi handing Vee a costume and a script. It reads as tender and it reads as a woman keeping her hand on the wheel of someone else's love life, and I don't think those two readings cancel each other. Both are true at once, and that's what unsettles me most about her by now — not that she's cold, but that she is never *not* directing, even at her most loving.

Then the ski chapter inside the chapter — Randi telling Vee about "John" — landed differently than I expected. I went in ready for pure escapist glamour and got something quieter and sadder: a whole seduction-fairytale (the lift line, the dry-cleaning bit, the sleigh, the marble ballroom) that ends not in triumph but in Randi lying awake next to a perfect, sleeping stranger feeling nothing, "the best the acceptable world could do for her," while she thinks about Pace and, unmistakably, about Vee ("Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of"). We get all of that in the recent-chapters material, not this chapter — but its ghost is all over this scene, because Vee doesn't get any of that interior truth. She gets the curated version — "it was perfect... everywhere but the bedroom" — delivered as a punchline, and I ached for the gap between what I know Randi actually felt in that hotel room and what she's willing to hand Vee. That's the dramatic irony this whole book runs on, working exactly as advertised, and it hurt in a genuinely productive way rather than feeling like a trick.

**Pace** barely appears (he's not on the page at all this chapter, only reconstructed through Vee's grief and Randi's readings of him), but the "Believe Me" and "The Usual" chapters just before this one did a lot of work on him for me — I trust that he loves her and genuinely cannot say the word, rather than that he's playing her. Sheri's blunt needling ("Then not saying it matters more to you than having her... that feels pretty shitty") did more to humanize him than anything he's said on the page; it also confirmed for me, a reader, that his silence isn't cruelty, it's a wall he doesn't know how to get down from — which makes Randi's confident diagnosis of him to Vee land as accurate and manipulative at once, which is an uncomfortable, effective combination.

**Swoon / pull.** The hug on the stairs got me — "Vee pulled a breath in, deep, off the warm skin of her neck... and it steadied her, and she pulled another, and didn't want to stop." That's real comfort, real desire, both at once, unlabeled, exactly the kind of thing this book does well. I wanted Vee to have that room, that door that shuts, that person who isn't going to make her explain herself. And even knowing what I know, I wanted Randi's coaching to work — I wanted Pace to open the door and have this be the thing that gets him to finally say it.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I don't trust Randi's motives are simple, but I've stopped being able to call it manufactured suspicion — the text is doing real work now, letting me watch her read Pace's psychology with an intimacy that no outside best friend could have, and letting me watch her withhold the one true thing about her own New Year's Eve (that it was empty, that she thought about Vee) while feeding Vee an edited, enviable version. That's earned, not paranoid.

**Erotic charge.** Almost none in this chapter directly — it's an emotional-repair chapter, not a heat chapter — except the coat plan itself, which is erotic by implication and by Vee's own shamed, aroused reaction to saying it out loud ("the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it"). That's a nice small charge, earned through her mother's voice intruding exactly on cue (the recurring "floozie" register, though not the word itself here).

**Friction.** None that bothered me. If anything the chapter is tightly controlled — Randi's manipulation is legible without the prose ever flagging it as manipulation, which I appreciated; nobody editorializes.

**Titles.** "My Friend Randi" is quietly devastating once you sit with it — it's Vee's own phrase for her (implied, never stated outright, but it's clearly how Vee would describe this relationship to anyone who asked), and the whole chapter is built on the gap between what "friend" is doing here and what the reader knows is actually happening. It doesn't give anything away going in, but it recolors everything once you're through it — a title that means "this is what she calls it," offered with a straight face, letting the irony do the rest. The book title *A Polite Invitation* and tagline about every yes being freely given feel closer than ever here — Randi is, quite literally, constructing an invitation Vee will hand-deliver herself, believing it's entirely her own idea.

**What I want next.** I want to see what happens at Pace's door — and I'm nervous for Vee in a way I wasn't a few chapters ago, not because I doubt his feelings but because I now can't stop seeing Randi's fingerprints on the moment before it even happens. Guess from here: I think the coat scene will work, emotionally, exactly as Randi predicted — but I suspect the cost of Randi being right so precisely, so often, is going to become visible to Vee eventually, and I'm bracing for that reckoning more than I'm bracing for anything between Vee and Pace.

Momentum holds, doesn't sag — if anything it's coiling toward whatever happens at Pace's door next.
