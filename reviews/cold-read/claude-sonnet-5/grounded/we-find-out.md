## Reader reaction

FELT READ

Okay — that title. "We Find Out." I clocked it before I even got a full paragraph in, and it's doing double duty in a way I only see once I've finished: on the surface it's Pace's line, the bet, "we find out" whether she enjoys it. But given everything the checkpoint has armed me with about Randi and Pace's game and what Vee doesn't know, it also reads like the book teasing me with a question it isn't actually going to answer this chapter — we don't find out anything about the game, only about Vee's body. That's a small, cold little joke sitting under a very warm scene, and I don't think it's an accident that the title is exactly the phrase inside the bet.

Vee, first. I love her in this chapter, more than I have in a while — the text she second-guesses ("she felt it, the nakedness of having asked"), the thing where she's never been the one to reach first, the being early, the sitting-in-the-car contingency plan she doesn't need. That's such a precise, small piece of shame-adjacent vulnerability, and it's not sexual shame, it's just wanting-shame, which felt true and grounding before the chapter gets loud. Then the tickle fight, the shorts theft, the flash — "she had never in her life done such a thing" — I bought every beat of her getting braver in real time, delighted by her own daring and half-shocked at herself. That's the Vee I've been rooting for since the checkpoint: hungry, funny, alive, finally let off the leash a little. The moment she notices she made him flinch — "she had just watched it flinch" — landed for me as a genuinely good character beat: she's cataloguing, for the first time, evidence that this wall of a man is porous to her specifically. That's earned tenderness, not manufactured.

Pace: this is the chapter where the checkpoint's dual reading of him is most acute for a reader who's been carrying it. Everything he does here — the slow hand up the thigh with his eyes locked on hers so she has "nowhere to put her own," the refusal to let her rush undressing, the "no theatre, no counting" gentleness that subverts what I (and Vee) expected from Randi's earlier story — reads on its own terms as exquisitely attentive, scrupulously consensual (he stops when she says don't, he waits for her nod, he narrates none of it as coercion, he checks and re-checks), and genuinely hot. And it is hot — this is by far the most erotic writing in what I've read of this book, unhurried and physically specific without turning clinical. But I can't read that slow hand rising up her thigh without also hearing "the pointing game," without hearing Randi in bed telling Pace "let her get there on her own." He is, right now, on the page, doing exactly what he told Randi he'd do — and I don't think Vee has any idea that this exact patience is a technique she's watched him deploy from day one, not a spontaneous response to her specifically. That's the discomfort the checkpoint trained into me, and this chapter is the sharpest test of it yet, because the scene is so tender that it's genuinely hard to hold both readings at once. I did hold them, though — the juice, the hairbrush plenty of scrunchies "more than a person could go through quickly," the curry — all of that reads as real, unhurried devotion, and I don't want to accuse it of being fake. But I also can't unknow that this is a man managing two women in parallel, one of whom doesn't know the other exists.

Randi doesn't appear in person here, but she's all over the chapter anyway — "Randi's story rose up in her like something she'd swallowed," Vee bracing for counting and theatre because of what Randi told her in the practice room, and then getting something quieter and more real instead. That's a lovely, quietly devastating touch: Vee unconsciously auditioning her own sex life against Randi's narrated one, using Randi as the emotional script for what she expects from Pace. It deepens my unease about Randi's storytelling — was that story calibrated to plant exactly this anticipation in Vee? I don't know, and the text doesn't say, but the juxtaposition invited the thought.

Swoon / pull: it's real. The bit that got me most was the aftermath — "he stroked her hair off her face, slow, over and over, and told her she was wonderful... until the word and his hand in her hair were the same thing" — that's just lovely, tender writing, and the "for the first time she could remember, treasured" line hit exactly where the checkpoint has been aiming me: a starving woman finally fed. I felt the swoon and let myself have it. The morning-after bathroom monologue ("My Victorian LARPing... does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky") is genuinely funny and humanizing, a good release valve after the intensity.

Erotic charge: peaks hard in the "we find out" sequence — the finger, the withdrawal, "the wet on his finger... like it was true. Like it was fine." That whole passage of him refusing to be disgusted by what she's ashamed of is the erotic and emotional center of the chapter, and it works on both registers at once. It goes slack, appropriately, into domestic warmth by the curry — a good landing, not a letdown, just a different key.

Friction: none that felt like the author's thumb on the scale — the pacing of the seduction (the standoff over "do you admit you lost," the slowness insisted on as "part of the bet") felt controlled and intentional rather than manipulative-feeling on the sentence level, even though I distrust the man doing the controlling. The line "he had never once seen anything she did land on him" doing double work (character beat + irony about her ignorance of his real permeability, which is Randi) felt a little on-the-nose in retrospect but not intrusive in the moment.

Book title / series title: "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" both keep humming under this chapter — the whole scene is an invitation issued and re-issued ("Do you admit you lost?"), consent checked and re-checked even inside a game with loaded stakes, and I keep thinking about the "long spoon" idiom (supping with the devil) every time Pace is this gentle. Nothing overtly diabolical happens here — quite the opposite, it's the gentlest chapter yet — but the title sits at my shoulder reminding me the warmth might cost something I can't see yet.

What I want next: I want to see whether the juice-and-curry domesticity holds, whether Randi resurfaces and what she's told (or not told) about this weekend, and whether Vee's noticing-Pace's-flinch becomes a thread — her starting to read him instead of just being read by him. Guess from here: I suspect the "we find out" title is going to keep echoing — maybe toward Vee eventually finding out about Randi, not just about her own body. That's pure speculation, not signaled directly, but the title primed it for me.
