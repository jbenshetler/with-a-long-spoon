## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still glowing a little from "The Induction" — that quiet, private triumph of Vee giving the room her leg and keeping the secret for herself — and this one takes that same charge and lets it go all the way. It opens so warmly: her texting first for once ("she had never once been the one to reach first"), the fifteen-minute wait that "sat there long enough that she felt it, the nakedness of having asked." That's such a small, exact detail of what it costs someone who's spent her whole life rationing herself to be the one who wants out loud. I liked her instantly again for it.

The horseplay section is genuinely fun to read — rare in this book, which tends to run on tension and careful choreography. The shorts-stealing, the flashing, her realizing she's faster than him — "quick and light, up on the balls of her feet in the heels, able to cut and reverse while all that mass of him had to gather and come around like a ship" — is playful, physical, funny, and it's Vee's body working *for* her for once, not against her. And then the one true, small, striking beat in the middle of the play: she pokes him and "the smallest thing, a tightening at the corner of his eyes, there and gone, but she caught it... she had just watched it flinch." That line did something to me. Through this whole book Pace has been described (by the checkpoint's memory and by the prose itself) as a wall, immovable, always the one setting the terms. Here for one half-second she gets to be the one who lands a blow. It's such a tiny thing and it mattered enormously to her — and to me as a reader, because it's the first crack of ordinary vulnerability I've seen in him that isn't performed or offered on his terms.

Then the game turns, and it turns exactly the way this book always turns things — consent as ritual, a bet instead of a command, "either you admit you lost, or we find out." I want to be honest about my body's response here: it worked on me. The slow hand up the thigh, the "she could have stepped out of it the whole way and didn't," the drawn-out reveal of her own wetness and her shame doubling on itself — it's explicit and it's patient and it never loses the thread of what her mind is doing underneath the arousal. That's the thing this book keeps doing well: it doesn't let the heat replace the interiority, it runs them in the same sentence. "The ease went through her worse than any sting had. Her body had decided without her." That's not just hot, it's *true* to who she's been built as — a woman terrified of her own appetite finding out, again, that the appetite was always there first.

The consent architecture is doing real work for me as a reader who cares about it. The bet is silly and games-y on its surface, but under it Pace keeps checking: he waits for the nod, he watches her face, he stops to let her set the pace of undressing even while insisting on "slow." It's coercive in form (a rigged bet she can't really refuse and still keep face) and tender in execution, and I notice I'm not bothered by that combination the way I might be — because the book has spent this many chapters establishing exactly how she wants to be pursued past her own denial, and because he keeps reading her, not overriding her. The line I keep returning to: "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness, anywhere." That's gorgeous and it's earned — it names precisely what makes this safe for her, the total absence of anyone she has to perform for.

Where it goes actually tender, and where I felt the deepest pull of the whole chapter, is the aftercare. The juice. "He hadn't planned any of what came next, she could tell, and that was the thing that undid her." Him lifting her head into his lap, stroking her hair, saying "wonderful" over and over until it and his hand become the same thing — I melted there, genuinely. And then the wonderful comic-domestic coda: the scrunchies with no note, "My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky," using his antiperspirant and getting his smell instead of her own. It's funny and human and grounds all that intensity back into two people who like each other. The curry-making closes it out sweetly, ordinary and full.

And yet — because I'm carrying the checkpoint's memory with me — I can't read the scar moment innocently. "Her palm passed over the old scar at the top of his inner thigh... the one she had found that first night and traced and never been told about. She didn't ask now either." I know things she doesn't: that this is the same man who is, per what I remember, still secretly partnered with Randi, that "may I choose" is a line he's used before, that this exact bedroom ritual — the quiet, the no-counting, the palm resting before it strikes — sits right next to my memory of the bench scene and Randi's own story in the practice room. Vee doesn't know she's re-walking ground Randi has already described to her almost point for point. That dramatic irony sits under the whole scene like a low hum I can't turn off, even while the scene itself is beautiful and consensual and warm on its own terms. It doesn't sour the tenderness — I don't distrust what's happening in the room — but it keeps me from being able to feel this as pure or private the way Vee does. "No week behind her, no stats class, no mother's voice, no Sunday, no Randi to be told" — that line lands differently for a reader who's been told things Vee hasn't.

TITLES

"We Find Out" is the bet's own phrase, lifted straight into the chapter title — on its face it's just about whether she "enjoys this." But sitting where I sit, with the checkpoint's whole architecture of secrets behind me, the title can't help but gesture wider: the book's entire tension is about who finds out what, and when, and this chapter's "finding out" is deliberately the small, safe, erotic kind — her own body's truth revealed to herself and to Pace — set against everything the reader already knows that Vee has not yet found out. It's a title that means exactly what it says on the page and also, for me, can't help but flicker toward the larger unresolved question hanging over the whole book. I don't think it's spoiling anything new, but it does sharpen the ache of what I already know she doesn't.

The book's tagline about "warm, wickedly explicit, and psychologically merciless in its tenderness" feels precisely right for this chapter — it is both those things at once, sincerely, in the same scene, without contradiction. Nothing here dreads yet. I just keep carrying my private knowledge like a stone in my pocket while she doesn't have one.

WANT/EXPECT NEXT

I want more of this ordinary domestic warmth — the curry, the scrunchies, the juice — because it's some of the most purely likable, unguarded writing about the two of them so far. And I'm braced, the way I've been for chapters now, for the moment the practice-room story and this bedroom start to rhyme too loudly for Vee not to notice, or for Randi to walk into a scene where Vee is glowing this obviously. No prediction stronger than that — just the same low-grade dread the checkpoint left me with, undiminished and unconfirmed by anything actually on this page.
