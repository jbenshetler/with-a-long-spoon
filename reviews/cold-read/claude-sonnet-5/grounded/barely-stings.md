## Reader reaction

Cast present, felt first: this whole chapter is Vee alone in a room with a stranger's hands on her, and it lands with more unease than anything since the "wrong hand" language I remember from earlier in the book turned literal on the page. Pace isn't here except as a memory of three words — "Will you?" — and Randi isn't here except as a memory of a lie of omission at a café table. And yet both of them are all over this chapter, more present in their absence than most in-person scenes manage with both of them in the room.

**How I feel about each character right now.** Pace, remembered here, is exactly the Pace I already have — "I'd like you waxed... Will you?" — the request dressed as a question, the courtesy that both is and isn't a courtesy, because the answer was never really in doubt and he knows it ("Yes," she'd said, "out ahead of her, her mouth answering before her mind had caught up to it"). I don't dislike him for it — he's consistent, not villainous — but this chapter makes the mechanism of his asking visible in a new, colder light: the "yes" harvested before the information that would let her actually weigh it. Randi, absent in body, does something worse to my trust than anything she's done in person. She sold Vee "barely stings," a glass of prosecco, "you'll walk out of there feeling like the most expensive thing in the room" — and left out, entirely, that there's a second half to the service, the part where a stranger says "roll over" like it's obvious. That omission reads to me as consistent with everything the checkpoint told me about Randi curating what Vee gets to know in advance — softening, editing, always one road ahead and never quite handing over the map. I don't think this chapter proves malice. But it does nothing to relieve my suspicion of her either.

**Swoon / pull.** The ending genuinely got me — alone in the room, hand on her own bare skin, "she was not thinking about her own hand at all. She was thinking about his." That ache for Pace's hand specifically, "the right hand, the one she'd kept everything back for," is tender and hot at once, exactly the kind of interior-desire writing this book does best: wanting rendered as anticipation, not performance. I felt that one in my chest.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** The line that did the most work on me was the "false floor" — Sondra saying "That's the hard part behind you," Vee believing it, relief flooding in, and then the second half arriving anyway. That structure (relief granted, then revoked, dressed as if it had been agreed to all along) is not subtle as metaphor for the whole book's "every yes was freely given" premise, but it's earned here because it's happening to her body in real time, not just argued at me. I don't distrust Vee — I ache for her small, real courage ("no joke was quick enough for this... what was left underneath with nothing over it was just that, just the plain animal size of it"). I do carry forward more wariness of Randi's editing than I had going in.

**Erotic charge.** It's working, but in a genuinely uncomfortable register — pain, exposure, and arousal braided together rather than heat played straight. "The wrong hand... held her the exact way she wanted to be held and it did not want her at all" is the peak for me: a real, felt violation of the grammar of care she's learned from Pace, without anyone actually doing anything wrong. That's sharp, specific writing about desire and its displaced objects, and it never tips into gratuitous — the chapter earns every beat of discomfort it asks me to sit through.

**Friction.** None that felt like the author's thumb on the scale — the pacing through the actual physical procedure (strip by strip, the "different country" of the last part) is patient without dragging, and the "kind of girl who" intrusion is placed exactly where the checkpoint's motif would predict, not overused.

**The titles.** "Barely Stings" is Randi's own line, lifted verbatim, and by the end it reads as almost cruelly ironic — not because the wax stings worse than promised (it does, but that's the least of it), but because the phrase is doing the same work Randi always does: minimizing what's coming so Vee walks in without her guard up. Once I'd read the chapter, the title didn't just illuminate it, it indicted the person who said it. As for the book's title and tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is nearly a thesis demonstration of that line: a yes given to a question that withheld half its own content, a body that keeps saying yes anyway because the alternative is a line she no longer believes she has left to draw. It's not subtle, but it didn't feel like a lecture either — it stayed inside Vee's skin the whole time.

**What I want next.** I want the weekend scene — Pace's hand finding her like this — and I'm genuinely anticipating tenderness there rather than dreading it, because nothing here indicts him as cruel, just as a man whose asking has a blind spot he doesn't examine. I'd also like, eventually, for Vee to notice what Randi left out — not necessarily confront her, just notice — because right now the pattern of Randi editing Vee's expectations keeps compounding without Vee ever catching it, and I want to see if the book lets her catch it even once. Guess from here, not knowledge: I suspect this chapter is quietly building toward Vee starting to feel the edges of what's been shaped around her, even if she can't yet name it as anyone's doing.
