## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into "Grace" still holding the kiss at the mixer in my chest — Vee and Randi, tongues and hands and that shattering "you really did a great job putting on a show," Vee's body still ringing with it days later — and this chapter simply doesn't go there. It's just Vee and Pace, alone at a cabin, for the whole span, and at first that felt like a held breath, almost a relief after the crowded, escalating chapters before it (the roadhouse, Sheri, the wax appointment, the mixer, the gym injury). By the end I understood the held breath was the point: this is an idyll, deliberately walled off from everything complicating it.

**Pace** — I go on liking him in the room and distrusting the room he's building. The cabin, the thermos of tikka masala carried up a mountain, the box he made with his own hands to hold a key — it's extravagant, patient, exactly his signature, and it lands on me exactly as it's meant to land on Vee: warm all the way through. But two things needled me. First, the tiny "shadow" that crosses his face when *she* describes *him* in the mirror — brown eyes, the birthmark by his ear — gone before she can name it, and then he immediately pivots the whole scene onto cataloguing her instead and never lets himself be looked at again. He spends the entire chapter making her radically, unbearably seen, and stays, himself, entirely unseen. That's consistent with everything the checkpoint told me about him — the walled interior, the refusal to be known — but seeing it dramatized this precisely, right at the hinge of an otherwise worshipful scene, sharpened my unease rather than softened it. Second: when she finally asks (with her body) for relief and he simply doesn't move — makes her wait past teasing, past comfort, until she has to touch herself in front of him instead — I felt the care and the control as the same gesture, which is very much his pattern (the fitting, "keep still," the burn he lets happen and then won't let her spend), but here it's turned toward something closer to surveillance dressed as intimacy, and the chapter's very last beat confirms it: "Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?" That is a request to be given continuous access to her most private self, asked in the same "may I" register as everything else he's ever asked her — and she says yes "easy and breathy and barely attended at all, the way you say yes to something small." Given what I already know and Vee doesn't — that Randi has spent the whole book extracting exactly this kind of confession and apparently delivering it to him in bed — that line did something cold to me even inside all that warmth.

**Vee** — this is the chapter where her body-shame arc pays off most completely and most beautifully. Every part he names, she braces for the verdict ("here it comes," she thinks, waiting for "too much"), and every time what arrives instead is reverence. The moment that undid me was the blindfolded masturbation — not the mechanics of it, but the psychology: "you can no more spank yourself than tickle yourself... it had needed him," and then the collapse of the word into pure sound, "you, you, you," coming with his name that isn't even his name. That's a genuinely moving image of a woman finally inhabiting her own wanting instead of performing around it. I ached for her there, unreservedly. And then, one breath later, she gives away the right to keep that wanting private, and does it so lightly she barely notices she's done it — which is exactly the book's stated bargain (every yes freely given) doing its quiet, terrible work in real time.

**Swoon / pull** — enormous, and I want to be honest that it worked on me almost completely while I was inside it. The cider-and-tikka-masala pairing ("those should not be friends" / "it does. It really does") is a lovely small image of things that shouldn't fit together fitting anyway — which, given what I know about this relationship's structure, reads as almost unbearably apt without the text ever pointing at it. The box-and-key is a genuine swoon: "You're more than a guest in my house. I've never given a key to anyone" — and then, immediately, the text does its own quiet work on me: "he had said *house*... The other one didn't come." I felt the ache of that with her.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — nothing on the page is a lie, exactly, and I don't distrust his tenderness in the room — it's real, moment to moment, the way it's always been real. What I distrust is the shape of the whole evening: an elaborate, engineered exposure presented to her as spontaneous discovery, ending in a request that turns her most private self into something reportable to him. The text earned that suspicion for me — it's not paranoia, it's pattern-matching against everything the book has already shown me about how confession gets extracted and where it goes.

**Erotic charge** — very high, and sustained almost the whole chapter without going slack; the blindfold monologue is the peak, both technically (the escalating, unhurried naming) and emotionally (it's doing real work on her shame, not just describing bodies). The only place it thinned slightly for me was the long middle stretch — hair, back, legs, waist — which runs a beat repetitive in structure (name, brace, be surprised it isn't cruel) before the vulva/masturbation sequence pulls it back into real tension.

**Friction** — very little. If anything, the chapter's total silence about the mixer kiss felt conspicuous to me as a reader — not a flaw exactly, more a held breath I kept waiting to hear addressed and didn't. That may simply be where the story is; I'm noting the absence, not accusing the text of anything.

**Titles** — "Grace" fits so well it almost aches: unearned favor, being looked at and forgiven rather than judged, and also the literal grace of movement he keeps naming in her. But by the end I also heard it as faintly ironic — grace as something *given*, entirely on his terms, in a scene where he controls exactly when relief arrives and what gets reported afterward. The book's tagline, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," has never sat closer to the surface of a chapter than it does here — the final "yes," given almost unconsciously mid-arousal, is the tagline made flesh. And *With a Long Spoon* feels newly apt too: this is the most purely indulgent, most feast-like chapter yet, and I found myself wanting the spoon to be longer than Vee's is.

**What I want next** — I want to see whether that final promise (reporting her private desire to him) ever gets paid off, and whether it connects, even obliquely, to what Randi already extracts from Vee — that would be devastating if the two channels ever converge on the page. I also want the mixer kiss to come back; it can't just have evaporated. Guess from here, marked as a guess: I suspect the unaddressed kiss is going to detonate later, precisely because this chapter let it go quiet.
