# Cold read (grounded) — Substitution

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I read this one with my stomach in a knot the whole time, because I already know what Vee doesn't. Two chapters ago Pace stood on a lawn pointing at strangers and Randi picked her — picked Vee — like a gift she was handing him, and now here's the "meeting cute" scene, played completely straight from Vee's side, and I can't unsee the seam. Every warm, disarming thing Pace does in this coffee shop I'm reading twice: once as the tender, exacting man I watched be patient and careful with Randi on the bench, and once as a performance I watched him decide to give. That doubling is the whole experience of the chapter for me.

**Vee** — I like her enormously and I ache for her already. The detail that got me was her reaching for Randi's "V.T., yes like the university" bit mid-introduction — she's *already* imitating Randi, has tucked away her friend's social moves to try them on, and it works, and she feels the "borrowed poise" sit on her better than she expected. That's such an exact, small humiliation-that-isn't-yet-a-humiliation: she doesn't know she's rehearsing to become more legible to the very people who are auditioning her. Her econ monologue, the "what's drawing you to it" question landing like nobody's ever really asked her anything — I felt real tenderness for her hunger to be listened to. She's not naive in a stupid way, she's naive in the ordinary, forgivable way of someone who has never had reason to suspect a kindness.

**Pace** — this is where my trust actually complicates. In The Bench he was patient and attentive in a way that read, to me, as genuinely caring even inside control. Here the same patience — the tutor's restraint, handing her back the pencil instead of solving it for her, the read of "the path that was a tutor's path and not a mathematician's" — is *the same move* rendered as courtship technique, deployed on a stranger he was pointed at like a target. I don't distrust that he'll be tender with her. I distrust that tenderness now, because I've watched him choose it as a strategy with Randi's blessing. The line "*Do you mind?*" when he reaches for her page — courtly, careful — used to read to me as respect. Now it reads as a well-practiced instrument.

**Randi** — the chapter's quiet gut-punch is in her hands. "Randi's hand was around her coffee cup... Twice the cup came up to her mouth and went back down, and no sip happened." Vee reads it as boredom. I don't buy that reading, and I don't think the text wants me to either — it left it genuinely open, which I respect, but my body read it as something closer to jealousy, or grief, or the cost of watching her own gift get received. This is the same woman who, two chapters ago, locked a bathroom door and decided "not ever" — and now she's sitting across from the man who found her, handing him another woman to be with. I don't know what she's feeling and the book doesn't tell me, and that not-knowing is doing real work on me.

**Cassie** — the one person on the page not performing anything, and I trust her completely. "He looked at her — the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." That's devastating and funny and precise, and it's the only unclouded voice in the room. Her flat "Goodbye, Vee" a chapter back already unsettled me before I knew why; now, watching this scene play out exactly as staged, it reads almost like Cassie already knows something is closing.

**Swoon / pull** — genuinely, yes, it worked on me. The Euler's-formula bit is lovely — "on their own these two are a struggle... put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain" — a real, unforced way to be charmed by a person's mind, and it doubles almost too neatly as commentary on what's actually happening at that table (two separate people's wants, combined, made suddenly legible). The "may I call you" landed on me the way it landed on Vee — genuinely disarming, old-world, not affected. I felt the swoon and the dread at the same time, which I think is exactly what the chapter wants from me, and I'm grateful it didn't try to resolve the tension for me either way.

**Erotic charge** — low and clothed here, which is the right choice after the Bench's heat — this chapter runs on anticipation, not skin. The peak is bodily but not sexual: "her chest was doing what her chest sometimes did when she had taken stairs too fast." That restraint made the earlier explicit chapter hit harder in retrospect, and made this one land as tender ache rather than heat.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — this is the first chapter where I have real, text-earned suspicion, not manufactured — I watched the plan get made, I'm watching it executed, and the gap between what Vee believes is happening (a chance meeting, a lucky notice) and what I know is happening (a chosen substitution) is the entire engine of my discomfort. I don't suspect anyone of cruelty exactly — Pace and Randi both seem to want good things for Vee, even inside the manipulation — but I don't trust that "good" and "honest" are the same thing here, and the book hasn't reassured me they are.

**Friction** — none that felt like the author's thumb on the scale. Cassie's lines are almost suspiciously sharp, but she's been established as exactly this blunt, so I bought it.

**The titles** — "Substitution" is doing a lot of quiet work: it's the calculus technique Pace teaches her (turn the hard thing into a thing you already know), but it's also, obviously, what's happening to Vee herself — she is being substituted into a role Randi picked out for her on a lawn, standing in for whatever "her" meant to the two of them. It didn't give the plot away outright, but on the page it recolored the whole scene the moment I registered it — a title that means two things at once, and the second meaning is the one that stings. *A Polite Invitation* feels exactly right for this chapter in particular — Pace's "may I call you," his courtesy, his "do you mind" — all of it *is* an invitation, and it is polite, and that's precisely the problem the jacket already promised: "every yes was freely given." I'm watching the first yes get given right now, and I know more than she does about what it's a yes to.

**What I want next** — I want to stay with Vee's POV as this develops, because I think that's where the ache lives — watching her fall for something real while I already know its provenance. I'm dreading, more than anything, the moment she finds out — if she ever does — and I'm curious whether Randi's unreadable hand-on-the-cup moment gets any clearer, or stays exactly this ajar. Guess from here: I expect the "see you later" pattern (Randi's earlier line to Vee) to echo again once Pace calls her.
