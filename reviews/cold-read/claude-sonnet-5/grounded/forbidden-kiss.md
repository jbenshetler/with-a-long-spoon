## Reader reaction

Okay, this chapter is basically two halves — a long, glowing morning of pure indulgence, then a small crack in the glass right at the end that I can't stop turning over.

**How I feel about everyone right now.** Pace is at his most purely lovable here — the burnt toast, the "I got mushrooms," the composure "reassembled piece by piece like a man picking up dropped change." That's the best kind of swoon: a man who is normally unbreakable getting visibly undone by his own girlfriend standing naked in his kitchen. I liked him uncomplicatedly for most of this chapter, more than I have in a while, actually — the bread timed to her waking, the sweats set out and not commented on, the fruit "opened... like the orange," the whole architecture of care. And then he goes down on her and it turns out he genuinely *likes* tasting her on his own mouth, keeps going back for it even when she flinches — and my checkpoint-trained wariness of him woke right back up. Not because liking it is wrong — it isn't, it's lovely — but because of how he handles the "no" that follows: reads it precisely (draws back the instant her legs don't want him to, reads "which no was which"), doesn't push, doesn't explain, doesn't kiss her mouth again for the rest of the scene. That last part is the one that unsettles me. It reads as considerate on its face — respecting her limit — but it's also exactly the kind of silent, competent management everyone in this book has been doing to Vee since chapter 3. He absorbed information about her and adjusted his behavior without a word, and she never gets to ask him about it, because she never even lets herself register that he *withheld* the kiss on purpose until it's already receding into a "he must have forgotten."

Vee herself is glorious in this chapter — funny, bawdy, fully in her body, doing the "princess" bit with real wit ("Some kept woman... It was the kind a man keeps *in* pleasure") and I loved that turn, loved her cataloguing his care as evidence rather than swooning passively. This is Vee at her most agentic and most delighted, and it's a real pleasure to read after the ache of December/January in the checkpoint. But the last two pages pull the rug slightly: her instant, generous self-narration machine kicks in exactly the way it always has with Pace — "he must have forgotten to wipe" — smoothing over a thing her body is visibly still troubled by (the tongue going out looking for something, finding nothing, "under the affection... minor-key, waiting"). That's the chapter's real ache for me: not that anything bad happened, but that Vee's habit of resolving her own doubt before she's finished having it is so ingrained she doesn't even notice herself doing it anymore.

**Swoon.** The whole breakfast sequence is just wonderful — the blood oranges "opened... nothing between anybody and the fruit," the persimmon salt, the burnt toast twice, the apron strap callback to the very first cooking scene. This is the tenderness axis of the book at full boil and I was completely swept along. The dominance-play in the bedroom (the pointing, "bad slave," her ruling from the cashmere) is playful and hot and — crucially — she's driving it, which after a book of being steered by other people's plans felt like a genuine gift to give her here.

**Erotic charge.** Very high, sustained, and it earns its length — the alternating-thigh teasing, the breath-only moment, the "he was not quiet about it," her orgasm with her toenails in the air. It's graphic without going clinical; the prose stays inside her sensation ("small lit windows going dark one street at a time") rather than mechanically inventorying acts.

**Friction.** None that reads as authorial thumb-on-scale — if anything the ambiguity of the ending (was it forgetting, or was it deliberate withholding, or is it nothing at all and I'm the one being paranoid after fifty chapters of dramatic irony training) feels earned rather than manufactured. I do notice my own trained suspicion firing on a moment that might be completely innocent — the checkpoint has taught me to read management into everything Pace does, and I want to be honest that the text itself doesn't force that reading; it offers Vee's own comfortable explanation right alongside the unease and lets both sit there.

**Title.** "Forbidden Kiss" — I read this as almost a joke title until the final pages, when it clicked: not a forbidden kiss between two people, but the moment her own mouth reaches for a taste "before any part of her had ruled on it," curious and unforbidden, and then gets slapped down by her mother's installed voice. The "forbidden" isn't erotic transgression between them so much as the shame-voice reasserting itself over her own spontaneous wanting — which is exactly this book's oldest motif (the floozie voice) landing on a brand-new, very specific target. It recolors the whole chapter nicely in retrospect — the entire glowing morning was building toward one small moment where her body says yes ahead of her permission, and she takes the yes back.

**What I want next.** I want to see whether that "minor-key, waiting" thread actually surfaces again — whether she tells Randi "nearly everything" and what "nearly" turns out to mean, and whether Randi (who reliably mirrors Vee's experiences back to her, per the checkpoint) has anything to say that lands close to home here. Guess from here: I suspect this small unresolved thing is a seed, not a throwaway — the prose spent real care making sure I noticed it lingering under the warmth rather than dissolving with the rest of the afterglow.
