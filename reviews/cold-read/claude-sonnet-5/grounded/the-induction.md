## Reader reaction

Felt read

This one is just Vee, alone with herself, for the whole chapter — and it's the best kind of alone-with-herself I've gotten from her yet. No Randi managing her, no Pace's hands on her, just Vee walking a stage in a dress with nothing under it and deciding, mid-stride, to let people see it. I loved it. I felt the swoon come up in my own chest on "She took a breath, and made the second step long." That's the whole book in one gesture — the choice to be seen, made a half-second before her head catches up to her body, the exact move the checkpoint told me to watch for (the "eyes ask, may I look" motif) except this time she's the one asking the room. It's her choice, hers alone, and it lands as pure triumph rather than something extracted from her. That distinction mattered to me — I've spent this whole book watching Randi and Pace draw desire out of Vee, and here for once nobody drew anything out of her. She reached in and took it herself.

Cassie is, as always, the person I trust completely on the page, and she gets the best moment of the chapter for me — "Go make your mother cry again," taking the phone, "You look—" and not finishing it. That non-finished sentence did more work on me than a paragraph of description would have. Cassie's line "Honor student," dry, with her eyes gone bright at the corners "which she'd deny to the grave," is exactly the kind of unshowy devotion that makes me trust her as the emotional ballast of this whole story. I felt real affection here, maybe the most uncomplicated warmth I've had for anyone in the book.

Pace is offstage entirely, and I noticed I didn't miss him with any anxiety — his absence here reads as genuinely regretful and unmanufactured, not a hidden agenda for once. "He'd been sorry about it in the quiet way he was sorry about things, no performance in it." I have no reason to disbelieve that; it's a small, ordinary logistics collision (a conference) rather than anything sinister. What got me was the detail that he made the dress for exactly this moment and won't see it worn for its purpose — and that Vee sends him the photo anyway, "the whole frame, uncropped," knowing "he would have no idea what else was in the frame. That part was hers." That's a lovely, quietly possessive gesture — she's giving him the image but keeping the interior experience for herself. It's the first time I've felt her hold something back from him rather than the reverse, and I liked her for it.

Randi doesn't appear in the room, but she's all over Vee's body — the heels she found and buckled, the toenails, "the frosted plum that woke and threw the light." I registered that with the low hum of unease the checkpoint gave me permission to carry: Randi dressed her for this, is invisibly present in every choice Vee makes on that stage, and Vee doesn't notice it as anything but a friend's generosity. I don't distrust Randi in this particular chapter — nothing here reads as manipulation, it reads as care — but I noticed the residue of my larger suspicion sitting quietly under the surface, unconfirmed and untriggered by anything on this page specifically.

Swoon and heat: the walk itself is the erotic center of the chapter, and it's not explicit sex at all — it's a woman choosing exposure in public, controlling exactly how much of herself to give a room of strangers, and her body answering "low and certain" a half-beat before her mind agrees. That's a different register of eroticism than the book's given me before, and it worked on me completely. The image of her sitting back down with "her hand at her hip again, the slit shut, decorous, a girl with a certificate" — the private joke restored, the room folding it away — gave me a little thrill of complicity, like I was the only other person who knew.

The one place I felt the prose lean a bit hard on itself: "whether that was pride or only the same heat still running, or whether, by now, the two came to the same thing" — a lovely closing thought, but it's doing exactly the kind of interior-naming the book usually earns through image rather than statement, and here it tips slightly toward telling me what to feel rather than trusting the walk itself to say it. Small thing, didn't break the chapter for me, but I noticed the seam.

Title: "The Induction" does the innocuous-institutional-surface thing I've come to expect from this book's titles — on its face it's just the honor-society ceremony, but it's clearly also about Vee inducting herself into something: a self who chooses visibility, who "gives the room her leg on purpose," who doesn't make herself small. It recolors nothing on a second look so much as it confirms the whole chapter is a graduation of sorts — not into the society, but into her own appetite. It didn't spoil anything for me; it clicked into place after the fact, which is the good kind of title.

Book title / tagline: I don't have a tagline line separate from the blurb's closing beat here, so I'm holding "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" against this chapter, and this chapter is the cleanest example yet of a yes that really was hers — no third party engineering it, just Vee and a stage and a decision. It makes me trust the swoon completely in this one instance while the larger dramatic irony (the game she doesn't know about) sits untouched in the background, waiting.

What I want next: I want to know what happens when Pace actually sees this photo, and whether he says anything about it, or just keeps it. I also find myself wanting a Randi-Vee scene again — the checkpoint left me watching for whether Vee's attraction to Randi becomes conscious, and this chapter, oddly, advanced that thread by omission: Randi's fingerprints are all over Vee's body in this scene and Vee doesn't seem to register that at all. That gap interests me. No dread yet, just pull.
