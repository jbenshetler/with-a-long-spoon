# Cold read — Gone

*scene: scenes/gone.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: sorority*

## Reader reaction

This one knocked the wind out of me a little, and not in the way I expected.

We're in bed with Pace and Randi — actually on the page together for the first time in my reading, after four chapters of this relationship being a known fact rather than a witnessed one. And the first thing that happens is Randi retells the bigfoot-shoe story I just watched happen, in Vee's chapter, as pillow talk. "The full bigfoot." I laughed at it in Vee's chapter and I laughed at it again here, and that doubling is exactly what got me — the same joy, but now it's currency between the two people who aren't supposed to both have her. Randi's fondness for Vee reads as completely real here too — "She got past me before I could help it," "nobody does both, she does both" — which makes it worse, not better. This isn't cynicism performing warmth. It's warmth that happens to be shared with the person Vee doesn't know Randi shares a bed with.

Then it turns colder without raising its voice. "She's not ready, though... You'll want to take your time." That's not affection, that's *pacing advice*. Randi coaching Pace on managing Vee's readiness, in the tone of two people discussing a shared project. And Pace's answer — "I want her to get there on her own" — sounds tender in isolation, but sitting inside this conversation it reads like patience-as-technique, not patience-as-restraint. I don't know which it is. The chapter didn't resolve that for me and I don't think it wanted to.

The line that actually chilled me was Pace's, flat and certain: "She's ashamed... Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body." No elaboration, no invitation to ask who. It's the first real crack toward Vee's unexplained past — parents "almost mentioned" before — and it lands as fact, not guess, in his mouth. And Randi's reply — "And you're showing her she doesn't have to be. That's why she's so gone over you." — turns his tenderness into a stated method in real time. I don't think either of them means it cruelly. That's what unsettles me.

The moment I trust most in the whole chapter is the one the narration doesn't explain: Pace describing Vee lighting up a room, and Randi going still — "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that costs something to hold." Then she covers it with sex. That's the first time I've seen something in Randi that isn't legible as warmth or as performance — something that might be jealousy, might be longing, might be both, and the page just... leaves it there. I respect that it didn't chase it down for me.

Erotically, this works — unhurried, the "criminal, what she keeps under there" moment where Randi performs her own desire for Vee's body on Pace's body is genuinely charged, and it also does real character work: it's Randi voicing want-for-Vee she can't fully hold apart from her want-for-Pace, delivered *to* Pace. That's new and it's the clearest data point yet on the unresolved kiss.

Friction: none I'd call a miss — the scene trusts silence (Pace not taking up "the rest of what she'd set down") more than most of the book has so far, and I liked being trusted with it.

Title — "Gone." Randi says Vee "got past me before I could help it," and later that Vee is "so gone over" Pace. Both readings sit in the word at once — Randi's own guard gone, and Vee already gone under Pace's hands, whether or not Vee knows it. It also just describes what this chapter does to the last one: the happy day is *gone*, recolored, the moment I read this.

I want the next chapter to be Vee's again — I need to be back inside her not-knowing after sitting this long inside what she doesn't know.
