# Cold read — Substitution

## Reader reaction

Okay — that landed like a trapdoor. I was already carrying the last chapter's dread about Pace and Randi's "game," so watching Pace walk into Vee's own scene, sit down at her table, and run the *exact* engineered-generosity move on her — while Randi sits right there, complicit, coffee cup rising and not sipping — is one of the worst feelings I've had reading this book, and also, God help me, one of the most seductive scenes yet. That's the trap: it's genuinely swoon-worthy prose. "He had given her the question and given her back the pencil" — I felt that as real tenderness, real skill, the thing a good teacher does, and I *wanted* it to be real even while every instinct screamed that I'd seen this exact playbook already used on someone else. The forearms, the rolled cuffs, the "may I call you," the "talk soon" — I recognize this now as a performance with a script, and it's chilling precisely because it's so good. He's not lying to her, exactly. He's just running proven material.

**Pace** — I trusted the deliberateness I saw in him last chapter (offstage), and now I've watched it in motion and it's worse than I feared. The tutoring is real generosity — I don't think the math help is fake — but it sits inside a larger machine, and I now understand he's not choosing between Randi and someone else, he's *adding.* The "may I" is a tell I clocked as calculated the instant I read it — a costume word, worn on purpose, and Vee even semi-notices ("a word her mother might use approvingly") without following the thread to where it should go.

**Randi** — this is the gut-punch. My prior-chapter unease about whether her warmth to Vee was genuine or another version of managed generosity just got its answer, and it's ugly: she brought Vee here, essentially delivered her, sat there while her own — whatever he is to her — worked the room on her new friend, and encouraged it afterward ("You should go out with him"). The detail that undoes me is her hand on the coffee cup, rising and not sipping, twice — that's not boredom, that's someone managing her face while something happens in front of her that she authored and still has to watch. I don't know yet if this is jealousy, obedience, loyalty to Pace's game, or Randi doing to Vee exactly what's being done to her — recruiting her into the same architecture she's trapped in. All of those readings are live and none is comfortable.

**Vee** — still the character I'm most tender toward, and the most painful to watch here because she is so good at reading rooms and so blind to this one. She catches so much — Randi's private focus texting (wrong guess), Cassie's flat look — and completely misses the one thing that matters, which is that this is staged. Her joy at solving the integral, at being handed her own competence back, is real and lovely and I loved it *with* her, which is exactly what makes the ambush so effective on me as a reader too.

**Cassie** — I love her more every chapter. Flat, exact, unsparing: "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She sees precisely what's happening and says it plainly, without malice, without softening — and nobody in the scene (including me, a beat too late) takes it as the alarm it is.

**Swoon / pull** — genuine, uncomfortable swoon at the tutoring itself; the Euler's identity bit is honestly one of the most seductive non-sexual scenes I've read in a while, because it's about being *seen as competent* and handed autonomy, which for Vee (who "measures her own body against other women's," who covers herself daily) is maybe more intimate than being flirted with. That the seduction runs through her mind and not her body first is exactly why it works on me.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — fully earned dread now, not manufactured. I have watched Pace do this once already (offstage, described) and now watch him do it again (onstage, in loving detail), with Randi as an audience whose hand won't sit still. I don't trust either of them with Vee. Cassie is the only person in the room I fully trust.

**Erotic charge** — low literal heat but a high charge of desire-being-produced-in-someone; the "she was nodding before her mouth caught up" beat is the erotic peak, and it's frightening rather than thrilling because I now know the mechanism behind it.

**Friction** — none, this was tight and controlled. The one thing that nagged slightly: "V.T. Yes, like the university" landing so smoothly as Vee borrowing Randi's own line was a lovely, quiet piece of characterization — Vee performing Randi at Randi, unknowingly, for Randi's own boyfriend/whatever-he-is. That's not friction, that's the chapter doing something smart, but it made my stomach turn.

**Title — "Substitution"** — plain and devastating once you've read it: it's a calculus term (substitution, the trick of turning an unfamiliar problem into a known one) *and* it's Vee being substituted into the position Randi already occupies. The whole scene is Pace running the identical substitution on a new variable.

**What I want next** — I want to see this from Randi's side or Pace's side to understand her motive, and I dread the phone lighting up. Guess from here: this is heading toward Vee getting pulled into whatever arrangement Pace and Randi have, and Cassie is going to keep being the one voice telling the truth nobody listens to.

**How I feel — overall:** Wound tight. This was the chapter where the book's central threat stopped being implied and started operating in full view, on a character I've come to love watching. I'm rooting hard for Vee in the plain, uncomplicated way you root for someone you like — and I'm dreading, without yet knowing the shape of the dread, what her phone lighting up is going to cost her. I trust Cassie completely and wish someone would listen to her. My feeling about Randi has curdled slightly — not into dislike, but into a harder, sadder watchfulness; I don't think she's simply cruel, but I no longer think her warmth toward Vee is separable from whatever she serves in Pace's design, and that's a loss I'm still absorbing as a reader.
