## Reader reaction

Something's shifted underneath me and I'm having to fight my own memory to read this scene straight. I *know* — the reader-state told me — that Pace is Randi's, that this is engineered, that Vee is a target being delivered. And yet the chapter plays it as a clean, disarming meet-cute, complete with the *may I* and the forgotten coffee and Randi's warm-eyed "he was cute, you should go out with him." That collision is the whole experience of the chapter for me: watching Vee fall, in real time, for exactly the machine I've already watched built.

Pace here is genuinely good — his handing back the pencil at "the exact line where she could solve it herself," the restraint of not solving it for her, is lovely craft and lovely characterization simultaneously. It's the tutor instinct that isn't showing off. I like him in the scene. I also can't stop hearing it as a demonstration piece — the same deliberate withholding-to-produce-desire that's already on record as his signature move with Randi, now deployed on a total stranger with the same clinical precision. The forearms, the "may I," the exact right amount of listening — none of it reads as accidental once you've seen how he operates. My affection for him and my distrust of him are running at the same time and neither one is winning.

Randi is the chapter's quiet marvel and its most upsetting figure. "You should go out with him" — said to the person she and Pace apparently arranged this whole afternoon around — landed like a small blade. Her hand on the coffee cup, twice raised and set down with no sip taken, read to me as the tell of someone managing her own face through something she isn't naming — jealousy, complicity, guilt, arousal, I genuinely don't know which, and I don't think the page tells me either. That's the ache of this chapter: Randi performing generosity for a friend while orchestrating that friend's capture, and doing it so well I can't locate where the performance ends and something real might begin.

And then Cassie — reintroduced, sharper here than her one scene in the last chapter. Her flat reporting voice ("That cute guy in line is looking at you"), her later needling ("He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt") reads to me as pure, uncomplicated honesty — the one voice at the table not managing anyone. I trust Cassie completely, which makes her unwittingly telling Vee the exact truth about Pace's attention (it was never for Randi) devastating, because Vee can't hear it as the warning it functionally is.

Swoon and dread are fully co-present for me. The math scene *worked* — I felt the little thrill of *e^ix*, the click of being handed the key instead of the answer, Vee's private elation. That's real seduction craft and I responded to it as a reader exactly as Vee responded as a character, which is uncomfortable in exactly the way I think it's meant to be. The heat here is almost entirely non-sexual — it's attention-as-desire, being chosen, "she felt, absurdly, chosen" — which for me is the most dangerous kind because it doesn't announce itself as wanting.

Friction: none that felt like a thumb on the scale — the dramatic irony is doing all the work honestly, letting me feel smarter and sicker than Vee without the prose winking at me.

Title: "Substitution" is doing at least double work for me — it's the math technique (turning the unsolvable into something you already know), and it's also, queasily, what's happening to Vee: she is a substitution for something else Pace and Randi already had running, plugged into a problem to make it solvable. I didn't catch the second meaning until I'd finished the chapter, and now it recolors the whole thing.

I want to keep reading badly — I want to know if Randi's warmth toward Vee is going to crack, whether Cassie says more, whether Vee ever gets close enough to the truth to flinch.
