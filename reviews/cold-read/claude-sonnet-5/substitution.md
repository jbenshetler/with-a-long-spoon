## Reader reaction

Okay. This chapter is doing something quietly devastating, and I want to sit with it before I say anything clever about it.

**The characters, where I stand with each of them right now**

Pace I trust in the sense that his hands are gentle and his attention is total — and I no longer trust that at all, in the sense that matters. After "The Bench" and "The Pointing Game," I know exactly what "may I call you" costs. I watched him build a piece of furniture designed to open a woman all the way and never once break his own stillness doing it; I watched him point at strangers on a quad like a man auditioning cattle for someone he loves; and now I watch him cross a coffee shop with his eyes locked on Vee's face the whole way and think, with real dread, *he has done this walk before.* Except — has he? The chapter doesn't tell me this is staged. It reads, on its own surface, exactly like a man being struck by a stranger in a coffee shop. It's the *jacket copy* and the *last three chapters* doing the work of suspicion, not this scene. I have to be honest about that: within these pages, Pace is unbearably good at math tutoring and unbearably good at listening to a twenty-year-old talk about econometrics for five minutes like she's the most interesting person he's met all week. If I didn't know what I know, I'd be swooning clean. I do know what I know, so I'm swooning *and* sick, at the same time, which is a genuinely uncomfortable place for a reader to be and I think that's exactly where I'm meant to be.

Vee is enormously easy to love right here — the "not looking at the door," the borrowed Randi-line about V.T. that she can't quite own ("suddenly wanting her own name back"), the private elation of solving the integral, the *he gave her back the pencil* insight that she has and then can't quite let herself finish having. She is so smart and so hungry to be seen being smart, and Pace hands her exactly that, precisely engineered or not. My heart goes out to her completely. She is not stupid — she catches nuance (the tutor's-path-not-a-mathematician's-path observation is genuinely sharp) — she is just radically undefended against a particular kind of attention, and this chapter is the attention arriving.

Randi is the character who moved on me hardest this chapter, and it's the most quietly awful writing in the packet. The cup that comes up to her mouth twice and goes back down "and no sip happened." That's it. That's the whole tell. She is watching the man she is — what, in love with? owned by? terrified of? — turn the full beam of himself on her new friend, and she sits there smiling the "go on, this is nice" smile with her throat closed. After "Substitution" I don't think Randi is a co-conspirator having fun. I think she is a woman drowning in real time at her own table, performing enjoyment of a scheme she started or was pulled into, watching it work on someone she's already let herself love a little (the "love you, girl," the hand flat on Vee's back). That gutted me more than anything in the more explicit chapters did.

**Swoon / pull**

The math itself, weirdly, is where the chapter got me most. "He gave her back the pencil at the exact line where she could solve it herself" — that's such a specific, correct kind of tenderness, the tenderness of respecting someone's competence, and it's rendered so exactly that I felt the same lift Vee felt. I wanted this man to be exactly what he appeared to be. That's the swoon and the horror in the same sentence, and I don't think the book is cheating to get me there — it earned both halves.

**Trust vs. suspicion**

Here's where I have to be careful, because the instructions are right that I shouldn't import certainty the page hasn't given me. Within this chapter alone, nothing textually confirms this is a setup — no wink to the reader, no interior Pace thought that says "there she is, the one Randi pointed at." The chapter is written entirely from Vee's naive POV and plays completely straight. My suspicion is 100% imported from having just read "The Pointing Game" and watched Randi say "her name is Vee" with her whole face lit up 48 hours (book-time) before this. The text hasn't shown me the wire between those two scenes yet — it's just sitting there in my memory, taut. I'll say plainly: I am dreadful with suspicion right now, but it's memory-earned, not page-earned-in-this-chapter.

**Erotic charge**

Low and slow-burn here, which is right for the material — a coffee shop, cuffs rolled back, forearms noticed a half-second too long, "may I call you." Heat 1. It's all anticipation, no consummation, and it works exactly as a simmer should.

**Friction**

None that bothered me. The chapter is unusually well-controlled — it resists over-explaining Vee's naivety, trusts small physical details (the cup, "goodbye" landing wrong from Cassie two chapters ago as an echo of tone) to do the work.

**Titles**

"Substitution" is a gut-punch title once you finish the chapter, precisely because it plays completely straight as a math pun (u-substitution, Euler's formula) for the entire read — and then lands, on the last page, as something else entirely: a girl being substituted into a slot Randi vacated, or opened for her, at a table she didn't know was staged. I don't know yet if that's literally what's happening, but the title did real work retroactively — it recolored the whole chapter the second I finished it, the way a good title should. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* both feel newly loaded too: the "invitation" here is Pace's "may I," polite to the point of chivalry, and I can't unhear the spoon now — someone is being fed, carefully, by someone standing very close with a long reach.

**What I want next**

I want to know what Randi does with what she's just watched happen at that table — whether the not-sipping was jealousy of Vee, jealousy of Pace's attention leaving her, grief, or calculation. I want a scene from inside Randi's head immediately after this one. Pure guess from here: I think this book is going to make me watch Randi choose to keep doing this to Vee even while it's costing her something real, and I think that's going to be the hardest material in the book to sit through.
