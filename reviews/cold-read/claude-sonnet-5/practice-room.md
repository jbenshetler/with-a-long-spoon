# Cold read (grounded) — The Practice Room

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this chapter already a little dazed by the run leading up to it — the dress, the shoe-shopping farce, "Rock" with its Daphne reveal, the dance-hall blowup and its aftermath, the kitchen morning with the swat that clearly startled Vee more than she let on. So "The Practice Room" lands as a kind of decompression chamber, and it's the first time the book has let me watch Randi work in *real time*, not remembered, not framed as a lunch — right there in a soundproof closet with the door shut on the rest of the world.

How I feel about each of them: Pace is still winning me over almost against my better judgment. "Beauty like yours made for the gods and loaned out — I'm not going to insult the loan by complaining I have to look up" is such a specific, disarming line that I understand exactly why Vee has no defense against him. The dish-soaking bit, the tickling, the swat that goes somewhere neither of them expected, the T-shirt folded at the end of the bed, "You're always beautiful" — all of it reads as real tenderness, attentive and playful and *earned*, not manufactured. I don't currently distrust his feeling for her. What I distrust — still, structurally, the same unease from the checkpoint — is the machinery underneath it, which this chapter doesn't touch directly but which I can't stop hearing hum.

Randi, though — this chapter moved my needle on her hard, and not gently. Watching her extract the story from Vee is genuinely uncomfortable to read, in the good/bad way erotica should sometimes be uncomfortable. "So. Did you deserve it?" is such a small, surgical question, and the fact that Vee answers it — agrees to it — a beat later than she means to, is the chapter's best piece of psychology. Randi isn't just curious; she's *steering*, the same way she steered Vee toward Pace to begin with, and now she's steering her toward a vocabulary and a set of associations (being punished, being "made" to answer for herself) that Vee didn't bring into the room on her own. And then the pivot to her own story — "Mine... Over his knee... And I had to count" — landing as disclosure but functioning as instruction, is chilling in a way I found genuinely gripping rather than merely voyeuristic. She's not confiding in Vee. She's *conditioning* her, with real warmth as the delivery mechanism, and Vee has no idea that's what's happening to her even as it's happening.

**Swoon/pull** — the porch moment ("Leave them on") and the T-shirt-behind-the-ear ("You're always beautiful") both got me completely; that's the tender register the book does so well, unguarded and specific rather than generic. And there's real ache in the small detail at the very end — Vee's arms slow to come down from the hug, noticing it "the way you notice a held note after the song has already moved on." That's the chapter's best line, for me — quiet, unshowy, and it names (without Vee naming it to herself) exactly how far past "friend" this has already gone for her.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — this is the first chapter where my suspicion of Randi stopped being purely structural (the "I know something the characters don't" dramatic irony from the checkpoint) and became something the *scene itself* earned on the page. The stillness "like a cat going still," the "attention so complete it was nearly a pressure, nearly a hand" — the text is handing me the discomfort directly now, not just letting me infer it from what I remember about the pointing game. I don't distrust that Randi feels something real for Vee. I distrust what she's doing with it.

**Erotic charge** — it peaks for me in the closet, oddly without any touching at all — pure voice, pure extraction, the story-within-the-story of Randi's own "over his knee" scene doing double duty as confession and as grooming, and Vee's body answering it in real time ("something turned over low in Vee's belly... arrived, the actual physical fact of it"). The bathroom-stall moment afterward — her hand moving before she decides, catching it and holding it flat against her own knee, refusing to finish what her body wants — is the most honest, interior beat in the chapter: desire she won't look at squarely, exactly the kind of moment I read for. It goes a little slack right at the very end, in the hallway goodbye, which reads correctly as anticlimax — the ordinary noise of the building, the routine kiss — but that's clearly the point, the comedown after being worked over.

**Friction** — very little. The Simpson's-paradox lecture bit earlier in "All Told" (recent chapters, not this one) reads a bit thumb-on-the-scale in retrospect — "outliers" this chapter, "same numbers, nobody touched one of them" last — but this chapter's own lecture beat (the two kinds of outlier, one that's local and one that "comes in through the part that carries forward, it's in everything that comes after") does feel a little on-the-nose as a mirror for what's happening to Vee via Randi's steady drip of suggestion — an outlier that reshapes everything downstream of it. I noticed it, but it didn't annoy me; it's restrained enough (delivered flat, undercut immediately by Randi's knee against Vee's) that it reads as motif rather than lecture.

**Titles** — "The Practice Room" is quietly perfect and does give something away, in hindsight: a soundproof cell where nobody can hear you, historically used (per the freshman-year joke Vee half-swallows) for something other than music, and where Randi runs Vee through call-and-response the way a rehearsal does — repeat this, again, "did you deserve it," "and God, it made me buck" — until the body learns the part. It's a title that means nothing going in and recolors the whole scene once you're inside it: this wasn't a chat, it was a rehearsal, and I don't think Vee knows yet what she's being rehearsed for. As for *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — the jacket line "every yes was freely given, that was the problem" is exactly what this chapter dramatizes in miniature: Vee's "probably I deserved it" is a real yes, hers, and also a yes Randi built the conditions for. That's the book's whole promise, delivered in a five-minute scene.

**What I want next** — I want to know what Randi is actually building toward, and whether Pace knows this scene happened, given "Gone" showed us how much he and Randi trade information about Vee in bed. I'm bracing (guess, not knowledge) for a scene where the three of them are in a room together and the accumulated undertow — the goodbye kisses, the "did you deserve it," Vee's held-too-long hug — surfaces into something Vee can no longer read as "just friendship." I want that scene and also dread it a little, because I like Vee too much by now to want her blindsided, even though the book has told me from the jacket that blindsided is exactly the shape of the story.
