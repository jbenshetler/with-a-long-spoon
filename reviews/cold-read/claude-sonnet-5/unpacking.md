## Reader reaction

Coming off "Forbidden Kiss" and "Back," this chapter is a deliberate exhale — and it works on me exactly that way. After the coat-drop reunion and the long, sustained heat of the morning-after scene, "Unpacking" drops me back into the dorm, into cold radiators and Cassie's cracked window, and I feel the temperature change in my own body, not just Vee's. That's the chapter doing its job.

**Vee** — I love her here. The retelling to Cassie is pure delight: "I got mushrooms," the persimmons, doing his flat delivery "cold." This is Vee genuinely happy, unguarded, funny — and it's a different register than the confessional-arousal reporting she does with Randi. With Cassie she isn't performing anything, isn't being harvested; she's just telling a friend a good story and getting a friend's real laugh back. I noticed how much I trusted this exchange compared to every Randi-debrief scene in the checkpoint — no charge underneath it, no agenda, just warmth. And then the shrunk sweater — "Eep—" and her mouth "set off without her" — the tiny, human, comic detail (the cranberry sweater felted into a doll and the dye running into her pants) landing as pure charm, an accident of domestic life rather than a symbol. It's the first purely funny beat I remember the book giving me.

The one moment that snagged something in me: "There wasn't anything important in it anyway," about the bag left in the truck. It's thrown away so lightly — but given how carefully this book has trained me to notice throwaway lines (the "salt" ritual, the "May I" formula, the scar deferrals), I clocked it as *maybe* nothing, maybe a tiny tell that she's already reorganizing what counts as hers versus what stays at his house. I'm not sure the text means anything by it. Holding it loosely.

**Cassie** — exactly as trustworthy and dry as ever, and I felt real tenderness watching her fold the whole chair around to listen, "the whole chair," giving Vee "the entire front of her attention." Her stories (the wrapping-box mother, the brother-in-law in snowshoes, "the baby is a baby") are so plainly, unglamorously funny that they read as a small corrective after a whole book of Randi's engineered anecdotes — this is what an actual friend's small talk sounds like, offered with nothing wanted in return.

Then the closing beat lands harder than its size: "My mother says thank you for the card. / She wants to know when she gets to see you again." Vee's hand stopping "partway to the towel," going to the collar of her robe instead — that's a lovely, specific gesture of something closing up in her without her willing it. I don't fully know what it means yet — guilt? distance from her own family transposed onto Cassie's mother? some awareness of how far she's drifted from the "real-napkins" question of who her people even are anymore? — but the chapter earns the ache by not explaining it, just letting Cassie "hear the size of things" and let it go. That's the chapter's best moment for me: understatement doing real emotional work, the opposite of the lush, itemized sensuality of the previous chapter.

**Pace, offstage** — not present here except through Vee's report and the "Back" excerpt bleeding into memory (him wanting to meet "her people," Cassie, Meg, Kayla, even Theo — "Bring Theo"). I liked that generosity a lot when I read it, and it colors how I read this chapter: he isn't hoarding her away from her whole life, he's asking to be let into it, all of it, no edges cut off. That's a genuinely warm, trust-building beat for him after so much checkpoint-level unease about his method and his silences. Good — I want to believe it. I still can't fully un-know what the checkpoint told me about Randi and the pointing game, so even his warmth reads slightly haunted to me, though nothing in this actual chapter earns suspicion of him — this is me carrying dramatic irony forward, not the page indicting him.

**Erotic charge** — basically none, and that's correct; this is the comedown chapter, a breath after the marathon of "Forbidden Kiss." The only body-warmth is the physical comfort of Vee's ordinary shower, "less pressure, less private, less hers... happy in it anyway," which reads as contentment rather than heat.

**Swoon** — small and real: the retelling itself (a woman glowing, unable to stop grinning at her own good luck), the image of the bunk "exactly one girl wide" and her arm going out to find only cold cinderblock instead of him — a lovely, understated ache that doesn't overplay the loneliness, just states the fact and lets the reader feel the size of the change from king bed to dorm bunk.

**Titles** — "Unpacking" is doing exactly what it says: literally Cassie's duffel half-emptied on the bed, and figuratively Vee unpacking the weekend for Cassie, item by item, plus the smaller unspoken unpacking she doesn't do — the bag left in the truck, the thing about Cassie's mother she can't quite answer. A quiet, apt title, not a giveaway, more a lens that only clicks after the last page.

I want to keep reading for whether Pace's whole-cast dinner actually happens (and what Randi does inside a room with Cassie and Meg and Kayla all watching her at once), and whether that last beat about Cassie's mother comes back around.
