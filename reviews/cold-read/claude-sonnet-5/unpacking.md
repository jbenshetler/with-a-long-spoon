## Reader reaction

What a whiplash of registers, and I mean that as praise — after the sustained, extravagant heat of "Forbidden Kiss" (and the odd, unresolved little needle of that mouth-kiss moment carrying over into "Back"), "Unpacking" is almost entirely cool water. It's Vee and Cassie in a dorm room with a highlighter and a duffel bag of white socks, and it's lovely, and it's the first chapter in a long while where I got to just like these two people being friends without an undertow of dramatic irony running under it.

Cassie remains the one person in this book I trust without reservation, and this chapter earns that trust all over again in small ways: the two-inch open window as "defended territory, all winter, every winter," the dry deadpan repetitions ("Mushrooms." "He salts the persimmons." "He burned the toast.") that are Cassie's whole love language — she doesn't gush, she echoes, and the echo *is* the warmth. And then the real full laugh, "the rare full one she never spent on her own jokes," at Vee's shrunk-sweater cover story — I felt genuinely glad for both of them there, the kind of glad that has nothing sexual or strategic in it, which after fifty-some chapters of engineered intimacy is its own kind of relief.

I did clock, with a small private wince on Vee's behalf, that "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody" — the shrunk sweater as cover story for the truth (that she had no clothes because she arrived in nothing but a coat, and that whatever happened with the laundry is not what happened to her actual clothes). It's a lie of omission rather than commission, mostly harmless, but it lands differently now that I know how much Vee already withholds the "real" version from Cassie and gives it to Randi instead — this chapter shows her doing the same instinctive editing even in a moment of total unguarded happiness. Not sinister, just habitual now, and a little sad to watch fossilize.

The single moment that actually got me was the tiny one at the end: "My mother says thank you for the card. / She wants to know when she gets to see you again." And Vee's hand going "without her" to the collar of her robe, the "Oh" coming out small, something going through her middle she has "no intention of examining tonight." That's the chapter's real charge, and it's not erotic at all — it's about belonging, about Cassie's family quietly claiming Vee as theirs the way Pace's house has been claiming her body, and Vee flinching from it exactly because it asks for something real rather than something performed. I don't know what that flinch is about yet — guilt, or overwhelm, or the simple vertigo of having two different lives asking to be let all the way in — and the chapter is right to leave it ajar; it doesn't explain itself, it just sits there "warm and specific, like a hand set flat between her shoulder blades," and lets me feel the size of it without naming it.

Trust-wise: nothing here reads as false. This is the register where the book lets me breathe and just like the people in it, which after "Forbidden Kiss" — with its slightly uneasy afterimage of "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been" still humming under everything — felt earned and needed rather than a let-down in intensity. I don't dread anything in this chapter; if anything I wanted to stay in this room a little longer, snowshoes-in-the-house story and all.

The title, "Unpacking," is doing gentle double duty — Cassie's literal half-emptied duffel, and Vee's verbal unpacking of the weekend to her — and it's also quietly ironic, because the biggest thing (the coat, the reconciliation's real terms, whatever "corrected by nobody" is covering for) stays folded up and unmentioned. Not a big reveal-title, just an accurate, modest one; it didn't give anything away, it just told me plainly what kind of scene I was getting, and it was right.
