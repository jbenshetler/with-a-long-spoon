# Cold read (grounded) — Peekaboo

*scene: scenes/peekaboo.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch020 (no window) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. I went in braced — the checkpoint has me holding this low unease about Pace's craft being "very good at reading and opening people" in a way that's tied to the pointing game — and this chapter just... didn't trip that wire. It let me have the swoon clean, for once, and I noticed myself noticing that.

**Pace.** He's exactly the man the earlier chapters built — the surprise he can't keep in his mouth, the grin, pulling her past her own body's assumption (toward the bedroom) and instead toward the thing he actually made her. That reroute is the whole chapter in miniature: appetite redirected into care. The dress itself is almost absurdly moving as an object — "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like no trouble at all." That line wrecked me a little. It's not a grand gesture, it's a hidden one, and the reveal that he finished it at some sleepless hour while she didn't know — that lands as devotion, not performance, because there's no audience for it except her, after the fact. I trust him more this chapter than I have all book, and I feel a little guilty about that given what I know and he doesn't know I know.

**Vee.** This is her chapter through and through, and she's luminous in it — the crying she "hadn't decided to," the floozy-word flaring and her staying anyway, the choice to strip the underwear rather than mince her steps. That moment — "The second came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed" — is the whole arc of her shame-to-desire pattern crystallizing into one small, decisive physical act. I loved her fiercely here. And the closing daydream, imagining the induction, letting herself be looked at without cost — "It just felt like what she was for" — is both a triumph (she's earned that ease) and, knowing what I know, a small ache, because I can't fully separate "what she was for" from the fact that she was, literally, chosen and pointed at for a purpose she doesn't know about. The chapter doesn't dwell on that irony at all — it's all mine to carry, not the page's to gesture at, which I appreciated. It didn't manipulate me into the unease; I brought it myself, and I could set it down too and just be happy for her.

**Swoon / pull.** Total, unembarrassed swoon, especially the dress reveal and the mirror sequence — the slit opening "to the hip," the underwear coming off deliberately, the walk to him "not the careful walk." That's the erotic peak for me, more than the sex itself, which is handled almost as a natural continuation ("there was nothing left for either of them to decide") rather than the main event. And then the coda — hanging the dress mid-act, "he'd taken it from her and hung it from the door with the two-handed care you'd give something alive" — that's such a specific, tender, faintly absurd detail (stopping sex to save a garment) that it reads as completely real rather than a scripted romance beat.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new suspicion earned by the page itself here — if anything this chapter is doing the opposite work, deepening my reasons to trust Pace's devotion is real and specific to Vee, not generic seduction technique. My structural unease is still there, but it's mine, carried over from the checkpoint, not renewed by anything on this page.

**Erotic charge.** Peaks at the mirror — the dress finding her shape, the slit, the underwear coming off — genuinely charged, slow-built, sensory ("came down her like water finding its level"). The actual sex is compressed and almost matter-of-fact by comparison ("his hands went to her... after that there was nothing left for either of them to decide") — which works, because the chapter has already spent its erotic capital on the dress and the looking. It doesn't go slack, it just shifts registers, from want to consummation to the quieter warmth of after.

**Friction.** Almost none. If I'm picking at something: the daydream of the induction leans a bit into narrated triumph — "she stood in the middle of the light and let them look, and the looking didn't cost her a thing" — which states the theme almost directly rather than just showing it. It's earned by everything preceding it, but it's the one moment where I felt the author's hand naming the arc for me instead of letting the mirror scene alone do it.

**Titles.** "Peekaboo" is doing sly, playful work — I read it first as the game structure of the chapter itself (he hides the surprise, then reveals it; she hides herself in shame, then reveals herself on purpose in the mirror; the dress is hidden and then unveiled), and it fits the lightness of the opening (his grin, "helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth"). But peekaboo is also a game about who controls what's seen and when — and given everything the checkpoint has told me about pointing games and engineered "chance," the title sits with a faint second edge I can't quite unfeel: someone is always choosing what gets revealed and to whom in this book. I don't think the chapter itself earns that reading — it's just the title doing double duty against my memory. Book title *A Polite Invitation* feels apt again here — this really was an invitation, wholly hers to accept, and it's the warmest, least fraught instance of that pattern so far. The tagline about appetite still sits underneath, waiting.

**What I want next.** I want to stay in this warmth a little longer honestly, but I also feel the book pulling me back toward Randi soon — the checkpoint has her attraction to Vee building and her withholding from Pace, and this chapter's total absence of Randi (not even mentioned) makes her felt more, not less. Guess from here: the next Randi-adjacent chapter will feel colder or more strategic by contrast, precisely because this one let Vee and Pace have something clean.
