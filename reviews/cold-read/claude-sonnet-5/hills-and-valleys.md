## Reader reaction

Whiplash, honestly. New chapter, new title, and I'm suddenly on a bike with two people I've never met before, one of whom is apparently Pace — inferred, not stated — riding with a woman named Sheri. It takes a minute to even confirm the "he" is Pace at all; the text never says the name. The only handhold is "his place" and, later, "Randi. That's not finished," which lands like a small gut-punch of recognition once I get there.

Sheri is vivid and new — bleached ponytail, small hard-driving frame, blunt mouth, two years of history with this man on this exact ride. "She remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather" is a great, unsettling line — it tells me instantly what kind of friend this is and what kind of talk they're used to having about women. I don't dislike her. I actually find her bracing, a relief almost, after the hothouse closeness of the last chapter — she's outside the Vee/Randi/Pace weather system entirely, and she says the plain thing: "Two of them at once... If that's not already complicated, it will be." That's the reader's own unease, spoken by a stranger who has no stake in protecting anyone.

What moves me most is the description Pace gives Sheri of Vee at the chair — "run her thumb down the grain where the arm met the leg... asked him how he'd gotten it to do that... pulled one of the math books off the shelf and not put it back like it had burned her." That's tender in a completely different register than anything Randi's given us — it's Pace noticing Vee's hands, her curiosity, without staging her at all. "He heard himself going and didn't stop" and Sheri's "Listen to you" — that's real, unguarded fondness leaking out of a controlled man. It warms me toward him more than anything so far; it's the first time I've seen him wanted for something other than what he can produce or direct.

But then there's the cruelty tucked right beside it — "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that" — delivered about a woman who spent Thursday night alone, glared at the floor, danced with nobody. Sheri clocking that loneliness and immediately pivoting to appraise her body is exactly the kind of casual, complicit ugliness that made me flinch. It's not cruelty from malice; it's just what these two do together, apparently, and Pace doesn't correct it — he just says "Vee" like he's protecting the name from the rest of the sentence.

Erotic charge: none directly, this is a cold morning bike ride, all breath and gearing — but there's a charge underneath it, in what's withheld and revealed. The line about Randi — "That's not finished" — recolors everything from last chapter instantly; two lines, and I know Randi is not resolved or discarded, she's active, ongoing, simultaneous with whatever this is with Vee.

Trust/suspicion: I don't distrust Pace here exactly, but I'm newly aware that he has a whole life and history — two years of Saturday rides, an established shorthand — completely outside anything Vee or the reader has seen. It's less "something's off" and more "the aperture just widened," and it's unsettling in the way discovering someone's ordinary life always is when you've only known them in someone else's crisis.

Friction: mostly the disorientation of not being told plainly who "he" is — I had to infer it, and I'm still only about 90% sure, since nothing on the page names him. That's presumably intentional but it cost me a beat of confusion.

Title — "Hills and Valleys" is doing double work: literally the ride's terrain (climbs that take the breath, descents where they talk) and clearly a metaphor for the women in his life, maybe for the whole three-body situation — ups and downs, Randi/Vee, or the rhythm of talk and silence in intimacy generally. It's a title that means something modest and true on the surface and probably more once I sit with it.

What I want next: I want to see whether "Vee" as spoken between these two men — sorry, between Pace and Sheri — ever gets back to Vee, and what "not finished" with Randi actually looks like on the page, especially against tomorrow night's scripted reconciliation. Guess from here: this may be building toward Vee walking into something more crowded and less controlled by Randi's choreography than either of them expects.
