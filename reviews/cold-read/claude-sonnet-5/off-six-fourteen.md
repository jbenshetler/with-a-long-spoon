## Reader reaction

This is the comedown chapter after the swoon, and I loved it — Vee spinning through her tiny dorm room, nearly taking out the closet door, is such a specific, unguarded image of joy that I felt it in my chest. "*Look at me*" landed exactly as intended; I wanted to look at her too.

Cassie is doing everything I trust her to do — the "how much did he give you to drink?" line isn't cynicism, it's love wearing its usual flat dry floor, and she drops it the instant Vee makes clear this was hers, freely. "Nobody did anything to me... I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose" — Vee needing to say that plainly, needing Cassie to have it, is the chapter's real center for me. It's Vee defending her own agency to the one person whose read she actually trusts, and Cassie believes her instantly and fully. That's warm, not suspicious — I don't doubt either of them here.

But the title did something to me the prose didn't quite do on its own: "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" — Vee reciting this like a dossier, delighted that she *knows where he lives*, is played by her as proof of intimacy and by Cassie as a joke about missing persons. I felt the joke land as a joke — but the address given as directions rather than an address ("It's a *road*, Cassie, it's not —") needled at me a little given everything I already know about how carefully Pace stages things. Cassie's "rich people hike, poor people walk" is funny and also quietly probing something real — money, control of terrain, a house Vee hasn't seen. I don't think the text wants me alarmed yet, just watchful; it's light-handed enough that I read it as texture, not a flare.

Erotic charge is basically nil here — this is pure afterglow and friendship, not desire on the page — and that's fine, it's not what the chapter's for.

**Titles.** "Off Six-Fourteen" is Vee's own phrase, offered as certainty ("See. I know where he lives and everything") — and it's a little sad/funny as a title precisely because it's such thin, secondhand knowledge dressed up as solidity. It made me want to see that house before I trust the space it names.

I want to see the hike, and the house, next — and to sit a little longer in this genuinely happy Vee before anything complicates it.
