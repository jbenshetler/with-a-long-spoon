# Cold read — Off Six-Fourteen

## Reader reaction

**Characters** — I'm just delighted with Vee here, and I mean it plainly: she's giddy in a way that reads as fully earned, the pillow-throw, the spin she can't stop herself doing, the "Look at me." This is the first chapter where I feel her as a person having a good night rather than a person being watched (by the reader, by herself, by Pace). Cassie is exactly as I've trusted her to be — the "how much did he give you to drink?" landed as protective instinct rather than suspicion of Vee, and Vee's answer ("Nobody did anything to me... I did it because I wanted to") is the character insisting, almost defensively, on her own agency — which is touching precisely because I, the reader, am holding information that complicates it without contradicting it. Pace isn't on the page at all this chapter, which is its own kind of relief — I get Vee unfiltered, without his method humming underneath every gesture.

**Swoon / pull** — the spin in the too-small room got me completely. "the room too small to hold what she had to do with it" is such a good, unguarded image of joy, and I felt the swoon along with her rather than watching it from outside. The retelling-of-the-kiss-to-a-friend structure is one of my favorite romance moves and it worked here — hearing her tell it is almost more moving than the kiss itself would have been on the page, because it's filtered through pure, unclouded happiness.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — nothing new happened to unsettle me about Pace since he's absent, but the chapter did something quietly ominous that I don't think it fully intends me to notice as ominous *yet*: "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel... he'll have water, he's not going to walk me off a cliff... I know where he lives and everything." Cassie's joking about state troopers, and Vee's own dossier-counting, reads to me — carrying forward everything I know about Pace's engineered method — as a small, cold thread under all the warmth: an isolated location, a man whose real methods she doesn't know, a friend who only has "off six-fourteen" to go on if something happened. I don't think the text is asking me to be afraid, and I want to be honest that this might just be my accumulated wariness projecting onto ordinary hiking-invite chatter — but the specificity of "so I know what to tell the state troopers" is a strange thing for the author to have Cassie say lightly, and it snagged me.

**Erotic charge** — none directly (0), but the chapter runs on the afterglow of desire rather than desire itself, which works fine — it's warm, not horny, and that's the right register for a friend-debrief scene.

**Friction** — very minor: "Look at —" but the rest of it wouldn't stay sitting down" is a lovely line, no friction there. If anything the chapter is almost too pure a release valve after a very controlled prior chapter — but I think that's the point and I don't mind it.

**Titles** — "Off Six-Fourteen" is quietly unsettling once I sit with it: it's not "The Hiking Trip" or "Real Shoes," it's the address, the location, the specific data point that in any other genre would be the detail you circle back to. Given everything I carry about Pace's premeditation, naming the chapter after *where he lives* rather than after the kiss or the joy in the room feels like the book pointing me, gently but unmistakably, at what actually matters going forward — not the celebration, but the geography of what's next.

**What I want next** — I want the hike. I'm eager and a little tense for it — eager because I want Vee to keep having this unclouded happiness, tense because "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel" is now lodged in me as a place I'll be watching hard when we get there.
