# Cold read (grounded) — What to Wear

*scene: scenes/what-to-wear.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch012 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

This one's a comedown chapter in the best sense — after the mountain and the truck, we land in the dining hall with Kayla and Meg, and it's a completely different key: bright, gossipy, ordinary. I liked that shift a lot. It let me feel how big the hike and the truck scene actually were, refracted back through Vee performing them for an audience that isn't Cassie.

**Vee.** She's glowing here, and it's contagious — the pride in "He's getting his doctorate. Math," the "flat and certain" impression of his voice, the fry-stealing ease of a girl who is, for once, the one with the story everyone wants. I liked seeing her genuinely happy and a little smug, not just swooning. But there's a small wince under it too — she catches herself performing ("She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care") and I felt the same pull the checkpoint keeps naming: Vee doesn't fully look at what she's doing, she just enjoys it. Meg's flat, kind "Everybody's different at the start... Sometimes they even are" landed on me exactly the way it landed on Vee — a small bell that "didn't catch on anything" for her but rang for me, because I know things she doesn't.

**Kayla and Meg** are new faces and I liked them instantly — Kayla's unbroken bright stream, Meg's fork-pointed patience ("you keep getting to it and going soft and skipping the middle") — they feel like real specific girls, not just an audience for exposition. Meg in particular, with her own boyfriend-Danny aside, gives the chapter its one moment of dramatic irony that isn't about Randi/Pace at all, which was a nice, human touch — not everyone's irony has to be sinister, some of it's just how young love always sounds like this.

**Pace**, offstage entirely, still dominates the chapter by report. Hearing Vee narrate the shirt and the truck and the siren secondhand actually deepened my unease more than reading it live did — the retelling smooths every edge into pure romance ("he moves us along... that was the end of that"), and I can feel the story hardening into legend in real time, the way first-love stories do. That's not suspicion of Pace exactly — nothing new here contradicts his tenderness — it's more a reader's ache: I know this story is being built on a plan she can't see, and watching her burnish it for her friends made that ache sharper, not because anything false happened on the hike, but because she's already mythologizing him.

**Swoon/pull.** The text-message beat is the real charge of the chapter — "It says, would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock. That's it." — the anticipation, the parsing of eleven words, Kayla's "That's not a message, that's a ransom note," the group project of Saturday hair — this is warm, funny, recognizable swoon, not manufactured. I felt it. I wanted to know what's behind that door too.

**Erotic charge** is low here on purpose — this is a debrief chapter, not a scene — but the anticipation of the house functions almost like charge-in-reserve: we know (from the checkpoint's furniture, the bench) more than Vee does about what could be behind that door, and that knowledge sat under the light comedy the whole time, not as heat exactly but as tension.

**Friction.** None that felt like the author's thumb on the scale — Kayla's insistence "There's no meal at two... He wants you for the afternoon" reads like ordinary teenage deduction, not a planted clue, though it did needle me a little given what I already know about that house.

**Titles.** "What to Wear" is the chapter's own small joke — Kayla asking what Vee will wear, no answer given — but it also quietly rhymes with the earlier "russet dress" beat on the mountain (the one she can't afford, the one he called beautiful without picking it back up) and with the whole book's costume-of-belonging motif — the cardigan, the borrowed boots. It didn't give anything away so much as gently underline a thread already running. The book title and series title still sit where they've sat — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon* — this chapter is pure invitation-anticipation, the literal invitation to his house, so it's doing exactly what the titles promised, just at low simmer.

**What I want next.** I want the house. I want to see what's actually behind that unadorned two-o'clock text — knowing the checkpoint's furniture, I'm bracing (guess from here, not fact) that this is where something Vee doesn't have language for yet gets introduced, and I want to be there for her when it happens rather than hearing about it fourth-hand from Kayla and Meg next chapter.
