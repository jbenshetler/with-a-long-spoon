## Reader reaction

Well. That landed differently than anything so far, and I want to sit with what actually happened to me on the page before I tidy it up.

The opening scene is the most explicit, most sustained heat the book's given me yet, and it works — "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it" is a good, confident line, and the moment the question ("Would you like to taste another woman?") arrives in her body before it arrives in her mind, and she comes from the *idea* rather than from any touch — that's genuinely erotic writing, the kind that earns the word desire rather than just describing mechanics. I felt that one.

But almost immediately under the heat I started to feel something I hadn't felt about Pace before: wariness. Last chapter I trusted him completely, in absentia, as the tender, patient, hand-built-bench man. This chapter I'm inside his head and he is *running something*. "He had decided this beforehand." "So. There it was." "He admired it, a little, even as he saw through it." He watches Randi build herself an escape hatch — *not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy* — and finds it *beautiful*, an aesthetic pleasure in watching her disguise her own want as a present to him. That's the line that turned something over in me. It's not cruel, exactly — he delays his own climax for her pleasure, he waits for her to be "gathered back into herself" before he asks the real question, he wants her to be able to say yes without having to look at it — but it is *managed*, top to bottom, and he knows it's managed, and he's glad of the management working. "He liked, even better, the places she surprised him" reads almost like a collector's satisfaction. I don't distrust him as a villain. But I no longer read him as simply generous. The generosity and the control are the same gesture, and the text doesn't resolve which one it is — it holds both, and I'm going to hold both too.

Randi, though — my sympathy for her went up, not down. After last chapter's "no door," this chapter is nothing but doors bursting open against her will: the first "Yes" torn out of her by the mere idea, the flinch when the question got serious, the "Her" at the sight of the redhead — "she did the thing she had not done once all afternoon: she did not look away." That bare, unmanaged "Yes" with nothing dressed around it. And then the recovery, the kitten voice coming back up over it like water closing over a stone — I ached for her there, because I've now seen (twice) what it costs her to be witnessed wanting something, and I've seen that her body keeps outrunning the performance no matter how good the performance is. The final "Yes" — the private bed-register one, said in daylight, breathy — felt like the biggest crack yet, and I was rooting for her in that moment purely and simply, glad for her, even while the engineering that got her there nags at me. And the beat right after it, him just wrapping her up and holding her while students walked past, "the fact of her pleasure stayed bright in him" — that's the one moment I liked Pace uncomplicatedly, no calculation in it.

And then — Vee. Finally, after however many chapters of absence, here she is, and she arrives gorgeous and unguarded and completely unaware she's being surveyed and selected: "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." I liked her instantly and helplessly, the freckles and the copper hair and the abundance the year's clothes weren't cut for — but I'm aware, and a little uneasy about being aware, that my first sight of her is as an object of two other people's engineered desire, not as herself. She hasn't had a word of interiority yet. She's radiant and she's bait, in the same frame, and the chapter doesn't ask me to reconcile that — it just lets both be true.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** earned wariness about Pace specifically, for the first time — not that he's lying or cruel, but that his tenderness and his authorship of Randi's exposure are inseparable, and he knows it and enjoys it. No suspicion of Randi; she's the one person in this chapter I trust completely, because everything she does, even the management, reads as self-protection rather than deception of anyone but herself. No read yet on Vee — she's barely spoken.

**Erotic charge:** peaks hard in the opening bed scene, especially the orgasm-from-the-idea-alone beat. Cools into a slow simmering tease through the walk (the kiss where she tastes herself on his mouth, "You dirty boy"), and spikes once more, quieter, at the very end — not sexual exactly, but charged, in the "Yes" kissed into his mouth.

**Friction:** the only thing that nagged slightly was how confidently Pace's narration reads Randi's interior — "Not the closed ones, then. Not the ones who'd have to be gotten through" — total access to her taste and psychology, stated as fact rather than inference. It's in-genre for a close-third built around him, and it's consistent with how attentive he's shown to be, but it does put a lot of narrative authority in the hands of a man I'm now half-suspicious of.

**Titles:** "The Pointing Game" is exactly what's on the page, no hidden angle that I can find — but it does something clever anyway: it names the mechanism (pointing, choosing) while letting "game" carry the euphemism that the whole chapter is *about* — desire dressed as play so it doesn't have to be looked at directly. And the series title, *With a Long Spoon* — I've been carrying that as unexplained ambient dread, and this chapter is the first time it clicked into something: sup with the devil, use a long spoon. Pace isn't a devil, but there's something here about keeping engineered distance from what you're feeding someone, or feeding yourself through someone else's hunger, that rhymes with the proverb for the first time.

**What I want next:** I want Vee's own eyes — I've had five-plus chapters of her being discussed, waited-for, or in this chapter, gazed at, and I'm hungry for a chapter that's actually inside her. I want to know how Randi carries this "yes" forward — does she tell herself it was only ever the game, or does something in her know better now. And I'm newly curious, in a way I wasn't before, about what Pace actually wants out of orchestrating this — whether it's purely generosity toward Randi or something he's building for reasons of his own.
