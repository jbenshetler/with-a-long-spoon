## Reader reaction

Well — that's a big swing from where I left off. Last chapter I was aching for Randi as a woman who can't put down her guard even alone in her own room; this chapter opens inside a scene where the guard is fully down, sexually, in bed with Pace — and then spends the rest of its length watching Pace *use* that opening to build something. I want to be honest about how conflicted this left me.

**Pace.** I'm meeting him properly for the first time on the page, and it's entirely from inside his head, which is new — before, he was a mentioned absence. What I find is a man of real, careful attention: he reads her body with precision ("He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it"), he's patient, he holds off his own pleasure for hers, he kisses her "for no reason but that he wanted to." That's genuinely tender. But laced all through that tenderness is something that unsettled me more than anything in the book so far — the deliberateness of the *engineering*. He asks "Would you like to taste another woman?" mid-orgasm, on purpose, specifically to catch her body answering before her mind can defend against it. He watches her scramble to re-narrate her own desire ("Only in a threesome. With you") and thinks: "he understood what she had done, because it was beautiful... She had moved it... The same act. A different owner." That line landed like a small alarm bell for me. He isn't just noticing her self-protection — he's *approving* of it, cultivating it, giving her "camouflage" so the wanting can come out "dressed as play." He calls this kindness. I'm not sure I trust that framing, even though the text hands it to me with total sincerity. It reads like consent being carefully manufactured rather than simply met.

**Randi.** My heart is still with her. The involuntary orgasm at the question, the flinch when it's asked seriously, the *helpless* body outrunning the careful mind — all of that tracks devastatingly with what I already know about her (the "no door" line from last chapter). What breaks my heart open here is the pointing game itself: watching her "shop" for a woman under a game's cover, giving verdict after verdict ("too expected," "she'd be a project," "God, no") with real, visible relief every time he lets her have an opinion — and then the arrival of the redhead. That "Yes" — bare, alone, arriving before she can dress it — is the first completely unmanaged thing I've seen from Randi in this whole book. It's genuinely thrilling to read. And then watching her recover, reach for the kitten voice, add "I like her" to make it a gift instead of a hunger — I felt the loss of that unguarded second like something in my own chest.

**Vee.** Here she is, finally, and she's wonderful on entry — "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Everything about her is built as the anti-Randi: uncurated, abundant, unpinned hair, a body the "grammar" has no vocabulary to grade. I liked her instantly, on sight, exactly the way Randi did. That's a real swoon moment for me — the campus description (the single burning maple, the wind, the ridge) is gorgeous and does real work setting up how *alive* Vee reads against everyone else on that path.

**Swoon / pull:** The bed scene, the walk, the maple, the redhead's laugh — genuinely swept me. I wanted the "Yes" to be simple and it wasn't, and that ache is doing real work.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** This is where I have to be honest — this chapter earned real suspicion of Pace for the first time. Not paranoia; the text itself hands me his calculation, twice ("He had decided this beforehand," "He chose it now, fast and without seeming to"). I don't doubt his tenderness is real. I do doubt that tenderness and engineering-her-consent-from-outside are compatible in the way the narration wants me to believe they are.

**Erotic charge:** Very high — the opening sex scene is explicit and unhurried and works completely; the pointing game keeps a low hum of arousal going the whole walk (the kiss that lets her taste herself again is a great beat). Never goes slack.

**Friction:** The one thing that nagged — the prose tells me repeatedly how *good* Pace's intentions are ("that mattered more to him than the answer did," "she deserved that gentleness. He wanted it for her") almost as if pre-empting my doubt. That felt like the author's thumb on the scale a little; I noticed I was being reassured rather than shown only.

**Titles:** "The Pointing Game" is exactly and only what it says — no ambush, but rereading it now it feels colder than it did going in, since I now know the game is also a mechanism for making desire deniable. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* both suddenly feel loaded — an invitation extended with exquisite manners can still be a kind of leash, and "long spoon" (which I only have as a title, no gloss) now nags at me as "keep your distance from what you're feeding."

**What I want next:** I want to see Vee's own interior — is she oblivious, curious, susceptible? I want to know if she has any idea what she's being pointed at. And I'm newly worried, in a way I wasn't last chapter, about whether Pace's "gift" is actually good for Randi or just easier for him to watch.
