## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction**

Well — there's Vee. And the way she arrives is the whole chapter's gut-punch: not through her own eyes, not even through a real meeting, but pointed at, chosen, and then *approached under a fabricated bump* by a woman performing spontaneity so well that Vee apologizes for a collision Randi engineered. I like Vee immediately and instinctively — the laugh with her head thrown back, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen," the redhead who moves like an athlete and dresses to be covered and fails at it without apology. She's drawn as the one open, ungoverned body on a quad full of curated ones. And I feel for her retroactively and pre-emptively, because I now know she has just been *selected* by two people running a private game, and doesn't know it.

Pace is the one who unsettles me most this chapter, and it's not really about the threesome idea itself — consensually proposed, consensually (if elaborately) routed around — it's the *pointing game* itself. He is narrating Randi's whole body as data (the recoil at "the bald social wrongness," the "coil… of a follow gathering herself," "he was watching her learn that she could") with a control and self-satisfaction that reads less like intimacy and more like an experiment he's running with real tenderness laid on top of it as garnish. "He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could" — I want to believe that's generosity. It might be. But it's also a man engineering, then standing back at a light pole to watch, a woman perform a manufactured "chance" encounter with a stranger who has zero idea she's being cast. That's the discomfort the page actually earned, not one I'm importing.

Randi, though — my sympathy for her, already high from last chapter, only grows. The opening sex is genuinely hot and genuinely tender (his slowness, "he had never needed to fill a silence," the kiss after with no reason but wanting to), and her involuntary orgasm at the *question* — "the question reached her body before it reached her" — is one of the most honest, unguarded moments the book's given her. Then watching her *manage* the fallout of that honesty in real time — turning "I want a woman" into "I want to give you a woman" — is devastating and completely consistent with the mirror-scene, self-concealment version of her I already know. The whole quad sequence is her doing, in public, exactly what she did with Brooke and Sloane and Kinsey: converting real wanting into performable wanting, live, under scrutiny. Except this time Pace is both the scrutiny and the one orchestrating it.

Erotically, the chapter peaks early — the bed scene is warm and explicit and does real character work (the question landing in her body before her mind, the deliberate slow third orgasm). After that it goes slack as sex and turns into something colder and more voyeuristic: Pace at his light pole, "far enough back to be nobody," enjoying his own experiment. That's a heat-to-unease pivot I feel the book meant me to feel, not one I'm inventing.

The title, "The Pointing Game" — exact and a little chilling once you're inside it. It doesn't hide anything; it names precisely the mechanism (he points, she picks) and, in retrospect, forecasts that this is a *game* with rules being established live, which makes Vee's unknowing arrival at the end land harder.

I want to keep reading badly — I want to know what Vee makes of any of this, whether she ever learns she was pointed at, and whether "Cassie" is the girl in the camel coat, the round-faced one on the steps, or someone still to come. Guess from here: Vee is about to be steered, per the jacket, into something she has no visibility into yet, and that's going to be the book's real tension — not whether Pace and Randi get what they want, but what it costs Vee not to know the shape of the game she's already inside.

**How I feel — overall**
Swept up and unsettled in almost equal measure, which feels like the right response rather than a contradiction to resolve. The bedroom opening is generous, hot, and genuinely moving — Randi's involuntary honesty is some of the best writing about desire the book's given me. But the campus sequence turns that honesty into material for a game Pace designed and now stands back to watch, and it lands Vee — genuinely likable, unguarded, drawn with real warmth — inside a scheme she has no idea she's part of. I like Vee on sight and want good things for her; I trust Randi's wanting completely and trust Pace's *care* for Randi rather less certainly than I did two chapters ago. Excited and wary, in that order, to see what happens next.
