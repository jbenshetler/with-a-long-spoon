## Reader reaction

This one landed hard and it landed uneasy, and I want to be precise about both halves rather than pick one.

Pace, first. My feeling about him has moved, and moved in a direction I don't love. Last chapter I had him on hold — attentive, maybe sincere, unresolved. This chapter closes some of that gap and I don't like what it closes toward. He is *reading* her the entire time — "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it," "He had decided this beforehand," "He worked the third one out of her deliberately." That's not just attentiveness, that's instrumentation dressed as generosity. He asks a question mid-orgasm-adjacent state specifically because he's testing what her body will say before her mind can intervene — "The question reached her body before it reached her." Then he watches her recover, watches her build the cover story ("Only in a threesome. With you"), and narrates his own admiration for how *beautiful* her self-deception is, all while planning to walk her out onto a quad and repeat the experiment in public, with strangers, until he gets the reaction he's fishing for. The tenderness is real on the page — he does touch her face, does kiss her slow, does say "that mattered more to him than the answer did" — but it sits right alongside a man conducting something closer to an experiment on someone he's sleeping with. I don't trust the warmth to be separable from the control. Both are on the page at once and I'm not going to resolve that for the text.

Randi — my sympathy for her, already high, went further. What's devastating here is watching the same mechanism from last chapter (the face, the recalibration, the "I can live inside this version, not that one") get used *on* her by someone else, deliberately, and dressed up as a gift. She isn't choosing this game from a position of freedom — she's being maneuvered into a shape where her wanting can only exist if she can attribute it to him ("a girlfriend offering her man a gift"). And it works, completely — she is delighted, lit up, doing the "happy footwork she didn't decide to do." That delight is real. I believe it. And it still happened because a man built a structure for her to feel it in. Both of those are true and I'm holding them together, not choosing.

Vee. Finally, two chapters late by my count, and she arrives gorgeously — the whole passage on her is the warmest, least clinical writing in the chapter, unpolished and unposed against every calculated woman before her ("laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen"). I felt real lift there, real relief after all the grading and dismissing — "too expected," "she'd be a project," "God, no." Vee is the one person on the page not being surveyed by Pace's game, and the one person Randi responds to with her whole unmanaged body. I like her instantly and I have zero information about her interiority, which is its own kind of ache — she doesn't know any of this happened to her, that she was pointed at, chosen, engineered toward. That she has a "stats class" with Randi and no idea what just occurred between two strangers watching her cross a lawn gives me a genuine chill, not manufactured — the text earned it by showing me exactly how surgical Pace's read of the crowd was.

**Swoon / pull** — the swoon is real and I won't undersell it: the bed scene has heat that actually works on me, unhurried and specific ("still swollen and still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended"), and the walk has genuine romantic play in it — "I point. You pick," her laugh "before she could decide whether to." I wanted to like this scene uncomplicatedly and for stretches I did. Vee's entrance is the chapter's high point of pure pull — I wanted to watch her too.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — earned suspicion of Pace, sharply so, for the first time. Not vague unease — specific: premeditation stated outright twice ("He had decided this beforehand," "He did not rush her... the whole art of it was that it stay a game"), and his own narration admiring his manipulation as craft. I trust that he wants her pleasure. I no longer trust that wanting her pleasure and wanting to run an experiment on her are different things to him.

**Erotic charge** — peaks in the bed scene, stays live and strange through the pointing game (the "maybe" kiss is the hottest single beat — tasting herself on his mouth mid-walk), goes cooler and more voyeuristic once he's watching from the light pole, which read to me as deliberate — the charge shifts from erotic to something more like surveillance-pleasure.

**Friction** — none I'd call a miss; if anything the chapter is disciplined about not explaining itself, which is part of what unsettled me — it trusts me to notice the calculation without a narrator flagging it as calculation.

**Titles** — "The Pointing Game" is exact and a little cold once you've read it — it names the mechanism, not the feeling, which now feels like the point: even the title treats Randi's desire as something being played, not something she owns outright.

**What I want next** — I want Vee's own interiority badly, and I'm wary of how she gets folded into whatever this "gift" becomes. Guess from here only: I suspect this game doesn't stay contained to Pace and Randi's bed.
