## Reader reaction

Okay. This one has teeth I didn't expect, and I need to sit with it before I can say anything smooth.

The sex that opens it is genuinely hot — the mouth-still-wet kiss, "he came up over her before she was done," her clenching around nothing — that's the good kind of explicit, unhurried and specific and not squeamish. But even in that first paragraph I clocked something: "He had decided this beforehand." That's Pace narrating his own strategy mid-lovemaking, and it set a tone that never really let up for the rest of the chapter. Everything after reads as *engineered* — tender, attentive, generous, yes, but also calculated down to the beat. "He worked the third one out of her deliberately, slowly, giving her more than she'd asked for" — I believe he means it kindly. I also can't stop noticing that he's running an experiment on her and clocking her responses like data: "the answer come up out of her before she made it a sentence — saw her stop, fully."

The pointing game itself is where I really started to feel torn. It's dressed as play — a fun, consensual little game between lovers — and Randi does visibly come alive in it, the "coil" of a dancer's readiness, the *shopping*, the laugh she "could not decide whether to" let out. That's real and it's lovely to watch her loosen. But I can't unsee that Pace built the whole afternoon as a delivery mechanism for a question he'd already gotten the true answer to in bed, and that he is *pleased with himself* for seeing through her cover story every single time — "he understood what she had done, because it was beautiful." That line stopped me. It's affectionate, but it's also the language of someone enjoying his own mastery over another person's psychology. I don't fully trust that this is only tenderness.

And then Vee. God — the way she's introduced, laughing with her head thrown back, "forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — that's the most alive anyone has felt on the page in this book so far, and I felt the pull of her instantly, the same helpless pull Randi felt. But she has *no idea* she's being pointed at, chosen, discussed. That dramatic irony is uncomfortable in a way the chapter clearly intends — this is a woman being selected for someone else's game without her knowledge, and the chapter lets Pace's satisfaction ("a deep warmth in his body at the sight of it") stand without complicating it. I don't fully condemn it — nothing here is coercive toward Randi, and it's textured with real gentleness ("that mattered more to him than the answer did") — but there's something in the hunting vocabulary ("he pointed," "the frame snapped into place," "the follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead") that keeps this from feeling like pure romance to me. It feels like romance with a strategist's hand on the wheel.

Yes" breathy escalation at the end — that landed on me hard, genuine delight breaking through her management. I wanted her to have that unguarded joy. I liked Pace best in the small human beat of just holding her while students walked past, no calculation in it.

I do think he is the one steering, every time, even while telling himself and us that the choice is hers.

It didn't spoil anything, but it did prime me to watch for the hand doing the pointing, which is exactly where my unease ended up.
