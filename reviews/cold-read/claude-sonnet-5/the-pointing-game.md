## Reader reaction

This chapter starts with me still holding the last one in my body — the bench, the crying, the locked bathroom door, Randi lying rigid under a duvet with her shoes still on. So I came into "The Pointing Game" braced for aftermath, for Pace to be tender and careful with something newly cracked open. Instead he's inside her again, and the very first thing he does is ask her, at the moment she's most undefended, whether she'd want to taste another woman. My stomach did a small drop at that — not because it's not hot (it is), but because I recognized the maneuver immediately from the last chapter: he waits for the moment control is gone and then puts a question there on purpose. "He had decided this beforehand." That sentence is doing a lot of work on me. It's not spontaneous curiosity in bed — it's an experiment, timed.

And yet. I have to be honest that the scene itself is beautifully hot regardless of what I think of the ethics simmering under it. The image of him sliding into her while she's still pulsing, kissing her with her own taste still on his mouth, is exactly the kind of unguarded, bodily-honest eroticism this book does so well. Her orgasm hitting "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — that's a gorgeous, exact rendering of desire outrunning the mind's permission. I felt that.

**Pace** — I am watching my trust in him narrow, chapter by chapter, in a way that feels earned rather than imposed. He is unfailingly attentive to her comfort and pleasure at the level of the body — checking in, giving her every joy before his own, waiting for her to be "gathered back into herself" before he asks again. That patience reads as real care. But there's a coldness running underneath it that this chapter makes explicit for the first time: "he had wondered whether the idea would land in her as a transgression or as nothing" — he is running a test on her, dispassionately, and feeling pleased with the data. "So. There it are." The clinical little beat of satisfaction sat wrong with me, on purpose I think — the prose doesn't scold him, but it doesn't need to; it just shows me a man cataloguing a woman's shame-response with real fondness and real detachment at once. When he "admired it, a little, even as he saw through it" — watching her dress her own desire in an acceptable shape (a gift to him, not a want of her own) and being pleased by the *craft* of the lie rather than troubled by what it cost her to need it — that's the moment I stopped fully trusting him. Not distrust that he'll hurt her physically. Distrust that he sees her as a whole person rather than a marvelously responsive instrument he is glad to have found the right key for.

**Randi** — my heart genuinely ached for her here, right on the heels of last chapter's devastating bathroom scene. She has just decided, alone under a duvet, that the version of her that begged is never coming out again — and here she is, four weeks later apparently, already handing her body's confessions over to Pace's careful, patient extraction, and still performing the recovery ("Oh, you dirty boy") the instant she's caught raw. The line "she had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping" is such a good, funny, sad beat — I liked her in that moment, avid and unguarded and enjoying herself, and it made the ending hit harder.

**The pointing game itself** — this worked on me exactly as escalating game-as-seduction; watching Randi veto option after option (too expected, a project, strong no) while her body tells on her the whole walk was genuinely fun and light and warm, real flirtation-as-play, until it wasn't. When Vee appears — "Her," Pace said, and pointed — the whole register changed. Randi's stopped stride, the bare "Yes" with nothing around it, no costume yet on it — that's the most naked moment either of them has had all chapter, more naked than the bed scene, because it isn't performed for anyone, not even for herself.

And then the gut-punch: I know who Vee is. The jacket copy told me on page one — "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one... a secret choice made about her." So when Randi choreographs that "accidental" shoulder-bump with a decade of dance training, when Pace stands back at his light pole "far enough back to be nobody," feeling "a deep warmth in his body at the sight of it" — I'm watching the origin of the whole novel's central deception happen in real time, dressed up as an afternoon game between two people who are, right now, genuinely delighted with each other and delighted by what they're about to do to a stranger they haven't even spoken to as anything but a body to be pointed at. That's a very specific kind of dread — not suspense about what happens, since I already know the shape of it, but the sick clarity of watching two charming, attentive, even loving people construct a trap with real tenderness for each other while they do it.

**Erotic charge** — high in the bed scene, genuinely hot and psychologically alive (the taste-of-yourself exchange, the deliberate slowness of his second question). It cools into something more voyeuristic and uneasy during the pointing sequence — not less charged, but the charge relocates from body to power. By the time Vee appears the "erotic" register has almost entirely become about watching Randi want, and about Pace watching Randi want — a triangulated arousal that's compelling but colder than the opening.

**Friction** — very little. The prose trusts me to feel the wrongness without naming it, which I appreciated — nobody says "this is manipulative," it's just shown, precisely, and I got there myself. If anything the transition from the bedroom to the campus walk felt slightly brisk — one paragraph and they're outside — but the chapter earns its length once the game starts; each vetoed woman builds real rhythm and I never got bored.

**Title** — "The Pointing Game" is exactly what it says on the tin, and I don't think it's hiding much on a first pass — but it does something clever anyway: by the time I finished I could hear "pointing" as also meaning something about direction, aim, being aimed *at* someone. Pace is the one holding the finger the whole chapter, literally and structurally, even while insisting "I point, you pick" as though that transfers the power to Randi. It doesn't, quite. As for the book's title and tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is the tagline turning into machinery in front of me. Randi's yes to the game is completely free and completely hers. And it's also the hinge that closes the trap on Vee before Vee has said a single word.

**What I want next** — I want to see this from Vee's side now, badly — I want the "she never knew there was one" promise to land. I'm also curious, and a little wary, about how the book will handle Randi's own unresolved crack from the bathroom scene — whether it gets acknowledged between her and Pace at all, or whether it just gets folded into "the game" and forgotten the way the bench got covered back into a table. Guess from here: I suspect that folding-over is going to be the book's whole method with Randi, and that it will cost her more than either of them currently seems to think it will.
