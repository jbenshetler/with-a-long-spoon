# Cold read — Bare

*scene: scenes/bare.md · model: claude-sonnet-5 · read after: under-the-rug*

## Reader reaction

This one lands like a gut-punch after last chapter's noisy, hopeful confusion. It's so quiet and so short and it does more work than the Chili's scene did with all its dialogue.

Vee alone, in the dark, doing the thing she's done all book — reaching for the shirt, reaching for the smell, trying to get comfort from the object because she can't get the word from the man. I felt for her hard in the opening beats: "there was no text that made it come clear" — that's the whole ledger's central ache in one line, and it's devastating because she knows it, she's not fooling herself about the mechanism even while she's still doing the thing anyway.

But it's the reversal that got me — the moment she realizes the shirt is warm because *she's* warm, not because he's in it. "It had promised him close and there was no one close." That's brutal and exact. I've watched her do this self-soothing all book (the shirt ritual, standing motif) and this is the first time the book shows the trick failing in real time, on the page, with her fully awake to its failure. She doesn't even get the comfort of self-deception here — she strips the shirt off and lies there cold and awake instead, and that refusal to put it back on felt like the most honest thing she's done since the Chili's confrontation. She tried demanding a name out loud last chapter; this chapter she can't even manage the smaller lie to herself.

Pace isn't on the page at all, but he's everywhere in absence — "gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him" is a quietly damning line, not accusatory, just observed. It reframes his ease from earlier chapters (post-coital, comfortable) as something almost enviable and a little unfair — he sleeps; she doesn't. I don't distrust him exactly, but this chapter sharpens the asymmetry in a way that makes me uneasy on her behalf rather than his.

Cassie is present only as breath in the dark below — sleeping, unaware, a marker of ordinary rest Vee can't have. No new feeling toward her, just the contrast doing its job.

**Swoon / pull:** Not erotic here at all — the charge is entirely in loneliness, not want. The closest thing to swoon is the memory-catalog of him ("the things he made with his hands. The rooms he kept warm for her") — and even that curdles by the end of the paragraph into evidence of what's missing rather than what's given.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion of Pace as a person — nothing sinister here. But my trust that the relationship's *shape* is sustainable took another hit. This isn't him doing anything wrong; it's the structural gap the whole book keeps circling, rendered starkly.

**Erotic charge:** Zero, and rightly so — this is the chapter that shows what's underneath the heat.

**Friction:** None — this is tightly controlled, no thumb on the scale. The prose trusts silence to do the work.

**Titles:** "Bare" — perfect and quietly devastating once I finished. It means the shirt off, obviously, but also her stripped of the self-comforting fiction, stripped of the word she needs, lying literally and figuratively exposed. Doesn't give anything away beforehand, recolors completely afterward. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — no new resonance to add, but the long-spoon idea (something kept at arm's length, a wariness in closeness) feels newly apt applied to Vee's own self-management here.

**What I want next:** I want to see whether this raw, undefended state carries into how she treats Pace next time she sees him, or whether she buries it again by morning the way she "forgave" him last time. Guess from here: it either forces a real conversation soon, or gets folded back into the pattern and the ache keeps compounding.
