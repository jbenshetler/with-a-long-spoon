# Cold read — Old Acquaintances

*scene: scenes/old-acquaintances.md · read after: fairytale*

## Reader reaction

This one ached, and it earned the ache honestly. Reading Randi's flawless-Gstaad night last chapter and knowing it was hollow, then watching Vee lie in a cold twin bed swallowing that same "*you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here*" as gospel and *envying* it — "even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had" — that's the cruelest and best thing the book has done to me so far. I know the fairytale is a lie. Vee doesn't. And the detail that guts me is that Vee *also* knows the money is dirty — "she'd been in the glass room when the name came up and Randi blanched" — and envies the mountains anyway. So the irony isn't simple; Vee sees more than Randi thinks and still gets played, because what she's starved for isn't truth, it's *life lived out loud*.

**Vee.** My sympathy for her is total and uncomplicated right now. The childhood-room stuff is beautifully done and never maudlin — "The room did not know her. It had known Vivienne… and had no idea who was in the bed." The bed her legs outgrew in middle school, knees bent, small in it again. That's the whole chapter's engine: she's been "breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." The food thread landed hard for me because I've been carrying the Pace-feeds-hunger motif — her mother's Christmas meat "had never once been the thing Vee was hungry for… she just hadn't had the word for it then." That's the hunger Pace named for her, now retroactively poisoning home.

The scissored photo detail is the standout craft moment: same induction picture, she sent Pace the whole thing (leg to the hip) and cut it off at the waist for her mother's phone — "she was the one who had divided it." That does more work than any speech could. It's shame and ownership in one gesture.

**Pace.** My unease about him sharpened, but through *Vee's* fear, not through any new fact. The Sheri thing came back with teeth: "*She's alone*, he'd said… had folded it away as pity, in November, when she was the one he cooked for. But about Christmas he'd said nothing at all." I felt her recalculating a kindness as a possible betrayal, and I don't know either. The chapter is careful — it doesn't tell me Pace did anything. It shows Vee *unable to know*, which is worse. His "he leaves you the room" trait, once tender, is now the thing that abandons her: "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." Same fact, flipped by absence. That's the book's whole method and it's working.

The passage that hit hardest: she reaches for her best memory — the fra diavolo night, the slipping sheet — "to be warm," and the fantasy *mutinies*. "The hair went gold and the leg went tan and the blonde sat where Vee had sat." She can't even keep her own memory; the imagined rival takes the sheet off her inside her own head. That's a genuinely frightening image of jealousy, and it's the erotic charge of the chapter — not aroused, exactly, but the fra-diavolo/garlic-blooming/plate-set-down-when-she's-aching sensory run is the same body-language the sex scenes use, so it lands warm and then curdles. The erotic register is being used as a wound here, deliberately, and I bought it.

**Randi.** Because I saw her chapter, I read her here as the false floor Vee is standing on. "The only girl she could talk to about real things" — and I know Randi has privately decided to keep the brunches exactly as they are and never say the true thing, and I know the "real things they talked about were mostly Pace" is Randi harvesting. Vee circling term-start as "the one sure thing the new year held" while Pace is "a blank" is exactly backwards from where safety actually lies, and the chapter lets me feel that without a single nudge. No new Randi facts, but my dread of her deepened purely from the collision of the two POVs.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** The text keeps its discipline — nothing here *tells* me Pace cheated or that Randi is scheming. Everything sinister is inside Vee's midnight catastrophizing, explicitly flagged as such: "what you believe at midnight, cold and alone." So the suspicion I carry is mine plus last chapter's, not this chapter overplaying its hand.

**Friction.** Very little. If anything the "poisoned Gstaad" / "she knew whose money it was" lines are the closest the chapter comes to leaning on me — it wants to make sure I remember Vee saw the PPP data, and it says it twice ("she knew," "I knew whose money it was"). It's not egregious, but it's the one spot where I felt the author making sure I connected a dot I'd already connected. Everything else trusts me.

**What I want next.** I want the term to start. I want to see the first brunch after the break with both these interiors now loaded in me — Vee arriving grateful and starved, Randi "keeping it exactly as it was." *Guess from here:* the rupture with Pace won't be resolved by Pace; Vee will get managed back toward him *through* Randi, who will "help" her interpret the fight — Randi producing with words the reconciliation Pace never has to spend. I dread that scene precisely because I'll be able to see it and Vee won't.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** New Year's Eve from Vee's side, alone in her childhood bedroom in Ohio (parents frugal, cold house, twin bed she outgrew). No word from Pace across the whole break; she drafts a text ("*are you*—") and deletes it. She scrolls photos: the couch selfie, the induction dress — and remembers she scissored the leg off the version she gave her mother while sending Pace the whole thing. She sends Randi a warm, undemanding text; Randi's curated "fairytale — never coming home" reply (which I know from last chapter is hollow) lands near midnight and Vee envies it wholesale, even knowing the money is dirty. Reaching for her best memory (the fra diavolo night, slipping sheet) to feel warm, her jealousy hijacks it and replaces her with an invented blonde rival. She recalls Pace cooking for Sheri ("she's alone") and that he said nothing about Christmas week, and can't tell fear from truth. Year turns; she has no one; she falls asleep still reaching, unable to name what she is to Pace.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee / Vivienne** — home in Ohio, in a room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her"; ruptured with Pace, no contact; starved for a life lived out loud; sees more than Randi thinks (knows the PPP money is dirty) and gets played anyway; shame and hunger fused; total reader sympathy.
- **Pace / Peter** — absent all break, "leaves you the room" (once tender, now abandonment); cooked for Sheri; silent about Christmas week; nothing proven against him — the menace is Vee's midnight fear, not fact.
- **Randi** — Vee's one confidante, "real things = mostly Pace"; her fairytale text is a curated lie Vee swallows whole; I carry from last chapter that she's decided to keep the brunches exactly as they are and never speak her real feeling; the false floor under Vee.
- **Sheri** — Pace's riding friend he fed at Thanksgiving; now a live jealousy-thread for Vee.
- **Cassie** — moral floor (offstage; took the induction photo).

**How I feel.** Dramatic irony at its most painful — I'm holding both New Year's POVs and Vee is standing on Randi as her "sure thing" while Pace reads as the blank. Deep sympathy for Vee; sharpened dread of Randi and of Pace's silence, all achieved by colliding the two chapters, not by the text pointing. Warm-on-top, cold-underneath, now with the warmth actively weaponized as a wound.

**Open questions.** Is the rupture over, and does Vee get managed back — likely *through* Randi at the first brunch? Did anything happen at Christmas / with Sheri, or is it all Vee's fear? Where's Randi's locked bracelet from? Still open: the scar, right-hand-only eating, sealed family, the weight loss, the nude photo, threesome timing. And the big structural one I keep circling: how exactly are Pace and Randi connected, and does Vee's PPP secret ever surface.
