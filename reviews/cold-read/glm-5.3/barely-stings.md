## Reader reaction

**Barely Stings — the felt read.**

This chapter got under my skin more than I expected it to. On paper it's an errand — a waxing appointment, thirty pages of a girl on a table — and instead it's one of the most inward chapters of the book, and one of the hottest in the strangest, most uncomfortable way.

What landed first was the waiting room. The two girls — one "assembling herself for a room to turn toward," one with "the loose warm look of a girl going home to someone" — and Vee sitting down between them and knowing which one she is. That's this whole book in a single image: desire done as private maintenance, the part nobody sees, for one set of hands. I believed her completely in that moment.

And then the chapter does something I felt almost ambushed by. Vee's arousal arrives in the *worst* place — "the wrong hand" section — and the book is honest enough to let it be disturbing and charged at once. The hand pressing her thigh, "Don't move," the grammar her body knows from Pace with "everything true scooped out of the middle" — that's the most precise, most unsettling erotic writing this book has done since Randi and the bottle cap. It's not the technician's touch that's hot; it's the hole it opens. "She lay under the wrong hand and ached for the right one and gave the woman nothing" — I read that line twice. The chapter is about fidelity, in a strange bodily register: keeping the lit part of herself "back behind her teeth where it belonged to him." Heat here is shame and longing fused, and it burned on me even though nothing in the room was sexy.

The friction — and it's real friction, deliberate I think — is Randi's omission. *Barely stings.* The title becomes an accusation. Randi sent her here with the name, the wine, the reassurance, and not one word about "roll over for me, hon." Vee notices it plainly: "the part Randi hadn't said." And crucially, the page doesn't tell me whether it was carelessness or design — Randi, who runs on knowing exactly what Vee will do and won't say, who watched the coat's lining bloom and said nothing, who said "I do" about the taste Vee called gross. I found myself not knowing whether to be angry at her, and I'm holding that open, because Vee doesn't fully close it either. She rolls over. "She was already so far past every line she'd have thought to draw that another line seemed like a joke." That's Vee's whole arc — her borders have moved so many times she's stopped being able to find them — and the chapter lets the unease sit without a verdict. Good. I don't want one yet.

Pace himself is offstage for almost the whole chapter and is somehow the largest presence in it. The flashback framing — "he always asked; that was the thing about him that undid her" — makes the contrast with Sondra's wordless competence do double work. Whatever Friday's surprise is, the chapter ends with the wanting aimed at him, and the last page — her own hand on the "bare astonishing smoothness," the sound "not one of pain" — tips from ordeal into genuine heat at last. The ache for his hand finding what she's done. That earned its swoon. I'm glad it ended warm; if it had ended on the table, this chapter would have been almost too cold to recover from.

Her mother's voice is back — "the kind of girl who" — the flat kitchen register that's been naming her shame since chapter one, and it "stayed where it was. It always did." That's the book's longest-running war, and it didn't win this time, but it didn't lose either. Vee pays with his card and walks out "the most expensive thing in the room, and no one in it knew but her" — which is Randi's phrase coming true, and I don't know whether to be comforted by that or notice that Randi's scripts keep becoming Vee's reality.

Trust check: no new suspicion of Pace — if anything the memory of him asking is the chapter's one steady warm thing. Randi I'm watching with one raised eyebrow now, the way I've been since "Things were really heated." Sheri is offstage and uncomplicated. And I'm noticing, gently, that Vee never texted Randi from the parking lot — the chapter ends with her walking out alone with it. First time in a while she's kept something.

**The title:** it's Randi's exact phrase — "Barely stings" — and the chapter takes it apart: it stings plenty, and the sting travels inward. A small betrayal wrapped in reassurance. Whether Randi meant it as one stays open. The volume — *A Place at the Table*, book three of *With a Long Spoon* — reads more ironic by the chapter: this was a table with paper pulled down it, and the price of the seat was paid in a currency Vee's mother would name and Vee is learning not to flinch at.

**Next:** Friday night, the surprise — his hands finding her. That's clearly where the heat is headed. And I dread/hope for the moment Vee tells Randi about the roll-over, because "Randi didn't mention it" is a sentence sitting in her pocket now, and I don't know if she'll spend it.
