## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

The Cassie half of this chapter undid me a little, and not for the reasons I expected. "That good?" and the grin that was "the entire story" — that's the friendship I trust most in this book doing its quiet work. But the moment that snagged me was the zipper. Vee reaching for it, drawing it halfway down, and stopping — because "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." That's the whole spine of this book in one gesture. She can give Cassie the dinner, the wine, even the cooking-after, but the middle she lifts out "clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole." The book used that ladle image before and it lands harder here, in her own room, three feet from the one person who's never tried to manage her. And Cassie "did not pull at the seam" — whether she felt it, "Vee couldn't have said." I believe Cassie felt it. That's who Cassie is on the page so far: the one who reads fast and whole and lets things stand. "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend," she says, "kindly, certainly, wrong" — wrong, and I love her for it.

And that half-second at the door — "something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name." The checkpoint's nagging "Goodbye, Vee" now has a sibling. Two flickers in an otherwise warm friendship. I can't read it and I won't pretend to.

Then the porch, and Randi — and this is where my unease sharpened even as the scene charmed me. The charm is real and it worked on me: "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one" — I'd want a friend who says that. The nails-and-toes interrogation, "you bought them hoping he'd give you no reason to keep them on" — that's the same relentless fond naming from the checkpoint, and it does Vee genuine good. "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." I felt that land in her, and in me.

But two things on this page kept me from melting all the way. First: "Randi had picked it. Randi picked all of them." Vee herself noticed, half a second behind, feeling "chosen, which was lit, which was a half-second behind." The book put the arrangement in Vee's own mouth and then let it slide past her. That's elegant and it made my stomach dip, because I know what she doesn't.

Second — and this is new, the thing this chapter earned fresh — the voice. "Something had come into Randi's voice over the last few minutes… the esses running long, the a's lifting, more of whatever it was than there had been when they sat down — and her eyes had gone liquid at the edges." Vee checks: one mimosa, mostly still in the glass. "So it wasn't that. Nothing came along to take its place… and Vee let the question go." The page showed me Randi *filling up* while she draws the love story out of her friend — while she makes Vee say "he was hungry. For me. In a way that made me feel… singular" about the man Randi herself has been sleeping with for a month. That's not nothing, and it's not drunk, and the chapter deliberately refused to name it. The checkpoint's "tight thing behind Randi's smile" just got a body: lengthened esses and liquid eyes. Whether it's wanting Vee, wanting what Vee has with *him*, or wanting the night she's engineering — I genuinely can't tell, and I love that the page won't tell me. That's the heat of this chapter for me, more than anything in the (elided) middle: the second press at the curb, Vee meeting it "and when Randi let her go she didn't look down the street." She's stopped scanning. That reads to me as trust growing on a foundation I know is false, and it aches.

The "old boyfriend" story sat wrong in a way I can't fully justify — I know Randi has a secret man, so any story she tells about men reads to me as cover, and "a boy in a hurry" told about Pace without naming him… my skin prickled. But I flag that honestly: the page didn't confirm anything; my suspicion is the checkpoint's, not this scene's.

Erotically this chapter runs on telling, not doing — and it works. The retold kitchen hour ("wrapped up in him, watching him cook," "nothing missing") is the warmest thing in the chapter, and Randi's nose for what Vee left out ("You went from taking your clothes off to watching him cook and left out everything in between") charges the elision itself. The mother's voice got one cameo and lost: "frosted-plum toenails her mother would have had a word for. The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." That's the arc I've been watching since the swimsuit, and it's landing.

Friction: very little. If anything, the chapter is so smooth it's almost suspicious — but that smoothness is the book's texture. One small hitch: Vee's "fear thread" ("how much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to") arrives and dissolves in a paragraph; I wanted it to last one beat longer.

**Title:** "Fed" — the food motif cresting. Fed by him (the dinner, the cooking at midnight), fed by her (Randi handing her feelings back clean, "the person you got to *tell*"), and the mimosa she prices and then doesn't ("the second one she did not price at all") — appetite reconciled, for one morning. It also, on second thought, has a small cold shine: who's feeding whom, and what is being fattened for what. Volume title *A Polite Invitation* — with the induction and the burgundy dress still ahead, "invitation" feels more and more like Pace's "may I" weaponized into architecture. I dread and want that night.

**Next:** I want the dress finished; I dread the moment Vee learns there was a plan — and I'm beginning to suspect (guess only) that Randi's unnamed surge in this scene is pointing at Vee herself, not just at the game.
