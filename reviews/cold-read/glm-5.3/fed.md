## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

I keep bracing for this book to let up on me and it doesn't. The whole first half of this chapter is the sun porch again — warm, chosen, mimosas — and I sat there enjoying it, genuinely, before the cold settled in from underneath: I know something Vee doesn't, and this chapter made me hold it while she handed over every piece of herself across a brunch table.

The gut-punch is structural and I felt it in my body: Randi's "sad little story" about the beautiful old boyfriend who was "so nervous, and in such a hurry, like the building's on fire." I don't buy that story for a second — her love life isn't barren, we've seen the bench — but what it's *doing* is worse than lying. It sets Vee's night against a bad one. "I only got dinner that night, dinner and a boy in a hurry, that was my whole evening. I'm running on fumes here." She is fishing details of Pace out of Vee's mouth — the patience, the no clock, "hungry for me," the midnight kitchen — and I watched her listen to her own lover praised by the girl he was aimed at, and I could not read her face from here. That's the ache of this chapter for me: is it appetite, is it surveillance for the plan, is it jealousy, is it all three braided? The page won't let me decide and Vee can't even see the question.

And yet — and this is where I have to be honest about the swoon — Randi is *good* at this, and parts of it landed as real warmth. "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." I believed her in that moment. And the moment after the teasing, when Vee's fear shows — "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky" — I felt Vee feel it, the word settling in and staying. Whatever else Randi is doing, she is also, in these beats, the friend Vee actually needed at fifteen and never got, and I felt how cruel it is that this is the person playing her. The toenail deduction ("You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands") worked on me the way it worked on Vee — that's a woman who knows, naming what Vee won't look at. The erotic charge of the chapter isn't in the room; it's in the *extraction*. Vee confessing the campaign — the new panties, "I wanted it, and I took it" — is a strip scene played at a brunch table, and Randi undressing her with questions has the same rhythm Pace's measuring did: closeness, a question, withdrawal, the next one. I noticed that rhyme and it made the back of my neck prickle.

The ending is the quiet escalation and it's the part that keeps me reading. The second kiss at the curb — the same two-press goodbye — and this time "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen us, the way she had the first time." That's Vee's shame reflex going quiet for Randi, the same way her self-watching dropped for Pace. She's being opened on two fronts by the same team and calls both openings by different names — "Pace was the thing the week bent around. Randi was the person you got to tell." That sentence is the most honest thing in the chapter and Vee has no idea what it's lining up.

Title: "Fed" — after the honeycrisp, "Let's go feed you," the froyo, the fra diavolo, this one names what Randi does at brunch: feeds Vee back her own wanting, named and forgiven. But feeding runs both ways here — Randi fed *on* it, and left "running on fumes" refilled. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels like it's still holding its promise — everything in this book is invitations, doors left open, "you don't have to tell me" — and the tagline's secret plan is confirmed and compounding. I dread the found-out moment more each chapter and want it more each chapter too. Guess from here: the induction, the dress, and Randi in the same room is where this converges.
