## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this one hurt in the best way. The whole chapter I was split in two — watching Vee float on her luck while knowing, from the bench and the pointing game, exactly what's under it — and the book kept tightening that screw without ever breaking the surface.

Randi. My feeling about her has gotten *complicated*, and this chapter moved it again. After "The Bench" I was chilled by her — the bathroom mirror, the deadbolt, the face going back up "like a key turning in a lock." But here she's genuinely warm, and the warmth is doing double work I can't fully separate. When she takes Vee's hand on the white table and says "Vee's my dear friend," I *meltted* — and I also sat there counting the cost she'd spent, because I know why she chose this girl. I watched her pick Vee on the quad ("Her," Pace said, and pointed — and Randi said *yes* with nothing around it). So when Randi holds her hand in front of Brooke, is that affection, is that possession, is that the game moving toward Pace's ends? I genuinely can't tell, and — this is the impressive thing — I don't think the page wants me to be able to. The line that got me most: "she had spent it on her without appearing to count it." Even Vee knows it cost something. Neither of them can say what.

And the dramatic irony in the date-report scene is almost unbearable. Vee telling Randi about Pace — *May I choose*, the little brass cages, "I stopped keeping track of myself" — and Randi saying "You let him choose for you. And you liked it," holding it up to the light. I read that line twice. Randi knows *exactly* what it means to be chosen for by this man; she spent a night on his bench finding out. Her "Mm," warm, going no further — is she recognizing him, or recruiting Vee? I held my breath. Same with her catastrophe story: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table... is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." That's Randi talking *about Pace without naming him*, laying the exact pitch for Vee — or it's just a story about a lacrosse boy, told the way she tells stories. The book leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but my skin was up the whole scene.

The swoon is real, though, and I won't let suspicion eat it. Vee being picked out loud — "the thing settle in her that settles when someone picks you out loud in a roomful of people" — landed hard, because she's the girl who made herself small at Chi Latte, who keeps the cardigan over the better shirt, who went under the water to be nobody. The Smoothie Bird scene got me in the gut before Brooke arrived: "she had been set up on a shelf," the light going out of her in the doorway. Her whole interior economy — being the most of what the room is selling and the one with the most fabric on — is so precisely *my* remembered thing that I forgive the book a lot.

Cassie's exit stung: "I'll see you at the dorm. Easy. Already going." And Vee explaining it away — "it isn't really her place" — while "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." Cassie's the one who saw Pace look at Randi "the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt," and the only person who asks the flat true questions. Vee drifting from her toward the bright room worries me, and I think it's meant to.

Erotic charge: low, and correctly so — this is a wanting chapter, not a having chapter, but the wanting is charged: Vee recounting the dinner, the names he said low, "things I couldn't have read out loud," made *me* flushed by proxy. Randi's knee-knock, the damp cool hand — the heat is all sideways and it works.

Friction: minimal. Randi's dialogue occasionally tips a shade performative for me — "I refuse to be responsible with any of them" is a hair writerly — but she's a performer by nature, so it holds. The taller silent friend with the coffee is a touch stock; I'll wait to see if she returns.

The title — **"Dear."** Before the chapter, nothing; now it's *dear friend*, the two words Randi chose in front of witnesses, warm and weighted and costing something. Given the cover line — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — "dear" reads as both the salutation of a letter and the thing you call someone you're fond of while keeping them at letter-length. The invitation framing now feels double too: Pace's "May I choose?" was a polite invitation, and whatever's assembling around Vee is another one, politely extended through Randi's hand. I don't know who's holding the long spoon yet, but this chapter made me feel the table being set.

What I dread and want next: I want Vee to find out — and I dread the shape of finding out, because the bench chapter taught me what Pace's attention *costs*. And I dread Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" retroactively. My guess from here: Vee ends up at the house off six-fourteen, and the pointing game's "yes" comes due.
