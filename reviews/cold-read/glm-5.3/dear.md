## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

This chapter felt like the book breathing out. After the dark of the bench and the cold of the sorority common room, "Dear" is almost entirely daylight and warmth — and I felt how much I'd been starved for that alongside Vee.

The bunk-bed scene with Cassie is the best thing here. Cassie's "How much did he give you to drink?" and Vee's indignant "*Nothing* — we had tea" made me laugh out loud, and it's doing real work: Cassie checking for the wrong kind of surrender and being satisfied it was the right kind. Vee's clumsy spin in the bean bag — "the room too small to hold what she had to do with it" — is the happiest single moment in the book so far, and the first time I've seen her joy be *hers* and not watched. I love Cassie more every time she appears; her deadpan dossier questions ("Is he rich? Rich people hike.") are exactly how a real friend loves you. And there's a small ache under it: Vee declining the smoothie place for Cassie ("it isn't really her place") reads kind on the surface, but the something that "turned over, small, and she let it lie" — Cassie getting folded away from Randi's world — landed as the chapter's quiet warning. I don't trust that nothing will come of it.

The smoothie scene tightened my chest. The room "full of girls who had all gotten dressed in the same room" put Vee right back into the Chi Latte dynamic — the cardigan "covering her like an apology," the high shelf of the window seat — and the arrival of Brooke hit me with real dread, because *I know what Brooke is*. Vee doesn't. Vee experiences her as "deep water… back in the boat," and the horror is that the text let me watch Brooke file her: "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over." Randi's "Vee's my dear friend," hand held on the table — that moved me, genuinely. It's the most generous thing Randi has done in daylight in the whole book, and I read it as Randi spending capital on Vee partly because she wants to. The irony is dizzying: Randi knows exactly what she and Pace are planning for this girl, and here she is publicly claiming her as *dear*.

Which is where the pull curdles for me. Vee's telling of the dinner is heartbreaking in retrospect — she narrates "May I choose?" and "I stopped watching myself" as the purest romance, having no idea that Randi is feeding all of it to Pace. "Because of him," she says, agreeing with Randi's framing when she'd meant to say "I made an exception" — she keeps letting her own story be edited into smaller, truer-seeming shapes, and it's beautiful and it's being harvested. "He'd ask, though… before he ravished me" went through me — her trust in his asking is real and earned, and it's exactly what Pace and Randi are counting on.

Heat-wise this is a low chapter and it doesn't need to not be; the charge lives entirely in Randi's one dropped register — "Ask and then ravish… the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume" — and that single low line did more to me than pages of the bench did. The title lands as Randi's public claim: *dear friend*. It also echoes the letter-like intimacy of the whole book's voice. I'm braced for the hike.
