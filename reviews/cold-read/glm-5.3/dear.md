## Reader reaction

The chapter title, "Dear," only makes sense at the end — or rather, it detonates in one line: "Vee's my dear friend." For the whole chapter it's a blank, a word on a door, and then Randi spends it out loud in front of Brooke, and I understood the title had been pointing at that word the whole time. What stung is that Vee feels it as *cost* — "she knew only, without being able to say how, that it had cost something" — and the reader knows the exact currency. Randi outing their friendship to her chapter president, the woman who is filing away "Pace" and his white pickup, is not just a warm gesture. Brooke heard the name Vee. Brooke saw Randi's hand on hers. The chess board just got another piece on it, and Vee handed it over herself, glowing, feeling *picked*.

And that's the double edge of this whole chapter for me, because it worked on me even as it chilled me. The smoothie scene is the warmest Vee-and-Randi chapter yet — the laughing, the "no bra is not an option for me," the shared shelf up on the high chairs — and I fell for it exactly as Vee did. But two things landed under it. First, the catastrophe story: Randi invents a bad French-restaurant date for Friday night — the night we know she was at Pace's, or at least the night of the bench-adjacent secret life — and tells it fluently, "like a story she had told before and would tell again." That's the face working. She lies to Vee the way she breathes, and the lie is *good*, and it doubles as a nudge: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you... is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She's selling Vee the exact thing Pace does best. Second, the reframe: Vee arrives with her own sentence — "I made him stop. And I kissed him. Me. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That sentence was hers; it was the one true thing she owned. And Randi takes it apart — "You kissed him... Because you couldn't not. Because of him" — and Vee, who "had the dignified version sitting right there ready," gives up the smaller, truer-*feeling* thing. Except it isn't truer. "I couldn't not" is Randi's cosmology, not Vee's. Vee's agency got quietly rewritten at a smoothie table, warmly, with a hand on hers. That unset me more than anything Brooke did.

Where it genuinely swooned for me: "Ask and then ravish." That beat — Randi's reply coming "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume" — is the hottest moment in the chapter, and nothing physical happens. Vee lets her own picture run, her hands running down her thighs, her voice going down after it, and Randi drops into it *with* her. The reader-advantage makes it burn: we know what Randi is imagining, and it isn't only Vee and Pace on a trail. That "beat late" is the hum in her ribs showing through. Also the oat milk — tiny, perfect characterization: Vee orders what Randi orders, "and felt faintly that she had said the oat milk because Randi was standing right there, and let herself not look at that either." That's the chapter's engine in one sip. Vee *let herself not look at that either* twice here — the oat milk, the turning-over thought about Cassie — and the book is patiently cataloguing everything she declines to examine.

Brooke landing in person: colder and more finished than I'd built her from the walk of shame. "The taller one's gaze finished its tour of Vee and handed back nothing but more smile" — that's a woman who processes rooms. And the tall silent one with the coffee is new, unnamed, doing slow surveillance; I don't know who she is and I don't trust her. "We should all get dinner sometime, which was lovely, and meant nothing, and was plainly the end of it" — perfect. But I also noticed Brooke's "We missed you Thursday" setting down "the fact of a Thursday Vee knew nothing about" — the sorority world asserting itself, the same world where facts about "Pace" are being filed. Randi's two lives are drifting toward each other, and Vee is standing exactly where they'll collide.

Cassie hurt me a little, and I think it's earned, not manufactured: her look going *past* Vee to Randi and being "folded away" before it comes back. Vee half-notices — "something turned over, small, and she let it lie." The "Goodbye, Vee" flatness from chapters ago is compounding: Cassie sees something about Randi ("she's not serious," per the checkpoint) and Vee keeps explaining her away. The smoothie dismissal — "it isn't really her place... not her thing" — is Vee sorting her people, and something in me winced at it.

Heat is low and that's fine — this is a warmth-and-unease chapter, and its charge is all in the "ask and then ravish" beat. Friction: minimal; the professor's opening is a nice wry frame, and the pacing of the Brooke interruption is exact. One small thumb: "two small skies where her eyes should be" is a lovely line but the chapter is generous with Randi's radiance in a way that sometimes feels like the book loving her a little more than the scene needs. Otherwise I bought all of it.
