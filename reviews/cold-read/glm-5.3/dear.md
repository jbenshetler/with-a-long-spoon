## Reader reaction

The opening of this chapter gave me the purest happiness the book has offered so far. Vee bursting into the dorm room still warm from the date, Cassie pulled up out of her chair by it, the two of them on the bunk while Vee tells the kiss — "I pulled him back and I did it. Me." That *Me* is the whole arc of the last three chapters landing in one syllable. And Cassie's "How much did he give you to drink?" followed by Vee's indignant *Nobody did anything to me. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose* — that's the exact sentence I didn't know I was waiting to hear her say. The author keeps giving Vee agency as a thing she discovers *about herself*, not something conferred, and I love watching it register. The clumsy spin that nearly takes out the closet door made me laugh out loud.

But I'm uneasy, and the text earned it twice. First: Brooke. She was the warm interrogation in the sorority chapter — "Pace. That's actually his name?" — and now here she is in the Smoothie Bird with her smile "arriving a moment ahead of her," and the exactness of it going "wider by some amount" when it lands on Vee, taking her measure "and stay[ing] warm the whole way down." Warmth as instrument. Vee reads it perfectly from the top of every high-school table: "Randi had just spent something on her, in front of the girls who decided what it was worth." And Randi *spent* it — "Vee's my dear friend," the cool damp hand held in plain view, no hurry. That landed on me two ways at once: it's genuinely moving, no one has chosen Vee out loud like that in a long time, and it's also Randi performing the choosing in front of an audience, spending capital rather than simply feeling a feeling. I don't know which it is, and I don't think the chapter wants me to settle it. The taller silent one doing a "slow, easy circuit of the table. Of Vee" — that's Sloane, I'd guess, from the Saturday-morning scene — was a small cold finger on my neck.

Second, and quieter: the chapter keeps asking me to watch Vee be managed, and she keeps not noticing, and that's beginning to ache. The drink order — beet and wheatgrass and egg white, "a drink the color of a bruise" ordered "as though it were her usual" — while Vee's own apple-math heartbreak sits in my memory from the bench chapter. Randi telling the story of the boring French date and the listening man, studying Vee's story as it's told: "You let him choose for you... And you liked it," held up to the light, filed. Randi's "ask and then ravish," arriving "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume" — that told me more about Randi than anything since "Do you think she'll like the bench?" She's not just listening to Vee's news; she's *collecting* it. She was there in the coffee shop when Pace walked in; I know, and Vee doesn't, that the man with the thick knuckles and the *may I* is already in Randi's bed. Watching Vee say "Because of him" — watching her give Randi the truer, smaller version of the kiss — while Randi holds that knowledge and smiles the full wattage, is the most uncomfortable pleasure in the book so far.

The small tell I keep turning over: Vee deflecting Cassie from the smoothie place. "It isn't really her place... Not her thing." And the narration handing me, almost offhand, "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." Cassie — the one who said *goodbye* instead of *see you later*, who read Pace's look at Randi like a great-aunt, who folded away whatever was in her eyes — is being slid out of the frame, and Vee is doing some of the sliding herself, comfortably. And then the follow-up beat: "Vee, who had come in lit, felt some of the light go out of her in the doorway without understanding why." The chapter has two Vees in it — the girl spinning in the bean bag, and the girl going "very slightly the wrong size for the room" — and the prose holds both without resolving either.

What still has me completely: Vee's warmth is real, all the way down. "She felt picked. She carried it the whole way home." I believe her being happy even while I flinch at what she can't see, and the book's title — *A Polite Invitation* — is starting to hum for me. *May I choose?* *May I call you?* The politeness is the seduction, on Pace's side at least. The chapter title, "Dear," — I read it first as the salutation of a letter, and after Brooke's exit line — "We should all get dinner sometime... which was lovely, and meant nothing" — and after "Vee's my dear friend," it's gone double: *dear* as in cherished, and *dear* as in a price being set. Something is being arranged around Vee by people with more information than she has. Next chapter is, I'd guess, the hike — the trail, the "real shoes," whatever Pace has in mind out past the gravel on six-fourteen. I dread it and want it in equal measure, which I suspect is the point of the whole book.
