# Cold read (grounded) — All Told

## Reader reaction

Vee walks into stats "fit to burst" and I walked in with her — that's the whole chapter's engine and it's a sweet one. This is a morning-after chapter, daylight edition, and the book does something I didn't expect after the heat of the dress chapter: it lets the happiness just *sit* in a fluorescent classroom. Two girls in one room, both hers, and Vee turned between them "happy in the middle." I felt that. The book has been building toward Vee having a place — "supposed to be there," ch 15 — and this is the first chapter where belonging is completely ordinary, no ceremony, just a seat left open two-thirds back.

Cassie got me again. "Your bed's made. It's the same bed you made. You didn't come home." — that's Cassie in four words and one eyebrow, and then the quiet gut-punch: she never looked at the professor once, because "Cassie did not care about the dress. Cassie did not care about any dress." The only part of any of it she has ever been able to see is Vee. That landed harder for me than the sex chapter did, in its way. "Was it a good night?" and Vee's unplanned "Oh, yes" — and *something eased in Cassie's face*. Whatever Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee" was back in chapter 4, this chapter quietly reassures me the bond is real and watchful in the best way. She's the one person in Vee's life with no angle.

Randi, though — Randi is the complication, and this chapter sharpened my unease while still charming me. "You lucky slut," low and delighted, is exactly the register of the velvet booth: intimate, pushing, fond. But I caught myself noticing *what* she does with the conversation. Vee's one snag in the gold — "I don't have shoes" — and Randi has her by the wrist, "the problem already solved in her hands": a Saturday shopping date, hers, "Don't you dare tell me no." On the surface this is friendship at its most generous. Underneath — and I hold this loosely, because the page doesn't say — Saturday is Randi steering again. The plan moved her into the seat next to Vee; now the plan's next motion may be moving her through a shoe store, close as a sister, toward whatever the induction night is for. And the "slut" landed lower than Randi meant, right on the body keeping "the night's account" — and Randi doesn't know she touched it, or does she? That's what I can't read about her, same as ever.

Simpson's paradox is the chapter's little spine and I loved it, uncomfortable as it made me. Each year on its own, the first man hits better; put the two years together and the second man does. Same numbers, nobody touched one of them. The book has been doing this with latent variables since chapter 4, but this one feels like it's pointed somewhere sharper: every individual scene of Vee's courtship is true and good on its own — the apple slices, the flannel, the dress. Aggregate them across the whole scheme, and the picture reverses. That's my dread taking shape, and the professor put it on a screen in a cereal-box voice while Vee "gave it a good forty seconds, honest attention, her pen even moving" and didn't see herself on the slide. Neither does the reader get to *prove* anything — that's the trap's elegance.

Heat: this chapter has no sex but it has heat in one specific pocket — "slut" landing on a body still sore "down low, and up the inside of both thighs, and in her nipples where they sat against her bra." For a second she's sure the whole room can read it off her. That's the morning-after rendered as charge, shame crossing into warmth on the same old wire, and it worked on me. Everywhere else the chapter is warmth, not heat, and that's fine — it's an afterglow chapter and the afterglow is genuine. No friction worth naming, honestly; maybe Randi's Saturday grab is *slightly* frictionless as plotting, but it's in character down to the heel height "Vee would need and hadn't known to need."

The title, "All Told" — I read it first as Vee telling them *all* of it (she doesn't; the dress is told, the night is told by implication, and the deepest things stay folded). But after the lecture it recolors: all *told* — the totals, the aggregated data, the years put together. What happens when you put everything together. And it sits right on top of the volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, which still hasn't shown its hand — unless the induction is the invitation, or unless Randi's Saturday is. The last line stings a little in a good way: the dress "still shown to not one living soul." It's hers and Pace's secret still, zipped in a closet, and everything it's for is coming. Next I expect the Saturday shoe date with Randi — my guess from here is that it turns out to be more than shoes — and I dread the first moment Vee's two worlds see each other in one room. But I'll keep reading exactly because the book has never once let me be sure whether it's being tender or setting a table.
