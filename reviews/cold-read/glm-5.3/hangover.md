This one got me. After a chapter of Sheri's loud warmth, "Hangover" opens quiet — a glass of water on a folded paper towel so it wouldn't mark the wood — and then keeps handing me small proofs of care (the bread that "had not existed when the world ended last night") while I wait for what the chapter is actually about. When it came, it landed harder than I expected. Pace saying "Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else" — I sat with Vee in that stillness, doing the same refusal to let it rearrange into something recognizable.

The tenderness here is almost unbearable in a dry-eyed way. Not the drinking, not even the caretaking — the moment Vee understands that when Daphne left, "the one had been all of it, and then the one was gone, and there was nobody left in the world to take the heartbreak to." That line went through me. Everything I've learned about him in fragments — the scar he won't explain, *less than staying*, no home since sixteen, the mother's name replaced with his own — slides together here without ever being spelled out, and the book trusts me to do the arithmetic with Vee. The Daphne material especially: Vee holding "the ugly grown-up word she kept pressing back down" without the text ever saying it. That restraint is the chapter's best move. It lets him keep his two good years as *his* truth while I hold the wrong shape of it alongside, "both true at once," and that doubled vision hurts in a way a confrontation never would.

His explanation — women misread the friendship, men make friends by competing, the window "on a timer" — reads completely true to him, no self-pity, the good order itself "the tell." Vee's response is exactly right too: no sorry, just the Sheri-hug, "whole weight committed." That callback to the taproom hug gave me a little ache.
