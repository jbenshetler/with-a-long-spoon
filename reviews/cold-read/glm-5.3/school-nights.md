## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

A quiet chapter after three hot ones, and I felt the book breathe out. It's Cassie and Vee in the room with the cracked window, and it's the first time in a while the story has handed Vee to the one person who wants nothing from her wanting. I read the whole scene warm — the streetlight teasing, "You're a public utility," the desk chair turned backward, the wordless flood. Vee drunk on weeks of him and pouring it out to the friend who takes it with "no face on at all." That's the Cassie I trust most in this book, and seeing her hold all that without flinching made me love her more.

But it also landed as a small ache. "You don't come home school nights." Five words and the chapter's title arrived — not a scandal, just an accounting. Vee's own bed sitting empty more often than not, her whole kit colonizing his bathroom drawer, and now this. The book keeps showing me the migration in increments: the flannel, the bag, the drawer, and now the empty room. Cassie says it kindly — "I don't get it. You've had boyfriends. You never went like this over one" — and that's the truest observation anyone in the book has made to Vee's face. Vee reaching for the word after "fine" and not finding it, laughing at herself reaching — that's her interiority exactly: she has the love (she confessed it to a sleeping man), and she can't hand it to her best friend except as "Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast."

That "it's all so fast" is where my unease flickered, because I hold what Cassie doesn't: it isn't fast at all. It's been designed slow and fed to her carefully for months, and every warm thing she listed to Cassie — the noticed burn, the warm house, the cooking — is also a mechanism. The burn on the side of her breast from the tomato: the detail that she's not wearing much in his kitchen now, delivered to Cassie as sweetness and to me as the shape of a habit being built. I noticed her hand go to the spot "without her knowing it moved." Her body tells on her even to Cassie, and Cassie catches it — "Not enough on" — dry as anything.

Cassie not pushing past the maybe felt like restraint and also like the drift the checkpoint keeps marking: Vee edits for Cassie even here. She gave Cassie the cooking and the burn and none of the middle — no spanking, no bet, no bathroom stall, no Randi-shaped wanting she won't name. "Maybe. I think so" to Cassie; the whole fire to Randi. I felt that asymmetry more sharply in this quiet room than I have in a while.

No suspicion of Cassie; my wariness stays with the plan, and this chapter didn't feed it directly — but "school nights" as a title frames the whole thing as a semester being consumed, and I felt the clock under the sweetness. Pace is unnamed here entirely, which I liked; he arrives only as effects — cooking, a cold cloth, a warm house. He's becoming weather to her.

Heat is low but not absent — the burn detail, the hand moving unbidden, "never once thought about how much I had on." Romance, though, is real and high: this is Vee's first spoken approximation of love, and hearing it come out "Maybe. I think so" in the safe room moved me.

What I want next: more Cassie, honestly; and I dread the night the "maybe" has to survive the truth of the lawn. Guess from here only.
