## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

God, this chapter. The dress in the window did it to me before anyone touched anyone — "the burgundy lit from inside itself and the plum beneath it deepening, a wine-dark glow with a shadow alive in it" — and then the detail that got to *her* got to me right through her: "that he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That's the whole man in one observation, and the whole book too, if I'm honest — everything crafted to look like it arrived finished. I cried with her and didn't mind.

What moved me most isn't even the sex — it's the three sentences where she stands at the mirror and the mother's voice fires and *loses*. "She looked like a lot of woman who knew exactly how much she was." After chapters of the apologizing stoop, the suit that gave her nowhere to hide, the "floozie" running on the same wire as the heat — this is the payoff, and it's earned. And then the underwear. She sees the pale edge cutting the line, and her first thought is "keep your steps small" — the old program — and the second thought "came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed," and she takes them off *herself*, and "she was the one who'd taken the edge away." That's the arc of this entire book in one gesture: ch 8's "I'm making an exception," ch 18's "I wanted it, and I took it," and now this. The shame crossing into wanting "almost before it was shame at all" — the wire, finally rewired.

The sex itself is the warmest and funniest the book has been. Standing-up sex has no business being this charming, but the logistics *are* the romance: "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," and him twitching in her hands at his own line, twice, and her laughing so hard he has to stop undressing her. I believed every second of it because it's the first time the sex has had comedy in it — the first night was sacred and shame-wrung; this one is two people who like each other's bodies and each other's jokes. The finger-cleaning — him studying her slick fingers "with the care he'd give a hem" before taking them into his mouth, eyes on hers — went straight through my knees along with hers. That's the hottest beat of the chapter and it's fully clothed-in-spirit, all attention.

Character feelings: Vee I adore without reservation, and she's *deepening* — the daydream at the end is new, an appetite for spectacle, "getting away with something," and it sits a little oddly beside the woman who scanned the windows at Randi's kiss. I don't distrust it; I just notice the want is growing a public dimension. Pace, in this chapter, is almost pure warmth to me — the kid-grin he can't keep in, the staying-up-all-night, the arm coming around her in his sleep. My suspicion of him is entirely from the checkpoint, and the page gives me nothing new to feed it; he left the afternoon to her, "whether, and when." And the dress itself — "cut to the actual shape of her, and to no one else's" — is the plan's gift and the plan's bait in one object, and the book still refuses to separate those. That's the unease under all this gold, and I felt it most at "no seam of effort showing on it anywhere" — because *I* know where the effort came from and what it's for. Randi is absent here, and I noticed her absence like a held breath; the induction the dress is *for* is her territory.

Friction: almost none. If anything, "the small place the tears came from" is a phrase the book has leaned on before and it leaned again. And I briefly wondered at her confidence that he'd "been up the night before" — but it's presented as a thing she knows without being told, intuition, and I'll let it stand. The ending daydream is doing a lot of quiet work and I trust it.

The title: "Peekaboo" — the slit, obviously, the leg given and taken back and given; also the game the whole book plays with what's hidden (the plan, her wanting, the closure with "no seam"). And his surprise, can't-keep-it-in — peekaboo, *there it is*. I like it; it recolors on second thought from playful to faintly ominous, given what I know about games. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation* — Book One of *With a Long Spoon* — reads truer by the chapter: "may I" as seduction method, and the spoon saying slowly that supper with the devil needs a long one. The invitation keeps getting more delicious and I keep accepting it.
