## Reader reaction

The dress chapter. Of course it's the dress chapter — he's been building toward this since the bolt of cloth, and I've been building toward it with her, and the payoff is everything I wanted and a little sweeter than I expected.

Where it landed hardest: the hanging dress in the window, "the burgundy lit from inside itself," and the line about him hiding the trouble so "the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like no trouble at all." That's him in one sentence — the same man who turned the heat up hours before she arrived. And then the mirror. "She looked like a lot of woman who knew exactly how much she was." I felt her shoulders come back with mine. The moment where *floozie* flares and she stays and looks anyway — that's the whole arc of this book turning over, the shame still arriving on schedule but no longer closing her. That earned every beat of it.

The comedy of the middle section genuinely surprised me. "Don't point down" / "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me" — the logistics of standing sex while protecting a handmade silk gown is the least glamorous thing in the world, and the book just... lets it be clumsy and funny, and it made the heat better, not worse, because they're laughing *together* in the middle of wanting each other. The finger-cleaning beat is where the charge peaked for me — "took two of them into his mouth... with his eyes on hers the whole while" — because it's him turning her shame-object (her own slickness, the evidence) into a thing he treats with a hem's care. "You're bad," she says, barely. Yes.

And the underwear moment — she takes them off *herself*, unprompted, so the slit runs clean. That's hers. Nobody asked. The line "she was the one who'd taken the edge away" is the whole book in miniature. I swooned harder at that than at any of the sex.

Randi isn't in this chapter, but she haunts it for me. I just watched her leave Pace's bed with her back to him, having asked "How long, Pace?" and poured her own wine — the first crack in the arrangement — and now Vee is getting the dress, the house, the everything, the exact gifts Randi's world is built to hand out. That Vee ends the chapter "getting away with something" while Randi doesn't even know the dress exists gave the happiness a shadow it didn't ask for. I don't think that's my paranoia; the last two chapters earned it — the untouched bowl, "It's selfish, Pace."

Trust check: nothing about Pace feels off. His one suspicious note — "he'd been up the night before" finishing the dress — is devotion, not manipulation, as far as the page shows. My real unease is about the *structure* Vee is inside, and it's unease she can't feel yet: she still doesn't know Randi and Pace planned her from the start. Every perfect gift arrives to a woman who doesn't know she was hunted. I'm not on guard about Pace the man; I'm on guard about the plot, and the page has kept that door open.

Friction: very little. If anything, the induction daydream runs a touch long, and "nothing left for either of them to decide" is a phrase the book has leaned on before — it worked again, but I noticed it. The "Ow. Don't point down" exchange I'd have cut a line from, maybe. Small.

**Title:** "Peekaboo" — the slit. The dress that shows and conceals and shows again with every step; the game of hiding and being found that Vee's whole sexuality runs on. It also reads, a little uneasily, as the book's game itself — things hidden from Vee, revealed to the reader. Second thought, it's cleverer than it first seemed. Volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels increasingly literal: every chapter of Pace's courtship is exactly that, an invitation phrased so the yes is hers. I'm braced for the invitation that isn't polite — whatever's coming between him and Randi.

Next: I want the induction, the dress in that room, the looking that doesn't cost her. I dread Randi finding out how far it's gone — the "tell me" that didn't come in *Portion* is going to come back as something colder. Guess only.
