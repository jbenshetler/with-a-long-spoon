## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

I came into this one still carrying the chill of "Portion" — Randi's untouched bowl, her eyes shut, the *tell me* that never came — and this chapter opens the drapes on all of it. The sun through silk was almost too much at first; I felt the whiplash of it. And then I stopped fighting, because the chapter earns every ounce of gold it pours. Pace *grinning*, "helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth one more second" — I've read him as contained for ten chapters, and this is the first time I've seen him genuinely boyish, and it undid me before the dress even did. When she put her hand over her mouth and said "You made this," wet, I was wet-eyed with her. The detail that got in: "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That's him all the way down — and it's also, I notice with a small cold flicker, exactly the shape of the game he and Randi are running on her. The same virtue and the same method. The book keeps handing me tenderness and machinery made of the same material, and I felt that doubling here more sharply than in the fitting chapter.

The heat is the highest the book has taken me. Not because it's the most explicit — "Famished" was — but because this is the first time the sex is *funny*. "Ow. Don't point down." I laughed out loud. The whole standing relay — the silk to protect, the elbows fending, the dress at half-mast while she laughs too hard to be undressed — is the most human sex scene I've read in this book, and the laughing made it hotter, not cooler. And the finger-cleaning beat went through my knees along with hers: "he took two of them into his mouth… with his eyes on hers the whole while." That's the hottest single moment in the chapter for me, and it's him being careful — checking her slick-covered hands before she touches the silk he made her. Care and heat fused into one gesture. That's this book at its best.

Vee deepens enormously here, and mostly through her own choices: she takes the underwear off *herself*. After chapters of her body "answering on its own" and her scrambling to hide the evidence, the turn — "she was the one who'd taken the edge away… the shame of it went through her hot, and it crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all" — landed as the real climax of the book so far, bigger than any orgasm in it. The slit opening to the hip and her deciding what shows. She *strode* to him. I've been waiting chapters for that stride and I punched the air.

My unease is real but it's all in what I know and she doesn't. "Getting away with something," the last line, is lovely — and also exactly what Randi and Pace are doing, and what Randi is increasingly *not* getting away with. Laying Vee's glow directly against Randi's cracked-cold dinner makes both read harder: I can't fully trust Pace's sweetness knowing he went from that bed, from a woman who wouldn't eat his food, to finishing this dress — and I can't tell whose thread the dress was finished on. The mirror "newly hung, the screw heads still bright" caught my eye and I'm holding it open — set up for her, or for something else? I don't know. One line did snag me as a reader: "It just felt like what she was for." Being looked at as *purpose* — that's the phrase I'd want this book to interrogate someday, and I suspect it will. Also the plum toes on his chest are now an answering image to Randi's blue toenails in her heels in chapter one, and that rhyme made me shiver pleasantly and unpleasantly at once.

The title: "Peekaboo" — at first I thought it was just the slit. Now I think it's the book's whole method said out loud. The bench hidden in the table, the dress with no findable closure, the effort hidden inside the gift, Randi's face over her selves, Vee's shame converting to showing — everything here is a thing revealed that was always there. Pace couldn't keep the surprise in; the book is about what happens when everyone finally can't. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, reads truer every chapter — "may I" is the whole seduction, dinner and the fitting and this afternoon all invitations politely extended. The series title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps its menace: you sup with something with a long spoon, and I now believe the thing being supped with is each other.

What I want next: the induction, in that dress, the room turning — and I dread it, because public debut means Randi adjacent, sorority eyes, and Randi is cracking. I have no suspicion of Vee or Pace *in themselves* — everything "off" is off in the architecture I know and they half-know. I'd guess (guess only) that the dress is going to do work neither of its makers intended.
