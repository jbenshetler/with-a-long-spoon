## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter braced for the blonde. Vee did too — that's the whole engine of the first page, and the book had been loading that gun since Christmas, when Vee built a rival out of a name and fed her every midnight ("I built her on the nights I felt stupid"). So when Pace said "Sheri" at the sink and the cold "arrived anyway, unscheduled, a train she'd been told was cancelled," my stomach did exactly what hers did. And then the demolition, when it came, was pure joy: five foot nothing, permed, crooked makeup, a longneck already parked — "The blonde didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand." I laughed out loud. The better trick is that the jealousy doesn't die with the blonde; "the heat in Vee's chest stayed exactly where it was. It had never needed the blonde" — it was his voice being glad at a woman's name. That's honest. That's how it actually works.

And then Sheri looked at Vee — that long, unhurried trip down the keyhole and the bare leg and the heels, arriving at an appreciative *oh* — and Vee understood it was *enjoying* her, and the laugh came out of me almost as hard as out of her. I've been watching women appraise Vee all book — Randi's frank once-overs, Kayla's nosiness — but Sheri's look is the first one with no math in it, and Vee standing in the wreckage of her own armory ("a girl in a short skirt in the dead of winter, armed sole to collar against a woman she had invented") is my favorite thing this chapter. The borrowed keyhole shirt, the induction heels on a taproom floor — she dressed for a duel and got adopted.

Pace blushing. I need to register what that did to me, because it did a lot. "And you get to motorboat them things?" — and the color going up past anything deniable — I have watched this man be easy in every room this book has put him in, easy turning women around a dance floor, easy saying "Vee's boyfriend" six times, and I have never once seen him blush. Vee's right that it's the single most disarming thing he's done. And it recolors everything Sheri tells us after: this loud little woman holds "some license to him she'd never seen issued to anyone." She's the one person he tells things to — "a philosophy. Of his *food*" — and the warmth that went through me at that was complicated by a small pinch for Vee, hearing that he goes on about her somewhere she isn't. She felt it; I felt it with her.

Then the middle of this chapter just reached in and moved things. Sheri's coming-out story — the tablecloth come off and nobody catching a dish, "the ones who owed me words weren't spending any" — is the plainest, least-performed grief this book has given me, and it landed because Sheri doesn't decorate it. And what it reveals about Pace is the thing I've been circling for forty-nine chapters: he drove her home without one question, came back with donuts, fixed the lock, stopped the faucet, bought the burger, and "never handed her one false word — no *they'll come around*, no *their loss*." That is exactly the man who said words are cheap to Vee in December and made her walk out. Seeing his refusal to promise anything revealed as the same discipline that made him the only person who didn't lie to Sheri — that's not an excuse for the winter, but it's the first time the refusal has looked like it costs him too.

And then, quiet as anything: "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen." My whole body went still with Vee's. Two years of Sheri's friendship and that's every word of it she's got — which means Pace gave her that much and no more, the scar still sealed behind it. I notice the book still hasn't shown me the scar's origin, and I'm content to wait; a door respected this long is worth opening slowly.

The ending undid me a little. The headlights held on Sheri's door until the porch light answered — "the exact same easy unannounced way he apparently did everything, everywhere, for everyone he keeps, and had never once mentioned" — and Vee riding home watching his hands at ten and two, "like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling." I felt enormous and quiet, the way she did watching Cassie talk to him at the roadhouse. This is a man whose love is all practice and no declaration, and this chapter let me see the practice from the outside for the first time.

One held breath as a reader: I know from December that Sheri knows Pace has "two of them at once." Vee doesn't. All night Sheri mentioned only "the redhead" — Randi never came up, and the folder shut "no seam." I don't know if that's mercy or just unfinished business, but I felt the absence, and I'm watching for it.

No suspicion of anyone this chapter — that's worth saying plainly. The heat was almost nil and I didn't miss it; the one crackle (the look, the motorboat line) is garnish on a chapter about something else. The title, "Another Round," is a pub pun on its surface — rounds kept arriving, Sheri ordering — but on second thought it's also this book's whole method: another round of the same man, seen from one more seat, and he keeps holding.
