## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter put me back in Vee's childhood bed and squeezed the air out of the room along with her. The cold-open details — the bent knees because her legs outgrew the bed in middle school, the lavender plugins, the furnace going into savings mode — landed on me as pure enclosure. This is the room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her," and this chapter is the fullest I've felt that: the ribbons, the little gold swimmers frozen mid-lift, the clarinet unplayed since senior year, friends who haven't texted in a year. The room "kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed." That line ached. She's a ghost in her own archive.

The phone section was the most honest writing in the chapter, and the hardest to sit with. Her thumb hovering over the field, deleting "are you—" letter by letter, *glad* he's not a man who lives on his phone so he'll never see the dots start and stop — that gladness is so precisely the shape of loving someone courteous while being furious at his courtesy. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That's the whole tragedy of Pace compressed into one sentence, seen from her side, where she can't know it's fear and not indifference.

The two pictures did something sharp to me: the induction photo she sent Pace whole and cropped for her mother at the waist — "Same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it." She's become the editor of her own life, and the cut always falls along the seam of the thing that made her feel most herself. And Randi's feed as "another country, and wealth dressed it as another world" — she envies the mountains *knowing* whose money bought them, keeping the MIRA secret she's kept all along, "not then and not after." My heart went out to her twice in one paragraph: for the envy, and for the discipline of the silence.

I believed the blonde-invention completely, because I'd watched her build it before. "She handed Pace the exact opposite of herself and named it the thing he would want the moment he was free of her" — a stripper's body vs. a fashion model's, her old shame vocabulary repurposed as a weapon against herself. And then the best memory turning on her: the first night, the sheet slipping, the fra diavolo, "nothing that needed saying" — and then the hair going gold at the table, "the kitchen smelling right." She cannot keep her own night. That's the cruelest sentence in the chapter and the truest; memory is no shelter when you don't know your standing.

What swept me, in the midst of the cold: the imagined arms at midnight. "The strong warm weight of them drawing her back into him — and it came, and it would not hold, because in the same breath she could not keep from it: whose he had them around instead. The wanting and the fear in the one image, and no way on earth to know which was the true one." That's the book's whole engine in one held image — she's holding Pace and Randi in the same midnight grasp and doesn't know that's *literally* the configuration. The dramatic irony burned in me the entire chapter: every line of her warm open-handed text to Randi ("i'm here if you want to talk. or not."), every line of Randi's reply ("miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋") reads as double to me and single to her. The 💋 at the end — Randi signing her two-ways love as emoji, unspendable anywhere else.

Suspicion: none new. Everyone here is exactly who I already knew them to be, behaving exactly as known. The chapter's dread is structural, not a betrayal of trust — though I'll admit the Pace-cooking-for-someone turn ("a whole week she couldn't see into… and she didn't get to know who") nudged me too. Sheri, I told myself. It's Sheri. But Vee doesn't know Sheri cooks at his table, and I'm only guessing from Thanksgiving.

Friction: minimal. The bartender-and-Randi reassurance passage ("they went straight through her hands. The floor was gone") is the one place the author's hand showed slightly — assembling the chapter's supports into a neat collapse. But it earned it. The ending, "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone," is a strong closing bell, though I confess "Old Acquaintances" as a title still feels oblique to me — the song is about friendship remembered at the year's turn ("should auld acquaintance be forgot"), which fits her holding both her people while setting neither down; it also makes me wonder, as a guess from here, whether the new term will bring an *old* acquaintance back into the frame — the Anthro class with Randi is circled on the calendar, and the chapter ends pointing straight at it.

What I want next: term starting. Randi's face lighting up. And Pace — who I know spent this same night drunk and sobbing over a truck's engine while she was inventing blondes. Two people parallel in misery, neither able to spend the word. Get me to Tuesday, 7 a.m.
