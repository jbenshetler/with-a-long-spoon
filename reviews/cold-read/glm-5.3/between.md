**Felt read**

The pull of this chapter for me is how ordinary it makes the extraordinary look. This is just dinner — the loft booth, the jukebox, the Tuesday/Thursday rhythm — and inside that wrapper the book slides a knife in. When Randi's thumb stopped on Vee's hand, I stopped with it. I've watched Randi steer every one of Vee's confessions for months, and I've wondered how much of her listening is appetite wearing a friend's face — and this is the first time the mask slipped *on camera*. "And you liked it," flat, "with none of the evening's music in it at all." That's not the voice she uses with Vee. That's the voice from under the performance — and Vee's body answered it with a reflexive "No" that came out "too fast." That one word, and the patch after it ("Not my thing"), is the most honest beat of denial I've read in this whole book, and the fact that Vee explains it to herself as *Randi protecting her from Sheri* — she builds a whole generous story about why Randi went still, rather than letting the simpler possibility in the door — is the book showing me exactly what Vee will not look at. She did it with the pointing game at the start and she's doing it now: the self-deception is the character.

And then the chapter hands me the other scene with the same ambiguity: the goodbye kiss where Vee *closes her eyes and waits* — "waiting on the hand." That's a girl who has learned Randi's choreography and now offers herself up into it. And the neck kiss, "in passing, and neither of them said a word about it." Neither said a word. The book keeps counting these things for me without letting Vee count them.

My feelings moved this chapter. Pace softened even further offstage — the waxing request, delivered with "It's part of your surprise," reads as his careful brand of desire: negotiated, named, sweetened. I trust him. Randi is where my unease sharpened: the thumb stopping is earned by the page — I'm not importing suspicion, the text did it — and then she recovers *perfectly*, and I don't know which Randi is real, and I don't think Vee does either. Sheri grew on me secondhand; hearing her through Vee's retelling, she sounds exactly as alive as she was in person.

The heat here is real and low-watt: the Sondra appointment, "bare," Vee's plan to make him come first, the foot along her calf. It's charge at the edges — talk about sex rather than sex — but the wanting is thick in it. Where it peaks for me is Vee's own off-the-leash imagining ("what he could mean to do with all of her") and that closing image: a name written on her hand, and the other thing she has "cold" — memorized, kept, not on paper. The unwritten thing is Randi.

Friction: Vee's "No" landing "ahead of anything she might have chosen" is wonderful, but Randi letting it sit unanswered bothered me pleasantly — I want to know what she's thinking and the book won't tell me, which is the right choice but an ache.

Titles: "Between" reads as the state of Vee's whole inner life — between Pace and Randi, between what her body answers and what her mouth says, between the two things she'll tell and the one she'll keep "cold." Book title *A Place at the Table* feels like it's about the dinner-table triangulation itself.

I dread and want Friday. The surprise, the waxing, the "come first with my mouth" plan — and Thursday's dinner, where the unsaid things keep accumulating interest.
