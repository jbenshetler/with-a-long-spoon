## Reader reaction

This is the chapter I've been half-dreading and half-aching for since chapter one — the book finally lets us back into the room where it started, and the room has changed.

The felt read: I have been waiting chapters for this without knowing it — the Pace-and-Randi scene, the two of them alone with the reader's worst knowledge, and it did to me roughly what I expected and also something I didn't expect at all. What I expected: the confirmation, on the page, that everything Vee believes is chance is discussed between these two like weather. The killer line for me wasn't the sex, it was "You taught me. Not with anything you said." — the pointing game retroactively revealed as Pace reading *Randi's* face, not the women's. Even the origin is worse than I remembered: he was already hard on the quad, already picturing both of them bound together on his pillows. The pointing "was only to find out whether they had chosen the same." I knew the architecture, but hearing him narrate it with such calm appetite still made my stomach drop a little.

What I didn't expect: the scene turning on *him* checking the gauge on *her*. "She consents — every time — or I'm done. It isn't you I'm checking on." I keep turning that over. Part of me wants to read it as his one clean line in the book — that even inside the orchestration there's a floor he won't go under. But the irony is savage and I don't think the page is unaware of it: the man running a secret campaign on a twenty-one-year-old asks his secret lover whether the target *consents*. And Randi's answer — "She's choosing it — every Saturday she chooses it" — is true and is also exactly what a person says when they've stopped believing the question is enough. "That isn't the same answer." "It's a better one." That exchange landed cold on me.

And then Randi broke me a little. The confession mid-sex — "I'm doing it for *you*... it's not a treat, it's *work*" — while her body says the opposite the entire time, the clench on the word "mouth," "she *took* it." The doubling is now fully visible to her and to me: she is narrating the kisses as preparation for the threesome her body already treats as the main event. And the moment the book has been building toward since the bench: "God, I want her. ... For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant." The second sentence arriving *after* the slip, a full second late, unconvincing to both of them. I believed the want and I believed the patch, and so did he.

The heat here is real and it worked on me — the mirror detail is the chapter's best trick, her face available to him (and to me) while she's arranged it so he can't touch it. Her running the ending herself, "Here. Like this," the fourth that "began to hurt." But the charge is inseparable from the dread now: every filthy beat is narrated through Vee, who isn't there and doesn't know. Even the sweetness afterward — "It makes me feel real. And some nights it frightens me" — is her getting from him exactly what she engineered for Vee to get. Both women were starving; he fed them both; the difference is one of them knows the kitchen.

The tenderness at the end genuinely moved me, which unsettles me. "Then I'll be careful with you" — I believe he means it, the way I believe the drawer and the heat turned up. That's this book's whole hold on me: care and design are the same act, and I still can't split them.

Friction: the chapter title "Four" — I assumed it meant chapter numbering until the last pages ("That's more than three"). Now it's the day's count, and I notice both couples keep the same ledger. It's a flat title that only pays off at the end; it didn't spoil anything for me. My only resistance was the sheer completeness of Randi's monologue — it answers several open questions at once (the kiss escalation is deliberate, or half-deliberate; "Sticky fingers?" now reads as less innocent) and I felt the author's hand assembling the dossier. But the "That's more than three" / "More than three what?" / "Nothing" beat bought it back.
