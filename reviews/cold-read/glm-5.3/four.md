## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

Well. That title. "Four" — I read it as a bare number and thought nothing of it, and then the chapter made me count along with Randi: three times before the evening even properly started, and then this one — *"That's more than three."* The chapter is the arithmetic of a day, and the arithmetic is a cover story both of them are running on themselves. The number is doing exactly what the brunch numbers and the stats lectures do in this book: stating the truth plainly while everyone pretends it means nothing. And of course the number that actually matters isn't the one in the title. It's the *other* three in the room — him, her, and the absent girl whose name neither of them has to say, because "her" is now a pronoun with only one referent in their shared vocabulary.

This is the first full Pace-and-Randi chapter since the bench, and I felt it land hard. What moved me most isn't the sex — it's that the sex is almost entirely *about Vee*. Randi arrives with a story (the stats class, the wet hair, the steel coffee cup, *"still full of your come"*), works herself up telling it, and every escalation on that couch is confession wearing the costume of report. The chapter's whole engine is Randi narrating the goodbye kiss at the brunch while Pace — deliberately, I think — keeps pulling the honest answer out of her: *"Does she want it?" "That isn't what I asked." "I didn't ask who it was for."* He interrogates her the way he interrogated the apology — gently, and without letting her off. And her answers collapse in exactly the order a person's defenses collapse: first the justification ("Somebody has to get her used to my mouth"), then the work framing ("it's not a treat, it's *work*"), and then — the moment the whole chapter bends around — *"I'm doing it for you"* answered with *"You don't have to do it for me,"* said kindly, and the sentence arrives before she can stop it: *"Oh, but I'm doing the work,"* and she comes on the lie mid-word. Her body ratifies what her mouth won't. It's the same grammar as the bench chapter, where her body said yes before she could; her own arousal keeps functioning as a truth-teller she can't cross-examine.

Pace moved for me here, and not entirely in a comfortable direction. The mirror detail is the coldest beat in the chapter: she puts her face where he can't see it, *"and she had forgotten the framed mirror on the wall... which gave him her face the entire time."* He reads her the way he read her on the September walk — *"Your face said yes before the rest of you got there"* — and he's proud of it. And it's tender, genuinely: *"Then I'll be careful with you."* But I watched the same skill that makes her feel *real* ("You see more of me than anybody ever has... some nights it frightens me") being deployed as an instrument, again, in a bed, aimed at a third person. The two things remain inseparable in him. I can't split them and I've stopped wanting to.

Randi, though — Randi is coming apart in the most controlled possible way. *"God, I want her,"* said into the couch cushion with *"nothing at all around it,"* then caught and rewrapped — *"For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant"* — which is the rewrapping move she's been doing since chapter one (the threesome was itself a rewrap of Pace's question). And the flatness when he asks *Is she happy?* — *"so you can stop checking the gauge. I have always known what I'm doing"* — that landed on me as armor cracking. She says "I have always known" the way people say it when it has just stopped being true. What got me most was the small accounting: *"She told me first."* A pause with something in it, over *food talk*. She is jealous of the ordering of confidences. That is not a handler's feeling. That is the feeling of a woman in love pretending to run a project.

The heat here is real and it worked on me — the slow breast play, the position she takes *"as though the position were a thing the sentence required,"* her taking over the fourth one because he's spent — but the charge isn't really anatomical. It peaks at *"I'm doing it for you"* / *"You don't have to do it for me,"* and at *"God, I want her"* — moments where wanting gets said by accident. The fourth, "drawn so thin... it crossed over and began to hurt," is the chapter's physical echo of what's happening to them emotionally: pleasure pushed past its own shape.

Friction: almost none. If anything, the one thing I'd flag is that the conversation-under-sex device is very deliberate — people completing argumentative sentences mid-act — and I felt the author's hand once or twice (the *"Sounds grueling"* banter placed a little too neatly). But it's earned by the voice.

The title of the volume — **With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception** — sits strangely with me. "A warm reception" reads as the reception Vee keeps receiving (the heat turned up hours early — literally this chapter's opening line), and maybe the long spoon as the long game: supping with the devil, or just the distance you keep while feeding someone. After this chapter I'd say the long spoon is Pace and Randi both, stirring the same pot and calling it service.

Where I dread and want to go: the secret is now visibly failing on the *holder's* side, not the victim's. Randi is in love, Pace is watching her be in love and said nothing about it to Vee, and Thanksgiving is coming — Vee in Ohio, Sheri at the table. My guess (only a guess): the anthro elective or the MIRA thread breaks something first, and when Vee learns she was chosen, she'll learn it in Randi's voice. I keep reading because I want what's coming and I'm afraid of it, which is the best reason there is.
