## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This chapter knocked the wind out of me a little, and I need to sit with why before I tidy it up. It's the first full scene since the very first chapter of the book that I've been alone with Pace and Randi without Vee — and it lands like a trapdoor. For twenty-plus chapters I've been reading their conspiracy as the reader's burden, the thing I know and Vee doesn't. Here it's just *them*, in the too-warm house, deadbolt over at four, and what I saw wasn't the plan. It was two people failing to keep their own feelings out of each other while using Vee's body as the shared language they're allowed to do it in.

The sex is explicit and hot — three times before the chapter even properly starts, then the couch — but the charge isn't in the mechanics. It peaks exactly where the honesty leaks through. Randi narrating the goodbye kiss to him while he's inside her — "She stands there in the sun with her eyes shut, waiting for me to come back to her" — is one of the most charged passages in the book for me, and it's charged because she's describing *falling for Vee* and describing it as reconnaissance. The clench "on the word," the thing she "had not decided to do and could not have stopped" — the body confessing what the mouth keeps re-filing as "groundwork."

That re-filing is the ache of the chapter. "I'm doing it for *you*. It's groundwork. It's not a treat, it's *work*" — and Pace, who in this house is the one person who never lies, says "You don't have to do it for me," and it goes through her "before she had any say in it at all." He's not asking her to stop wanting Vee. He's *releasing her from the alibi*, and it wrecks her. "Say something nice to me" from a woman who hands out approval like a tray. Her hands shut on his arm, "tighter, and then tighter again." She takes the blanket, the chill after, "the way it always did" — this is an old, practiced intimacy between them, and I felt how deep it runs and how little Vee knows it exists.

And then the two lines I can't stop turning over. First: "How did you know, that day? To point at her." — and his answer: "I pointed. I watched your face. And your face said yes before you did." I'd carried the assumption — I think I've been carrying it since the beginning — that Pace chose Vee and sent Randi to fetch her. This says the *pick was Randi's*, that her face gave the yes on the dining-hall quad before her mouth gave the bare "Yes." Whether that's true or just the version that lets him hand her the ownership tonight, I genuinely can't tell, and the book leaves it ajar. It recolors everything: if Randi chose Vee, then everything since — the collision, the brunches, the kiss — is a woman circling her own want and calling it tradecraft.

Second: "That's more than three." "More than three what?" "Nothing. Nothing at all." She's counting. She's keeping score against Vee's brunch report — *we were three times, they were more* — and she laughs it off wrecked, and he doesn't push. That's rivalry and wanting wearing arithmetic, and I loved it and winced at it in the same breath.

Where do I stand on people? Pace — I still can't settle him, and this chapter deepened the knot rather than loosening it: he is tender here too (the blanket before she asks, the careful "Is she *happy*" vs "radiant" distinction, "You don't have to do it for me" as an act of mercy that doubles as exposure). But he's also still running both women, and the question "Does she want it?" — asked slow, almost out of her — is him checking his gauge, which is also the plan checking itself. Randi — my heart moved toward her despite everything, or because of everything. "I know exactly what I'm doing. I have always known what I'm doing" is the most defensive sentence anyone has said in this book, and the flat level thing underneath the warmth told me she knows it isn't true. And she's still the woman who carries Vee's confidences into this bed — "eating with the sound off" arrives here secondhand, and "She told me first" has a small possessive hook in it that he correctly lets go by.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new to suspect — the suspicion was already fully lit — but its *texture* changed. It stopped feeling like a scheme and started feeling like three people's real wants colliding inside a frame two of them built and can no longer live inside honestly. Which is worse and better.

Friction: almost none. The cold open ("The deadbolt had gone over at four... He had put the heat up at three") is so compressed I had to re-read to realize the POV had left Vee entirely — a deliberate jolt, and I took it as one. The title "Four" gave me nothing before I read; after, it's plainly the count — his "with the right encouragement," her "that's more than three" — the couple keeping score against the other couple, and I suspect against Vee's reported "two, three" too. It's a small, sly title, and I think it's also the number of people in this book, which I'll hold as a guess, not a claim.

The series line — *With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception* — reads truer and crueler now: supper with a long spoon is how you eat with the devil, and the warm reception is the house with the heat turned up, and both women receive it. I dread and want Thanksgiving: Vee in Ohio, Sheri at the table, and the clock running on the anthro class, "just us," Tuesdays and Thursdays, seven to eight-twenty — wet hair at eight a.m., Randi watching it darken through a sweater. The endgame feels like it's closing in on the threesome the plan was built for; what I now doubt, for the first time, is whether *Randi* can survive getting what she asked for.
