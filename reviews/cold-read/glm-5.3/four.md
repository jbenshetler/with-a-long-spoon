## Reader reaction

The deadbolt went over before her coat was off and I knew within three lines whose afternoon this was — and my stomach did a little drop, because a chapter in Pace's house on a Saturday means Randi in it, and I've been waiting (dreading, wanting) this ever since the brunch. And the chapter *worked* on me, both ways at once: it's the hottest chapter I've read in this book, and it's the one where the two people I'm most suspicious of said the quiet part out loud — or came so close to it that the almost is the point.

The heat here is real and it's doing something the book has never let me watch before: Pace and Randi's sex has always read as armored, practiced, a thing with a bench and a safeword behind it. This is the first time I felt *her* lose the run of it. The whole first movement — her hands on the rail, her face turned away, telling him the goodbye kiss in slow detail while he fucks her — that's the "May I" dynamic flipped over, and it scalded me: he's the one drawing the confession out by degrees, exactly the way he drew Vee's body out at the fitting, and Randi keeps *giving him the material* because she's carried it for two days "waiting to share." "She took it. And then she stood there on the sidewalk in the sun looking at me like there was nothing better in the world" — I believed that, and the clench on the word "mouth" told me everything her mouth wouldn't.

What moved me most wasn't the sex, though — it was the two moments she can't dress up. "Is she all right?" / "She is radiant." / "That isn't the same answer." — and her flat, level "I know exactly what I'm doing. I have always known what I'm doing." That's the bench woman talking, the face defending the woman, and I noticed that the more she insists it's a project ("it's not a treat, it's *work*"), the more the sentence falls apart. "I'm doing it for *you*" → "I didn't ask who it was for" → "God, I want her." That's the whole book in one exchange, and I felt it land in my chest, not just my head. My feeling about Randi moved this chapter: from "falling against her will" to *caught*, plainly, out loud, twice — "For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant," which fools neither of them. And then the aftermath, her pulling his forearm in "tighter, and then tighter again," "You see more of me than anybody ever has… It makes me feel real. And some nights it frightens me" — that undid me a little. She and Vee are both being *seen* by the same man, and neither knows the other is.

Pace: still the man I can't settle, and the chapter sharpened it rather than resolving it. The tenderness is fact again — the blanket before her hand goes out, "Then I'll be careful with you," meant entirely — and in the same mouth, "You don't have to do it for me," which is either the kindest thing he's ever said to her or the cruelest bit of leverage in the book (he says it while holding her still on him, and feels what it does to her before she has any say). And the answer to "How did you know to point at her?" — "Your breath changed and you gave her a *maybe*… I pointed at her and watched your face" — recolors everything I remembered from the pointing game. He wasn't picking Vee for Randi; he was reading Randi. The pick was Randi's yes, and both of them now half-know it. That didn't make me like him less, exactly, but it made him bigger and colder in my head — the man who reads people the way he reads pans and proof.

Friction, honestly: almost none. The mirror device ("she had forgotten the framed mirror… which gave him her face the entire time") is the only place I felt the author's hand a little — it's a bit *placed* for the theme of being-seen. But the couch speaking, "the way it always did" about the deadbolt, "That's more than three" / "Nothing at all" — those earned their keep. The title, "Four," took me a moment: the count, hers, the thing she says into the wood and retreats from — matching Vee's "three times, in one evening" from the photo chapter. The two women are each keeping a tally of the same man's days, and the number is the chapter's whole meaning for me: this happened *while* Vee was thirty minutes down the road wanting him. The volume title, "A Warm Reception," keeps reading darker to me — warmth as something prepared in advance, the heat turned up hours before she arrives, hospitality as design.

I have no suspicion of Cassie, still, and she's absent here, which felt right and also like the story clearing the stage for the three of them. Thanksgiving next, most likely — Vee in Ohio, Sheri at his table — but what I dread and want is the anthro class, "just us," Tuesdays and Thursdays, seven to eight-twenty, and the MIRA folder sitting in a shared Google Drive somewhere. And now I know both of them want her. The reader's the only one who knows that all three of them do.
