## Reader reaction

What a warm little chapter — and what a strange, doubled feeling it gave me, because I spent the whole thing holding my breath around the joy rather than inside it.

The scene itself is pure glow: Vee bursting through the door still carrying the dinner, Cassie pulled up out of her chair by it, the bean-bag spin, "Look at me." I loved this. The last chapters have shown us Vee under water — literally, in the pool chapter, hanging weightless where nobody could see her — and here she is *up*, spinning in a room too small to hold her. The best beat is the one she insists on hardest: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That's her naming the very thing that makes Pace different for her — that he leaves the last inch, gives back the pencil, doesn't argue when she sets a rule. Her joy is real and I don't want to sand it down.

But I can't not feel what the book has let me know and hasn't let her. Two chapters ago I stood on a quad and watched Pace point at a redhead and say "Her," and watched Randi fake a collision and come back grinning with a name — Vee — and a class schedule. Now Vee is lying in a bean bag *counting it off like a dossier* — "I know where he lives and everything" — believing her own cleverness has produced a proof of safety, and every fact she counts is a fact that was arranged for her. The meeting at the coffee shop, the froyo text, the way the whole courtship has moved like clockwork. I don't think Pace is a villain — the book has spent real care making his patience and consent-mindedness feel true (the "may I," the *red* word, giving back the pencil). But the tenderness I watched him give Randi ran alongside a man who engineers meetings and points at women like choices in a case. So when Cassie asks about state troopers as a joke, and when the chapter's title turns out to be "Off Six-Fourteen" — the *whole address*, the gap where a real answer should be — I felt the title land as a small cold spot in all the warmth. Vee has a dossier with a hole in it. She hasn't seen the house. There's a bench in that house.

Cassie keeps being the best thing in every scene she's in. "How much did he give you to drink?" — asked flatly, half-teasing and entirely not, and Vee's flash of surprise that anyone would even ask. Cassie is the only person in Vee's life doing the math nobody else does, and her earlier "Goodbye, Vee" — the word for a longer parting — is starting to read like a person who noticed something and let it stand. I don't know what. I'm not guessing further than that.

Where the heat went: nowhere, and that's fine — this is an afterglow chapter, the room temperature kind, and it earned its warmth. What I want next is the hike, and what I dread a little is Vee standing in that house, out past where the road goes to gravel, with her borrowed shoes on. The book is titled *A Polite Invitation* and this chapter was the politest thing in it so far — which is exactly why I'm watching the door.
