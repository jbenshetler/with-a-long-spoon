## Felt Read

This chapter landed hard on me, mostly as heartbreak. The dance lesson on the concrete pad was pure Pace — patient, technical, generous — and while it was happening I was completely swept up. "There's no sorry unless you take off a foot" made me laugh out loud, and the moment the dance *gave* — "the decision pulled from her somehow… She was busy" — was one of my favorite beats in the book so far. It's the same thing he does in bed, transposed: her wanting to stop deciding, and him giving her something to follow. I felt the heat of that. His hand at her back, "the information goes through here" — it's the consent choreography of their whole relationship rendered as dance instruction, and it worked on me completely.

Then the bar. The blonde was clearly a jab — "knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to" is the ugliest possible framing of everything Pace actually is, and I knew it was poison while I read it. The bartender's counterweight — "Doesn't take advantage… Not that plenty haven't tried, hoping he would" — confirmed what I believed about him. What gutted me was Vee's spiral: *floozies* coming back, the slut-shaming of herself ("she'd let herself be played: a few dates and she'd spread her legs like a slut") — her mother's voice fused with her desire, exactly as it's been since chapter 4, arriving here as self-punishment and then punishment of him. "She'd punished him for being the man she wanted him to be" — that line is the whole chapter.

The blonde is the one genuinely new unease: she knew Randi-levels of familiarity with him, "her face turned up to his like a woman who already knew the shape of him." That's the page's fact, not my invention, and I noticed it. But I read the blonde as malicious rather than truthful — the text mostly supports Pace's decency. My trust in him holds; my ache for Vee is what grew. The closing beat — going to Randi's to "fix it" — lands dread in me, because she's walking her shame straight into the person who engineered so much of it.

The title: "Lesson" — the dance lesson, obviously, but also the chapter's crueler one: what Vee learns tonight about herself, her jealousy, her mother's voice. It didn't give anything away; it earned itself twice over.
