## Reader reaction

Well. That chapter did something to me, and I want to be honest about how much of it was heat and how much was something closer to vertigo — because the two got braided together in a way I don't think I've felt in a book before.

First, the obvious: Randi is playing Vee like an instrument, and the page knows it. The whole practice-room scene is Randi at her most surgical — "did you deserve it?" dropped so casually that Vee answers before she hears what she's agreeing to, and I watched her walk into it the way you watch someone step on ice you can see is thin. And yet — and this is the part that unsettles me about *myself* — I found it hot. Vee's unraveling in that soundproof closet is written from the inside so completely that my suspicion of Randi and my being carried along with Vee's body happened at the same time, in the same sentences. Randi didn't touch her. She told a story, and Vee fled to a bathroom with the bolt loud and would not say yes to it. That last restraint — "To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it" — is doing an enormous amount of work. The chapter is about a yes Vee is circling and refusing to arrive at, and the book keeps refusing to close the door on it.

I felt for Vee so much it almost hurt. The crossed arms "she tried to make look like settling," the too-fast "Yeah," the laugh that comes out too high — every one of those landed as a body telling the truth her mouth is declining to. And the ending: her arms slow coming down off the hug she hadn't meant to hold. She "put it down to that" — the sleeplessness, being full of him — and I don't believe her, and neither, I think, does the page. What I don't know is whether *Randi* believes her. The "couldn't tell whether she'd meant anything in the world by it" is exactly the note to leave things on, and I'll leave it there too.

On the trust question: my suspicion of Randi is now fully earned by text, not just carried from what I know. She baited that conversation deliberately — the wet hand, "not the love tap. His," the cat going still. She took an afternoon Vee came in proud of and turned it into a confession, then a demonstration. Whether that's appetite, or tenderness, or the pointing game moving to its next phase, I genuinely can't parse — and that ambiguity is the most charged thing in the chapter. Notice she said "Mine" — the ex with the hand that slipped — and told that story with her eyes on Vee's face the whole while. I'm not going to pretend I know what that was for. But Pace's tenderness still reads true to me and Vee both; nothing in the weekend retelling dimmed him.

The lecture — outliers, two kinds, the one that "comes in through the part that carries forward" and is "in everything that comes after" — I underlined it right along with Vee, because it's the loudest quiet thing the book has done since latent variables. That kitchen swat is in everything that comes after now; this chapter is the proof. If it's the second kind of outlier, it isn't a moment, it's a bend in the road. I notice the book keeps handing me stats lectures as its own private commentary track, and it works on me every time and I don't resent it, which surprises me.

Friction: very little. If anything, Randi's ex-boyfriend monologue ("apologized to the furniture... and God, it made me buck") tips a hair toward performance — it's such a *gift-wrapped* story, exactly the story Vee needs to hear at exactly that moment, and I mean that as suspicion the text earned, not a flaw. And the "Sometimes it's the mouse that roars" line did nothing for me — I don't know what it means and Randi said it too knowingly for me to shrug off.

The title: "The Practice Room." On the surface it's just the closet with the piano. But practice is repetition toward a performance, and everything in that room was rehearsal — Vee rehearsing the telling, rehearsing (again) the feeling, Randi rehearsing the scene she'll presumably want to direct eventually. And the freshman-year "everybody used them to..." that Vee wouldn't finish — the room itself carries a sexual history before they even sit down. It recolored the whole chapter on second thought for me. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels more pointed after this than ever; and the cover-board line, "With a Long Spoon — Book One," still means nothing to me concretely — though a long spoon is what you need for a feast with the devil, and after this chapter I find myself thinking about that line harder.

What I want next: I want the induction and the burgundy dress — that's been gathering for chapters. What I dread and want at once: whatever it is Vee won't say yes to yet. I'm guessing, from here only, that the next step of the game involves Vee's wanting arriving *with witnesses*, and I hate how much I want to read it.
