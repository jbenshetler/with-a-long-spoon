## Reader reaction

The chapter title — "Some of Mine" — got me. On first read I took it as Cassie's line about the Cocoa Pebbles: *do you want some of mine.* A small offering, a want the size of a bowl. And that's exactly what the chapter does: Vee can't answer "is this what you want?" — the big want, the door-shaped thing behind Saturday — so Cassie shrinks the question down to cereal, and Vee says yes to that. The title takes the biggest question in the book right now (what, and who, does Vee want) and answers it in the only currency Vee can spend tonight: a bowl someone else hands her. It's the hau again, honestly — Dr. Marsh's gift that binds — Cassie giving from her own box the way Pace gives from his kitchen. Vee is a girl who keeps being fed by the people who love her, and keeps being unable to name what she's hungry *for*.

What landed hardest: Cassie. She's been the quiet spine of this book for sixty chapters — "He looked at you like he'd forgotten there was anyone else in the room" — and here she gets the whole final movement of a very charged chapter and does the most Cassie thing imaginable. She doesn't push. She asks the exact right question — not *what happened*, not *what are you two*, but "Is this what you want?" — and when Vee swings the old "you don't like her" plank at her, "Cassie stepped over it without once looking down." That line gave me a real pang of loyalty-love for her. She's the one person in Vee's life with nothing at stake except Vee, and the worry under the flat delivery — the same worry from "do you love him" — reads as pure. And then the ending: "You didn't have dinner." She saw everything she needed to see in Vee's face and chose to feed her instead of cornering her. That's the tenderest thing in the book since Pace made her the drawer.

The Vee of this chapter frightens me a little, in the way the book has been carefully earning. "She actually went and looked" — that sentence stopped me cold, because this is a girl who has spent the entire book *not* looking, and here, asked simply enough, she tries — and hits the edge of the shape and backs off before she can "make out its size." The book is being scrupulously honest with me: she's not in denial anymore, she's in the doorway. Literally — coat still on, half in and half out of the room, half in and half out of the answer. And the small tell I loved: "her neck said yes." Her body answered Cassie's question before her mouth found the deflection. The neck is now the ledger.

Friction: honestly, none of consequence. The chapter is short and quiet and earns every beat of its shortness. If anything, the "postmark" image — Cassie reading the night off her skin — is lovely enough that I noticed it was lovely, but that's a quibble of mine, not the book's.

Erotic charge: low by design, and correctly so — this is the decompression beat after three of the hottest chapters in the book (the dancefloor, the confession-trade, the Monday night of the held-down hand). But the heat isn't gone; it's *residue*. The neck still ringing through the whole scene is the chapter's charge — an erogenous afterimage carrying the weight. That's craft: I feel what happened Saturday–Tuesday without any of it being re-staged.

My feelings moving: Vee — my sympathy deepened even as my worry did. She's now lied to Pace outright ("No. Nothing."), and this chapter shows her lying to Cassie by evasion, and the book makes me feel *why* each time without letting me hate her for it. Randi — she's offstage here, heels going off into the dark, and her absence is loud. "Having you at the dance was so great" is still ringing from last chapter; I keep turning over whether the neck-kiss at the door was a test ("How do you like it?") or a want slipping its leash. I genuinely can't tell, and I don't think the book wants me to yet. Pace — tender in memory, and I keep thinking about that flicker across his face and the fact that he *defended himself* for the first time ever. He saw the parking-lot kiss. He knows there was more than "nothing." I'm braced for that, and it's a guess from here.

Suspicion: my one live suspicion is of Randi's bid to brunch — she lit up "like she'd been handed a present" when Vee asked, and I can't help remembering the checkpoint's open note about "the skirt, the mixer, whatever Randi had decided they were doing about it." Something is being orchestrated and Vee is inside it. But the page hasn't confirmed anything, so I hold it loosely.

Titles: volume is *A Place at the Table* (Book Three of *With a Long Spoon*), and this chapter — a friend offering cereal, a bowl in two hands — feels like the volume title made literal at its humblest scale. Everyone keeps making Vee a place: a drawer, a chair, a bowl. The question the volume seems to be asking is which places she'll actually sit down in, and whether she'll ever pull up a chair for what's behind the door.

Next: brunch with Randi on Saturday, and Pace holding a lie. I dread the collision and I want it. Guesses only.
