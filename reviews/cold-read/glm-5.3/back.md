## Reader reaction

The first thing this chapter does to me is let me exhale. After "Missed a Spot" — which left me with Vee's tongue hunting for a taste on her own lip and a question she wouldn't ask herself — I came in braced. And instead the book gives me the sink. Him washing, her naked on the lacquered chair with her wine, talking at his back. It's the arrangement the whole relationship has been built on, rendered as pure domesticity, and it relaxed me before I could worry about it.

And then he says it: "I want to meet your people." My body answered that line the way Vee's did. After months of the hidden life — everything behind the shut door, the fight about what they are "out there" — he is *volunteering to go out there*. He heard her December demand in a way I didn't expect: not by saying the word, but by offering the publicness. That undid me a little, and it undid me honestly. He still hasn't said it. She still hasn't gotten the word. But he's answering the *ache underneath* the word, and I felt that as real movement.

The passage about the friends nearly made me cry, and I want to be specific about why. The boots. "Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off." He remembers a half-sentence from a trail in October. He has been keeping her people the way he keeps everything — silently, exactly, on a shelf. Vee realizing he's been *listening* all fall, not the polite kind — that's this book's love language, and it landed on me fully. The Cassie moment is the small miracle of the chapter: she won't build a dinner somebody has to be a good sport at. "She'd watched somebody be fine about it before. She wasn't going to look at when." — that's Cassie at the sorority lunch, and I felt the book quietly proving Vee has become a person who protects people. That's growth the text earned.

But here's where the reader-only knowledge sits like a stone in my stomach. She says "Randi" — she's already building the table with Randi at the center, and *we know what he knows and she doesn't*. He and Randi have been lovers all along; the "coffee shop nine words, mostly math" was theater. When she pictures "her two favorite people at one small table finally getting to find out about each other," I couldn't share her warmth cleanly. The dramatic irony is now walking toward a literal table. And "Bring Theo," said flatly while scrubbing a pan — I felt the non-jealousy as deliberate, him *showing* her there's no wall with this ask, and I chose to take it at face value; the page gave me nothing darker there.

The Chili's picture arriving on its own — the fork squared to the table edge, "I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you" — is the best moment of characterization this chapter. She doesn't ask him to fix it; she asks him to pick the place, because she *knows his limits now* and routes around them with him instead of against him. That's the relationship maturing. And his answer, "Let me handle it," with that almost-happiness — the man who couldn't say the word choosing to say *this* — is the closest thing to a declaration he's given her.

Title: "Back" is doing triple duty and I love it — she's back (after the break, after the coat night), her back (the listening back at the sink), and the book is back from the fallow break chapters. It doesn't give anything away; it describes exactly where we are. Heat is low here — one naked woman and one clothed man at a sink — but the charge is tenderness-charged: her nakedness is ordinary now, which is itself the erotic achievement of the whole arc. What I want next: the party. I dread it and want it in equal measure — every named person in one room, Randi and Pace performing strangers, and Sheri exists somewhere with opinions. I can't wait and I'm afraid.
