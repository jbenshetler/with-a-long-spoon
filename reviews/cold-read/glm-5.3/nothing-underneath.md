## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well. I put the book down and just sat there for a minute. This is the chapter I've been reading fifty chapters for — not because of the sex, though God, the sex — but because this is Vee taking the whole engine of the book into her own hands. Every threshold she's crossed — the green satin, the induction long-step, the photograph — was her saying yes to something he offered. This is the first thing she built entirely alone and carried to him. "She was the one showing." That line landed in my chest.

The middle section, her alone in the dorm, is the hottest thing in the book so far, and it's her by herself in a lamplit mirror. It worked on me completely — the black heels going on *first*, "last thing, only thing," the laugh, the giggle at her own toes in frosted plum. I love that polish kept up over Christmas in her old bedroom "for a man she'd no longer been sure would ever see them again" — that tiny detail ached. And then her stopping herself at the mirror, unfinished on purpose, wound tight to carry to him: that's the shame-heat wire fully owned now. Her mother's word "came up out of old habit and found nothing to hold to." I actually put a hand over my mouth at that. The whole book's spine, in one line, released. And the dark bloom soaked into the coat lining — once it would have shamed her; tonight it "only told her she meant it." Yes. That's the transformation paid off on the page, not announced.

The drive nearly did me in — the cruiser scare, the hand leaving the wheel, the second denial. It's reckless and a little heightened, but she's been strung along by two patient schemers all book, and there's something true about a woman who has spent since she was fourteen folding herself small finally driving too fast down a black road with everything out. I bought it. Kayla with the laundry basket was the right comedy at the right moment — "nodding along to a laundry report in this condition" — and the door banging open on its hinges through the whole first round of it, neither of them moving to shut it: that's the book's door motif turned inside out. All those doors left to her, and now she's the one who knocked and opened.

And Pace. "The wall was simply gone, taken down all at once by what she'd done, and what stood under it was so plainly glad and so plainly wrecked." He still hasn't said the word — she says it herself: "nothing needed saying now." And I want to be swept, and I was, but I have to stay honest: the name "Vee," warm in his mouth, made her laugh turn to tears because it "hadn't been the word she'd come for then." And then: "tonight there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." That's the line that sits uneasily with me. She got everything she drove out for — I believe she did, this page convinces me — but the word is still unspent, and I know from ch 50 he knows it and chose not to spend it. "I missed you so much" with his voice cracking is real, the closest he's come. But the sex answered her question and the question was words. I'm glad for her and I'm keeping the receipt. Sheri's "say it" hasn't been answered; it's been *dodged beautifully*, and I noticed the dodge arrived as exactly what Randi prescribed — "Words are the whole war." Which is the thing I keep chewing: Randi built tonight. The heels, the plan, the pep talk past "slutty." The reader knows Randi and Pace are lovers and co-architects, and Vee just carried out a mission designed by one arm of the game to the other. I don't doubt Randi's love or even that the advice was good — it was — but every warmth in this book has been both true and instrumental, and this is the most successful operation yet. That unease never touched the heat for me, but it sits under it.

The pancakes. "Made and not poured" — the whole food-as-love language brought home. And him on one knee with his face cracked open, "I missed you so much," is the closest to the word he's ever come — the kneeling, the crack, the forehead kiss. Cassie's "Hmm" at the end was perfect, and I grinned at "Those stay on."

The title: perfect, double-edged — nothing under the coat, and nothing held back, nothing underneath the gesture. Also faintly ironic, because there *is* something underneath — the game she doesn't know about, the word he didn't say. The volume title, "A Polite Invitation," reads almost wryly now.

What I want next: the word, or the truth about the game — and I dread the second colliding with the first. Guess from here: the Anthro elective with Randi starts Tuesday, and I suspect Vee's newly claimed want and Randi's shelved love are going to be put in one room at 7 a.m.
