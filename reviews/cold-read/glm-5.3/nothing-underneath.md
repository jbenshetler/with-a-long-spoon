## Reader reaction

The title got me. I read "Nothing Underneath" on the cover of the chapter and thought: that's the coat plan, that's obvious. Then the chapter made it double. Nothing underneath — and everything underneath. The chapter is forty minutes of what's underneath: her own hands on her own body, the shame-voice arriving and "finding nothing to hold to," the body telling on her (the bloom in the coat lining) and her *letting* it. The title stopped being a pun about the coat somewhere around the mirror scene and became a claim about Vee herself: there is nothing under her anymore, no hiding layer, and she went to him that way.

This chapter swept me completely. The preparation sequence is some of the best sustained heat in the book — and it's heat with Vee's whole interior riding on it. Her standing at the mirror, deliberately touching herself, deliberately *stopping*, twice ("denied a second time and no nearer anything that was hers to take" in the car — I gasped at the cop-light fake-out) — this is the shame-and-wanting braided thing this book does better than anything else I've read. The line that owns the whole chapter: "tonight she would take all the too-much of herself… and finally use it for the one thing it had always been for." That's Vee flipping the "stripper's body" shame she's carried since chapter fourteen and making it a weapon. I was rooting for her out loud.

And the doorway scene. His aborted "Vee, I l—" and her cutting him off with the coat — I actually put the book down. Because here's the ambivalence I can't shake: she *heard* him start to say the word. The one she went to war over in December. The one she drove all night to avoid having to hear, because if he says it there, on the doorstep, mid-apology reflex, it's an accident, not a choice — and she wants it chosen, at the right time, for real. So she answers with her body instead of words: "the words had failed her and this would not." Whether the word will ever come, properly, the page leaves open, and so do I. But the sex reads as them: "hard and fast and graceless, which was the truth of them tonight." Then he puts his head on her breasts, calls her by name, and she cries — and I did too, honestly. "Nothing needed saying now" — I'm suspicious of that "now." It feels true tonight and fragile by morning.

Then the kitchen. Pancakes at midnight, and the apology she never got in December: "I'm sorry. I never meant to hurt you. I missed you so much." On one knee. His voice cracking. That's the romance peak of the book so far for me — more than the doorstep, more than the sex. He still hasn't said *the* word. She says she "had everything she had driven out through the cold to get," and I believe her, tonight. But I notice she's decided not to want the word "tonight" — "there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." That's a reprieve, not a resolution.

Kayla in the hallway is the funniest, most excruciating laundry-monologue cameo, and the Kayla beat ("If there were any justice she'd be struck where she stood") had me laughing while squirming. Cassie's Sunday "Hmm." is perfect — one syllable, the eyebrow, everything.

Suspicion: none of anyone. Randi isn't on the page past the borrowed certainty in Vee's head, and the heels — those heels — carry her fingerprint all over this scene. The shoes "not there to hold the shoe on" from last chapter pay off exactly. If anything I feel the machinery of the Randi plan humming underneath my happiness: Vee is back inside his care, exactly as designed, still not knowing she was ever chosen. That ache is mine, not the book's.

I wanted the blonde to die — and Vee killed her at the door: "no man put on music like that for a woman." The music detail was genuinely tense for a half page.

**What I want next:** I don't trust this peace to hold, and I don't want the word crisis resolved — I want the three-body problem back on the table. Guess from here: Randi's Brooke ultimatum and this reunion are on a collision course; the threesome configuration Pace kept building in his drunk kitchen picture is nearer than Vee knows.
