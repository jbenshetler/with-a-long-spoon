## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter. After the last few — the Mimosa brunch, the PPP outlier, Randi's unraveling in "Four" — this is a breath of pure warm air, and I sank into it gratefully.

The opening did something to me immediately: Vee calling her own five-night sabbatical "her own doing, which is the part that still sat wrong in her" — that's her whole character in one line. She wanted him to *fight* for the nights, and "he never said so. He only opened the door and waited on the far side of it." That's Pace exactly as I've come to love him: consent as a way of being, not a checkbox. And then the hallway striptease — the inside-out denim, the white cotton socks, the bra he holds so she has to unwind "like something off a spool" — that's the funniest and hottest thing this book has done in chapters, and it's hot *because* it's funny. She's laughing while hopping on one leg. He's enjoying himself "so plainly." That ease between them is earned; it's what ten weeks of careful slow Pace has bought.

The sex itself is almost incidental and it works: "her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private." That's the book's whole vocabulary of shame-turned-heat in one clause. I loved that.

The pizza scene quieted me down in the best way. "This came apart on her tongue into all its separate pieces and let her count them" — that's not about pizza anymore, and the book knows it without saying so. Vee tasting her life.

And then the flannel. I had to put the book down. The shirt from ch10 — the rain, the truck, the thing she's been sleeping in every night she isn't with him, washed because "one day last week it didn't smell like anything at all." Her asking him to wear it all weekend so she can sleep in his smell on the nights that aren't his — that's the most naked confession of love this book has produced, more than any "I love you" could be, and neither of them says the word. Him putting it on right then, over his other shirt, "two shirts in his own warm kitchen because she'd told him to" — that undid me a little. His answers are always acts.

No suspicion of anyone this chapter — it's one of the rare chapters with no machinery visible, no Randi, no plan, just the two of them. Which, knowing what I know and Vee doesn't, makes it both sweeter and sadder: she's building a home in a house whose owner is holding her panties in his dresser drawer for another woman. The drawer motif lands hard here — Vee's green scrunchies in "his" drawer (which we know holds Randi's secret too). I don't think Vee has any idea of the parallel, and the book doesn't wink; it just lets the two truths sit in one drawer. That restraint is why I trust this author.

The title: "The New Ordinary." It's the chapter where the extraordinary has become their texture — pizza, drawers, a shirt. It names exactly what Vee said in "All the Time": *it's just how it is now.* It's also gently ominous to me, because I don't believe this ordinary is stable — the outlier chapter sits right behind this one in my memory, and ordinary is what's about to be punctured. Guess from here: the Randi situation is nearing some kind of break — "God, I want her" and the crying morning can't stay contained.
