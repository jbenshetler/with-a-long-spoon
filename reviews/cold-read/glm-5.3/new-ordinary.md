## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction — "The New Ordinary"**

God, this one landed soft and deep. Five nights apart, and the whole chapter is the reunion, but the reunion isn't really the sex — it's the flannel. I'll get there, because I'm still a little wrecked by it.

The opening got me in an unusual way: Vee being the one to call the separation, and then being quietly furious that he *let* her. "He hadn't argued and he hadn't gone cold and he hadn't made her pay a cent for it, and the plain grace of that had cost her more than a fight would have." That's such a true thing about desire — she wanted to be missed loudly enough to argue with, and his grace reads as absence of want even though everything else in the book says otherwise. It's also the first time his patience, the thing I've been half-suspicious of since the checkpoint, worked *against* him in her accounting. Interesting.

The doorway kiss and the undressing-gauntlet down the hall: this is the funniest and hottest sex they've had, and it's hot *because* it's comic. The hopping one-legged out of the cold jeans, "no dignity anywhere in any of it and she'd have burned the house down for him" — that line is the whole chapter's key. She's past shame here; the mother's voice never shows up once. And when he stops her at the bathroom door — "his hand came to her waist and she was stopped, no effort in it anywhere" — I felt that in my sternum. He can arrest her momentum with one palm and no force, and she *gives* it: "a moment she had never decided to give it." That surrender-without-shame is what the whole arc has been building, and it doesn't feel worked-for here. It just is.

The sex itself is fast for her — "shamefully fast… her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private" — and I laughed out loud at that sentence. This is the book's sense of humor, and the book's honesty, fused. The second time, the slow one, rode quieter and I rode it with her.

Then the pizza, and I mean this: the pizza section made me ache. "He wasn't performing any of it for her… He'd have made the pizza exactly like this for an empty house. The care was only the shape his attention took when it came down on a thing" — and she can't tell which of them, her or the dough, got the larger share, and doesn't mind. That's the most clear-eyed thing Vee has ever thought about Pace, and it cuts both ways, and I don't think the page is deciding for me. This is care and design being the same act *again* — the same thing my checkpoint said the drawer and the dress do — but now told from inside her happiness, where it's just true and good. Her tasting every flavor separately, "the difference between cotton candy and a bowl of cut strawberries" — that's her whole awakening rendered as taste, the same motif as the thin-sliced apples and "eating with the sound off," extended. I loved it.

And then the flannel, and I teared up right along with her. "It stopped smelling like you." The shirt she rationed and washed and slept in — that motif from chapters back, where she washed it and I winced because washing it meant losing him — comes back paid in full: she's not giving it up, she's asking for it to be *recharged*. "I need you to wear it for me. The whole weekend. And give it back to me Sunday so it smells like you again." That's the most openly needy thing she's ever said to him, and his answer is the book's answer — "as it always was" — he doesn't say anything, he puts it on right then, two shirts in his own kitchen. And her closing thought, the circuit of it — carry it away full, sleep in him, bring it back gone faint to be filled — that's the chapter's title explained: this has become *the new ordinary*. A drawer of her in his bathroom, a shirt on a weekly charge cycle, a man who always hears the car. The extraordinary domesticated. Which is either the happiest thing in the book or the most complete settling-in to the shaped ground she doesn't know she's on, and I genuinely can't decide which, and I don't think the page wants me to yet.

One low note: Randi is entirely absent from this chapter, and after ch 40's MIRA discovery sitting unexploded between them — and the nude photo breach my checkpoint says Pace doesn't know about — the quiet worries me more than a scene would. Nothing felt off *in* the chapter; it's what's *not* in it. Sheri at Thanksgiving is coming, and Vee in Ohio without either of them. I dread and want both.
