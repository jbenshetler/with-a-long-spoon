**Felt read.**

That chapter title, *A Clean Plate* — it works on me three ways, and none of them are the plate of short rib. Vee leaves nothing on the plate tonight, eats "like she means it" and scrapes the polenta to the china, and the same is true of the story she serves Randi: by the end she's handed over everything, even the part she meant to keep. And then there's the other clean plate — the sanded, "crossed-a-white-cloth-clean" version of the parking lot she tries to serve, which Randi refuses to accept. "Don't you fade to firelight and leave me wondering. You just made it smaller. Give me the real one — the harder one." That's the hinge of the chapter, and Randi's best moment in the whole book for me: she doesn't want the polished performance, she wants the truth, and she knows the difference on sight.

The felt center of this chapter is the confession I've been waiting for someone to drag out of Vee since chapter 47: the taste. And it lands exactly as complicated as I'd hoped. Vee builds it as a comedy bit — "Ugh — gross. That's — no. Wrong" — she's been *polishing* the bit, practicing it in the shower, and the polished version arrives and Randi dismantles it with three words: "I do." The whole scene where Vee says "But it's gross" too loud — "too loud for a settled thing" — and her body has "no complaint" while her mouth performs the objection, is the book's whole method in miniature: shame and desire running on separate tracks while Vee narrates the shame out loud and lives the desire underneath. When she says "he's enthusiastic" before her veto machinery can catch the word, I felt the truth arrive ahead of her — same mechanism as the bathroom stall in the last chapter, where Randi's face showed up at the top of her orgasm. Her body keeps testifying against her official story.

Where did it land on me? The wrist. "Randi, who stays? Everything I know about men says the wrist is a toll booth — you pay it and drive on. He parked." That made me melt on Pace's behalf all over again. And the two kisses on the neck — Vee deliberately going back to Tuesday's spot, the kiss landed "before any of her had convened on the question," and Randi's body doing "a small quick thing, a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long" — the ticklish deflection is a lie both of them let stand, and I let it stand too, happily. The Lifesaver tapped against Randi's wrist, the wintergreen shared "one lecture, one flavor" — quiet, warm, and not nothing.

Suspicion? No, but attention. Randi's "I have" — said plainly, in the restaurant-recommendation voice, eyes on Vee the whole length of it — felt like the most honest thing she's said in chapters. And her stillness during the telling ("she'd gone still… all her lights aimed") is not how you listen to a friend's boyfriend stories; that's how you listen when it's you in the story. Vee still can't see it. "You picked well" from Randi is almost unbearable in that light — she's praising Vee's taste in a man while sitting across from the thing she wants.
