## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter landed on me soft and then, thinking back, less soft. We're in Pace's POV, after sex with Randi, and for most of a page I got the thing I've been half-waiting for since chapter 1: Randi undefended. She's lying on his chest, loose, and she talks — really talks — about Vee. "I keep waiting to get bored of her — I get bored of everybody — and it doesn't come." "She got past me before I could help it." I believed every word of it, and it ached, because it's the admission she can only make in the dark, to him, about a woman she can't say any of this to directly. Her wanting Vee has been the engine under this whole book; here it's out of the case, briefly, in her own mouth.

What moved me most was her *eating*. The checkpoint is full of Randi refusing food — the untouched bowl in ch18, the salad-only meal, the thin apple slices as her rationed self. Here Pace fixes her cutlets and she eats **the whole plate** and then, when he asks, says no thank you and takes his hand. I actually teared up a little at "When she set it down, there was nothing left on it." That's not a sex scene doing that; that's the book telling me she's momentarily safe. She also says "Pace, darling" and asks him to pour more wine into a half-full glass — little testing gestures of domesticity she's never allowed before. The chapter she's in is almost the inverse of ch18: there she left the main dish untouched and turned her back; here she asks for food and sleeps against his chest.

The unease: it's real and the page earned it. When Pace describes Vee — "she spends the entire time sure she's too much, and she's the most alive person standing there" — Randi goes still in a way the text explicitly marks as "a different one, the kind that takes holding," and answers "Yeah" after "a beat too long." That's the jealousy from ch18 surfacing and being swallowed. Then she redirects into sex, and Pace's arousal is fed by a private image of Randi and Vee *together* — which she doesn't know he's picturing. There's a small sting of dishonesty humming under the tenderness: both of them are lying a little, in the afterglow, about how much of this is about Vee.

**Heat**: the second sex scene is warm but short, deliberately languid; the charge is more in the naked confession than the act. The most erotic beat for me was Randi drawing down his body "showing him with her own body the thing she was describing" — narrating Vee's body while performing her own. That's the book's whole dynamic in one gesture.

Title: "Gone" triple-loads — Vee is "gone over" Pace, Randi can't get "bored of her," and Randi is *gone* on Vee herself. It reads mostly as Randi's confession, and it doesn't give anything away. Volume title *A Polite Invitation* still feels right: everything in this book is invitation-shaped.

What I dread/hope next: this feels like the calm before Vee learns the truth (the open question I've carried since ch3). Randi saying the feeling out loud makes the reckoning closer, not further.
