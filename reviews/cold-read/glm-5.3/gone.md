**Felt read.**

This chapter knocked something loose in me, and it's Randi's. After chapters of watching her run the table — the smooth voice, the managed face, the mercy that might have been management — the book finally let me see the seams, and it did it in the smallest gestures: she stayed down after. She asked "Is there anything to eat in this house?" — a first. She put on his undershirt without looking for the drawer, like she already knew where it was, and pulled it over her knees. And she ate. "The chicken and every stalk of the asparagus, and the couscous to a line he could have drawn with a ruler" — after the bowl with the single line drawn through the sauce in *Portion*, that untouched bowl that sat between them like an accusation, this is her saying something with a fork she couldn't say with her mouth. I felt relieved and frightened at once, because everything Randi has ever needed has gone somewhere inaccessible — and now it's leaking.

The leak is the ache of the chapter. "I didn't think I'd like her this much. I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come. She got past me before I could help it." That's the woman who decided in his bathroom that the one he found goes back behind the face, "not ever" — and the found one is out on the kitchen floor wearing his shirt, chin on her knees, saying "It's a little pathetic. I love it." And then the moment that actually hurt: Pace, at the sink, describing Vee to the woman who found her for him — "she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her" — and Randi goes still with "the kind that takes holding," and all she can produce is "Yeah. She is." I don't know what that tightening is. Jealousy of Vee, jealousy of *him*, want of Vee, or the grief of watching him love the thing she pointed him at. The page doesn't say, and I'm not going to fix it.

What she does with all of it is the most Randi move possible and it's also the hottest thing in the chapter: she turns him by the hip and takes the shirt off on the kitchen floor and uses Vee's body as dirty talk — "God. What she keeps under there," dragging her own breasts across his chest one way and the other, showing him what she's describing. It's misdirection and confession in one motion; the wanting is real, the object is borrowed, and Pace knows it — "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." I believed his care completely there, the way he's always refused to put a hand on what's hers. But I also felt the thumb of the plan one more time: he imagines both of them at this table, all three of them smiling, and "He wanted to tell Vee. He could not, not yet." *Not yet.* The endgame is still moving.

The second sex scene is charged in a completely different register than the first — she rode him with her eyes shut before, angry about something; now it's slow, and "the blue on her toes the last composed thing left of her," and after, the chill takes her and she *stays*, and sleeps. The shift from *Portion* is the whole chapter in one line.

Vee isn't on the page except as the beloved object of both their attention, and that's its own ache — the two people she trusts most, quietly deciding the shape of her life over chicken and asparagus. "Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body," he says, certain, low. He's right, and it's tender, and it's also a man who pointed at her at a light pole. The title, *Gone*, reads to me as double now: Vee, "so gone over you," gone on her credit card, gone in the way of the happy; and Randi — the composed Randi is *gone*, at least for tonight, and I don't know if she comes back. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, still promises some threshold moment — the induction, or the moment the plan is spoken aloud — and I dread and want it at once.

Next I want the induction night, the dress and the shoes in the room that turns. What I dread: Randi, having eaten and stayed down and wanted out loud, trying to stuff herself back behind the face. And *Sorority* left the blue toenails on the shoe-bench moment — her thumb on Vee's plum toes — humming under all of this. Guess from here: the wanting Randi won't name is going to be the thing that breaks the plan open, not Pace's patience.
