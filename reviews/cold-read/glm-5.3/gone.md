## Reader reaction

Oh, this one undid me a little — and I mean that in a way I'm still sorting, because this is the warmest chapter I've read with Randi in it, and I spent most of it braced for the door to slam, and it didn't.

The chapter opens with her *staying*. After everything — the leaving straight after sex, the turned back, the uneaten stew with the line drawn through it — Randi lies down on Pace's chest and stays. And then she talks. Really talks. "I didn't think I'd like her this much. I keep waiting to get bored of her — I get bored of everybody — and it doesn't come." That line landed on me hard, because it's the most unguarded thing she's said in the whole book, said against his chest where she doesn't have to hold a face. And what she says about Vee — the bigfoot shoe story, "She was *so* embarrassed. That was kind of hot" — that's Randi's desire for Vee arriving in her own mouth again, in that joking register she keeps it in. But then: "She's so free. Her head goes back, and all that hair— God, she's lovely." There's no threesome framing left on it at all. It's just a woman telling a man, in the dark, that she's a little in love with someone. She might as well be telling him about herself.

And Pace — I watched him do the thing he does, and I felt both sides of it. "Tell me," she says, and he gives her the Vee picture, and I know from chapters past that "tell me" is their game, their channel, the only way Randi lets Vee into this bed. But this time it felt different in his body too: he's *letting himself look* — Randi over Vee, then just Vee wound in his sheet. The heat of this chapter, for me, peaked exactly there, in that layered wanting — three people all desiring the same missing woman, in one lamp-lit room. That's a 2, nearly a 3. The sex itself was lovely and slow, "the blue on her toes the last composed thing left of her," but the charge was already in the talk before it.

Here's what gripped me most, and where my unease sits: the moment Pace says "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body," Randi goes still — a different stillness, "the kind that takes holding." Because *she knows who taught Vee to be ashamed*. Not literally, but she knows the machinery she's been running, the steering, the questions at lunch, and I think the stillness is the first crack of conscience or fear of it. "Yeah," she says, after a beat too long. "She is." That beat too long is the whole book in one pause. And then she shifts her hips down and finds him hard and covers the moment with her body. I read that as flight — she felt something true coming up and she fucked it away. The page doesn't say so. But I felt it.

And then the kitchen. This is where I completely lost my defenses against Randi. The woman who wouldn't eat his food, who left straight after, who drew a line through the stew — she asks him to make her something, wears his undershirt with her knees pulled up inside it, eats *everything on the plate*, and says "That was lovely." I nearly cried, honestly. It's the first time in the book she's let him feed her and stayed to be fed. The wine glass half full, "pour me a little more" — she doesn't even want the wine; she wants him standing there holding the bottle, serving her. That's her love language finally bending toward his: he feeds, thin slices, small plates — and she takes it. And "he left it there" when she takes his hand — that small permission held open.

I also caught the little rattle: "your *grandsupervisor*?" — her chin on her knees, teasing him, and him smiling at the stove. There is real tenderness here, both directions. This might be the first chapter where I believed Pace and Randi actually love each other and not just the game.

The title, "Gone" — it took me the whole read to feel it, and it only means something as a negative space: nobody is gone. Vee is absent from the page but more present than she's ever been — she's the third body in the bed, the lamp in the description, the whole subject of the pillow talk. And Randi, who has been "gone" from this house after every sex scene lately, is the opposite of gone tonight. If the title points anywhere for me, it's at that reversal. It also faintly echoes Randi's "She's got it bad" — everyone in this bed is a little gone on Vee. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation* — book one of *With a Long Spoon* — still reads as the seduction of Vee into whatever Pace and Randi are building, and this chapter feels like the quiet consolidation before that invitation is actually spoken aloud. My dread: the warmer Randi gets, the harder the fall when Vee learns the collision was staged.
