## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This chapter undid me a little, and I think it was designed to. After chapters of watching Randi and Pace through Vee's unknowing eyes, we finally get the architects' pillow talk — and it's the warmest, most married thing in the book so far. The sex is incidental; the domesticity is the content. "Will you make me something?" and he's smiling before he's answered. She knows which drawer his undershirts are in. She eats *everything on her plate* — after chapters of the small bowl, the drawn line in the sauce, the mother who lives on egg whites — I actually felt my chest open when she gathered the last of it with her fork and the plate was empty. She asked for food. She asked. That's the loudest event in the chapter, and the page clearly knows it.

The "tell me" motif turns over beautifully: he answers it here, unasked, giving her Vee freely — "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter" — and Randi goes still, the held stillness, the "faintest tightening," before she deflects into sex. Her jealousy has changed targets; she's no longer pressing about *Pace's* weekends with Vee. She's jealous of what he has: "You've spoiled her for being hurried," and worse, that undefended face before the kiss. And the thing she says — "I didn't think I'd like her this much... She got past me before I could help it" — is the plan dying of its own success, out loud, in the one bed where it was made. I believe her. I also believe she still doesn't have anywhere safe to put it.

The hottest beat for me wasn't the sex, it was her voice dropping when she says "What she keeps under there" and dragging herself down him while describing Vee — Randi wanting out loud, in his bed, and *his* arousal spiking "not [at] the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi herself wanting." The page literally names it: the plan's fuel is becoming Randi's want itself.

Trust: Pace still reads good — "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" said low and certain, and "he didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold" — he keeps her confidence even here. And this is the chapter's cold edge for me: Vee exists in this room as a *shared possession*. "You do nice work." They appraise her like a piece he's been commissioned for. "God, you two" over her head, her sex life dissected — "it's romantic" — by the two people she trusts most. I knew this was the structure, but hearing it in bed pillow-talk makes it colder than Randi's steering ever did. And "How's your dissertation?" — the proof where the difficulty is "you can't show it's the answer" — felt like the book winking at itself; I noted it and let it pass.

Chapter title: "Gone" — I assumed it meant Vee being "that gone" over Pace ("so gone over you," Randi says). But the word belongs to Randi too: "God, you two" is her going where she resolved not to go. She's gone. Title gave nothing away; it deepened after.

I want the induction, and I'm braced for it — the dress, the mother, whatever goes wrong. Cassie and the induction feel like where this breaks.
