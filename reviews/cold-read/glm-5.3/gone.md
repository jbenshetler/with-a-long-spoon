## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

Oh, this one got me — and it unsettled me in almost the same breath. After "Portion," which left me genuinely worried about these two (the uneaten bowl, the no-*tell-me*, the back turned), "Gone" opens with the answer before I could even frame the question: she stayed down. He notices it "the way he noticed the lamp, and let it be," and I exhaled. Whatever cracked in Randi that night seems to have cracked something open rather than broken it. She asks — for the first time ever — "Is there anything to eat in this house?" And she eats. "The chicken and every stalk of the asparagus, and the couscous to a line he could have drawn with a ruler." After chapters of food refused, menus policed, the salad eaten while the bowl sat untouched — watching Randi eat his cooking was its own kind of climax in this book's language. I felt relief in my body.

And then she says the thing: "I didn't think I'd like her this much… She got past me before I could help it." Said into his chest, in the afterglow, to the one person she runs nothing on. I believe it. That's the complication I've been circling since the lawn — Randi's delight in Vee has always read as real alongside the machinery, and now the book has put it in her own mouth, to him, in the room where she sets her face down. My feeling about her moved this chapter: less the manager at the table, more a woman being overtaken by something she started. Which doesn't make it kinder. It might make it worse.

Because the rest of the chapter is her running Vee like an instrument anyway. The yeti story told to him with "a beat, fond. 'That was so cute. She was *so* embarrassed.' Softer. 'That was kind of hot.'" — that landed in me two ways at once: I laughed at the retelling (the full bigfoot, the stomping), and then I felt the shape of it, Vee's most mortifying moment carried home as foreplay between the two people she trusts most. Same with "she's got it *bad*. It's a little pathetic. A beat. I love it." That's affection and appetite in one mouthful. The book keeps refusing to separate them, and so do I.

The hottest passage, for me, was not either of the sex scenes — it was the kitchen. Her putting on his undershirt (echoing Vee's flannel, and Vee's stolen sheet — this book keeps dressing its women in his shirts, and each one means the same thing differently), the drawn-up knees, "all of her was inside it but her face and her hands and the blue of her toes" — the blue toenails again, Randi's one step out of line from chapter 1, the last composed thing left of her. And then the striptease where she strips *Vee*: "What she keeps under there… It's *criminal*, Pace. But you already know." She drags herself down his body describing Vee's body. The heat peaks exactly there — and it's triangular heat, desire running through a missing third person, and the page knows it: "It went through him — not the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi… wanting something out loud in his kitchen and not able to stop it." Even his arousal deflects off Vee back to Randi. I don't think the book is being careless there; I think it's telling me whose want is actually breaking loose.

The title, "Gone." All week I'd have read it as Vee — "how far gone she already was," Randi's word for her. But the chapter's real gone is Randi: "She got past me before I could help it." She's the one going. The flannel/shirt handoff also quietly rhymes her with Vee — these two women are converging on the same man, the same rooms, the same shirts, and I felt that convergence as both swoon and dread.

Friction: very little. If anything, Pace's monologue about Vee ("like somebody found the lamp") is the one beat that felt a shade saintly — but the payoff (Randi's held stillness on the chair, "Yeah… she is," then the sudden reach for his jeans as cover) redeems it; her inability to sit inside his praise of Vee was the truest note in the chapter. And the stillness echoes the ch 1 bathroom decision — "not ever" — straining again.

I trust the tenderness completely on this page; the plan has half-dissolved into feeling, and that's what scares me. What I want next is the induction, and I dread it: the dress, the shoes, all three of them in one room, and Vee still not knowing she was ever chosen on purpose. Guessed: someone says Vee's name at the wrong moment. Or Vee sees something — the white undershirt in the wrong drawer.
