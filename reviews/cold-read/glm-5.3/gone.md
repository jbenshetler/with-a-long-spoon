## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well. There it is — the chapter I've been braced for since the light pole, and it didn't land the way I braced for it.

The first thing I felt was a kind of vertigo, because this is *their* side of the wall again, the first time since chapter 3. And the moment of crossing was almost physically disorienting: we open on Randi astride him "loose as she only went afterward," in the house where I've been watching Vee learn to want. It's the same bed. The lamp he keeps warm for Vee is warm for Randi. I knew this — the checkpoint told me they were established lovers — but *knowing* it and lying in it are different, and the book made me lie in it.

And then the chapter did the thing that keeps making this book hard to hold at arm's length: it made the manipulation and the tenderness the same breath, on the page, out loud, between the two people doing it. "She's not ready, though… You'll want to take your time." "I want her to get there on her own." That's the plan being discussed in bed, casually, between fucks — and yet what's actually being discussed, in the words that are said, is *patience*. He wants her to arrive at her own wanting as her own act, which is exactly the thing that has made his courtship feel so clean from Vee's side. It's sinister and it's sincere in the same sentence and I still can't pull them apart. I've stopped trying, honestly — I think the book won't let me, and I think that's the point of a title like *A Polite Invitation*.

But the real event of this chapter is Randi coming undone about Vee, and it undid me a little too. "I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come. She got past me before I could help it." That is not a manager talking. That's the woman who said the helpless "Yes" on the lawn before any cover arrived, now saying it with the cover stripped off, in the dark, to the one person she can't perform for. The yeti-shoe story retold from her side — I laughed, and then her flat "That was kind of hot" put a small chill of recognition on my own laugh, because *I* hadn't admitted that about the shoe-store scene either, and Randi just did, out loud, in bed, to Pace. Her want for Vee is no longer deniable on the page. It's still unowned — she hands it to him as pictures, "What she keeps under there… it's criminal" — but the woman who decided in the bathroom that the found-self goes back behind the mask "not ever" is leaking. The mask is leaking.

And Pace: "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" — said "low, certain, without heat." That's the most I've ever heard him claim knowledge of Vee's interior, and it's *correct*, and it's knowledge he has because he studied her before she ever saw him. My two-directions-at-once feeling about him didn't resolve; it deepened. His speech about her lighting up a room is the single most loving thing anyone has said about Vee in this book — and he says it to Randi, in the bed where the plan lives. The moment that cut deepest: "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." He gave Randi her wanting back as *hers*. That's his whole ethic, applied to the woman he's conspiring with.

Heat: yes, it worked, though it ran cooler for me than Vee's chapters — deliberately, I think. The second ride is gorgeous but it's the *conversation* that's the erotic engine: Randi dragging her breasts down him while describing Vee's body, wanting out loud for the first time, the tightening. Heat as leakage, not performance. And that's hot in a way the book has earned.

Title: "Gone." I read it three ways before I finished the chapter and can't pick — Randi's "She's got it *bad*… it's a little pathetic. I love it" (Vee is gone over Pace); Randi getting gone past her own boredom threshold; and possibly Pace, who doesn't answer "So soon?" — gone already, somewhere, while Randi rides him. It's a short, blank, ominous little word and I like that it won't sit still. The volume — *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation* — now reads almost unbearably apt: the proverb is about supping with the devil, and everyone's being very polite about the invitation.

No friction worth naming; the one moment I paused at was "It's a little pathetic. I love it" — cruelty and adoration in one breath — and I don't think that's the author's thumb, I think that's just Randi. Next: the induction has to be coming, and I dread the book making Vee's triumph night the night the two threads meet. That's a guess from here.
