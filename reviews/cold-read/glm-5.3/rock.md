## Felt read

This chapter undid me a little. After twenty chapters of careful, curated heat — measurements, dresses, folded panties — Pace hands Vee his real name, and the whole book tilts.

The firelight scene is the warmest thing in the book so far: robe, quilt, cold tea, his palm simply resting over her breast "without pressing, without asking." That's Pace's whole erotic grammar in one gesture. Then the Ohio monologue — the Cool Whip containers, the spice rack with its paper seals, "whatever was in it may have been old enough to vote" — is the funniest and most naked Vee has ever been on the page, and Pace receiving it "so exactly that the telling itself became a pleasure" made me melt. I was leaning into him the way she was.

And then it goes dark in a way I didn't predict. "My real name is Peter." Twenty-two — I genuinely sat up. The desk-in-the-hall story landed like a stone: "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" is the best line of the chapter, and the fact that *she* says it, and he answers "That is approximately what happened," told me more about his restraint than ten chapters of "may I." But the Daphne section is the one I'll carry. The page does something brave: it refuses to let me settle. He lays it out in good order, defends it, "named it luck" — and Vee forms "the clean ugly grown-up word" and holds it down under her tongue. The book lets both readings sit: his account is coherent, believed-by-him, *and* the arithmetic lands low and heavy. "You were so young" — that's all she lets past. I held my breath there. That's the checkpoint's Daphne door finally opening a crack, and it's more disturbing for being told this gently.

The scar moment — his hand pressing hers down onto it, "Not tonight" — is the chapter's real title-sense: the rock he won't turn over yet. And the ending, her wrapped around him thinking about Randi ("I had stumbled into her"), re-frames the whole book: six weeks, one piece. I dread and want the collision I can see coming (Randi's jealousy, the credit card, the secret) — but tonight the book just let them be warm, and I let it.

Title: "Rock" — I took three ways: the stability she's found in him, the "rock" of his history he won't lift yet, and the Newton's-laws cosmology he speaks. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* keeps recoloring — nothing here was less than polite, and nothing here was fully honest either.
