## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. After "Gone" — where I sat behind Randi's eyes and watched her fail to get bored of the girl she was sent to manage — this chapter does the opposite: takes away the plan entirely and leaves me in a quiet room with two people under a quilt, and I believed every minute of it. That's what's unsettling about it, in the best way. For forty pages this book has been teaching me to read Pace two ways at once — the man at the light pole and the man who takes two of her fingers into his mouth — and tonight I forgot the light pole. That's the honest report. By the end I was so far inside the warmth that I had to remind myself there's a Randi at all, that she's elsewhere in this same night, and that everything he told Vee here, he also kept.

The felt peak of the chapter isn't the hand on her breast — though that's lovely, the palm holding "the soft weight of it without pressing, without asking," warmth as fact rather than request. The peak is the sequence around Daphne. I've known her name since the beginning — the door he pushed past, the thing that cost more than he knew anything could cost — and the chapter hands it to me almost casually, in his flattest voice, and Vee does the arithmetic the same second I do. "Nineteen and fifteen. Two years." And she holds the word down under her tongue. I held it with her. What wrecked me was "That he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck" — that's the whole book's method in one line, and here it's turned on him. He believes his own cover story about his own wound the same way the courtship is a cover for the plan, or the same way Randi puts her found-self back behind the face. Everyone in this book builds furniture over the thing that happened. I didn't expect to find Pace's own version of it, and I certainly didn't expect it to make me trust him *more* even as it made him more legible as a person who was once a boy nobody protected.

The math monologue — the paintable-inside-infinite-outside, the coastline, Newton's three sentences — did exactly to me what it did to Vee: "the world shifted very slightly around her, not because she understood the mathematics, but because he did." I'd read "It's the language the world is written in" twice to be sure he'd said it that plainly. And then her realization about the lift — "You're right here. It's the lift. It's the same face" — landed with a small click, because it retroactively names what I've been feeling in every sex scene: his total presence. That's the erotic charge of this chapter, weirdly, and it's real even though almost nothing happens below the waist. His hand settling on her bare hip and staying. Her hand finding the scar. "Not tonight" — with no wall in it, "only a door with a time on it." I'm as curious about that scar now as I am about anything in the book, and I love that she left her hand there, palm to palm over it.

Small frictions: "My real name is Peter" — the way the chapter's title just sits there in his mouth, flat, no motion. It's a lovely beat, but I notice the book withheld his age until asked and gave it in a single word, and I did a double-take at "twenty-two" the same way Vee did — I'd been carrying a vaguer, older sense of him. That's on me and on the book both, and I think deliberately: he's barely older than her, and he's been playing thirty-five. Also — and I want to say this carefully — the unopened saffron, the hall-desk, the Daphne arithmetic: this chapter is almost entirely backstory exchanged under a quilt, and there were stretches where I felt the author enjoying the dialogue's rhythm a shade more than the scene needed. Only a shade. The cumin-Ohio exchange is genuinely funny ("Whatever was in it may have been old enough to vote" got a laugh out of me) and "So the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other" is exactly the kind of line I've fallen for him on before. But I noticed the shape of a *conversation chapter*, and I let it take me because it earned it.

Suspicion: honestly, none tonight — and I want to register that as data, because I've spent twenty-odd chapters holding the plan like a stone in my shoe. What I do hold: he gave Vee the name Peter, the scar's location, and Daphne's outline, and he took *his mother out of the answer* — Vee noticed, and so did I, and that little deletion is a door held ajar. He still hasn't told anyone there was a game. And from "Gone" I now know Randi's read on him at this exact stage: "I like taking my time with her," "I want her to get there on her own." This quilt is the taking of his time. The intimacy I swooned over *is* the machinery, and I knew that going in, and I swooned anyway. The book keeps winning that way.

Title: "Rock" — I read it first as nothing, then as the ridge and the stars, the geology under the pretty sky; then, sitting with it, as Peter, the flat plain rock of the real name against "Pace," a name with motion in it. He gave her the standing-still one. Volume title *With a Long Spoon — Book One: A Polite Invitation* still reads to me as the long spoon for supping with the devil — the whole courtship is the polite invitation — and this chapter is the invitation at its most disarming.

Next I want: the scar. The induction and the burgundy dress in actual motion, and whether Randi's "she's not ready" clock and Vee's arrival coincide. And I dread/want the moment Vee learns the coffee shop was staged — I keep testing the story against it and it keeps holding.
