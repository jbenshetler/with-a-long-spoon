# Cold read (grounded) — Bare

*scene: scenes/bare.md · model: glm-5.3 · memory: ck-ch040 + raw ch041..ch044 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This is the bleakest chapter so far, and it's barely three pages long. It reads like the coda the last two chapters were building toward — the whole book has been slowing into this: her best sentence offered on the porch ("I had been eating crackers my whole life"), his careful not-answer, the fight and the magnificent sex that still didn't produce the word, and now this — a bunk bed, a cold room, and a girl taking off the only comfort she has left.

The shirt is the whole chapter, and it's devastating because of what the book has taught me to read in shirts. The flannel from the hike was the first thing of his she kept; in "The New Ordinary" she washed it and made him re-scent it, and that scene was a love scene in everything but sex. Now she puts it on with nothing under it, "the cloth straight onto her, the way she wore it when no one could see" — that's as close to him as the night allows — and for a while it works. And then the sentence that knocked the wind out of me: "it was warm because she was warm... the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." That's not just about a shirt. That's the entire shape of my unease about Pace rendered in one image — everything warm in this relationship may be her own heat, bouncing back to her off surfaces he arranged. The shirt is the dress and the drawer and the bench again: care and design, the same act, readable both ways. She can't tell if she's held or handled, and neither can I.

What moved me most is the ending, and it moved me in a hard direction rather than a warm one. "The cold of the room came down on her bare skin all at once, and it was worse. It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." She stays. This is a girl whose whole story began under a cardigan — a body to be hidden — and here she strips to nothing in a cold room and endures it as a kind of honesty. Every substitute failed: the phone ("there was no text that made it come clear"), the shirt over a shirt ("it hid what it was"), the shirt on bare skin (her own warmth given back). So she gives up on substitutes. She leaves the shirt "down in a heap where a dropped thing lands" — not folded, not on the pillow, and that unordered heap might be the saddest single image in the book, the reliquary dropped. For the first time she chooses the true cold thing over the warm false one, and she doesn't sleep. I don't think this is despair exactly — it feels like the moment before a person stops accepting substitutes. Something is going to get asked.

Cassie asleep below her is doing quiet work — the ballast present but unconscious, the window cracked its two inches, the streetlight off for the night. Even the one person with no hook in her can't be reached at this hour. That loneliness is earned. And Pace — my feeling about him didn't turn here so much as settle darker. In "Seconds" I watched him "assemble something he meant rather than producing something easy," and I couldn't decide whether that was a man guarding a wound or a man rationing truth. This chapter adds nothing he does; it only shows the cost of what he doesn't do. The reader in me knows he's sharing a bed with Randi some nights and steering Vee like weather; the reader in me also remembers the drawer and the juice held to her mouth. Both readings stay live, and the shirt scene holds them perfectly: his smell is "everything he does" — and everything he does has never once included the word.

Erotically this is quiet but not dead — "the cloth straight onto her" and the breathing him in off her own shoulder has real sensual charge, comfort indistinguishable from desire the way it's been all book. But the charge curdles by design; by the end she's bare and cold and it's grief, not heat. That's the right effect. No friction worth naming — maybe one beat where the "no text could answer" meditation states its thesis a touch plainly, but the chapter is short enough to get away with it.

The title "Bare" triple-lands: bare of the shirt, bare skin where the old shame used to demand cover, bare of the word at the center. It's the fourth one-word title in a row (Cropped, Seconds, Under the Rug — well, that one's two) and each has been a scalpel. The cover board — WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION — means more to me now than it would have at the start: the long spoon is the proverb about supping with the devil, keeping your distance from what you dine with, and "a polite invitation" is exactly Pace's whole grammar — "May I," the courtly asking, the question in bed that started all of this rewrapped as a gift. The book promised me nothing on the jacket beyond that line, but that line now reads ominous in a way the early chapters' happiness buried.

What I want next, badly: for the pile of small waits to spill. I want her to say the words out loud to a human face — probably Cassie's, whose flat questions always land truer than anyone's charm. What I dread, marked as a guess: the anthro class and the MIRA outlier are sitting loaded, and Thanksgiving's aftermath (Sheri at his table, Vee home having said "boyfriend" with a question mark on it) has left seams everywhere. My guess is the break comes through Randi, not Pace — something in her face at brunch finally saying what she can't stop saying. I'd follow this book anywhere right now, but I notice I'm no longer reading it for the swoon alone. The cold at the end got into me too.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee; Cassie (asleep in the room, breathing, never awake on the page). Mentioned-only: Pace (his contact name, his shirt, his bed across town).

**Heat:** 1 — the shirt on bare skin and the breathing-in of him carry a real sensual charge, but the chapter spends it on absence; the wanting is grief-shaped, not flame.

**Romance:** 1 — her love is enormous but it fails to land anywhere on the page; the tenderness is all memory and missing, and the strongest beat is its refusal.

**Motifs & images:** His smell / his shirts (recurs from ch 11–12, the rationed flannel, "The New Ordinary"'s re-scenting — now it fails her); the phone and the empty message field (echo of the photo she *did* dare to send, ch 38 — now nothing can be sent); the cardigan / clothes that hide ("over her own shirt it was just a thing she wore. It hid what it was" — first sighting of the concealment logic stated that plainly); the pile of small waits from "Seconds" growing ("the place was not empty" → this joins it); the unordered heap vs. his ordered world — the drawer of sorted things vs. the dropped shirt; the cracked window and Cassie's two inches of cold (her flat guardianship, present even asleep).

**Symbolism:** The shirt as reliquary that turns out to be a mirror — "the heat in the shirt was her own, given back" stands for the whole open question of the book: warmth or orchestration, held or handled. Lying bare in the cold as first honesty — the body once hidden now uncovered not for anyone's eyes but for truth's sake.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens — the girl who takes what she wants ("I wanted it and I took it") now shown unable to take the one thing, and choosing discomfort over soothing, which is new and real. Cassie consistent even unconscious. Pace doesn't act here, but the chapter sharpens the question his not-acting asks; nothing flattens.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Short, compressed, earned. The three failed comforts (phone, layered shirt, shirt on skin) each get exactly enough room; the ending doesn't linger a word too long.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Three chapters running on the same ache — the unnamed word — and I felt the repetition risk, but each has escalated: the attempt, the fight that proved nothing, now the stripped-bare vigil. The honeymoon momentum is clearly over and something is coiling. Holding, tightening — I'd not want a fourth chapter of pure ache without movement; the book owes the coil a spring release soon.
