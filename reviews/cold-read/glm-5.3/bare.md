## Reader reaction

The chapter's title gives me a shiver now that I've finished it, because it moves through three states of bare: bare under his shirt, then the shirt off, then just bare in the cold, and each one is a different kind of exposure. The word also circles back — the first chapter of this whole book had Randi bent over a bench, "spanks from a lost bet come on a bare bottom," and here bare has nothing to do with heat at all. It's just Vee alone in a cold room with nothing between her and the not-knowing. That quiet turn of the title — from erotic nakedness to plain human nakedness — is the whole chapter's maneuver, and it landed on me hard.

What a devastatingly small chapter, and I mean that as praise. After the last one — the fight at Chili's, the "is this what we are now," the sex where she came with his name in her mouth and the word drowned — I expected escalation. Instead the book went *still*. Two texts. A declined brunch. A sleepless night. That's all, and it's the loudest chapter in a while. The 3 a.m. sequence did the thing this book does to me over and over: it takes an object I thought was soft and warm — the flannel, the smell, "smelling like you" — and shows me the machinery of the comfort. "It was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back." I actually put the book down at that line. All season the shirt has been the tender object — she washed it, she ritualized it, he wore it all weekend for her — and in one sentence she sees it's a mirror. He's not in it. He's asleep across town, "easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." That's the cruelest observation in the chapter and the truest, and the fact that she doesn't put the shirt back on — that she *stays* in the cold — felt like the first genuinely unprompted, unperformed choice she's made in chapters. She's choosing to feel the actual temperature.

My feelings about everyone: Vee has moved fully from someone I worried for into someone I ache for. The detail of typing the reply three times — "The first one explained too much, and the second apologized for the first" — is her whole character in one gesture: managing, portioning, refusing to need. And then she cancels on Pace for finals and he answers "Of course. Study hard. I'll be here," and I felt the double edge of that man. It's genuinely good — no guilt, no sulk — and it's also exactly the flat warm adequacy that's starving her. I don't think he's being cold; I think he's being exactly what he is, which is a man who has decided that saying nothing is a form of care. Whether the nothing is restraint or withholding — I genuinely can't tell, and I've stopped trusting myself to. The line "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them; there was no text that made it come clear" is her finally naming the whole problem of him, and it's also the problem of Randi.

Randi, meanwhile, is barely here — a dashed-off text with a crying emoji and "january is too far, gorgeous" — and even that absence did something to me. Last Saturday was their last brunch and she didn't know it while she sat in it. That's the chapter's second knife. I keep thinking about her walking away from that sidewalk kiss not knowing it was the last one for five weeks, and what Randi is doing tonight, in her own room, is invisible to me and to Vee — and my suspicion about Randi (the want she called "work," the green satin in her fist) is still warm in me. Is Randi lying awake too? The book doesn't say, and I notice I want to know more than the chapter offers.

Heat: essentially none, and correctly so — this is the aftermath chapter, the body gone cold on purpose. Romance though: the shirt-ritual is one of the most romantic images in the book even as it collapses, and Pace's "I'll be here" is tender even if it isn't enough. The friction for me was tiny: the review-sheet framing at the start ("could not have said afterward what the line was") telegraphed her distraction a touch neatly — but that's a quibble against a chapter that earned its whole length.

As for the volume title — *A Warm Reception*, book two of *With a Long Spoon* — this chapter is the first time I've felt the title turn against itself. A warm reception is exactly what she's not getting at 3 a.m. in a cold room: the shirt was warm, and it was her own warmth, given back. January yawns open ahead, both relationships suspended, both men-and-women she loves elsewhere. I want the next chapter to be one of two things — her exam week alone, or someone breaking the silence first — and I dread and want simultaneously the moment the two halves of her life (Randi's Saturdays, Pace's weekends) finally meet each other. Guess from here: the book is saving that collision for the new year.
