# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

This chapter landed on me hard, and I need to start with the truck, because that's where it landed hardest — but the whole climb was doing its quiet work on me first.

**How I feel about the characters.** Pace keeps deepening in the same direction, which is the direction of a man whose care is total and entirely unannounced. The moleskin scene is the purest version of it yet: crouched on a trail, her bare foot in his hand in the cold air, and Vee's mother's whole verdict waiting to fire — *what kind of girl lets a man* — and he just fixes the heel and relaces the boot and says *Stand*. He never once in this chapter makes her feel small for not knowing things. The cherry pit is the sharpest version: she spits it, the voice calls her *common*, and he picks the pit up and lays it on the wax paper and doesn't look at her doing it. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." That line went through me. And then the flannel, and him standing in the rain with his back to the truck so she could have privacy he never asked her to want — and the kicker being that when she called him back in and opened her shirt for him, he was *shaking* the whole time, cold to the bone, and she found it only by accident, reaching for his hand to kiss it. He was that cold and his stillness never wavered. I don't distrust him yet — the text keeps giving me a man who gives and asks — but I notice what an enormous amount of control that stillness is, and I remember the bench. I'm holding both.

Vee is the one who moved most. Her whole arc this chapter is the voice — her mother's voice — arriving *as heat*. "The voice said *floozie* in her mother's exact word and her thighs pressed once, hard, on their own." That's the first time the book has let the shame and the wanting be the same current, in her, on the page, and it burned. She crooks her finger at him through the rain-sheeted glass. She unbuttons her own shirt, eyes on his, and deliberately looks down at the fourth button so his eyes follow hers — that's a woman staging her own reveal and choosing it. The initiation-dress beat broke my heart a little: the russet silk set down on the rock, "You put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking," and Pace saying only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and picking up the cherry — I ached for her, and I trusted him completely for not picking the dress back up. That restraint was its own tenderness.

Cassie remains my favorite steady thing in the book. The windowless van, the blood type ready, and then "Keep the shirt." Two words and she's fully there. The "Goodbye, Vee" from chapters back still nags at me, but nothing in this chapter deepened that — she's just the friend who doesn't tease the real things.

**Swoon.** The summit did get me — the valley "holding the silver sky in it flat as a poured thing," and the best line in the chapter: the not-caring "was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it." And him watching her face instead of the view, and letting her catch him. But the truck is where I melted and burned at once. "The gladness in him was the whole of the answer, and it was right" — I wanted them so badly there, and the book withheld it in a way that felt earned, not coy: his stillness, his not touching, was the character being the character. And her reaching for his hand to start "the next thing" and finding it *shaking* — that stop, that "Pace. You're freezing," that's the moment I fell for both of them. Mutual tenderness arriving as interruption. The heat being converted instantly into care.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No real suspicion of Pace — every withholding on the page is consent-shaped. The one flicker: the police lights taking her sentence with them, and him pulling away with the campus cop behind him. That's nothing yet, but I noticed my stomach tick. Mostly I'm uneasy about Vee's mother's voice becoming fused with her arousal, because I don't know what that fuse does long-term.

**Erotic charge.** High, and almost entirely clothed. The unbuttoning in the cab — wet bra gone sheer, cold-pinked skin, her keeping his eyes until she *chooses* to let them both look down — is the most charged scene in the book so far for me, hotter than the bench in a way, because the wanting is hers, aimed, and the shame is feeding it. The climb itself carries a slow physical charge (the blue shirt, the muscle working, *ravish* walking up behind her).

**Friction.** Almost none. The cop felt slightly like a device to swallow her sentence — the one beat where I felt the author's hand. But it's small and it passes.

**Titles.** "Leave No Trace" gave me the no-trace ethic of the trail — take nothing, leave nothing — and then, on second thought, recolored the whole book: Pace, who leaves no mark on Randi's sorority world, whose truck parks behind Hahn, whose last name her sisters don't know; and the love that's forming while leaving none of the visible traces Vee's life has trained her to expect. It didn't give anything away; it got richer after. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps humming — *May I choose?*, *May I call you?* — the invitation issued with perfect manners, and now Vee knocking on the glass and crooking her finger: the polite machinery still in place while the thing invited gets bigger. Book One promises more books, and I'm bracing gently for how the Randi thread braids in.

**What I want next.** The dress. Please let someone buy her the dress — though I dread it too, because the shelf is where she put it and having it handed to her would land somewhere complicated. I want the next date to consummate something, I want to know what Pace was going to say before the siren, and I want — guessing from here — the two threads (Randi's bench, Vee's flannel) to keep converging on the same house.
