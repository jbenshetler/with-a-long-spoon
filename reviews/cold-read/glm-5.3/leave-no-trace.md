## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

The summit did a number on me before anything erotic happened. "It was huge, and it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it… and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part of all of it" — that's the pool underwater again, the place where she isn't a problem, and the fact that the book let her have this *unengineered* pleasure (the view isn't a move; even Pace's blue shirt gets swallowed by it) let me relax in a way I haven't for chapters. And then he was watching her face instead of the valley, and let her catch him, and gave her the rest of the looking alone. I melted there, honestly. Whatever the plan is, the man on this page is paying attention.

The moleskin scene is where the chapter's title locked in for me — "Leave No Trace" — and it's doing double duty. He packs out her cherry pit without making her small ("He picked the pit up between two fingers… and didn't look at her doing it"), he fixes the boot, he leaves no trace of correction, no mark on her dignity. And then the chapter turns it: the one trace he can't erase is the flannel she's standing in at the end, and the thing that happened in the cab "holding still in the warm air." He leaves no trace and leaves a flannel. I love that the title is also the woods ethic — this book hides its meaning in plain household objects the way it always has.

The undressing in the truck is the hottest thing in the book so far for me, and it's hot precisely because *she* drives all of it — she raps on the window, she crooks the finger, she chooses to look down at the fourth button so they both see. And the mother's voice arriving as fuel instead of brake — "wanted to be the very thing the voice said" — is the interior turn I read this book for. The shame converting to heat, on the page, honestly. What undid me, though, was the ending of it: she reaches for the next thing and his hand is *shaking*. He stood in the rain so she could change in private, and never said one word about it. She was about to give him the next thing and his cold stopped her, and she chose warmth over the more her body was going at. That restraint — hers, his — is the chapter's real climax for me, and I mean that as praise.

The dress beat aches. "You put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking" — and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and doesn't pick it up again. Given everything I know about the plan and about how this man gives, I'd bet money (a guess from here) that the russet silk shows up before the induction. I both want that and dread it, because every gift she receives tightens the web she can't see. That's my unease, and it's old unease, not new: nothing on this page felt off in itself. If anything, I keep noticing how much of this chapter runs *against* the machinery — the rain, the siren, the shaking hand are things nobody planned, and the chapter is at its warmest in exactly those unplanned places.

The cop and the siren taking her sentence with it — that friction landed as deliberate ache rather than author's thumb, but I felt the yank of it: I *wanted* the small thing she was going to say. And Cassie's absence registers; Vee went up a mountain and came back in a man's shirt with nobody in her life the wiser. The drift continues without a word spent on it.
