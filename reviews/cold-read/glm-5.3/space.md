## Reader reaction

What a chapter to be handed after the last few. I've been living in Vee's head so long that the cold-open chapters — the ones back behind the plan — hit like a change of altitude. And this one is the mirror I didn't know I was waiting for: I read "broken-in" from inside Vee's skin, the shoes, the scarf, the thumb behind her knee, and now I get the same morning played back through Randi's mouth, in bed, with Pace inside her.

First, the felt read: this chapter is hot, and it's hot in a way that made me uncomfortable and complicit and unable to look away. "She made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it. / I heard it." That line landed like a stone in me. Everything tender in the shoe scene — the holding of her balance, the catching, the steadying — is re-narrated here as appetite, as *taking*, and Randi's enjoyment of the telling is unmistakable, physical, coming up in her body while she speaks. The generosity of "broken-in" was real; so is this. That's the book's whole trick again: the two truths not splitting apart but sitting in the same sentence.

Randi worries me more after this chapter, and also charms me more. "I wanted to keep her" — said to Pace, plainly fond, and I believed it entirely. But the scene under the skirt is Randi taking something Vee never offered. Vee thought she was being dressed; Randi was touching. And there's the moment at the table — her folded clothes, the hand flat on the surface, "it was not the look you give a table" — I've learned to read this book's small stillnesses, and that one hums. She's in his house, where Vee now also is. Something's being compared. I won't guess further than that the comparison is happening.

Pace came off more sympathetically than he has in a while. "He did not say what Vee had said to him at the bar... those were his, and that was Vee's" — he keeps her confidence even from his co-conspirator, and his relief that she went *somewhere*, to Randi, felt unguarded. And "Vee would come or she would not, and he would not text her. That was not a thing he had decided. It was only what he was" — that's the version of Pace I like trusting. Though of course Randi has already stage-managed the whole repair: "Tomorrow night. You go in there sure." He thinks he's leaving her room; her friends have furnished the room she'll walk into. The dramatic irony is now the air the book breathes.

The structure of the evening interests me too: this is the second "cold" chapter in a row where the plan-side couple takes the bed while Vee, offstage, spends her day getting ready. Randi's two complaints — "she's not ready," the uneaten stew — seem answered tonight by sex that's different: she comes *to* him, she asks for his mouth (then stops it — "Not like that. Not yet," which reads to me as wanting the other thing, the telling, the shared picture), she stays down afterward. This is Randi at her most open, and it's open *about Vee*. Her jealousy, if that's what it is, has fused with her want; she's not competing with Vee anymore, she's having her through him. That's the thinnest, sharpest ice the book has walked yet, and I felt every step of it.

Erotic charge: peaked twice — "I heard it," and the blue lacquered toes clawing the sheet, the callback to ch 1. The prose in their bed scenes has a precision I find genuinely erotic rather than clinical; it works on me. What kept it from being purely hot is that I kept seeing Vee's face from this morning in the same room where the story of her is being used as fuel. That friction is the charge.

The title: "Space." I read it first as what he gives her (the not-texting, the day). By the end I think it's also the space Randi just occupied — Vee's one blind spot, the gap between what she showed her friend and what her friend did with it. And maybe the space all three of them are circling in. It sits oblique but not empty. The volume title, *A Warm Reception*, gains another shade — the house that receives everyone warmly, the "reception" Vee is being dressed for, the welcome each woman gives the other while holding something back.

What I want next: tomorrow night. Vee walking in wearing the black, the scarf, the cognac heels, "sure." And I dread it and want it in the same breath, because I know now that her sureness is Randi's construction. My guess (only a guess): the repair scene will be lovely, and something in me will keep hearing Randi's "I heard it" underneath it.
