## Reader reaction

The chapter opens on Pace alone with shavings on his shoulders and a Friday he doesn't know what to do with — "no one was coming" — and then the Mercedes engine comes up the hill and Randi's "whole face opened" at the door. That face stopped me. The text itself flags it: "She had never once come through that door pleased and let it show like this." After chapter 18's turned back and untouched bowl, I'd been carrying Randi as a wound tightening around itself. Tonight she's lit. And I know what lit her — I was in Randi's room this morning with Vee, I watched the Edna Mode laughing fit and the cognac shoes going on under the long skirt. Her glow tonight is *Vee's* glow, reflected, and the deadbolt going over under her hand — that old first gesture, usually a closing — read to me this time like someone arriving to stay.

The couch conversation undid me a little. "I wanted to keep her," Randi says of Vee's nine-o'clock confession, and I believed it completely; it's the warmest thing she's said about anyone in the book. And Pace — "those were his, and that was Vee's" — he won't hand over what Vee said at the bar even to Randi. That discretion is the same muscle he used on the walk after the hike, and it deepens my trust in him past everything. But one line snagged: "The day was in it, as if the day were his to give, and he set that down beside the rest." Even Pace registers that Randi is speaking like the schedule belongs to her — she authored Vee's approach this morning ("give it a whole day so it reads like something you *chose*") and now she's authoring Pace's waiting too. He notices, and sets it down. I set it down too, but my hand stayed on it.

The sex burned me, and I want to be honest about *how*. Randi stops his mouth before she comes — "Not like that. Not yet" — she won't be given it; she takes it while telling. And the telling is the act: "I had her standing in my room this morning. In her bra." What lands hardest is "She let me." Because I was there. I read that scene, and to Vee it was dress-up, two girls and a problem and a whole morning — "standing in her bra in front of Randi was no event." Randi has it as an offering. The thumb in the back of the knee *happened* — Vee's breath "did something she hoped wasn't out loud," and Randi, mid-ride, panting, confirms: "I heard it." So it's not invented, it's *edited*, and the editing is simultaneously the hottest thing in the chapter and the thing that keeps a cold finger on my neck. "I haven't stopped thinking about her" — the want said out loud at last, undisguised, in the one bed where it's allowed to exist. My body was fully in the scene — the blue lacquer "the only color on the bed," her voice going "somewhere without her" — and my mind kept cutting back to Vee giggling on the rug. Both at once. That doubleness is this book's signature and here it's working at full power.

The clothes on the table: she folds them — she, who historically leaves — looks at her hand flat on the surface, jiggles the white-gold band. "It was not the look you give a table." I don't know what that is. The band has been an open question since chapter 15 and here it gets touched at the exact moment she behaves like someone staying the night. I hold it open. And then "Give her a day," she says into his chest, half asleep, "as if she were promising him something. *She's going to be so beautiful when she walks in. You wait.*" Tender — and also the pointing game still running, one remove down. Randi is dressing the stage for Vee's entrance, from both sides of it. I felt both things and I'm not picking one.

Then Sheri. New, and instantly vivid — the small frame, the bleached ponytail, the way she remarks on women "the way other people remarked on the weather." And here I have to mark a suspicion as a suspicion, because the page never says it: the blonde at the Rusty Nail. Sheri was there Thursday — "After you left Thursday, I kept an eye on her." She comments on Vee's backside unprompted. She has two years of history riding this handlebar, and the text tells me outright she "could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years." The blonde's line to Vee — "knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to... he's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night" — reads to me like a woman who knows his habits from the inside and is burning. I could be wrong. I'm watching her.

Pace talking about Vee without deciding to — the walnut chair, the burned hand, "He heard himself going and didn't stop" — is the most unguarded I've ever seen him, and Sheri names it: "Listen to you." He doesn't know what there is to listen to. That's the chapter's sweetest ache. And Sheri's closing arithmetic — "Two of them at once... If that's not already complicated, it will be. But I bet it's worth it" — is the first outside view of the arrangement anyone has given, and it said my dread out loud.

**Space.** The title means Pace's signature move to me now: the room he leaves, the text he won't send — "That was not a thing he had decided. It was only what he was." But it's also the space inside Randi ("Not yet"), and the space between what happened in her room and the version told in his bed, and the fact that Vee, absent from every scene, is the gravity both halves orbit. The whole chapter is space around her. The series title, *With a Long Spoon* — the proverb about supping with the devil — reads more like a warning label with every chapter: how close can anyone safely get to this table? Book Two's *A Warm Reception* keeps promising warmth, and delivering it, while the geometry under it stays hidden. What I want next is Vee walking in — the black dress, Randi's shoes, the scarf, "you walk in there knowing" — and I expect I'll get it, since the ride was Saturday morning and the appointment is Saturday night. What I dread is the bill, in every sense: the credit card, and the moment Vee learns who Randi is to Pace. Sheri says it's coming. I believe her.
