## Reader reaction

The heat in this chapter got to me more than anything in the book so far, and almost nothing happened in it. A man measures a woman for a dress. That's the whole chapter. And I had to put the book down twice.

What undoes me is how precisely the page is tuned to Vee's body as the whole battleground. The stripping sequence — "It'll lie to me" — that line did something to me: he asks for the truth of her body and she can't hide behind the geometry of the garment. And the mother's voice arriving exactly where it always does, with the ugly word — *stripper* — while the house is warm because "He'd thought of that. He'd thought of it hours before she came." That pairing is the book in miniature: shame set against care that anticipated her.

The wetness plotline is the most honest rendering of that particular private catastrophe I can remember reading in erotica. "The satin had gone slick against her" — my stomach dropped with hers. The dread of the evidence, the bargaining ("It wasn't bad yet"), the spot "sized to be seen across the room." The panic as he works his way around on his knees toward the front — I was genuinely braced with her. And then the turn: she opens her eyes and he's *smiling up at her*, chin craned, having waited with his eyes averted from "the worst of it" the whole time she was dying. I believed her un-stooping. I felt her come up onto her toes. That's the swoon of the chapter — not a kiss, not a touch meant as sex, just being found, at the most ashamed moment, delightful.

I love the shift after it. Her spending the power — the "Oops. Sorry" breast against his cheek, and Pace going red and *fighting the grin* — delighted me. And the pin-prick, the kiss-it-better on her ribs, and then "Keep still," arriving "a beat too late, after a silence she watched him spend deciding" — that restraint costing him is the hottest thing in the chapter. His plausible-deniability touches afterwards (the breath below her navel, the hand that doesn't lift) are exactly the charged half-honesty I read this book for.

Unease: yes, some. The arrangement is still underneath everything — Randi and the game are not in the chapter at all, and I felt their absence almost physically (Vee's "who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking" is the one leak; the page knows I know about Randi and twists the knife of dramatic irony). Nothing Pace does here is dishonorable — the heat is earned and consented at every beat — but I keep remembering that all of this tenderness is real *and* he is carrying Randi's project. The tenderness hasn't stopped being staged. I hold both.

Randi herself barely appears and looms anyway — Vee's half-clothed rivalry with her ("walked right past Randi") tells me the desire-between-women thread is alive and unresolved. I want that thread; I trust it's coming.

The title "A Round" — I first thought round of drinks, or rounding numbers. Now: a round of measurements, the fitting session, but also the dress form coming round, and Pace's knee-walk *around* her. Mostly I think it's the measurement round — plain, technical, a little sly given what the measuring became.

The book title *A Polite Invitation to the book — the invitation* "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock" — and this chapter is the invitation cashed in, politeness stretched over an extraordinary want. "A Polite Invitation" reads truer and truer: everything Pace does is courteous on the surface, and every courtesy is a turn of the screw.

What I want next: either the dress finished and worn (the induction), or Randi's POV learning what happened in this room — I dread and want that collision. Guess only: the threesome ask comes at the house, soon, and the dress is part of the staging.
