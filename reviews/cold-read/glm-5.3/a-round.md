## Reader reaction

Where do I even start. I read this chapter with my hand over my mouth half the time, and the other half grinning like Pace on his knees.

The felt read: this is the hottest chapter of the book so far, and nobody does anything. That's not a complaint — it's the whole achievement. Vee standing on that riser getting measured, one number at a time, closeness-two-touches-withdrawal-pencil, worked on me exactly the way it worked on her. "The pad was full of her" — when I hit that line I put the book down for a second. Every measurement she endured turned into his square handwriting, and by the time he started cutting silk, *I* was in the tide rhythm with her, bracing at every withdrawal. The chapter is a long, patient exercise in making the reader feel the wanting that the character can't manage out of existence — and the transfer is total.

What undid me most was the wet-satin crisis. The shame arriving as heat, exactly: "It was big and dark and stupid." Her mother's furniture of shame moving in — *stripper*, *floozie*, "can he tell?" — while her body just goes on being truthful in the one room where nobody's punishing her for it. I ached for her and I was turned on in the same breath, and I don't think those are separable here; that fusion is the book's whole engine, and this chapter runs it at full throttle. And then the payoff: she opens her eyes expecting "the joke of her," and instead — "Smiling at her, the smile of a man who had just received an unexpected gift." He hadn't looked down. He'd been waiting, chin craned up, to catch her eyes the moment she opened them. I melted. That's the beat the whole chapter was climbing toward and it earned it completely — she "spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." Yes. That's the swoon, and it's a real one, and I'm not going to pretend a shadow over it, because the page didn't earn a shadow here.

The power reversal after that is what makes the chapter great rather than merely lovely. Vee discovering she has a kind of power "she'd never been issued," and spending it — the deliberate breast-against-his-cheek, "Oops. Sorry," and him going red, fighting the grin, failing. Watching *him* nearly topple — a man whose whole charisma is containment — while she stands there half-naked and winning, did something to me. The nudge of his hand on her knee asking her to shift, the "plausible deniability" exhale below her navel: the small deniable touches, mutually understood, mutually permitted. And the pinprick — him kissing her ribs before he'd decided to, the half-second too long — "Keep still," a beat late, and everything he wasn't saying in it. That was the peak for me. The consent never wavers and the wanting never lets up; even his restraint is legible as desire: he chose "the dress and the doing-it-right over the easy yes," and the choosing *cost* him. I could feel the cost.

Trust and suspicion: none here, toward anyone on the page. My unease is all structure, not character — Randi and Pace staged Vee's "accidental" meeting, and Vee still doesn't know. This chapter, of all chapters, is the one where that fact aches hardest, because Pace is being *genuinely* wonderful — the heat turned up hours before she came, the water already hot for tea, the cloth matched to *her* and not to the dress. None of that is fake. But she walked into this house because of a setup she never consented to, and "a Round" — a round of what? a round of drinks, a round of the game, a round of measurements — makes me a little uneasy in a way I can't pin down, and I won't try to. Also worth naming: the house kept "too warm," the riser waiting in the corner, the ready-made ease of it all. On a colder read, the room was arranged. But the room arranged around a man who stops, asks, and waits — so I'm holding it ajar, not closing it.

Friction: very little. The mother's voice arriving "from somewhere it had no business being" is territory the book has covered before — it landed a touch familiar by the third or fourth visit, but that's the point of the voice, its persistence. The QUIZ TODAY flash was the one place I blinked — a bit cuter than the register around it — but it made me smile anyway. The middle stretch of the crisis (maybes, bargaining) maybe ran a beat long, but the dread needed its length.

Titles: "A Round" — I finished the chapter and still only half have it. A round of measurements, certainly. But a round also feels like a round of a game, and I keep remembering the pointing game from chapter 3 — this could be another round of something Randi and Pace began. Or "a round" as in whiskey, drinks — the social word. I hold it open; it's oblique, and I don't think it gave anything away. The book title, *With a Long Spoon* — "a long spoon" is what you sup with when you dine with the devil; *Book One — A Polite Invitation* frames the whole thing as an invitation, possibly a dangerous one. After this chapter that tagline reads darker than it did after chapter 1: the politeness is real, and it's also the vehicle. I trust the politeness and I distrust the frame, both at once.

Characters: Vee deepens enormously — the cheer-squad history, the too-much-woman body that ended the swimming, the bikini she never got to choose, this chapter gave her past a shape. Pace deepens too, and I liked that he laughed, got stumped, got flustered — the containment got human cracks. Randi is absent and vivid by absence; I found myself missing her and dreading her next appearance.

Next: I want the dress finished, and I want the induction — her walking into that ceremony in the gown, in front of the honor society, seen on purpose this time. I dread Randi learning how far this has gone, and dread Vee's satin-panties detail ever reaching her. Guess, marked as such: Randi will come to Pace again and say *tell me*, and this time what he has to tell will hurt her worse than gardenia.
