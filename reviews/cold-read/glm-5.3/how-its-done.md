# Cold read (grounded) — How It's Done

## Reader reaction

The Cassie half of this chapter might be my favorite scene in the book so far. It's the cleanest pleasure I've had reading it — Vee tumbling in with the story she can't hold, Cassie catching the shirt ("*Vivienne*"), and the whole comic escalation of "are you sure he's not gay" and "I was grinding on *him*." I laughed out loud at Vee patting her own ass like a witness indicating a diagram. But underneath the comedy the scene is doing something quiet and true: Vee tells Cassie almost everything and it holds. "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" — that's her translating Pace into her own grammar now, the "may I" become hers. And Cassie's answer, "I think I have it," is the closest anyone in this book gets to just *receiving* Vee without steering her. My trust in Cassie, already total, settled deeper.

Then the lunch, and my skin started prickling. Everything Randi does in that booth is masterful and I hated watching it work on me anyway. The scarf story first — which is Randi handing Vee a pornographic picture of herself tied up and helpless, and Vee's heat rising "a heat she had no shelf for." Then Vee catches the tightness, the thumbnail on the glass rim, and I felt the dread with her — and then Vee *chooses* the kinder reading, "because you want it to be true," and I watched her talk herself back into safety. That's the chapter's real event: not what Randi extracts, but that Vee does the extracting to herself. The left-hip benediction ("all the best ones start at the left hip") is pure handler's patter — warm noise that means nothing and moves Vee one *yeah* at a time toward the thing she meant never to say. And then, at the very lip of the confession — the wet panties, the shame-arousal, the thing "she had folded up the smallest and pushed the deepest" — Randi closes the door: "You don't have to tell me."

And I genuinely don't know what to do with that. The reader in me wants it to be mercy — Randi watching the same woman she's orchestrating toward the bench, refusing to take the secret before it's given, the way Pace refused to take what Vee didn't bring. It reads like a line she won't cross. But it also reads like a professional knowing exactly when to leave a fish hooked rather than landed — the secret left half-out "with nowhere to go" is not the same as the secret kept. I'm holding both, because the page holds both, and that ambiguity is where all my unease about Randi lives now.

The kiss at the curb undid me a little. Randi's hand on the hinge of Vee's jaw, the second press with "something in it the first one hadn't" — my pulse went up, and then Vee's fingers went to her own mouth and the chapter ended with her scanning the windows in fear, and I sat there cold. That's the whole book in one gesture: heat, then the policing voice arriving with a headcount of dark windows. Vee's explanation ("*French,* it's a French thing") is the flimsiest self-deception she's performed yet, and she builds it "almost before her mouth was her own again." The want is getting too big for the shelves she has.

The money details landed hard too — the right side of the menu, the Rombauer costing more than everything on hers, the card she won't let Randi cover. Vee's pride is real and it's buying her a seat at a table where she's being worked. And the Mercedes closing around her "like the car was tucking itself in around her" — every luxury in this book arrives as something that *envelops* Vee. She notices Randi's plain white-gold band, the one fixed thing; I noticed that I've never been told where it came from.

Friction: minor. "All the best ones start at the left hip" is so exactly calibrated I almost felt the author's hand — but Randi is established as this good, so it earned it. The tit-goddess line made me wince and laugh at once, correctly.

The chapter title — "How It's Done" — lands triple for me: the bra-and-tailoring line ("it's just how it's done") is literally it; but underneath, both halves of the book are "how it's done" — how a woman is unhinged from her shame, by the tailor and by the friend, one with patience, one with patter. It doesn't spoil; it chills slightly on reflection. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels more pointed every chapter — every trap so far is an invitation, and every one has been accepted.

Next: I dread and want the dress's finishing, and whether Vee ever gets to finish her sentence to Randi. Guess, but I fear the bench is getting closer, and that Vee's secret — the wetness, the wanting — is the thing the whole plan is actually about.
