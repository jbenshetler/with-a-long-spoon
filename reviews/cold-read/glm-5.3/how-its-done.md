## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

I came out of the measuring chapter flushed and disarmed, and this chapter is the come-down — but the kind that turns out to be its own high. Vee telling Cassie the story is pure delight: Cassie deduces the shirt ("Vivienne." "Don't."), and I love her for it. "I was on a box" repeated twice made me laugh out loud. But the scene does something quieter too — the thing Vee can't say to Cassie, she circles: "Like I was supposed to be there." That's the whole afternoon compressed.

The lunch scene is where the chapter earned its title. Randi's interrogation is one of the most charged stretches of the book so far — and it's all fully clothed, all words. "Then he's right in front of you. On his knees. Looking up. And what did he *see*?" I felt Vee being walked, "one small *yeah* at a time, down and down." That's the heat of the chapter: not the confession itself, which never comes — Randi pulls the door shut with "You don't have to tell me." I read that as her taking the secret for herself, wanting it, not wanting to share it with anyone, least of all Pace. The knuckle bite told on her.

I do feel for Vee's suspicion — she notices the tightness, the thumb under the nail of the water glass rim, and *chooses* the kind interpretation because she wants it to be true. The page lets both readings sit there, and I sat with both. The money details hurt in the best way: the wine costing more than her whole lunch, the debit-card drop, "tipped well, because the other thing she would not do was be the broke girl who tips badly." That's Vee's entire character in one gesture.

And the ending. The kiss. I didn't see it coming and yet it was completely prepared — every warm touch from Randi has been building to a mouth that lands on a mouth. Vee's scramble to explain it away ("*French,* it's a French thing") is exactly her mother's furniture being dragged back into the room — and the colder thought, the windows, the fear of being seen, lands like the book's whole theme turned inside out: being seen was beautiful in Pace's sunroom and dangerous in front of the dorm. That double is the chapter's real work.

The bondage anecdote read as Randi handing Vee a wrapped picture of herself — helpless, laughing, letting go — with Pace's name sanded off. The Hermès scarf story is her way of teaching Vee the room exists. The "cold in the house" / "Cold... Hmm." moment was the sharpest beat — Randi catching the lie and letting Vee think she got away with a correction.

Where I am with people: Pace deepens — the heat turned up hours before she arrives is the most Pace detail imaginable. Cassie is the true friend; the gap between what Vee tells Cassie and what she almost tells Randi says everything about who has her trust and who has her *want*. Randi frightens me a little now, in the interesting way — the smile "less performed, a thing spent," the interrogation, the thumb at the glass rim. Not villainous. Hungry. The chapter title "How It's Done" now reads as the lie Randi sells Vee ("nobody measures over a bra... it's just how it's done") — etiquette as seduction. And the book title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps proving itself: everything is invitation-shaped, nothing is forced, and the pull is all the stronger for it.

Guess from here: the dress is being built as bait, not couture; and someone at those dorm windows will matter eventually.
