## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

Okay. I need a minute, because that lunch scene did something to me that the truck and the measuring didn't quite — it *worked* on me, and then it turned in my hands, and I'm still a little winded.

First, the Cassie half. This is the Vee-and-Cassie show at its best, and it moved me how completely Cassie has come around without abandoning herself. "Vivienne." "Don't." — that's their whole grammar in two lines. And her genuine, funny, unembarrassed absorption of it all: "Are you sure he's not gay?", Vee's clinical "definitely not gay" testimony, both of them laughing on the bed. What landed hardest was Vee's "Like I was supposed to be there," and Cassie's hand on her knee, once, and taken back. These two are the clean water of this book and I felt the warmth of the whole scene because of it. Also — "I locked eyes with him. And then I let him look." Vee *naming her own yes* out loud to the one person she trusts, and Cassie answering "You did," quietly. That's the consent made real, retroactive and glad.

Then the Mercedes, and my stomach started going down before I knew why. The smell of the leather, the window tucking itself in around her, Vee reading the right side of the menu first "the way you read a road in fog" — the money in this chapter is doing real work on me and on Vee simultaneously, and I felt her checking her scuffed pump under the booth like a sock coming down in class.

And Randi. The tickling story — I'll be honest, that story *got* me. It's the closest we've ever been allowed to see her real thing (bound, helpless, control taken — the bench, in costume), told as a joke about a beautiful idiot, and the picture it put in Vee's head ("Randi naked and helpless and laughing... that finished surface finally cracked all the way open") is a heat she "would have died before naming." That's the chapter's whole engine in one paragraph, and it's the first time Vee's buried want of Randi has come this close to being *felt by the reader as heat*, not just noted as fact. Heat rating honestly climbs on that paragraph alone.

Then the extraction. It's beautifully done — "It's just how it's done," the easy authority of "anybody who's had something made knows that," and Vee giving up her shame piece by piece, "led, one small yeah at a time, down and down." And here's where my swoon curdled into something colder, because I *watched it work on me too*. "Which hip?" "The left one." "All the best ones start at the left hip" — Randi's winging it, improvising the ritual of normalcy, and it lands like a benediction, and Vee drifts into the dreamy place, and the secret rises in her throat — the soaked-satin secret, the one thing she told no one, not even Cassie —

"You don't have to tell me."

And the door closes *softly*. And I sat there and felt the cruelty of it, and I'm still not sure how much of it was cruel. This is what unsettles me more than anything in the book so far: Randi pulled Vee all the way out onto the ice and then refused to catch her. Was it mercy (the secret stays hers, as Pace kept the froyo confession)? Was it jealousy (Randi can't hear Vee's arousal *at Pace* because of what it does to her)? Was it control — take everything, give nothing of herself? The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but I notice Randi got her whole afternoon's entertainment and Vee got left holding an unlatched secret, "half out of her with nowhere to go." The hush was "simply over." That coldness in the exit is real and the text earned my unease — Vee herself reads the tightness in Randi's thumb on the glass and reaches *past* the kinder interpretation toward "she thinks I'm awful," and then talks herself back. I don't fully trust her talking-herself-back.

And the kiss. Randi's mouth, the second press "with something in it the first one hadn't," and Vee's fingers going to her lips, and the frantic self-explanation — *French, it's a French thing* — explained away "almost before her mouth was her own again." And then the scan of the windows, counting dark squares, the fear of being *seen* wanting this. That ending landed hard on me: Vee's immediate instinct isn't what the kiss meant to her, it's who saw. That's her mother's furniture, applied now to Randi. The book is moving the shame from Pace-shaped places into Randi-shaped ones, and I felt that migration in my chest.

I do still feel the pull everywhere — the singing in the car, "the best one of her life," Vee not minding being looked at "not one bit." And the tenderness in Cassie's half is entirely real. But the lunch is the first time the hunt has felt like a hunt to me from the inside of a scene — Vee walked in with a pressed, safe version and was *dismantled into telling the truth*, and the woman who dismantled her kept her own face perfectly shut. I don't think Randi is cruel; I think she's barely holding herself together (that thumb under the nail — she does the bottle-cap thing, remember), and Vee is the one paying retail for it.

The title: "How It's Done" — it read three ways for me by the end. The innocent one: the dress, the craft, *that's just how it's done*, measuring over a bare line, Patience. Paying attention. The seductive one: Randi's line, delivered as knowledge Vee's been missing her whole life — the letting-go speech, "a little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." And the dark one: the extraction itself. This is how it's done — how you get a guarded girl to hand you her most hidden thing and thank you for the privilege. All three fit and I don't think I have to choose yet. The series title, *With a Long Spoon — Book One: A Polite Invitation*, sits heavier after this chapter than before; the long spoon is the one you sup with the devil with, and "polite invitation" is exactly what every step of this has been — nobody has *once* done anything to Vee she didn't say yes to, and the yeses keep getting easier to obtain.

Next: I want the dress finished; I dread and want the moment Randi's two worlds touch in Vee's view. Guess only: that "see you soon" and the kiss in the fire lane will have consequences — someone will have seen, or Randi will do it again and Vee won't be able to explain it away a second time. And I keep waiting for Cassie to walk into a scene she shouldn't.
