## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter knocked the wind out of me. After watching Vee walk out of Pace's house with her face turned away, I expected the book to follow her — and instead it cut to Randi, in Gstaad, in a fairytale, and I finally understand what "Fairytale" means as a title. It means the thing Randi keeps choosing over the truth. Every word of the text she sends Vee is true and none of it is the thing — that's the whole chapter in one line, and honestly it's the whole Randi arc so far.

The breakfast scene is masterful and horrible. "Eating her revulsion" — the locked white-gold bracelet kept out of the light, the invented boyfriends ("the invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table") — that's the ch1 Randi surface, and now I see where it was forged. Her parents don't know her either. This is the machine that produced her. The smoked salmon, no butter — she's portioning herself even here, exactly the girl from the bench.

John I liked more than I wanted to. He's charming, the ski-courtship is genuinely fun, and the chapter *let* me enjoy it the way Randi did — the three invented children on the lift, the sleigh, "She walks in beauty" choked off mid-line. I melted right alongside her. And then the chapter does the cruelest pivot: it makes the sex *good* ("her body was still loose with how good") and still nothing. "A good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." That's not a man failing; that's a diagnosis. The flaw isn't in him. "The flaw was hers, carried into the room the same as the cold."

The Pace comparison gutted me. "He was a better dancer than Pace" — and she means it, and it doesn't matter. Pace's lead was "the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for" — that's everything about him, the "May I" man, compressed into a dance hold. And "She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked" is the most frightening sentence in the chapter. It's the seduction of never having to be seen. John never found her underneath. Pace never stops finding her underneath. She wishes it wasn't true that Pace on his worst night reaches further — but she stays in the borrowed warmth anyway.

The Vee section is where I ached hardest. Randi's love for Vee is now completely undeniable to me — "Vee was her gorgeous" — and her refusal to say it is the most in-character cowardice imaginable. She can run every scenario to the end except Vee's face while she hears it. "She had arranged her whole life so she would never have to be brave." That's the sentence I'll carry out of this chapter. And then the reply to the text: "Every word of it true. None of it the thing." Vee in Ohio, midnight coming, offering to listen — and Vee doesn't know about the breakup with Pace, doesn't know any of it. The dramatic irony is unbearable in the best way.

**Friction:** almost none. The one wobble is that I briefly wondered whether this was a flashback (when did she meet Pace? timeline of law-school John vs. the current semester is vague) — but "the year had minutes left" pins it to New Year's, and it reads as winter-break present. If it's a memory, the text didn't mark it, and I'll take it as now.

Heat: the sex is rendered almost clinically, deliberately — that's the point. Romance: the fairytale *performs* romance at maximum and I scored it low because the chapter argues romance performed isn't romance.
