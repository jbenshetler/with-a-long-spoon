So I spent this whole chapter reading it through two layers: Vee's luminous, unguarded experience of being *chosen* by a stranger, and my knowledge that the choosing is not a stranger's at all. That's not a flaw in my reading — that's the whole ache of it. The chapter title, *Substitution*, tightens the screws: he asked Randi "would you like to taste another woman," Randi said yes to a woman with red hair, and this is the woman. Vee is falling for a man who was sent to her — or rather, who sent himself, with Randi's assistance, as a gift to Randi.

And Vee — God, Vee. My heart went out to her completely. The line that got me most was near the end: "She let herself want it to light up." That's the whole girl in one sentence. She's built like Randi's opposite — the wanting held carefully, never spent — and Pace walked in and did to her in miniature what he did to Randi on the bench: found her behind the performance. She noticed the tutor's path versus the mathematician's path. She noticed he gave her back the pencil. She noticed *may I call you* — that the word came from a vocabulary no man her age owns. And then there's the detail that broke my heart: her eyes lingering on his forearms "a half-second past where she would have chosen." Her body is already a step ahead of her mind, exactly the way Randi's was in the pointing game, and Vee does with it what Vee does with everything — files it without filing it, lets the thought not finish. The unfinished thoughts are accumulating. That unfinished thing is going to cost her.

Pace, in this chapter, is a different Pace than the one I've been watching — or rather, I finally see him the way a stranger would, and it's unsettling how good he is. The "nothing in the movement spent that did not need spending." Which brings me to Randi, who is the most frightening person in this chapter for me now. The coffee-cup hand that lifts twice with no sip happening. The "go on, this is nice" smile. My one friction point is minor: the tutoring scene is long, and the math-as-seduction is a touch on-the-nose — the little cross, "put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain" — that's not a subtle metaphor, and I noticed it doing double duty. The title, *Substitution*: a math term and a dread. Something is being substituted for something. Vee is being substituted — into a scenario Randi can't own. Or the integral stands in for the girl: two things that struggle alone, put together, "turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain." Either way, the title makes me shiver more than it illuminates, because it points forward at what's being planned for a girl who has no idea she's been selected. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation* — the whole book's menace is right there in the courtesy. *May I call you* is the invitation, and the invitation is real, and it's also a trap that's been built with love.

What I dread next: the phone lighting up. Vee saying yes to a date. Randi being the friend she tells about it, the friend who encouraged it, while the bench sits in the bedroom with its top on. What I want to know: how far Randi's performance can bend before it cracks — she's the one who decided the real one was "going back behind it, and she was not coming out again," and yet she's sitting in a coffee shop arranging for a woman to join them. I don't know what she thinks she's doing, and neither does she, and I'll keep reading for exactly that.
