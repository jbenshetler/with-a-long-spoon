## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The whole time Pace was crossing that coffee shop floor, I was holding my breath, and not for the reason Vee was. I know what she doesn't. I watched Randi stop dead on the quad and say "Yes" with nothing around it; I heard her ask, low against his chest, *do you think she'll like the bench?* So when this pleasant, ordinary-looking man "picked her out of the whole day" and came over — *pardon me, it sounds like you're having trouble with math* — the scene ran for me on two tracks at once, and the gap between them was almost unbearable. Vee feels chosen. I watched her feel chosen. And she *was* chosen — three days ago, on a lawn, by the girl sitting next to her sipping coffee and smiling the *go on, this is nice* smile. The swoon and the dread arrived in the same heartbeat, and I can't split them. This is the most seductive chapter in the book so far and also the coldest thing under the warmest surface, and I don't think the book wants me to choose between those readings. I don't want to. But I keep hearing that bell tinkle twice — once coming, once going — like a stage cue.

Vee in this chapter just about breaks me, because the page renders her wanting with such tender, unguarded specificity: the half-second too long on his forearms, "they were not a math tutor's forearms," the stairs-too-fast chest, the shame of watching him check his watch, wanting the phone to light up and *letting* herself want it. And that name bit — she borrowed Randi's bit. "Vivienne Thorne. V.T. Yes, like the university." She'd tucked it away from watching Randi do it, and it "landed," and she felt the borrowed poise sit well on her — and Randi sat right there and gave her name as one bare word, *no* adjective thing, no parents thing, and "waited." I read Randi's silence at that moment as something enormous: watching her own move performed back at her by the girl she picked. What it *meant* to her — pleasure, grief, appetite, all three — the page deliberately doesn't say. Her interior is closed this chapter; all I get is the cup that comes to her mouth twice and no sip happening, the hand in her lap, the busy-ness at the edges of Vee's attention. Randi, who got a whole bathroom-mirror chapter of interiority, is now performing even for *me*. That moved her for me — deeper, and less safe.

Pace — I'm sorry, but the tutoring scene made my skin prickle in both directions. The *way* he teaches is genuinely beautiful: asked for the pencil with his eyes first, wrote a margin note and not a rewrite, "gave her back the pencil at the exact line where she could solve it herself," the path chosen "so the solving stayed hers." That's the same man who checked in at the bench and waited through the begging and didn't knock on the locked door. The care is real. But Vee names it herself without knowing she's naming it: "the path of a person who wasn't performing." He *is* performing — this entire meeting is a piece of stagecraft, and the fact that every courtesy inside it is sincere doesn't make it not a hunt conducted with exquisite manners. When Randi lobbed him the n-minus-one question and he answered her "the way you'd answer anybody," I almost laughed out loud. The staging is that good. *May I call you?* — the word "may" hitting Vee like a word from another vocabulary — lands on me as pure irony, because I know the invitation was issued days ago by proxy. The volume is called *A Polite Invitation* and I finally understand the title is not entirely a compliment.

Cassie is my favorite person in this book right now. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" — she clocked him instantly, and she named it without a flicker, and she's *right* in a way she can't possibly know the half of. Cassie's flat reads on people keep landing like small knives. And her "Goodbye, Vee" from last chapter is still sitting oddly with me — the whole word, flat and finished — and here she is again, alive and present, so whatever that was, it stays unexplained and I'm leaving it ajar. What I notice is that Cassie is the only person in the scene with no layer under the layer.

The title worked on me twice. Mid-chapter I thought it was just the calculus — turn the thing into something you already know — and then the substitutions kept stacking up: the technique that solves the problem, Vee substituting Randi's charm for her own, and underneath it the thing I suspect the book is actually about — one woman being substituted into another woman's place in Pace's life, with the first woman holding the door. And that little mark he draws, two things that struggle alone, "put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain — easy to name, easy to know what to do with." If that isn't a thesis wearing a margin note, I don't know what is — but it earned it; it's *in* the math, it's not pasted on. It also retroactively colors the quad chapter: right angles, two things, one plain thing. I'm watching for that shape now.

Friction: very little. Vee's two unfinished thoughts — "He is here for the math help. He is also—" — are doing exactly what Randi's "not a key she could sing in" did, cutting off mid-sentence, and I *feel* the parallel as intentional and it works, though I noticed the machinery noticing itself once or twice. "Finishing a PhD. *More or less.*" — that pause is a small tell and I filed it without knowing what it's telling me. What I want next: the call, obviously — I'm dying for the phone to light up even knowing what it's a door into. What I dread: the bench, and the words *red* being taught to a girl who thinks she's just been flirted with in a coffee shop. That's my guess from here, and I'd rather be wrong.
