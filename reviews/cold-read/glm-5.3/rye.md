## Reader reaction

Something small and relieved in me unclenched reading this. After the truck — the voice and the heat and the siren stealing her sentence — I half expected the book to keep climbing. Instead it did the thing I've come to trust it for: it came back down to the room, to Cassie, to the one witness in this whole book who isn't performing or steering anything. "You're alive." / "I had your blood type." That's Cassie doing what Cassie does — the joke that's also a bucket of cold readiness, the dry-run humor of a girl who was genuinely braced for the worst, and hearing it out loud tells me her wariness of Pace was louder than the page had let on. And then it dissolves. "Cassie was rapt." Four chapters ago Cassie gave the flat patient look and "Goodbye, Vee," and now she swivels her chair around and just *listens*, dry thing set down, and the friendship I was worried was drifting gets paid a whole visit. I felt the room get warmer.

Vee telling it is the chapter. I love the register the author found for her — "I think there's a whole situation under there," "I'm reporting what I observed." That's the girl who got through Intermediate Micro describing a man's body like field data, and it's funnier and truer than swooning would have been. And it's *her voice now* — a week ago she couldn't keep any of Randi's words, only the feeling; tonight she narrates in detail, in order, with her whole face open. That's the change, played quietly: she has a story about herself that she likes telling. "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain." Look at you, Cassie said, and meant it. So did I.

The scene is chaste, but it's not cool. "And then we ate," delivered "the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" — that line did a lot of work on me; the sandwich has become the erotic memory, the shared water bottle and the rye standing in for everything the flannel stood between. And the retelling of the truck scene is charged in a way the original only partly was, because Vee is *owning* it now in front of a witness: "I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." Three beats, and the "Me" landing hardest. The mother's voice is nowhere in this chapter. She told Cassie the whole thing — the bad-shoe, the shirt, the shirtlessness — and the voice never came. I noticed that the way you notice a pain that stopped without your catching it going.

Cassie's last exchange is the emotional peak for me. "Vee," soft, just her name, "like setting something down where it wouldn't break" — and Vee's "I know" — and then, because neither of them can hold that register long: "Borrow real boots next time." / "I'm keeping the shirt." / "Keep the shirt." That's the most tender three lines in the book so far, and it's about a borrowed flannel. Cassie, who sees everything, has just heard that her roommate stripped for a man after three dates, and her whole response is to endorse keeping the shirt. No verdict. I trust her completely.

**Friction:** almost none. If anything, the chapter is a resting beat, and a short one — I'd have liked one flicker of Cassie's earlier clear sight. She heard "he stood outside in the rain so I could change" and didn't blink at the choreography of that man, the same man she read as a great-aunt-looker. Her earlier suspicion evaporating without a word is the one thing I didn't quite buy. But I also buy that Vee glowing at 360 degrees is hard to interrogate.

The title: "Rye." The sandwich's bread — the dark seeded loaf, the detail Vee keeps and reports. On second thought it's also the whole meal of the chapter: the story Vee brings home from the mountain is a *fed* story, rye and horseradish and cherries and a warm shirt, and the title names the humble thing that carries it. It's a homely title after "Leave No Trace," and it fits a homely chapter — the aftermath domestic, chewed over, digested. Volume title still *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation*; the spoon keeps promising me the long-handled dining with the devil that Vee still can't see, and this chapter is the far side of that spoon — the warm hollow in its bowl.

What I want next: Randi. I notice the plan has been silent for two chapters — Vee's fall has been running on its own fuel, and I keep thinking about the pointing game sitting in Randi's hands. The induction dinner and the russet silk dress are sitting on the rock where Vee set them down; I'd bet (a guess from here) that someone picks that dress up. And I'm braced for the moment Brooke's file, Randi's want, and Vee's open face all land in the same room.
