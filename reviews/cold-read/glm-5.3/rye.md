## Reader reaction

Okay. I read this one slow, mostly because it's a short chapter doing a very particular thing: it's Vee telling Cassie the mountain, and the whole chapter is the retelling — we don't get new events, we get Vee *replaying* the truck for an audience of one, and what she chooses to keep and how she says it is the content.

And it works on me, honestly. The retelling-as-chapter could have felt like a rerun, but it doesn't, because the telling changes what happened. "And then we ate" — "she said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" — that's the line that got me. The sandwich, the cherries, the carried-out pits: she's moved the whole erotic weight of the day onto food and care, which is where this book has always put it, and I felt the swoon of that. And then she goes past it. "I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." There's the pattern again — "on purpose," her signature word from the kiss — and each time she says it, she's assembling herself a little more out loud. The girl who put the russet dress back on the shelf is now the girl who knocked on the glass. That arc landed on me even harder in the retelling than it did live, because she has to *claim* it to Cassie, has to decide how to narrate herself, and she narrates herself as the one who chose.

Cassie, again, is my favorite person in the book. "You're alive." "No windowless van." "I had your blood type ready." The dry floor under the gladness, again — and then the moment where she goes still and *stays* still, and finally just says "Vee," soft, "like setting something down where it wouldn't break." That's the warmest Cassie has been allowed to be, and the page earned it. Her "Goodbye, Vee" from chapters ago still sits unresolved with me, a small burr, but here she's nothing but the friend who is glad and slightly stunned that it's going this well. "Borrow real boots next time" / "I'm keeping the shirt" / "Keep the shirt" — that three-beat close is quiet perfection. I actually smiled out loud at it.

The swoon: the shirt. The whole chapter lives inside the flannel, and I love that it's *on her* — his only dry shirt, his gym-bag shirt, soap-and-man smelling, and she kept it, and he didn't ask for it back. That's the most erotic object in the chapter and nothing in it is explicit. The chapter's heat is all memory and residue: "His hands, though." "I think there's a whole situation under there." And the retold striptease, which in the previous chapter burned hot, is now told as almost liturgy — "he looked at me like I'd handed him something. And he didn't touch me." I notice what she *doesn't* tell Cassie: the voice, the *floozy*, the heat that came with the shame, the wanting-to-be-seen. That stays inside her. She tells the act and keeps the want. Which is very her, and very this book.

The one thing I notice, gently, is Pace standing in the rain while she undresses — it's genuinely decent and also, knowing this book, it's the *performance* of decency she'll remember forever. But it worked on me before I got cold about it, and I'm letting it stay worked.
