## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

Well. This chapter knocked the wind out of me a little, and not in the way the previous ones did. For chapters now I've been falling into Vee's warmth — the froyo, the summit, the flannel, the truck — and this one yanks me back behind the curtain and goes: *look what it's made of.* Randi arrives at his house still wearing the afternoon, takes him to bed, and uses the hike — the hike I read two chapters ago as the most tender thing in the book — as fuel. "Her hair smells sweet… under it, faint — chlorine." He *knew* she wanted to hear it: "He had known since the quad what she wanted to hear about." And the thing that really got me, that I had to put the book down over: she asks twice. "Tell me." Twice. The second time *torn out of her* — "I want us" — and I honestly couldn't tell, sitting there, whether that was her want for Vee, or her terror of losing him into Vee, or both braided together. That's the chapter's real sex for me: not the riding, the *asking*.

Which isn't to say the sex didn't work. It did, hot and physical, and the hottest thing in it was her watching him watch her take him in — inch, stop, inch, stop, "her eyes on his." That's Randi's decade of control turned on Pace, and it's gorgeous and a little frightening. But there's a cold seam running through it that I can't unsee: while he's inside her, she's using Vee's body in absentia — soft stomach, sweet hair, chlorine — and I kept thinking of Vee soaked and shivering in that truck cab, feeling *picked* and *chosen* and true, and here the choosing is being metabolized by the two of them as a shared erotic object. The dramatic irony stopped being delicious here and started to hurt. "He did not say how he knew. That she had given him herself… and he kept it." That line is the coldest in the book so far. What Vee did in the truck — the bravest, most on-purpose thing she's ever done — is now currency between their mouths. And Pace *knows* it should be hers ("It's hers," he said of the froyo confession) and spends it anyway tonight, because she asked. His consent-fastidiousness has a hole in it exactly the size of Vee's absence.

And then the ending. God, the ending. The details stacking up with no comment from him: "the chair by the door with nothing on it" (her bag — she didn't bring one, wasn't planning to stay), she skips the lying-on-him afterward, she's out the door before his breath is back, she never once let him answer "How was it?", and — the one that landed hardest — **"she had never left after, not once since the first night."** He sets it beside the empty chair and "left them both there." That's the mirror scene from chapter one doing its work at last: *not ever.* She's pulling away from him, possibly toward Vee, possibly just back behind the face, and he can see the shape of something wrong without being able to name it. And him eating alone at the table — the *table*, the one with the bench hidden under it — making more because he's "still hungry": the book's whole hunger-language in one quiet image. Everyone in this book is starving.

Where's my distrust now? Deeper. Pace's tenderness still reads real, but "he had no wish to make her work for it tonight" — he *curates* what he tells her, holds back the shirt, hands her the hair and the stomach. He's managing both women now, and managing is what he does. Randi I felt almost grief for: her want is genuinely out of her control ("the want, too big for her, come down on him with nowhere else to go"), and the not-staying is armor going back up. And the title, *Underneath* — after "Rye" and "Leave No Trace" and "Dear," it's the most naked title yet: underneath the wool skirt, underneath the face, underneath his answers, the machinery under the courtship. It didn't spoil anything; it named what I was already holding.

What I want next: Vee, badly — I've been away from her one chapter too long, and the two storylines are now magnetized. What I dread and half-want: the night she sees Randi's deadbolt habit up close. That's a guess from here, but the deadbolt went over in this chapter's *first* paragraph and turned again at the end — the book keeps rattling that lock.
