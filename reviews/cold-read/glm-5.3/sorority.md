## Reader reaction

Okay. This chapter made me happy in a way that has a crack running under it, and I want to be honest about both.

The morning-after glow worked on me completely. Vee walking into stats "fit to burst," underslept, still sore in specific places the text keeps tactfully itemizing — "a good soreness no gym had ever given her, down low, and up the inside of both thighs" — I felt lit up with her. And the two-girls-in-one-room pleasure of it is real: Cassie deadpanning "Your bed's made. It's the same bed you made. You didn't come home" while Randi performs the scandal — that triangle in the lecture hall, Vee turned between her two friends while Simpson's paradox runs unattended up front, is my favorite classroom scene in the book. The professor's reversal — same numbers, different answer depending on how you group them — landed as more than a lecture. I don't know if I'm supposed to take it as a key to this whole book, but I can't not: Randi seen one way (warm friend, spending real capital on Vee) and seen another way (a woman reading a dress, reading a foot, reading polish), and the totals disagree depending on who's doing the combining. I'm flagging that as my own inference, but the page put the paradox directly over a chapter where Randi does exactly two contradictory things.

Because here's the swoon first, and I mean it: the shoe store got me. Both stores. The silly one — Randi in six-inch bedazzled platforms asking gravely "Do these go with burgundy?", Vee with the yeti shoe — is the warmest the two of them have ever been together, and it's the first time their friendship has felt reciprocal, Vee giving as good as she gets, Randi's *real* laugh "the one that costs her something to give." I melted at that. And then the boutique: the shoe that "held her... being stood up by one." That phrase undid me. The whole shoe plot is a seduction, and it worked on me even while I watched it working — Randi on one knee, Randi's thumb across the plum toenail (the polish she *interrogated Vee about at brunch*, that detail coming back made my stomach drop), Randi reading the polish and saying only "Mm." That single "Mm" is the most charged syllable in the chapter. Vee's hand shooting out to Randi's shoulder to stop the room tipping — that's heat, and neither of them names it, and the not-naming is what makes it burn.

And then the card. That's where the crack opens. Vee putting back the debit — "the one that only ever spent what was already behind it" — and laying down the credit card "with nothing under it," knowing the paying-it-back part doesn't exist yet, while right beside her Randi rings up a multiple of it without glancing down: that juxtaposition is doing something deliberate to me, and it ached. Vee's rationalization is written so faithfully from inside — "the shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed" — that I believed it right up until I didn't. I've watched this book's shame machinery long enough to know that tonight, or Thursday, or whenever the statement comes, that number is going to arrive in her mother's voice.

TheRandi problem is now genuinely two things at once for me. The stillness in the lecture hall — "for once there was nothing at all on her face" — and the long breath at the shoulder seam, and Vee's own reading of it (jealousy of *him*, of being sat up nights over) is a kindness I don't fully share. Vee keeps reaching for the kinder explanation of Randi — she did it at lunch, she did it at brunch — and the book keeps showing me the tight thumb on the glass rim underneath. When Randi said "He didn't make this for you. He made it *to* you," I got chills; it's the truest sentence anyone has said about Pace, and it came out of the mouth of the woman who is orchestrating him. I still don't know if I'm suspicious or just watching a woman want. But the "same feet, baby" reveal — engineered, delighted, marital — felt like another door being opened for Vee to walk through in the direction Randi chose. Randi picks all the restaurants; Randi picked this day.

Cassie nearly broke me this chapter, in the smallest way: the whole forty seconds of the lecture spent watching what the dress did to her friend, "because the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." After weeks of being left behind at coffee shops, Cassie gets one scene where she is simply *loved* by the text, and it's the easiest, least performed love in the book. "Was it a good night?" "Oh, yes." Something eased in her face. Me too, honestly.

Pace is asleep in memory and offstage here, reframed by one word — "a dress your *lover* made you" — which did to Vee exactly what it was designed to do ("Pace rearranged in her own chest into something adult and lit"). That's Randi steering again, a small vocabulary gift, and I felt the pull of it even as I clocked the steering.

The title — "Sorority" — sits oddly and interestingly. No sorority house, no Brooke, nothing Greek on the page. Read plainly it means sisterhood, and the chapter is Vee choosing her sisters: Cassie's fierce quiet attention, Randi's expensive, ambiguous devotion. But knowing Randi literally *is* the sorority in this book — Standards, the locked doors, the faces kept — the title also names what's happening *to* Vee inside the friendship: being rushed. Court is being paid to her. Bid day is coming, and I don't know in what form. (Guess, clearly marked.) The volume line — *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation* — keeps feeling truer: the long spoon is what you sup with the devil, and everything in this book is polite invitations.

What I want next: the induction, obviously — the room stopping — and Vee walking in late in the dress and the shoes. What I dread: the credit card statement, and whatever the induction does to Randi watching it happen. The heat here is a simmer pitched high for a no-sex chapter — the thumb on the toe, the held foot, the "Mm" — and the romance is the friendship at its warmest, even with the crack under it.
