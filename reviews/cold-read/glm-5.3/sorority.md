## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter felt like the book exhaling. After Peekaboo's intensity — the dress finished, the standing sex, the whole house glowing — I expected a comedown, and instead I got something warm and low-stakes and secretly load-bearing: a day of shopping. And it worked on me completely, mostly because of who the chapter spends its time with. Vee between Cassie and Randi in stats, then Randi and Vee alone. The two relationships the whole book has been braiding, on-page together, and the contrast is the point: Cassie watching Vee's *face* for forty straight seconds while the professor talks, not caring about the dress at all — "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see" — versus Randi reading the *dress*, running her fingers up the seam, naming charmeuse. Two kinds of love, two kinds of attention, and Vee gets both at once. I felt lucky for her.

The stats lecture was my favorite small thing. Simpson's paradox sits right in the middle of the chapter like a wink: same numbers, different framing, the conclusion flips. I'd be lying if I said I didn't read that as the book's own machinery showing itself — Randi and Pace looking at the same Vee and arranging different conclusions — but the professor's flat delivery kept it playful rather than heavy-handed. And the trap of it landed emotionally too: what looks obvious piecewise reverses when you put it together.

Randi keeps pulling me in two directions. The quiet beats are the ones that get me: in the lecture hall "nothing at all on her face," the long breath at the shoulder seam, and Vee's reading of it — jealousy "not of her. Of him, of a man who sat up nights." That's Vee's generous interpretation, and I want to hold it open rather than settle it. The thumbnail-across-the-toenail moment is the chapter's hottest single beat for me, and it's fully clothed in a shoe store: Randi kneeling, the thumb across the plum polish, Vee's hand shooting to her shoulder to steady the room. The plum polish — which Randi *already* knows the meaning of, from brunch, from "that face isn't about a color" — being touched, and nothing said, just "Mm." That's a woman who knows exactly what she's doing, and I don't think the page lets it be innocent.

The shoe store comedy landed, genuinely funny — yetis, the pink rhinestone platforms, the flapping flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal" — and it's the first time I've believed the Randi-Vee friendship as *friendship* and not just management. But the ending is where the real friction lives. Vee putting the credit card down — "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it" — hit me hard, and the page is careful to make her the agent: the dress is "owed" the shoes, the arithmetic "took the wheel." And beside her Randi rings up a multiple of it without glancing down, and Vee does the comparison and spends anyway. That's Simpson's paradox again in money: Randi's spending is nothing, Vee's is a month she'll feel. I ached for her and winced for her at once. The chapter plays it as triumph — "Love you, girl," the laughing sidewalk — and I mostly rode it as triumph, but the debt is sitting there next month and I don't think the book has forgotten it.

Cassie's cameo deepened her rather than thinning her: "Your bed's made… You didn't come home," delivered pleasant and forensic, the eyebrow held in reserve. She sees everything, asks for nothing. My trust in everyone is intact — Pace and Cassie fully, Randi with that held-open question of what she's actually feeling versus performing.

Titles: "Sorority" is odd and interesting — the chapter is almost entirely about *pairing* off (stats class, the shoe trip), and Randi is the sorority's face in it; the word has hovered since chapter 2 and still hasn't been named as anything ominous. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps feeling apt — everything in this book is invitation-shaped — and the cover tagline of Book One keeps its promise of slow courtship. I liked this chapter; it's a breather with teeth.
