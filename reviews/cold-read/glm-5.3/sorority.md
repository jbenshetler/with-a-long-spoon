## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

God, this chapter. It's the happiest chapter in the book and one of the most quietly expensive, and the two things are the same thing. Vee walks out of Pace's house at eight in the morning carrying the whole night in her body — "a good soreness no gym had ever given her, down low, and up the inside of both thighs, and in her nipples" — and the chapter just lets her be lit all day. I beamed through most of it. Her sitting between Cassie and Randi in stats, "turned between the two of them, happy in the middle," is an image I'll keep: the two women in her life, one on each side, and only one of them manufactured the seating.

The class scene worked on me harder than I expected. Cassie never looking at the professor, only at what the dress had done to her friend — "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see" — that undid me a little. And her "Was it a good night?" / "Oh, yes" exchange is Cassie's whole grammar of love in four words. It also recolors that old "Goodbye, Vee" for me: Cassie watches, asks once, and lets things be. Whatever that word was about, it wasn't about not loving Vee.

Randi — I keep being charmed and then being checked, and this chapter did both. The shoe-store comedy is the most purely *fun* the book has ever been; the yeti shoe, the rhinestone platforms ("Do these go with burgundy?"), the real laugh "that costs her something to give" — I laughed out loud, actually laughed. That friendship is real on the page; I refuse to believe it's only the plan anymore, whatever the plan started as. But then two moments landed harder than I think Randi knows. The first: "He was up all night finishing it… I watched him sleep" — and "for a breath Randi was somewhere else, behind her own face," with Vee reading it as envy of her beautiful boys in a hurry. The dramatic irony there *hurt*: Vee is pitying Randi for lacking the exact man Randi has, the man who stayed up all night — and Randi can't say so. That's the first time the secret has cost Randi something I could feel as *pain* rather than management. Whatever the bathroom decision was — "not coming out again" — it's straining.

The second moment: the thumb across Vee's big toe. One stroke, "Mm," and Vee's hand shoots out to Randi's shoulder to stop the room tipping. That's the hottest five lines in the chapter and it's a fully-clothed foot in a shoe boutique. The book has been keeping Vee's unnamed heat for Randi on a low simmer since the tied-and-tickled story; this is the first time it's touched her skin, and the page did not explain it away — Randi looked up and "said nothing at all." I noticed that silence. I don't know what it is yet — appetite, affection, the plan advancing, some mix — and I'm deliberately not deciding, because the chapter didn't.

The credit card is where my unease actually lives, and I want to be honest that it's mixed with the swoon. The chapter *earns* the purchase: "the shoes were not a want she had to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed" is Vee's whole arc in one sentence — she's stopped apologizing for wanting. I felt the triumph. And I felt the trap underneath it, because this is also a broke college junior putting shoes she can't pay back on an emergency card, in a boutique Randi led her to, on an afternoon Randi framed, for an induction that exists because Randi's world admitted her. Vee signs "and let none of it reach her face, because the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch" — and I read that as the old mask machinery, the face taking the looking so the self needn't be touched, now running in service of a purchase. Whether the book wants that echo or not, it's there on the page for me. The counterpoint is Randi's own loafers rung up at several times the price without a glance — a demonstration of the class of ease Vee just borrowed to imitate. I don't think Randi *engineered* the card moment. But the whole day gently taught Vee to stop doing the TJMaxx math, and I noticed who the teacher is.

Simpson's paradox, meanwhile, is the book grinning at me. "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them" — put the two years together and the answer reverses. I'm not going to over-read it, but a chapter about a friendship and a courtship where every individual piece looks one way and the aggregate might look another, *titled by a chapter where the professor explains exactly that* — I felt the wink. It's the latent-variables motif again, wearing cleats.

The title: "Sorority" felt slightly oblique to me until the induction daydream of last chapter and the coming Saturday — this is the chapter where Vee buys the armor for entering Randi's world, the shoes that "stop the room cold." And *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation* — "a long spoon" is the supper-with-the-devil proverb, and this chapter is the most polite invitation yet: laughter, shoes, a marriage joke ("same size, same everything… we can share"). The tagline-level promise of the cover reads darker than the chapters have delivered so far, and I'm content to keep falling for it as designed.

What I want next: the induction, in the dress, in the shoes — and I want to be a fly in Pace's workshop when the plan's next move gets discussed, because Randi's flat "How long are you going to take?" from last chapter is still humming. Guess, marked as a guess: the induction night is where the threesome frame surfaces. And I dread, a little, for Vee's bank balance and her heart, in that order.
