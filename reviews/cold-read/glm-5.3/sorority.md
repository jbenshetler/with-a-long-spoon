## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I'm still warm from "Peekaboo," and this chapter keeps the warmth going but changes its flavor — the swoon here isn't Pace's, it's Randi's, and that's exactly what unsettles me, because Randi is the one running the table.

The opening beat is pure delight: Vee putting the dress *on* to show Randi instead of unzipping the bag, because "the wanting got there before the plan, the wanting to be *seen* in it." That's Vee's whole arc in one sentence — wanting out loud now, on purpose. And Randi's reaction is the best thing in the chapter. "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." It landed on me the way it landed on Vee; the word names the thing she cried over in the sun porch. But it also gave me a small cold thrill, because *of course Randi can read the dress that precisely.* She's been read that way herself — on the bench, being measured by the same man's attention. Her expertise is intimate knowledge of his work. Vee hears a friend's insight; I hear the collaborator's signature.

The shoe-store comedy genuinely worked on me — the yeti shoe, the pink rhinestone platforms ("Do these go with burgundy?"), the laugh "that costs her something to give." I laughed, and I believed the friendship in that aisle completely. That's the book's trick working on me again: the manufactured thing and the real thing occupying the same page without letting me separate them.

Then the boutique, and my chest tightened. Vee's quiet — "a quiet the exact shape of what was in her account" — is the most painful specific in the chapter, and the least sexy, except it flips: the plum-toenail moment is the hottest two seconds in the book between Vee and Randi so far. Randi holding the foot, "Mm," one thumb across the big toe, and Vee's hand shooting out to Randi's shoulder to stop the room from swinging. The page charges it and then walks away from it, and so does Randi — "smiled and said nothing at all." Neither Vee nor the book names it, and I'm not going to either, but my skin registered it and so did Vee's.

The credit card scene is where my unease earned itself. "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it" — that's not a shoe purchase, that's a surrender, and Randi is standing right there, having produced the line "a dress a man made *to* you doesn't want to go with everything." Vee spends money she doesn't have to walk into an induction that Randi's sorority runs, in a dress Randi's lover made, on shoes Randi chose. Everything in this chapter is exactly what the plan would want, and it all *also* reads as friendship, generosity, delight. I can't tell whether I just watched a friend splurge alongside a friend or watched a girl get dressed for a party she doesn't know the theme of. The book keeps refusing to make those separate things, and I keep falling for it as designed.

Friction: the "practically a marriage" beat — same shoe size — felt a touch on-the-nose as intimacy symbolism, though I'd be lying if I said it didn't charm me. And the older couple watching "just two girls being silly over shoes" is a nice, quiet first: the world glancing at them as a pair.

The title, "Sorority," sits oddly with me — nothing sorority-specific happened except the induction looming ahead. It's pointing somewhere, presumably that night. The volume line, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, still reads as a warning about supping with the devil at arm's length, and I trust that reading more every chapter.
