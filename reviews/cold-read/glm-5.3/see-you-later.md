# Cold read (grounded) — See You Later

## Reader reaction

The first thing I have to sit with is the vertigo of the point of view. For two chapters I've been inside Pace's head and inside Randi's, and I *know* what that collision outside the dining hall was. I watched Randi run it like a play — the geometry, the half-beat of hand on arm, Pace at his light pole. And now I'm inside Vee, and the collision is the best thing that ever happened to her. "She'd carried the morning of it like an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter life." That line went through me. This whole chapter is the con from the other side, and the other side is *sweet*, and that's what makes it ache — every warm thing Randi does, I'm reading double. The squeeze of the forearm that felt so spontaneous on the lawn is now a gesture I've seen deployed with intent. I don't know which squeezes are real. That's the chapter's whole engine for me, and it runs cold and hot at once.

And I have to be honest about the heat: this is the most charged chapter of the book for me so far, and nobody touches anybody. Vee's want is enormous and completely unexamined by her — "a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance, except the distance was three feet now." She's falling for Randi in a way she has no vocabulary for, and the book keeps showing me her looking at women (the floozies, "she could wear what they were wearing") and then closing the door on the look as fast as her mother's voice can close it. That suppression *lands* on me as heat. The lavender latte, the knees nearly touching on the high stools — I felt all of it more than I felt the bench, honestly, because Vee's wanting is so unarmored and she doesn't even know it's wanting.

Cassie. I love Cassie. "Four words a day, like nine of them land" — Randi's line — versus Cassie actually saying about nine words, every one of them load-bearing. She doesn't take the bit's open door. She puts her name down flat. And then the "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday" — and she doesn't fix it, she *lets it stand*. The chapter is titled "See You Later" and Cassie is the only person in it who declines to say it. I don't know what she saw on Vee's face when she looked at her before leaving — "something passed over her face, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name" — but as a reader I name it anyway: Cassie clocked something. The hat brim down, going up the aisle. Something about Cassie reads to me like the one person in Vee's life who sees clearly and says almost nothing, and the title tells me the book knows it: everyone else in Vee's life is a "see you later," people who will move on, and the goodbye-that-stays-put is the one that cared. That's a guess from here, but it's where the title points for me.

Randi unsettles me more this chapter than in the bathroom mirror, because here she's *working* and it's beautiful work. The accent that appears when she talks at length — the esses, the arched a's — which Vee files under "somewhere abroad" and I file under *wait, we've spent whole chapters in Randi's head and there was no accent*. Or the "Randi, like the adjective" bit, which has "a place at the end where the other person was supposed to laugh" — I watched her run a script, the same scripts the bench stripped off her, deployed on a girl who can't see them as scripts. And the worst/best part: some of it might not be performance. Her deciding "I'm not asking, I've decided," the wattage — is that Randi the girlfriend-shopper doing her errand, or Randi genuinely delighted to have a project, or both? The book won't tell me, and Vee can't tell, and I can't tell, and that's the point. What I *do* trust: Randi chose Vee because Pace pointed, and Vee was chosen "for reasons she didn't need to understand" — that line is doing quiet, terrible work.

The Chi Latte sign scene is my favorite beat in the chapter — the Greek joke you're only allowed in on once you're walking under it with the right person. Vee's whole self-ranking inventory of the room (the floozies, the mauve one, "she was the one covered to the collarbone") tells me everything about her interior: a girl with more than anyone in the room and a rule against showing it, same grammar as Randi's portioned apple, different dialect. The two of them are mirror hungers and neither knows it.

Friction: very little. Maybe the professor's "latent variables" is a little too neat an underlined-gift; I noticed it and thought *symbol*, which took me out for a second. And the transcript-gone conceit ("the feeling was total and the transcript was blank") is lovely but the chapter does explain it twice. Small thumb on the scale. Otherwise I'd have read it in one breath.

Volume title — *With a Long Spoon, Book One: A Polite Invitation* — the invitation is happening right now, and it's genuinely polite, coffee and lattes and *love you, girl*, which is exactly the long-spoon problem: you can dine with the devil graciously and it's still the devil's table. Next chapter I expect (guessing) the deepening — Vee pulled further in, maybe toward the house, maybe toward Pace being mentioned. What I dread: Vee discovering, later and badly, that the whole thing began with a finger pointed across a lawn. That's the wound being set up, and I'm already wincing for her.
