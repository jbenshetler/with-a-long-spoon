## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

God, this chapter *hurts* in a way I wasn't braced for, and it hurts because of what I know that Vee doesn't. Last chapter I watched Randi collide with her on purpose — a folded-down three seconds of execution "so clean it looked like nothing" — and felt the thrill of it. Now I'm standing inside the mark's chest, and the thrill curdles. Every beat of this chapter that makes Vee float made me wince on a delay: *we should sit together* meant; Randi arriving at the back of the row "with her whole face lit, as though the thing she had been hoping for had in fact been this"; the hand flat between Vee's shoulder blades. I know it's a performance aimed at a target Pace pointed at. Vee experiences it as being chosen "by exactly the person she'd have chosen to be chosen by." That's the cruelest sentence in the book so far, and the page earned every word of it.

And yet — and this is why I love this book — it's not simple. Randi's warmth may be a mission, but the wanting underneath it is real; I watched it crack her open on the quad (*yes*, with nothing around it). So when Vee sits there keeping "the feeling of being a person who got to sit at this table with this girl" and losing the transcript — "the leaning-in was the whole content" — I'm aching twice: for the girl being hunted without knowing it, and a little for the hunter, who can't tell herself the truth about why she's this good at it.

Vee herself: instantly real to me, and instantly the most interior character we've had. The Chi Latte room-reading did something to me — "she was the most of what the room was selling and she was the one with the most fabric on, both at once, and she did not look at either fact straight." That's Randi's apple-calculation from the other side, a whole system of appetite-police installed in a mother's voice, complete with *floozies* arriving "whole and in her mother's voice" — and then the longer beat of looking at the underdressed girls than the word accounted for, and the flash of *she could wear what they were wearing, and better*. There's want behind the censor and the page lets me see it without Vee seeing it. That's the exact register this book works in, and it landed on me hard.

Cassie. My suspicion, if I have one, lives here — and honestly, mostly it's fascination. She declines the coffee flat, doesn't take Randi's offered bit ("Cassie. — Cassie," one word, pencil back up), and then says "*Goodbye*, Vee" — the whole word, "flat and finished, the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Vee smooths it over and I couldn't. The chapter is *titled* for Randi's parting line, and Cassie's goodbye is the shadow version sitting right beside it. Is Cassie reading something? The "something passed over her face" that Vee "would not have been able to name" — I can't name it either, and I'm choosing not to. But I noticed the book made me notice. That's the one place I feel a chill.

Erotically this is a quiet chapter, but it's not cold: Vee's "flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance," the knees nearly touching at the high table, the citrus smell — it's charge at a simmer, all of it one-sided and unaware, which is its own kind of heat. The bench hangs over everything without being named once; Randi asked "Do you think she'll like the bench?" and this whole hour is the long answer in motion.

Friction: almost none. If anything, the Cassie-goodbye beat is a touch deliberate — the narration underlines the word's oddness twice — but I'd rather feel the author's hand there than miss it. The "latent variables" Vee writes in her notebook and underlines "going nowhere" made me smile; I don't know if it's a joke about what's hidden under the surface of this whole courtship, but I suspect it, and I suspect I'm supposed to.

The title means two things now, and they don't point the same way. Randi's "see you later" is the hook, warm and certain. Cassie's refusal of it is the warning. I don't know which one the book means, and I'm not deciding yet.

What I dread next: Vee brought closer in — to Randi, and to Pace, and to that bench. What I want: more of Vee's inside. What I expect — guess only — is that the sweetness keeps working on her exactly as it's designed to, and that the first thing to crack won't be her virtue but her appetite, the one she keeps under the cardigan.
