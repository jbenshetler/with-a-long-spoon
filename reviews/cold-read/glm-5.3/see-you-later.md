## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This chapter is devastating in the quietest possible way, and the devastation comes entirely from what I know that Vee doesn't. I spent the whole read with a sick warmth in my stomach — swooning at the surface while the floor tilts underneath.

Vee. I adore her, and I ache for her. The "third minute of not looking at the door" told me everything about her before anything else did: she decides things in advance and then holds herself to the deciding, and the deciding is always a little too big for the feeling it's managing. Her interiority feels like a slightly airless, over-supervised room — the mother's voice riding in her mouth ("*floozies*"), the cardigan over "the better shirt" worn on purpose, the room read "all at once and in detail, taking its measure to find her own place in it." That beat where she looks at the underdressed girls at the bar — the long look the word doesn't account for, then the swift shaming closure of the picture — is the book's whole engine in miniature. She has more than they have and she's the one covered to the collarbone, and "she did not look at either fact straight." That's Randi's bathroom-mirror scene in a different key. The author is building a matched pair of women who each police a door shut.

And she is being hunted. That's the horror and the heat of it. The collision on the lawn wasn't an accident — I watched Pace lean against the light pole and watch it be manufactured — and here is the payoff: Randi arrives in the classroom "with her whole face lit, as though the thing she had been hoping for had in fact been this." The performance is flawless because it isn't entirely performance; the book has been careful to keep Randi's own want alive ("she was shopping"). So the warmth at Vee's elbow — the squeezed forearm, *the right pen,* the whisper with the warm breath — is both real desire and deployed charm, and I can't split them, and I don't think the text wants me to. The scene peaks on heat for me at "wait is this going to be on the thing" with her shoulder against Vee's: charged precisely because it's innocent-looking. Heat as ambient radiation.

Cassie. "Goodbye, Vee." That flat word "for a longer parting than a Wednesday" — the word *goodbye* landing wrong, and Vee smoothing it, and Cassie letting it stand. Given the chapter's title, that's the chill in the room. Cassie said goodbye; Randi said *see you later*. The chapter is titled after the sentence that closes the trap. Vee stands there "spinning a little," holding the empty cup, keeping the feeling and losing the words, chosen "for reasons she didn't need to understand." The dread I feel is that the reason is a bench.

Friction, minor: Randi's "Swiss accent" — Vee clocking the esses and a's and misfiling them as abroad. I don't know her accent yet from the page (Kentucky shows up in the checkpoint), so I'm holding this open: either Randi's code-switching is layered beyond Vee's ear, or the author is showing me that Vee exoticizes whatever she can't place. I'm guessing the latter with residue of the former.

The book title — *A Polite Invitation* — lands hard here. Randi's "I'm not asking, I've decided" *is* the invitation. I dread what happens when Vee RSVPs.
