## Reader reaction

God, this chapter. After two chapters inside Pace's head and Randi's head — both of them knowing exactly what they're doing, even when Randi doesn't know she knows — the perspective flip to Vee is almost vertiginous. Everything I've been watching from the operator's side, I'm suddenly watching from the mark's side. Except "mark" isn't fair, and that's exactly the ache of it.

The whole time at the lecture and at Chi Latte, I knew things Vee doesn't. I watched Randi stage that collision on the quad *for Pace*, watched Pace point at Vee like an item on a menu — "Her," he said, and Randi said "Yes" with nothing around it. And now I'm sitting inside the girl who got walked into, and she experienced it as *the best hour she'd had in months.* She walked home not feeling her feet. And the thing is — I don't think Randi was performing all of it. That's what's killing me. The warmth in the lecture hall felt real. The wattage felt real. But I know the origin story of this friendship, and Vee doesn't, and every warm beat lands on me twice: once as sweetness and once as dread. When Randi squeezed her forearm, I physically remembered that being a *move*. And then Randi does it and it just… feels like Randi.

The scene that undid me most was the coffee shop interior — but not for the reasons the shop intended. It's Vee reading the room, and that line: "*Floozies,* Vee thought, the word arriving whole and in her mother's voice" — and then immediately, under it, "a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for." That's the whole book in one gesture. Her wanting arrives disguised as judgment, in her mother's borrowed voice, and the page *lets me see the seam* the way it did with Randi's begging in the bathroom. And the cardigan: "she had worn the cardigan over the better shirt on purpose this morning… and the room did not know it was a choice." Two women now, hiding the same kind of thing behind fabric and faces, and neither of them knows about the other yet. That parallel is doing more work on me than any scene in the book so far.

Cassie worries me, and the text earned that worry honestly. "Goodbye, Vee" — the whole word, flat, the word for a longer parting — and then she *let it stand*. "She didn't fix it." The page is doing something with Cassie I can't read yet, and I refuse to guess beyond noting that something passed over her face leaving the lecture hall that Vee "would not have been able to name." First sighting; I'm holding it open.

The loss of the transcript is the most quietly devastating thing in the chapter: "She had been keeping the feeling… and the feeling was total and the transcript was blank." She can't retrieve a single sentence Randi said. Whatever she fell for, it wasn't content. And the chapter title — *See You Later* — which read like a throwaway before, now reads like a spell that was cast *on* someone. To Vee it's "flat fact… the queen having decided there would be a later." To me it's a hook that will keep her warm and compliant while whatever Pace and Randi are building gets built around her. The book's title too — *A Polite Invitation*, with the volume called *With a Long Sion* — no, *A Polite Invitation*: I felt that title click into place this chapter. The pointing game was a polite invitation. Chi Latte is a polite invitation. Nothing is being demanded of Vee. She is being *welcomed*, and that's exactly what makes it work on her.

The professor's lecture was almost too neat a wink — "latent variables. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can." I smiled, but I also rolled my eyes a hair; it's the one moment the author's thumb showed. Everything in this book is a latent variable you infer from behavior: Vee's want, Randi's want, what Pace is actually doing. Cute, a little on the nose, forgivable.

Heat-wise this is a fully clothed chapter and I still felt the charge — but it's Vee's charge, the standing-too-close warmth of it, the shoulder against her shoulder in the lecture, the hug with the hand flat between her shoulder blades. My heat rating is about her wanting, which is enormous and completely unexamined by her.

What I dread and want next is the same thing: Pace meeting Vee. Or worse — Randi beginning to consciously aim at her, now that I know Randi's instinct is to hand her own appetites to a man to own. "She will find a woman for *theirs*." Vee is the woman. And Vee, walking home warm with an empty cup, has no idea she was chosen before she was ever met.
