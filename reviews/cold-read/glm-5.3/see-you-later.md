## Reader reaction

The POV switch knocked the wind out of me a little, in the best way. Three chapters inside Pace's head — his calm omniscient fondness, watching Randi like an experiment he's fond of — and now I'm handed to Vee, and the whole world flips. Randi from the outside is exactly what the bench chapter promised: better lit than the morning, warm on contact, a girl whose friendliness lands like a key in a lock. And I know what Vee doesn't. I know the collision on the lawn was engineered, that Pace stood at a light pole and watched his girlfriend aim a decade of dance training at this exact woman, that the whole blooming friendship Vee is carrying home warm began as "Her," and a nod, and *go meet her*. Watching Vee not-look at the lecture door, deciding three separate times not to look, while I know she has already been *selected* — that's the best kind of dramatic irony, the kind that aches instead of smirks. She feels chosen. She *was* chosen. Both facts are true at once and neither one cancels the other, and the chapter never once winks at me about it.

And here's the thing that got me most: Randi's warmth might not even be a performance here. That's what unsettles me. On the lawn she was executing a play; but her *yes* on the quad had already come out bare — "Yes. I like her" — before she reached for the costume. In the lecture hall she's delighted, genuinely, by Vee's right pen and by Cassie's flatness. So the warmth is fake *and* real, and I can't sort it, and I don't think Randi can either. The bench chapter ended with her deciding the found girl goes back behind the face, not ever — and yet here she is, lighting up a stats lecture for a girl she was sent to collect. Which Randi is this? The page holds it open and so do I.

The "floozies" beat is the chapter's center for me, and I need to be honest about why: Vee's mother's voice arrives in her head — *that's not the kind of attention a nice girl* — and Vee looks longer at the bare midriffs than the word accounts for, and then covers herself with the thought that she'd fill out the crop better than any of them, and then keeps the cardigan buttoned anyway. I *melted* at that and I also flinched. It's Randi's apparatus in a different key: Randi has the sorority scale and the egg-white mother, Vee has the nice-girl censor and the cardigan-on-purpose. Two women, both with an inherited voice policing their bodies, both letting a man's-or-the-world's approval decide how much wanting they're allowed. The book is rhyming them and I don't think that's an accident I'm inventing — the cardigan does what Randi's smile does. And "she was the most of what the room was selling and she was the one with the most fabric on, both at once, and she did not look at either fact straight" — that's a line I'd underline, because it's the whole engine of this book stated as quietly as it can be stated.

Cassie. Four words a day, the flat *Cassie*, the ball cap, the declined coffee — and then "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word. And she doesn't fix it. Vee names the wrongness — not *see you later*, the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday — and smooths it over, and something passed over Cassie's face that Vee "would not have been able to name and did not try." I have a guess and I'm marking it as a guess: Cassie sees something, or intuits something, about where this Randi business goes. But I genuinely can't tell if that's the page telling me or if Cassie is just a person who says goodbye plainly and doesn't do social repair. The chapter title is Randi's parting line — warm, certain, promising a *later* — and the chapter's other parting word is Cassie's flat finished *goodbye*. Those two words are the chapter. One is a door held open, one is a door closed. I don't know yet which door Vee is walking through.

The volume line — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — reads more ominous to me after this chapter than before it, because this chapter is nothing but politeness. "Come to Chi Latte with me… I'm not asking, I've decided." The latte purchased, the stool patted, the sign she's finally let in under. An invitation is how all of this started — a polite one — and Pace's whole method is politeness: he never takes what isn't handed to him, he waits, he lets you decide, and then you find you've decided exactly what he was waiting for. Every warm gesture in this chapter reads double for me, and that's not suspicion the text failed to earn — it's knowledge I can't put down, carried in from "Do you think she'll like the bench?"
