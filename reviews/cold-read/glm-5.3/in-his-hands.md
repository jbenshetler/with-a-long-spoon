## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

This chapter left me wrung out in the best way — it's the most purely happy chapter of the book, and I read every line of that happiness with a little ache under it, because I can't fully forget what I know and Vee doesn't.

The opening is gorgeous: her folding down over him, hair curtaining them both, and then him rolling all the way around to lay his head on her breast. That inversion got me — the man who has spent thirty chapters holding, placing, gentling, asking to be *held*. "He takes such comfort there," she thinks, building her tender theory that his mouth on her isn't about sex — and then her nipple betrays the theory and "rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end." That's the Vee I'm rooting for: she theorizes about him and lets the evidence delight her instead of threaten her. And "He's all mine" — plain, possessive, unfraid. Compare that to the girl hiding in a cardigan. The arc of her wanting keeps landing.

The photo scene is where the chapter peaked for me, and not just as heat — as *trust*. "Would a picture help?" is her initiating again, her whole pattern. But what undid me was "believing him was the brave part." She names the cold sane thought — *any girl's* thought about a picture like that — and then sends it anyway, and the phrasing "gave him myself to keep" is the most naked line in the chapter, more naked than the nudity. And then the reader-nerves: I couldn't help a beat of held breath at that promise — "Never. I'd never share your photograph" — knowing what I know about Randi's reporting, "The whole hour." The photo he'll never share with anyone; everything else, everything else gets shared. I don't know that he'd cross *this* line. But I noticed my own flinch, and I noticed the page didn't earn distrust of his *face* — the boy's grin over the phone is real. My wariness is about the architecture, not the man in the room.

The aftercare both directions — juice last time, water first this time, poured for her first — and then the cooking. I could smell that kitchen. The lemon-zest rain, the garlic under the knife-flat, the wine she mourned into the pan. The food-and-appetite motif has become the book's warmest music: she *tastes* now instead of eating, "and there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was." The girl who couldn't eat a cheeseburger in chapter 3. That's not decoration; that's the whole redemption arc served on a plate.

And then the ending. "I don't go back." Four words, each "set down clean and apart from the last," and Vee takes her hand off the thing without asking — which is its own kind of love, and also its own kind of door closed. The Daphne-shaped hole, the parents shaped like nothing at all, "This is my home." I felt the chill of it arrive and pass through him and leave. He doesn't lie and he doesn't open. And then the pivot to Sheri — "She's alone," said the way "a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed" — killed Vee's flicker of jealousy and honestly killed mine. Two people with nowhere else to be, one table. Except: Vee still doesn't know Sheri exists, and now Sheri's Thanksgiving is *at his house*, the same week Vee flies home. I'm guessing from here — but the chapter ends by placing those two facts next to each other, and I don't think that's nothing.

Small friction: "You do this to me" and the mutual-satisfaction banter ran slightly sweet for me, one shade past where I'd have written it down. And "Again?" then "Again," then the scene break — the second sex is handled almost entirely in ellipsis, which I actually liked; the chapter isn't about the count.

The title, *In His Hands*, is finally spent here: the woman laid out under his arranging hands, the photo placed *in her hand*, the whole self "gave him myself to keep." Being in his hands has been the book's premise — literally, since the lawn. What I notice is how much of the chapter is *her* hands on the arrangement. The volume — *A Polite Invitation*, Book One — still reads as the plan's polite face; an invitation, presumably, still pending.
