## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

The yes came "before the question had finished, fast, a yes with no floor under it" — and that's this whole chapter for me. Everything Vee does here is a yes with no floor under it: the skirt she takes without protest, the dance she can't stop, the kiss she doesn't start and can't end. This chapter landed on me hard, and not gently.

I felt the swoon first. Randi in the foyer — the French braid "so tight and precise it didn't read as braided so much as inlaid," the aqua running up into her eyes so you couldn't tell where cloth stopped and girl started — is the most beautiful the book has let her be, and Vee's looking is the most honest wanting she's done. When she catalogues Randi's mouth ("wet at the center like something just bitten") while "tonight she belonged to Randi," I melted. And then the kiss itself: this is the hottest scene in the book for me, and nobody's hands go anywhere forbidden. The touch of tongues, "salt and lime and under it Randi, particular, the specific *her* of her" — that went through me. Heat like a live wire.

But the shame-shame-heat loop is what makes it ache. The skirt climbing while Randi holds her wrists, the green thong "coming nearer to being everybody's" business — and then "What kind of floozie did that" — her mother's voice arriving exactly on schedule. Vee "found out twice over: once to the room, and once, worse, to herself." That's the book's whole engine in one sentence, and it's rendered so tenderly I didn't feel manipulated by it.

And the ending. The cruelest, gentlest thing: Randi folding the night back up neat — "I almost believed it" — while Vee's body "went on ringing, open, still sure there was more coming." Vee laughing along in a voice that "knew how to do this even now" while her hands shake. I ached for her. Randi's deflection feels less like cruelty than a cover — she came back from that kiss "a moment behind," blinking, shaking her head; whatever it did to her was real too, and she ran from it. But she *engineered* it: she chose the skirt she knew would climb, held Vee's wrists so she couldn't pull the hem down, hand-picked the audience. Whether that was seduction by proxy or just a bit that got away from her, the page deliberately doesn't settle — and the fact that Vee *wanted it more than she had let herself want anything* is the truth the "show" excuse can't quite contain.

Randi deepens here: "I chose" — answering Brooke's ultimatum (ch45) by choosing Vee, out loud, in front of the chapter president. That landed as genuinely moving even as it unsettled me, because Brooke's warmth ("Any friend of Randi's") reads as exactly the surveillance I've feared all along — she recites Vee's whole history and skips the wrecked Friday on purpose. That kindness frightens me more than a threat would.

Cassie earned her keep too — dry, accurate, unbothered, "That hem's not going to stay put," the eyebrow that stays up. I trust Cassie's eyebrow more than almost anyone's words in this book.

The bracelet called *Love* — a white-gold band that "never came off" and needs tools to remove. Vee loves it without knowing what it means, and I don't fully either. Is it a collar-adjacent token? From whom? The book won't say, and I'll leave it ajar.

Friction: almost none. "It's a rumor" made me laugh out loud. The one wobble is Harrison — I believed Vee's forgetting him instantly.
