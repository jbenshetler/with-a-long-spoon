## Reader reaction

The morning-after voice got me immediately — "the funk had been a choice, all night, and she was done making it" — Vee deciding her way out of despair, cold water "like a verdict," the sad-clown face repair. I was rooting for her before she got out the door. And the chapter keeps that momentum all the way through: this is Vee with a plan, striding across a "show-off" of a day eating an apple, and the book gives her to me as someone actively choosing her life for once. After the night she had, I needed this, and I sank into it gratefully.

Randi's scene, though, is where the chapter lives, and I have to be honest about being pulled in two directions at once, because the text earned both. On the one hand: the swoon. Randi reading Vee's whole night off her "like a label" and saying "Oh, honey. Come up" — I melted. Randi holding her hand through the confession, and then turning the guilt inside out — "you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about" — felt like real friendship doing real repair work. The Edna Mode bit had me laughing out loud with them; "the small dark one and the tall red one, laughing in the cold bright room" is exactly the friendship I want for Vee. And Randi's advice about tomorrow night — not going in panicked, going in sure — reads as genuinely wise. I believe her when she says Pace "doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." She knows him, and I know why.

But then the shoe scene, and I have to report what my body did. Randi kneeling under the hem of the skirt, the strap closing at the ankle, the hand bracing high on the back of Vee's thigh, the thumb pressing "once into the soft hollow behind her knee," and Vee's breath doing something she "hoped wasn't out loud." That is the most charged passage in the chapter, more than the kiss, and I don't think I'm inventing that — the page wrote it that deliberately. "Somebody kneeling to tend to her, and Vee left to stand there and be tended to and never watch it happen." That's heat. And Randi's "shoe whore" line, arriving "like an endearment — the worst words always did, between them" — the book is telling me plainly that this is the register between them, that the worst words are their love language, and I felt it land warm and not as a joke at all. And then the goodbye kiss: "she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." That gave me a small chill of delight — the not-bracing is the whole story of Vee and Randi in one gesture.

Now the unease, and I want to be careful to only claim what the page gave me. The title. "broken-in." She walks in wearing "the scuffed leather pair... low and worn soft" — that's the surface meaning, the boots. But then the new heels: "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." Read that sentence against the title and tell me it's only about shoes. The chapter is one long process of Vee being fitted — literally measured onto, strapped into, dressed, scarfed, kitted — by Randi, for Pace, on Randi's schedule ("Not today" — "an instruction, and Vee stopped"). It worked on me as warmth right up until the moment it also worked on me as *training*. I'm not saying Randi doesn't love her — I believe the love is real; "Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet" is a joke and also isn't. But I know things Vee doesn't. Vee still files Randi's Kentucky vowels under "somewhere across an ocean" and builds the Swiss school with the lake and the grandmother, and I watched her do it with an ache — the reader can hear Kentucky; Vee can't afford to. And I know Randi's "something moved in her face" when she looked at Vee's bare feet, and Randi's jealousy from the shoe-store chapters, and that she was, five minutes ago, asleep in the same house whose president greeted Vee with "the house had a folder on her anyway." So when Vee says she'd "walk into traffic" for the woman who said "dinner in Paris," part of me swooned and part of me flinched, and the chapter built both on purpose. The shoes worth "a whole semester of her books" also extend that thread of Vee's debt — the emergency credit card from ch20 now becoming gifts she can't refuse. That's not resolved dread; it's compounding.

Small frictions: "the totem" for the folded shirt stopped me a half-second — I got it, but it winked. And Randi's closet described in palette-language ("bone to camel to oatmeal... ending on one deep oxblood like the last note of something") is beautiful but borders on the author enjoying Randi's taste more than the scene needs. The Evil Queen line — I honestly can't tell if it's throwaway or thesis, and I'm content to leave it ajar.

The volume line — *With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception* — after this chapter I feel the warmth is entirely real and entirely the trap's material. "A warm reception": Brooke's switch-on smile, Randi's "come up," the sorority house Vee finally enters. The chapter delivered exactly that title's promise. Next: I want Vee walking into Pace's house tomorrow night in the black and the oxblood-adjacent cognac heels, and I want it to go right — I'm on her side completely. What I dread, as a guess from here, is the math ever arriving: Vee seeing something that tells her Randi and Pace know each other. The dress, the shoes, the schedule — everything Vee wears to that door was chosen partly by his lover. I noticed. Vee hasn't.
