# Cold read (grounded) — broken-in

## Reader reaction

The morning-after of the dance, and I ate every word of it. Vee lying still tracking Cassie by sound while pretending to sleep — that's the book's whole register compressed into one image: performance, concealment, the body knowing things before the mind admits them. And I notice the shirt has become a "totem" now, folded and set on the pillow like a votive. Her wanting has gone liturgical.

What moved me most in this chapter is how quickly the book let Vee climb out of the pit. Last chapter ended with her sleepless and self-condemning; this one opens with "the funk had been a choice, all night, and she was done making it." Cold water on her face "like a verdict" — and she chooses the verdict. That's her arc in miniature, and it lands because the previous chapter earned the fall honestly. Her walking to the sorority house "with length in her stride," finishing the apple like "a person raised right" — I was rooting for her out loud.

And then Randi's room, which is the richest sustained Randi-Vee scene we've had, and it worked on me in two directions at once, which I suspect is exactly the design. The warmth is *real to read*: Randi waking for this, taking her hand, the reframe — "You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about" — is genuinely good friend-work, the kind of thing that would help a real person. The Edna Mode bit made me laugh out loud, both of them bent over in the cold bright room. The trust between them feels earned on Vee's side absolutely.

But I can't unknow what I know. Every beat of this scene that reads as friendship is also the plan operating at full efficiency: Randi scheduling the apology ("Not today... Tomorrow night"), scripting the outfit, installing the shoes and the scarf on Vee's body like she's dressing a display. And the dressing scene went further than the page acknowledged, I think. "Stand up, top off" — and then Randi circling her slow, reading the line of her, "He doesn't stand a chance," which "went straight through Vee, warm and a little ridiculous, because it was *Randi* who'd said it." The kneeling under the skirt, the thumb in the hollow behind her knee, Vee's breath doing something she hoped wasn't out loud — that is charged, plainly charged, and the chapter knows it: "something moved in her face, there and gone, that Vee didn't have a name for and was too happy to go looking for." Vee's unnamed heat at Randi is no longer a whisper; it has a whole morning of physical contact attached to it. The goodbye kiss at the door — soft, lingering, "the small second press" — and Vee noticing she didn't brace. That's the arc of all their kisses in one line. She's not afraid of being seen anymore; she's being *fitted*, and she likes being fitted. Randi built the dress's shape into her mirror weeks ago, and now she's literally lacing her into shoes and teaching her hands the knot.

The shoes themselves carry the class ache beautifully — "a semester of her books," the math she "decided not to finish." And the moment where the architecture of a good shoe holds her — "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less" — I stopped and reread that line twice. The book keeps saying the truest things about Vee sideways, through objects. That's submission rendered as self-possession, which is this whole book's argument.

My suspicion of Randi held steady rather than grew — which is itself unsettling. The "Not today" instruction reads as care and as management in the same breath, and I've stopped being able to pull those apart, which I think is the point. Brooke at the door is the chill note: "the house had a folder on her anyway." Brooke, who knows about the truck behind Hahn and the too-long pause, greeting Vee warmly, logging her. I don't trust Brooke at all — the warmth that "gave back nothing but more warmth."

The title, "broken-in" — it nags at me. It's Vee's voice about the boots ("worn soft"), about the shirt that's hers now, about shoes that "told her where to be." Being broken in as comfort rather than damage. But there's a colder reading sitting right there — Vee is being broken in, gently, expertly, by two people — and the chapter gave me both without deciding. That's good titling; it doesn't give anything away so much as name the double thing after the fact.

Volume title *A Polite Invitation* — still the pointing game's whole grammar: everything asked, "may I," and now Randi's "Not today," an invitation deferred. The cover's promise of courtesy as the delivery system for appetite is being kept to the letter.

What I dread and want: tomorrow night at Pace's door, Vee armored in the black dress and the borrowed silk. I *want* the repair — I want her to walk in sure and be received. And I dread it because every step of her certainty has been stage-managed, down to the scarf knot, by the woman whose closet she'll be standing in borrowed shoes from. Guess from here only: the repair will work, and it will feel wonderful, and that's exactly the trap tightening.
