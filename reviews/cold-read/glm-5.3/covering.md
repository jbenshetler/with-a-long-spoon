## Felt Read

Where do I even start. This chapter got me hot and it got me *aching*, and then it put a knife in at the end that I'm still holding.

The dinner is the warmest scene in the book so far for me — the loft booth, the jukebox "climbing its pipes for nobody," Vee's coat turned lining-out on the hook. Randi and Vee at a table together is the pair I most want time with, and here they finally get an evening that's *legal and paid for.* The underwear story is the best thing Randi's whole voice has ever done: "Laundered. Folded. Robin's-egg blue." — "She'd had them for days. She waited for him." That dropped my stomach. Randi has never given Vee anything this naked, and she gave it unprompted, in trade for the pancake story, and it *cost* her — the glass rim going under the thumbnail, the voice coming "down out of the story's key without her seeming to decide it." The Kentucky of it. Her mother knew her color. Randi's whole armored life in one anecdote.

And then Vee's retelling of the coat night — this chapter understands exactly what I read erotica *for*. "Which shoes." "Your shoes." I actually made a sound. The relived heat builds and builds — the foot on the chair edge, "Could he see you were ready?" — and then Vee goes to the bathroom and finishes herself with **Randi's face behind her eyes**, and the text does not let either of us pretend otherwise: the face asking *how much could he see* becomes *he could see you were ready*, and "Vee went over hard." Followed immediately by the rationalization — "Because that had been about Pace, obviously. All of it. About Pace." I don't believe her, and I don't think I'm meant to. The page has earned my suspicion here; the "obviously" is doing all the work that "it's a French thing" used to do.

Then the trap closes. "Things were really heated. It's a good thing you got relief." I felt that line in my chest — the blue lights arriving again, "whole, certain, out of nowhere." Randi *knew*. She watched Vee go down those stairs flushed and came back glossy-eyed and read her through a floor. There's something terrifying and thrilling in how pleasant she is about it: "pleasant as a hostess." And Randi doesn't flinch away from it — she *says* it, at a table, and holds her eyes. That's not disgust; that's possession. The foot out of its shoe against Vee's ankle that "stayed there." Randi's want is barely subtext now.

The ending is the quiet devastation: Vee relieved that the restaurant closed before she had to tell the kiss-that-went-sideways story. "She'd tell it Thursday." The girl in the mirror who "did not look the least bit sorry," and the promise she doesn't believe. That little stone of relief — the first thing Vee has ever *withheld* from Randi — is the most important thing that's happened to their friendship, and the book lets me feel it land without naming it.

Heat-wise this chapter is a 3 for me. Nobody touches anybody, and I'm flushed. That's the chapter doing its job.
