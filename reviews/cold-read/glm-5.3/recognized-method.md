The heels at the door. I felt that missed-stair jolt right with her — she's spent the whole book looking up at him, her body has *learned* him that way, and she arrives at an apology wearing borrowed height. And then his answer: "Beauty like yours made for the gods and loaned out — I'm not going to insult the loan by complaining I have to look up." That line undid me a little, the way his extravagant plain-voiced lines always do, and it did its work on her too. But what wrecked me was "Thank you for telling me what she said." Not *it's fine*, not absolution — gratitude for the exact thing that was hardest to hand over. He keeps doing this: refusing the closed door, taking the full weight out of her hands "like the pie." I keep trusting him more even while I remember the light pole, and this chapter didn't resolve that tension, it deepened it — because his care here is flawless and I still know the care is also the method.

And then the kitchen. The tickle fight was pure delight — her lobbying for the soak, "you're scrubbing things that want to be left alone," the whole domestic comedy of it — so the single smack landed on me the way it landed on her: no warning, and then that bright hot line going straight up through her. My stomach dropped and then caught fire. Here's what I can't stop noticing, and the page absolutely earned it: **Randi's tied-and-tickled story from chapter 17** — the helpless laughter, the heat Vee "had no shelf for" — just replayed itself in Pace's kitchen, tickling tipping over into the sting, and Vee's body answered the same way Randi's story made it answer. The hiding of her face in his neck so he can't look at her, the talking as cover, "a quiet room was a room with a question standing in it" — that's the mask-structure of this whole book working at full pressure, and it's the hottest thing in the chapter precisely because she won't look at it and I can't stop.

"Please." Attached to nothing. The title sits oblique and a little cold next to all that warmth, and I think it's meant to. The book's line — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — keeps promising that a long spoon is what you sup with the devil, and I keep hoping this devil is only hosting dinner.
