## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

This is the smallest, quietest chapter in a long while, and it worked on me like a balm after the heat of the last two. It's just two girls in an overcooked dorm room, one of them back from a weekend of mushrooms on toast and multiple orgasms, and the other one mostly reading a book with one eye and the room with the other. I didn't know I needed the comedown until the book gave it to me.

What I love most is the laundering of the weekend. Vee can't tell Cassie the real story — the coat, the doorstep, the heels — so she tells the truest version she can, all food and laundry and burnt toast, and the chapter lets us watch her edit in real time. "And your clothes?" — "Eep." The doll-sized cranberry sweater is doing so much work: it lets Cassie's suspicious little ledger ("Pace. Shrank your sweater.") be a joke instead of an interrogation, and it lets Vee keep the record "exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." That line made me laugh out loud. Cassie asking about the clothes isn't prurience; it's Cassie doing arithmetic she chooses not to say out loud. Their friendship continues to be the steadiest thing in the book.

And then the chapter slips the knife in almost invisibly: "My mother says thank you for the card. She wants to know when she gets to see you again." Vee's hand going to her collar, the "warm and specific, like a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck." Cassie's mother — the mother who lost the job, whose layoff was a chapter of its own — wants to see Vee, and Vee's answer is Spring break, sometime, hi. The book doesn't punish her for the smallness of it; Cassie just "recaps the highlighter, done with the subject the exact moment before it would have needed anything from either of them." That's Cassie's whole character in one gesture.

The flannel shirt's return closes the loop beautifully — him, "but going faint," and her plan to have him wear it back to full strength. And the last image: her hand flat against the cinderblock, and "the wall was only a wall." That's the whole arc of the last three chapters in miniature — the flannel chapter of December said the shirt's heat was her own; now the wall is just a wall because the real warmth is out west of town and she trusts it's waiting. Two nights of acreage versus a bunk "exactly one girl wide," and she falls asleep content. That's a different Vee from the one who lay sleepless on New Year's holding both Randi and Pace and neither.

I notice what's absent: Randi. Vee mentions "we're going out Tuesday after Anthro" was last chapter's plan; here Tuesday isn't mentioned, and the weekend is told entirely through Pace. After the foyer kiss and Randi's shut door, I'm braced for that lunch. And I keep a small unease alive, honestly earned by the text: Vee still doesn't know the shape she's standing in. Her happiness is real and I root for it — and the book keeps letting me hold both that and the knowledge only I have.

The title: I read it first as Cassie's duffel — literal, half-unpacked socks and tees. On second thought it's Vee telling the weekend, unpacking it into shareable pieces; and, obliquely, the thing she can't unpack yet — what she is to him now that they're "we're—" (she never finishes the word, and neither does the book). The chapter gives away nothing ahead; it just settles. A Place at the Table still points toward the dinner party, and this chapter feeds that: the party is now Pace's project too — "Let me handle it."
