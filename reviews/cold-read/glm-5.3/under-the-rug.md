## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This one hurt in the good way — the way this book keeps hurting me, which is Vee lying awake in the dark of the best thing that has ever happened to her, unable to take any of it in because it has no name. The chapter opens right where "Cropped" left the wound: the word "right there, simple, wanting out," and her fear of "the chance it came back empty." And then her mouth betrays her sideways — "Is this what we are now?" — too loud, wrong-sized, a daytime voice in a murmur room. I felt that in my teeth. Anyone who has ever had a carefully built sentence collapse into the wrong question at the wrong volume knows that exact shame.

My feelings moved this chapter, on both sides of the bed. Pace: the Chili's scene gave me something I'd never seen on him — "a flicker of plain irritation, there and gone, and he dropped his eyes... as if he didn't want her to have it." Months of reading this man and that's the first crack in the calm, and it humanized him enormously. And then his explanation, when it finally comes, landed on me so hard I had to put the page down: "In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you. Somewhere quieter, I can. And I do. I'm listening now." That re-colored the whole dinner retroactively — the eyes slipping to the screen, the fork squaring to the table edge (that's his razor squared to the counter edge, his drawer squared, the same ordering hands, now working under overload). He wasn't cold; he was drowning in noise and trying to hear her. I trust him more than I did at the chapter's start.

And Vee — she went somewhere harder and I followed because the page never lied to me about what she was doing. "Some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold that night, knew who'd carried a pie up whose porch steps after — and she said it anyway, because she needed the line of it to hold." That's the self-deception I read this book for, named honestly on the page instead of smuggled. Her needing him "made her mean," and the meanness is hers, and she half-knows it, and the "forgiven" she wears like a coat — "warm and large and easy to wear" — is the most quietly devastating thing in the chapter.

The dramatic irony burns here in a way the chapter itself never touches, and I have to be honest that my unease comes from what I know, not from anything this page earned. "If we're just sex, I'd like to know." Oh, Vee. It isn't just sex — and it also isn't only yours. I know about Randi's Fridays, about "I'm doing the work," about the pointing game that started all of this. When Vee says he's given her "every last thing but the one," I'm holding the thing he's given someone else: the sight of her, chosen on a lawn. The title presses on me here: everything not-knowable gets swept under, and the biggest thing under this particular rug is the one she can't see. But I also hold what the page holds open — his stillness over knots and proofs, the way "I love how good we are together" might be evasion or might be a man who genuinely doesn't have the word either. I won't fix it more firmly than the page does.

The heat worked, and where it peaked was exactly where she begged it to: "the control she'd been begging him to lose, gone all at once." The whole sex scene is charged by the wanting-for-the-word — her orgasm "took it back down, drowned it clean... She came with his name in her mouth and not the other thing." That's the hottest thing in the chapter, and it's hot precisely because it's about what isn't said. And then his — the first time he's gone helpless and urgent, and it should have answered her question, and instead it opens the wound wider: "what she had of him she could not have held up to anyone, could not have proved. It lived only here." Ouch.

Friction: very little. The one gap I noticed and Vee didn't press — his explanation is about *hearing* her, and her question was about being *seen* in public. "We'll ever be seen in public again" never quite gets its answer; he answers the noise, not the visibility. I don't know if that's the author's thumb or Pace's honest limit, and I leave it ajar. And the dead nachos — "to food what a billboard is to a painting" — is the food motif doing its cruelest work: he's ruined "fine" for her forever, and she knows it, and she asked for the nachos anyway. That ache was earned.

The title: before reading, "Under the Rug" meant nothing to me. Now it means the place — "She put it where she put things she did not know what to do with. The place was not empty." And it means this chapter's whole motion: she sweeps the not-knowing back under, twice — once on the porch in "Cropped," once tonight after having everything and nothing. The volume title, *A Warm Reception*, reads more ironic by the chapter: the warmth is real, the house is warm, the reception at Chili's is fluorescent and dead. The series line — *With a Long Spoon* — keeps promising me that this supper comes with a long spoon for a reason, and I keep waiting to need it.

What I dread and want next: the pile under the rug is getting heavy. The Anthro class with Randi is coming in spring, Randi is circling, the MIRA record is sitting closed on a laptop. My guess, marked as a guess: Vee's hunger for the name will keep growing until it finds somewhere else to feed — and Randi's mouth is right there. I dread it and I want it.
