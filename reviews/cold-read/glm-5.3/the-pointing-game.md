## Reader reaction

God, this chapter. After the bench chapter left me chilled — that bathroom mirror, the deadbolt, "the face had been lying for years" — I didn't know what I wanted next except that I feared for her. And instead the book gave me... joy? Real, sexual, sunlight joy, and I feel almost dizzy from the reversal.

The bed scene worked on me completely, and not for the explicitness alone. "Would you like to taste another woman?" landing in her body before it reached her — her coming so fast "his body could not account for it" — that's the hottest thing in the chapter precisely because it's an involuntary confession. The heat here is the heat of the *unconscious yes*. And then the aftermath, which is where this book keeps earning me: she rebuilds herself into "Only in a threesome. With you," and Pace narrates the maneuver — "The same act. A different owner" — and I believed his read completely, because the bathroom chapter taught me exactly what that machinery looks like from inside her. What moved me more is that the narration is *fond*. He isn't contemptuous of her camouflage. "He let the camouflage stand. It was the wrapper the wanting needed to come out in." I swooned at that, honestly — a partner who works with the defenses instead of tearing them.

The pointing game itself is a swoon and an unease at once. As pure scene it delighted me: "I point. You pick," the little verdicts ("Too expected," "You'd get both or neither"), the library-steps girl getting a "Maybe" and a kiss that carries her own taste back to her. The book's warmth is doing its work — she's *happy*, glowing, doing happy footwork she didn't decide to do. And the writing tips its hand about Vee before she's named: everything the sorority grammar grades is absent from her, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — the exact thing Randi cannot do. That's the book's whole axis in one image. The chase itself is dazzling and a little sinister: "A decade of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually taught under the mixers and the letters, all of it folded down into three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing." I admired it the way Pace did, and then caught myself — she ran a covert op on a laughing stranger, and I enjoyed watching it. The book made me complicit in his delight.

Trust vs. suspicion: I still have the residue of the bathroom. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is the chapter's knife — she's already translating Vee into the machinery of *their* dynamic, already offering the gift. Pace heard it and answered with "Let's go feed you," which I loved for its tenderness and mistrusted for its deflection. My unease is not that anyone is unsafe — the consent on this page is meticulous, even in the seduction structure: he waits until after the wanting shows, and only then asks "is this one mine to take?" — but that the *pattern* is: Pace finds the hunger, feeds it, and Randi keeps deciding, at some sub-verbal level, what to do with being seen. The last chapter ended with a lock turning over. This one ends with her lit up and kissing him in public. I don't know yet whether this is recovery or a new and deeper form of being carried. I hold it open.

Friction: the middle of the walk occasionally over-narrates the typology of campus women — the parade of "no"s is a touch schematic, Pace enjoying his own perceptiveness. But the Vee introduction is worth every staged "no" before it. Title-wise, "The Pointing Game" is exactly what it says, and the chapter title gave little away except in retrospect — the game is real and it's hers to win. The volume title, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — the packet shows me only the chapter title and the recent chapters; no jacket or tagline was supplied, so I can't read the cover promises beyond the series title itself, which still puzzles me slightly (a long spoon — for supping with the devil, I'd guess from here) and now feels like it's starting to point at Pace's competence as the thing to be careful of.

What I want next: Vee, in the flesh, talking. I want to know if the laughing girl is also a watching one. And I dread the first time Vee and the bench occupy the same room. Guess, marked as a guess: the "maybe" girl on the library steps isn't done — Pace filed her.
