## Reader reaction

Where do I even put myself after this chapter. The last two chapters were so *interior* — the bench, the bathroom mirror, the walk of shame past Brooke — that I'd braced for more of Randi's sealed-up aftermath. Instead the book opened a window and let the whole thing happen in daylight, and I'm a little dizzy from it.

**The felt read.** The opening caught me off guard: the table with its top back on, walked past "without looking at it" since Saturday, and then Randi arriving and saying *let's do something different.* After everything she decided in that bathroom — *the one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again* — I expected her to manage him from behind the face for a while. And she tried. The afternoon did exactly what the last chapter set up: her body answering his question about tasting another woman before her mind could dress it, that hard instant orgasm, the flinch, and then the *beautiful* maneuver — "Only in a threesome. With you." I actually gasped at that line, not because it was surprising but because it was so exactly her. She takes a want that would be unsurvivable and re-titles it as a gift with her boyfriend's name on the deed. "The same act. A different owner." That's the chapter, right there, and Pace naming it so coolly made me shiver a little.

And then the quad. This is where the chapter got under my skin in a way I wasn't guarded against. I *liked* the pointing game — the playfulness of it, "I point. You pick," her learning in real time that the choosing was hers. I loved watching her verdicts sharpen from propriety-recoil to *shopping*. That shift came through her hand, through her walk, and I believed every beat of it. The beanie girl on the library steps getting a "Maybe" — and Pace stopping to kiss her, letting her taste herself on his mouth — was the hottest moment in the chapter for me, hotter than the opening sex, because it was the game and the bed braided together in public light.

But here's where I have to be honest with myself: this chapter is also where I felt the first real unease, and the page earned it. "Go get her." The collision Randi manufactures with Vee is gorgeous to watch and it is a *con*. Vee apologizes — it's the redhead's hand that flies to her own mouth — and Vee doesn't know she's being shopped. Randi doesn't know, exactly, what she's doing either; that's the tenderness of it and the menace of it, both at once. The last chapter's hum in her ribs was about *figure out what to do about this* — and this is what she did. Not by facing anything, but by finding a woman and lying about why. "For us?" "For us." That reframing — everything through him — is her safety mechanism running at full speed, and Pace, who *saw* all of it, let it stand because it moves them where he wants to go too. I don't distrust him the way I might distrust a colder character — the bed scene was full of real care, "I'm not the one being asked" is genuinely good rule-setting — but I notice how much of this is him steering while appearing to follow. He chose the wall for its angle on the doors. He had his eyes on Randi, not the girl, when he pointed. Everything in this book is somebody watching somebody and deciding what to let them believe about themselves.

Vee. Oh, Vee. She walked out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bare to the sky and I fell for her exactly as hard as Randi did, and I'm worried about her precisely because of it. She's the only person in the whole cast who doesn't *run* her own presentation — "nothing pinned and nothing trying to be." Against the sorority grammar she reads as the thing that can't be graded. And she's about to be pointed at, picked, invited over to a house with a bench under a tabletop. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" said in a low daylight voice was the line that closed my stomach. Not because I think Pace is a predator in a crude sense — but because I remember Daphne, and the door, and how carefully he's learned to respect closed doors *that he isn't holding open himself*. This whole chapter is Pace holding doors open and hoping she walks through them, and the question I'm left sitting with is whether Vee will ever be told what she's walking into.

Also: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is a line I'll be turning over for a while. It's Randi *daydreaming*, out loud, wanting — the girl who went back behind the face saying the most naked thing she's said in daylight. That cracked me open even as it worried me.

**Erotic charge:** worked, deeply. The opening scene peaked with the question and her involuntary answer; the walk was a slow burn that never touched skin; the kiss with her own taste on his mouth was the peak. Nothing went slack. The one moment that went slightly clinical for me was the schematic quality of Pace's interior cataloguing ("closer, but no; not the expected, not the matched pairs") — a touch too much sommelier.

**Title:** "The Pointing Game" — plain, apt, and it holds the chapter's doubleness: a game they play together and a game played *on* her, with her as the pointer's instrument. It didn't spoil anything; it told me almost nothing until the rec girls, and then it meant everything. Volume title *A Polite Invitation* is now retroactively perfect — "go get her," "for us?" — the whole book is courteous hands steering people toward doors. I have no cover tagline in my packet.

**Friction:** minor. "I point. You pick" landed slightly writerly-smart. And Pace's gratitude monologues ("He felt grateful for the glimpse") occasionally tip into explaining his own virtue. Mostly I stayed swept up.

**Next:** I dread and want the Vee chapters — I suspect (guess only) she'll say yes to something without knowing the full shape of it, and I suspect the book will make me complicit in enjoying that. I also wonder what Brooke knows; the Saturday grilling can't be done with us.
