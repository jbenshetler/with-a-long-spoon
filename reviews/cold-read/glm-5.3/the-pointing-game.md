# Reader reaction — "The Pointing Game"

**Felt read.**

Oh, this chapter got me. It got me because I watched the whole thing happen from the inside — the question in bed, the flinch, the recovery, the walk, the pointing, and then *her*, the redhead, and Randi's stopped feet. But let me be honest about the shape of my response: this chapter is doing two things to me at once and both are strong. There's the heat, which is real and worked — and there's a slow coldness underneath it that I don't think the book is hiding, I think the book *knows*, and that's what's making my spine tingle in a way that isn't only arousal.

The bed scene first, because it deserves it. "He slid into her then, into all that grip and flutter, *before* he kissed her" — that took me, the deliberate timing of it, taking her while she's still undefended. And then the question: "*Would you like to taste another woman?*" and her body answering *before she could make the wanting mean anything safer*. That's the hottest beat in the chapter and the most exposing one at the same time. I melted at how precisely the page renders that — wanting something so new it outruns the machinery you built to manage wanting. And Pace reading it, cataloguing it ("He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer") — that's the tenderness and the eeriness of him in one breath. He is so *careful* and so *clinical* at once, and I keep swinging between finding that care deeply hot and finding it a little like watching someone handle a specimen they're fond of.

Then her recovery: "Only in a threesome. With you." The page spells it out — she hands the appetite to him and makes herself its instrument. "A different owner." That landed with a small ache. She can't own the wanting, so she gift-wraps it. And he sees through it and lets it stand. That's where the swoon and the unease braid together for me: his *letting her keep her cover* reads as kindness, and also as a man who knows exactly what he's holding.

The walk itself — I loved it. The burning maple, the wind, the pointed-at girls each getting a verdict ("Too expected," "You'd get both or neither"). I laughed at the sprinter/pair analysis. Randi coming *alive* as a shopper is genuinely delightful — the moment he notices she stopped being a good sport "a quarter mile back" and her body is asking *that one? now?* — I felt the charge of it, the game becoming real appetite in daylight. That's the swoon. Her happy footwork at the end, the squeaked *yes*, the breathy register she never uses outside his bed: I was fully won over. This girl, who lay on her bed in yesterday's clothes not-crying one chapter ago, is *glowing*.

And Vee. The arrival of Vee is written like weather — the laugh with her whole body, the red hair sparking copper, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That contrast with Randi's whole managed existence is doing so much work. She's the thing Randi can't be and, I suspect, the thing Randi wants in more than one sense. The stopped feet, the *yes* that comes out alone: I believed it completely.

But — and this is where I hold both — the last lines chill me. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" said in the register she only uses in his bed. Randi has taken the bench, the thing that broke her open and terrified her and made her lock a bathroom door, and made it a *party trick she's excited about*. That's the same move as the threesome reframing: she survives a want by handing it to him. Pace's response — "Let's go feed you" — dodges the question and answers it at once. And Pace engineering the whole afternoon ("the angle it gave on the doors"), standing at his light pole watching her aim a decade of training at an unsuspecting girl who just *bumped into her by chance* — that's the coldest thing in the chapter. Vee doesn't know she's being hunted. The collision was beautiful and it was a con. I'm not scandalized — I'm *riveted* — but I don't fully trust Pace's goodness anymore, and I noticed that I'm not sure the book wants me to. He's not coercing Randi. He's doing something subtler: he keeps finding what she won't admit to and building a room around it before she can refuse. That's the story, and I'm in.
