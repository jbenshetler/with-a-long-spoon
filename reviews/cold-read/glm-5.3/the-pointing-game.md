## Reader reaction

The question in bed took me the way it took her — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and I read it with the bench chapter still under my skin, so I knew exactly what it was doing. Her body answering before she could dress the answer: that's the whole grammar this book has built with her, the begging that came from below the face, and now the wanting that arrives as an orgasm before it arrives as a thought. I believed it completely. What I believed even harder was the recovery: "Only in a threesome. With you." I actually put the book down for a second, because that is *precisely* the woman who stood in the bathroom mirror deciding "not ever" — she can't own the appetite, so she re-deeds it to him, makes herself "a girlfriend offering her man a gift." The page named the maneuver, and it named it in his voice, and he saw through it and *let her have it*. That's the tenderness I trust in this book: he takes her yes in whatever wrapper she can survive it in.

Pace — I'm still with him, still a little in love with him if I'm honest, but the line "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did" made me pause. Not because it's wrong here — it isn't, the answer was true — but because it's a policy, and policies about what a woman's body means are exactly how you get it wrong eventually. He is engineering her self-discovery, down to the choosing of the retaining wall "for the angle it gave on the doors." I notice it. I don't distrust him for it yet — everything he engineered this chapter was aimed at *her* getting what she wants, and he explicitly waited for the heat to leave the room so she could "weigh it and choose." But he's playing with something he read in a mirror that she never showed him, and I'm holding that.

The game itself — the pointing — swept me up completely. That's the swoon of this chapter for me, more than the bed. The procession of candidates and her verdicts ("Too expected," "God, no" with real feeling in it, the long-held *maybe* on the library girl) was genuinely fun, and then Vee came out of the dining hall and the prose changed register — "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — and I understood the choice before Randi said yes. Of course it's her: the one woman on campus whose body Randi's whole apparatus can't grade, laughing with her throat bare. And Randi stopping mid-stride, not looking away for the first time all afternoon — "the follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step" — that landed in *me* somewhere low.

The staged collision was the hottest thing in the chapter for me — a decade of dance and three years of sorority training "folded down into three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing" — and it's also where my unease lives. Vee doesn't know she's being hunted. Vee apologized. Vee laughed with her whole body and had her shoulder bumped by a girl who had chosen her twenty minutes earlier like a dish she hadn't ordered. There's nothing cruel on the page — Randi was warm, the hand stayed only a half-beat long — but I felt the machinery close over a stranger, and the stranger is the most alive person we've met outside that house. And then the last line: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I read that twice. It's lit up and it's ominous in the same breath — Randi glowing, and what she's glowing about is feeding the bench a person. The bench that made her cry in a bathroom and decide, quietly, *not ever* — except she came back, she said "Let's do something different," she initiated. Whatever got locked in that bathroom, the lock didn't hold, or she's chosen a different door out. I don't know which, and I don't think the book has told me.

Friction: very little. Pace's total legibility of Randi ("he'd have felt seduced and forgotten he'd asked anything at all") occasionally reads as the author and the character sharing one set of eyes — no one is that transparent — but it's been his character from the apple slices onward, so I buy it as *his* self-image even where I doubt it as fact. The orgasm-on-the-question was nearly too neat, and it survived only because the bench chapter did the work of establishing that her body runs ahead of her face. The title gave away nothing; it names the game plainly, and only recolors on the walk when "I point. You pick" turns pointing into an institution between them.

The volume board — *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation* — the long spoon is the one you need to sup with the devil, and after this chapter I feel the idiom working: this is a story about keeping a careful distance inside something hot, and the "polite invitation" has now literally been issued, to a third person, on a campus quad, by accident. I want Vee to say yes and I'm afraid of what the bench will do to someone who laughs like that. I dread and want the next chapter in equal measure, which is exactly where this book has had me since the bathroom mirror.
