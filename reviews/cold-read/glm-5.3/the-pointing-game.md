## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

The bench chapter left me hollowed out — that bathroom mirror, the face deciding the found girl was never coming out again. So I came into this one braced for aftermath, and instead got the opposite of aftermath: Pace asking "Would you like to taste another woman?" mid-stroke and Randi's whole body answering before her life could get a word in. And God, it worked on me. The orgasm arriving ahead of thought, "she came, hard... far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — that's the hottest thing the book has done, because it's the bench scene's payoff in reverse: last time her body betrayed her into feeling, and this time it betrayed her into wanting, publicly, undeniable, *while he's inside her*. I felt the swoon of that, genuinely, no shadow over it.

But the chapter I actually can't stop thinking about is her recovery. "Only in a threesome. With you." The page spelled it out for me — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift... The same act. A different owner" — and Pace saw through it and let it stand. There's something almost unbearable about how well this man understands her and how little she knows she's understood. He narrates her armor better than she does. That's the ache of the book now for me: watching a girl whose entire survival is performance be loved by a man who watches the performance *and the girl under it, simultaneously, all the time*. I want her to have this. I'm also a little afraid for her.

The walk itself — the game — did the thing the best seduction scenes do: made the whole landscape erotic. He points, she picks, and by the library walk *she's hunting ahead of him*, and I felt her stop being a good sport a paragraph before the text told me she had. Each candidate was a little X-ray of her: "too expected," "you'd get both or neither," the "God, no" at the girl who runs the room. And then Vee. The book gave her an entrance like a struck chord — laughing with her whole body, "a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — which lands harder because that sentence is precisely what Randi has never once been. Of course Randi stopped walking. Of course "Yes" came out bare, "with nothing around it." I felt the pull as much as Randi did.

The managed collision afterward was pure pleasure to watch — a decade of dance folded into three seconds of choreographed accident — and the little "Yes" squeak, the happy footwork, was the most unguarded we've ever seen her. Warmer than the bench, honestly, in its way. That's what moved me: the bench broke her open in the dark; this broke her open in *daylight*, in front of strangers, grinning.

Friction: the POV stays almost entirely with Pace, and there were moments his fluency in reading Randi felt slightly like the author's fluency wearing a costume — "he saw the precise instant... he admired it, a little, even as he saw through it" — he's always right, always two beats ahead, and while I trust him (he's earned it with the safeword care, the waiting, the closed-door lesson with Daphne), I notice I never get to catch him being wrong. And a small flinch: "Go meet her" — for one beat I worried he was pushing her past her pace, but the text earned the push: he waited until *after* the wanting showed, until she asked with her eyes, and the nod was permission, not command. Okay. I'm with it.

The title now reads double: the pointing game is the literal game on the quad, but it's also everything this book does — pointing at things Randi can't yet look at directly and making her choose. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels newly exact: the whole chapter is an invitation she gets to dress up as her own gift. Next, I expect (guess only) Vee — the stats class, the copper head on the lawn — and I both want it and dread it, because Randi's face is very good at lying and Vee laughs like someone who can't. That collision is what I'm reading toward.
