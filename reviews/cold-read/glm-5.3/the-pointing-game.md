## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

Well. That title earns itself twice over — the pointing game is literally what they do on the quad, but the whole chapter is Pace pointing at the thing Randi won't look at directly, and her learning to point back. By the end she's pointing at Vee on her own. The book's cover line, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, reads differently now; "a polite invitation" is exactly what Pace engineered this whole afternoon to be. The wanting handed over disguised as a gift. Nothing in the packet promises where this goes except that it goes further in, and I'm leaning in.

Where the last two chapters left me was actually wary of Pace — the bench chapter ended with Randi sealing herself back behind the face, the bathroom lock, "not ever," and I was braced for him to bulldoze that. And this chapter does something sneakier and, honestly, sexier to me: he works *around* the lock. He asks the question in bed, where her body answers before her mind can police it ("The question reached her body before it reached her"), and she comes so hard it shocks them both. That beat landed in me like a struck bell. The moment she flinches at the sober re-asking, then slides into the "only in a threesome, with you" performance — I admired the prose's cold clarity about what that maneuver *is* ("The same act. A different owner") while my heart went out to her, because I recognized the survival move. And Pace seeing through it and letting it stand felt tender in a way that complicated my wariness. He's not innocent — "I point. You pick" is a man handing her a loaded thing and calling it her choice — but the gentleness is real too. Both true at once. That's what keeps me reading him.

The quad stretch is where the chapter got me hot in a way the explicit opening only partly did. The first scene is warm and well-made but a touch brief for the heat of what it sets up; the *walk*, though — Randi moving from good sport, to shopper, to that stopped-in-her-tracks stare at the redhead — is the erotic spine of the chapter. Her wanting arriving faster than her cover story. "God, no" at the confident blonde, "Maybe" at the girl with the beanie, and then the *bare yes* for Vee. The escalation worked on me precisely because the page scores it: every earlier woman gets a managed verdict, and Vee gets no verdict at all, just the word falling out.

Vee herself — I love her, instantly, and I'm a little on guard about how much I love her. She's written as everything the sorority grammar excludes: big, loud, laughing with her throat to the sky. My suspicion isn't of Vee; it's of what happens to a person like that when she's aimed at by people this practiced. Randi's collision maneuver is described with an admiration that made my stomach tighten even as I enjoyed the craft — "so clean it looked like nothing." Vee apologized. She has no idea she was hunted. I'm bracing for that to cost something, and I hold it open, not decided.

The last lines did me in. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — asked in bed-voice, in daylight, her head on his chest — and he deflects to "Let's go feed you," which is its own tenderness (the apple; her appetite). I noticed she *ate*: "I'm hungry" said out loud, even if the burger was still a no. Small, and it moved me.

Friction: very little. The one wobble is that the cold-eyed commentary sometimes double-dips — the "different owner" insight is spelled out twice for us (the analysis, then "He was plainly excited about this"), a thumb on the scale telling me what to think about her maneuver. And "He was happy, plainly and simply" earns its plainness; but "the game was working, the gift was moving toward her hands" slightly over-narrates a moment the grin had already carried. Small taxes on an otherwise unbroken spell.

What I want next is Vee's inside — she's been pure exterior so far, a body seen across a lawn, and I know from the book's habits that the interior is where it lives. What I dread is the lock turning again, this time in Randi while Vee is in the room. Guess, only a guess.
