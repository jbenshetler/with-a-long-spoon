The bench chapter left me worried about her — that bathroom mirror, the smile reassembled, "she was not coming out again." And then this chapter opens with her walking back into the bedroom and *letting the breath leave her in a whoosh* and asking for something different. I didn't expect that door to open again so fast, and I don't fully trust it — which I think is the right response. The page holds both things at once: she is genuinely wrecked-open with him (the "taste of yourself" exchange is the warmest, most mutually present sex they've had — "ardent before she could think better of it" is exactly her), and she is still capable of the managed recovery he "admires... even as he saw through it." The threesome answer — "Only in a threesome. With you" — is written as her brilliant dodge, and I believed it completely: she takes an unsurvivable wanting and re-packages it as a gift to the man. The book keeps showing her doing this, and keeps showing him seeing through it, and I'm gripped by the question of what happens when the packaging stops working.

The chapter title, "The Pointing Game," is literal and chilling and hot all at once. The campus walk is the best sustained scene so far — the heat is entirely in the *looking*. When she stops being a good sport a quarter mile back and starts *shopping* ("her body asking *that one? now?* into the space before his hand came up"), the charge ratcheted up beautifully. The beanie girl's "Maybe" and his kiss with the taste of her still on his mouth — that's the erotic peak for me. And Vee: the description of her laughing with her head thrown back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — I fell for her on the page the way Randi did. She's everything the sorority grammar can't grade. Casting Vee against Randi feels like the book setting a fire next to a sealed room.

Where the unease comes in: Pace *pointing at strangers in daylight* is genuinely creepy on the women's behalf — he is selecting real people who didn't consent to being auditioned. And "Go get her" — sending Randi to engineer a collision with a real woman using "three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing" — the manipulation is rendered seductively, and I felt both the heat of it and the wrongness of it. Vee doesn't know she's being hunted. Randi asking "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is the line that lands last: her want and the instrument of it fused into one breathy question. And "Let's go feed you" — after "No. I can't eat that" — reads as care and as something more possessive.
