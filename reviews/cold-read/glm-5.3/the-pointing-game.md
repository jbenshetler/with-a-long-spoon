## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Oh, this chapter got me. It got me because of the two chapters before it — I came in still carrying the weight of "The Bench," where Randi stood in that bathroom mirror and decided *the one he found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again.* That was one of the most quietly devastating things I've read in this book, the deadbolt line, the not-crying that "felt like there being no door." And then "Standards" showed me the cost of the face going back on — Brooke's warm little interrogation, and Randi lying down on her bed in last night's clothes with her shoes still on. So when this chapter opened in bed, mid-act, I was braced. I expected the locked door to stay locked.

And then the question — *"Would you like to taste another woman?"* — and she came so fast and so hard that her body answered before she could. I felt that in my own chest. Not just the heat of it (though yes, that too — her legs locking him in, "astonishment, and under it the helplessness"), but the *relief* of it: after a whole chapter of watching her wall herself off, here was the want bursting out through the one door she can't lock, because she didn't build it. Her body said a thing her mouth would never have said. That's the book's whole engine in one orgasm.

The moment that undid me most, though, was the recovery: "Only in a threesome. With you." And Pace *seeing through it and loving her for it* — "The same act. A different owner." I read that line twice. It's exactly right about her, and it's told with this terrible tenderness, because he's not fooled, he's just patient. I trust him more than almost anyone in this book, but "trust" isn't quite the word — I *believe* in him the way you believe in a good surgeon. He's handling her, surgically, and I keep having to ask myself whether I'm watching care or a very skilled experiment. The chapter mostly settles me toward care: "he wanted her gathered back into herself when he asked, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did." That's the consent I came for. He won't let her say yes from inside the fog, and he builds her a way to say it "laughing, dressed as play." I felt held by that.

The pointing game itself — I'd be lying if I said it didn't work on me erotically. It did. The whole quad walk is foreplay conducted through strangers, and the escalation is delicious: the too-expected mirror-girl, the matched pair ("you'd get both or neither" — her taste is showing and it's *funny* and it's *hers*), the beanie girl on the library steps getting the first "maybe," and the big confident chair-of-everything girl getting a "God, no" with real feeling. I was playing along, guessing her picks before she made them. When her body started *leaning forward asking for the next point* — "She was shopping" — I grinned out loud. The follow breaking frame before the lead calls the step. That dancer metaphor is doing so much work in this book.

And then Vee. Red hair sparking to copper in the wind, laughing with her head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That's the whole point, isn't it — she's the anti-face. Everything Randi has spent twenty-one years building, Vee doesn't have, and doesn't miss. And Randi's bare "Yes," out alone, "with nothing around it" — after a chapter of locked doors and held breaths, that single word cracked me open a little. I wanted it for her. I wanted her to have this.

Which is exactly what unsettles me. Pace watching from the light pole, "happy, plainly and simply," while Randi runs a three-second social operation so clean the redhead apologizes to *her* — I watched him watch her hunt, and I thought: he built a bench, and now he's building this. "He had no name for the game yet, only the live private charge of something he imagined they could make together." Private charge. He's being moved too, and I don't fully know by what. The POV loves him, but the POV loved him during the bench too, and she locked a bathroom door afterward. My suspicion isn't of his motives — it's of his confidence. He decided things for her in bed ("He had decided this beforehand"), he chose the wall by the dining hall "fast and without seeming to." Everything she chooses, he has staged the choosing of. I'm not sure he knows the difference anymore. And I'm not sure she does either.

Friction: almost none. If anything, the prose's omniscience is so total it verges on showing me things no one on the page could see — but that's been the book's register from the start, and here it pays. The one beat I paused on was "Go meet her" coming so fast after the "yes" — I wanted one more breath of her getting to *keep* the wanting before it was converted into action — but her looking back for permission, "not before... but after the wanting had already shown," and him simply letting her see his face, won me back. That beat is the chapter's real sex scene, honestly: "*is this one mine to take.*"

The title — "The Pointing Game" — is straightforward now, the game itself, though I notice he "points" and she "picks," and the book's title, *A Polite Invitation* (Book One of *With a Long Spoon*), now reads exactly like this chapter: an invitation wrapped so courteously she can accept without ever admitting she wanted to be asked. Supper with the devil, long spoon. I don't yet know who the devil is supposed to be, and I like not knowing.

What I want next: Vee, in the flesh, on the page. I want to know if she's as free as she looked from forty feet. I dread — and want — the sorority finding out. And I dread the moment the game stops being a game, the moment Randi has to look at what she wants with the lights on. Guess from here: Vee is going to like her back, and that's going to be the thing that breaks the face for good — because Vee won't want the face, she'll want the one who said *yes* with nothing around it.
