## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well. The bench chapter left me hollowed out — that bathroom mirror scene, the deadbolt, "the one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." I came into this chapter braced for aftermath and for one long stretch I didn't get it; I got a girl who apparently *did* come back out. She's on him, saying "let's do something different," hands in his hair, glowingly present. And honestly I was a little betrayed at first — did the last chapter just get reset? But then I stopped fighting it, because the text isn't pretending the reset is real. She walks in with the table top back on, doesn't speak of it, and neither does he. Everything unsaid between these two is still unsaid; they've just laid horniness over the top of it, which is exactly how people like Randi survive things. So the unease never left me, it just went underground — same place she put the crying.

The bed scene worked on me more than I expected. "You like the taste of yourself" — *yes*, that beat landed, the little caught warmth of her answer. And then the question. I gasped, actually: "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and she comes, instantly, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That is the whole book in one thrust. Her body confessing before her face can get a mask on it. And then the later, clothed, careful re-ask — "Do you actually want to taste another woman?" — and the flinch I felt through *his* skin. Then the recovery: "Only in a threesome. With you." The page names it plainly — "A different owner" — and that's her, the face doing its work, handing the appetite to him so she never has to hold it herself. I believed every second of it. This is the most honest dishonesty I've read in a while.

Pace continues to fascinate and unsettle me in equal measure. He's so careful — he waits for the heat to leave the room, wants her "gathered back into herself," gives her the camouflage and lets it stand. That's tenderness, real tenderness. But there's also the omniscient smugness of him — "he took the body's answer for the true one" — and the pointing game is him running an experiment on her with the tenderness as part of the apparatus. I don't think he's cynical; I think he genuinely wants her to have this. But he is steering, always steering, and he's enjoying being right about her, and I noticed that. The "Go meet her" moment — he watched her want it first, *then* gave permission — is either the most generous thing he's done or the most controlling, and the chapter deliberately won't let me split those. Good. I don't want them split.

The quad stretch is the warmest this book has run, and it swept me up. The wind, the burning maple, the cheeseburger she won't let herself eat ("I can't eat that," easy, automatic — that small cold line amid all the sun). And the game itself: "I point. You pick." Watching Randi stop being a good sport and start *shopping* — the coil gathering through her hand, her body asking *that one? now?* — that was genuinely hot, fully clothed, nothing happening but walking. Heat without a single act. The beanie girl's "Maybe," the savage "God, no" at the blazer girl — she's got taste, and it's specific: not the mirrors, not the ones who run the room, someone who takes up room and is glad to. Someone the grammar can't grade.

Enter Vee. And the page goes all in on her — the copper hair, the snub nose, the freckles, the laugh with her whole body, "a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That last phrase, against everything Randi is, made my chest hurt. Of course Randi can't look away. Of course the word comes out "alone, with nothing around it." The collision Randi engineers is choreography and I watched it with real admiration — the redhead apologizing to *her* — and the ending, "Do you think she'll like the bench?", low against his chest, in daylight, made me put the book down. That sentence has a deadbolt in it. The bench broke Randi open; now she's wondering if it will break Vee too, or whether Vee is the kind of person who doesn't need breaking. I don't know which reading to hold and I'm not going to force one.

Title: "The Pointing Game" is literal — but it also names the whole mechanism of the book. He points; she picks; the game points *at her*. Cover line: *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation.* The "long spoon" idiom — supping with the devil — sits over everything now. A polite invitation: the pointing game was exactly that, for both women. I don't yet know who the devil is at this table, or whether there is one, or whether there are three spoons.

What I dread: Vee walked into this laughing, unaware, and everything we know about what this house does to the guarded. What I want more of: Randi's interior — the last chapter's mirror gave us one naked look inside her; this chapter we mostly stayed outside, in Pace's read of her. It worked, but I miss her already.
