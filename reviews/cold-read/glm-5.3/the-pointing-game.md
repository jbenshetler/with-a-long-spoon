## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter still holding my breath from "Standards" — Randi lying on her made bed in yesterday's clothes, shoes on, not-crying — and this chapter answered that silence with something that scared me a little, because it worked on me completely. The opening is quiet and deliberate: *Let's do something different,* she says at the table with its top back on, "not a question. She handed him the day as she handed him every one." That's the line that set the whole chapter's key for me. She keeps handing him things — the day, the evening, the wanting — and he keeps taking them, and each handing-over feels both like trust and like the slow emptying of a woman who has decided (in that bathroom mirror, "not ever") never to hand over the real thing. So the sex that follows has this doubleness running under it: it's the warmest, most mutual scene they've had — she's "not performing now," the first orgasm is genuinely hers — and I still felt a shadow, and I think the page earned it, because the chapter itself keeps insisting on the difference between her body's answers and her mouth's corrections.

The middle of the bed scene is where the chapter hooked me hardest. The question — *Would you like to taste another woman?* — and her body answering before she can: "she came, hard... far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That landed on me as heat, honestly, and also as a kind of ambush she didn't consent to in advance. But I want to be fair to the text: Pace is scrupulous here in his own way. "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." He waits for the heat to leave the room, wants her "gathered back into herself... free to weigh it and choose." He hopes she can say something true "without having to sit up and put a name to it." And then — and this is the beat that gave me a chill of admiration for the writing — she takes the escape he's built her, but she takes it *sideways*: "Only in a threesome. With you." He names it perfectly: she "handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument... The same act. A different owner." I believed him that it's beautiful. I also believed it's a door closing dressed as a door opening. Both are true, and the chapter knows it.

Then the walk, and the title's game. This is the stretch that genuinely swept me up — the burning sugar maple, the wind off the ridge, and the two of them walking through it while he points and she picks. "I point. You pick." That line did something to me: the choosing is hers, and he *sees* that — "He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could." I watched her go from propriety-offended ("the bald social wrongness of it") to the low "Too expected," to "Maybe" at the beanie girl on the library steps, to shopping — "She would have told him she was being a good sport... She was shopping." That progression is the hottest thing in the chapter for me, hotter than the bed scene, because it's desire becoming legible to *herself* in real time, in daylight, in public. And the kiss after "Maybe" — her own taste reaching her again, "her composure had a crack in it" — yes. That landed.

And then Vee. The girl at the dining hall doors — built on a lavish scale, red hair sparking copper in the wind, laughing with her whole body, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Everything Randi is not, or rather everything Randi has spent twenty-one years not allowing herself to be. When Randi stopped mid-stride and did not look away, when the "Yes" came out "alone, with nothing around it — the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it," I felt that in my chest. And the collision! I knew what I was watching — three seconds of execution so clean "it looked like nothing" — and I loved watching it and was slightly frightened by it at the same time. She is *very good* at this, and the skill is the same skill that fixed the smile in the bathroom mirror. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — that question, low, with her head against him, is the chapter's last turn of the knife: she's already folding Vee into *their* apparatus, the gift-framing, the owner-transfer. "Let's go feed you," he says, and I noticed the apple again — his noticing of her appetite, the care and the management of it in one gesture.

Friction: very little. The one place I briefly felt the author's thumb was the "fictional vignette" of the blazer girl — "This one would not have waited to be asked. She brought Randi in and kissed her" — the fantasy inset confused me for half a beat before I caught the register shift. And there's a persistent low hum in Pace's competence, his perfect reads of everyone, that sometimes reads as omniscience more than attention; here it mostly felt earned. The "God, no" at the chairing girl — the one who'd kiss Randi first, the one with the authority Randi can't afford — felt true and right as her limit.

Trust vs. suspicion: I don't suspect Pace of anything bad, exactly — his care reads as real, the consent scaffolding is real — but I'm increasingly unsure the book wants me to be sure. And I've begun to ache for Randi precisely because she keeps succeeding. The title tells me this was a game with rules; the rules were "I point, you pick," and she won — and the prize is a woman she wants, filed safely under *for us*. Where it goes from here I can't guess, except this: Vee laughed with her whole body at a friend. If she's real the way the page drew her, she may be harder to manage than the sorority grammar is built for.
