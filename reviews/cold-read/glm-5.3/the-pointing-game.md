## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

Well. After "Standards" — that chapter of held breath and the face lying in the mirror — I didn't know what I wanted more: for Pace to keep pulling Randi open, or for someone to stop him. This chapter answered by making me complicit. I spent most of it grinning like Randi on the lawn.

The bed scene first: it's hot, and it's doing something I didn't expect. "Would you like to taste another woman?" and her body answers before she does — "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That landing worked on me two ways at once: it's genuinely electric, the suddenness of it, her astonishment, and it's the single most honest thing she's done in the book. After a whole chapter of her sealing the face back on, the wanting comes out sideways through her body before her mind can intercept it. And he *sees* it and doesn't take it as her answer — "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." Whatever my unease about this man, that line earned him a lot of credit with me. He keeps asking anyway, later, after the heat has left the room, when she's "free to weigh it and choose." That's the consent I trust this book to write.

And then the flinch. The half-second where the question stops being bed-talk and becomes a real thing asked. I felt that in my stomach, because I know exactly what it costs her — I was in that bathroom with her, I was on that bed with the shoes still on. And then the recovery: "Only in a threesome. With you." The narration names it plainly — "The same act. A different owner." That's the sentence of the chapter for me. She hands the appetite to him and makes herself its instrument, and the book doesn't pretend she did anything else, and he doesn't either. He lets the camouflage stand. Twice, actually — "He let the camouflage stand," and later "He let her have it." I noticed the repetition. Whether it's mercy or management, the page keeps it ajar, and I'm leaving it ajar — but I noticed that he's *always* the one who sees truly and decides what to do with what he sees. That's the flicker of unease I carry about Pace: not that he wants wrong things, but that the whole book runs on his reading of her, and she never gets to read him back.

The walk itself — this is where I melted. The quad, the burning maple, the wind in her ponytail, "a single scrunchie, the one flourish she'd allowed herself." This writer does landscape like interior weather. And the game itself is seduction of the reader as much as of Randi: "I point. You pick." I watched her turn from being a good sport to *shopping* — "a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves" — and the moment on the library steps, the "maybe" girl with the green beanie, when he kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth and her breath catches — that's the hottest beat in the chapter, more than any of the bed. And the chairwoman blond getting the fast "God, no" — I laughed, the real feeling in the *no* telling me more about Randi than any of her yeses.

Then Vee. And I mean *Vee* — that name is in my cover packet's fine print of double-names, so I clocked the register, but the page itself made her unmistakable: the laugh with the head thrown back, "a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Of course it's her. Everything the sorority grammar excludes — scale, abundance, copper in the wind — and everything Randi's been starving herself away from. Randi's bare "Yes," with "nothing around it," landing before the costume gets there — that's the second honest thing she's done in two chapters, and the first she's done in daylight. I was fully swept up. The collision she engineers is three seconds of clean, readable, gorgeous social machinery, and Pace watching from the light pole "went to the root of him," and I felt the both-of-them in it too.

Then the last line: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — and my stomach dropped, pleasantly and not. Because the bench is where Randi got broken open, and she's proposing it for Vee like a gift, like a party trick — "we've got a stats class together" — the managed surface already reasserting around the wanting. And Pace doesn't answer it. "Let's go feed you." Which is tender and is also a redirect, and I don't know which. That's what I love about this book: I genuinely can't tell how much of Pace is a man loving a woman open and how much is a man assembling something, and the page refuses to settle it.

Title: "The Pointing Game" is literal and it isn't. He points; she picks. But pointing is also what you do to a thing to make it exist as an object of want — and the whole chapter is Pace pointing at Randi's own desire and saying *that one*. Volume title, *A Polite Invitation* — after this chapter the irony bites: the invitation is polite exactly where the thing invited isn't. *With a Long Spoon* still means nothing to me, and I like that.

What I want next: Vee again, immediately. What I dread: whatever happens the first time the polished surface and the laughing-in-public girl are in the same room. Guess, marked as a guess: the beanie girl's "maybe" isn't done, and Randi's "unsurvivable" word from the bathroom is going to be tested by how much she likes watching, not being watched.
