## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came out of this chapter warm all the way through, and then — like Vee checking her mirror after a morning in a lit box — I caught myself and asked how much of that warmth I should trust. Because this is the brunch chapter, the "no story" chapter, and it might be the most charged thing the book has done since the bet. Nothing happened. Everything got reported. And the person it all got reported *to* sat in a dark green booth and took it like a woman being paid in a currency she was never supposed to want.

Vee first: I adore her here, and I think she's crossed some line she hasn't noticed crossing. Her whole opening confession — "We have so much sex, Randi" said "plain and a little helpless," the "sex brain," "I used to be a person with thoughts" — is her naming her own appetite out loud for the first time not as a confession of shame but as a *wonder*. And Randi's answer is the truest thing anyone has said to her in the book: "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud." That line undid me a little, the way the best lines here do — because it's exactly right, and because it's being said by the person who helped arrange the hunger. Randi absolves Vee of the exact thing the plan set out to grow, and I felt the warmth and the vertigo in the same swallow. The eggs Benedict bit — climbing the right-hand column past the soup, "the daring a little easier each time" — continues the book's food-and-appetite pulse perfectly, and the yolk running gold, her hand going back "before she'd decided anything," is appetite rendered as pure image. That's her whole arc in one breakfast.

Randi is the one I can't stop watching, and this chapter moved her for me more than any since the bench. Twice the mask slipped, and both times the text earned it and then let it pass unexplained, which is exactly how it should be. First: Vee's "like he wants *me*, with the body attached," and "Randi didn't answer" — her eyes "bright and a little unfixed," the head-shake, the catch in her breath, "It doesn't," low and not quite even. I read that as Randi hearing described, from inside, the exact thing she decided in chapter 1 never to let anyone do to her again. Vee is accidentally telling her what being *gotten through to* feels like, and it landed on Randi like weather. Second: "Just us," said "softer than the plan called for, a small roughness under it, there and gone." Vee "took it for the ordinary pleasure of a plan made." I don't. I've watched this woman's feeling for Vee outrun the game since the boutique, and it's not outrunning it anymore — it has outrun it, and she's standing in the wreckage of her own ch 1 vow handing out absolution.

And the escalation I can't decide how to feel about: the photographs. "Does he have a brother?" was pure Randi, and I laughed. But then she drew the full picture out of Vee — "you only gave him *half*?" — a gentle, delighted flinch-bait that worked exactly as designed, and Vee, *not wanting to be the girl who flinched*, handed a nude into Randi's actual hands. My wariness here is real but complicated: Vee did choose it, thumbed to it "before the rest of her had voted" (that phrasing is doing so much work), took pride in it, came out happy. Nothing was taken from her. And yet I know something she doesn't — that everything she gives Randi gets carried back into Pace's bed, and that Randi *filed* it, again. The irony that burned brightest: Vee told Pace "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone," and within a week the photograph has been shared — shown to Randi's eyes, at Randi's own request, kept "never shared" only in the narrowest technical sense. Whether Pace and Randi count each other as "anyone" is exactly the question, and I suspect it's the question the whole book is walking toward. The checkpoint told me a disclosure scheme is the coming engine; I don't know that as a reader, but I can *feel* the pieces being assembled here — the picture, the class enrollment together, Randi now inside Vee's Tuesdays and Thursdays too.

The goodbye kiss is the chapter's quiet turn, and it's Vee's: **she** held the kiss, leaned up the last half-inch "taking a second of it for herself, not ready to give the morning up." "She noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." That's the woman who refused to touch herself in the bathroom one chapter ago now taking her wanting out in small public installments without naming it. The unnamed thing keeps moving. And Randi's answer — holding the eye contact "a beat past what a goodbye needed" until Vee almost felt it as strange — mirrors Randi's own bathroom-door move: leave it ajar, see if the door gets pushed. I don't know that she's doing it on purpose anymore. That's what makes her frightening and sympathetic at once.

Small friction: the "Miss November" and "showing him your toes" lines are pure Randi and landed fine. The one beat I paused at was Vee's interpretation of Randi's stillness — "she went warm with something close to pride, that a Saturday of hers could do that to a woman like Randi" — but that's not authorial thumb-on-scale, that's Vee's self-editing rendered honestly, and it's consistent with everything she does with Cassie. The moment I most believed: "I've never once heard it start" — the music, the heat turned up before she arrives. The house has been preparing for her from the beginning. She still reads it as luck.

The title, "All the Time" — I read it first as Vee's "the amount of it," the sex and the attention running continuously. On second thought it's darker: the *plan* has been all the time, every warm detail she recounts as evidence of love — the playlists already playing, the cooking, the slowness — the reader knows some of it was built. I can no longer fully tell which parts are him and which are the game. I don't think Vee will be able to either, and that's the dread I'm carrying: not that she'll be hurt by a secret, but that she'll be unable to distinguish the real love from the scaffolding. Anthro with Randi, seven a.m., "it'd be like stats, except we'd have picked it" — even the picking, I now doubt a little. And Pace in the last line: she's "pointing it back toward Pace… already, thirty minutes out, beginning to want." The book keeps feeding her and she keeps coming back hungrier. So do I.
