## Reader reaction

The brunch chapters keep being my favorites, and this one landed soft and warm and then, at the very end, quietly knocked the wind out of me. This is Vee fully arrived somewhere — not in a scene, not in a dress or a spanking or a game, just *happy*, and telling Randi about it in the total, unguarded way she now tells Randi everything. The whole chapter is one long outpouring, and the outpouring is the plot.

What swept me: "like my mouth had been asleep since I was born and nobody told me. Like I'd been eating with the sound off." That's the book in one sentence. And the moment she finally asks the question under everything — "Am I turning into some kind of — sex pervert?" — and Randi answers, "You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*." That's absolution, and it's the exact thing Vee's mother's voice (*floozie*) has never once given her. Randi hands her the reframe her whole life needed. It's the most generous Randi has ever been on the page, and I melted.

And then the two moments the chapter is really about. First: Randi going still and unfixed while Vee describes being *wanted with the body attached* — "Randi didn't answer" — that catch in her breath. I've read enough of Randi's thumbnail-pressing and glass-rim pressing to know what wanting looks like on her, and this is it, undisguised. Vee misreads it as her story landing on a friend; I read it as Randi hearing the exact relationship she engineered described back to her as bliss. Something in that should ache for her, and does.

Second: the photograph. Vee showing Randi the cropped one, then — because Randi needled her ("you only gave him *half*?") — the *uncropped* one, the one she sent Pace. I actually gasped. Vee is now showing Randi her intimate pictures of herself — she doesn't know she's handing them to Pace's *girlfriend*, the woman who helped pick her. The dramatic irony is almost unbearable, and the book isn't playing it for cruelty, just letting it sit there. And the toes line — "Showing him your toes, eh?" — blue polish, the nail polish from ch20, Randi kneeling at her feet: the fetishism is all Randi's and Vee still doesn't see it.

The goodbye kiss inverted me. Up until now Randi has always been the one who takes the extra beat. This time *Vee* leans up the last half-inch. "She noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." That's the most honest sentence about Vee's whole method of living. The drift I've been tracking — her want for Randi, unnamed, shelved as friendship — just crossed a line Vee herself drew. And she drives back to Pace already wanting him. She has no idea she's holding two.

Suspicion: none of anyone is *off* — but the Anthro elective. "Just us," Randi says, with a roughness under it. A standing weekly appointment, chosen, just the two of them. Vee thinks she's making a friend plan; I think Randi is being handed exactly what she asked Pace for ("How do I get her from *us*"), except Vee built it herself. That's the dread and the swoon together.

Friction: minor — "Miss November" and the photo exchange ran a shade long for me, and the waiter-free brunches are getting familiar as a container (third or fourth one). But the writing about the eggs Benedict going everywhere — "the middle, when it goes everywhere" — is so bodily and true I forgive it everything.

The title, "All the Time": her sex-brain, the "the amount of it," the music that's always already playing ("I've never once heard it start") — wanting as a permanent condition now, not an event. It fits. Book title "A Warm Reception" — the reception keeps warming; I don't know where the heat ends.

What I dread next: Cassie is out (she did her elective "with that oddball Wardy" — Cassie being edged out of the Tuesday/Thursday slot stung a little), Thanksgiving approaches, Pace has parents he won't go back to. Guess from here: the concealed arrangement surfaces, and this brunch — the photos, the class, the kiss — is the last of the easy before it does.
