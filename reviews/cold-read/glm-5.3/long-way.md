## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Okay. I'm smiling and I'm a little bit braced, and both of those feelings are because of the chapter title. "The Long Way" — that's the walk they take at the end, the not-toward-anything walk, but it also whispers the whole shape of this book back at me: this is the long way 'round, Pace meeting Vee through Randi's staging, and me knowing what Randi knows and Vee doesn't. I keep reading these Vee scenes with a stone in my shoe, and it's the stone from *Substitution*: Randi's hand busy in her lap, the cup that comes up and no sip happens, *he wants to see you naked.* The collision on the lawn, *Her name is Vee and we've got a stats class together.* I can't unknow that, and so every warm thing in this chapter lands twice — once as the sweet first-date chapter it is, and once as a thing moving through a piece of staging I can see the edges of. The froyo date was genuinely lovely and I still flinched at "You're not unsure," because Randi isn't coaching a friend there; she's closing a sale she opened on a quad.

But let me give the chapter its due, because it earned it. The frozen yogurt bit is my favorite thing in the book so far: "There's a system. Tart on the bottom holds up under the heavy ones. You put chocolate on tart and it's an argument the whole way down." That's Pace exactly — the man from *The Bench*, the one who thinks about the order of things, the one who "has thought about most things" and says so without apology. And the callback! *He studied the flavor placards with the same attention he'd given her calculus.* The apple sliced thin because she eats more when the cut sides show — this is the same attentiveness, and Vee is standing in the river of it now without knowing there's an upstream. The detail that got me most is the small one: her cup overfills, "too much, the cone of it leaning," and *usually she heard her mother before she'd finished — that's plenty, that's enough now — today there was nothing.* That's the appetite chapter again. Randi portioning herself down to nothing; Vee discovering the voice gone quiet on a walk with a man who pays for both before she finds her wallet. And then the ending — she confesses this is the nicest anyone's been in a long time, braces for it to be made small, and he just... lets it sit, and says "Your tart's holding up." She looks down "and it was, the whole stack of it, still standing." I actually got a little misty at that, and I'm suspicious of my own mistiness because of what I know, and I'm going to hold both.

The banter at the top with Randi and Cassie is doing quiet work I liked. Cassie's *"As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again"* — one semester of Vee staying up until two keeping a business major alive in a video game — is a perfect Cassie line, flat and funny and revealing: Vee is a person who over-serves the people near her. Randi's *"The rule's for boys you're not sure about. You're not unsure"* is true as friendship and double-true as management; Randi is very good at making the push feel like the pull. And "He looked like a man who wants you... He wants to see you naked" — Randi says it grinning, and it's a joke, and it's also the most literal thing anyone has said all chapter.

Erotically this is a 1 by the scale's own definition — no charge on the page, this is courtship — but the *longing* is doing something. Vee's stomach dropping "the drop of a stair missed in the dark" when the phone lights up; wanting to fall through her seat at a glance at his watch; "She wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on." That last line is the whole chapter and possibly the whole trap: Pace's attention is the most seductive thing in this book, and I've watched it take Randi apart on a bench, and now it's turned on a girl who doesn't know it's a known quantity.

My suspicion is precise and it's about the *Substitution* scene read in sequence: the coffee shop was a setup. I'm not inventing that — the collision on the lawn was choreographed, Randi steered her to the place on Wilson, Randi's hands were busy while Vee talked, and Randi said *you should go out with him* with all the warmth of a woman handing off a gift she'd arranged. What I can't tell — and the chapter rightly won't tell me — is what Randi's hand is doing on her own body while she watches Vee light up. Whether the gift is cruel or generous or both. Pace on this date is impeccably consensual, unhurried, giving her the pencil back. But "he wants to see you naked" came from the woman who selected her, and "I've thought about most things" is starting to sound like a warning label.

Titles: "The Long Way" recolors on second thought — the long way is the walk, but it's also that this book is going the long way 'round to some destination I can feel but can't see. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* with the series *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon line is the supping-with-the-devil proverb, and every chapter makes the devil politer. The invitation feels extended to Vee now. What I dread and want is the same thing: the chapter where Vee and Randi's wanting and Pace's architecture all get into one room. Guess from here — the froyo stack standing up is the image I'd bet comes back down.
