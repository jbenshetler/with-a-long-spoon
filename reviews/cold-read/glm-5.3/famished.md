## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This is the chapter I've been waiting ten chapters for, and it landed harder than I expected. The whole book has been about Vee's appetite — the title finally pays off the hunger that's been running under everything: the meal where she "ate without watching herself," the satin slick against her on the couch, and now this. "Famished," she says, and hears it a beat after he does — that little delay is the whole chapter: her body keeps telling the truth slightly ahead of her.

What swept me most is that the shame doesn't get erased, it gets *burned through*. "The shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her" — that's the sentence I'll remember. Every other sexual peak in this book has come with a door slamming (Randi in the bathroom, the deadbolt, the arranged smile). Vee is the first character who keeps her eyes open through her own want. The loop — *floozie*, heat arriving a beat behind the shame, "no seam, they ran on the same wire" — is the book's honest answer to Randi: what Randi hides, Vee is slowly learning to carry lit.

Pace deepens for me here, and my wariness of him sharpens in the good way. "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" is a strange, real, perfect line — a man flagging his own body's want as something she might need protecting from, consent living all the way down into the sex itself. And yet I notice he's also *watching her the whole way* — "there was nowhere to put her face where he would not see it." The same gaze that felt like a gift in the fitting ("His eyes asked, *May I look*") is now total, and she can't escape it. It's still tender, but I felt the first flicker of it as surveillance. That's earned by the page, not by suspicion — she says "he was looking at her. Not at her body — at her face... reading her while it happened."

The scar high on his inner thigh — her hand *stopped* on it. The text gives us his birthmark by his ear as the match, so the patch on his side is the same uneven pink. That's what Pace has been keeping behind the "door easing shut" when parents came up. I'm holding it open; the page does.

The ending undid me. Her folding the panties "in thirds, the spot centered" and handing them to him — "Trade" — is Vee taking the thing that has shamed her most (the evidence, the wetness, the *want made visible*) and turning it into a gift and a claim at once. It mirrors Randi's "red" from chapter one: a small word doing an enormous amount. And Pace taking them, with "something in his shoulders was private" — that's Randi's privacy trick, inverted and healthy. He receives without narrating.

Friction, small: the "too hard" question — I buy it, but it's the first line that made me pause and reread; it's odd by design and I'll let it. And the last week's Randi-lunch thread ("You don't have to tell me," the kiss in the car) is humming under this whole chapter — she dressed *for their first time*, she chose the green, she painted her toenails plum "without thinking too hard about why." Vee is dressing for Randi's questions as much as for Pace's eyes, and she doesn't know it. Neither does Pace, about either of them. I dread and want the day Vee learns the pointing game.

Title-wise: *Famished* is the double hunger — food and touch and being seen — finally fed at once. The book's tagline-level promise ("A Polite Invitation") now reads as the entire courtship: everything Pace does is invitation. The volume title's "long spoon" is starting to feel like the price of dining with the devil, or like two people feeding each other at a long table — I don't know yet, and I like not knowing.
