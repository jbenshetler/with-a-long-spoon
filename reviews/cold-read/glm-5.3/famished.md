So that's the door finally opened — and I notice it was *her* hand on it the whole way. "Will you show me your bedroom?" pulled out of him before his care could arrive, the apron strap tugged loose, "Famished" said a beat before she heard it. The chapter keeps giving Vee the initiative and keeps Pace asking exactly one question — "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — which is the least romantic and most honest sentence in the whole scene, permission and compliment fused into one, and it undid me a little. Everything I've loved about this book's heat was here: the shame running on the same wire as the want, and the book's whole thesis landing in one line — "The shame was still running and she was not watching herself do any of it." That's the flicker of the pool, the dropped self-surveillance, finally in bed.

The swoon is real and I'm not going to shade it. Him going to his knees to take the skirt off "like a man learning ground he meant to come back to," his mouth staying *in* the wet of her, glad of it — "nothing in how he held her wanted her to be any less than she was" — I melted. And him watching her *face* during, coming with her when she turned away: that's the looking-motif escalated to its most exposed. The scar high on his inner thigh, noticed and unexplained — first sighting of whatever "more or less" and the closed "parents" door are hiding. I held my breath at it and the page moved on, correctly.

But I have to be honest about the hum underneath all this warmth: I know what the reader knows. The whole courtship was built in a bedroom, and this bedroom is its harvest. What kept the unease from souring the scene is that the page played it entirely as *her* wanting — she dressed for it, painted her toenails for it, bought the green set "for their first time" while telling herself none of it meant anything. He took nothing; she brought everything. And still — my chest hurts a little for her, because the more gladly she gives, the higher the fall when she learns there was a plan. Nobody has asked that question yet, and this chapter is the closest the book has come to making me feel its weight.

The ending. "Trade." She folds the panties in thirds, spot centered, and hands them over — and *something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private.* I sat with that a long moment. It's the flannel shirt logic inverted: now she's the one leaving a piece of herself in his house, deliberately, an exchange rather than a gift. Given everything I know about Pace's rituals (the shirt he never asked back, the folded garments of chapter one), this landed as her being invited — unknowingly — into an economy he already runs with another woman. I don't know that. It's a guess from here. But the privacy in his shoulders felt like the page agreeing with me.

Small frictions: the bedroom itself. White walls, low bed, plain sheets — I kept waiting to see the bench, and it wasn't there. The room where Randi came apart doesn't seem to be this room, or the book chose not to look. That absence is either a continuity I'm misremembering or a deliberate withholding, and it itches. And the mother's-voice loop ("*floozie*") running through the sex worked for me right up until it repeated almost verbatim twice in a paragraph — the one place the chapter's engine showed.

The title: *Famished* — the feeding motif's crest. "Let's go feed you" all the way back in chapter three, and here the appetite is finally answered on both wires at once: she says the word, he smiles, and the chapter gives her sex first and fra diavolo after, in that order, both without shame closing her. Next I expect the induction, the burgundy dress's debut, and — I dread this — Randi somewhere in that room watching. I want to keep reading. I want to protect Vee. Both at once.
