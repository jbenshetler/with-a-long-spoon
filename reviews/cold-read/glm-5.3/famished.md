**Felt read**

Well. There it is. The chapter the whole book has been walking toward, and it landed on me harder than I expected, mostly because the shame didn't go away when the sex arrived — it ran *through* it. That's the thing I keep loving about Vee: her mother's voice doesn't get defeated, it gets *fed into the circuit*. "*Floozie,* said the voice. That was the worst it had." The worst it had. Not the worst it could do. She's outgrown the word without ever quite shutting it up, and the proof is the line "she could not find the top of it and she did not try. It was the first time that had been true." That's the whole arc of eleven chapters in one sentence — not shamelessness, but shame that stopped closing her.

The pre-dressing sequence almost got me more than the sex. The plum nail polish "she chose without thinking too hard about why," the toenails painted early — a direct callback to Randi's blue lacquer from chapter one, which I half-smiled at and then felt a small chill at, because Randi's polish was armor and Vee's is offering. And the green set: "it would be silly to take the one and leave the other." She's kidding herself beautifully, and the book knows it, and lets her. The flannel at night, the smell "gone thin after a week of her wearing it, so that she had to chase it" — that image ached.

Pace. "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" is the single Pace-est sentence in the book — the consent question wrapped in the euphemism, asked from his knees at the exact place he's been circling since the fitting, and Vee understanding it was "a real question" and "something else as well." Then the moment where he stops governing himself and she realizes she'd wanted that without knowing it — that undid me a little. And the scar, high on the inside of his thigh, noticed and unexplained. A door left ajar, and I'm leaving it ajar.

The sex itself burns — heat fused with exposure, her hips "chasing it, greedy," his eyes on her face the whole way. The most charged beat for me wasn't the acts, it was that he *watched what each return did to her face* and she gave up getting away from it. That's the fitting's thesis consummated: being seen at her most shameless and not once found wanting.

And then "Trade." She comes back, drops the sheet, and folds her ruined green panties with the spot exactly centered and hands them to him. I sat with that a long moment. It's her taking the thing she'd have died over at the sun porch — the wet evidence, the humiliation — and *making it a gift*, with her own hands, in the kitchen light. "Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private." I don't know what he felt and I'm glad I don't.

Where I'm braced: Randi isn't here at all, and the last chapter ended with Vee's mouth still warm from Randi's kiss, scanning the dorm windows in fear and something else. The two threads — Randi's mouth, Pace's bed — are now both lit, and Vee's want on the page has never once been good at staying in one room. I don't dread it exactly; I dread-and-want it.

The title: "Famished" does the double work — her "Famished," said and heard a beat late, was the yes before the yes. And the book's board title, *With a Long Spoon* (Book One — *A Polite Invitation*), now reads to me almost ominously: the proverb is about keeping distance from the devil, and nobody in this book is keeping distance anymore. *A Polite Invitation* — Pace's whole seduction style, "may I choose," "would you be bothered" — the politeness carried her all the way into this, and the invitation has been accepted.

I want the dress, the induction, and whatever Randi does next. I want it badly and I'm slightly afraid of all three.
