## Reader reaction

God, this chapter. It's the bathroom chapter again — the *exact* shape of chapter 30, her hand caught at the threshold — except this time the door she won't walk through has Randi's face on it, and she knows it now and still can't name it. I read that stall scene with my heart going, because the book has been building to precisely this: "Randi's face arrived behind her eyes... She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was." That's the whole unadmitted thing in one line. And then she stops — "the bright wanting curdled into something low and ashamed and obedient" — and I felt that curdle in my own chest, because *obedient* is a chilling word for what her body does when it's looked at by that woman. She obeyed a face that wasn't even in the room.

The brunch itself is the hottest fully-clothed scene I've read in this book, and it's hot the way the practice room was hot: Randi draws the story out of her one door at a time — "How does a girl get from a pantsing to a bare bottom. Walk me." — and every confession lands in Vee's body on the page, in the full sun, with mimosas. "Her body, which had been climbing the whole morning one careful confession at a time, simply reached the top." Yes. I was right there climbing with her. Randi's mixer story is calculated — I know it's calculated, I've watched her do this twice now with stories that aren't quite stories — and it still worked on *me*, the picture of "dress up, panties to the side, knees wide" arriving in Vee "entire." The heat in this chapter is the point and the danger in the same body, which is what this book does better than anything else I'm reading.

"Sticky fingers?" — I actually put the book down. The floor left the room for me too. And then the pastry plate, and "she could not tell... whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one. She sat down. She did not find out." That refusal to resolve is exquisite and maddening in equal measure, and I honor it, because Vee can't tell either. But *I* can't tell, and I know what Randi is. Randi knew. She had to know — she sat there and watched Vee go, "easy, no curiosity anywhere in it," which is the tell: Randi is never not curious. She was waiting. Whether that line was mercy or a hook, I genuinely can't parse, and the pastry — the *pastry*. "One neat bite... 'Delicious'... did not touch it again." Randi takes one bite of everything and lets the rest sit there. That's her, isn't it, that's the whole woman in a gesture.

And the ending. The kiss at the curb — the worn-in ritual, now with "a little more in them" — and then the glaze on Vee's lip. "A pastry she had never once touched." So the sweetness on her mouth is Randi's, transferred. Vee walks away wearing the taste of the thing she wouldn't take. I actually shivered. That's the most loaded image the book has given me since the folded panties, and it recolors backward: the pastries on the plate were Randi's mouth, offered, one bite taken and refused. "One Bite" — the title. Take one bite of the wanting and stop, like everyone at this table does. Randi bites once and sets it down; Vee touches herself barely and draws back; even the drink is one mimosa each. Everyone in this book is rationing a hunger. Except that Vee is the one being fed up to a full meal she hasn't sat down to yet.

The "best friend in the whole world" beat landed harder than I expected — the floor going out from under her "but kindly this time, sweetly, in a direction she had no guard up against." That's the tragedy and the warmth at once: the *truest* sentence Randi says all chapter may be that one, and it's said by the person steering her. My feelings about Randi are now genuinely split down the middle — I believe the affection is real ("she got past me") and I believe the elicitation is deliberate, in the same sip of coffee. The mixer story itself — I don't entirely buy the blond boy existed as told, or existed and mattered; what mattered was its function. I noticed, too, she never mentions Pace. Two women at brunch both freshly spanked and only one of them knows the other's history on that score.

Friction: almost none. The "clinical blue" bathroom light telling her she's "someone caught" is maybe a hair on-the-nose, and the mimosa-budget mercy is a lovely character beat but I've now seen the cheaper-drink maneuver twice. Small complaints against a chapter that had me by the throat.

Volume: *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation.* "One Bite" as a chapter title now reads as almost cruel in retrospect — it told me the shape (a taste, a stop) without giving away the turn, which is the best kind of title. The volume title still hums underneath: you sup with the devil with a long spoon — a careful, arm's-length tasting of something dangerous — and Book One being *A Polite Invitation* makes me think the invitation proper hasn't even been spoken yet. This brunch was the appetizer course, literally. I dread and want the same thing: the day Vee finds out the table was set before she walked in. And I want her hand to get where it was going, once, with her eyes open.
