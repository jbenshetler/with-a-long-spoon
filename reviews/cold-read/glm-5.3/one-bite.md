**Reader reaction — Chapter 31: "One Bite"**

The felt read:

This chapter got me in the body before I got it in the head. It's all light — white muntins, cold flutes, ten in the morning — and that's exactly what makes it scald. Vee's confessions at that brunch table did something to me I wasn't braced for: she isn't telling Randi a story, she's *re-experiencing* it, and the prose makes us re-experience it with her. "He rubbed first. Before each one. And after, mostly, he kissed the spot." I felt that line in my spine. The whole middle stretch — her narrating the stripping, the heels, the covers under her cheek — reads as the hottest thing this book has done since the fitting chapter, and nobody's even touching anyone. It's shame transmuted into heat at a tablecloth, out loud, for an audience of one.

And that audience. Randi is doing what Randi does — the mimosa echo ("Two," she said, and never opened the wine list), the "walk me," the relish of the extraction — but the mixer story pushed her somewhere new for me. She narrates a nearly-sexual disaster and then the masturbation-in-a-stranger's-bedroom anecdote *directly at Vee*, watching her the whole time ("her clear eyes right on Vee's"). Whether she's working her or just telling the truth, I can't fully tell, and I don't want to resolve it. The page doesn't. "Dress up, panties to the side, knees wide, doing a private dance" — that image is the hinge of the chapter, because it arrives in Vee, *entire*, and she has to leave the table.

The bathroom scene is the most honest writing about arousal and shame I've read in this book. Her body "so far out ahead of her permission that it shamed her even here" — and then Randi's face arrives behind her eyes, and she *stops*. The near-orgasm denied not by the interruption of the world but by an internal censor wearing the face of the person she wants. That curdling of the bright wanting into "something low and ashamed and obedient" hit me hard, and it also unsettled me — it's the most explicitly Vee has connected Randi's looking with control. The phrase "could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was" is the chapter's whole thesis in one line.

The two ambushes afterward. "Sticky fingers?" is nearly cruel — I gasped — and the pivot to the pastries is either mercy or gaslight, and the text refuses to say. "She could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one" — perfect, and I stayed just as unresolved. Then the goodbye: "You're my best friend in the whole world" lands like a warm knife. The floor goes out from under her "kindly this time." And the last image — the glaze on her lip that she never tasted, sweetness arriving secondhand from Randi's mouth — that's a kiss she didn't consent to and didn't refuse. I closed the chapter with my pulse up.

Only friction: the ending glaze-transfer felt a touch *authored* — one image too clever for its own good. But it earned it. Title makes sense now: one bite of the pastry, one bite of everything — Vee fed by hand, always a step removed from wanting.
