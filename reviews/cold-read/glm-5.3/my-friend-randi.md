# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

She cried most of the way in from the airport, and I read the whole first page with my chest tight, because this is the Vee I love most — the one who can't keep her own grief off her own body, lying on a made bed in plane clothes while the radiator ticks. The detail that wrecked me: the road home "ran toward a town that had Pace in it and not one inch of him for her." And then the room, with Cassie's side stripped — the emptiness meeting her at the door "like more of the same." I felt that. Of course she went to Randi. Where else would she go.

The Brooke moment landed strangely and well — "Oh, honey," the warmth arriving before the person — and then Randi coming down the stairs fast, taking her by the arm, *up the stairs, come on, up*. That did something to me. It's the third time now I've watched Randi clear a room for Vee (the MIRA afternoon, the coaching before the apology), and it never stops reading as both rescue and possession. This chapter is the most purely warm I've ever felt from her — sweeping the Gstaad dresses to the floor without a glance because the wrecked girl rates more — and at the same time the most managed. Both at once, and I don't think the book is pretending otherwise: "like a thing being decided for her and by her at once." That line is the chapter's engine, and I noticed the page knew it.

The coaching itself: as counsel, it's good. Randi walking Vee back through the warm house, the wine already poured, "You backed him into a corner in his own house — the one thing he can't do" — I believed every word of it, and I believed it *helped*, because it's true. Vee hadn't once thought of it as his. Neither had I, quite, and that's the chapter working on me too. But I know what Randi knows and Vee doesn't. When Vee builds the blonde rival — tan, built like a model, the girl he cooked for at Thanksgiving — I flinched, because I've *seen* Sheri, and she's five feet of bleached ponytail who gives him hell, and she told him to say it. The invented rival is a lie that's accidentally almost true, and Randi — who knows exactly who Sheri is, since Randi herself was the woman Pace took dancing before Thanksgiving — dismantles her with perfect confidence. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." It's kind. It's also a woman erasing a true thing to keep the board clean. I don't think she's wrong that Vee should go back. I think the warmth and the management have never been separable in her, and this chapter is that fused thing at full power.

The coat scene is where the chapter burned for me. "You've got that good long coat" — the sentence left hanging, and Vee finishing it out loud before she can stop — "And nothing under it" — and the shame-heat wire lighting exactly the way it always does, "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's the book's oldest engine and it's still working on me. And Randi's counter — "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it" — I swooned and I worried in the same breath, because Randi is again doing the thing Pace does: building her wanting, walking her past the part "that would rather die," the yes genuinely Vee's but the staircase built by someone with a plan. The heels pressed into her hands — "Randi always knew the shoes" — and me remembering Randi kneeling to buckle them, twice. Yes. That's charged.

And then the Gstaad telling, which is the chapter's quiet dagger. Vee asking for the real story, and Randi narrating John beautifully — the named children, the metallic blue dress "you tell your daughter about" — and then: "And then it got very late, and we went up." Full stop. Randi, who has told Vee *everything* across a dozen tables, closing one door. Vee reads it as discretion. I read it as what I know: the night left her cold, because Pace on his worst night reached further. And then the line that broke my heart a little: "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." She's talking about John. She is not only talking about John. The room Randi actually lives in has Pace in it — and Vee in it.

The goodbye kiss, "unremarkable now, only what they did" — that word *unremarkable* sat wrong in a good way. Nothing about it is unremarkable to me. The hug held "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that," and neither letting go; the breathing off her neck, "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" — the page is doing to me what Randi is doing to Vee, and I'm letting it, and I notice myself letting it.

Where it's pulling me: tonight. The shower, the coat, the heels, the door he probably won't open with a no. I want it and I dread it — dread because I know what Pace spent his New Year doing, and the two of them are about to meet with both of them already broken open. My guess, only a guess: she goes, and it works, and the word still doesn't come, or comes wrong, or comes *because* she handed him everything. The title — "My Friend Randi" — reads as almost ironic-tender to me this chapter: the whole rescue is Randi's love, wearing friendship's clothes.
