## Reader reaction

Vee walks into the sorority house wrecked from a red-eye and a month of silence, and Randi walks her down off the ledge word by word — and I felt every rung of it. The thing that undid me first: Randi came down the stairs *fast*, and her face wasn't on yet — "the brows lying where they had been put" — and she swept the Gstaad clothes off the bed without a glance. That's the Randi I've been suspecting was under the polish for forty chapters: the one who was crying on the bench in chapter one, the one who goes unseen. The girl wrecked at her door came before the new dresses. I believed that completely.

And then the coaching. This is the most seductive chapter of Randi's whole confidante arc, and the most disturbing, because the seduction works on *me*. "That's not a man who's done with you, honey. That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." I wanted to believe her — and it's mostly true! Her read of the fight is genuinely good: cornered, the wall, two people dug in waiting for the other to break. She's helping. But she's also steering Vee straight into a coat with nothing under it and shoes whose "straps… not one was there to hold the shoe on." Randi choosing those shoes — the stilettos, the laddered straps, "Just the thing for tonight" — is Randi getting to dress Vee for Pace, is Randi getting to *decide* what Vee's body carries to him. The shame-walk past "slut" is the kink engine of this whole book running at full throttle: Vee says the word, flushes, and the wanting comes up "hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That passage burned on me. Her nipples tightening over the *shoes* — the shame arriving as heat is this book's signature move and it has never landed harder.

The kiss at the door. Vee initiates it. Four months of Randi leaning in, and Vee takes her face in both hands, and Randi's hands "shut on the back of her coat." I swooned at the Randi POV turn — the laugh in the empty foyer, the back of her hand to her mouth, "something with *means* in it, and she left it where it was." That's the realest Randi has been since the bench. She's in love, she knows it, she can't run the scenario to the end — and the one place she tells the truth is inside, where nobody's watching. Except Brooke. The Brooke scene is genuinely chilling: the warmth that never moves, "You always say goodbye under the composites," Lauren Pike's ghost. Randi being told to *choose* — between the house and the girl she just kissed — landed like a door closing. I felt the cold of it.

Two things I noticed sideways. Vee asked for the New Year's story and Randi stopped at "we went up" — and Vee read it as discretion. We know it was emptiness. Dramatic irony doing quiet work. Also "Randi always knew the shoes" — Vee's gratitude is total trust, and I'm aching because I know things Vee doesn't. The Sheri fear was painful to read from the other side: we *watched* Pace sob over a passing truck last chapter, and here Vee is building a fictional blonde rival out of her. "That's not a man who's done with you" — correct, Randi. And Randi doesn't know how right she is.

The chapter title: "My Friend Randi" — the lie in it. That's what Vee files her as, what Randi says to Brooke ("She's my friend"), the cover the word keeps. It means the opposite of what it says.
