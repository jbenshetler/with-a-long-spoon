## Reader reaction

The deadbolt, again — the book opens this chapter on the same sound "The Bench" opened with, and I felt the rhyme before I'd read three lines. But everything else has flipped. Last time she came in and there was the whole little ritual, the hello kiss, the apple, the economy of two people not performing. Tonight there's "no hello in it." She was holding it the whole way over. Whatever happened in that frozen yogurt shop — whatever grew between the last chapter and this one while the book wasn't looking — it has been working in Randi all week, and she walked through his door already burning.

And what she wants isn't his hands first. She wants the *story*. "Tell me." Three times, escalating, the last one hot against his ear while she puts his hands where she wants them — and this is the chapter's whole engine for me: Randi has dressed the wanting up as wanting *him* telling her about *Vee*, and she's using her body to get the telling, and both of them know the telling isn't really what she's after either. She's after the girl. She sat on him and asked "Did she eat all of it?" and I heard that question double — the froyo, sure, and then everything "The Bench" taught me about Randi and appetite, the girl who portioned herself to almost nothing, who ate thin slices only, whose hunger Pace found and answered. Her first question about Vee is whether the girl *takes*. That landed in me hard. That's not idle curiosity; that's a woman asking whether the thing she wants is even possible — can a person just build a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop? Is that allowed? Is it survivable? The froyo chapter read sweet; through Randi's mouth it reads like an interrogation of her own entire life.

The heat here worked on me more than "The Bench" did, honestly, because it's all displaced. Nothing is happening — she's straddling him on a couch, kissing him with wine, no bench, no undressing — and it's the hottest chapter so far, because every kiss is a delivery mechanism for the tannin, and the tannin is Vee. The title! He poured one glass, not two. "Everything he was going to have of it he was going to have like this" — through her mouth, at one remove, the way he's having Vee: through Randi. Tannin is the thing in red wine that catches, that dries your mouth, that the wine needs to keep — and Randi's making herself the glass it comes in. I don't think she knows that's what she's doing. Or she half-knows. "I already do," she says, easy and bright, and then finishes the glass in one swallow like she's killing the topic and takes his face in both hands. That move — the brightness deployed over the held thing — is her signature now, and each time I watch it, it costs her more on the page. His read of her: "a want too big for her face, and keeping the face over it anyway." That's the whole person in one line.

Pace: I keep liking him and keeping one eye open. The reassuring things are here — he withholds Vee's "embarrassing" confession deliberately, *it was hers*, which is the Daphne lesson still holding; he didn't drink so he could be fully present. But there's the other thread I can't unsee: he engineered all of this. He pointed, she picked, he sent her over, and now he sits back and receives Randi's hunger as a report from the field — "he had known she would come; he had known since about the second block of the walk home." He's very gentle and very much running a design. I don't distrust his tenderness; I distrust his certainty. He thought he knew what the bench night was, too, and she locked the bathroom door.

And Vee — offstage entirely, and huge. She "built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop," she's "easy to listen to," she made him laugh — wait, no, "I made her laugh," the correction is its own little self-portrait — and the withheld true thing ("the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo") sits under the whole chapter like the hum under Randi's ribs. Pace is keeping her secret. Randi is keeping her wanting. Two people on a couch, everything unsaid, wine going down.

What I want next: Vee's side of the week. Did she wait by the phone, did she tell Cassie, did she notice anything? And I want the collision — Randi in the same room as Vee, now that I've seen how much of Vee Randi already wants. My guess, only a guess: the three of them in one room soon, and Randi's face doing more work than it can hold.
