**Felt read:**

God, this one. The whole chapter is one held breath, and I felt it in my own chest the whole way through. Randi arriving and kissing him "as if she had been holding it the whole way over" — that's not the girl from the kitchen in "The Bench," the one who warmed up by increments. Something has changed in her between then and now, and the change isn't calm. She's hungry. And what she's hungry for is *the story*.

What landed hardest on me is how completely the heat here is routed through wanting-to-hear. She straddles him, puts his hands on her, and it's all in service of two words: *tell me.* The wine is the perfect vehicle — he pours one glass, doesn't drink it, takes it only secondhand from her mouth. "Everything he was going to have of it he was going to have like this." That line did something to me. It's the tannin of the title: the dry, dark, catching thing, taken only through her. The chapter runs entirely on her need, and his willingness to feed it — the temperature is hers, and it's a 3.

But the beat that got under my skin most isn't the sex-adjacent heat at all. It's the quiet after "She's easy to listen to." Her thighs don't tighten — they *set*. A smile "put on a beat after it was called for." That's the girl from the sorority common room and the bathroom mirror, holding a want too big for her face, and Pace *reads it and is moved by it*. The chapter ends with her taking him to bed (I assume — she holds out her hand) and I don't actually know whether she's going to that bed to have him or to keep him talking, and the page rightly leaves that ajar.

The two of them are in different rooms, though, and the book keeps letting me feel it.
