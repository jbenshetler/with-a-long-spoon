## Reader reaction

The first section is the hottest thing this book has done, and almost nothing in it is sex. It's Randi alone in the car, top down, rehearsing Vee for Pace's consumption — and the heat comes from how much *she* is the one consuming. The freckles "going fewer and paler down into the shadow between her breasts until there were none" — she's been studying the girl in the lecture hall, cataloguing her, and now she's composing the striptease in her own head, with herself cast as the one who undresses Vee *for him*. "Held up in Randi's two hands and turned to him. *Here.*" That image undid me a little — not because of him, but because of the nakedness of *her* wanting wearing his wanting as its costume. This is the same move from the pointing game — she made her hunger into "a girlfriend offering her man a gift" — but it's gotten less safe. The fiction is thinning. "By the time the gravel came up under her tires she was wet" is the least performed sentence in the whole chapter.

And then the scene on the couch. The wine is doing so much work I want to name it: she drinks, and kisses the wine into his mouth, and he never pours himself a glass — "Everything he was going to have of it he was going to have like this." That line went through me. He's feeding her appetite *through* her appetite; she thinks she's extracting his evening from him, and the whole interrogation is her being fed. The "Tell me" — three times, the last one arriving "hot against his ear" as a single italic word — is her taking the top role, the director role, the one the bench briefly broke her out of and the bathroom put back behind glass. She's rebuilt, and this is the rebuilt version: not less wanting. More wanting, better armored.

What landed hardest, and hardest to watch, was the moment it slipped. "She's easy to listen to." Her thighs *set* instead of tightening. "The flat of her hands stopped in his hair." The whole chapter's undercurrent is right there: Randi can be erotically thrilled by Vee as a body, a picture, a fantasy staged for Pace — but Vee being *listened to*, being known in the way Pace knows people, that's the thing her face can't hold. "A want too big for her face, and keeping the face over it anyway." I don't know yet whether what's under it is jealousy of Vee or terror of what Pace did to *her* on the bench — she's decided he can have "the face" and never the one behind it, and now here he is with a girl who doesn't seem to have a face/body split at all. I hold that open. The page held it open.

And he lied to her, gently: she asks "Did she make you laugh?" — testing whether Vee charmed *him* — and he says no, and then withholds the "nicest a person has been to me in a really long time" thing on the grounds that it's Vee's. He's managing Randi. From where I sit it reads protective, of both women — but I noticed my stomach tighten a hair, remembering he also "let the camouflage stand" on the walk. He curates what each of them gets to know. So far I trust the tenderness of it; I'm just no longer certain trust is the right frame for a man who stage-manages this smoothly.

The title, "Tannin" — the wine's dryness, the thing that catches at the inside of your lips. It's what's passed mouth to mouth, and it's also *her*: something dark and astringent under the sweetness, the bite that arrives after the swallow. The evening he "tells" is bright — froyo, a leaning cup, a walk — and everything it does to her is dark. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation* — that still reads as the pointing game, "I point. You pick," the whole book's machinery dressed as courtesy. Nothing given away; if anything I misread it at first as cozier than this. Next I expect — a guess only — Vee's phone lighting up again, and Pace beginning to build the second strand, and I dread it and want it in equal measure, because Vee's last chapter ended with her falling for him honestly, and nobody in this book tells anybody the whole truth except by accident.
