## Reader reaction

She came over *starving*, is what that opening is. I've watched Randi manage every doorway of Pace's house — the deadbolt, the composed hello, the letting-him-decide ritual — and tonight she throws all of it out before he's off the couch. That alone landed on me: four weeks of choreography, discarded. Whatever she's been carrying since the pointing game, she carried it straight here and put her mouth on it.

And what she wants is *the story*. "Tell me." Twice, then the third time hot in his ear — she's not asking to be told about frozen yogurt. The whole scene is Randi on his lap, wine in her mouth, passing it to him kiss by kiss, working his hands up under her top herself — and all of it is appetite aimed at a girl who isn't in the room. The wine struck me as the perfect vehicle for it: she drinks it, he doesn't, everything he gets of it he gets *from her mouth*. That's the chapter's whole economy in one image — Randi as the conduit, Pace consuming Vee only through what Randi chooses to pass along. And the tannin itself — the dry catch of it, the title — something sweet-adjacent that dries you out, darkens the mouth. The chapter is sweet on the surface (froyo recap, playful interrogation) with that drying underneath.

Where it peaked for me: "She built a cup, like nobody had ever told her when to stop." Because I know what that sentence means. I watched Vee build that cup in the last chapter, felt the absence of her mother's voice, watched her let it get away from her and *not care*. And Randi — who slices apples thin and eats exactly as much as she's decided to — feels the sentence in her thighs before her face catches up. The body-first reaction is the pattern of this book all over: her body heard "a girl who doesn't stop herself" and answered before she could arrange anything. That's the hottest thing in the chapter, honestly — not the wine-kissing, which is lovely but is also Randi *performing*, running a seduction she's good at. The thighs setting tight around him — that's involuntary. That's the real want, the one from the quad, bigger now, "and he had done nothing to grow it but leave it alone."

Then the turn: "Did she make you laugh?" / "No. I made her laugh. She's easy to listen to." And Randi goes quiet — not the turned-on quiet, the *level* quiet. "Her thighs did not tighten now; they set." I felt my own small chill there. Because Pace is describing Vee to Randi in terms that flatter Vee and diminish nothing — and Randi is sitting on a man who is telling her, gently, that the girl she wants is also a girl he *likes*. The menage plan has her giving him a gift; suddenly the gift has an interior. Her "I already do," easy and bright, followed by draining the glass in one swallow — she's back on the wheel, managing, and I don't fully trust the brightness, the same way I didn't trust the smile she arranged in his bathroom mirror.

And the last line: "He had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else." It's him all over — the man who wouldn't take his own coffee in the shop, who gave Randi the whole night without taking anything for himself. But it also reads as appetite from the other side: he's feeding on Randi's wanting, on her relaying Vee to him, and that's the only wine he needs. There's something both tender and slightly unnerving about how much he *enjoys watching her want*.

The thing he withheld — Vee's "nicest a person has been to me in a really long time" — he keeps because "It was hers." I melted at that a little. This man and his doors. He knows what's private even when holding it back costs him a good line.
