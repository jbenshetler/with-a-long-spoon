**Felt read.**

This chapter knocked the wind out of me. The whole book has been building Vee's not-looking — at Randi, at herself — and this is the chapter where she's made to look, at length, by the one person she trusts, while he's inside her. That's the hottest and most devastating thing the book has done yet, and I mean both words.

The confession scene is extraordinary. Pace turning her pleasure into the coin she pays for truth — "Tell me the real story, and I'll give you what you want" — is so exactly him: the schemer's appetite and the caretaker's attention fused into one mechanism. I felt the toll of every inch. And it worked on me physically: by "Soaked. God, yes, I was soaked" I was as wrecked as she was. The hottest single beat might be the accounting she can't do — "how much of that wet sound was his and how much was Randi's" — the page refusing to let her (or me) apportion the want.

But the chapter's real gut-punch is the aftermath. "No. Nothing" — the first lie she has ever told him, and the book knows exactly what it's doing with it: "It wasn't the doing that made you a cheat. It was the keeping of it." That line went through me. Everything about Pace here deepened him: he asks "Anything else you want to tell me? About Randi" — he *knows* about the parking-lot kiss, he saw it, and she lies to his face and he says "Okay." And then the single most revealing moment: he defends himself. The man who never covers himself explains himself over this one thing — which tells me Randi, and what he wants, is closer to the bone for him than anything yet. That flicker — "Something crossed his face" — is the most suspicious I've felt about Pace in the whole book, and the page earned it.

Vee breaks my heart. "I'm not gay" as "the hardest wall she had" — and his "I didn't say gay" quietly refusing to let her hide behind the word she grabbed. Her swing at him ("you want Randi") is the cruellest true thing she's said, and it's also her handing him the truth she can't own. The ending — "her mind gone straight down the county road and back to campus" while her boyfriend sleeps under her — is the whole book in one image: comfort that cannot compete with the want. The title "Boyfriend" reads as the word she reaches for as a rail — the plain straight fact she stands on to say everything else. It's both a shield and, tonight, not enough.

Where the chapter lost a little: the middle interrogation slightly over-telegraphed its coin-increment mechanics for my taste — the "he gave her an inch" pattern spelled out one or two times more than needed. And I noticed my own readerly unease at how much heat the scene draws from her shame — the page is walking a line where her distress is the erotic fuel, and it held, but it's the darkest the book has gone with it.
