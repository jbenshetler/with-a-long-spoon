## Reader reaction

The dress finally got its walk. That's what this chapter is, and it landed on me harder than I expected — the whole book has been building to this: the shy girl, the mother's voice, the slit "to the hip," and then a long deliberate step. "She took a breath, and made the second step long" is the best sentence in the chapter, maybe the best in the book so far. All that shame — *floozie*, the cardigan, the hand held low over the slit — and she answers it not by hiding but by giving the room her leg "on purpose." And the text is honest about the machinery of it: "her body agreeing… coming up to meet a thing she'd decided a half-second before her head was sure of it." Heat and shame and pride running on the same wire, exactly as she's been written all along.

Vee moved, genuinely moved, for me. What got me was the passage about her parents — "they had gone around it twice before her mother said they'd be right there on the phone, honey, in the bright voice she kept for the things she couldn't give her" — and then the sentence that arrives on its own: "nothing at all under the silk, and nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of." That's the thesis of her whole arc said quietly, and it broke my heart a little. The ceremony she built the fantasy around (the dramatic entrance) doesn't happen — "No one is going to turn on their own," she notes, and then she *makes* them turn. The room's murmur felt earned.

Cassie — I love her more every chapter. "You look—" and not finishing it, "which from Cassie was a standing ovation." That's exactly the Cassie I've been watching since "Goodbye, Vee." And her being the one holding the phone, the one who documents the walk — the loyal friend who sees the person and not the glamour. The contrast with Randi is sharp: Randi taught her the walk, the shoes, the posture, and Randi isn't there (of all weekends — the one thing on the calendar she couldn't move; I don't fully buy that the president of the social Greek universe couldn't contrive it, and part of me wonders if the author cleared Randi out of the room on purpose). Cassie, who never dressed her, is.

Pace absent in body, present in silk — and the photo she sends him, uncropped, "That part was hers." The heat of the chapter isn't sex, but it's heat: her being looked at, on purpose, half-naked under formal silk in front of a microphone, and keeping her face level. The photo text is a small private erotic act — she knows the dress will do for him what it does, and she knows what he can't see. That's the hottest thing here and it's fully clothed.

Suspicion: nobody feels off. Pace's absence is accounted for honestly (Berkeley, he was "sorry in the quiet way"), and the chapter doesn't strain to make anyone a villain. The one needle of unease I allow myself: "she had wanted to wear the dress… so to wear the dress as it was built to be worn was to wear nothing under it." Pace built it that way. I don't think that's accidental on the book's part — the dress as Pace's desire rendered onto her body — but the chapter lets her own the choice, and that keeps it warm rather than creepy.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the parents' absence is milked a touch (a few too many restatements of the same loneliness), and "streetlight" — Cassie's nickname for her, presumably a movie-star joke — threw me for a second; I let the page tell me what it meant. Title: the book's title, *A Warm Reception*, promised something like this; the chapter title "The Induction" is literal but also her induction into herself. Book two has been the book of her becoming.

What next: I want Pace's answer to the photo, and I dread/want Vee's two worlds colliding — the open question since the pointing game. Everything in me says Randi's absence here is the book clearing the stage before it drops something.
