## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

What a breathe-out chapter. After the intensity of the doorstep, the naked kitchen morning, the cunnilingus scene with its small crisis, this book sets down all its weight and just… lets Vee have friends. I didn't know how much I needed that until it arrived. "Across" is a breakfast chapter — mimosas, waffles, Kayla being Kayla — and it's the first time in a long while I've watched Vee sit inside her life without any shame narration running underneath. That landed warm on me.

Kayla is still pure joy — "This is the last morning of my life and it's going to be peanut butter crackers" made me laugh out loud. And Meg: "The cord was stupid." She's the friend who knows how to make it right "in their language" and doesn't editorialize, and her maple-bacon-and-Matrices story is the perfect sideways blessing on what Vee actually did (and Vee knows it, and the book lets the parallel stand unspoken). I love that neither of them knows what the "language" Vee used actually was. The flash on the porch gets told as comedy — "In front of *God*" — and Vee "never had to say one word about the drive." The book is being honest about how much Vee curates: what she brings to this table is true, and it's a cropped version. She cropped the induction photo for her mother and she crops herself here, and I don't fault her for it; that's just what she does now. But I notice it.

What moved my feelings: Vee. She's been so interior all book — shame, wanting, the unsayable name for Pace — and here she's just *competent at friendship*. She reads the right side of the menu "out of old habit" and then refuses the arithmetic. She invites them somewhere expensive on Christmas money and doesn't flinch. The girl who was rationing herself in September is buying brunch, and the book marks it. And when the check comes, the refusal of being covered — "nobody letting anybody cover anybody" — is the healthiest five words this book has ever printed. It quietly refuses Pace's whole economy of provision. I felt that as a deliberate chord: Vee is learning to be the one who provides, in her own smaller currency.

Pace isn't here, but he's the chapter's offstage gravity: "He's handling it." The way Vee says it — "and heard how much she liked saying it" — I liked it too. She's trusting him with something. And Wednesday's dinner terrifies me a little. This is the first time Randi and Pace will be in the same room with Vee *as Vee's people* — the two of them who selected her, performing strangers at one table with Cassie (who "senses something wrong about Randi") and Kayla (who leaks everything) and Sheri's friend-the-diner-world and Theo. The book has been building the dinner since "Back": "Bring Cassie, too. And Meg. Bring Theo." Everything I know that the table doesn't is going to be in that room. That's not suspicion — the text earned the dread honestly — it's the geometry of the story. I want it and I dread it.

Heat-wise this is a zero-heat chapter and that's correct; the heat is all in Kayla's retroactive horror about the socks. Romance, though — there's real romance in Vee's face when she reports the reconciliation: "We're good. We're better than we were." That's the strongest romantic beat, and it happens over waffles, secondhand.

Friction, small: the chapter is a touch short and a touch slight after the run of heavy chapters — a palate cleanser, and it earns its length, but the Meg-Danny period story is the only place my attention flickered. And "Across" as a title: I first read it as the table — friends across from her, everyone across from everyone. But it's also Randi across that Wednesday table, and Pace, and the distance between what Vee tells and what happened. The book's title, *A Place at the Table* (Book Three of *With a Long Spoon*), is suddenly not a metaphor anymore — Pace is literally building a table, and the tagline-adjacent promise of "everybody at it" is Wednesday. That's where this is pointing.

**Structured block**

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Kayla, Meg. Mentioned only: Pace, Cassie, Randi, Theo, Danny, Sheri (Kayla's "a man who owns a *house*" brushes the wealth, not her).

**Heat:** 0 — breakfast comedy; the only erotic content is the retold porch story played for laughs.

**Romance:** 1 — "We're good. We're better than we were" carries warm secondhand tenderness; nothing on-page between lovers.

**Motifs & images:** Food as care — first sighting of Vee as the *provider* of a meal rather than the receiver of one (repeat/inversion of Pace's feeding motif); the cropped story (repeat — like the cropped induction photo, she curates what her audience sees); "their language" — Meg's bacon-and-Matrix apology reframing Vee's coat (first); splitting the check three ways (first — refusal of provision); shoes and coat retold as comedy (repeat of ch41 doorway, from Kayla's side).

**Symbolism:** The split check reads as the chapter's quiet thesis: friendship as an economy where nobody carries anybody — the anti-Pace transaction, and maybe a small rebellion against being kept.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens — confident, generous, socially commanding in a way we've never seen; Kayla consistent chorus; Meg deepens slightly with the Danny story. Pace grows in absentia ("He's handling it").

**Pace — within the chapter:** Short, quick, all in one scene; no drag. The Kayla bits run a beat long but that's her function.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate release valve after three heavy chapters; momentum holds because Wednesday is now two days off and loaded. I expect (guess from here) the dinner chapter next, and I both want it and fear it.
