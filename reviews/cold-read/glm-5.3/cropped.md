## Reader reaction

The title gave me a meaning I didn't expect. Going in, I assumed "Cropped" was about the photograph — Vee's mother has the induction picture, "the version they have stops at the waist," and that's a lovely, sly image: her parents literally hold a cropped version of her life. But by the end the title has turned inward. Vee crops *herself* — for her parents, for her father's frame of what a boyfriend is, for Cassie, even for Pace. She builds the sentence about crackers and a meal, checks it carefully so it's "not *I love you* — not in any shape he could take it for," and sends the cropped version of her love across the quilt. And he crops his answer right back. *I love how good we are together.* That's the whole chapter: everyone handing each other the waist-up version.

The cafeteria scene with Cassie quietly gutted me. "So what is he — not to your dad. To you," and Vee's answer is "Both. Probably both." Cassie has been the flat, steady one all book, and here her precision turns tender: she doesn't push, and Vee is "grateful. She was also, a little, not." That "not" is Vee in miniature — she's starving for someone to push, to make her say it, and Cassie's kindness leaves her alone with the unsayable. The parents material is rich: the father pricing the dress, the mother's collarbone touch, "weird is worse than wrong," the mother's nurse face watching her eat. Vee is lying to everyone she loves by cropping, and she knows it, and it's the same shame-adjacent honesty she's been negotiating all fall.

Then the porch. I felt her whole engineered confession — weeks of assembly, memorized like a poem — and my stomach dropped with his answer. "I love how good we are together" is the most devastating non-answer in the book so far. It's warm. It's plausible. He even adds "I never had this either," which is *more* than she asked for on paper — and it's still not the sentence. The page is scrupulously fair to him: two breaths, the aborted hand movements, the long kiss on her hair. He heard her. He chose this. Or maybe it went past him. The text refuses to decide, and I refuse to decide with it.

The laundry coda is where it landed hardest. "Either end of it hurt. Maybe he understood and chose to say it like that. Maybe it had gone past him entirely." The pile of small flickers she won't look at — that's a place we readers have been filling all fall, and now she's admitted it exists. She keeps the shape of the not-saying; "the cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech." I ached for her. This is the first chapter where the warmth costs her something she names.

Heat is low by design; this is the chapter where the wanting turns into grief, and it works. My trust in Pace is newly complicated — not broken, the page doesn't earn a verdict against him — but the "wait" behind her breastbone is now mine too. The open question: does he *have* an "I love you" in him, or is the withholding structural? And I dread Vee learning the biggest crop of all — Randi.
