## Reader reaction

**Heat:** 2 — Vee's attraction to Pace is building, and the shower-bench imagining of his hands gives it a sharp charge, but this is a quiet chapter; the chapter's real charge is in the water, not the wanting.

**Romance:** 1 — no romance present on the page; but the "Don't answer tonight" story and the thread she can't answer is courtship in its earliest, most oblique form.

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Cassie. Pace appears only in Vee's memory (dinner invitation, froyo walk) and via his one unanswered text. Randi does not appear and is not mentioned by name in this chapter.

**Motifs & images:** Water and holding versus release (first sighting, chapter-level); drowning as the shape of Pace ("*that* kind of swimmer — the quiet water over the top of him"); the rolled towel and the body as "problem" versus the water's "uncomplicated belonging"; hands and what he does with them besides math ("first vs. repeat: at the coffee shop, then in the shower"); heat/heat of shower; chin-level, game face, the entrance walk (first sighting, but it rhymes with how Vee walks into Chi Latte with her face put on); "She put her face on" — new here but the face/mask motif clearly echoes the book's larger pattern.

**Symbolism:** The pool is doing explicit symbolic work: the deep end as the one place Vee is "nobody's to look at," where "nothing could be done to her body and no one was doing it." The synchronized-swimming scull — "the little figure-eight that keeps you down and turned and yours" — reads as an emblem of self-containment. Cassie standing at her fixed depth, "arms spread along the surface like a woman leaning on a fence," feels like a portrait of a particular kind of steady, bounded love — she goes exactly as far as she goes, no farther, and says so plainly. The title "Water Wings" I read as ironic-tender: floaties, the aid for children who can't yet swim — Cassie's "You won't need floaties with *those*!" is the bleacher's eye, the idea that Vee's body is all the buoyancy she needs — versus the truth the chapter shows, that Vee's real safety in the water comes from skill, and her real danger isn't in the water at all.

**Characterization:** Cassie deepens sharply, and it's the chapter's best work. Her refusal to shout at the hecklers, her "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman," her standing at ribs-deep like a person — this is the character who said "Goodbye, Vee" in the last chapter, and the "Goodbye" retroactively reads even more like Cassie knowing something. Vee is also consistent and deepening: her split between the "meant to arrive" walk and the "grieved a little" underwater is the clearest statement yet of the body/mask division Randi embodies from the other direction. Pace appears only in memory and is more interesting for it: "Don't answer tonight" is either very confident or very controlling, and the chapter wisely lets Vee's own framing — "Who does that?" — carry the ambiguity. Her thumb hesitating at the keys is the whole chapter's answer.

**Friction:** Minimal. The bleacher heckle is a little broad as a device — "Legs for *days*" arrives right when the chapter wants to show Vee's body being looked at without her consent, and it's the most "authored" moment. But the payoff — Cassie's deadpan "Tossers" — earns it.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Well-proportioned. The pool scene is the longest movement and earns it; the transition to shower/bench is a small hard cut that works (bench, curled hair, curled question). The final beat with the unanswered text is a perfect close — the red light as a held breath, no resolution.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Holding steady and building laterally. The last three chapters have been a steady drum — encounter, approach, first date — each advancing Pace-and-Vee one notch while the Pace/Randi current keeps running underneath. This one deliberately steps off that line for a whole chapter with Cassie, and it's the first time the book spends real, unhurried time inside Vee's interiority without the frame of being desired. I trust the book more after this chapter, not less.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** The book slowed its foot off the Vee-grooming gas and spent a chapter in the water, and I'm the better for it. After three chapters of Pace's calculating omniscience, watching him only through Vee's memory — where he is genuinely more likable — and watching Cassie just be a friend, has me re-invested in Vee as a person and not just a target. That said, the Pace/Randi material is still the book's most dangerous, and the questions it raised about Pace's carefulness — his withholding, the satisfaction he takes in Vee's not-yet-knowing — remain fully live. Whatever happens next, the plate is set for Saturday.

**Suspicion:** Cassie is the one character I trust without qualification. Pace, as filtered through Vee's memory here, is likable — "Don't answer tonight" is the single most interesting thing he's done, and Vee's own "Who does that?" is the right reaction, and the book doesn't resolve whether it's respect or control. Randi, offstage, is not trusted by me at all — not because I dislike her, but because I know what she and Pace have been building, and Vee does not.

**Erotic charge:** Present, low, and honestly earned — Vee thinking about his hands "which was really not the place" is the only explicit flicker, and it lands because the chapter doesn't push it. Cassie's splash is the most physically affectionate moment, and it isn't erotic; it's warmth, and the heat is better for being that clean.

**What the title means to me now:** "Water Wings" — the floaties jab lands as the bleachers' insult, and the title takes the insult and reclaims it. Vee does not need floaties; she has "swum in six inches of water with her mother's voice in her ears." The deep end is where she's safe. The title makes me nervous in the best way, because the book keeps showing us people who can swim just fine in their own element — Vee in the water, Cassie in the shallows — and keeps arranging for them to be asked to swim in Pace's water instead. The Pace/Randi thread has been about appetite and surrender; this chapter quietly asserts Vee has her own pool, her own capacity for depth, and it's not Pace's. If anyone's the "water wings" here, it might be Pace — the thing you hold onto to stay up in water that's over your head, taken off once you're sure it's safe.

**What I want / expect / dread next:** Saturday dinner. I want to see Vee in the evening-light with Pace again, and I am dreading what I suspect is Randi's vantage on that same dinner. Guess from here: the Saturday dinner happens, and the book lets us see it through eyes that know more than Vee does — most likely Randi's, possibly Pace's. And the chapter title "Water Wings" makes me worried in a specific way: in the water, Vee is the one person we've seen who's fully competent, fully at home, the one place nothing can be done *to* her body. Pace's whole pattern, so far, is noticing what a person is under their own control and getting them to hand it over. I don't want the book to take Vee into water she knows how to swim in and make her drown there, and I don't want her to become Randi, and I'm bracing for both.
