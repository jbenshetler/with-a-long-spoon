## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

This chapter gave me a warmth that keeps doing exactly what this book does at its best: turning Vee's shame into heat by making her choose the exposure herself. "Made-Up" is a quiet, gorgeous hinge — after the last two chapters' plunge into the hardest material (the spanking scene that ended "shame wants a witness, and in his bed there was no such thing"), this is Vee taking what she learned and *performing* it. Not performing for him — offering. The Halloween conceit ("tonight they're costumes") lets her stage two reveals as a game she controls completely, and the whole power of the scene is that she does control it: she puts him in the recliner, she tells him "No" when he asks to help, she silences him with a flat hand when he starts up. It's the inverse of every early chapter where Pace engineered her choices; here she engineers his. And he *obeys* — and that obedience is the hottest thing in the chapter. "He didn't touch her. His hand came partway up and he put it away again, and the putting-away cost him something" — I felt that restraint as intensely as any sex scene so far.

My pull on Pace deepened again. "You missed the debut. This is the walk the room got" — she's giving him the induction he couldn't attend, and the line "the dress has crossed a stage for everyone but its maker" landed as genuinely moving. When he says the dress is "for the world" and the naked woman in the heels is "just for me," that's the most romantic thing he's said. The choice-sunrise-sunset line is a little pat, but his *behavior* carries it.

The costume two reveal burned. "The heels stayed on. That was all of it." The blush "going anyway" as "the entire costume" — that's the book's whole engine in one image: shame transformed into deliberate gift. And her closing "she did not cover one inch of it" is Vee at her furthest point from chapter one's cardigan.

My only unease lives with Randi: her hands buckled those heels on Vee, and Vee still doesn't know Randi is Pace's lover. Every chapter that passes without that disclosure makes the coming collapse heavier. But nothing in this chapter made me distrust Pace — his discipline here is the cleanest it's looked.

One friction: the sunrise/sunset answer is the first time Pace's plain-voice extravagance felt like a line rather than a truth. Small thumb on the scale, quickly forgiven.

The title "Made-Up" doubles beautifully — the makeup she applies, and the Halloween premise of being "somebody else on the one night a year nobody had to explain it." Vee, made up, made up. It also whisper-touches the lie Randi and Pace are living, without pointing at it.
