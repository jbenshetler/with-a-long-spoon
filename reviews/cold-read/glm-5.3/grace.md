**Heat:** 3 — sustained, dominating charge; the naming-her-body scene and the blindfolded masturbation are among the hottest chapters in the book.

**Romance:** 3 — the key, the box, the "it's whose this one is" speech; the clearest romantic declaration Pace has ever made, without ever saying the word.

**Cast present (in person):** Pace, Vee. No one else appears; no one is even mentioned by name — Randi is entirely absent from the chapter, which itself feels meaningful.

The flannel blindfold — the shirt she sleeps in when he's away, repurposed (repeat: the flannel motif from ch10 onward). Firelight and clear gray daylight — light as the agent of exposure (repeat: the sun-porch fitting, the induction walk). "Would you like an adventure?" (their established word). The plum lingerie (repeat: plum polish, plum sweater — plum is becoming Vee's color). Tikka masala (repeat: ch16's fra diavolo; Pace feeds her).

**Symbolism:** The key: a place made instead of a word spoken — exactly the emptied-drawer gesture at larger scale. The blindfold: she cannot see herself; his voice becomes her only mirror — the inversion of a lifetime of her own hostile mirror-gazing. The unnameable taste ("no word I'd trust for that") standing against his refusal to say the other unnameable word.

**Characterization:** Both deepen. Pace: his verbal lavishness here is new — the man of "words are cheap" spends a thousand of them on her body, which reads as the overflow of what he won't say about love. Vee: the "you" answer — her hand saying Randi's image and her mouth saying "You" — is the chapter's live wire; the text lets the lie stand and so do I.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The Saturday-night scene is long and earns every minute; the middle "naming" section risks catalog-sameness but the small-toe and back-of-knee digressions keep it alive. Nothing dragged.**Felt read:**

The first thing that hit me, before anything else: Randi is not in this chapter at all. Not mentioned, not named, not behind anyone's eyes — except once, and I'll get there. After three chapters of her everywhere (the trial, the brunch, the gesso), her total absence felt like held breath. And then she came back in the one place Vee couldn't stop her: "the first thing there, for one held breath, was a dark curtain of hair falling forward, and under it a mouth coming up to her slow." I read that and my stomach dropped. Vee turns from it "so fast it was almost not there at all" — the page does not let me say what she was thinking, only that she was asked what she was thinking and answered *you*, *you*, *you* to the man inside her while the image she turned away from was Randi's mouth. That is the book letting the tangle stay tangled. I'm not going to resolve it for the text.

The rest of the chapter is the warmest thing this book has done in sixty-plus chapters. The key in the handmade box — "You're more than a guest in my house. I've never given a key to anyone" — I genuinely got misty. This is Pace doing the thing I've watched him do all book: he cannot say the word, so he builds the word. The drawer, the dress, and now a brass key cut to his door carried up a mountain in a box he planed himself. And the text knows I know: "the old wish rising and this time meeting something solid under it to stand on... the word she wanted had only ever been air." That's the whole relationship in one paragraph. She keeps settling for objects because the word never comes. I ached for her and I felt the notch of the missing word exactly as she does.

The blindfold scene is the hottest thing in the book so far, and it's because of the voice, not the acts. Him narrating her body to her — the freckles, the weight of her breast, the "spill is the part I want most" — lands precisely on every shame Vee has carried since chapter 4 (*floozies*, *stripper*, the too-muchness). He names the exact things she hides and calls them the treasure. When he said "Your body heard itself talked about and wept for it" I had to put the book down. And then the cruelty-turned-gift of "Do you want relief?" / "Go ahead" — making her masturbate in front of him — is the most Pace thing imaginable: he's asking her to do the thing she's done alone all book (ch54!) in his presence, with his voice as the only mirror. It burned. The word *you* at the climax, broken down into "you, you, you" — she gets to mean Pace here and I believe she mostly does, but the page leaves the Randi flicker just behind it, unfixed.

Friction: the naming litany ran a touch long for me around the small-toe / ankle-knob stretch — a moment where the inventory felt like inventory. But it recovered. And I noticed and loved the small shadow: the oyster-pale silk in the box, "she had felt that silk against her skin somewhere before, and could not think where." I suspect — guess only — that's the silk from the dress project. The book is quietly wiring things.

**Titles:** "Grace" — I read it as the theological register the anthropology class has been building: grace as unearned favor. Vee is given what she never earned and could never ask for: being seen fully and not found wanting. It recolored the whole scene on second thought. The volume title, *A Place at the Table* (With a Long Spoon, Book Three), now reads less ironic than I once took it — this chapter literally gives Vee a key to a place.

**Next:** I dread and want the fallout of her "yes" — *tell me when you touch yourself thinking of me*. That's a leash, and a tender one. And Saturday's brunch is coming; Randi's sad eyes are waiting. Guess, clearly marked: the two women are on a collision, and Pace just built Vee a home.
