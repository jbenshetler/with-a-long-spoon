## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

There's a cruelty in this chapter that the book never announces, and it's all in the kitchen. Vee, standing there in his shirt with her wine, tells Pace about Randi — the collision outside the dining hall ("nearly knocked her over. It's a small world"), the brunches, the funny little accent, the goodbye kiss "on the mouth. And then she comes back and does it again." And I'm sitting there with everything I know that Vee doesn't: the pointing game, Randi's yes, the text messages, the brunch confessions being carried straight back the other way. Vee thinks she's handing him trivia about her new best friend. She's actually narrating the plot to one of its two authors, and he stands at the stove with a spatula and asks questions. "You like her." "You'd think she'd have her pick." I read those twice. Is that a man being sweetly curious about his girlfriend's friend, or a man listening to his own arrangement reported back to him as happy accident? The page doesn't tell me, and that's exactly what makes my skin prickle. The one moment the mask (if there is one) slips is when she describes the mouth-kiss and "his grin spread, and then it left, and under it was the look she knew from his bed, and that look was no joke." She reads it as jealousy-lite and kisses it away. I don't know what it was. But it was *something*, and Vee filing it under "don't be silly, not that kind of French" is her doing what she does — finding the safe shelf for a thing that has no safe shelf.

And God, the "froyo day" — "You remember Randi? From the froyo day." Of course he remembers. He paid for the cups.

My feelings moved on Vee more than anyone this chapter. The love is out now — not said to him, but let be true in her own chest, and that private naming is the most intimate thing the book has done with her. "She loved him, and he had never once said it to her... she found, tonight, that she could live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying." That line ached, because I don't fully believe her — I think she *will* go hungry for the saying eventually, and the whisper of "Peter" to his sleeping shape is already the hunger leaking. That he only gets his real name when he can't hear it. And then Cassie, flat and kind: "Do you love him." And Vee says *maybe*. That maybe did more damage to me than a yes would have. She's said it true in the dark to a sleeping man and can't say it awake to her best friend — because awake there's the speed of it, and somewhere under that, I suspect, the whole strangeness of how this started, the collision she "can't reconstruct." The book keeps her that honest.

Cassie I love more every time. "You're a public utility." "You don't come home school nights" — not a question, no accusation in it, just the count handed to Vee to do herself. She doesn't say a thing against the maybe, and doesn't say I know. That's the steadiness Vee keeps drifting from, and it lands as loss without the book raising its voice.

The counter scene is my favorite thing here. All her disorder flung across the one clean surface in a clean man's house — her whole self colonizing his order — and he looks at it, and says nothing, and puts his mouth back to her shoulder. "Not one word. She let her breath out... and carried only the smallest tail of it out of the room." I felt that tail. It's the same *did he mind, he never said* rhythm as the unsaid love — she's learning to live on inference with a man who withholds words as a matter of course, and the book is quietly asking whether that's peace or training. I genuinely don't know, and I like not knowing.

The pool brought me all the way back to ch7's Water Wings — same water, same chlorine, but she's alone in it now, bleachers folded, and the suit "had nowhere left to go on her and hadn't for years. She looked at the water instead of at that." That's the book's whole method in one sentence. The set as liturgy — "You were just already in it," like a hymn's second verse — and the questions wearing off stroke by stroke until only the shape is left. Then the four strokes where she can't tell "whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him." That frightened me a little, honestly, more than anything Randi does. Because I've been watching Pace be extraordinary for thirty chapters, and this is the first time the text lets the possibility in that some of it is Vee's own manufacture. Four strokes, and the wall comes. She doesn't let herself finish the thought. I noticed that she didn't.

Heat is quieter here than ch29–30 — but it's present: the porch spin, "even with her clawing at him, he'd been slow," the stove kiss where she finds him hard and says "After you feed me" (her boldness keeps growing and it delights me), the "after" on the couch. It's the heat of settled wanting, not peak. Where it peaks for me is actually the mirrored bathroom embrace, arms crossing under hers, his eyes on the counter-spread of her. That's a 2, verging warm.

The title, "Not in Your Bed," I can't fully place. The phrase itself never appears in the chapter. My guess from here: it's about Randi — where she's touching Vee now is everywhere *outside* Pace's bed, in brunches and shoe fittings and goodbye kisses Vee calls French. Or it's about Vee's love, which lives everywhere but in speech. It sits oblique for me; I'm holding it open.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** holding, after the run of high-heat chapters — a breath chapter, deliberately, and it earns it; the interior work (Vee's love, the maybe, the four strokes) is the actual event. I want the whisper to reach him awake; I dread Randi learning Vee said *maybe* instead of yes; and I dread more, now, that the built-man question gets finished.
