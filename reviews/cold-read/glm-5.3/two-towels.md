## Reader reaction

The kiss at the door landed on me hard — and then the *stopping* landed harder. She's been waiting since the mountain, since the cop's lights "swinging up to end the night before she got the chance at it," and finally takes it, and it's everything — and then he's the one who draws back. "The stopping was his." And the book names exactly what that does to her: "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop. That had always been her job, never theirs." I felt that line in my sternum. It's the same thing she's been telling Kayla and Meg — he asks, he waits, he hands it back — but experienced in the body instead of said out loud, and it rearranged something in her mid-kiss. The want stays lit with nowhere to put it, and the whole chapter runs on that low current. That's the heat of this one: not an act, a whole afternoon of desire kept at a simmer because the man will not take what's being offered until it's offered plainly.

And the door. "He said *my bedroom* like he'd said the kitchen and the office — no weight on it... and left the door shut. Something in her dropped." Oh, that dropped *me* too, even though I know him by now. She dressed for that door — satin under everything, "hoping the man won't hand her a reason not to show it to him" — and he walks her past it, weightless. Here's where I have to be honest against my own guard: I know Pace is a performer. I've known since the pointing game that his kindness is staged, that Randi chose Vee and he closed the sale. But this chapter keeps making me forget it, the way Vee keeps forgetting it. The patent, the shirt with no tag, the cloth on the table — every beat is him being extraordinary, and I keep swooning, and then a small cold voice says *this is the sales floor.* The chapter never once confirms that voice. Nothing on the page is false here — but the burgundy where she asked for brick-red, "he hadn't matched the dress she'd described. He'd matched *her*" — that's a man doing something a person who wants to be unforgettable does. I hold it open. Maybe it stopped being a game chapters ago. Maybe it never did.

The tour structure worked on me even though it's literally a real-estate walkthrough. Because every room is a disclosure, and she reads him off surfaces the way I do: the closet floor ("she could have read half of him off that closet floor without a word"), the shoes paired and squared, the KitchenAid worn to shine, the office in its chaos, the shop cleaner than her kitchen at home. The only room withheld is the bedroom — and the only other closed door in the house is the bench, which she doesn't know is a table and I do. That's the dramatic irony humming under the whole visit: she's being courted inside the building where Randi was bent over the bench, where the deadbolt goes over first thing. "Two towels on the rod" — that title. I thought it was just domestic texture until I sat with it. Two towels, and she doesn't know the other one is for whom. Or it's the offer itself: a place kept for someone. Either reading gives me a small chill.

The error-correcting codes moment was the chapter's sleeper hit: "You do it every time you open your mouth... Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it." Given a book that is *about* messages surviving noise — half-said confessions, siren-interrupted declarations, Vee leaving things unsaid — that landed as more than a lecture. I don't know if the author means it that way. It landed that way anyway.

Friction: almost none. The "parents" flinch was well-played and I want to know what's behind it. The only thing I didn't quite buy was how quickly she cracked the shirt mystery — but the *no tag* detail was planted in ch10, so it earned the payoff. Kayla's "Bring a change of clothes" has me braced for this visit to be a seduction; instead it was a measuring tape and "if you'll let me," and that inversion — afternoon, no meal, no bed, a *dress* — is exactly the swoon. "He couldn't make it without seeing her. That was the only thing standing between her and it — her own willingness to be looked at." That sentence is the whole book for Vee so far. She said yes. Chapter title promises measuring in "good light" next; my guess (from here only): the measuring scene, and I both want it and dread that Randi will hear about it.
