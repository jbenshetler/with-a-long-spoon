## Reader reaction

The first thing I felt was the kiss — she finally got it, the one the cop stole from her on the mountain, and I was right there with her ("a thing waited-for finally arriving"). And then he stopped it. His breath uneven, his choice. I sat up exactly the way Vee did. That small reversal — *he* draws back, the man, the one whose stopping she's never seen a boy do — is the hottest thing in the chapter for me, hotter than the satin she's wearing underneath everything. The whole book has been teaching me that Pace's power lives in restraint, and here it is again on the doorstep.

The house tour worked on me completely. I read him the way Vee reads him: off the closet floor ("one right pair for each thing and no more"), off the kitchen that is neither of the two kinds she braced for, off the graph paper. The KitchenAid "worn to the shine of a thing used every day" — that's this man in an object. And I loved her "Sanskrit" joke and that he "didn't explain it down to her size, and she was glad of that — glad he stayed exactly as far past her as he was." That's the most Vee sentence in the chapter: she *likes* the distance. She doesn't want him leveled to her; she wants to look up the slope. That tells me something about why the handing-over-of-choice soothes her, and the chapter never says it outright. It shows it in her eyes.

The bedroom door stopped me. She dressed this morning *for* that door — "the satin a girl wears hoping the man won't hand her a reason not to show it to him" — and he said *my bedroom* the way he said *kitchen* and left it shut, and "something in her dropped." I felt that drop in my stomach, her small humiliation and her instant covering of it ("didn't let her face do a thing"). It's the exact shape of the lesson this book keeps teaching through him: he decides what's on the table, and the withholding is its own charge. I both ached for her and felt the pull of it — the closed door is hotter than an open one, and I don't think the book is done with it.

The patent scene undid me a little. His "lit up like a six-year-old who'd just found out everything there was to know about dinosaurs" — the MRI thing, "somebody sick, somebody scared, a kid who can't lie still… out of the tube in a fraction of it," and then "It does other things. That's the part I like." Vee standing there "not knowing what to do with her whole chest" — that was me too. It answers the money question I've been carrying since chapter 1 (cheap counters, built furniture) in the warmest possible way, and the small closing at *parents* keeps Daphne-shaped questions alive without forcing them.

And then the cloth. I did the same double-take she did: the shade is wrong — *oh, it's not the right—* and then the mirror, the cloth against her hair. "He hadn't matched the dress she'd described. He'd matched *her*." I actually put the book down for a second. The flannel with no tag — she checked, chapters ago in story time, filed it under "some category that made no sense," and the answer was *he made it*, and the callback landed perfectly because the book planted it and then let me forget it exactly the way she did. "You made the shirt," she said, and I believed every beat of the figuring-out.

The measurement offer is where the chapter peaks for me erotically and it's fully clothed: "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so he can see how it'll fall." That's him asking, formally and explicitly, to look at her body — the body she presses "down the tops of her thighs" and covers with cardigans — as *material*, as a thing to be measured for beauty, with her yes required. And she says "Yes — of course, yes," no hedge. I felt the heat of that yes in my chest. This is the book's whole engine in one scene: consent as the arousal.
