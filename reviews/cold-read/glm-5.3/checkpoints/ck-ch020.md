### Impression
I trust the tenderness more than I want to and distrust the plan more than the prose lets me forget. Pace reads as genuinely good — the *may I*s, the deferred pleasure, the Daphne-shaped respect for closed doors — and yet everything Vee believes was chance was built by him and Randi in bed, which sits cold under every warm gesture; I keep both truths because the book keeps giving me both. Randi moves me and worries me in equal measure: her wanting is real and half-starved, her steering of Vee's confessions is surgical, and the moment she closed the door on Vee's secret ("You don't have to tell me") I couldn't tell protection from retreat. Vee has my whole sympathy — the shame and the heat on one wire, the happiness arriving faster than she can guard against it. Cassie is the one person with no angle, and her flat "Goodbye, Vee" and folded-away looks are the honest alarm in the room. Brooke I distrust on sight. Right now my suspicion sits entirely with the plan and its two architects — and even there, unevenly: I believe Pace's care is real even while it serves the game, and I believe Randi's "I want us" is going to cost all three of them something the pointing game never priced.
