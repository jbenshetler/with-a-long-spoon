### Impression
I trust Cassie completely — she is the book's ballast, and her flat questions land truer than anyone's charm. Vee I love watching: her hunger waking up, the way she takes things now on purpose, the streak of brave comedy (the pantsing, the canoe shoe) — my only ache for her is that her happiness is built on ground she doesn't know is shaped. Pace I can't settle: his tenderness reads as fully real — the drawer, the dishes, the way he asked "But what happened?" — and so does the orchestration; the same patience that feeds her is the patience that runs her, and he chooses not to tell her he's sleeping with her best friend. Randi moves me more than I expected: the bench chapter made her legible — the woman who decided never to be seen again, now unable to stop being seen *by* Vee — but she is also the one ferrying Vee's secrets into Pace's bed, and "Sticky fingers?" still feels like a held card. Sheri and Cassie are the two people whose goodwill has no hook in it. Suspicion sits squarely on the secret between Pace and Randi; everything warm in the book is also evidence, and I'm reading with both eyes open.
