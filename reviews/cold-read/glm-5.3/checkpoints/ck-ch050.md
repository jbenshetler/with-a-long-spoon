### Story so far
Cold open, before Vee exists for us: a woman arrives at Pace's too-warm house, is fed apple slices, is undressed with ceremony, and put on a walnut-and-leather bench he built; she is made to beg in a voice she's never used, cries without knowing it — and at "There you are, Randi," we learn the woman is Randi. In the locked bathroom afterward she meets her own face, understands she was more alive being *seen* than ever before, and decides the found one goes back behind the face — not ever. The bench returns to being a table. Next morning she lies to her sorority (Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey) about a paper and a study group.

Weeks later: in bed, Pace asks Randi if she'd like to taste another woman; her body answers before she can. On a campus walk he invents the pointing game — "I point. You pick." Randi rejects the expected, the matched pairs, the room-runners; at the dining hall doors a tall red-haired girl comes out laughing at the whole of herself, and Randi says a bare "Yes." Pace says "Go meet her." Randi stages a collision so clean the redhead apologizes to *her*; her name is Vee; they share a stats class.

From Vee's side: the collision is luck; Chi Latte under the Greek-letter joke she finally gets let in on; Randi's warmth total; "See you later" a fact. In stats, Randi sits beside her and Cassie; Cassie says "Goodbye, Vee" wrong. At the coffee shop Vee is stuck on a calculus problem; Pace arrives (Randi texting at the table), asks "May I?", gives her back the pencil at the line where she can solve it herself; "May I call you?" — she hears the *may*. Froyo, the long way, "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time." At the pool with Cassie, the remembered underwater freedom and the splash-fight that outbrawls grief. Dinner at Christine's: "May I choose?"; the evening goes soft; under the tree she states her rule and then makes the exception herself — first kiss, hers.

The hike: borrowed boots, the moleskin, the summit lake and burning maples, cherries, "we take nothing and leave nothing," and her offhand confession of the unaffordable russet dress ("That sounds like a beautiful dress"). Rain; he gives her his only dry shirt and stands in the storm so she can change; she knocks on the glass, unbuttons for him, sees him shaking with cold, and asks to be taken somewhere warm. The cop's one bark ends the night. She keeps the shirt. Invited Saturday to his house, she tours it: the closet floor of one-right-pair-per-thing, the handmade oak, the workshop, the patent that buys it all, and on the sun porch a bolt of burgundy silk matched *to her*. He offers to make the dress; she says yes. The measuring: bra off, the riser, the tide of touch-and-withdraw, her soaked satin terror, and — when she finally opens her eyes — his face turned up, waiting, delighted, not having looked down once. Something rearranges in how she stands. She spends new power; the pin-kiss; "Keep still." That night at home she tells Cassie most of it, and the green satin, frosted plum, and "Famished" become the first night: shame and heat on one wire, never closing over her; then the sheet, the fra diavolo, and "Trade" — she folds the panties into his hand. Randi's lunches draw it all out of her ("You love being looked at"; "You wanted it. And you took it. For once"); the goodbye kisses on the mouth begin, explained away as French.

The dress finished, she discovers the slit and strips her underwear on purpose to make the line clean; standing sex in the den; the induction: she takes the long step on purpose, gives the room her leg, and sends Pace the whole uncropped frame. The bet: she pantses him, he catches her, "we find out" — the proper spanking, gentle, with soothing and kisses, and the loss of her last denials. Halloween: costume one the dress, costume two "what I had on under it" — heels only. Randi's brunches keep pulling the confessions further out (the spanking, the photos shown half-then-whole); Vee's body answers the telling itself, twice sending her to bathrooms, once stopping her own hand at the image of Randi's face; "Sticky fingers?" and the glaze on a lip that never touched a pastry; "You're my best friend in the whole world."

Meanwhile the reader is allowed behind the curtain: Randi and Pace in bed together after her shopping trip with Vee, conspiring — "She's not ready… take your time" / "I want her to get there on her own" — and Randi, gone still at his description of Vee, admitting she got past her defenses. Sheri, on a bike, files it: "Two of them at once."

The Rusty Nail: he teaches her the three-step on a concrete pad with a taped boom box; at the bar a blonde poisons the well ("all us girls love him"); Vee wounds him with his own decency and stays alone; the bartender clears it up — "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." Randi coaches the apology (sure, not sorry; tomorrow; he leaves you the room), dresses her in her own shoes and scarf, and the goodbye kiss lands without a brace. The apology is received the way he receives everything; the weekend that follows contains the kitchen swat and her bright hot line of response — the thing he saw and she covered with her mouth.

Deepening: the drawer he makes for her; the whisper of "Peter" to his sleeping body; his history by the fire (college at fifteen, the desk in the hall, Daphne at nineteen; "It was the best two years I'd had"; the scar — "Not tonight"); the photograph he arranges her for and she sends; Thanksgiving apart — Sheri at his table ("She's alone"), Vee home with parents who price the dress and get the cropped frame; the MIRA afternoon, where Vee watches Randi's face collapse over her father's record and gives her somewhere to put her hands, saying nothing, ever; the five self-imposed study nights and the reunion; the flannel trade ritual.

The unnameable thing breaks through in order: the porch ("I had been eating crackers my whole life…") answered with "I love how good we are together" — her one attempt, retired; the Chili's night, where the loud room takes her voice from him and she reads his overwhelm as cold; the makeup sex and the dark afterward ("no name for the best thing in her life"); the sleepless night bare in the cold; and finally the confrontation — "What am I to you?" — "You're the most important person in my life" — "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me" — his silence, his half-lifted hand, her name called once behind her, and the cold that was worse.

Break: Gstaad. Randi performs daughterhood for a hearty father and an exquisite mother, picks up John in a lift line, is led faultlessly through a white-tie New Year's, and lies afterward in borrowed warmth feeling nothing — "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best" — and names to herself, at last, what Vee is to her; she will not say it; she will keep the brunches and let Vee arrive on her own; she texts her the fairytale, every word true, none of it the thing. Vee, in Ohio, under the shelf of little gold swimmers, watches the countdown alone, invents a blonde successor out of midnight spite, cannot keep even her best memory from turning into that blonde, and holds both her people while setting neither down. Pace, at the amber-lit diner with Sheri, lays his true bricks and is told: "She's told you what you have to do. So say it." — "I can't." — "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her." Home, whiskey-sick, he finishes the story for the reader: Daphne was the only woman he ever told; he taught himself the words were the difference between survivable and not; he knows he loves Vee and chose the smaller truth because he was weak. An engine on the highway lifts his head; the truck goes past; he sobs over the table, then picks up the fork and eats anyway.

### Impression
**Vee** — I love her, plainly. Her hunger, her courage at each threshold, the long step at the induction, the way she keeps other people's secrets (Randi's face over MIRA) even mid-heartbreak. The midnight blonde-inventing was petty and true and human. I want the word for her, from someone.

**Pace** — tenderness and frustration in equal measure. Every deed is right and I believed the deeds; but ch 50 confirmed what I suspected — the withholding is fear wearing the costume of principle, and he knows it. The Daphne history makes it forgivable and not excusable. Sheri's verdict is mine: "that feels pretty shitty." And yet the care was never counterfeit. Both true.

**Randi** — the most complicated seat in the room. The ch 1 bathroom scene made her legible to me before she was likable; the game made her a danger to the girl I love; and then she got caught by it, exactly as the jacket promised. Gstaad broke my heart for her — the cold she carries, the two-ways love she's shelved. I don't trust her machinations; I believe her love.

**Cassie** — the one person I'd trust with anything. No reason to doubt her at all; her flat, unprying care is the steadiest thing in the book.

**Sheri** — gruff gift of a character; I'd take her read on anyone.

Where trust sits: with Cassie entirely; with Pace in deeds, not words, and I now know why; with Randi least and most at once — she is the keeper of the book's central betrayal and its most closely held love, and I don't know which one Vee will meet first.
