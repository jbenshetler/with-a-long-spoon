### Impression
I trust Cassie completely and feel a real tenderness for her — she's the only person in Vee's life who wants nothing from the wanting. Vee herself I'm rooting for with my whole chest: her hunger is real, her initiations are hers, and watching her come into the wanting ("I wanted it, and I took it") is the book's best pleasure. Pace unsettles me precisely because his care keeps proving genuine — the rain, the waiting face, the pie lifted out of her hands — while I hold, in the other hand, the fact that he and Randi chose her on a lawn like a dish. Randi is the one I can't stop watching: dazzling, funny, useful in the exact way Vee needs, and hollowed in a way she decided (ch 1) never to show again — and now the girl she was sent to gather has gotten past her. The suspicion and the warmth run on the same wire, which I suspect is the point. Right now my trust sits with Cassie and the barmaid; my wariness sits with the plan, not with any single act of it — because so far, on the page, nothing has been taken from Vee that she didn't reach for first.
