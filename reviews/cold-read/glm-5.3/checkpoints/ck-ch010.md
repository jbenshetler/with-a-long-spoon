### Impression
I am wholly fond of Vee — her hunger, her discipline, the shriek-laugh in the pool, the "look at me" spin — and I want her happiness to be real, which is exactly the trap: it *is* real, every yes is hers, and it's being grown for her by two people she trusts in ways she can't see. Pace holds me in two places at once: the tenderness on the page is genuine and unlike anyone else in her life (he gave up a whole night's pleasure out of care; he keeps her confessions hers), and yet I watched him set the board before she ever entered the room — the "may I" grammar that undoes her is also the instrument. I don't distrust his care; I distrust his certainty that he knows what's good for other people. Randi moves me most and worries me most: her bathroom mirror decision ("not ever") is the loneliest thing in the book, and her held wanting on the couch is real love trying to wear a costume — she's lying to everyone, including herself, and spending real money ("dear friend") while she does it. Brooke I trust not at all; the warmth is the surveillance. Cassie is the one person I believe without reservation — the flat, dry, keeping-watch kind of love — and it stings a little that Vee is already learning to route around her. Right now my trust sits almost entirely with Cassie, my wariness with the plan's architects, and my hope with all three of them, because nothing in it is being done *to* Vee without her own yes — and that's the problem, and the pull.
