### Impression
I trust Pace's tenderness and don't trust its innocence at the same time — everything he does to Vee is genuinely careful (the pencil back, the accepted no, the quiet left in the text), and yet I've watched him design her whole courtship from a bedroom question. My wariness of him is about architecture, not cruelty; so far he has taken nothing from Vee she didn't hand over, and he's the only man in her book who asks. Randi pulls at me hardest: I've seen under the face — the begging, the mirror, the thumbnail — and I ached for her, even as she executes the collision with three seconds of clean choreography and lies to Vee's face about her empty love life. There's real heat in her bare "yes"; the plan and the wanting are tangled in her, and I don't think she's untangled them either. Vee I feel pure tenderness for — her gladness is unguarded and the reader's advantage sits on her like a hand she can't feel; every chapter she moves closer to the thing she doesn't know. Cassie is the one person I'd trust with my life in this book, and I notice the story is quietly moving her out of the room. Brooke I don't trust at all — the warmth is real the way a weapon is oiled. If I have one unease beyond the secret: nobody in the plan has asked what happens to the girl when she finds out there was a plan.
