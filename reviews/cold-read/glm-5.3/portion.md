# Cold read (grounded) — Portion

*scene: scenes/portion.md · model: glm-5.3 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch022 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter knocked the wind out of me a little, and I mean that as praise. We've been inside Vee's happiness for three chapters straight — the driving-home-bare joy, the "I wanted it, and I took it" — and the book has been letting me float in it exactly the way Vee is floating. Then "Portion" cuts to Pace's kitchen and Randi's bowl, and the whole warm thing tilts.

First, the dinner itself. I love this house — the pot on since three, the small bowl that's hers, the apple bowl. The small bowl is such a quiet gut-punch: two women, two kinds of care, the same crockery. And Randi arrives and does everything Vee does — the little back-and-forth kiss, the perfume — and then draws one line through the top of the sauce and doesn't eat. That line through the sauce is the chapter. The whole fight is conducted through that untouched bowl. He ladles it, he notices, he leaves it. He's a man who notices everything — I've known that since the light pole — and he honors what he notices by not naming it. When he covers the bowl and refrigerates it "for her" at the end, I actually ached. He still doesn't know what he did. Neither do I.

My feelings about Randi moved this chapter, and moved darker. The checkpoint has me split on her — warmth real, management real — but here I watched something come *out* of her that I've never seen on the page: "her voice do that," flat, nothing on the words. The mask isn't on. That's the woman from chapter 1's bathroom, the one who decided the found-self goes back behind the face — and this is what it looks like when the face comes *off* instead. "It's selfish, Pace." I believed her and I didn't. She's right that his patience costs her something — she's watched him kneel for an hour and finish a hem, she's spent a month being the instrument of a plan she set in motion and can't steer. And she's wrong, or at least the page won't let me say she's right, because everything he said back was the Pace I've been half-in-love with for twenty chapters: "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything."

But then the bedroom. This is where the chapter burned me, and not pleasantly — deliberately not pleasantly. There's heat in it, in the mechanical sense: her on top of him, one motion, riding hard. But it's the coldest sex in the book, including the cold open. "She did not say *tell me*." I've only heard about that ritual secondhand, but the way the chapter builds it — every night she's said it, and he lies under her *waiting for it* — I felt the absence like a missing stair. Her eyes shut, his hands on her hips, "whatever this was, it was not for him," and he gives it to her anyway. That's Pace in one sentence: he gives her what isn't for him because it's hers. And afterward — no lingering, the cashmere, the back turned, his hand staying out of her hair. The crying that "goes somewhere inaccessible" — I think it went somewhere tonight, somewhere even he couldn't follow, and the page stayed properly outside it. I don't know what she's feeling. Jealousy of Vee? Wanting to be the one who's chosen? Wanting *Vee* and not wanting to want her? The checkpoint's been holding that question open since the thumb on the glass, and this chapter poured fuel on it without answering it. I'm not going to guess beyond noting I felt it as appetite and grief in the same body.

Pace — my two-directional pull on him got worse, in the interesting way. This is the first chapter where I've seen him *not know*, and it humanized him more than any of his perfections. "He did not know what he had done." The designer of the whole courtship, and when the woman he's loved a month comes at him flat-voiced, he can't reconstruct the evening for the fault in it. I believe his defense of Vee's pace — "As long as she needs" — and I notice, with the dread I've been carrying since chapter 3, that Randi's accusation lands somewhere sideways of where she aimed: "the place that had knelt an hour at a pedestal not touching a girl who had been wet through her clothes for him, and finished the hem." Even his conscience inventory is a list of restraint performed. Is that saintliness or is that the plan's whole technology? The book still won't split those, and I still can't, and I'm starting to suspect that's the point of the title. *A Polite Invitation* — "may I" as a way of taking.

The title, "Portion": at first I thought food, obviously — the portion he serves in the small bowl, the portion she won't eat. But by the end it's the whole geometry of the book: everyone gets a portion of Vee, and Vee gets portions of everyone, and nobody gets the whole of anyone — Randi gets the sex without the *tell me*, Pace gets the gladness without knowing what he did, Vee gets the love without the plan. Randi self-pouring the wine — "He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house" — was the loudest line in the chapter for me. Small rebellions against being taken care of. Blue toenails, and now a self-poured glass.

Friction: almost none. If anything, the chapter is so compressed it read fast — the fight is all subtext and the sex is over in a paragraph, and I mean that as the *feel* of it, which suits Randi's mood. The one moment I'd flag: "the want, he thought, with an edge on it tonight, from somewhere" — the author letting me see Pace reach for an interpretation and fail. I'll allow it; it's his failure, not the narrator's thumb.

Where I'm left: the induction and the burgundy dress are still hanging out there, Randi's bathroom decision ("not ever") just visibly cracked, and Brooke is circling. What I dread and want at once is the night those wires touch — Vee in the dress, Randi watching, Pace holding every string and not knowing what he did. Next chapter I expect (guess only) to be back in Vee's daylight, and I'll be reading her happiness differently now, knowing what's running under the street where she drives.
