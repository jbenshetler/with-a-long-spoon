**Felt read**

This is the coldest chapter of the book so far, and the coldest thing in it is how much tenderness is spent and refused. "You cooked. … You shouldn't have." That line landed in my stomach — she heard it come out wrong and let it stand anyway. Pace feeding Randi in the small bowl, the one the apple slices go in, is the whole first chapter compressed into a prop: the man who learned her appetites down to the width of a slice, the woman who draws one line through the top of the sauce and puts the spoon down. "It's wonderful" — and he registers how strange it is that she's never once praised his food in his kitchen. She's offering him something real, and neither of them can carry it across the table.

Randi is cracking, and I felt it before the text confirmed it. The rim of the glass under her thumbnail — that's the bottle cap from chapter one, the same self-aimed pressure, now at his dinner table. Then the interrogation: "How long, Pace?" "She's not ready." "Or you're not." That flat voice he's never heard from her. I read this scene as Randi finally saying the thing she's been hiding — not exactly jealousy of Pace's time with Vee, though it's that too, but the *want* underneath: Friday happened without her, and she was told about it secondhand, at brunch, by the woman she wants. She poured her own glass. He's poured every glass she's drunk in this house — tiny detail, devastating — and she took that from him tonight. She's taking back control of every ritual he owns of her.

And the sex is awful in the precise way: she skips the ceremony (no inch-and-stop, no eyes, no *tell me*), shuts her eyes, uses him. He understands "it was not for him" and gives it to her anyway. Then the turned back. The whole chapter is her portioning herself — eating the salad she didn't ask for and refusing the meal he made for her — and him lying behind her unable to name what he's seeing.

Pace's line "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything" — I believe him, but I notice what he can't see: the game he and Randi run *is* the terrain Vee chooses on. Her consent is real at every step, and she's choosing inside an arrangement she doesn't know exists. That gap is the whole unease of the book, and this chapter is the first time the two architects have fought about it in the open. Randi calling his patience "selfish" cuts because she's half right — his patience is also his appetite, and Pace even half-knows this ("it's selfish" lands and hurts). His recap at the end — "he did not know what he had done" — terrifies me a little, because the answer is: the thing he's been doing all along with both women, watching, withholding, deciding. He covers her bowl and refrigerates it "for her." Even his kindness is portioning.

Small friction: Randi's accusation arrives slightly ahead of my full understanding of it — "It's selfish" is doing a lot of work I can't fully parse. But I think that's earned; Pace can't parse it either.

The title "Portion" is perfect: the small bowl, the untasted stew, the half-eaten salad, the rations of herself Randi dispenses, the portion of Vee each of them is fighting over. Nothing given whole.

Heat: the sex is explicit but almost anti-erotic — it's the first sex scene in the book I didn't want. That's clearly the point. Romance: the tender gestures (chair pulled out, bowl saved) are real but rejected.
