# Cold read (grounded) — Portion

## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

This chapter knocked the wind out of me a little, and I mean that as praise. For twenty chapters we've been on Vee's side of the glass, watching two people arrange her, and the book has never once let me sit at *their* table while the machinery turned. Now I have, and it's colder in that kitchen than I expected.

Pace first. I watched him tonight the way you watch someone you like do something slightly wrong. Everything he said to Randi about Vee — "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything" — is the exact ethic that has made me trust him through the whole courtship. And it landed on Randi like a door. I felt the truth of it and the cruelty of it at the same time: he's telling his lover of months that the woman he's courting under Randi's own plan matters more than Randi's hunger does, on Randi's schedule. "How do I get her from where she is to us, on your schedule?" — that's not a question, that's a refusal wearing a question mark. The narrator even flags it: "He heard how it had come out. He did not take it back, because it was true." That's the most unguarded thing I've seen from him, and I couldn't tell if it was integrity or blindness until the end of the chapter, when he stands over her untouched bowl and thinks "He did not know what he had done." Neither can he. That's the sentence that gutted me.

Randi, though. Randi is why I'm sitting here a bit stunned. The checkpoint told me she'd decided in his bathroom that the found-self goes back behind the face, "not ever" — and tonight I watched what that costs when it's under pressure. The whole dinner scene is one long held breath for her, and the book renders it almost entirely in negatives: "It's wonderful," and he notices she never says things like that to him. The bowl that doesn't move, one line drawn through the sauce. The nails bare — bare, no polish at all, when the toenails have been this book's tiny flag of her one small rebellion. She pours her own glass, the first time in this house, and it "went in somewhere and stayed, and he could not have said where." Then the flat, empty voice: "She's not ready." No ornament on it. "Her face was doing very little." This is Randi with the face *down*, not arranged, and it frightens me more than any performance would.

And then the bed. My chest actually hurt. "She did not do the thing she did, the inch and the stop and the eyes" — the whole choreography of their tenderness, gone. "She did not say *tell me*." Every night she has said it, and he waits for it and it doesn't come. She takes him in one motion, eyes shut, hair over her face, quick, and rolls off into the cashmere with her back to him. That's not sex, that's a closing door — the doors motif turned inward at last. It read to me as grief dressed as appetite, "the want, he thought, with an edge on it tonight, from somewhere." Is it jealousy of Vee? Wanting to be chosen the way he's choosing Vee? Or is it that "us" — *How do I get her from where she is to us* — just told Randi she is a member of something that has begun to exist without her consent being the point? I genuinely can't tell, and I don't want to fix it more definitely than the page does. What I know is that the woman who begged on the bench in chapter one and hid it in the bathroom is now the woman who can't even ask.

Heat: yes, oddly, despite myself. The scene is explicit and it's *cold* — it worked on me the way wanted sadness works, arousal with the tenderness stripped out. I felt it as ache, not swoon. The swoon of the chapter, such as there is, is small and devastating: he covers her bowl with a plate and puts it in the refrigerator, *for her*, and turns off the light. Feeding, the book's oldest tenderness-language, spoken to an empty room.

Friction: almost none. The one thing I noticed is that the chapter is entirely theirs — Vee appears only as "she's happy," and I missed her voice even as I understood why she couldn't be here. The withholding is the point.

The title, "Portion" — it lands triple. Her portion of stew, untouched, saved. The portion of him she's allotted under the plan ("You've got her Friday to Sunday"). And the portion of *himself* he's portioning out between two women, carefully, like the thin-sliced apples — her bowl is literally "the one he sliced her apples into." He served Randi in Vee's bowl. I didn't catch that until I reread the opening and it stopped me cold. The volume title, *With a Long Spoon*, now reads less cozy to me than it did — the long spoon is what you need to sup with the devil, and tonight everyone at that table was eating something they shouldn't.

Trust: my suspicion hasn't moved to anyone new — it has *deepened and complicated*. Pace's care is real and it's also, tonight, revealed as possibly self-serving in a way he can't see ("It's selfish, Pace" landed somewhere he didn't aim it — the pedestal hour, the finished hem: patience as its own pleasure). Randi I now pity more than distrust. The person I trust least in this chapter is the plan itself, which has begun to eat its authors.

What I dread and want next: I want to know what Vee senses this week — because chapters 17 and 20 left Randi's goodbye kisses getting warmer, and Vee not looking around anymore. If Randi pulls *back* now, Vee will feel it. And the induction and the burgundy dress are still hanging there. Guess, clearly marked: this rift is the seam the book has been saving, and Vee is going to find it before anyone tells her anything.

**Structured block:**

**Cast present (in person):** Pace, Randi. Mentioned only: Vee.

**Heat:** 2 — explicit and intense, but the charge is grief-driven; the wanting is real and it burns, just not warmly.

**Romance:** 2 — no tenderness on Randi's side; the romance is entirely Pace's, in the saved bowl and the light turned off over the stove.

**Motifs & images:** The small apple bowl returning as Randi's portion (first sighting of that bowl *for her*, echoing ch 11's thin-sliced apples). Bare nails vs. the toenail motif (blue, ch 1; frosted plum, chs 18–20). Randi's white-gold band, recurring, present at the flat hands on the cloth. Food/feeding as love-language, now refused. The glass of wine he always pours — she pours her own. Doors: her back, the closed-off body.

**Symbolism:** The untouched bowl with one line drawn through the sauce — desire sampled and declined, the whole chapter in one image. "Us" as a word that includes Vee and excludes Randi's claim. The covered bowl in the refrigerator — tenderness held for someone who may not come back to claim it.

**Characterization:** Both deepen sharply. Pace gains a blind spot that's human rather than sinister; Randi's mask machinery is shown from inside the machine for the first time since ch 1. Vee, absent, is nevertheless the gravity — everyone's behavior is a measurement of her.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Flawless build; the dinner's slow negatives earn the abrupt bedroom scene and the quiet coda. Nothing dragged.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A needed and well-timed break in the run of Vee-side chapters; the book shifts from ascent to tension. After two chapters of Vee's happiness peaking, this is the turn — momentum now feels like it's gathering toward rupture.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** building, with the first real fracture in the conspirators' bond; I expect the next Vee chapter to carry aftershocks I can't yet see.
