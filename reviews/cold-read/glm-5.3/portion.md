## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This chapter landed on me like a hand laid flat on the chest — quiet, heavy, and I didn't fully feel how much it hurt until the refrigerator at the end. The whole thing is dinner, and nobody eats.

Randi is frightening and heartbreaking here in equal measure. The whole opening ran cold for me before I could name why: "You shouldn't have" — that's not modesty, that's a woman girding herself. And the bowl. The small bowl, the one he slices her apples into — the book has taught me to read Pace's feeding as his whole language of care, and I watched her not eat it with a physical wince. One line drawn through the top of the sauce, a cosmetic proof-of-tasting, and then "He left it." He's learning, or already knows, that he can't make her take it. The apple-slice tenderness of chapter one has come to this: he covers the bowl with a plate and puts it in the refrigerator, *for her*, and turns off the light. That last image is the loneliest thing this book has done. He still doesn't know what he did — "He looked for it, back through the evening from the gravel forward, and it was not there" — and I believe him. That's what aches. Both of them are being truthful and the truths don't touch.

What I *did* feel, sharply, is the accusation landing somewhere real even though Pace can't see it. "She's not ready." "Or you're not." That flat voice — "he had never heard her voice do that" — got under my skin. And "It's selfish, Pace." My read: Friday happened without her. Vee and Pace crossed a line Randi scripted, and now the two of them have something together that she's outside of, and her want — which I've watched since the bench, since "I want us, torn out of her" — has curdled into jealousy and she can't say the true sentence under any of the false ones. She's not wrong that something was taken from her. She's also not honest about what she wants or why she's angry. Both. The self-pouring of the wine did more work on me than the shouting in a lesser book would have — "He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house," and she just took the bottle. That's a door closing.

And then the sex — the worst sex in the book, deliberately. Every ritual of theirs reversed: no inch-and-stop performance, no eyes on his, no "tell me." The word never came and he *waited for it* and I waited with him. "He understood it was not going to, and that whatever this was, it was not for him, and he gave it to her anyway" — that's Pace in one sentence, generous to the point of self-erasure. But I noticed he didn't withhold it as leverage or ask her anything. He also didn't touch her hair after. Even his patience has a floor, and I felt it quietly hit.

Trust and suspicion: my suspicion of Randi is now earned and text-supported — but it's earned as *pain*, not as villainy. She watched him be glad and said nothing more about Vee. That restraint from the woman of "Tell me" is its own alarm. And I noticed the thing Pace can't: she came from Vee's table, where Vee told her everything, glowing. Randi has now *had* the telling — the thing she used to demand from him in bed — and it didn't feed her. My guess (from here only): the Randi–Vee kiss and the Fridays are colliding, and Randi is going to have to want Vee openly or lose them both.

Friction: almost none. The chapter is short and spare and every object is doing double duty. If anything, I missed being inside Randi — but I think the *not* being inside her is the point of this one; we're locked out with Pace.

The title: "Portion." Both bowls — his eaten, hers untouched — the portion she won't take, the portion of Vee he's had and she hasn't. It recolored backward for me: all of Pace's feeding across the book was portioning out care, and this is the chapter someone doesn't swallow it. Series title, *With a Long Spoon* — the long spoon you sup the devil with, and I'm feeling now that it's the three of them each trying to share a meal without touching. Book One: *A Polite Invitation* — the invitation is starting to feel less polite. What I dread and want next: I want the scene where Randi finally says her want out loud, to Vee, in her own voice. I dread it because I don't think the book will let her do it cleanly.
