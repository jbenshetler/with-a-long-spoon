**Felt read:**

This chapter landed in me low and quiet, the way the radiator heat stands in that room. It's one woman alone with a shut window and her own body, and it's one of the most honest chapters this book has given me — because the war it stages isn't with Pace or Randi, it's with the verdict in Vee's own head, and she loses it by about a breath.

The window frame held me before I consciously noticed it. She comes in and shuts Cassie's two inches — *"the room began at once to belong to the radiator"* — and the whole first half reads as a woman claiming a sealed room as hers, a room where nothing escapes, where everything she makes stays put. And then, after, the same window has to be *shoved* back open — swollen, resisting — and January comes in "like water through a broken door," and I understood the chapter's whole shape: the closing was desire, the opening is shame doing housekeeping before Cassie comes home. That shove, bracing one hand above the other, physically cost her something, and it cost me something to watch. She airs out her own pleasure like a smell someone might catch her in.

The heat worked on me the whole way. She's in Pace's flannel, open over bare skin, sleeves rolled twice — the shirt as stand-in, and she knows it, and the montage of him arrives in pieces the way memory actually does: wet sounds instead, cashmere instead, the groan inside her. But the chapter's real engine is the inspection scene — the fingers drawn out *"coated nearly to the knuckles, more of her than she would have admitted even alone"* — and I felt the old verdict arrive right on schedule: *excessive, the shameful plenty of her made visible.* That's her mother's register, and the book has taught me to hear it. And then she *smells* it, braced for the stink the boys' jokes promised, and finds a hothouse, *"hers, unmistakably hers"* — and I melted a little, honestly, watching her meet her own body as a fact instead of a flaw.

And then the reach. Fingers at her lips, tongue forward, *"and stopped. A breath's width remained."* Nothing in the room tells her why, and nothing in the room told me either, and that's exactly the chapter's meaning held open the way I want it held: she *can* — the wanting is there, the smell is there, Pace's untroubled glad face is there — and she cannot. She wants his mouth carrying it back to her. She wants what she felt *before any part of her had convened on the question.* The wanting is for the pre-objection moment, and the objection lives in her own tongue. She comes with the window shut, climaxes hard and alone and half-dressed in his shirt, and then the shame arrives wearing a practical coat — air the room, wipe the hand, button the flannel "respectable to mid-thigh." That buttoning is the whole book in one gesture.

What moved me most is also what unsettled me, and the page earned both: Randi is not in the room but she is all over the climax of the thought. *"Had Randi's looked like this?... A mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers."* This is the second time Vee's body has arrived at Randi at the very brink — the restroom scene did it first, Randi's face asking *how much could he see.* She files it under Pace, "obviously, all of it," and I notice that the filing gets less convincing each time. I'm not suspicious of anyone in this chapter — nobody is doing anything to anyone — but I am *watching* Vee now, watching where her mind goes when she lets it off the leash.

Cassie deepens in absentia — the parka gone, the book tented mid-sentence, the two-inch habit that tells me she runs warmer rooms than she admits to wanting. The false laundry story is still standing between them, and Vee airing her pleasure out of the room before Cassie returns made me feel that distance, gently. Pace deepens too, in memory only: *"untroubled and glad"* is maybe the tenderdest three words this book has spent on him.

Friction: the mid-scene fragment splices (*"His crooked deep"* — I assume *crooked finger*, and the compression made me stumble once) momentarily read as typos rather than memory, but I caught the rhythm on the second beat and it stopped bothering me. The strand-stretching section went a beat long for me; the point was made before the last pull-apart. Small costs.

**"The Reach"** as a title sat oblique until the last third, and then it named the whole thing precisely: an arm's-length gesture, undertaken, held, and not completed — *"The unfinished reach was still there, one breath from her mouth."* It didn't give anything away; it only landed.
