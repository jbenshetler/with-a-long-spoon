**Felt read.**

The office scene did something to me I wasn't braced for: it made me like Randi. For chapters I've watched her want Vee sideways, through shame and through Pace, and here she walks into Pace's office in a beret and plays the dirtiest game of cooperative fiction I've read in this book — "my heroine," "the sorority girl," all of it conducted in front of a postdoc who mostly faces the wall. The "sorority girl isn't acting" moment near dropped my heart, and the fact that she says *I* instead of *she* — "I'm trying to find a natural way to get her tied up" — the slip he catches and files. And him answering with "counter tension... maybe even cause her some pain" — the two of them negotiating their own scene in literary-criticism code while Stephen types loudly on a dark screen. That was the hottest thing in the chapter, and I mean that literally: the home scene that follows is more explicit and I stayed *warmer* in the office.

The wax scene worked on me, with a wince. It's the most tender and the most extreme this book has gone at once, and it earned both: "Only if you want to," the safeword exchange (Brooke means stop — that line is going to live in my head; what it says about what Brooke means *to* Randi), the buckle placed in her fingers so she's never actually trapped. I trusted the whole scene because the page kept handing me the consent architecture under the theater. And the aftercare — peeling the wax coins off in order, showing her each piece — is the most intimate thing Pace has done in the whole book.

But the end of this chapter is what I'll carry. Randi, cold, alone with the dress, masturbating to the image of Vee in the finished burgundy with Pace watching — and then sliding the silk offcut *inside the lining of the bodice*. She's leaving a piece of her want literally inside the dress Vee will wear to her induction. It's a secret Vee will wear against her skin without knowing. After two chapters of Randi icing him out, punishing Pace with speed and silence, this felt like the truth coming up: the wanting never went away; it went underground. Whatever was broken on Sunday, it didn't kill the desire — it only made it lonelier.

The characters: Pace deepens for me here — he's stumped by "Since when?" and *has nothing*, and that small defeat ("In mine, she'd ask for it" / "Since when?") landed as the realest thing in the chapter. He believed he was the author of asking. Randi's fiction of "doing it for a friend" is so thin the scene lets it be thin; even Pace punctures it ("Then wouldn't it be easier for him to seduce the redhead himself?"). What unsettles me is not suspicion of anyone but the structure itself: Vee still doesn't know she was chosen, and this chapter shows the machinery running on both sides of her — Pace's fantasy of her sounds, Randi's fantasy of her mouth — while she sleeps in a shirt that smells of him. The title "Colors" lands on the burgundy, the oyster lining, Randi's blue toenails "like something under water" — the palette of want throughout.

I want the induction. I want Vee in that dress. I dread the strip of silk against her skin — that's a detonation with a long fuse.
