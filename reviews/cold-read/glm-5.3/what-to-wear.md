## Reader reaction

The bench. He's taking her to the bench. That's where my head went immediately, and I'll own that as a guess from here — but everything in this chapter feeds it. Randi asked back in chapter three whether Vee would like the bench, and the whole book since has been a slow corridor leading this unguarded girl toward that room, and now the invitation has arrived, stripped of information, and Vee is treating it like a riddle to be delighted by while I sit here knowing what Randi knows and Vee doesn't: she was picked. That asymmetry has been the engine of the book for me, and this chapter turns the key one notch harder.

What I love about this chapter is how ordinary and warm it is. Vee laughing with her whole body, Kayla's bright unbroken stream, Meg finishing her sentences like a calm older sister — Vee has friends, a table, a corner in the gold light, and she's telling the Pace story as a *story*, getting it better in the telling. That felt true and sweet and also faintly awful, because she's narrating herself into a romance plot while I know she's standing inside an arrangement. "He's different. I know how that sounds." I believe her when she says it — and I believe Meg when she smiles and says "Sometimes they even are." Neither of them knows *what* he's different at.

The most striking beat for me was Vee explaining the thing she can't explain: "I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." That's the chapter doing exactly what this book does — locating the charge not in the act but in the *asking*, in the handing-over. For Vee, who polices herself in her mother's voice, being asked what she wants and getting it is the hottest thing that's ever happened to her, and she can't name it. Kayla reading the invitation as a come-on ("Two in the afternoon is not dinner... Bring a change of clothes") made me laugh and made me ache, because she's reading the subtext better than Vee is, and she doesn't know how right she is — but wrong about the shape of it. There will be more than one person who knows that house.

The message itself: "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock. That's it." I read it twice like Vee did. Pace's *may* and his restraint have been established, so I actually read it as careful rather than sinister — he's leaving the choice entirely to her, as he does, which is both the thing Vee loves about him and the thing that unsettles me: consent as his signature move. The unease here is real but it's earned by the whole arc: Vee still doesn't know Randi and Pace know each other. That's what turns "what do I wear" from a fun question into the title question — what to wear, what self, what mask she'll show up in — and the book has already taught me that clothing is where Vee's self-consciousness lives (the cardigan over the better shirt). She goes through his door without the facts. Two o'clock, "no meal at two." Randi's name is nowhere in this chapter and she's everywhere in it.

Heat-wise this is the quietest chapter yet — no bodies, no touch — and yet the charge of the last three chapters carried in: the shirt on her pillow at "this exact hour," the twelve days counted without counting, the wall-climbing. It's heat by report, and it worked on me. I notice the last chapter's "I want us" from Randi right next to this — Randi wanting "us" and Pace setting Saturday for Vee within hours of her leaving. My guess from here: Randi is going to be there Saturday, or expect to be.
