# Cold read (grounded) — What to Wear

## Reader reaction

The felt read:

I came into this chapter still warm from the truck — from Vee's astonishing, deliberate unbuttoning, from the flannel, from "you're freezing" — and this chapter is the comedown, the processing, and it's a different kind of pleasure: gossip-pleasure, friend-pleasure. But it's also the quietest escalation yet, because the phone buzzes and everything changes. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock. That's it." I felt that buzz in my own chest. We know what's behind that door — or at least we know what I suspect is behind it: the warm house off 614, the Steely Dan, and the bench. I don't know that the bench is Saturday's business, but I've been carrying that bench since chapter one, and the pending hike resolved into the mountain instead; now the house itself is on the table. My dread and my hope are in the same hand.

The chapter's texture delighted me. Kayla and Meg are new — really new, first sighting, and instantly alive. Kayla's bright unbroken stream ("That's not a message, that's a ransom note") and Meg following "the Pace situation in installments like her shows." This is the first time Vee has had a *chorus* — a table of women who know and want the details — and it does something the Cassie reports never quite did: it makes the Pace courtship *public*, a story with an audience. Which of course is its own quiet irony for me, because I know the story already has an audience. Randi has been getting "the full report" at smoothie hour all along. Vee thinks she's telling the shirt story fresh; she's telling it again to the second audience someone arranged. No — Kayla and Meg aren't arranged, I don't believe that, nothing on the page says so. But the doubling of "reports" is there, and I noticed my own flinch when Vee held the phone "out of reach" — information being portioned, again, is a motif this book keeps touching.

Vee deepened for me in one specific way: "He asks me first. The small things… and I'm the one who can't think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." That's the most articulate she's been about her own desire-mechanism — she names the paradox *and* hears it "sounding like nothing." She's begun to know what works on her. That half-frightens me, actually, because the thing that works on her — being asked, being given real choices — is precisely the instrument Pace was trained on, the Daphne lesson, the "May I." Her kink, if we can call it that, is *genuine consent as spectacle*, and it's real and it's also engineered, and she said it out loud at a table full of fries without knowing either half of that sentence.

The heat here is low but real — a 1, warmth at the edges. The retold shirt story re-charged the truck scene slightly ("Kayla: 'Stop.'"), but the actual electricity of this chapter is anticipation. Kayla's "There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat" is the chapter's real voltage — Kayla does in one line what a whole scene of interiority would have done, and I loved that Vee "let it sit." The wanting is now being named by other people on her behalf, which is exactly the book's pattern: Vee's desire arrives via proxies, permissions, others' vocabulary. She kissed him at the tree because of Randi's coaching; now the invitation is being decoded for her by Kayla. Everyone is holding the door for her to walk through, and she still thinks she's the one walking.

Where I flinched: "Twelve." She hadn't even had to count. That landed as sweet *and* as a small ache — she's tracking this like a pregnancy test. And the moment the noise drops away — "She'd had every piece of him she'd been handed, and never once been through his door, and now there was a door" — the door motif came back and I sat up. Doors and locks have meant everything in this book: the deadbolt Randi turns, the bathroom lock, Daphne's door she taught him to respect. Now Vee is being invited through Pace's door, and I can't not feel that the door-motif's whole weight is being carried into Saturday. Is this the open door the other doors were practice for, or another door that turns out to have a lock she can't see? The page didn't say. I'm holding it ajar, because the page did.

Also: I noticed the mother's voice is *absent* this chapter. Vee narrates the make-out story ("I backed him into a tree") at a full table without one flicker of floozy, without the verdict arriving. The voice landed hard in the truck scene; here, nothing. Either she's genuinely quieter now — the flannel on the pillow, the picked feeling — or the telling to friends puts the voice outside the room. Either way I felt it as movement, and I don't fully trust it to stay gone.

Suspicion check: nothing new earned suspicion of anyone. Kayla and Meg are warm and clean; the message itself is its plain self — "the least information a human being has ever sent me and I have read it a thousand times." I have no reason to doubt the invitation's plainness. But I'll say my reader's dread plainly: two o'clock, Saturday, no stated purpose, his territory. This is the first time the plan (if it's running) is bringing Vee to ground Pace controls completely — no restaurant, no public trail, no campus. And Randi isn't in this chapter at all, which I felt as an absence the way I felt Cassie thinning last chapter. The two women who know the truth are both out of the room while Vee gets ready. Cassie, notably, doesn't hear the invitation on the page. That drift is continuing and I don't like it — Kayla's "we're doing hair Saturday" is filling the space Cassie's "That's a man. That's a boy" used to hold.

The title, "What to Wear," is doing lovely double work. It names the dinner-party problem Vee raised on the summit — the russet silk she "set down on the shelf" because she "can't afford it. Obviously." Pace heard her say it and put it down gently and picked up nothing. I don't know if that comes back — a gift would be exactly the kind of generosity that doubles as architecture, and if a russet dress appears Saturday I'll feel both the melt and the chill. But the title also means the subtler costume question: what does anyone wear *to be looked at* by Pace, what self does Vee put on for his house — the girl who unbuttoned her own shirt, or the good enough black one. Kayla's question ("what does she even own that says his house without saying trying too hard") makes the title literal; the summit made it symbolic; I suspect Saturday will make it both.

Volume title: "With a Long Spoon — Book One: A Polite Invitation," jacket promising two lovers with a secret plan around an unknowing girl. A polite invitation is exactly what just arrived — twelve polite words, no stated purpose. The book's title is converging on this text message; every invitation in this book has been polite, and the spoon implies dining with the devil at arm's length, the long reach, the not-quite-touching. I read it as: politeness as the long instrument by which something dangerous is tasted.

Next: I want Saturday. I dread Saturday. The two are the same want. Guesses only: Randi will be at the house, or arrive at the house, or be discussed — the plan's third act is owed and this door is where I'd put it. And I keep the russet dress on my shelf.
