**Romance:** 2 — the invitation lands like a courtship beat, and Vee's day-counting ("Twelve") is the warmest, most naked thing in it.

**Felt read:** This chapter feels like exhaling — the whole book has been half-tilted toward obsession and shadow (Randi's locked doors, Pace's held information), and here I get Vee in a warm pool of her own life: golden dining hall, Kayla pouring out of her curls, Meg steady with her fork and her shows. Her friends are doing what friends do — processing the last chapters through them so Vee (and I) can hear them out loud. It's the book letting Vee be witnessed being happy.

The middle is the chapter's real heat, even fully clothed. "It's making me lose my mind, is what it is. And I can't even explain why — that's the worst part." That's the thesis of Vee's whole arc said in her own voice: a man asking what she wants is the thing undoing her, which is devastating because we've seen exactly what Pace's asking-forwards tenderness is calibrated to do. Her hand on her own arm, "without knowing it" — the body saying it before she does, same as ever. And "twelve days" with no counting — she's keeping vigil. That's heat even though nothing happens but a buzz on a table.

What I notice about what Vee leaves *out*. She tells the flannel story straight and charming — but not the part where she took her shirt off in front of him on purpose. She edits to the gentleman version. That's the same split that has her keeping the flannel on her pillow: she tells the shivering-man part and holds the "me, on purpose" part back. And then the invitation arrives — "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock" — and I know, and she doesn't, what's in that house. The bench under the tabletop. Randi's deadbolt. The whole reader's dread arrives in a text message with "no information," and Kayla — who has no idea how right she is — says "what do you wear" while I'm thinking about what's under the long table. Her mother's voice, or Randi's world, has never once entered this chapter. That's exactly what makes it ominous: the sweetness is real and it's a walk toward a door I've already been inside of.

I liked Kayla and Meg as *people* instantly — Kayla "at a professional level" of scandal, Meg's "Sometimes they even are" landing as the one grain of salt the chapter earns. Meg's "what do you even own that says his-house without saying trying-too-hard" is the title's question turned over to the table, and it never gets answered — the chapter ends in orange light with the question still open, which is right.
