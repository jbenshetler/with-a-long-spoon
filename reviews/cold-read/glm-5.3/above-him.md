# Cold read (grounded) — Above Him

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

Well. I finished this one with my hand actually over my mouth, and I'm going to try to be honest about the order in which things happened to me, because the chapter is built to land in an order and it landed exactly that way on me.

First, "Above Him" as a title — I spent most of the chapter thinking it just meant the sex, the being-lifted, or lying there watching him sleep above her. Then section two: the bathroom mirror, "she watched the two of them in the glass, her with her arms still up in her hair, him folded around her," and then the counter, and I thought — no. Every reading of it flips. Above him physically at the door, swung up off the ground. Above him in the drawer, literally: he folds his whole ordered self down into the lower drawer and gives her the top one, *the easy one, the one your hand goes to first.* And above him in the thing she says to his sleeping shape: "Peter," the name she alone was given, whispered over him while he's under her breath, not meant to wake him. The title kept turning over all the way to the last line, which is one of the loveliest moves the book has made on me yet. It gave nothing away beforehand; it retro-illuminated everything.

The swoon, because there was a real one: the drawer. I want to say that plainly before any suspicion gets near it, because the drawer earned its swoon on the page. "He'd folded his whole ordered self down into a single drawer. He'd given her the top one." After two chapters — chapters! — of Vee reading silence where she wanted words ("He never said"), this is the answer, and it's an answer in exactly his grammar: not "I love you," but *a cleared drawer and a run of liner with room to spare, room for more of her.* I put the book down for a second. That landed harder than any declaration would have, and I think that's the honest reader-response, not a trick of the prose. The scrunchy form, the dozens, "no note, there was never a note" — the book has been teaching me his language for thirty-some chapters, and this chapter let me hear it spoken back whole. Vee gets it too: "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead." That's the romantic peak of the volume so far, for me.

The heat is quieter here than in "We Find Out" or "One Bite," but it's not absent — it's the afterglow kind, retrospective and lived-in. The doorway lift, "a bright stupid sound she'd have died of anywhere else," the catalogue of how he always brings her over first ("She hadn't known a man would want to") — that's heat as character rather than heat as event, and it worked on me as warmth more than as burn. What actually stung a little was the weight loss. "Her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, and she'd never asked it to," the bras riding higher, the clothes bought a size up because the racks never believed a build like hers. On the heels of the food motif — the fed girl, "she ate like a field hand at his table now" — there's something complicated happening to her body in all this, and I noticed the book noticed it. I don't know yet what it means. I'm holding it open.

And now the shadow, which I have to name honestly because the page earned a small one: she loves him. She's let it "be true in her own chest where she couldn't take it back," and she's counting his acts as evidence of feeling he "said in nothing." From inside her skin, that reading is completely convincing — it convinced *me* all over again, the salmon, the warm tomatoes, the coming back to her an hour later. But I'm the only one in the bed with the other fact: he and Randi chose her on a lawn, and the patience she reads as love's restraint is also the plan's. The worst part is that I can't split them anymore — that's what this chapter did to me. The care and the design have fused into one indistinguishable behavior. When he folds himself into one drawer, is that the man or the strategy? The page gives me no way to tell, and Vee has no drawer in which to keep that question. It unsettled me precisely as much as it moved me, no more, and I don't think it's an accident the chapter ends on "hers": her drawer, her scrunchy, her place.

Randi sits over this chapter without appearing — the glaze on Vee's lip from "One Bite" is still, for me, in the room — and her absence is almost louder for it. Cassie absent too, and I missed her; she's the one person whose ledger with Vee has no hidden column. The mirror beat in section ii was the one place my unease sharpened past romance: his eyes move down to her sprawl on the counter and *hold there a second*, and she waits. "He did nothing." And the book itself leaves the tail of it — *Had he minded. She couldn't tell. He never said.* Three days later the drawer answers it generously — but I clocked that this man's silences can be read generously or read as control depending on what you already know, and Vee only has the one way to read them. That's not a complaint; that's the book's whole engine turning.

Friction: very little. If anything, section i moved fast for me — the conference-week gap is dispatched in a paragraph, and after the slow ceremony of the last few chapters I felt the summary-ness of "He'd cooked after, the way he near always did" for about a page before the salmon particulars grounded it again. The bra/weight passage is doing something I can't name yet and I'd rather it stay unnameable than be explained to me next chapter. I trust this book not to explain.

Where do I think this is going — guesses only: the drawer is a cohabitation door opening, "room for more of her" feels like a literal promise, and I'd wager the "bag" becoming a presence, then a toothbrush, then weekends becoming weeks. And I keep waiting for the bill on the plan — Sheri's "that's not finished," the vow from chapter 1, the induction photo he received with no idea what else was in the frame. The book keeps handing Vee everything she reaches for; I've been waiting chapters for the first thing taken. Not yet. Tonight I mostly just feel the sweetness and the one cold thread braided through it, and I'll keep both.

**Symbolism:** the two drawers — his whole ordered self folded down into one, hers on top: devotion as demotion, and the geometry of the title; the drawer "prepared and waiting" as the plan's own image (a place made for her before she asked — which is also literally what Randi and Pace did).

**Characterization:** Pace deepens — his love-language given a concrete grammar (making places rather than saying words); the counter-pause keeps him readable in two directions at once, which is the book's best trick. Vee deepens: the love-admission is handled exactly right for her — private, unsaid, unretracted. Nobody went thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** section i compresses a week briskly (the one drag); sections ii–iii are perfectly short and earn every beat — the drawer reveal needs no padding and gets none.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** building — after the high-heat run of "We Find Out" and "One Bite," a tenderness-and-settlement chapter is the right change of note, not a sag; the intimacy is escalating in scope (a drawer is bigger than a spank).
