## Reader reaction

This chapter undid me in the best way. The opening — her folded over his chest with her hair making a red curtain around their faces, kissing him "inside it, blind" — is the tenderest post-sex moment the book has given us, and it belongs entirely to Vee's POV, which means I finally trust what I'm seeing. "He's all mine" is her thought, and I believed it, and my heart rose with her.

The photographs. That's the center of the chapter, and it's a study in how this book does heat: the most explicit thing on the page is arousal, and the most explicit thing under the page is trust. Her asking "Would a picture help?" while tapping her nail on her tooth — that's Vee at her bravest and most reckless, and the text doesn't pretend otherwise. She names the risk herself: "the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this," and then "believing him was the brave part." I love that the chapter lets the danger of the photo sit there honestly. Pace promises never to share it, and I want to believe him — but the whole book so far is a machine of things Vee doesn't know (that she was chosen, that Pace reports to Randi), and so that image now exists as a thing that could be seen by eyes she never planned for. That dread isn't on the page; the page is warm. But it's there in me, and I think the book knows it.

The erotic charge is high and strange in the best way: he arranges her "like a woman in an old calendar," kissing every place his hands have set her — hip, shoulder, knee — before taking the picture. It's worshipful and a little uncanny, Pace as the photographer and composer, and Vee willingly the composed object. "She was composed, arranged, a girl who had decided to be looked at" — that's the whole arc of Vee's shame-to-display journey in one sentence. The detail that he uses *her* phone, and she sends it to him — she gives him herself to keep. It's explicit, warm, and consensual to its bones. Heat 3 for me.

Then the drawer. If the photo is the chapter's heat, the drawer is its heart. The scrunchies missing from the counter — a genuine stomach-drop, the same "had he minded?" dread she's been carrying since "Not in Your Bed" — resolved by him having *moved his own things down a drawer* to give her the easy one. "There was no note. There is never a note." That's Pace saying love the only way he says anything, and it landed on me harder than the sex. Against her "I love him" (unsaid) and his never saying it — this is his saying it.

The Thanksgiving exchange is the one shadow, and I think it's deliberate: "I don't go back," each word "set down clean and apart." After all the open doors around his family (checkpoint has them all still open), this is the firmest closure yet — and the coldest. Sheri gets mentioned fondly and without jealousy, which puts the flicker of Vee's jealousy ("another woman at his table") down as small and human; it went out fast for me too, the way it did for her. "She's alone" — that's a good man talking.

Trust vs. suspicion: no one felt off to me on the page. Pace's want is plain; Vee's bravery and hunger are her own. My only unease is the photograph itself — not suspicion of the text, just awareness of what this book has taught me about where Vee's images travel. The title "Above Him" — I read it as the literal image of his head on her breast, her above him for once, the reversal of every dynamic this book has run; also her rising above her own shame. Nothing given away by the title.

I want next: Thanksgiving apart, the photo somewhere it shouldn't be, and Randi's brunch question still unanswered — whether that was a door or a trap.
