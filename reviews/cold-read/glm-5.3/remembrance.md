## Reader reaction

**Felt read.** This chapter made me hot and then cold, in that order, and I think that's exactly what it was built to do.

The sex between Pace and Randi is the most physical the two of them have been on the page since chapter 6, and it works on me — but not the way it wants to work on Pace. Randi arrives uninvited, naked under her dress on purpose, and demands he "fuck me like you mean it." The heat is real (Heat: 3, earned by sheer wanting — his three days of pent-up Saturday, her planning), but the whole act is scored to Vee. He's carrying Saturday: "her breast against his jaw… *Oops.*" When Randi says *Tell me*, he refuses — "It was not his to hand across" — and my chest tightened at that. He's keeping Vee's secrets even from the woman he shares the plan with. And then Randi asks the question that reframes everything: **"How are you going to tell her about us?"** — asked mid-act, softly, "as if she were asking where he'd parked." That's the most chilling line of the book so far, and the text means it to be. His hesitation physically slows his body. Her stillness — "the heat went out of her hips" — then her revival at *you get her to want it.* He decides, in his own reasonable voice, that telling Vee the truth would shrink the gift. "Vee had never been given anything that wasn't explained first, and priced." That sentence almost persuades me — and that's what unsettles me most. The withholding now has a philosophy. Both of them are choosing not to tell her, and the chapter lets them feel loving while doing it.

And then the bite. "I wasn't thinking," she says — but I don't buy her not-thinking, and I don't think I'm meant to. Her teeth closing on his shoulder until his hand snaps to her face and "*Don't.*" His refusal is instant and comes "harder than anything he had ever said to her." Something old is under that — Daphne, maybe. It's the first time I've seen him flinch at her.

What moved in me: the ending is genuinely tender — "she had come out here for him and lain on him after," the shirts, the dark visit to the burgundy silk under its sheet. And the final text, *Dinner Friday?* answered *Yes.* Vee has no idea what Friday is. I read her "Yes" as the most ominous word in the book.

Where I stand on everyone: my sympathy for Pace is eroding even as my attraction holds — his fantasy of the "sound she would make" for Randi's mouth is voyeurism dressed as generosity, and "she would not know, until she did" landed like a knife. Randi frightens me a little now — "She'll remember *me*" with Kentucky all the way up in it is naked appetite for being Vee's first, an appetite she wants inscribed on Vee's whole life. And Vee — asleep in her dorm, texting back *Yes.*

Title: *Remembrance.* It's Randi's word — she wants to be remembered, "eighty years old and she'll remember the first one." Not memory as tenderness; memory as conquest.
