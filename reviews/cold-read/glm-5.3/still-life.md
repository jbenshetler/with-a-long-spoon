## Reader reaction

**Still Life** did something to me I wasn't braced for. The waxing chapter before it left me uneasy — the wrong hand on the table, the rolled-over part Randi never mentioned — and I came into this chapter carrying that unease, waiting for the surprise Friday night to tip the same way. It didn't. It inverted it. Everything the salon took from her control, Pace gave back: the whole painting scene is the right hand arriving where the wrong one was. When the brush first touched her palm I actually tensed — and then the walnut, the parsley, the grape, each color arriving with a kiss and a taste, and I understood the game before she did and *melted*. The grapevine growing out of her open palm up her arm. The melons. Her laughing "they're melons" and him answering "Not finished." That's the swoon of the chapter for me: not the orgasms, the ritual of it — every pigment fed to her mouth before it lands on her skin, so she learns the painting by tongue before she feels it by brush.

The charge is real and sustained. The gesso sequence is the peak and the riskiest thing in the book so far: him dipping the brush in *her* and painting her with herself. It should have been too much and it wasn't — because the chapter had spent an hour teaching her (and me) that stillness was the currency, and her surrender of the choice ("the relief of no longer making the choice broke something loose inside her") is the orgasm, almost literally. Her body answers the shame with more wetness and the text doesn't flinch from either half. I believed all of it. This is the hardest I've felt the heat since the spanking chapter, and warmer, because it's built out of tenderness — the cherry pit into his palm "as naturally as though she'd given him something precious" is the line I'll keep.

And then the taste. She goes searching his mouth — "a trace. Salt first, which might have been him." That's the unfinished reach from her dorm room, the fingers that stopped a breath from her mouth, brought to him and *still not closed*. He kissed her, and she couldn't separate the flavors, and the question went unanswered. I love that the book keeps this ajar. Last chapter she couldn't close the distance with her own fingers; this chapter his mouth carries the answer and even that stays ambiguous. The motif is doing real work: her appetite for herself keeps arriving and keeps being interrupted one breath short.

But the ending is what sits with me. She doesn't send him the photo — *he made it and handed it back.* "So nobody on earth had seen it but her." And then she tries to imagine Randi's voice over it and the imagination fails — "it went somewhere she couldn't follow" — and she files it away. Not that she'd ever show her. She's been telling Randi everything since October; this is the first thing she keeps. And I felt the keep. That's the chapter's real title to me: a still life is what you arrange and look at, and this image of her — painted, opulent, "not one part of it apologizing for the rest" — is hers alone, and the lie of omission starts right there at "Tomorrow was Randi's." She's not hiding it from Randi because it's too much. She's hiding it because imagining *giving* it to Randi went somewhere she couldn't follow — the same breath she stopped at in the last chapter, and I don't think the book is done making her stop there.

Pace deepens yet again — the scar, kissed without asking, let go of; "Now I can see more of you" is the gentlest dirty line in the whole book. Vee's pride read as earned and real, not spun. No suspicion of anyone; everything here was clean. My one small friction: the painted-fruit conceit flirts with preciousness, and "She was lit right down to the floor" is the kind of sentence I notice as a sentence. But the laugh he gives when she smears paint on his face carried the whole whimsy problem for me — it's play, and both of them know it.

What I want next: the mixer with Randi, and whatever the kept photo does to the space between them. Guess from here — the withheld image will matter. This book is teaching me that everything Vee doesn't hand over eventually has a price.
