## Reader reaction

The grin Vee walks in with carried the whole chapter for me before a word was said — "the grin was the entire story." And this chapter is about exactly that: the gap between the grin and what's under it, what she keeps and what she gives away.

What landed hardest was the telling-it scene, because I've now watched Vee tell this night three different ways in three chapters — the lunch version to Randi, the full-body version to Cassie last chapter wasn't (Cassie got the *fitted-dress* version; the sex happened after). Now the sex itself gets the ladle treatment: "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole." That image stopped me. It's the most precise sentence the book has found for Vee's whole architecture of not-looking — and the book is honest enough to add "and told herself it was a kindness. She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." That's the self-deception named on the page, not by me. She learned the editing trick somewhere between Randi's lunch and now, and I noticed *who* she learned it watching.

The zipper moment is the chapter's real scene. Vee, who has spent this whole book being undressed in rooms, freezes at her own zipper three feet from Cassie — because taking the skirt off would drop the secret onto the floor: no underwear, given away, folded in thirds, "Trade." The comedy of it ("I forgot my." — gesturing at nothing) sits right on top of something tender. Her body has become the confession she can't make. And Cassie, whose read is always fast and whole, calls her "boyfriend" — kindly, certainly, wrong. That word hit me oddly. Not wrong about Pace existing; wrong in a way I can't pin. It's just a word Cassie would use. But the book has been so careful about what nobody names, that a casual naming made me sit up.

I keep noticing the mother's voice losing its grip. "The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning." Last chapter the floozie-loop was still running as fuel; here it's barely a formality. Something in Vee finished opening and the shame didn't close her even in the retelling. That progression feels earned, not rushed — the book spent ten chapters buying it.

Cassie remains the person I trust most, and the half-second at the door — "something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name" — is the one shadow in the chapter. I don't know what it was. It could be nothing; it could be Cassie clocking that her roommate is further gone than she can say. I leave it ajar because the page does.

**Toenails** as a title: frosted plum, painted for him, hidden in the ballet flats, now her mother's verdict walking around in the open — the smallest token of the whole seduction and the one she can't hide from Cassie ("I have eyes"). The book title, *With a Long Spoon — Book One: A Polite Invitation*, still reads right: everything Pace does is an invitation, and the long spoon is the distance you keep with the devil, which nobody in this book is keeping yet. The dinner invitation, the house, the dress — all polite; the politeness is the engine.

Heat is low here by design — it's the afterglow chapter — but the *heat of the kept middle* runs under it: "the night ran under all of it, a second channel that had not shut off once." Want that survives its own consummation. And "She had never in her life been so happy" over a bare drive home — that line landed on me with real weight, because I know things she doesn't, and the happiness is unguarded in a way that makes me brace for her.

What I want next: the dress must be finished before the induction — I want that scene more than anything. And I dread, mildly, Randi hearing the full report, after "You don't have to tell me" closed that door so softly. Guess only: the Randi side of the story is due.
