# Among Friends — Reader reaction

The felt read first: I came into this chapter with the open question from ch48 hanging over it — *what happens when Pace and Randi meet at the same table* — and the chapter knew exactly what I was afraid of and delighted me instead. The dread was the triangle detonating in public; what I got was the opposite: three people playing their private instruments quietly, in tune, in a room full of strangers, and nobody hearing anything but music.

**Randi arriving first** was the perfect first beat. Of *course* she beat everyone to Vee's things — "she beat everyone to Vee's things, always had" — and parked square to the door. The chambray shirt, heels "a full register too nice for the tile": that's Randi in one image, refusing to come down to the venue's level. And when Pace says *"Vee's boyfriend,"* and Randi's hand closes hard around Vee's — I read that grip the way Vee read it, as fierce happiness, but I also felt the double in it. She's squeezing the bones of the hand attached to the woman she loves, at the moment that woman's relationship gets named out loud. The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar. Randi's toast is the chapter's real gut-punch: "meeting Vee was the highlight of my year," delivered while *looking at Vee the whole way.* And then the glass turning a slow quarter at a time under her thumb — the thumbnail rim-pressing, first sighted in ch1, back again — tells me she's gone somewhere Vee can't follow. Randi is quietly wrecked and Vee misreads it as being *moved*. That's the ache of the chapter: Vee loves her "so much it ached" and doesn't know what she's looking at.

**Pace.** This chapter is why I love him. "Vee's boyfriend" six times — the running joke that isn't a joke, the man with "exactly one credential and no plans to produce another." That's his answer to the declaration problem: he can't say *I love you* still (he never did, even in ch46), but he'll spend a public word he's never needed. And the way he works the room — asking Danny about *his* bench, giving Theo the downbeat, asking Cassie about "the Bricklayers" — is Pace's love language made social: he notices everything and gives it back. Cassie's "You can stay" might be the best line in the book. Two words, the whole goalkeeper audit passed.

**The stinger.** Vee's half-second of jealousy at Pace-and-Randi laughing — "a shape she knew from Christmas" — is the honest note the chapter needed. She invented a rival once; the trapdoor instinct is alive. It passed, and I believed it passing, but it confirmed the Christmas wound isn't closed.

**The heat.** This chapter runs hot in a way I didn't expect from a party chapter. The restaurant itself is chaste — but everything Vee narrates is charged: she's wet since *Vee's boyfriend*, she tells us so plainly, and the parking lot and the truck scene carry the release. It peaks for me at the offering gesture — "her own fingers, dragging the sticky lace off herself and holding it out of his way, an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second" — that's consent as continuous yes, and it's the hottest sentence in the chapter. The memory of the coat's dark bloom returning mid-drive, and September's cold fan: the callbacks did land on me.

**One line I'll flag as friction:** "if somewhere in her a glass was still turning a slow quarter at a time, she couldn't have heard it tonight" — the closing metaphor reaches a little hard, but it earns its place by refusing to let the Randi ache vanish just because Vee came.

The title, *Among Friends*, is the whole design: the triangle in public, everything visible, nothing named. What I dread next: the debrief dinner with Randi tomorrow, because Pace told Vee that Randi said brunches are "the highlight of my week" — and *knows* is a live word now.
