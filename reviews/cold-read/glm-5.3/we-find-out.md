## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I came into this one still humming from the induction, and the very first thing the chapter did was hand me something I didn't know I wanted: Pace alone. "Thunderstruck" through the siding, the worst air guitar she's ever seen, "both arms doing something nobody should do sober." I laughed out loud with her on the gravel. After "Rock" gave me the desk in the hallway and the boy who taught himself trigonometry, this felt like the last door opening — the private dork under all that stillness. And Vee *watching like a thief*, hand over her mouth — that's the whole book's engine in one image: her seeing him when he doesn't know he's seen.

Then the play, and I stayed giddy the whole first half. Something new is loose in her: she texted first — *I miss you, can I come by early* — the nakedness of it flagged exactly right. She pokes him and he *flinches*, and the text knows what a landslide that is: "He had been a wall since the day she met her." She strips his shorts off as a joke and then flashes him and the page lets her be astonished at herself along with me. I believed every bit of the bravado because the book keeps showing me the cost of her shame — so her flashing him in the den lands as real liberation, not performed sass. I wanted her to have it. And then she swats him and "it was like slapping the workbench," and his *hand* comes down, and she goes still, and the chapter turns over from romp into the thing it's actually about.

The bet is where my pulse went up. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." This is so precisely Pace — the engineered choice, the door built so her yes can walk through it wearing a fig leaf — and the text doesn't pretend otherwise; she says "You can't prove it" with her chin up and both of them see through it. But what got me was how clean the machinery stayed: she says "Don't" and *the hand leaves — gone, no pause, turned up like empty hands* — and the loss of it is what teaches her what she wants. That beat is the best consent writing I've read in this book, because it makes the withdrawal itself the erotic argument. Her "Yes. We find out" — there's the chapter title, and I only caught it there, half a beat before the light went on: the title was her yes all along.

The wet finger is where it burned hottest. The shame of "the shameful plenty of the wet," the old mother-wire — and then him looking at it the way he looked at her under the tree the first evening, "like it was hers, and good." And then the spanking itself, which is where the chapter quietly did its deepest work *on me*, because I've been waiting for Randi's story to detonate since the practice room. Vee braces for the theatre — counting, loudness, the show Randi described — "and what came was nothing like the story." No counting. His mouth pressed gently to the warm pink of her. I felt that difference in my chest, because I know what Vee doesn't: Randi told her that story days before, and I can't unknow it, and I can't stop wondering whether it was a gift or a preparation — a story that would leave Vee's body braced and wanting before Pace ever touched her. I hold that ajar. The page hasn't said it, and Pace's version was so tender, so unperformed, that the suspicion can't fully land. But "no Randi to be told" — Vee keeping this from her now too — made the asymmetry in this triangle ache a little.

The ending undid me completely. The juice, faintly ridiculous. "Wonderful, wonderful, until the word and his hand in her hair were the same thing," and "for the first time she could remember, treasured." Then the scrunchies in the morning — no note, *more of them than a person could go through* — the plenty of them saying "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." That's the shirt-fold, the burgundy silk, the whole grammar of this man: love rendered as objects fitted to the exact person, in future tense. And the curry — toasting spices, "more vegetables than she was used to seeing go into one pot" — after the whole cumin conversation on the sun porch, that's him building her a childhood casserole out of everything her beige Ohio one never tried. I melted. Her talking to the mirror about her "Victorian LARPing" made me laugh; her meaning it about the bag made my chest tight.
