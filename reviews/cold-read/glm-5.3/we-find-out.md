## Reader reaction

She texted him first. That's where this chapter got me, before anything else happened — "I miss you. Can I come by early?" from a girl who has never once reached first, and then couldn't sit in the car and came at three anyway. The whole chapter is that escalation told in the body: she pokes him and he *flinches*, and I felt the ground shift with her. He's been a wall since the day she met him, and the page let her see the wall give, once, at the side of her fist. Something unlocked in her right there, and in me.

And then the chase. I laughed out loud at the shorts waved like a flag over a taken field — "Ha!" — and at her flashing him, "the small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it." That line is the chapter's engine in miniature: Vee keeps outrunning her own self-accounting and catching herself doing it. The flash was hers, the speed was hers, the swat on his workbench backside was hers. When his hand came down across her underwear and she went still, I went still too — this is the thing from the kitchen, the thing from Randi's practice room, the thing she would not say yes to in the bathroom. And here is the bet: "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." The title. She says "We find out," and the yes is fully hers — he takes the hand away at "Don't," all of it, turned up empty, and she has to ask for it back. That's the book's whole erotic grammar in one gesture: nothing taken, everything offered, and the offering made to cost her something. I trust the consent on this page completely, and the heat ran through me the whole way.

The finger is where it burned hottest and cut deepest at once. "Too easy. The ease went through her worse than any sting had." Her body deciding without her — that's her old shame arriving as heat, and the page knows it: "the wet and then the shameful plenty of the wet." And then his face, the same face as under the tree, looking at the wet "like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." I melted there. That's the tenderness I trust in him — the rain, the waiting face, and now this. The denial chorus ("It's not. That isn't. I don't—") while her hips rise to meet his hand — "she could not have said which one of them she was lying to" — is the most honest the book has been about her, and it didn't feel like the author's thumb; it felt like her.

What came was nothing like the story — no counting, no theatre, no Randi-script. He gentles her first, sooths after each one, kisses the struck place. The contrast with the practice-room story is sharp and I don't think accidental on her end either: she braced for Randi's version and got this instead, and I felt the relief as heat. "Slow. For me." He asked, and "it was hers now, give or keep, and she loved it." The shoes kept on "made the rest of her nakedness louder" — the black strappy heels, the ones on credit, doing their work. And then the sex: the same ease that humiliated her in the light becoming "only true," and that sentence — "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no such thing as a witness" — is the best line in the chapter, maybe the book so far.

The after. The juice. He held it to her mouth. "Wonderful, wonderful, until the word and his hand in her hair were the same thing," and she fell asleep "for the first time she could remember, treasured." I was gone. That word — treasured — landed harder than any spank in the chapter, and I let it.

Then two things I hold carefully. The scrunchies: deep green with white dots, more than a person could go through, "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." It's tender, and it's also — I can't help noticing — a keeper's gift, a plenty that assumes duration. Given everything I know about the plan, I read it warm and read it wary at the same time, which is how I've read him for thirty chapters. And her bathroom monologue: "Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together," "I'm going to start bringing a bag" — she's moving in by inches and narrating it to his mirror. The scar passed under her palm again, unasked, "a thing about a person you've decided to keep" — the open question stays open, and I liked that the page didn't cash it.

The curry closes it in the book's own language: spices toasted, a dozen vegetables, the bowl looking like childhood gravy and tasting like none of it. Being fed, again, by the man who feeds her. The heat of it "came up late and caught her by surprise" — the chapter in one sentence.

My distrust hasn't moved, and hasn't grown: the care on the page keeps being real, and I keep holding the lawn in the other hand. What I want next is Berkeley behind him and the weekend to hold; what I dread, as a guess from here, is that "we find out" is the door Vee walked through on her own — and that Randi was told. "No Randi to be told" was in the bed; the telling will come after. I'm aching to see whether her arms stay slow coming down from that goodbye too.
