## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter is the comedown, and I mean that in the best sense — the exhale after two chapters that ran at full burn. After "A Round," which was the hottest sustained scene in the book so far without a single sex act in it, we get Vee in the dorm room telling Cassie, and the whole chapter is Vee *re-narrating* what I just lived through — which turns out to be its own kind of pleasure, because I get to watch which parts she keeps, which she comedizes, and which she can only approach and back away from.

What moved me most is what Vee keeps leaving out. She tells Cassie about the bra, the box, the measuring, even the heat turned up hours in advance — but the wet panties, the panic, the eyes-shut moment, the grin he was wearing when she opened them: none of that makes the report. "It was a lot," she says, of the measuring, and then pivots to the hem. And the hem she can only do sideways: "He was on his knees, and looking up. And I was up on the box looking down. And I kept waiting to feel wrong about it. I didn't." And then, the line that landed hardest on me: "Like I was supposed to be there." That's the whole book's engine in one sentence. She was *supposed* to be there — the reader knows she was *arranged* to be there, that the riser and the warm house and the burgundy silk were all a trap of true material closing around her — and her body experienced the design as destiny. I got a genuine chill of the double-read at that line.

Cassie remains my steady warmth. "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy, and not getting any?" is exactly the friend she is — and her stillness at "May I look," the "I think I have it," is Cassie doing what Cassie does: witnessing without decorating. And yet. There's a small thing nagging me. Cassie's "Are you sure he's not gay?" beat — the puzzle-piece frown, "the way she worked a puzzle when the pieces wouldn't fit" — for a second I wondered if Cassie's pieces aren't fitting for a reason I know and she doesn't: the Randi-shaped reason. Pace's attention slides off Randi like she's somebody's great-aunt; if Cassie ever watches him around other women, his strangeness might read differently. I hold that loosely — it may just be a friend's tease — but it's the first time the text has let Cassie's suspicion brush against the secret.

The comedy works. The "package" bit had me laughing out loud — Vee patting her own ass "brisk and clinical, like she was indicating it on a diagram" is the funniest sentence in the book — and then the turn, "I was grinding on *him*," lands the way the best confessions do: wicked, warm, and true. This is Vee not just thawing but *spending* — the girl from the riser who found out she had power she'd never been issued is still here the next day, delighted with herself. That continuity feels earned, not flattened by morning-after reticence.

My friction is small: the chapter is almost pure recap, and there are stretches where the comedy is carrying scenes I'd already read. The "not gay" run went on a beat long for me. But the re-telling isn't wasted — it shows me Vee's edit of her own afternoon, and the edit *is* characterization. What she keeps at the bottom and finally surfaces ("I was grinding on him") is the thing that's growing fastest in her, and she can only say it laughing, from flat on her back on her friend's bed.

The title, "Turned Up" — I read it first as the thermostat, the heat turned up hours before she arrived, the detail that undid her when she understood it. But sitting here after the whole chapter I think it's also her: turned up, dialed higher, the volume of herself raised. Maybe both at once — his careful warmth and what it's doing to her. The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps darkening for me with each chapter: every invitation so far has been polite and every one has been aimed.

What I dread and want next: the sewing is "a couple of weeks," the induction is the deadline, and Randi is nowhere in this chapter — which itself is a held breath. The last we saw her she left Pace's bed and his house for the first time ever, after "I want us." I'm braced for the evening the two women's threads cross again — and for the moment the dress, made from a confession Vee thought she'd let go of, walks into a room full of people. Pace — chapter to chapter: holding, deliberately, a rest beat on a rising arc; the book earned the pause.
