## Reader reaction

**Felt read.** This chapter put a knot in my chest and then tightened it, slowly, the whole way. Almost nothing "happens" — a swim, a lecture, a refused kiss at a dorm door — and it's one of the most charged chapters in the book for me.

The Sunday at Pace's house worked on me strangely. She spends the whole day initiating sex with him, and I read it exactly as the text frames it: something in her "let go for as long as it lasted, and then, after, took itself back up." That phrase is the chapter. She's using him — not cruelly, she loves him, but as a place to put what she can't hold. Then the montage of every goodbye kiss from Randi, and the question she can't stop: *What if she meant them?* I melted and ached at once, lying there with her head in Pace's lap cataloguing kisses. When she shifts it back onto herself ("It was her. She'd taken the best friend she'd ever had and made it this") I felt the full sad weight of how she's built: she'd rather convict herself of corrupting the friendship than admit Randi is in it with her.

The Monday bunk scene is the best writing in the chapter — her hand flat on her stomach "a lid held down on a pot," refusing the one thing that would make it true. "As long as she didn't give it the thing that would make it true, it could stay only wanting." That's Vee's whole moral machinery in one sentence, and I believed it in my body. I also felt the honesty of Pace's shadow here: "if anything the message ran the other way, and Pace had made a point of telling her so" — his games have licensed things in her that she now can't steer.

Tuesday night: the Lifesaver ritual broke me a little. The wintergreen "gone soft," Randi's fingers on the inside of her wrist, her pulse leaping "up under it to be found." That graze is the peak of the chapter's heat for me — fully clothed, a fingertip, and I felt it more than most of the explicit scenes. Then *"Having you at the dance was so great. I've wanted to do that for the longest time."* The word *having*, arriving with the same half-beat lag it had in his bed. The text lets it stay double — casual affection or the other thing — and I'm holding it open rather than deciding.

The door scene is where my feelings about Randi got complicated all over again. Vee deliberately turns her mouth away — the first clean, deliberate refusal she's managed — and Randi answers by kissing her neck, deliberately, at "the exact place she'd put her own mouth three times and been told not to weaponize." *"How do you like it?"* That's teasing, but it's also a woman who knows exactly what she's doing, and I can't tell if it's affectionate play or appetite wearing a grin. Probably both, and that's what makes her Randi. And then Vee — having just dodged the kiss — invites her to *brunch*. Out of the ringing neck, not out of thinking. I sat with that contradiction: she can't take the kiss and can't refuse the woman. My trust in no one is broken; my pull toward both of them is intact.

Cassie at the end: "What was that with you and Randi?" — and Vee can't answer. Cassie is the book's conscience asking the question the reader has been asking for thirty chapters, and the fact that Vee has no easy lie ready for *her* (unlike for Pace) landed hard.

The title, "Coming Due," points at debt — Marsh's reciprocity lecture lands in the same chapter, obligation and payback, gifts as debts laid on people. The hau from ch51 wanting to go home. Vee's wanting has been accruing for months on borrowed terms, and the chapter reads like a payment notice arriving. The volume title "A Place at the Table" now feels ominous as much as warm — who gets the seat, and what it costs.

Friction: minimal. The Marsh lecture is a touch conveniently on-theme, but Marsh has been doing that all book, so I bought it. Nothing felt like the author's thumb.

What I want next: the brunch. I dread it and want it the way Vee does. Guess from here: Saturday brunch is where something breaks — either the truth or the kiss.
