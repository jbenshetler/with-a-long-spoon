## Reader reaction

The felt read:

I came into this chapter still warm from the pool chapter, still carrying the taste of "Tannin" — where I knew, and Vee doesn't, that the man pouring wine for Randi is the man Vee went home grinning about. That's the charge this book is running on now, and "May I Choose" turns the dial down to almost nothing on plot and all the way up on texture, and it worked on me completely. This is a chapter about being fed, in every sense, and I felt fed.

Pace, here, is exactly the man the coffee shop promised and I keep half-bracing for that to break. The "May I choose?" — the title, landing in his mouth at the menu — gave me the same little key-in-lock feeling Vee had. What moves me about him is that the chapter *shows* the machinery of his care but from inside her experience of it, so it reads as tenderness rather than design: the text message that "didn't ask her to answer that night," the street side of the sidewalk, weighing the page "not because he needed it but to weigh what he was choosing for her." The right hand. I noticed the right hand again, and Vee noticing it as "a pattern now" — some consistent rule he keeps. I have a suspicion I can't fully earn from the page yet, only that it felt deliberate when I read it, the way everything about him is deliberate. I'm holding it loosely.

Vee deepens for me every chapter. The hunger passage is the best thing in the chapter: "it kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the book's whole engine said plainly, and I believed it in my stomach. Her losing the thread of his talk and letting it go — "the not-needing-to was the most restful thing she had felt in as long as she could remember" — made my chest hurt a little, because I remember the Chi Latte chapter: she kept the feeling and lost the words with Randi too, but there it was intoxication and here it's surrender, the same blank transcript meaning two different things. That echo is doing real work.

The swoon is the hand. "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly." I read that line twice. It's too much for a hand and the chapter knows it's too much for a hand, and that self-awareness is what let me melt instead of wince. And then the ending — she deploys the rule ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"), and he doesn't argue, doesn't lean, just closes it and starts walking, and *she* is the one who stops, the one who says "But… I'm making an exception." That's the cleanest consent beat in the book so far and it's also the hottest, because the wanting stays hers. He gave up the kiss and she took it back. I actually said oh, out loud.

Cassie got her one scene at the top and it was perfect and short — "That's not a boy. That's a man" — and the detail that she teases Vee about everything *except* this tells me everything about what Cassie sees. My fondness for Cassie keeps compounding. And there's a small cold thread underneath the warmth that the chapter didn't point at but I can't unfeel: Randi chose Vee. Randi sent Pace to her, or at least Pace's pursuit arrives one chapter after "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I'm not sure Vee's good luck is only good luck, and I don't know if I'm supposed to feel that. I do, a little, every time he does something exactly right.

Erotically this is a low-simmer chapter — no bodies, hardly a touch — and it's charged anyway, because the whole dinner is seduction by feeding, and the feeding is described with exactly the attention the bench scene gave to spanking. The drip on his hand that she watches while losing the thread — that's the erotic peak, and it's a drip of baba ghanoush. Fine.

Friction: very little. If anything, the prose leans a little hard on the "she didn't notice X until she wasn't doing it" construction — she didn't notice she'd stopped watching herself eat, didn't notice she'd stopped waiting for it to go wrong, didn't notice noon — but the repetition reads as a pattern of her interior life rather than a tic, so I forgive it. The title: it's the menu line, and it's also, obviously, everything — Pace's whole manner is asking *may I choose for you*, and Randi's whole crisis in "The Bench" was about who chooses. Nice that the book doesn't underline it. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps reading truer and truer — politeness as the delivery system for something enormous — and the series title, *With a Long Spoon*, still means nothing to me except a faint feeling that whoever wields it shouldn't.

Next: I want the first kiss (which the chapter ends one breath before), and I'm bracing for the triangle to close — for Vee to discover, somehow, that Randi and Pace share a house and a bench. I dread it for Vee and want it for the book, which is the right ratio to keep me reading.
