## Reader reaction

There's a moment late in this chapter that sums up everything Pace is being built to mean: Vee has lost the thread of what he's saying — "She didn't, this time. The thread went and she let it go and nothing bad happened." That's the whole seduction in one line. This chapter is the first one told almost entirely through Vee's appetite, and I fell for it completely: "it went down into the hollow and the hollow only opened wider… down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the same hunger the book has been tracing since the thin-sliced apple — appetite as the thing every woman in this book has been taught to ration. Randi's appetite was rationed by a sorority scale and a mother's egg whites; Vee's by the mother in her head ("that's plenty, that's enough now"). Tonight nobody stops her. "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was" — and Pace "didn't seem to notice either, or to think there was anything to notice." That's the tenderness of this book at its best: the care is invisible, structural.

The swoon, for me, is the kiss at the end — and specifically *she initiates it twice*. He stops. He waits. "He left the last inch where it was." She says her rule out loud — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he simply believes her and lets it close, and then her body overrules her reflex. "But," she said, "I'm making an exception." That landed as the hottest beat in the chapter, and there's no heat in the conventional sense at all — it's the *permission* that's erotic: "let herself want it, longer than she'd have thought she could bear to. Then she leaned in and took it."

What I know that Vee doesn't keeps pulling at me. The reader knows "May I choose?" isn't Pace being considerate by accident — it's a technique, the same one that broke Randi open on the bench: hand the woman the yes, make her ask, make the wanting hers. He's done this before. The nine Arabic words, the right hand only, the "Finishing a PhD. More or less," the age she "set down without opening" — the text keeps leaving those doors ajar and I keep refusing to close them. So my feeling about Pace is genuinely double: on Vee's page he is the best man in the book so far, and on mine he's a man running a script I've already watched work on Randi. Both are true. That's what the book is doing to me and I don't resent it — I relish it.

The title landed before I read a line — *May I Choose* telegraphed the chapter's mechanism — but I don't think it spoiled anything; it *framed* it. Given everything with Randi, that "may" has a shadow behind it now. Volume title, *A Polite Invitation*: the phrase has stopped sounding polite to me.

What I dread and want next: Pace knows. He knows exactly what Randi has been doing (Tannin told us she's been curating Vee for him — the freckles, the buttons), and I cannot tell whether Vee is being courted or *recruited*. Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee" keeps echoing. That's my guess-from-here: the collision on the lawn, the sit-together, the coffee — all of it may have been staged, and I dread Vee finding out almost as much as I dread her not minding.
