# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. After the bench and the mirror and the locked bathroom door, after Randi's face going back on like armor, I needed this — and the book seemed to know it. This chapter is warm all the way through, and it's warm *at* Vee, and I felt every degree of it.

Pace, here, through Vee's eyes: I keep waiting for the two views of him to clash — the tender, patient craftsman Randi knows and the man who tells a woman on a bench "no" until her please stops being a performance — and they don't clash, they just sit side by side, and that's the unsettled hum this book leaves in me. But tonight, in this restaurant, with this girl, I only felt his care. The text he sends that doesn't need an answer. "May I choose?" — three words, and he *waits over the menu*, "not because he needed it but to weigh what he was choosing for her." That's the same man who sliced the apple thin because she'd eat it that way. He chooses for people the way other people give gifts. And Vee's answer — "Yes. Please" — is the same *please* Randi couldn't get right on the bench, arriving here easily, unbroken. I noticed that and it ached a little.

Vee is becoming my favorite person in the book. The hunger in this chapter is doing double work and I don't think the page is pretending otherwise: she doesn't eat all day, the hollow "opening in her since midday," and then the food goes "down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the bench's lesson taught in a different key — Randi's appetite portioned and policed, Vee's appetite so total she'd stopped registering it as hers. The moment she notices she'd "stopped watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was" — that landed hard, because we've watched Randi do the exact same calculation in the exact same register. These two women are starving in parallel, and Pace is feeding them both.

And that surrender. "The relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." Yes — that's heat. Not a hand on anyone, and I was warmer reading this dinner than half the explicit pages before it. The whole meal is a scene about trust built out of food and listening, and it works on me because the book earns every beat: he doesn't reach to explain the menu, he doesn't lean in for the kiss, he leaves the last inch "where it was" and she closes it. The kiss made me grin out loud — "the grin came up in her before she could do anything about it, and she went back for more with it still on her mouth, and felt him smile inside the kiss." That's the best beat in the chapter. Warm, specific, hers.

The right hand thing — "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept" — I noticed it and I'm holding it open. A first sighting or a pattern I can't decode yet. It's the one small itch in an otherwise seamless evening, and I think the page wants it to itch.

Cassie, briefly: "That's not a boy. That's a man." Cassie remains the person who says the true flat thing and nothing else. I love her and I worry about her a little — the "Goodbye, Vee" from last chapter still sits oddly with me.

Do I trust Pace? Here's the honest answer: in this chapter, entirely, because on the page with Vee he does everything right and the text earns my ease. In the wider book, no — not "no" exactly, but *held open*, because I've seen what his care produces and I've seen Randi lock a bathroom door. The pointing game came back to me at dinner more than once: Randi picked Vee, and now Vee is on a date with the man who pointed at her, and neither of them knows it. That's not suspicion of the text — it's dread I'm carrying from three chapters back, marked as mine.

The title: "May I Choose" — his question at the table, but by the end it's recolored. He asked it about dinner, and then spent the whole evening giving *her* the choices: the last inch, the kiss, the exception to her rule. And it echoes "May I call you?" from the coffee shop. The *may* is his grammar. I don't think the title gave anything away; it deepened on rereading. The volume title — *With a Long Spoon, Book One: A Polite Invitation* — reads truer every chapter: the politeness is real and it's also the mechanism, and a long spoon is what you need for a deep pot, and there's a pot here with at least two women in it.

Heat: this is a 2 for me — sustained, quiet, the wanting all through it, peaking at the kiss and at the taut hand between them under the tree. Romance: 3, no question — the hand-holding alone ("it felt less like something beginning than something resolving") is the most romantic sentence in the book so far.

What's next: the dread is Vee learning who chose her, and what the bench is for. Guess only.
