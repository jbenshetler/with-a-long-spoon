## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter. This chapter undid me a little, and I need to say first that the undoing has a knife in it, because I know something Vee doesn't, and the book is letting me hold both feelings at once.

The dinner itself worked on me completely. The whole middle stretch — the menu she can't read, "May I choose?", the little dishes whose names are "closed doors with something behind it" — that's the swoon of the chapter for me. I loved watching her stop watching herself eat: "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." After the water-wings chapter and the bleacher hecklers and the cardigan buttoned to the throat and Randi's apple calculations, that line landed hard. Vee's whole book so far is a body policed into ornament, and here is one evening where nobody — not her mother's voice, not the room, not even herself — is taking attendance. "It kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." Yes. That's the line I'd underline twice.

And the hand. "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe." I read that with my stomach dropped, in the good way and then immediately in the bad way, because I know what I know. The rule reflex at the tree — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — coming back up right at the worst moment, and then her feet refusing to go, and "But... I'm making an exception": that's her wanting something and *choosing* it out loud, rule overridden by appetite, and as a beat of character it's gorgeous. She's been led all evening in a dance she didn't know she was in, and at the end she takes the step herself.

But I can't unknow the bench. I can't unknow the pointing game. Pace pointed at a laughing redhead outside a dining hall and said "Her," and Randi went and engineered a collision, and now — this is a *guess from here*, but it's a strong one — Vee's stats class and this dinner are downstream of that afternoon. The chapter supports the suspicion without confirming it: everything Pace does here that melts me is the same instrument we watched him play on Randi. The patience. The "May I choose?" — I heard the echo of "May I call you?" and felt the chill of it even while my chest warmed. The detail that won't leave me alone is the right hand: "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." The page flags it as a pattern Vee notices but can't read. I can't read it either — I genuinely don't know what it means — but I know the book wanted me to see it, and I'm keeping it in my pocket.

So my feelings about everyone are complicated and rich right now. **Pace**: I like being across the table from him. He's warm, he's careful, the not-arguing at the tree was genuinely lovely. And I don't trust him further than I can throw him, not because he's done anything cruel but because I've seen him *aim* someone. With Randi the arc was "he found the one behind the face and it broke her"; with Vee the pattern reads as "he finds the want and feeds it." Feeding Vee — literally, food she didn't have to police — is the kindest version of that I can imagine, and it's still a man choosing for her, skillfully, with a purpose I can't see. **Vee**: my whole heart. She's so clearly starved — for attention that doesn't want to consume her, for a body allowed to be hungry. Her delight is unguarded in a way Randi's never is. **Randi**: she barely appears here except in Cassie's room at the start, and my feeling about her hasn't moved so much as deepened — the bathroom mirror, the face going back up, and now her invisible hand in Vee's fairytale. I feel for her and I'm braced for her. **Cassie**: remains the conscience. "That's not a boy. That's a man." She said it with admiration, and I wanted to grab her by the shoulders. Cassie is the only person in this book with clean eyes and no agenda.

Erotic charge: low and deliberate, and working. Nothing happens but the hand-hold, and the hand-hold *burned* — "too much for what it was." That's real heat to me, heat as tension, the wanting outrunning the act. The chapter runs on appetite rather than sex, and food is doing the erotic work — steam off torn bread, the drip caught off his hand and the way she "lost the thread" watching his mouth. That's charged on purpose and it got me.

Friction: almost none. The one wobble is that the dramatic irony is doing so much work that the chapter is almost *too* smooth — Pace is flawless in a way that, had I not seen the bench and the pointing game, might read as a fantasy of attentiveness. Because I *have* seen them, the flawlessness reads as characterization, not laziness — his perfection is the tell. If I didn't know what I know, I might have found him a touch too idealized. The title, "May I Choose," recolors on second thought the way the best ones do: first read as sweet, the tender surrender at the menu, Vee's relief at handing it over. Second read, it's his question, his line, the running grammar of him — *may I call you, may I choose* — a man who always asks, always means it, and always, always holds the frame. And it catches against Randi's "I point. You pick." Choosing and being allowed to choose are the whole engine of this book, and I don't yet know whose choice Vee's exception tonight actually is.

Volume title — *With a Long Spoon, Book One: A Polite Invitation*. I take the long spoon as the proverb's — he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon — and it chills me a little now; politeness as the devil's idiom ("may I," held doors) fits Pace's courtesy so exactly that the title reads as a warning label on everything I just swooned over. "A polite invitation" is exactly what this dinner was. I'm invited to keep enjoying him, and I will, warily.

Next: I want Vee happy and I dread the shape of her education. I'm guessing Pace and Vee's warmth is real *and* instrumental, and that the collision that introduced them will surface for Vee eventually — that's the detonation I'm reading toward. I dread Randi meeting this evening across a table she arranged. And I keep circling the right hand.
