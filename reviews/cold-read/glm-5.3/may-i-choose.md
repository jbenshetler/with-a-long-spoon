# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter undid me a little. I've been watching this book run two currents — the hot, ominous machinery of Pace and Randi, and then this quieter, sweeter line where Pace is courting Vee — and I half expected the dinner date to feel like the author's thumb pressing the scale: the same tricks, rehearsed, aimed at a new girl. Instead it landed on me soft and enormous, and I spent most of it with my chest aching in the good way.

Vee in this chapter is everything the pool chapter made her. "I'd love to it" — typed long, trimmed down, sent from a crosswalk — that's her exactly, the girl whose yes arrives whole and who then folds it to a survivable size. And the detail that got me most: she didn't eat all day, the dress and the other dress and Cassie delivering verdicts eating the afternoon, and then the hunger "went down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's the whole book in one sentence, and it echoes the apple — Pace feeding a girl who's been portioning herself. He's doing it again, and the text knows it even if Vee doesn't: "she wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." Randi's apple slices and Vee's tabbouleh are the same act. I swooned at that and it also chilled me slightly, because I know what he does with a hunger once he finds it.

Pace — my feeling about him is genuinely double now, and I think the book wants it that way. Everything he does here is impeccable: "May I choose?" asked, not taken; the one question about what she won't eat; the kiss where he eases back and waits twice, "the last inch where it was," and she closes it herself. As a reader who needs consent to be real, I believed every beat of the shadow scene — the reflexive rule coming out of her mouth ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"), him letting it close with no charm turned up, her feet staying put, the "But." That's the hottest thing in the chapter for me, hotter than anything on the bench, honestly — the wanting made hers. And yet. "I point. You pick," he told Randi, and that's what he's doing with Vee too, just at geological speed: creating the conditions, letting her think every step is hers. Whether the step being hers makes it true or makes it *better bait* — the book is holding that ajar and so am I. I noticed the right-hand detail filing itself without resolution: "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept." Vee thinks it's one more thing he knows; I think it might be one more thing he's *concealing*. I hold it open.

The loveInterestLine on Randi's side — I kept remembering "Tannin," her arriving hot to hear about "a girl," and now here's the girl, being fed and kissed and wanting. The dinner reads completely differently knowing what Randi knows. That's dramatic irony doing real work: Vee thinks she's on a first date; from Pace and Randi's side this is almost choreography. I don't distrust *Pace's* tenderness on the page — it reads unguarded, the Christine warmth, "I'm losing" — but I distrust the frame around it, because I've watched him engineer want before and call it finding.

Friction: very little. The only soft spot was the shadow/tree kiss scene telegraphing itself ("what came next was suddenly obvious") — but the text immediately complicated it with her rule, so it earned its way back. The hand-holding passage ("it felt less like something beginning than something resolving") tipped slightly purple for me, one breath past what the moment needed, but I forgave it because it's true to how a first hand feels at twenty.

Title: "May I Choose" — it's his question at the table, and it's the whole engine of the book. He asks it of her over a menu; he asked a version of it of Randi with "what about her?"; the pointing game was Randi learning she could choose. Everyone in this book is being offered choices that are also, quietly, being set up. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, keeps meaning more every chapter — "May I call you," "May I choose," the unlocked doors — the invitation is the seduction, and politeness is the instrument. Still uneasy, still swooning, in equal measure.

What I want next: I dread and crave the collision — Randi and Vee in the same frame again now that Vee has tasted the mint. Guess, from here only: Pace's "more or less" about the PhD, and the right hand, are the loose threads I'd bet get pulled.
