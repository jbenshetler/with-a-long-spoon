This chapter did something to me I wasn't braced for. After the heat of the bench chapter and the chess-game chill of the pointing game, this is the quietest, warmest thing the book has given me — and it swept me more than either of those. It's a dinner date chapter where nobody's body is exposed, and it's the most tender and aroused I've felt reading this book since the apple slices.

Pace on the page here confirms what "Substitution" began: the way he asks *May I choose?* — echoing "May I call you?" — makes those two words feel like a habit of soul, a whole ethic compressed into a courtesy. And the beautiful thing is what Vee does with it: "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." The book is quietly building a grammar of consent as eros — Randi's begging on the bench, and now Vee surrendering the menu — and I'm starting to feel that *the asking is the heat.* The kiss at the end is the payoff of the whole system: she states her rule, he genuinely lets it go, and then she breaks it herself. "She kissed him." He leaves the last inch hers. That's the hottest thing in the chapter and there's nothing below the collarbone in it.

The feeding echoes are everywhere and I'm sure they're deliberate-as-feeling: Randi's mother polices her appetite; Randi portions herself ("I can't eat that"); and here Vee eats *without watching herself eat* — "the hollow only opened wider… into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." Her body being "a problem" (the pool chapter) meets its counter-statement: an appetite fed, not judged. "She built a cup, like nobody had ever told her when to stop" — Pace said that to Randi; now he's the one not telling Vee when to stop. The right-hand detail is the one thing that snagged my suspicion — the text flags it deliberately ("some consistent rule he kept"), and I don't know what it means yet. Filed, not resolved.

Randi is offstage and yet hovering over everything, because I know what she and Pace are building Vee toward. That's where the unease lives: this dinner reads as pure, and I *want* it to be pure, but I know the scene underneath it. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" sits under every bite. The dramatic irony is starting to hurt — Vee thinks she's on a date; I think she's being courted for a design. That's my suspicion, and I want to be honest that it's mostly knowledge the book gave me earlier, not anything this chapter earned on its own. This chapter earned almost the opposite: the last man I'd warn her about.

I want the next chapter desperately and I dread it: Vee is falling for a script I've already seen the writer of.
