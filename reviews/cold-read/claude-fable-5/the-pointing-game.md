**Felt read**

The question in the middle of it — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and her coming *on the question*, before her mind could get anywhere near it: that is the hottest single beat this book has produced outside the bench, and it worked on me completely. I felt her helplessness in my own chest. And then the deflection, "Only in a threesome. With you," delivered against his throat, all silk — I understood it instantly because "Standards" taught me the grammar. The same act. A different owner. Pace narrates that move like a connoisseur admiring a chess defense, and here's the thing: I admired it too, and I also went a little cold, because I know something he doesn't. I was in that bathroom with her. I heard the deadbolt: *he can have the face. She is not coming out again.* So when Pace reads her body's answer as "the true one" — "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did" — I flinched. That's his whole method in one sentence, and after last chapter I no longer trust the method the way he does. Her body may be honest. But she has already decided what he gets, and he doesn't know a decision was made.

And yet — I have to be fair to the page — the chapter kept surprising me out of that wariness. The pointing game is *delightful*. The rhythm of it: too expected, both-or-neither, maybe, God-no. "I point. You pick" made me grin, because he isn't choosing a woman for himself, he's teaching her that she's allowed to want one, and watching her go from "good sport" to *shopping* — "she had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back" — was genuinely joyful to read. Her wanting is real. That's what disarms me. The bare "Yes" when Vee comes out of the dining hall, the dropped stride, the helpless staring she'd done at no one else all afternoon — the page insists that one wasn't performed, and I believe the page. Even the costume she puts back on ("Uh — yeah. Yeah. I like her") arrives visibly *after* the truth.

Vee herself: I fell for her in one paragraph, the way I was clearly meant to and don't resent. The head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — against Randi, who has never once forgotten it, that's devastating contrast. And it's exactly the being-seen axis the bench chapter ran on. Randi begged when Pace saw past the face; here's a woman who apparently lives unwatched. Of course Randi stared.

Then the collision. I want to name the swerve honestly: watching Randi engineer that "accident" so cleanly that *Vee* apologizes — "a decade of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually taught under the mixers" — thrilled me and unsettled me in the same breath. It's the first time we've seen Randi aim herself at a person, and she's frighteningly good. Vee has now been pointed at, chosen, bumped, and charmed, and knows none of it. Pace at his light pole, seeing all of it and hearing none of it, felt like the book showing me its own shape. And the last line — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — landed like ice in a hot drink. Whose want is that sentence carrying? A gift to him? A hunger of hers wearing his name again? The page leaves it open and I'm leaving it open, but it recolors the whole sunny walk: they just picked a stranger for the bench, together, glowing.

Small frictions: Pace's interpretive fluency occasionally over-explains — "because it was beautiful... The same act. A different owner" spells out what I'd already caught, and "If pleasure was also a bridge" made me pause, because that's the one place his gentleness shades into strategy on the page: seeding the want while she's undefended, then telling himself the real asking waits for daylight. He *did* wait, and he wanted her "free to weigh it and choose" — I believe his care. I also notice it serves him. The cheeseburger beat ("No. I can't eat that," and his closing "Let's go feed you") is the apple again, and it aches.

The chapter title is literal and perfect — the game *is* the chapter — and it gave nothing away I minded. The cover board, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* (no tagline or jacket in my packet), is doing more work now: sup with the devil with a long spoon, and I still can't tell who's the devil at this table — Pace, Randi, or the arrangement itself.
