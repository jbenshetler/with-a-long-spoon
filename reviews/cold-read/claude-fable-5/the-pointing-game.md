## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the chapter the jacket promised, and reading it arrive was a strange double experience — hot and cold at once. The opening in bed worked on me, plainly. Pace timing the question — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — so it hits her body before her mind, and her coming on the question itself, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — that's the most erotically effective beat in the book so far, and also the most sinister thing he's done, because he's using her orgasms as an interrogation instrument. The line that chilled me most is stated right out: "A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was... a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there." My whole locks-and-doors ledger just snapped into focus. Pace opens doors. Randi has no door left for grief (last chapter) — and he keeps installing new ones to walk through.

My feeling about Pace moved hard this chapter. In The Bench I was ambivalent — care that sees vs. proprietary plans. Now the proprietary side is winning. He tells himself the pretty version ("Pleasure was not a trick. It was a true thing he was giving her — and if it was also a bridge...") and I don't buy the "if." He asked the daylight question wanting her "free to weigh it and choose" — and then didn't tell her what the walk was for. That's the tell. "Every yes was freely given" — I can now see exactly how the trap works, and it's this: he engineers the conditions of the yes and then venerates its freeness.

And yet — Randi shopping. Watching her verdicts sharpen ("She'd be a project," "You'd get both or neither," "God, no") was genuinely delightful, funny even, and Randi surprised me with sympathy again: her deflection — "Only in a threesome. With you." — read out for exactly what it is, the appetite handed to him so she doesn't have to own it, "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable." That word *unsurvivable* is doing enormous work and it lands because I've watched her mother's food-policing and the closet of bone and camel. Her "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger — automatic, no glance — is the whole architecture in four words, and "Let's go feed you" as the closing line made my skin prickle. Appetite is the book's real subject; the sex is the vector.

Then the redhead comes out of the dining hall — out of the *dining hall*, laughing with her throat bare to the sky — and I knew before Pace said "Her." This has to be Vivienne. "Her name is Vee." Reading her described as everything Randi's grammar can't grade — abundance, unpinned hair, laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten... that she could be seen" — I felt actual dread alongside the pleasure of the writing, because the jacket already told me what these two are going to do to her, and the engineered "collision" (Randi's dance-trained shoulder-bump so clean "the redhead had walked into *her*") is the first move of the con, executed on the page while I watched. The redhead apologizing for it broke my heart a little in advance.

Friction: almost none, though Pace's interiority occasionally over-explains a beat I'd already felt — "the flinch was the sound of the catching" is one clause past necessary. And I noticed the POV never wavers from Pace; Randi is entirely read from outside, which is the point but also kept me at a slight cool remove during the walk.

The title: "The Pointing Game" is exact and, on reflection, awful in the right way — "I point. You pick" dresses predation as generosity, and Randi *laughed before she could decide whether to*. The book title's long spoon (sup with the devil) now has a face; the question is which of these three is the devil, and I'm no longer sure it's only Pace.

What I want next: Vee's own eyes, desperately. My guess from here — next chapter or soon we get the stats class, Randi's approach as "best friend," and I dread how good at it she'll be.
