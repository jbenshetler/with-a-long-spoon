## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well. There it is — the jacket's game, on the page, and I got to watch it assembled from inside the man doing the assembling. My first sustained time in Pace's head, and it's genuinely destabilizing, because he's *pleasant* to be inside. Attentive, patient, generous, funny ("I point. You pick."). And also this: "A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was, by some old architecture he did not examine, a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there." That sentence made my skin prickle more than any of the sex. He doesn't examine it. The narration told me he doesn't examine it. That's the scariest thing in the book so far — the bench was built by a man who doesn't look at his own blueprints.

The erotic charge is real and high in the opening — the question "Would you like to taste another woman?" detonating an orgasm out of her before she can process it is the hottest and cruelest beat yet, and he *names* the cruelty: "It was not fair to ask it now, and he knew it was not fair. He asked it anyway." I keep being seduced by his honesty about his own dishonesty. The line "if it was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her" — he's rationalizing, and half-believing it, and I half-believe it with him, which I resent.

Randi broke my heart a little in the reframe: "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner." That's the same machinery as the cheeseburger — "No. I can't eat that," automatic, no glance back. Her wants are only permitted when relabeled. Pace sees this perfectly and uses it as his frame. "He let her have the frame" — kindness and exploitation are literally the same gesture here and I cannot separate them, and I think that's the point of my discomfort.

The pointing walk itself was a delight to read — I'm ashamed to say it, it's a predatory catalogue and I was *shopping along with them*. Each rejected girl taught me Randi ("Not the closed ones... not the ones who ran the room"). And then Vee's entrance is the best writing in the book so far: "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Given everything this book is about performed surfaces — Randi's arranged smile, Brooke's perfume-voice — a woman who forgets she can be seen is the exact opposite of everyone we've met. That's why Randi's bare "Yes" lands so hard, "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it." Randi's most honest word in three chapters, and it's the one that dooms Vivienne.

Because that's what soured in my stomach at the end: the engineered collision. Vee's *own hand flying to her mouth*, apologizing for a bump Randi manufactured — the mark apologizing to the con. And Randi comes back "glowing," doing "happy footwork she didn't decide to do." Her realest joy so far, and it's built on a stranger's ignorance. "Let's go feed you" as the closer, from him, after she refused the cheeseburger — he's feeding all her appetites now, and I don't know which of them he's farming.

One tiny friction: "the girl was never who he was watching, Randi was" — I'd already understood that; the line underlines it. Small thumb on the scale. Also I don't strictly *believe* the mid-coitus orgasm from a sentence alone, but the chapter sold it emotionally so I let it go.

The title gave itself away only in retrospect — "The Pointing Game" reads innocent until you've watched it; now "game" carries the jacket's full freight ("It began as a game"). And the book title clicked for me this chapter: sup with the devil with a long spoon. Pace keeps his distance — the light pole, "far enough back to be nobody" — while Randi does the touching. Who's holding the spoon, though? Every yes here *was* freely given: Randi's "Yes," her orgasms, her walk across the lawn. And every one was elicited by a man who staged the conditions. The tagline is no longer abstract.

What I want next: Vee's point of view, badly. I've only seen her through the hunters' eyes and she was the most alive thing on the quad. *Guess from here:* the stats class is the vector; Randi will "coincidentally" befriend her, and the friendship the jacket promises ("listens like no one ever has") will be real and instrumental at once — same act, different owner, again.
