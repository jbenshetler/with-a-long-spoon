## Reader reaction

The chapter did the thing I least expected after "Standards": it made me grin. And then it made me check the grin, because I know something Pace doesn't, and this whole chapter is built on him not knowing it.

Start with the heat, because it starts there. The bedroom scene is short and it detonates. "Would you like to taste another woman?" — asked while he's inside her, while she's "still pulsing around nothing" — and she *comes*, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That beat floored me. An orgasm as involuntary confession. It's the hottest thing in the chapter and it isn't even an act; it's a question. And then her recovery — "Only in a threesome. With you," all silk against his throat — I watched her do the move he names: take the appetite and change its owner. Except: I was in that bathroom with her two chapters ago. I heard the deadbolt go over — *not tonight, not ever, he can have the face.* So when Pace narrates, so fluently, what she's doing and why, I no longer fully trust his readings. "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That *usually* sat in me like a splinter. Her body keeps telling him the truth — the bench proved it can't lie — but the woman running the body has decided he gets the mask from now on, and he thinks the mask came off for good. "It was the best idea he'd had in a month" landed on me like a stone dropped down a well.

And yet — the swoon is real and I won't pretend otherwise. The walk is gorgeous. The one maple gone over early, the wind off the ridge, her scrunchied tail "flirting with the wind when she turned her head and giving it nothing when she didn't" — that sentence is Randi entire. And the game itself swept me up: his patience, "I point. You pick," her laugh escaping "before she could decide whether to," the moment he feels her stop being a good sport and start *shopping*. The kiss on the path with her own taste still on his mouth — "Maybe," he agreed, mild — made me exhale audibly. This is the courtship-of-the-hidden-want material I read for, and it's played warm, unhurried, consent threaded through it in Pace's particular way: he waits for the afternoon to go ordinary before asking the question in daylight, wanting her "gathered back into herself... free to weigh it and choose." I believe he means it. That's what makes it hurt.

Then Vee. The entrance is a full-page aria and it earned it: the copper hair nothing pinned, the freckles, and above all the laugh — "throat bare to the sky... like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." She is the anti-Randi: uncontrolled where Randi is curated, unwatched where Randi is nothing *but* watched. Randi's bare "Yes" — one word, "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — before the girlfriend-costume comes back up, is the chapter's second confession, and it moved me more than the first.

But the collision. God, the collision. I watched it with Pace's delight and my own skin crawling slightly, and I think both responses are the page's. The staged accident is described with the loving precision of craftsmanship — "execution so clean it looked like nothing" — and what it *is* is a trained instrument being aimed at an unsuspecting person for the first time. Vee apologizes for being walked into. Vee has a stats class with her now. Vee knows nothing. The book's cover board says **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, and standing on that lawn I finally felt both halves: sup with the devil with a long spoon — and I just watched a polite invitation get manufactured out of thirty feet of geometry. Who's the devil here, though? Pace, with his benevolent certainty? Randi, hunting behind the face? I genuinely can't fix it, and I don't think I'm meant to yet. No tagline in my packet beyond the board line, so that's all I have — and it's plenty ominous now.

The chapter title, "The Pointing Game," is almost named on the page ("He had no name for the game yet") — it tipped me slightly that a game of pointing was coming, but not at whom or for what, so it read as apt rather than spoiling.

Small notes: her "No. I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger, automatic, closing with his "Let's go feed you" — the feeding thread from the apple chapter is alive and it aches. And her ending grin, the happy footwork "she didn't decide to do," the daylight "*Yes*" dropping into the bed-register — that joy read *real* to me, which is the confounding thing: the deadbolt is thrown and the wanting is still escaping through the cracks. I don't know which one is winning. Neither does she, I think.

What I dread, marked as a guess from here: that Vee — warm, unguarded, laughing at the sky — is being walked into something neither of these two fully understands they're carrying. I want the next chapter now.
