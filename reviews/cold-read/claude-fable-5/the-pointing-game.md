## Reader reaction

I have no suspicion left about what kind of book this is, and this chapter is why. After two chapters spent almost entirely inside one bedroom and one bathroom mirror, "The Pointing Game" walks out into daylight — and the daylight turns out to be more dangerous than the bench.

The opening pages are the hottest thing in the chapter and among the hottest in the book so far: him sliding into her before she's done, "still pulsing around nothing," the question asked into "the warm disorder of her" — and then her body answering it with an orgasm nothing mechanical can account for. That landed on me exactly as it landed on her: ahead of thought. "She had wanted it, and her body had said so before she could make the wanting mean anything safer" — I believed every word of that, and I felt the heat of it. And then, immediately, the line that put a cold finger on my spine: "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That's the whole book in eight words, and I don't know yet whether it's wisdom or hubris. After "Standards," I know something Pace doesn't: I watched her decide, quiet as a deadbolt, that he could have the face and the one he found was never coming out again. He thinks her "only in a threesome — with you" is a beautiful costume he's graciously letting the wanting wear. I think it's the face doing exactly what she promised the mirror it would do. Both might be true at once, and that's what has my stomach tight.

Because here's the thing — her resolve is already leaking. The bare "yes" when Vee comes out of the dining hall, "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it"; the dropped stride; the "pure helpless looking"; the happy footwork she didn't decide to do. The chapter kept showing me the real Randi breaking through the managed surface, and every breach thrilled me and worried me in the same breath. The engineered collision was a genuine delight to read — "the accident appeared entirely to happen to her," and it's Vee's hand that flies to her own mouth. I grinned at that. Then I remembered I was watching a predator's geometry taught by a decade of dance, aimed at a laughing stranger, and the grin got complicated.

Vee. One paragraph and she's the most alive person in the book — throat bared to the sky, laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." She is everything Randi polices out of herself: abundance unapologized for, hair given to the wind, a body "chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task." Of course Randi's taste, hunted through all those rejected mirrors and matched pairs and room-runners, lands there. That progression was the chapter's quiet masterstroke — I learned Randi's actual desire alongside Pace, one "no" at a time, and the "no"s taught me more than the yes did.

My unease with Pace has sharpened, and the text earned it. He's tender, patient, genuinely wants her free to choose — "that mattered more to him than the answer did" — and I mostly believe him. But he also asked the question while she was "undefended," having "decided this beforehand," and he narrates her interior with a confidence I now know was wrong once already: he left "The Bench" thinking she'd met more of herself, while she was locking the door forever. His generosity and his engineering are the same gesture, and I can't pull them apart. I'm not sure he can either.

"Let's go feed you" closed the chapter perfectly — the apple, the thin slices, "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger. He keeps finding her hungers and answering them. The question the book keeps not asking out loud: what happens when an appetite this long-starved is finally fed?

The chapter title is literal and mildly self-announcing — I knew a game was coming before the game had a name, though "He had no name for the game yet" made it feel discovered rather than telegraphed. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* (no tagline or jacket copy in what I was given) — is recoloring fast: sup with the devil with a long spoon, and this whole chapter *is* a polite invitation, engineered to look like chance. I still don't know who the devil is. Pace, for how surely he arranges other people's wanting? Randi, behind the face? I'd rather not know yet.

What I dread, as a guess from here: Randi pursuing Vee wearing the girlfriend-gift costume, wanting her for herself, with Pace certain he can read every register of it — and someone genuine walking into the middle of two people who are each, differently, performing. I want the next chapter now.
