## Reader reaction

The moment Pace asked it — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and she came, hard, before her mind could get anywhere near the question, I actually put my hand over my mouth. That is the single most electric beat in this book so far, and it's electric precisely because I've been in her head and he hasn't: I watched her stand at his bathroom mirror and decide, *quiet as a deadbolt going over*, that he could have the face and never her again. So when her body answers ahead of her — twice, the orgasm and then the bare "Yes" on the quad, "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — I'm watching the one thing she cannot deadbolt. The wanting leaks out under the door. It's thrilling and it hurts a little, both.

And here's the disjunction the chapter runs on, and I felt it the whole way: Pace thinks the game is working. He's tender about it, careful about it — he waits for the heat to leave the room before he asks for real, he wants her "gathered back into herself... free to weigh it and choose," and I believe he means that. But he reads her flinch as the want showing itself, and I know the flinch has a sibling — the flinch when he called her beautiful on the bed — and I know what she resolved after it. "He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." That *usually* sat in me like a stone. He's not wrong about her body. He may be very wrong about what she's going to do with him while it speaks. Her "Only in a threesome. With you" is the mirror-woman in action — the narration even names the maneuver, "The same act. A different owner" — and he sees through it and calls it beautiful and accepts the costume. He thinks accepting the costume is generosity. I'm no longer sure the costume ever comes off. The page left that ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but the gap between his happiness and my knowledge is the widest it's been.

The pointing game itself swept me up more than I expected. The walk is gorgeous — the one maple "gone over all at once, a single tree burning orange in a field of things that had not yet decided to change," which is doing exactly the work you think it's doing and earned it anyway. And Randi coming alive as a *chooser* — "I point. You pick," and her laugh before she could decide whether to — that's the swoon here. Watching her stop being a good sport and start shopping, feeling for the next point before he gives it. Then Vee: head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." I fell for her in one paragraph, which means I fell exactly where Randi fell, which is the chapter doing its job on my body.

But the engineered collision cooled something in me, and I think the page meant it to. Randi aiming a decade of training at a stranger so cleanly "it looked like nothing" — the redhead apologizing for a collision that was done *to* her — is dazzling and predatory in the same three seconds. Vee has no idea she was pointed at, weighed, acquired. And then the last line, "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — delivered low, against his chest, already putting a girl she's spoken forty words to facedown on the furniture. It's hot. It genuinely is; the register she says it in got me. And it assumes a yes that nobody has asked for yet. That's my unease, and it's earned on the page, not manufactured: this book has been scrupulous about Randi's consent and has just introduced a person who hasn't been offered any yet.

One small friction: Pace's narration occasionally does my reading for me — the full decode of her threesome maneuver ("she had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument") is so complete it left me nothing to work out. It's in character for him, but I felt the explanation land a half-beat after I'd already gotten there.

The chapter title named the game before Pace did — he "had no name for it yet," but I did, so a little was given away; harmless, though. The volume title finally clicked for me this chapter: *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, keep your distance — and *A Polite Invitation* now looks like it's walking up the path with a canvas bag and copper hair. Who the devil is, I honestly can't say yet: the man who builds benches, the girl behind the face, or the wanting itself. Guess from here: Vee says yes to something before she knows what she's saying yes to, and Randi's ownership shuffle — *his* appetite, *their* transgression, never hers — is going to cost someone, possibly Vee first.

I want the next chapter now. That's the truest thing I can report.
