# Cold read — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well, there it is. Chapter three, and the machine the jacket promised finally starts turning — and I'm unsettled by how much I enjoyed watching it turn.

Body first: the opening sex worked on me, hard, and the peak isn't the mechanics — it's the question. "Would you like to taste another woman?" landing in her body before her mind, making her come "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — that's the hottest and scariest thing in the book so far, in one gesture. Hot because it's true to how wanting works; scary because Pace is *reading* her the way you'd read an instrument. And then the line that stopped me cold: "A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was, by some old architecture, a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there." My motif ledger lit up like a switchboard — doors, doors, doors. Chapter one was locks and thresholds; chapter two ended with Randi's grief having "no door." Now Pace declares himself, in his own POV, as a man constitutionally incapable of leaving a closed door alone. I believe him and I'm afraid of him.

This is my first sustained time inside Pace's head, and my ambivalence has sharpened into something more specific. He is genuinely tender — the forehead kiss, "for no reason but that he wanted to," the insistence on asking the real question "in daylight" when she's "free to weigh it and choose." I don't think he's lying to himself about wanting her to have a clear yes. But then: "did not tell her what the walk was for." He values her consent to each *step* while withholding the shape of the staircase. The cover tagline is doing its work on me now — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." I can see the trap being built in real time, plank by plank, and every plank is a kindness.

And Randi — oh, Randi. The reframe ("Only in a threesome. With you") is the best character beat yet, and Pace's decoding of it is devastating: "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner." That's the same woman who can't cry because there's no door, who keeps one oxblood item in a bone-colored closet. Her wanting has to be laundered through someone else's ownership to be survivable. And Pace *lets her keep the costume* — calls it "the kindness" — which is either the kindest or the most predatory thing in the chapter and I genuinely can't decide, which I suspect is the point of the whole book.

"I'm hungry." "Do you want a cheeseburger?" "No. Easy, automatic... I can't eat that." — that little exchange gutted me, with the mother's food-policing from chapter one behind it. And the closing line, "Let's go feed you," recolors it instantly: is he going to feed her the meal she can't allow herself, or is Vee the meal? Both. That's a nasty, perfect last line.

The pointing game itself — I felt the queasiness Randi felt at first ("the bald social wrongness of it") and then, exactly like her, I got seduced by it. Each rejected candidate teaches me her taste by negative space: not the mirror-image sister, not the sealed pairs, not the closed projects, not the performers. And then Vee — Vivienne, has to be, "Vee" — arrives and the prose goes up a register: "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." After two chapters of women who are never *not* performing being seen, that description is a thunderclap. Of course Randi's "Yes" comes out bare, "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it." The engineered collision — Randi's craft so clean "the redhead had walked into *her*" — was gorgeous to watch and awful to know about. Vee apologizing for a collision that was done *to* her: that's the whole book in miniature, isn't it. The jacket told me she'd never know there was a game. I just watched the game's first move, and she said sorry for it.

Friction: minor. The candidate parade ran maybe one candidate long (the blazer girl felt like the taxonomy completing itself rather than a needed beat), and "he was, by some old architecture" edges toward the author explaining Pace to me. But the length mostly earns itself, because Randi's shift from "good sport" to "shopping" needs the mileage.

Titles: "The Pointing Game" is exactly what it says and still recolors on landing — because the game's rules turn out to be "I point. You pick," which means the title is about Randi being taught she's allowed to choose, under cover of a man's game. And *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon" — I'm now watching three people sit down to that supper, and I honestly can't tell yet which of them is the devil. Maybe the wanting itself is.

What I dread/want next: Vee on the page as a person, in her own right, before the machinery closes on her. Guess from here: the stats class is the vector, Randi befriends her "innocently," and — per the jacket — the falling gets real and the frame Randi built ("for him, with him") won't survive it. Also carrying forward: Randi still hasn't decided "what she was going to do about this" from chapter one. I think the pointing game *is* what she's doing about it — she's found somewhere to put the unbearable wanting. That can't hold.

**Story so far**
Ch.1 (The Bench): Randi, ~3 weeks into a secret affair with Pace, lied to Brooke to visit his isolated house. He revealed the restraint bench he'd built for her; consensually (safeword "red") spanked/edged her past her scripts to raw orgasm and tears — "There you are, Randi." Aftercare; she locked herself in the bathroom, faced the mirror, reassembled; resolved to "figure out what she was going to do about this."
Ch.2 (Standards): Next morning, she returned to the sorority in last night's clothes; Brooke ran a warm seamless audit (paper, location, his name, math PhD, "not his section?"). Alone in her room: sink run unused, phone face-down, lay under the inadequate duvet, could not cry — "there was no door."
Ch.3 (The Pointing Game): Some days later (now four weeks in), a Tuesday. Mid-sex, mouth still wet from her, Pace asked "Would you like to taste another woman?" — she came instantly from the question; the wanting is real and forbidden. Afterward, asked in daylight, she flinched, then reframed: "Only in a threesome. With you." He accepted the frame as exactly the vehicle he needed. Walked her onto the quad without saying why; began pointing at women — "I point. You pick." She rejected the mirror-type, the sealed pair, the closed project, gave a "maybe" to a warm open girl (he kissed her, her own taste on his mouth), rejected the room-runner. Then a red-haired, lushly built, whole-body-laughing girl came out of the dining hall; Pace pointed; Randi stopped mid-stride, stared, said a bare "Yes," then dressed it as girlfriend-generosity. "Go meet her." Randi staged a dancer's-precision collision so clean the girl apologized; two minutes of talk; Randi returned glowing: "Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together." He: "Let's go feed you."

**How I feel**
Randi: deep sympathy plus new fascination — her forbidden wanting is real, her survival mechanics (reframing ownership) are heartbreaking, and her competence (the collision) is chilling. Pace: first interior access made him more likable and more frightening simultaneously — genuine tenderness, genuine care for her consent, and a stated compulsion to open every closed door; he engineers while withholding the design. Vee: instant protective tenderness — the only person in the book who laughs without managing it, and she just apologized for the trap's first move. Brooke: still wary. Mood: seduced and complicit — I enjoyed the game as it happened and feel implicated for it; dread and appetite in equal measure heading into whatever the stats class becomes.
