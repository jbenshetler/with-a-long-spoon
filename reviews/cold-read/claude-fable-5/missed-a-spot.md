# Cold read (grounded) — missed-a-spot

## Reader reaction

The morning after the coat, and the book gives me the thing I didn't know I was waiting for: Vee alone in that house, naked by circumstance and then by choice, and *happy* — happiness rendered so specifically (the acreage of the bed, the bread timed to her waking, "even his absence had been cooked in advance") that I spent the first third of this chapter just grinning. And then it takes the happiness somewhere I didn't expect, and leaves a grain of salt in it, literally, and I'm still turning it over.

**How I feel about everyone.** Vee has never been more herself. The pleasure-slave → pleasure-princess reversal, counted off on her fingers barefoot in his hallway — *he brought her off first, always; he burned toast because she existed* — is the girl from the cardigans doing arithmetic on her own worth and getting a right answer for once. And the sink scene: "Missed a spot," the finger on the phantom smudge, the breast along his cheek — a deliberate callback to the riser, where she once did it and he held still, except now *she* runs the game. She wags a finger at a man whose hands have already closed on her waist and he *obeys*, puzzled, "outranked, and not yet informed of the new order." I cheered. She learned "nothing was the whole trick of it... in a doorway, two nights ago" — she is metabolizing her own victories now, building on them. That is growth I can feel in my chest.

Pace — my half-trust holds, and gained a degree and lost a degree in the same hour. Gained: the way he takes her "no." She sputters "Ugh — gross" mid-kiss, the child's hand across her mouth, and he goes still, reads *which no was which*, wipes his mouth in one economical pass, and gives her jaw a low "Okay" — no sulk, no push, no explanation, and afterward he never once returns to her mouth until she comes and takes it herself. That is the most trustworthy thing a lover does on a page and it made me ache. Lost, or at least shadowed: Vee's own late-night question, the one that "would not lie flat" — *why does he like it so much.* And her sleepy, tidy self-soothe — he must have forgotten to wipe, *this once* — with the darker version glowing under it: "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been —" and it slides away from her. It did not slide away from me. A man that exact does not forget things. Either every after-kiss for months has been carefully wiped clean — managed, like everything in that house — or she has been tasting herself all along and never knew. The page holds it open and I'm holding it open, but I felt the cold spot under the warm blanket.

Randi appears only in the last paragraph and it still tightened my stomach. "She would tell her everything. Well. Nearly everything... Randi would know what to make of it. Randi always did." Vee is about to carry this strange small live thing — her mouth's own curiosity, the taste her tongue went hunting for *unsent* — straight to the woman who hands her plays like a queen lending a sword. Given what I know Randi wants, and what Randi and Pace once named this whole project, the thought of Randi getting her hands on *this* particular confession made the hair on my arms move. Vee felt nothing but anticipation. That gap is the whole book.

**Swoon.** Enormous, and I'm not embarrassed about it. The breakfast is one of the most seductive sequences in the book and there's no sex in it: the blood oranges done "the way he did everything," rind and pith falling away "until nothing white was left... nothing between anybody and the fruit" — reader, I saw what the page saw ("he was opening her as he had opened the orange," the chapter says it outright later, and it *earned* saying it). The grains of salt on the persimmon "waking the fruit up." Her tongue, educated by "a fall of his table," reading the mushrooms in order. And the small perfect things: the sugar bowl he keeps full purely to make a face over; the cashmere blanket that "simply appeared," which is how he gives gifts — no moment where you had to say thank you — exactly like the drawer in the fall. I melted repeatedly and on schedule.

**Erotic charge.** Peak, sustained, and *doing work*. The tease at the table — banking his slipped glances "like earnings," the toast burning behind him — is playful heat; the bedroom is something else. The pointing game (wrist, arm, breast, *lower*) keeps her sovereign right up until "the finger was not steady this time, and both of them knew it, and it was not a command now and both of them knew that too" — the exact line where performance and wanting stop being distinguishable, which the walk down the hall had already promised ("somewhere in those few yards she stopped being able to tell the performance from the wanting"). The oral scene is the hottest this book has run for me, and the hottest part is the old wire firing: "the cringe and the heat one arrival, indivisible... There was so much of her. There always was" — and he doesn't pardon it or wait for it to thin, "He was drinking it." Shame arriving *as* heat, on the page, explicit and tender at once. And then the taste — her tongue reaching "before any part of her had ruled on it... curious, unforbidden, hers," then the installed voice ("no, that's wrong, that's not a thing you do" — the mother's wire, unmistakably) slamming down over a yes her body had already said. And then, unsent, her tongue going hunting for it again, and the orgasm breaking her open mid-kiss "out of nowhere." That sequence is psychologically the truest thing the book has done with her shame since the fitting. It worked on me completely.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Of Vee, none — she's transparent to me and to herself in about equal, heartbreaking measure. Of Pace, the suspicion is the one the *page* planted: the carefulness question. I want to be honest that my mind flickered, for half a beat, to something worse — a taste "that had not been there this morning," and I know who else has a key to this man — but the page doesn't support that today; he was at a farmers market at dawn buying food for her, and the salt her tongue found is almost certainly her own. I'm naming the flicker and setting it down. The real planted thing is smaller and better: how much of this house's perfection is *maintenance* she's never seen the seams of. "Nothing in this house happened at an accidental hour" was a swoon-line in paragraph six and a suspicion-line by the end. That's the chapter's trick and it's a good one.

**Friction.** Almost none. If I strain: the one authorial nudge I felt was the italics spelling out the buried question — "*Why does he like it so much*" — where the scene had already made me ask it; I didn't need it underlined. And "a gladiator, once. Her beauty had turned him" made me snort — but it's *her* purple thought, mid-float, and the book knows it's ridiculous ("the sentence had lost its verbs" a paragraph later), so it's characterization, not a miss. One tiny delight against an old motif: she's "never once heard it start" — the music — and here she watches him thumb it going. Even the house's magic is showing its hands now that she lives inside it.

**The titles.** "Missed a Spot" is doing three jobs and I admire all of them: the flirtation at the drying rack (her invented smudge, the oldest trick and the best), the wiped mouth ("he must have forgotten to wipe. This once."), and — the one that lingers — the spot *she* keeps missing: the question that "settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting." It didn't give anything away going in; going out, it recolors the whole chapter. As for the cover: *A Warm Reception* has never been more literal — a warm house, bread timed to her waking, a table set — and never more double-edged, since the jacket told me the table was set *for* her before she ever sat down. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — hangs over this breakfast like every other meal, and this was the most beautiful meal yet. No tagline on the front; the blurb's closing line ("two games... she doesn't know there are three") is the thing whispering under that final paragraph about Randi.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the Tuesday dinner and I dread it in equal measure: Vee handing Randi the coat story *and* the taste story, and Randi — who loves her and steers her, and I still can't find the seam between those — deciding what to make of it. My guesses from here: Randi will do something with the taste confession, gently, in the direction of Vee's unnamed want for women; and the "how carefully" question will come back — this book does not plant a minor-key note and let it rot. I also can't stop thinking about the emptied orange husk "wrung out in his fist at the end like a rag." Probably just breakfast. Probably.
