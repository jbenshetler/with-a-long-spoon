## Reader reaction

This chapter did the thing this book keeps doing to me: it took a scene I'd already lived through from inside Vee — the shoe fitting, the thumb in the soft of her knee, the sound she hoped nobody heard — and played it back to me from the other side of the wall, and I went hot and cold at the same time and could not put it down. "I heard it," Randi says, riding him, and I felt that land in my own body before my conscience got a vote. That's the honest report. The scene of Randi narrating Vee to Pace mid-stroke — the black lace, the walk-around, "She let me" — is one of the hottest things the book has done, and it is built entirely out of a girl's trust being handled by two people she'd die for. Both of those are true at once and the chapter refuses to let me pick.

What moved most for me is Randi. She comes through that door *pleased and letting it show* — the page says outright she's never done that — and Pace, being Pace, "did not ask it anything." Her want for Vee has fully stopped being a costume. "I wanted to keep her" isn't performed; neither is the half-asleep "She's going to be so beautiful when she walks in. You wait," which lands almost like a gift she's making him, or making herself. And then the same mouth says "She's so easy, that girl. It isn't fair" — fond, chilling, both. I also can't stop looking at the moment she pulls his head up off her, hard enough to hurt: "Not like that. Not yet." After the bench, after the vow, I felt the ghost of *he can have the face, not that* in it — but the page doesn't say so, and she does come apart loudly enough by the end that I genuinely can't tell whether the wall is holding, moving, or being renegotiated in real time. I'm leaving that ajar because the chapter does.

Two quiet revelations rang like bells. First: "Three weeks in… he had lifted the top off and shown her what he had built" — so the bench night was *early*, three weeks into them, which re-places chapter one for me and makes everything since it longer and stranger than I'd assumed. Second, the folded clothes: she carries them to "the table against the wall and set it down on top… and it was not the look you give a table." She knows what it is now. She still lays her things on it. That one sentence did more to me than the whole sex scene.

And the machinery, God, the machinery. Yesterday I watched Randi tell Vee, kindly, finally: *not today, tomorrow night, go in sure, he won't text you.* Tonight I watch her hand Pace the other half: "Give her a day," said "as if the day were his to give." She choreographed both dancers and neither knows the other got instructions. What softens it — barely — is Pace noticing: "he set that down beside the rest." He's collecting pieces, the way he collected the untouched bowl. Meanwhile his own discretion is quietly heartbreaking: he won't tell Randi what Vee said at the bar because "those were his, and that was Vee's" — a man keeping scrupulous confidences inside a lie the size of a house. And "he would not text her. That was not a thing he had decided. It was only what he was" — I believed that completely, and it's exactly what Randi told Vee it would be. Whether that's Randi knowing him or Randi *using* him, I can't apportion.

Friction, honestly, almost none. "The tit goddess was generous with that girl" jolted me — but it's Randi's low register, the coarseness she only uncrates in that house, and I bought it. If anything snagged, it's how completely Vee's worst night has become their foreplay; not a craft complaint — the book means for that to sit wrong in my mouth while it works on me, and it does.

The title: "Space." It kept refracting. The space Vee wouldn't close in the dance frame; the space Pace insists on giving her ("she chooses it… or it isn't anything"); the Friday-shaped space Randi walks into and fills — Friday, which was supposed to be *Vee's* day. I even noticed, and can't un-notice, that "Pace" sits inside the word. It's an oblique title that got richer as I read, not a giveaway. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · Book Two: A Warm Reception* — keeps paying out grimly: every reception in this book is warm, that's the whole trap; the house heated before she arrives, the face that opens at the door, the "Oh, honey. Come up." No tagline in what I was given beyond that line.

What I want next is tomorrow night — Vee in the black dress and borrowed shoes at his door, both of us knowing the reconciliation was staged down to the day, and me rooting for it anyway. What I dread (guess from here): that the "us" Pace let himself picture at his table — Vee in a sheet, Randi in his shirt — is coming, and that Vee will walk into it dressed by one of them and made by the other, still the only person in the room who doesn't know what the furniture is for.
