## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one's pure warmth, and I noticed how gratefully I sank into it — girls at the good dining hall, gold light, fries getting stolen. After the hike and the retell to Cassie, this is the *third* pass over the same material (shirt, rain, cop, tree kiss), and instead of feeling repetitive it felt like watching a story calcify into legend. "She could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care" — that line is doing a lot, and I felt a small chill under the laughter. Vee is becoming the author of a Pace who is partly her own construction. Which is exactly what Pace and Randi did to her, from the other side. She's building him while they built her.

Meg — finally on the page, and I like her immediately: "ate like she had all night and let a sentence finish." And she's the one who lands the only real check in the chapter: "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think." That is the truest sentence anyone has said to Vee in twelve chapters, and it "didn't catch on anything." Third sensor stood down — Cassie twice, now Meg — except Meg wasn't stood down, she was simply glided past. That bothered me more than anything overtly sinister could.

New data on Pace: twenty-two, powerlifter, lifts then writes six hours. Fine — it explains the "built under the jacket" note, and it's characteristically disciplined. Vee's pride wearing it is sweet and a little painful to watch.

Then the phone, face-*up* this time (I noticed — hers have been face-down before), and the invitation. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." My stomach actually dropped a little, the good-bad way. I've been waiting for the house off six-fourteen since chapter one — that's where the bench is, where Randi is, where the whole machine lives. Vee reading a ten-word text a thousand times while her friends plan her hair, and I'm sitting here knowing the bench exists. Kayla's "He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes" is dead right and she doesn't know how right.

Erotic charge: mild but real — all anticipation, no contact. It works because I know what "two o'clock" likely means and Vee half-knows.

Friction: almost none. Small confusion — is this Meg the floormate with the boots from before? The description is fresh, like an introduction, and I couldn't tell if it's the same Meg or a second one. I'll hold it loosely. And "six weeks running" of sneaking into the athletic dining hall gave Vee a fun deviousness I hadn't seen — a nice touch, not friction.

The title: "What to Wear" — Kayla's scandal ("He didn't say what to wear") and Meg's closing question. Innocent surface, but against the russet dress, the flannel, Meg's boots — this book keeps dressing Vee in things chosen or given by others. And "not one word about what it was for" is the whole jacket tagline in miniature: an open door, no information, every step hers. *With a Long Spoon* — supping with the devil is getting very literal: she's finally going to his table.

Want next: the house. Badly. Dread: also the house. Guess from here — Saturday is the first bed, and something of Randi's will be in that house for Vee to see or miss.
