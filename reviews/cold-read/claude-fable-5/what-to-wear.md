# Cold read (grounded) — What to Wear

## Reader reaction

This chapter is sunlight — the gold dining hall, the stolen fries, three girls not getting up to go — and I spent the whole of it grinning with a stone in my stomach, because I know what house she's been invited to. That's the trick of my position in this book and it has never worked on me harder than it did here. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." Vee reads it a thousand times and her heart does something "quick and light and stupid, and she let it" — and I'm the one sitting on the other side of the table knowing that house has a locked front door Randi turns behind her, Steely Dan in the front rooms, and a bench disguised as a table that is "waiting for the next time." Kayla's joke — "He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes" — is the funniest line in the chapter and also the one that made my skin prickle, because Kayla is guessing and I am not.

How I feel about everyone: Vee is at her most winning here, and what wins me is how *big* she's allowed to be — "laughing with her whole body, loud and easy, the kind that made the next table look over and then want in." That's the exact quality Pace pointed at on the quad, the thing that made Randi say her bare "Yes," and seeing it in its natural habitat — among friends, unengineered — made me feel the theft that hasn't happened yet. I also loved learning she *has* this life: Kayla, Theo, a whole dining-hall ecosystem I'd never seen. The book had shown me Vee mostly as Cassie's roommate and Randi's project; this widened her, and it matters that she's not a friendless waif — she's a girl with options (Theo asked; she said friends; he waves) who is choosing this man freely. The jacket said nothing was taken she didn't hand over. This chapter quietly makes that claim more true and therefore more terrible.

Pace, in absentia, deepened in two directions at once. The powerlifter reveal — "You don't expect it off a man who does math for a living" — retroactively explains the forearms, the ease with a struck-dumb Randi over his lap, the "whole situation under there" Vee clocked on the trail. And "he writes for six hours and stops. 'My writing's no good after that'" is such a precise piece of the man: discipline as identity. Everything he does is measured, portioned, stopped at the right moment. It's what makes him wonderful on a trail and what makes the courtship feel machined. Meg's gentle "Everybody's different at the start… Sometimes they even are" is the one adult sentence at the table, and I love that Vee "let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." That's exactly right for her — and exactly the small alarm the chapter lets ring once and doesn't lean on.

One line snagged me in the best, most uneasy way: "Vee felt the smile arrive before she'd cleared it." That's Randi's vocabulary — the smile "arranging," always a fraction late, from the Brooke scene. On Randi that language means the mask; on Vee it means joy outrunning permission. Same mechanism, opposite valence. I don't know if I was meant to hear the echo but I heard it, and it made me ache: one woman's face works to hide her, the other's betrays her.

Erotic charge: low and warm — this is the retelling chapter, girl-talk as afterglow. The heat is all borrowed from the truck scene ("You kept his shirt." "He didn't ask for it back."), plus the flannel "folded on her pillow at this exact hour," which is quietly the most intimate image on the page. The charge here is anticipatory: the unexplained two o'clock, the door she's never been through. "She'd had every piece of him she'd been handed, and never once been through his door, and now there was a door" — that sentence is doing enormous work, because doors in this book are never just doors. Randi locks them; Daphne built one; Vee is about to walk through the one they both stand behind.

Friction: almost none. Kayla is an instant, complete person ("scandalized at a professional level" — perfect). If I squint, "That's not a message, that's a ransom note" is one banger too many from her, and the chapter is a low-stakes breather right after two low-conflict chapters (trail, then Cassie debrief, now a second debrief of the same date) — the Rye/What to Wear pairing means I've now heard the shirt story three times. But the third telling is doing something the second didn't: I watched Vee hear "the story getting better in the telling and did not care," which is character, not padding. Still, I'm hungry to get off the retell and into the house. Also noted: no Cassie, no Randi, not even by name in Vee's mouth beyond the story. Cassie's absence from the invitation flurry pinched a little — Kayla's claiming "we're doing hair Saturday" is Cassie's usual seat.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing new to suspect *in* this scene — nobody at this table has an angle, which is part of why it's so pleasurable. My standing dread is all offstage: was this invitation Pace's alone, or was it discussed on a Thursday I don't get to see? The text can't tell me, so I hold it open.

Titles: "What to Wear" is Kayla's cry on the surface, and it's also, I think, the russet dress — the silk "like the red of a brick" Vee set down on the rock last chapter and Pace pointedly did not pick up. A chapter about an invitation with no dress code, sitting right after a chapter where she confessed the one dress she wants and can't have — those two are talking to each other. (Guess from here: that dress comes back, possibly from him, and it will cost her something to take it.) And under both: this is a book where women dress *for* things — Randi's cream cashmere worn for Pace, Vee's cardigan over the better shirt — so "what to wear" is really "what self to put on to walk through his door." *A Polite Invitation* has never been more literal: eleven words, no explanation, and she's read it a thousand times. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — Saturday at two is the next yes, and I can feel her giving it already.

What I want next: Saturday. I want it and I'm braced for it. Guesses from here: the house visit is the consummation, or at least the threshold of it; she will see the "table"; and some object of Randi's — the perfume, a forgotten thing — is in that house waiting to be almost-found. I'll keep reading tonight if I'm allowed.
