## Reader reaction

The nachos are what I keep coming back to. Not the sex, not the fight — the chip going soft under its pile of bag-cheese while Vee realizes she can't get back to the woman who would have loved it. "He had ruined it for her, was the thing. Not just this." That sentence scared me more than anything in the chapter, because it's the whole book's cost sheet in nine words: every appetite he's opened in her has closed a door behind it. The awakening I've been rooting for all fall — the eating, the wanting, the taking — turns out to have a converse, and she just tasted it in a Chili's off 460.

And the fight — God, the fight is so humanly ugly and right. She has the word in her mouth, can't say it, and what comes out instead comes out "at the wrong size for the room." Then the false accusation: "You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail," when "some back room of her knew which of them had gone cold that night, knew who'd carried a pie up whose porch steps after — and she said it anyway, because she needed the line of it to hold." I winced and I believed every syllable. This is the interior book I signed up for: not a misunderstanding but a self-deception, performed knowingly, and then the second one on top of it — "she told herself the thing that let her go: that she had forgiven him." *Forgiven.* She awarded herself absolution for a fight she started with a lie. The title told me where that went before I finished the page.

Pace in the restaurant moved me in a way I didn't expect. The eyes slipping past her to the screen, the fork squared to the table edge, the flicker of irritation he drops his gaze to hide — and then his explanation: "Not the sound of it. The whole of it, all at once. In a room like that I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you." I believe him. The page backed him before he spoke — her cynical reading of his truck-thaw "didn't sit right even as she thought it." A boy who entered college at fifteen, who builds soundproofed rooms of attention one person wide — of course the roaring middle of a Chili's takes him apart. I felt real tenderness for him there, and "I'm listening now" landed on me almost as hard as it landed on her. And still: it is the second consecutive chapter in which she reaches for the word and he hands back something adjacent and warm. After "I love how good we are together," this is a pattern now, not a moment. She's noticed the pile she won't look at; I'm looking straight at it, and I know something she doesn't about what might be sitting at the bottom of it — a small pale woman with a white-gold band. That's a guess from here, but it's the guess: he cannot say it because of what it would make him with Randi still in his bed.

The sex is the best kind this book does — meaningful, not decorative. She wants his control to break, "for the want in him to get the better of him," and then it does: "he got over her and into her almost in one motion... like a man who'd run clean out of the strength to wait." I felt that give in my own chest. She finally has the thing no patience could counterfeit — him helpless, urgent, "shaking, undone, hers" — and the chapter's cruelest turn is that even that isn't the thing. "He'd given her his control and all of his body and every last thing but the one." Her sleepless accounting afterward — his spend in her, "marked all over with where he'd been," his, and unable to prove he's hers — is the ache the whole night was built to deliver. Her fear that this might be "just what a man could do to a woman when he was this good at it" brushes the actual truth from the wrong side, and I had to sit with that.

The title is exact and a little merciless: the word under the rug, the lie under the forgiveness, the pile she won't look at — and per the cover board, this is *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*. The jacket's line I carry — every yes freely given, that being the problem — keeps confirming itself: even her forgiveness tonight was freely given, and wrong. Randi doesn't appear once, and I felt her absence like a held breath. What I dread next is the word finally getting said — by either of them, to either woman — because I no longer believe it can be said honestly by him in any direction.
