# Cold read — Off Six-Fourteen

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This is a small chapter and I read it grinning, and then I read one line and went cold, and I want to be honest about the order of those two things.

The grin first: this is the sweetest register the book has hit — sweeter even than the dinner, because the dinner had Pace in it and this has only the girls. Vee talking "since the door," the brass-cage tea already being retold as a story, the spin that "nearly took out the closet door and was over before it was really a dance at all — the room too small to hold what she had to do with it." That's joy rendered physically and I believed every inch of it. And Cassie is a delight — the highlighter set down, the sock feet, the "flat dry floor" under the gladness. "How much did he give you to drink?" is exactly the right question and I loved her for asking it.

But the line that stopped me, and it's the emotional center whether Vee knows it or not: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." Reader, I nearly said it out loud: *that's the tagline.* "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." Vee stating her own free agency as the thing she most wants her friend to have — plain and certain and completely true at the level she can see it, and completely false one level up, where a pointing game and a staged collision and a coached opener sit that she's never heard of. The book let her articulate its own trap in her own proud voice. That hurt more than any dark scene could have.

And the second cold thing, the title thing: the whole address is "off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." Cassie flags it as a joke — "That's the whole address" — and it IS funny, and it is also the jacket copy verbatim: a man "who lives alone at the end of a long drive." The chapter's title points straight at the isolation and dresses it as banter. Vee counts her dossier "like plenty" — "I know where he lives and everything" — and she knows a road number. The hiking invitation is the first move out of public spaces: coffee shop, froyo counter, restaurant, and now a trail past where the road goes to gravel. My stomach registered that before my head did.

Trust and suspicion: nothing new against Pace on the page — "he'll have water and everything" and "real shoes" are, taken straight, considerate. That's the problem; everything about him takes straight. Cassie asks all the right questions (drink? name? troopers?) and gets satisfied by all the answers, which is what a good sensor does when the surface is genuinely clean. She's not disarmed carelessly; she's disarmed *correctly*, which is worse.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and right for the beat — this is afterglow, girl-to-girl, the kiss retold in the small quiet voice. The retelling itself has a little heat ("I pulled him back and I did it. Me.") but it's joy-heat, not sex-heat.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the "Is he rich?" beat runs a line long, but it plants a real question — I don't know if Pace has money either, and now I notice I don't. The chapter earns its brevity completely.

Randi: still nowhere. Three chapters now. Vee's happiest night gets retold to Cassie, not to Randi — and I keep thinking Randi will get her own retelling, offstage, in a very different register.

Book title: "with a long spoon" — sup with the devil — sits heavier every chapter the devil stays this kind.

What I want next: I both want and dread the hike — the first time Vee goes past where the road goes to gravel. Guess from here: either the hike chapter, or Randi finally back on the page to collect the debrief, and I honestly don't know which I fear more.
