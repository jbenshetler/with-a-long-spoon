# Cold read — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: water-wings*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter worked on me almost embarrassingly well, and the whole time a cold thread ran under the warmth, because I know what Vee doesn't. That's the book's trick and it's fully operational here: I got to enjoy the best first date I've read in a long while *and* feel sick about it.

The text itself is a small masterstroke of Pace's method: "he didn't ask her to answer that night... he'd built the quiet into it without being asked." Vee's aside — "The other ones always made you answer. Right then, on the phone... So they'd know where they stood" — told me more about her dating history in three lines than a backstory dump could. And Cassie, my beloved control group, delivers the chapter's most quotable verdict: "That's not a boy. That's a man." Cassie's approval scares me. If the honest sensor is charmed, Vee has no alarm left.

The lobby beat — Vee so absorbed in watching him that the elevator times out on her, "begging the doors for mercy" — was funny and tender and completely believable. Her reading of his clothes ("The boys in this building bought new shoes to look like they'd lived, and he just wore the ones that had") is Vee's flaw-hunting reflex finding *nothing to hunt*, which is exactly the danger.

The title lands mid-chapter and it's a knife. "May I choose?" — three words, asking permission to take over. It's the consent style I've been tracking since chapter one, and here it's applied to *food*, which has been the book's loaded currency all along (the apple, "feed you," the froyo system, the mothers' policing). He asks, she says "Yes. Please," and the relief of surrender goes through her "more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would be." That sentence is the whole novel in miniature, and it's an honest sentence — she notices the surrender, she just doesn't know it's engineered. The bright dish and the smoky one — "one to wake you and one to fold you under" — she even *sees* the design and reads it as care. It IS care. That's the trap. Tart on the bottom, again, in a new register.

The eating itself carried real charge for me — not sex, but the erotics of appetite unwatched: "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was." The hollow "that had been empty a lot longer than since noon" — the mother's voice territory again, and this time she doesn't even hear it silence; it's just gone. And then losing the thread of his talk and *letting it go and nothing bad happened* — for a girl whose whole life is "the small constant work of keeping up her end," that's the deepest seduction in the chapter, deeper than the kiss.

The right hand. "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." My stomach dropped a little there. Vee reads it as one more thing he knows that she doesn't — and I don't know what it means either, but the narration flagged it hard enough that I'm certain it means *something*, and I suspect it's a rule from his other life, the one with Randi in it. First time the book has waved something at me that I genuinely can't decode. It felt off in the good, earned way.

The hand-holding paragraph is the romantic peak and it frightened me most: "like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly, and was only now where it belonged." That's falling-in-love language, real and lovely — and it's happening to a girl inside a game she doesn't know exists.

Then the kiss, and the best structural move of the chapter: she says her rule — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and he *doesn't argue*. No lean, no wait. He smiles "like she'd told him something true and fine about herself" and starts them back. And that acceptance is precisely what makes her stop, pull the hand taut, and say "I'm making an exception." Every yes freely given. The tagline has never been more literal. He got the kiss by genuinely, visibly not needing it, and I can't even tell anymore how much of that is technique and how much is the man. Maybe that distinction is dissolving for him too — the jacket promised it would.

Friction: almost none. The mint-tea "surfacing" passage ran a touch long — the softness/warmth/low-and-warm register repeats enough times ("that low warm not-quite-here she still hadn't shaken") that I felt the author leaning on it. And where's Randi? No text, no debrief, no shadow. Her total absence from a chapter this important is itself loud — I noticed it and it made me uneasy, which may be the point.

Where's the long spoon? Sup with the devil, use a long spoon — I'm more convinced than ever that Vee is dining with someone she should be keeping at distance, and instead she just handed him the menu.

What I want next: the goodnight, or the debrief — I dread the Randi debrief. If we cut to Pace and Randi discussing this evening, this kiss, it will hurt exactly as much as the book wants it to. Guess from here: the right-hand rule will come back and Vee will learn what it means in the worst context.
