## Reader reaction

# Felt read

Well, that worked on me, and I'm not thrilled about what that means.

The chapter is Vee's first real date, and read innocently — which is how the page insists on being read, because there is not one whisper of Randi or the game anywhere in it — it's one of the loveliest date chapters I've read in a long time. The text at the top, "a man saying a thing he'd already decided and leaving all the rest of the room to her," is the whole method of the book in one sentence, and Vee names it as a kindness. Cassie's benediction — "That's not a boy. That's a man." — landed hard on me precisely because Cassie is my trusted instrument, the one character who reads people right, and she just co-signed him. Either Pace's care is real enough to fool Cassie at one remove, or it *is* real, which the jacket keeps promising ("Both are falling as hard as she is") and which I keep half-believing and half-refusing.

The title did something to me before I finished the first page. "May I Choose" — I assumed it would be Vee's line, her agency, the tagline's "every step is hers." Then it turns out to be *his* line, at the menu: "May I choose?" and her "Yes... Please." Handing the choice across the table. That's the tagline enacted in miniature — her yes freely given, and the relief of surrender framed as rescue. The chapter even tells me plainly what it's doing and I still felt the warmth of it: "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would."

What genuinely got me: the eating. The mother-voice thread pays off enormously here. "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was" — and he "didn't seem to notice either, or to think there was anything to notice." That last clause is Pace's signature move, the deliberate non-noticing that lets her stop performing, and I can't tell anymore whether it's grace or technique, and the book seems to want me suspended exactly there. Same with "the hollow only opened wider... something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That's not about hummus.

Chills, and not the good kind, at: "one to wake you and one to fold you under." Vee thinks it dreamily; I read it as the book winking at me about what's being done to her. Also "the right hand again... one more thing he knew that she didn't" — a pattern she's clocking without understanding, and neither do I, and it's filed. And the hand-holding beat is where the romance peaks and where my dread peaks at once: "less like something beginning than something resolving." Resolving toward what he arranged, is the sentence her POV can't finish.

The kiss ending is beautifully built. Her rule comes out — the old braced self — he honors it *instantly*, no lean, no charm, lets it close and starts walking. And that release is exactly what pulls her back. "I'm making an exception." Her feet stayed; her yes was freely given; that was the trap, says the cover, and I felt the trap close warmly around both of us.

Erotic charge: low-explicit, high-simmer. The shower/razor paragraph from across the lobby — "down to the part of him she'd only get close enough to smell" — is the horniest sentence in the chapter and it's about broadcloth and soap. The food is fully eroticized ("wanting things she had no names for, content for once not to know") and it works.

Friction: almost none. One thumb-on-scale moment — "the bright thing and the dark thing, one to wake you and one to fold you under" is a shade too legible as authorial signal riding Vee's dreaminess. And I noticed the chapter's total Randi-silence: Vee doesn't think of her once, not even to report the date. After ch.6's "you should go," that absence feels engineered — did Randi already know? Of course she did. The reader knows what Vee doesn't: this perfect evening had a production team.

*With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, I've long suspected — sits heavier after a chapter this sweet. The sweeter the meal, the longer the spoon should have been.

What I want next: I dread the debrief. Randi asking Vee how it went, or worse, Pace reporting to Randi in bed. *Guess from here:* the kiss gets narrated back to Randi and something in Randi doesn't like hearing it — the sipless cup, again.
