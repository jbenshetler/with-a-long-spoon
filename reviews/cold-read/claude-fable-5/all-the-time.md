## Reader reaction

The whole chapter is a brunch, and I came out of it feeling like something enormous happened — which is exactly the trick this book keeps playing on me.

First, the warmth, because it's real and it carried me: Vee at full beam is irresistible. "We have so much sex, Randi" — plain and helpless and laughing at herself — and then the confession underneath it, "Am I turning into some kind of — I don't know. Sex pervert?" I ached for her there, because I know exactly whose voice she's asking permission against. And Randi's absolution is the best thing she's ever said: "You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*, and now you're not, and you've gone and decided that's the flaw. It's the least flawed thing about you." That's true. That's the truest thing anyone has said to Vee in thirty-odd chapters, and it came out of the mouth of the person running her. I felt both things at once and couldn't pick, which is this book's whole method by now.

Then the crack in Randi. When Vee lands on "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around," and Randi's eyes go bright and unfixed and her breath catches — "It doesn't," low, and not quite even — I went cold with tenderness. Vee reads it as her story being good. I read it against the bathroom mirror in chapter one: Vee just described, artlessly, the exact thing Randi decided Pace would never be allowed to find in her again. She's hearing the life she locked herself out of, narrated by the girl she picked off a quad. And then "the beam came up full again" — the face, retrieved on schedule. Saddest beat in the chapter.

And then the line that made me actually stop: "'Just us,' Randi said — and it came out softer than the plan called for." *The plan.* The narration said it, casually, mid-warmth. I gasped at it — the confirmation that even the anthro class, this sweet thing Vee reached for because she didn't want the morning to end, has a plan under it, and that Randi's feeling is leaking past her own design. I'll be honest about the friction too: whose head is that line in? This is Vee's chapter, and Vee can't know there's a plan. It's either a deliberate chill slipped under the door or a POV wobble, and it worked on me either way, but I noticed the hand.

The photo. Vee showing the cropped one I understood; Vee pulling up the uncropped one because "Legs like yours, though — and you only gave him *half*?" landed wrong on her pride — that's Randi's oldest move, the soft withdrawal that makes Vee volunteer, and I watched it work in real time. "I gave him *all* of it," and she puts her nude in Randi's hand. My stomach did something complicated: half of it was Vee's genuine joy at finally having a friend who can take all of her, "the real thing and not the tidied-up version" — and that joy is not fake, I felt it — and half of it was me counting who has now seen what, and remembering that Randi's debriefs travel. And "Showing him your toes, eh?" — softer — put the boutique kneeling and the thumb across the plum nail right back in the room.

The kiss at the curb is the chapter's real event, and it's Vee's. She's the one who won't let it end, leaning up the last half-inch after Randi, and then — this is the line — "She noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." After the practice-room bathroom, after pinning her own hand to her knee, that sentence is a door quietly coming off its latch. She hasn't named anything. But she stopped refusing it, and the book let it happen without fanfare, and I believed it completely. Randi's long steady look afterward — "a beat past what a goodbye needed" — I couldn't read, and I don't think I'm supposed to.

Small dreads, honestly earned: "The weeks in between ran gray" — her life is saturating only where these two people are. Cassie is out of the anthro class, out of the mornings, and I noticed. And Vee tipping well and "saying nothing about the number" — that card is still hollow underneath.

The chapter title, *All the Time*: it's the wanting that doesn't stop — "the more we do it the more I *want* it" — and the attention that's on her all the time, and, darker, the thing Randi has and spends: all the time in the world, the long game's patience. It didn't give anything away; it deepened after. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*, no tagline shown on it — keeps humming under mornings like this one: every yes at that table was freely given, and I remember what the jacket said about that.

I want the next chapter now. Guesses from here: the anthro class becomes Randi's private theater; the kiss Vee stopped refusing goes somewhere neither of them will call anything; and someday Cassie sees a photo, a kiss, a plan — something — first.
