## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I read this with my stomach tightening the whole way, because I knew — I *knew* — where the phone was going the moment Vee caught herself on "the phone's for the music, and—". The last chapter ended with Pace's promise, "I'd never share your photograph," and I flagged the noun then; I did not expect the answer to arrive this fast or this elegantly. He never has to share it. *She* does. And look at how she does it: Randi says "You don't have to show me" — "And that was the thing that changed her mind." That is the whole book in one line. Then "Legs like yours, though — and you only gave him *half?*" and Vee, stung at being filed as a girl who flinched, hands over the uncropped nude. Every step hers. I felt actually a little sick, and also — I have to be honest — the scene is hot. Randi looking "past a glance, past what a verdict needed," Vee's foot going under the table, "I gave him *all* of it" — the charge is real, and it's the charge of transgression Vee doesn't know she's committing.

And the crack in Randi. When Vee says "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached," Randi's eyes go "bright and a little unfixed," breath catching, and she says "It doesn't," low and not quite even. Vee reads it as her story landing. I read it as Randi hearing that the man *she* loves gives Vee something he — what? Doesn't give her? "It doesn't" — doesn't stop? Doesn't sound unhinged? It's opaque and it hurt, and for one paragraph I felt sorry for Randi, which after thirty-seven chapters is disorienting. The bow I said was drawn is quivering.

"You're just finally hungry out loud" — I've been waiting for that phrase since the floozy-voice chapters, and Randi is the one who hands it to her, "like an absolution." It's the kindest, truest thing anyone says in this chapter and it comes from the person running the game. That's the trap in miniature: the absolution is real *and* it's grooming.

Then the quiet moves: the Anthro class — "Just us," and "something crossed her face and was gone." The on-ramp. And the goodbye kiss where *Vee* is the one who won't let it end, leaning up the last half-inch, "took a second of it for herself." Vee is walking toward the threesome under her own power, exactly as advertised. The title, "All the Time" — Vee's sex-brain wanting it all the time, Pace taking forever, and, colder, Randi getting all of it, all the time, at these tables. The tagline has never fit a chapter more literally: "You don't have to show me."

Friction: almost none. Maybe the Benedict yolk paragraph runs one beat long, though the "best part. The middle, when it goes everywhere" line is doing obvious erotic double-duty and I enjoyed it. Vee paying the check without reading the number — quietly enormous for her; the book earned it across twenty chapters of price-column reading.

What I dread, marked as a guess from here: Randi reporting to Pace with the photo fresh in her eyes; the Anthro class becoming daily proximity; the threesome proposition landing on a Vee who now trusts Randi with "all of it." I turned the page wanting and fearing the same thing.
