## Reader reaction

This chapter did something to me that I want to be precise about: it made me happy the entire time I was reading it, and it left me with my stomach dropped, and both of those are the truth.

The happiness first, because it's Vee's and it's enormous. Her brunch monologue — "We have so much sex, Randi," plain and helpless, and then the cascade after it — is the most purely joyful she's ever been on the page, and it's joyful because none of it is a scene. She says it herself: "It's not a thing that happened... It's just how it *is* now." After thirty chapters of events — the fitting, the bet, the induction walk — this is the chapter where the love has no edges, and hearing her try to describe an edgeless thing ("Like my mouth had been asleep since I was born and nobody told me. Like I'd been eating with the sound off") wrecked me a little. Her worry — "Am I turning into some kind of — I don't know. Sex pervert?" — is the mother's voice making one more pass, and Randi's answer is the best thing Randi has ever said: "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud. You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*." That's an absolution, and it's *true*, and I felt Vee's throat do the thing mine was doing. That's the impossible position this book keeps me in: the person feeding Vee the truest kindness of her life is also the person running the extraction.

Because it is an extraction — and this time I watched every step of the machinery and still couldn't call any single step a lie. The phone comes out for the "safe" pictures. "You did *nothing* worth a picture last week? Nothing?" Then the release of pressure — "You don't have to show me," the soft door that has worked on Vee since chapter 17 — and Vee's thumb finds the cropped nude "before the rest of her had voted." And then the goad that closes it: "you only gave him *half*?" — pride weaponized so cleanly that Vee hands over the full uncropped photograph to prove she isn't a girl who flinches. I sat there with last chapter's line ringing: "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." "Never. I'd never share your photograph." *He* kept that promise for exactly one chapter; *she* broke it herself, gladly, to the one person in the world she should least have handed it to — and she experiences the breach as intimacy, "There was no one else she could have shown." That's the book's whole thesis compressed into a phone crossing a tablecloth. Nothing was taken from her. She put it in Randi's hand.

And yet — and this is where the chapter is better than a con — the moment that hit me hardest wasn't machinery at all. Vee reaches for what Pace's attention feels like and lands on: "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around." And Randi breaks. Eyes "bright and a little unfixed," the small head-shake, the catch in her breath, and the two lowest words in the chapter: "It doesn't." (Answering "God, that sounds unhinged.") Vee reads it as her story landing. I read it against the mirror in chapter one — the woman who was wanted exactly that way, found all the way through on the bench, and who chose, permanently, never to be found again. Vee is living, out loud, over eggs Benedict, the precise thing Randi locked herself out of. Whether that catch is grief, envy, longing for Vee, longing for what Vee has, or all four braided — the page doesn't say and I'm not going to say it either. But it's the realest thing Randi has done since the bathroom vow, and it wasn't performed for anyone. Vee wasn't even supposed to see it.

Then the anthro class. "Sign up with me?" is Vee's idea — that matters, she's the one reaching now — and Randi's "Done... Obviously," then "Is Cassie in?" (checking the field), then "Just us," which comes out "softer than the plan called for, a small roughness under it." That line is doing double duty and knows it: the plan's voice and the want's voice in the same two words. And the goodbye — the thing Vee has "come, without ever quite admitting it, to wait on all week" — where *Vee* is the one who won't let the kiss end, "leaning up the last half-inch after Randi as she started to draw back," and — this is the part that got me — "She noticed it, and decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." That's new. That's not the not-letting-herself-know anymore; that's a small, private yes she doesn't file under anything. And Randi holding the look "a beat past what a goodbye needed... until Vee had almost begun to feel it as strange" — I genuinely can't tell which of them is further out over the edge now.

Erotic charge: real, but all of it secondhand and simmering — the retold sex heats the booth (Vee going pink, the small rock in her seat when "Take pictures" comes out) without ever tipping into the body the way "One Bite" did. The yolk is the horniest thing in the chapter and I mean that as praise: "this is the best part. The middle, when it goes everywhere" — a woman eating with the sound on at last, and also, if I let it, a line about everything in her life that's running and pooling where she can't see it.

Friction: almost none. If I squint, Randi's photo maneuver is so frictionless it verges on showing me the author's hand — but Vee's pride is so well established that I believe every beat. The check moment ("said nothing about the number") kept the credit-card dread alive in one clause, which is exactly the right amount.

The title: "All the Time." On the surface it's Vee's answer to everything — they do it all the time, the attention is on her all the time, the music is always already playing ("I've never once heard it start" — which, read cold, is also the truest sentence in the book about her entire romance: she has never once heard anything in this story start). And it's Randi, who could be anywhere, having all the time in the world for the small news of Vee's life. The book's title and tagline hang exactly where they always hang: "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is nothing but freely given yeses, the photo above all. A polite invitation was extended over brunch, and Vee accepted it, and issued one of her own ("Sign up with me?"), and I don't know anymore who is inviting whom.

What I want next: I dread the photo's afterlife — I have no evidence Randi will misuse it, and real evidence she's half in love, and I still felt cold watching it cross the table. I want the anthro class, "just us," and I'm afraid of it. Guesses from here: the "It doesn't" crack widens — Randi's want of Vee is outrunning the plan, and either she or Pace is going to say the quiet part first. And Cassie, who is now explicitly "out," has been moved off the board without anyone touching her, and I don't think she'll stay moved.
