## Reader reaction

The book has done this to me before — handed me a chapter from inside the machinery and made me watch the two operators be tender with each other — but it has never done it quite this coldly, and I read the last page with my hand over my mouth.

First, the confusion I have to be honest about: **May**. The recent chapters left me in January — the mixer, the gym, the cabin — and here is "the flat May light that came in low on a Sunday," and Randi saying "I can't wait to taste her," which means the threesome still hasn't happened. So either months have passed off-page, or this book is doing again what it did with chapter one: sliding a Randi-and-Pace panel into the timeline wherever it wants and letting me feel the vertigo. Small snag that deepens it: Vee texts a photo of "her own front door, the little wreath she kept on it year-round" — a girl in a dorm doesn't have a wreathed front door. I can't place this chapter exactly in time, and I don't think I'm meant to yet. I'm leaving it ajar, uneasily.

Now the body of it. The first bedroom scene is genuinely warm — the laced fingers, the "three times today," Randi laying her flattery down "like stones across a creek" and Pace watching her do it with affection — and I was almost lulled. Then "I can't wait to taste her" gets loose from her, and the chapter turns into the thing that has scared me about Pace since the quad: *he hears the wish under the cover, and it comes to him "not as something to be worked out but as something to be granted."* That sentence is the whole man. It's the moleskin, the drawer, the heat turned up early — the same tenderness, the same engineering — pointed now at arranging one woman's body as a gift left on his own skin for the other. "He had not washed the afternoon off. He'd left it on himself on purpose." I read that line and the forbidden-kiss chapter detonated retroactively: the "faint edge of sharp and salt" Vee's tongue went hunting for in his mouth, the tidy explanation she built ("he must have forgotten to wipe"), the minor-key question she let settle and keep its small light on — *how many times, and how carefully*. Not forgotten. Curated. He does this. He has done this to her. The kindest reading of Vee's mouth-scene just died on this page, and I feel a little sick, and the sickness is earned by the text, not by my guard.

The taste scene itself is scorching — I won't pretend otherwise. Randi's stillness, the "muh," the horror and the want naked on her face at once, and then "Dirty boy," wrecked and breathy, and going back down "like it had been set in front of her and might, any second, be taken away" — the book's hottest transgression yet, and it burns precisely because it's wrong in a way both of them have chosen and Vee has not. And the inversion is exquisite and awful: in "In Her Place," Randi's mouth *removed* every trace of herself from him, handing him back clean for Vee. Here she takes Vee's traces *into* herself and keeps them. The two chapters are mirror panels, and Vee is the substance passed between them in both.

And then the ending, which is the reason this chapter will stay with me. Pace looks at Randi's face — "gone smooth and blank and far" — and *he knows the face. He had seen it once before.* So have I. I saw it in chapter one, and I know what it meant: not that he'd been that good, but that he'd reached the self she bolted the door on, the "not ever." He reads it as satisfaction. He reaches for her open hand, and it does not close on his, and he tells himself she's only spent — and I don't believe him, and the chapter doesn't ask me to. The last image, the music "playing on... through the dark of the front rooms where no one was," is as desolate a closing note as this book has struck. What did that scene cost her? The page holds it shut behind her blank face, and I'll hold it shut too — but that unclosed hand is the loneliest thing I've read in this volume.

**The titles.** "Ignition — The Arrangement / After She Leaves" — a strange double title, and it names its own two halves: the arrangement (the plan, the bed, the "Tuesday") and after she leaves — which is Vee's taillights, and also, I think, Randi leaving herself, gone somewhere behind her own open eyes. "Ignition" I take darkly: something has been lit here — the threesome machinery restarted in earnest — and ignitions precede fires. *A Hot Meal*, on this chapter, is nearly unbearable: Vee served, literally, as taste. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — has never sat closer to the plate. No tagline was supplied on my cover.

**What I dread next** (guesses from here): that Tuesday's "gift" pattern escalates; that the threesome is coming and Vee still knows nothing; and that Pace's confident misreadings — of Randi tonight, maybe of Vee all along — are the fault line the book will eventually break on. I want to keep reading and I'm braced.
