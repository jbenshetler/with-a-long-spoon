## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

A short breather, and a good one. After two chapters of pressure — the Rusty Nail detonation, Randi building tomorrow night's armor — the book hands me a cold Saturday road and a person I've never met, and I relaxed into it almost immediately. Sheri lands whole in about six paragraphs: small, fast, faster than him on the climbs "and never once let it go unmentioned," remarking on women "the way other people remarked on the weather." I like her instantly, and I notice how rare that is in this book — she's the first person in twenty-eight chapters who seems to want nothing from anyone.

The quiet payload is Pace. This is the closest I've been to his unguarded interior since Rock. "And then he was past the name before he'd decided to be" — he *gushes*, the walnut chair, the thumb down the grain, the math book "not put it back like it had burned her." He heard himself going and didn't stop. Sheri's "Listen to you" and his "He didn't know what there was to listen to" is the most persuasive evidence yet that whatever this started as, he's genuinely gone on Vee — and genuinely without self-knowledge about it. After the jacket's "Both are falling as hard as she is," this is the falling, caught on camera by the one witness who'd notice.

Two lines carry real intelligence for me. First, Sheri's surveillance report: Vee sat alone, glared at the floor, nursed one drink, danced with nobody, left alone — so I now know what Vee's Thursday looked like from outside, and it corroborates her misery honestly. Second: "'Randi. That's not finished.'" Flat, factual, no guilt in it. That chilled me a little more than the chapter maybe intended — he says it the way you'd say a project isn't finished. And Sheri, running "the arithmetic anybody could run": "Two of them at once… If that's not already complicated, it will be." The book putting its own tagline in a bystander's mouth, and Pace not answering. That non-answer is the loudest thing on the page.

Suspicion: none of Sheri, oddly. The one flicker — she "kept an eye on" Vee at his implicit behest or her own initiative? — reads as loyalty, not machinery. "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years" tells me she's a formidable ally and would be a terrible enemy, and "She couldn't take your heat" is Pace *protecting Vee from his own friend*, which moved me.

Erotic charge: essentially zero, and correctly so. Even Sheri's "Hell of a backside on her, though" is weather-talk.

Friction: almost none. The title's "First Ride" made me brace for a recurring Sheri strand — the book announcing a series before earning it — but the chapter's so trim I don't mind. The em-dash title format is new; it reads like the book opening a side door.

Title: "Sheri — First Ride" is purely structural — introducing her, promising more of her. *With a Long Spoon*: Sheri is the first person who sups with none of these devils, which is maybe why she can see the arithmetic clean.

Want next: tomorrow night at Pace's door. This was a lovely intake of breath before it. Guess from here: Sheri becomes Pace's honest mirror the way Cassie is Vee's — and "That's not finished" is going to matter.
