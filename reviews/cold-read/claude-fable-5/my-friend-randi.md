# Cold read — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter got me. The drive from Roanoke — crying because there's no one in the car to keep it from — is the truest thing in the book's cold stretch, and then the chapter turns into what I now realize I'd been starving for since the walkout: Randi and Vee in a room with the door shut. And it's the most double-exposed scene the book has done yet. On one layer, this is genuinely the best friend anyone could have. Every single thing Randi says is *true* and *kind*: "the fear's a liar, and you haven't slept"; the forensic reconstruction of the last night ("He'd cooked. He had the wine out. He'd warmed the house for you"); dismantling the phantom blonde with one surgical question — "How do you know any of that?" — and then, instead of humiliating Vee for inventing her, handing the shame back as tenderness: "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one." I've been waiting two chapters for someone to kill that phantom, and Randi does it in four lines, with love. My chest actually loosened.

And on the other layer I know what Vee doesn't. "This girl. Sheri. Tell me what she looks like" — Randi *met* Sheri at the dance hall. She's asking a question she knows the answer to, and letting Vee describe a blonde model when the real story is that the brunette dancing with Pace was Randi herself. Watching her hold that card while stroking Vee's hair "like she would a child's" is genuinely chilling in a way the book has never quite been before, because none of it reads as cruelty. She loves this girl — Gstaad told me so — and everything she says is in Vee's interest, and it is also, every word, moving Vee back toward Pace's bed, which is where the original game wants her. "Every yes was freely given." Vee will drive to that house tonight having decided it herself. Except she didn't. Except she did. That's the trap, and this chapter is the tagline enacted, and I felt the floor of it the whole time.

The coat scene is where the erotic charge lives, and it works on me precisely because it's routed through shame. Vee saying "It's such a slutty thing to do" — "the word cost her" — and Randi's reframe: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." That's the jacket copy in miniature: shames drawn out and handed back as gifts. And Vee's own body confirming it — "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it" — is the book's whole erotic engine stated on the page. It's not explicit and it's hotter than several chapters that were.

The Gstaad retelling wrecked me quietly. I read chapter 45. I know that "And then it got very late, and we went up" covers a night where she felt *nothing*, and that "she looked down at her hands a moment" is the one crack of the real thing showing. Vee reads it as discretion. And the exit line — "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — is a lie told as a joke, and it lands as a joke, and Vee laughs for the first time all day. Randi lying about Gstaad to make Vee laugh, when the truth of Gstaad is *I realized I love you* — that's the saddest sentence in the chapter and it's played as a punchline.

One small itch: the black heels. "Randi always knew the shoes" — Vee takes them without looking. That felt marked. I don't know what it means, but Randi pressing a specific object into the night she just architected pinged something in me. Maybe it's nothing. Maybe Pace will know those shoes.

Friction: almost none. If anything, Randi's speech is *slightly* too flawlessly effective — "That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" is exactly right about Pace in a way that reads less like a friend guessing and more like someone who knows him, which of course she does, and I can't tell if Vee should have noticed how sure she was ("I know so"). But that's not a flaw; that's the dread working.

The title, "My Friend Randi" — it's Vee's phrase, Vee's possessive, and it aches. She has no idea what she possesses. The one uncomplicated word in it, "friend," is the most complicated word in the book.

What I want next: the coat, the door, tonight. I dread it a little too — Pace has something from the truck "that could keep," Vee will arrive with nothing under a coat, and *both* of them have been coached and neither knows it. Guess from here: the reunion works, it's incandescent, and it re-arms everything.
