# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

The title chapter — the book's own name finally lands on a chapter, and of course it lands on *this* one: Randi at her absolute best-friend best, every word of it warm, every word of it true, and the whole thing standing on ground Vee can't see. I read most of it with my chest tight in two directions at once.

First the ache. Vee crying alone on the drive from Roanoke, the homecoming she'd pictured "going by exactly as she'd pictured it, and it was nothing" — that's her voice, and it hurt. Cassie's stripped bed, the ticking radiator, the invented blonde sitting on her chest. And then the thing that undid me: she goes to Randi, and Randi comes down the stairs fast, gets her past the eyes, shuts the door, and holds her — "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that, and neither of them let go." Vee breathing her in at the neck — bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet — "and didn't want to stop." I know what that hug is to Randi. Vee doesn't. She reads it as friendship and drinks it like something else, and the page lets both be true, and I just sat with it.

Then the vertigo. Randi's counsel is *good*. Every line of it. "You backed him into a corner in his own house. Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do." She dismantles the imaginary Sheri with one clean question — "How do you know any of that?" — and it's genuinely the kindest, sanest thing anyone could do for Vee. And I know why it's so accurate: she's describing her own lover. "The kind who can't say the thing" isn't intuition, it's inventory. And I caught the smaller, colder thing too — Randi has *met* Sheri, froze her out at the Rusty Nail, could kill the fantasy in five words ("she's tiny, honey"), and can't, because she can't admit she's ever stood in Pace's world. Even her mercy has to route around the lie. That's the book in one move: the comfort is real, and the comfort is compromised, and nobody on the bed knows both at once but me.

The coat. God. Vee finishing Randi's hanging sentence herself — "And nothing under it" — and then the mother's voice arriving on cue, the word she has to be made to say, "It's such a slutty thing to do," and Randi walking her down "off the ledge a word at a time" into "That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." I felt that land. It's the book's engine bared: "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." I want her to do it. I want Pace to open that door. And underneath the swoon there's the other reading I can't put down: Randi is dressing Vee for another man's bed — her own black heels pressed into Vee's hands, "Randi always knew the shoes" — sending the woman she loves back to the man she shares in secret, bare under a coat. Is that love giving Vee what Vee wants at cost to herself? Is it the old game still running? The page doesn't say, and I'm not going to say either. Both were in the room.

And then the John story, retold as fairytale — and this was the quietest heartbreak in the chapter. Vee, wistful: "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it" — the exact knife, and she doesn't know she's holding it near Randi either. Then Randi, who "could narrate anything," stops at the bedroom door. "And then it got very late... and we went up." Looks down at her hands. Vee reads discretion. I read the Gstaad bed, the nothing, the realization that lives on the other side of it. The cover line — "the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — is the face, flawless, and it got a real laugh out of Vee, and it cost something I could see and Vee couldn't.

Suspicion: I don't suspect Randi of malice here — I want to be exact about that. What the text earned is dread, not distrust of her tenderness. The tenderness is the truest thing she has. The dread is structural: Vee is about to walk to that door tonight, and I don't know what's on the other side of Pace's month of silence, and the plan was authored — again — by the person who authored everything.

Titles: "My Friend Randi" doubling as the book's title recolors the whole chapter — this *is* the friendship, at full strength and full falseness, the possessive "my" doing all the work of Vee's not-knowing. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* — sat heavy on the coat plan: showing up as the answer is the politest invitation there is, and Vee is supping without the spoon. No tagline in my packet beyond that.

What I want next: the door. I'm turning the page fast. Guess from here: he opens it, and it either breaks him open or breaks my heart — and some part of me is braced for Randi's heels being the detail he notices.
