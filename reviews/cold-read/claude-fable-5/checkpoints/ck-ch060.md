### Impression
**Vee** — wholly mine, more than ever. This span is her springtime: the pleasure-princess reframe, the clean plate, the party where her worlds fused with her at the center, the laugh that dissolved a month of invented rival in one breath. Even her one closed door — the reach, one breath short — moved me more than any of the crossings, because it's the last room her mother still holds. My dread has sharpened in exact proportion to her joy: every threshold she crosses "on her own nerve" turns out to have two gardeners.

**Pace** — this span broke my ability to hold him gently. The caring side has never been better evidenced: the party, the remembered half-sentences, Sheri's whole testimony, the headlights on the door, the "Okay" with no sulk, "boyfriend" spent plainly six times. And then chapter 59 sits in the middle of it like the bench in the bedroom: the same week, in Randi's bed, trading Vee's kisses as intelligence, coming on the shared wanting of her. He loves Vee — Randi named it over him and he couldn't deny it — and he is running her like a long proof at the same time, and I no longer believe those are separate activities in him. The blush at the taproom was the realest thing he did all span; I'm holding onto it.

**Randi** — the ache deepens on both edges at once. The mother's dinner (robin's-egg blue, "They're your color") explained more of the locked bracelet than any chapter yet, and her toast at the roadhouse was true all the way down. But "I don't," level as a fact across the white cloth, was a door being oiled — love and curriculum in one two-word gesture, which is her whole signature. "I want her to know exactly what she does to me" is the most honest sentence she's said in the book, and it terrifies me precisely because I believe it.

**Sheri** — I trust her completely and now so does Vee; she's the book's one clean window, and the only person holding all the cards who wishes nobody harm. **Cassie** likewise — her "You can stay" is the closest thing to a verdict from an honest court this book has, and I noticed she issued it before the reader would have.

**Brooke** — unchanged: warmth as instrument, holding the name "Pace," which is now printed on Vee's whole world.

Overall: the jacket says Vee is a willing player in two games and doesn't know there are three — and I believe I can see the third being set: the taste, the neck, the "I don't," the door raised and not yet opened. The tenderness keeps being real. The yeses keep being real. And I am still ahead of her on every step of the floor, watching the two people who love her best deal the next card.
