### Symbolism
- The **bench as long table**: a beautiful domestic surface with its top lifted off to show the purpose underneath — Randi herself, and the whole arrangement around Vee.
- The **stats course** keeps handing the reader the book's method: *latent variables* (the factors you can't measure, inferred from the ones you can), *Simpson's paradox* (the same numbers reversing when you see the whole), the *outlier that corrupts everything downstream* — Vee is being taught, in a lecture hall, the tools that would expose her own situation.
- **Gabriel's-horn / coastline talk** (ch25): a thing you can fill with one cup but never finish painting the outside of — Pace offering Vee his inside while the outside (parents, scar, Randi) stays uncoverable.
- The series title on the jacket, *With a Long Spoon* (he who sups with the devil…), and the blurb's "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — held loosely, as marketing, but the pages keep confirming the shape.
- **Locked doors**: Randi's bathroom lock (the first she ever turned in his house) vs. Pace's one closed bedroom door, opened only when Vee asks; Daphne's door he once forced.

### Impression
**Vee** I love without complication — the book has built her joy so carefully, appetite by appetite, that watching her flourish is genuinely happy reading, and that's exactly what makes it ache: every yes *is* freely given, and she gave the last one (her silence over MIRA) as a gift to the person best positioned to hurt her. Her want for Randi is now visible to everyone but her.

**Pace** I mostly trust in the room and cannot trust in the architecture. Everything he does with her is patient, consent-shaped, restorative — and it was all commissioned. His own history (fifteen, Daphne at nineteen, the hall desk, "I don't go back") reads like a man rebuilt around attention as the only safe currency; I believe his tenderness and I believe he's fallen for her, and I still hold the fact that she was *pointed at*. The scar and the parents sit like the closed bedroom door.

**Randi** is the character I watch closest and feel most for against my own judgment. The bench chapter earned her everything: a woman who touched being real once and bolted the door on it forever. She is using Vee — the lunches are extraction, the stories are seed — and she is also, visibly, being taken by her, and by whatever went still in her when Pace praised Vee. The MIRA revelation gave her a wound she didn't earn. I don't trust her; I can't dislike her; I'm braced for her to be the one the whole thing breaks on.

**Cassie** I trust completely — the book's one uncomplicated loyalty, and the one person whose radar has pinged correctly at every turn. Her flat "Goodbye, Vee" in chapter 4 is still the truest sentence anyone has said about where this is going.

**Sheri** I like and suspect knows more than anyone; the blonde at the bar I don't trust at all. Brooke is warm surveillance — no reason yet to think she's more than the house's memory, but she keeps a file.

The tone overall: I am ahead of Vee exactly as the jacket promised, and falling for it anyway — which I take to be the point, and the thing to keep watching myself do.
