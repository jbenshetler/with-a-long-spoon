### Impression
**Vee** I love and fear for in equal measure. Every yes really is hers — the book keeps proving it — and that's exactly the trap the jacket promised; watching her thrive inside an arrangement she can't see is the whole ache of reading this. Her courage keeps outrunning her shame and I keep cheering, then remembering who built the track.
**Pace** I half-trust, which is more than I expected. His care is minute, real, and unperformed — the moleskin, the drawer, the heat turned up hours early — and his want for Vee reads true (the diner, the cracked voice). But he pointed at her on a quad like a man ordering, he runs two women out of one warm house with the same tenderness, and the thing he won't say is starting to look less like reticence than like a governed refusal with a history (Daphne, the parents, the scar) I haven't been shown. Tender and unnerving at once.
**Randi** is the book's live wire. Ch 46 rehabilitated her badly and beautifully: the cold isn't cruelty, it's armor, and her love for Vee is the first thing she's ever had that isn't currency. I believe it — and I also watched her steer Vee at every single fork, including back into Pace's bed, and I can't tell where love ends and management begins. I suspect she can't either. I feel for her more than I trust her.
**Cassie** I trust completely — the one person with no angle. **Sheri** I like and side-eye: the only one who tells Pace the truth, and possibly the blonde who nearly wrecked him and Vee (unproven). **Brooke** is friendly weather over a filing system; the file she's building is a hazard nobody in the story is watching. No reason yet to doubt Meg, Kayla, Christine, or the bartender. The thing I'm braced for is not a villain but a collision: too many rooms know half of this, and Vee is the only person in the book who knows none of it.
