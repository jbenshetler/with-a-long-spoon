### Story so far
The book opens on **Pace and Randi**, already secret lovers: in his warm house he unveils a bench he built for her, spanks and edges her until her performance apparatus fails and she begs — really begs — and he says "There you are, Randi." She weeps, and alone in his bathroom resolves that he found her once and never will again: he can have the face. Next morning Brooke smilingly interrogates her at the sorority (the paper alibi, the name "Pace," "he doesn't teach you, does he"), and Randi lies smoothly and then lies rigid on her bed, not crying. Weeks in, mid-sex, Pace asks if she'd like to taste another woman; her body answers with an orgasm before she can dress the want, and she relocates the desire into a gift: "Only in a threesome. With you." He takes her walking and invents the pointing game — he points at women, she picks. She rejects the polished, the paired, the closed, the room-runners, and stops dead at a lush red-haired girl laughing with her whole body outside the dining hall. "Her," he says; "Yes," says Randi, bare, before the costume catches up. Randi engineers a collision, gets a name — **Vee** — and a shared stats class.

From ch4 the book lives mostly in Vee. Randi befriends her dazzlingly (Chi Latte, "love you, girl," "see you later"), while roommate Cassie watches flat-eyed and says an odd, final "Goodbye, Vee." At the coffee shop on Wilson, with Randi texting just beforehand, Pace arrives, helps Vee crack an integral with Euler's formula while keeping the solving hers, listens to her econ dreams, and asks "May I call you?" Froyo that evening; a dinner at Christine's Middle Eastern place where she hands him the ordering ("May I choose?") and, having announced she doesn't kiss a man she's known two days, pulls him back under the tree and makes an exception. A hike: he fixes her blister, feeds her rye-and-horseradish sandwiches and cherries ("we take nothing and leave nothing"); she mentions the unaffordable russet dress from a magazine; freezing rain; he gives her his dry flannel and stands out in the rain — and she knocks on the glass, locks eyes, and strips her wet shirt off for him. A campus cop ends the night unkissed.

He invites her to his house: the tour (handmade furniture, the patent, the one closed door), the sun porch with burgundy silk — he made the flannel, and he will make her the induction dress. The fitting: bare on a riser he built, measured, aroused past hiding, and when he finally faces the evidence he is *smiling up at her*, delighted; she stops making herself small. Girl-talk interleaves: Cassie gets edited versions; Randi gets more — at the velvet restaurant Randi teases out "actually it was really *hot*," names Vee's love of being looked at, and gives her the first mouth-kiss goodbye, which Vee files as French. Vee buys green satin and frosted plum, and on the Friday (ch18) they consummate it — his mouth first, her shame running with the heat and never closing her — then fra diavolo at midnight, and she trades him her folded panties for the night. She tells Cassie a ladled-out version; tells Randi more at brunch, where Randi absolves her appetite and teases out the planning ("you bought them hoping he'd give you no reason to keep them on").

The dress is finished and glorious; she learns it must be worn with nothing underneath; they christen it. Shoe-shopping with Randi: comedy at the discount store, then the boutique where Randi kneels to buckle four-inch black straps on her (same size seven — "we can share"), and Vee puts a terrifying number on her emergency credit card rather than apologize to the dress. Meanwhile (ch24) Randi is still in Pace's bed, telling him Vee talks about him constantly; "she's not ready… take your time"; he: "I want her to get there on her own." He names what Vee does to a room; Randi goes still.

Sunday she returns in his sweats and Randi's heels; Cassie's one eyebrow; Vee explains nothing, too happy to.

### Impression
**Vee** I am wholly for — she's the most alive thing on the page, and watching her appetite come out of hiding, yes by chosen yes, is the book's joy. My tenderness for her is edged with dread: every brave thing she does, she does inside a frame two other people built and haven't shown her.

**Pace** I like against my own better judgment, and mostly cleanly: the care is real, meticulous, unperformed — the heat, the water bottle, the drawer, the dress. The reader's ledger also holds the bench, the pointing game, and "I want her to get there on her own," which is either patience or management, and I genuinely cannot always tell which. His "I can't" reads as damage, not withholding-as-power — Daphne and the hallway sit under it — and Sheri's verdict is mine too. He is the caring side of the irony and one of its architects at once.

**Randi** is the book's ache. I started the bench chapter inside her and I can't unfeel it: the girl who begged once and locked the door forever. Everything she does to Vee is a using and a loving in the same gesture — the coaching that saved the relationship she helped fabricate, the fairytale text sent from the coldest room of her life. I don't trust her plans; I do, increasingly, trust her love, which is exactly what makes her dangerous.

**Cassie** and **Sheri** I trust without reservation — the two people in the book who want nothing and see clearly; Cassie's flat "Goodbye, Vee" in ch4 still reads as the book's earliest alarm.

**Brooke** I'm wary of on pure mechanics: warmth as instrument, and she's holding the name "Pace."

Overall: I have fallen for it exactly as the jacket said I would — the tenderness is real, the yeses are real, and I am ahead of Vee on every step of the floor she's dancing on, waiting for the lid to come off the low table of odd proportions.
