### Impression
**Vee** I love the way you love someone you're ahead of and can't warn. Her whole arc — shame turning to heat turning to appetite turning, finally, to the nerve to ask *what am I to you* — is the realest thing in the book, and she's earned every inch of it herself, which is exactly what the jacket promised would be the problem. My tenderness for her is uncomplicated; my fear for her isn't. She's being loved by two people and lied to by both, and the lies are load-bearing.

**Pace** — I believe his love completely now; ch 49–50 removed all doubt. His care is real, meticulous, and beautiful, and it began as a project two lovers picked off a lawn, and I can't unknow that. The Daphne wound explains him without excusing the diner verdict Sheri handed down, which I co-sign: not-saying it currently matters more to him than having her. Warm toward him, and waiting to see whether he's brave.

**Randi** — the most dangerous and the most heartbreaking person here. The bench chapter made me grieve for her before I'd watched her run a single play on Vee; Gstaad made it worse. Everything warm she gives Vee is true *and* is technique, and she knows it, and she's chosen the coward's version of love — proximity without confession — because she's never had to be brave. I don't trust her hands on Vee's life for one second, and I can't stop hoping she gets out from behind the face alive.

**Cassie** — trust without reservation; the book's one clean instrument. **Sheri** — likewise; she's the only person who tells Pace the truth and I'm grateful every time she's on the page. **Brooke** — no evidence of malice; just power, awake and filing things; worth watching. Everyone else I have no reason to doubt.

Overall: the tenderness is real everywhere and the ground under it is false almost everywhere, and the book keeps letting me enjoy the seduction exactly as Vee does before reminding me what's holding it up. Every yes was freely given. That is, still, the problem.
