### Impression
**Vee** I'm wholly on her side — the pricing of menus, the mother's voice, the pool grief, the appetite finally answered. Watching her flower is genuinely joyful, which is exactly what makes the frame ache: every triumph of "I took it, for once" was arranged for her to take. I believe her wants are real and hers; that's the book's cruelest kindness.
**Pace** I like him more than I can justify. The care is meticulous and, on the page, sincere — the moleskin, the heat, the pencil handed back, the door not opened, the kiss he stopped. But he pointed at a girl on a quad and built a courtship on a concealed lover, and his courtliness makes the deception harder to see, not easier to forgive. Deep tenderness, deep unease, in the same man. The Daphne fragment suggests he's paid for trespass before and made rules of it — rules that don't cover this.
**Randi** The most magnetic and the most frightening person here, and the one I ache for anyway — the bathroom mirror, the thumbnail, the duvet chapter live under every warm "love you, girl." Her jealous flickers when Pace shines on Vee, her tight stillness at "he looked at me and I was fine," her soft real laughs breaking through the performance: something is getting real in her, on both fronts, and she may be the least prepared of the three for it. I trust nothing she says and can't stop watching her.
**Cassie** The one person I trust without reservation. Dry, loyal, watchful; whatever she's holding (the "Goodbye, Vee," the folded-away looks) I suspect it costs her, and I hope the book returns to it.
**Overall** No one has yet been cruel; that's the trap. The jacket promised I'd be ahead of Vee from the start and fall for it exactly as she does, and — irritatingly — I am.
