# Cold read (grounded) — Turned Up

## Reader reaction

This chapter is the exhale after "A Round," and I didn't realize how much I needed it until Cassie put the highlighter down. After a whole chapter of Vee's skin singing and her shame spiraling in soaked satin, we get her home, in the dorm, with the one person in this book who has no angle — and the chapter is almost pure pleasure to sit inside. I grinned through most of it the way Cassie must have.

What lands first is who gets this debrief. In "Dear," it was Randi at the Smoothie Bird drawing out the report of the restaurant date, shaping it, feeding lines back ("You let him choose for you. And you liked it"). Here, the fitting — the biggest, most naked thing that's happened to Vee yet — goes to Cassie. Nobody frames her answers. Nobody says "because of him" for her. Cassie asks "How did it feel?" and then waits, and Vee gets to find her own sentence: "Like I was supposed to be there." I felt actual relief that Randi is nowhere in this room. That relief is mine, imported from what I know; the chapter itself is all warmth, and I want to be honest that on its own terms nothing here is ominous at all.

Cassie is the star of this one for me. The stillness when Vee explains the thermostat — "He turned it up hours before, probably. As soon as he knew I was coming" — and then "Oh, Vee," face gone soft: that soft face carries a lot, and I can't fully read it. It's moved for Vee, plainly. Whether there's a private ache underneath — the girl who screamed in the hall when the letter came, who reaches over and puts her hand on Vee's knee "once, and took it back" — the page doesn't say, and I'm leaving it where the page leaves it. That once-and-took-it-back gesture sat in me longer than anything else in the chapter. It rhymes with "Goodbye, Vee" without repeating it.

And Cassie is funny. "Are you sure he's not gay?" made me laugh out loud — it's exactly the friend-question, the puzzle-worker refusing to let the pieces sit wrong ("He's had you standing in his living room in your underwear, and he picked up a *tape measure*"). And Vee's rebuttal is the best beat in the chapter: the brisk clinical pat of her own ass, "His *package*. Right here… Definitely not gay," and then the thing she'd kept at the bottom surfacing: "*I* was grinding on *him*." Two girls throwing a pillow back and forth over it. That's the register this book does better than almost anything — filth and innocence in the same sentence, nobody punished for either.

Now, the honest reader-note on that: "I was grinding on him" and the felt "package" go a shade past what "A Round" actually showed me — I saw her lean into his work, press her breast to his cheek on purpose, bend into it "more than the dress asked"; I never got his body against her ass on the page. I don't read this as an error. Two chapters ago she "could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care," and this is her doing it again — or telling Cassie something the close-POV didn't render. Either way it's characterization, and I'm holding it open rather than deciding which.

Just as telling is what she doesn't tell. Cassie gets the bra, the box, the geometry of his knees — but not the wet satin, not the shut eyes and the counted breaths, not the smile she opened them onto. "It was — I don't know. It was a lot," and "I kept waiting to feel wrong about it… I didn't." She keeps the most exposed hour of her life partly private even from Cassie, and gives her the shaped version. That's the same girl who kept no transcript of the Chi Latte hour. She lives in the feeling and narrates the plot.

The swoon here is secondhand and still works: "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look.*" That line retro-lit the whole fitting for me — she has now internalized his grammar so completely she's coining "May I"s he never said. "He didn't use the word. But yes." As a reader who knows the courtship was engineered, that's the line that pulls both ways: it's genuinely beautiful, and it's also the sound of the architecture becoming her own voice. The chapter doesn't press this; I felt it anyway, quietly, and I'm not going to pretend it darkened the room. Mostly the room stayed gold.

Erotic charge: low flame, deliberately. This is a fully-clothed chapter about a mostly-unclothed afternoon, and the heat is all in the retelling — the "bra too!" pantomime, the geometry she lets "say itself." It simmers rather than burns, and after the sustained ache of "A Round" that's the right rhythm; a second scorcher back-to-back would have flattened both.

The title, "Turned Up": it's the thermostat — turned up hours before she arrived, the detail that undoes both women in this scene — and it's his face, turned all the way up from his knees waiting for her eyes to open, which is the image Vee circles without naming. And it's Vee herself, dialed up, lying on Cassie's bed hugging a pillow. A title doing three jobs and giving nothing away beforehand; I only heard the chimes after. The book title keeps sharpening: "he left me a door" is this chapter's polite invitation in miniature — every door left open, every step hers. And the series title's long spoon stays where it's been all along: I'm supping with these chapters gladly and keeping count of what the jacket told me — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." This chapter is nothing but freely given yeses, recounted with joy, and that's exactly why it aches at the edges.

Friction: almost none. If I reach — the pacing is two debrief chapters ("Rye," now this) bracketing the fitting, so the Vee-tells-Cassie beat is starting to repeat structurally; this one earns it by being funnier and deeper than "Rye," but I wouldn't want a third in a row soon.

What I want next: the dress finished, the call, the induction — and I notice the bench and the closed bedroom door and Randi's whole silent machinery are all still out there, unmentioned for two chapters now, which reads like the book letting the happiness bank up before spending it. Guesses from here: the dress reveal or the induction brings Randi back into contact with the Pace-side of Vee's life; and at some point Vee is going to retell one of these stories to Randi and I'm going to feel the difference between the two listeners like a dropped temperature.
