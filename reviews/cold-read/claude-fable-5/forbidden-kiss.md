# Cold read (grounded) — forbidden-kiss

## Reader reaction

This is the exhale the book owed me after the coat night, and it paid in full — and then, in the last two pages, it slid something under my tongue that I can still taste.

The morning-after opening is pure pleasure to read. Her hand going out "before she was anyone" and finding cold cotton, the one lurch back to December before his voice arrives — *farmers market in the morning* — that's exactly how a body wakes after a near-loss, and I felt her heart come down off its ledge with her. Then the long luxurious inventory of the house: the bread timed so she'd wake inside the smell of it, "even his absence had been cooked in advance." That line is gorgeous and — because I know what she doesn't — it's also the whole book in six words. Everything in this house happens at a planned hour. She reads it as love. I read it as love *and* as method, and the chapter never once makes me choose, which is its genius.

The "pleasure slave / pleasure princess" turn made me laugh out loud, honestly — the shimmy, the giggle, the distinction landing "low in her, wicked and happy at once." This is Vee at her most winning: taking a submissive fantasy, counting it off on her fingers (he brings her off first, he sews, he shops, he cleans), and flipping the crown onto her own head. And the chapter then *plays* the flip: her posing at the counter, the toast burning because she exists, "I got mushrooms" — the funniest line he's ever had, composure reassembled "like a man picking up dropped change." I was rooting for her the whole way. The wagged finger at the sink, the walk down the hall where "she stopped being able to tell the performance from the wanting" — that sentence is her arc in miniature, and it's earned.

Then the sex. This is among the hottest chapters in the book, full stop. The pointing game — wrist, arm, armpit, breast, breast, *lower* — her commanding until the command stops being a command ("the finger was not steady this time, and both of them knew it"), the detour to her feet that made her trill, "nobody's princess for a second." The oranges earlier were foreplay and I knew it while I watched him wring the membranes out in his fist: "he was opening her as he had opened the orange" — the chapter set that up in daylight and cashed it in bed, and I felt it land. The held-open breathing, the noisy relish, the "old fused thing" rising — the cringe and the heat one arrival — and him *drinking* it, not pardoning it: that's this book's deepest chord, shame and desire on the same wire, and it's never been struck harder. Her hand in his hair making itself let go, finger by finger, because ending it was unthinkable. The whited-out second orgasm. All of it worked on me completely.

And then the kiss. This is the chapter's real subject and I didn't see it coming. Her tongue reaches for her own taste *first* — "curious, unforbidden, hers" — before the installed voice ("*no, that's wrong, that's not a thing you do*") slams down over its head. "Ugh — gross," the back of her hand, the child's gesture — and then her legs clamping him in: "No — not that... Don't you dare stop." Two different noes, and he reads which is which, wipes his mouth, doesn't sulk, doesn't push, doesn't explain. That beat is why I trust the sex in this book: her no is honored instantly even mid-stroke, and her yes stays entirely hers to take back — which she does, pulling him down herself, and coming *mid-kiss*, out of nowhere.

But the tongue going hunting — "a faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning. Or found nothing." The page holds that door exactly ajar and I am leaving it ajar, but my stomach knew something before my head did. Sharp and salt, in his mouth, "under the taste that was only him." I know who else has been in this house. I don't know that's what it was — the page says "if it had been there at all" — but the afterglow section confirms the chapter means business: *why does he like it so much*, the thought that "would not lie flat," standing in the corner of every night like this one. And the tidy explanation she reaches for — "he must have forgotten to wipe. This once" — arrives "tidy and kind and she took it, grateful," which is precisely how Vee has taken every kind surface this book has handed her. The question "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been —" slides away and *does not go all the way*: "it settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting." That's the most quietly frightening sentence in the chapter, and it's frightening because everything around it is so warm.

And then the ending: she'll tell Randi. "Randi would know what to make of it. / Randi always did." Reader, I made a sound. She is going to carry the taste-question — *why does he like the taste of a woman so much; what was that salt* — to the one person on earth who knows the answer, who is in love with her, and who converted this whole story out of the question "would you like to taste another woman?" The chapter doesn't know it's doing this. I do. That's the jacket's promise ("lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does") executing itself perfectly.

Feelings moved: Pace up and sideways at once — the "Okay" against her jaw, the mouth left patient for her to take back, gentling at her "as if she had done something young and dear" — that's the tenderest he's ever been, and simultaneously I can't stop hearing that the taste of a woman is a thing he goes down on *glad*, and there are two women. Vee I adore; the pleasure-princess joy is the freest she's been all book. Randi, offstage, has never loomed larger.

**The title.** "Forbidden Kiss" looked like romance-novel boilerplate going in — the one truly generic-sounding title so far — and the chapter detonates it: the forbidden kiss is her *own taste* in his mouth, forbidden by the flat clean voice installed before she could argue. And it re-arms pointing forward: the tongue that "already said yes" before the mind arrived is the same tongue that went hunting for salt. Given the jacket ("wanting the woman beside her") and *With a Long Spoon* hanging its devil's proverb over every warm meal — a breakfast built like a case being made, a man who eats her like fruit — the title feels like a door the book just unlocked without opening. *A Polite Invitation*: the sweats folded at the foot of the bed, offered and declinable. She declined. Every yes was freely given.

Friction: nearly none. If I strain, the mushroom-tasting paragraph ("almost masculine, if a flavor could be a gender") leans a half-step hard on the flavor-as-body theme the ending will make explicit — but it reads as her sensibility, not the author's thumb, and the crust's "crackle that carried all the way across the kitchen" is such good breakfast writing I forgive everything. The chapter is long and earns every inch.

What I dread and want, as guesses from here: Tuesday's debrief. She's going to hand Randi the salt question across a little table, and Randi — "queen lending a sword" — will do something with it. I don't know whether it's the next move of the game or the moment Randi's cover starts to cost her. I turned the last page wanting Tuesday *now*.
