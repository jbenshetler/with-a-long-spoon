## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me. After the coat night I wanted exactly this — the morning after, the proof that the reunion holds — and the chapter hands it over and then, in the last two pages, slips a splinter under my thumbnail so gently I didn't feel it go in until I was already worrying it.

The whole first movement is pure swoon and I'm naming it as swoon. Vee waking into "the acreage" of his bed, the bread timed so she'd wake inside the smell of it — "Even his absence had been cooked in advance" is such a good line I said it out loud. And her deciding *not* to wear the sweats — that's the payoff of fifty chapters. The girl who buttoned cardigans to the chin looks at the mirror-girl in nothing but a scrunchie and rules her "honestly, magnificent." The pleasure-slave-to-pleasure-princess reversal, counted off on her fingers barefoot in the hallway ("he brought her off first. Always"), is Vee's whole arc done as comedy: she checks the evidence and the evidence says she is the one being served. I laughed at "I got mushrooms" — a man whose composure is a law of nature, broken twice now, reassembling it "like a man picking up dropped change." And the toast-burning game, her banking his slipped glances "like earnings" — the erotic charge here runs through *play*, and it's the happiest sex-adjacent writing in the book so far.

The sex itself: yes, it worked on me, fully. The pointing — her finger as scepter, wrist, breast, breast, lower — keeps her sovereignty on the page the entire time, and the blood-orange echo ("he was opening her as he had opened the orange, nothing hurried, nothing skipped") is the kind of image this book does better than anything else I've read: the breakfast prep was foreplay and I knew it and it still landed. The peak for me is the shame material — "the cringe and the heat one arrival, indivisible... He was drinking it. His palms held her open and nothing in them asked her to be any less." That's the book's thesis in her body, and it's rendered with total tenderness. His groan "arriving not in her ears but in her flesh." The cashmere taking "the ruin of her, and even that was luxury." Sustained, graphic, and never clinical for a second.

And then the kiss. The chapter's whole title snaps into place in one paragraph. Her tongue goes to the taste of herself *first* — "curious, unforbidden, hers" — and then the installed voice arrives ("no, that's wrong, that's not a thing you do") and slams it shut over the head of a yes already given. "Ugh — gross," the back of the hand, "the child's gesture." That's the mother's voice again, the floozie wire, and I believed every syllable of it: the one shame Pace's table hasn't yet re-taught. What he does next is why I half-trust him more than ever: goes still, starts to withdraw, reads "which no was which" when her heels lock him back, wipes his mouth, "did not push it and did not sulk and did not explain." That is the consent grammar this book has been building since "May I choose?" and it is genuinely moving — a no honored mid-stroke, without punishment.

Except — the afterglow autopsy. This is where the chapter turns quietly devastating. His mouth never comes back to hers. "Only the one door, closed the moment she had closed it" — and I heard Daphne's door behind that sentence like a bell. He doesn't punish; he *closes*. Cleanly, warmly, maybe permanently — I've seen what closed doors mean in his history, and Vee hasn't. Her tidy explanation ("he must have forgotten to wipe. This once.") is self-deception performed live on the page, and then her own tongue betrays her, licking her lip "looking for something," and she refuses to ask what. "It settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting." That's the interior work I read this book for — the want she will not look at squarely, caught in the act of not-looking.

And the ending. Reader, I made a sound. She's going to tell *Randi*. "Randi would know what to make of it. Randi always did." The question standing in the corner of the room — why does he like the taste so much, and what would it mean to like it herself — is about to be carried, voluntarily, to the woman who converted "would you like to taste another woman" into "only in a threesome, with you," the woman whose kisses Vee already prolongs. Vee is walking her own forbidden taste to the exact person positioned to open that door, and she thinks she's bringing brunch gossip. The dramatic irony is almost unbearable and the chapter never underlines it once. That restraint is why it works.

The title: I'll admit "Forbidden Kiss" made me brace for something else going in — Randi, honestly. Instead it's a kiss forbidden by a voice installed "before she could have argued with it," refused by her own hand a beat after her tongue said yes. It didn't give anything away; it recolored perfectly. The book title too — a polite invitation is precisely what his wet mouth was, and precisely what she declined; and the long spoon hovers over that Tuesday dinner already. The jacket's "she never lets herself notice how much she grows to want Randi" is converging on this taste question from two directions and I can feel it.

Friction: almost none. The princess/slave/gladiator fantasy riff runs one beat long for me ("Groomed by her own gladiator" tipped slightly cute), and "a smell with shoulders on it" made me blink — but I'd trade nothing else. The food writing earns its length; the persimmon-with-salt beat ("It wakes the fruit up") is doing quiet double duty and I noticed and loved it.

What I want next: the Tuesday brunch, badly — and I dread it equally, because I now know exactly what Randi will do with this offering, and it will look like kindness. Guesses from here: Randi reads the confession as the opening she's been waiting for and makes some first move toward the "taste" — a suggestion, a story, maybe something bolder; and the closed mouth-door does not silently reopen — Vee will have to notice it, or ask, and that conversation could go somewhere tender or somewhere very cold.
