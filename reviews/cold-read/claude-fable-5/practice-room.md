## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well. That was the debrief I was braced for, and it was worse and better than I feared — worse because Randi got everything, better because the chapter is just gorgeous at doing it.

The stats professor strikes again, and I actually laughed out loud with recognition this time: two kinds of outliers, and "the second kind comes in through the part that carries forward... It's in everything that comes after." Vee dutifully writes *never all the way gone* and doesn't apply it to herself — "She did not think about herself at all." That line is the closest the book has come to winking at me, and I'll allow it, because the lecture-as-thesis is an established running joke between me and this author by now. The spank is an innovational outlier. It's in every value after. So, for that matter, is Pace's tutor.

The power detail that got me most wasn't erotic at all: Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist. "For weeks it had gone the other way." Vee now *initiates* the extraction. Randi doesn't even have to cut her out of the herd anymore; the herd animal walks itself over and asks to be milked. And poor Cassie saying "see you Thursday to the side of Vee's head" — Cassie is always the honest instrument, and here she measures exactly how far Vee has turned away from her.

Then the practice room. A soundproof cell "where everybody used to" — Vee can't finish the sentence, Randi says "I do know," pleasant as weather, and the venue itself is telling me what this conversation is. And Randi's interrogation is a master class I could watch forever with my skin crawling: "So. Did you deserve it?" — and Vee, laughing too high, agrees, and "She didn't hear what she'd agreed to. Randi did." That pair of sentences is the whole novel. Then the surgical strike: "His hand was wet... You said he was doing dishes. So his hand was wet." Randi reconstructs the sensory detail Vee skipped and hands it back so vividly that Vee re-lives it in the chair — nipples aching under thin jersey, arms crossed, "couldn't for her life have said whether it worked."

Erotically, the peak for me is Randi's story — "the mouse that roars." Over his knee, counting out loud, the hand that slips "right between," "by then it's welcome. Believe me. Very welcome." It's hot, genuinely, in that talked-into-existence way the book does best — and it is also, I'm nearly sure, a *placement*. Is the mouse story even true? Or true-but-about-Pace? "Slight little thing" doesn't sound like Pace, so either it's a real ex or a fabrication built to normalize the exact next step: spanking that ends in sex, framed as something worldly women receive gladly. Either way Randi has just installed the bridge from love-tap to bench, and Vee ran to a bathroom soaked through, "made by nothing but a woman talking in a chair."

And then the moment I found genuinely moving: Vee catches her own hand and holds it against her knee. "To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." The cover tagline is standing right there. She *withheld* a yes — one of the very few in this book — and it broke my heart a little that her refusal is already downstream of a story engineered to produce the want.

The ending, the held hug — "She noticed it the way you notice a held note after the song has already moved on" — Vee not letting go of *Randi*, and rationalizing it ("She put it down to that"). The Randi-side of the triangle is warming on Vee's side now too, and Vee has no category for it.

Friction: almost none. If anything, Randi's clairvoyance ("His hand was wet") teeters on omniscience — but the text covers it; Vee *did* say dishes. The title: "The Practice Room" is perfect and a little sinister on reflection — a soundproof room where people rehearse, where "everybody used to," where Vee just rehearsed, out loud, the thing the bench is waiting to perform. Practice for what, is the question I don't want answered as much as I do.

Guess from here: Randi reports all of this to Pace verbatim, and the next escalation at the house — a real spanking, invited or maneuvered — arrives feeling like Vee's own idea. Which it now half is. That's the trap.
