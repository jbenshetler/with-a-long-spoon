# Cold read (grounded) — My Pleasure

## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter is a knife, and it's a beautiful one, and I'm still holding it by the blade.

The cut from "The Reach" to this is the cruelest thing the book has done to me yet. Vee's last chapter ended *"Her mouth was still empty"* — her fingers a breath from her lips, unable to close the distance — and this one opens with Randi coming against Pace's mouth and then, within a hundred words, pulling him down and tasting herself off him *"with a low sound that went straight through his chest."* No hesitation. Then the thumb from the corner of his mouth, between her lips: *"There. Now it's mutual."* Everything Vee spent three chapters circling, flinching from, wiping on the sheet — Randi does it as a greeting. I felt that in my body before I felt it in my head: heat first, and then the ache for Vee arriving a half-second behind, like thunder after the flash.

And the worse revelation, the one that quietly detonated a whole earlier chapter: **he presents the wet mouth on purpose.** *"He gave her time to see it."* Randi even names it — *"Showing off."* *"I didn't ask for the presentation."* So Vee's tidy, kind little explanation — *he must have forgotten to wipe, this once* — is rubble. He didn't forget. It's a practiced gesture, a thing he *does*, a thing another woman receives as a gift in the same bed-language. That small minor-key question Vee refused to chase — *how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been* — this chapter answers it over her sleeping head, and the answer is *always, and with her.* I actually put the page down for a second there.

**How I feel about them now.** Randi wrecked me. This is the most of her real face I've been shown since the bench, and it comes out in fragments she can't govern: *"I miss her during the day. More at night."* The humor stripped off her at *"I want her to know exactly what she does to me."* And the proxy scene — her hand spread over her own breast, *"Her breast in my hand. All that softness. The weight of it,"* offering her own nipple to Pace's mouth as if the wanting *"could cross through the body she offered him"* — that is one of the strangest, hottest, saddest things I've read in this book. She is making love to Vee through the only body in the room that has also touched her. I believe her love completely now. I also watched her fish — *"She kissed my neck again"* — and watched him fish back — *"She kisses me first now." "She looks at you, too."* They are mapping Vee's desire from both banks of her, trading sightings like scouts, and they do it mid-stroke, and Vee's name is the third body in the bed: *"her mouth stayed open around Vee's name without saying it."* I can't decide if that's devotion or surveillance and I think the truthful answer is that it is both at once and the book wants me unable to pry them apart.

Pace moved for me too — in both directions. *"She makes me happy,"* flat, at his volume for three, is the plainest thing he's ever said about Vee, and it lands *because* it's said to Randi, where there's no performance possible. And then Randi hands him the word Vee can't say — *"You know she loves you, right?" "She hasn't said it." "She doesn't have to."* — and *"his face softened before he could do anything about it."* That softening undid me. And then: *"She's not the only one in love." Pace looked at her. He couldn't tell her she was wrong.* The page leaves that sentence open — not the only one meaning him? meaning Randi? meaning both of them, this whole bed? — *"The open sentence stayed with it,"* the chapter says, and I'm leaving it exactly that ajar, because it is somehow more devastating unfixed. Two people in love with the same absent woman, telling each other so while they fuck, and neither of them will carry the word out of that room to her. That's the warmest, loneliest thing in the book so far.

**The heat.** Peak, no argument. The control play — his hands nearly spanning her waist, lowering her in increments while she fights to hold still for him, *"Showing off again." "You noticed."* — is gorgeous, and the power keeps changing hands the way it only can between two people with years of grammar: he takes the lead, she permits it, and then late in the scene she lifts his hands off her waist, sets them flat on the mattress, *"and moved under her own command."* After chapter one's bench, I felt the weight of that. She lets him find her only so far now; the rest she administers. Even the *"fuck doll"* line — which jolted me for a beat, a register I hadn't heard from her — resolved into character: it's Randi handing him a script, sweet-voiced, and staying its author. And the cleanup — her tongue gathering *"what had spilled over him,"* *"then took him deeper when cleanup no longer named what she was doing"* — is the dinner-table joke from Thursday (*"Did you swallow all of it and wipe your mouth?"*) made flesh. She was teasing Vee with her own repertoire. I see it now.

**One sting I want on the record:** he kisses the inside of her wrist. *Stays* there isn't said, but it's the same kiss — the one Vee marveled at, *who stays? He parked.* The same tenderness, spent on both. The checkpoint in me whispered *heat turned up before she arrives,* and I flinched on Vee's behalf.

**Friction.** Almost none. The mirroring with "The Reach" is very pointed — empty mouth, full mouth, back to back — and I noticed the design for a second before it swallowed me again. That's the only thumbprint, and the payoff earns it.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** I don't suspect anyone of anything new; I *know* things now, which is worse. What the chapter shifts is the game's temperature: *"You like hearing me want her." "Yes."* The old machine is not dormant. Randi's Gstaad resolution — let Vee arrive on her own — is cracking on the page: *I want her to know.* Something is moving.

**The titles.** "My Pleasure" — the waiter's phrase, the servant's phrase, right after two chapters of pleasure-princess-and-slave. Whose pleasure is this chapter? Randi's, finally, on the page and in her own command — and also Vee, who *is* the pleasure both of them are having in absentia. It gave nothing away going in; coming out, it's a small courteous horror. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* (no tagline or jacket copy supplied in what I was given) — the long spoon proverb has hung over every warm meal in this book, and here is the warmest room yet, and it's the one Vee can never be told about. Every warm reception in this series has a second door.

**What I want / dread next** (guesses from here): I think the threesome is coming back onto the table — Randi's *"I want her to know exactly what she does to me"* and Pace's *"She looks at you, too"* read like two people talking themselves toward a door. What I dread is Vee learning about the wet kiss the wrong way — that gesture is a landmine now; the day she learns it's a ritual he shares, her one tidy mercy-explanation becomes the proof of everything. And some traitor part of me wanted this chapter to go on — that's the book working exactly as the spoon proverb warned.
