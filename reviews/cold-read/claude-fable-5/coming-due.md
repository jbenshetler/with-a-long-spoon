# Cold read (grounded) — coming-due

## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter hurt in the best way — the quiet kind of hurt, the morning-after chapter where nothing "happens" and everything is happening.

**How I feel about everyone right now.** Vee is breaking my heart with her discipline. That top-bunk image — the hand held flat on her stomach, "a lid held down on a pot," because "as long as she didn't give it the thing that would make it true, it could stay only wanting" — is the single truest sentence about her the book has written in a while. She's not repressing out of prudishness; she's doing epistemology with her own hand. If she doesn't touch, it isn't real yet. I've watched her run this exact machinery since the fitting-room chapters, and it never stops landing, because every time the machinery loses a little more ground. Randi I feel more double about than I have since Gstaad. On the page she's flawless, warm, "in every particular exactly herself" — and that's the line that got me: "nothing about her had changed and all of it landed different." That's the change being all Vee's, and the chapter is honest enough to keep it there. But the neck kiss. Randi returned the kiss Vee had planted twice — same spot, "How do you like it?" — and I genuinely cannot tell, and I don't think the page wants me to tell, whether that's a girl playing tag or a woman who has been keeping score since October and just moved a piece. She remembered *don't you dare weaponize that* and then weaponized it. I'm holding that open, but my pulse jumped at it, and not only Vee's. Cassie, four words at a time, once again sees everything: "What was that with you and Randi?" — and the fact that there was *worry* under it, not her usual flatness, scared me more than anything else in the chapter. And Pace is barely here — a warm body reached for in the gray — and honestly that absence is the point: Vee spends the whole chapter with "no him for it," and I felt his uselessness to this particular problem as sharply as she did.

**Swoon / pull.** The wintergreen. That whole beat — the forgotten Lifesavers (she's handed one over every class, and tonight she forgot *clean*), then Randi's fingers grazing the inside of her wrist, "her pulse leaping up under it to be found" — I had to stop reading for a second. The book has taught me what the inside of a wrist means in this world; Pace parked his mouth there two chapters ago, and Vee called it a toll booth nobody stays at. Now a fingertip's accidental drag there from Randi outguns a whole weekend of Pace. That's devastating and gorgeous. And the ending, Vee's own voice coming "out of the ringing, not out of any part of her that was thinking" — *"Brunch."* She turns her mouth away from the kiss, takes the miss on purpose, wins the whole night's battle — and then her body books the next appointment before her mind can vote. I actually said *oh no* out loud, fondly.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Two lines went cold on me, and I think the page earned both. First: "Standards committee — I've got to go discipline a bad girl," delivered as a martyr's groan. I know what I know from chapter one — what Standards was the alibi for, whose paperwork, what Randi's nights contain that Vee can't imagine — and hearing her toss that phrase off with a laugh, to Vee, felt like watching someone juggle a knife at a child's party. Vee hears a committee. I hear a door. Second, fainter: "I'll make sure you get home safe" — the Rusty Nail blonde's poisoned phrase, back in Randi's warm mouth. That one might be my own nerves; I flag it and hold it loosely. But the *steering* is textual and unmistakable: Vee tries to buy her way out of the night and "somehow she'd bought more." Randi doesn't take a no; she converts it. She has done that at every fork since September, and this chapter did it again in miniature.

**Erotic charge.** High, and almost entirely untouched — which is exactly this book's specialty. The chapter runs on denial: the sore nipples against the sleep shirt with "no one pressed to her to want it gone," the knee against knee for an hour and "what the holding-still was costing her," the wet bloom of the mouth on the neck and the sound that gets out before she can catch it. The Sunday opening — reaching for him before he's awake, taking the book out of his hands, and still driving home "full of him and not, somehow, filled" — is the thesis of the whole chapter in one line. It peaks at the wrist and the neck. Nothing goes slack; there's no clinical inch in it.

**Friction.** Almost none. If I reach: the word *having* gets a lot of freight — "Having you at the dance was so great" carrying an entire ontology on its back — but Vee's whole character is a girl who hears words a half-beat late and then can't put them down, so I bought it. The chapter is short and knows it.

**The titles.** "Coming Due" — and there's Marsh at the lectern teaching reciprocity, the three obligations, the gift as "a debt you laid on someone whether they wanted it or not." The book has been feeding Vee lectures that caption her own life since Bessel's correction, and this is the barest one yet: she has been *given* so much — the shirt, the gown, the drawer, the friendship, the coaching, the shoes — and the hau of every gift wants to go home. Something is being called in. The dread in the chapter — "something that had already happened and hadn't yet shown its size" — is the invoice arriving before the amount is filled in. The cover board gives me *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION*, no tagline in my packet. "A warm reception" gets more ironic every chapter: warmth is this book's delivery mechanism — the heated house, the held seat, the hug she can't refuse — and the spoon proverb hangs over the wintergreen passing between their fingers like a grace said before a meal I'm afraid of.

**What I want / dread next.** Saturday brunch, which Vee proposed with her spine instead of her brain, and which Randi received "like she'd been handed a present." I want it and I'm braced for it in equal measure. Guesses from here: Cassie's question is the first crack in the dam of Vee's editing — I think Cassie pushes, or watches harder, and I think the two half-known worlds (the dorm's Pace, the house's Pace, the Rusty Nail's two women) are one bad coincidence from touching. And I don't believe the lid on the pot survives brunch. I don't know if I want it to.
