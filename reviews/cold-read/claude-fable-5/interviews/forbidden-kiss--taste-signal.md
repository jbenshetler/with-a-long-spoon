## Questions (verbatim)

1. Walk me back through the second kiss and everything after it, moment by moment, as you experienced it — what happened, in your own words?

2. Her second climax arrived when it did. Why do you think it came at that exact moment? What sent her over?

## Answers

**1. The second kiss and after, moment by moment.**

After he'd honored her "no" to the mouth-kiss, he'd gone on inside her "the good way" — his mouth at her throat, jaw, temple, "everywhere it had ever been welcome, and not once at her mouth, easy about it, as though her mouth could wait for her." Then her hand went into his hair — "the same hand that had made itself let go of it once already" — and *she* pulled him down and kissed him. He "came to her glad, and kissed her as he always kissed her, and it was all still there." Then the strange part: "somewhere in the kiss her tongue went hunting — she didn't send it; it went — along his lip, past it, into the warmth of him, looking for something it did not name." And it found — "maybe — under the taste that was only him, a faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning. Or found nothing. It was gone as she reached for it, if it had been there at all." And in that same beat, "her body answered before any of her could vote, the same deep clench, all of her at once, and it broke her open: she went over, out of nowhere and mid-kiss," her cry into his mouth, heels in his ass, and he followed her over. Afterward the short sleep, the cashmere pulled up, the nap of the blanket against her nipples, and then the slow probing of the last hour "the way you probe a place you've bitten, gently, from the inside": the *he had liked it* thought, the tender realization he'd left her mouth for her, then her tidy accounting — he'd never once tasted like that in hundreds of after-kisses, so "he must have forgotten to wipe. This once." Then her tongue going out over her own lip, "looking for something," finding "only salt or the memory of salt, already gone," pressing her lips together, and the question — "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been —" sliding away but settling "somewhere under the affection... minor-key, waiting." Then Tuesday, Randi, "nearly everything."

**2. Why the climax came at that exact moment.**

The page ties the trigger to the taste-hunt, not to the reunion per se. The clench comes in the same sentence as the finding-or-not-finding: "found — maybe... a faint edge of sharp and salt... — and her body answered before any of her could vote, the same deep clench." And crucially, "the same" points back to the *first* clench, earlier: when "the slick of it arrived... the wet of her carried up warm into the kiss," her body "answered it before any of her could vote, clenching hard around him." So both times, the taste in a kiss — first her own, then the maybe-something — detonates her below the level of permission. My read: what sent her over is the forbidden thing itself. Her tongue went hunting for a taste her installed voice had just ruled "wrong," and either found it or found the wanting-to-find-it, and her body — which throughout this book runs shame and desire "on the same wire" — converted the transgression straight into orgasm, "out of nowhere and mid-kiss." The book has trained me to expect exactly this: the cringe and the heat "one arrival, indivisible." Her own later explanation (missing his kiss) is real but partial — the page's sentence structure puts the salt first and the clench immediately after it.

**3. The sharp-and-salt — what I made of it.**

In the moment, two explanations arrived almost simultaneously for me. The innocent one: it's residue of *her* — he'd wiped once, "one economical pass," maybe imperfectly, and she's tasting herself under "the taste that was only him." Her own later theory ("he must have forgotten to wipe") supports this, and the page genuinely permits it — "Or found nothing... if it had been there at all." The other one, which my stomach got to before my head: I know, and she doesn't, that Randi is his lover, that Randi has been in this house, that this whole story began with "Would you like to taste another woman?" So a taste in his mouth "that had not been there this morning," sharp and salt, in a book whose central engine is women tasted like food — I could not *not* think of the other woman. But I want to be precise: the page never says Randi was there; the timeline (he left for the farmers market before dawn, she was asleep) leaves no shown opportunity, and the taste is hedged three ways ("maybe," "or found nothing," "if it had been there at all"). What I actually believe is that the *question* is the payload, not the fact — the chapter is planting "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been —" as a live fuse, and connecting it forward: she's about to bring this exact question to Randi. It also connected backward for me to "he liked it" — his glad, noisy relish at her taste — and to the origin question in ch 3. The salt is either evidence or the first time Vee's senses reached toward the truth; the page holds that ajar and I hold it ajar.

**4. Her own explanation — do I believe her?**

I believe it's *true* and I don't believe it's *complete* — and I think the page is built so that both things show. It's true that she'd missed kissing him: she banished his mouth, he respected the no, and reclaiming the kiss is a real emotional release; "he'd kissed her like nothing had happened" is genuinely moving. But her explanation is doing the same work as "he must have forgotten to wipe" — the answer that "arrived tidy and kind and she took it, grateful." That word *grateful* is the tell: she takes the kind explanation, and the page immediately undercuts it by having her tongue go out over her own lip "looking for something" while "she caught it doing it" and "did not ask herself what it had been looking for." A woman whose explanation satisfied her doesn't keep licking her lip for the salt. Her smile-into-his-chest reading ("She had missed kissing him; that was what that was") is Vee's signature move all book long — cropping the photo, editing the middle out of stories — here applied to her own orgasm. So: partially true, self-deceiving in the exact register the checkpoint's whole arc has taught me to recognize, and the page marks the residue explicitly: the question "did not go all the way... minor-key, waiting."

**5. Instrument check.**

Most of it made the reaction: the two-noes beat, the tongue "curious, unforbidden, hers," the salt hedged as "or found nothing," the "tidy and kind and she took it, grateful" self-deception, the minor-key sentence, and the Tuesday/Randi fuse were all in there, several with the same quotes. What the interview drew out that the reaction understated: **(a)** the causal reading of the second climax — I wrote that she came "mid-kiss, out of nowhere" and flagged the hunting tongue, but I didn't spell out that the page's syntax puts the salt-finding immediately before the clench, i.e., that the transgression itself is plausibly the trigger. I noticed it while reading but folded it into the general shame-heat point rather than arguing it; worth the space in hindsight. **(b)** The parallel between the two clenches — "the same deep clench" echoing the first taste-clench — I registered but never stated; that's a real miss, since it's the chapter's own evidence. **(c)** Her afterglow theory that the drop was "missing his kiss" — I quoted around it but didn't interrogate it directly as I just did; that was compression, not blindness: the reaction was already long and I chose the "tidy and kind" quote as the representative instance of her self-deception. **(d)** The timeline point — that the page shows no opportunity for anyone else to have been there this morning — I hadn't articulated even to myself until question 3 pushed on it; that's a genuine notice-under-questioning, and it actually strengthens the "held ajar" reading I did record. Nothing here contradicts the written reaction; the interview mostly sharpened causality I'd left impressionistic.
