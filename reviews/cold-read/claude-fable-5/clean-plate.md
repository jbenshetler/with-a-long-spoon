## Reader reaction

The three of them at that little loft table — Vee, Randi, and the story between them — and I didn't want the chapter to end. Here's my read.

**How I feel about everyone right now.** Vee I love with a lump in my throat this chapter, because the book put her whole predicament in one image without a drop of cruelty: she can *do* everything — swallow a man in a parking lot, hold herself open on a county road, come loud and not care — and she cannot *say* it across a white cloth. "It was true — every word of it true... and she could not make her mouth say it back across the cloth." That gap between her body and her mouth has been the book's whole subject and here it's finally sitting at the table with her, and Randi names it out loud: "You *did* it. And you can't even say it." I felt that land in my own chest.

Randi moved for me this chapter, and I want to be precise about which direction. The interrogation — "Did you take it all?" "Didn't spill a drop?" — is the most nakedly *wanting* she has ever been in Vee's presence. Her breath goes "not quite steady." Her knee comes to rest against Vee's and stays. Her fingernail rides the rim of the glass while Vee describes a mouth parked at the inside of a wrist. I know what that fingernail means; I watched it during the robin's-egg-blue story on Tuesday. She is not just collecting the debrief anymore; she's living in it, and the mask is slipping in measurable millimeters. And then the chapter's quiet detonation: "*I don't.*" Flat. Level. Followed by "It was powerful. It turned me on" — delivered, devastatingly, "in the voice she used for restaurant recommendations." That's Randi telling the truth about herself, possibly for the first time at one of these tables, disguised as no big deal. It wrecked me a little.

And here's my honest ambivalence, which the page fully earned: what Randi does in this chapter — refusing the sanded version ("Don't you fade to firelight... Give me the real one — the harder one"), then quietly dismantling the installed voice that made Vee say *gross, wrong* — is the kindest thing anyone has done for Vee's shame in fifty chapters. It is also, exactly, what a woman would do who once said "only in a threesome, with you" and has been playing a long game ever since. The mushroom joke — "Did you swallow all of it and wipe your mouth?" — is filthy and hilarious and it is also *the same wire*: taste, enthusiasm, appetite, a woman's flavor. I can't tell where the love ends and the management begins, and I no longer believe Randi can either, and I think that's the point. I'm not calling it sinister. I'm calling it *loaded*.

Pace, offstage entirely, comes out of this chapter glowing — refracted through two women retelling him. "He parked" at the toll booth of her wrist. "He opened me like the orange." That callback made me put the book down for a second; the blood oranges were the most-him thing I'd ever seen done to food, and Vee found the same sentence I did.

**Swoon.** The Lifesaver. That tiny wordless transaction — the candy tapped against Randi's wrist, "the palm closed and neither of them looked away from the front, and for a while the faint cool of wintergreen came off them both, one lecture, one flavor" — while Dr. Marsh stands at the front lecturing on *grooming* as the true currency of intimacy. The lecture captioned the whole book again (the stats course used to do this; the anthro course has picked up the shift without missing a beat) — "watch who grooms whom in a troop for one week and you can draw the whole politics of it" — and I sat there doing exactly that: watching who grooms whom. Randi grooms Vee. Has since October. And now, the neck kiss: Vee "already looking at the neck," "smiling before she'd decided to," landing the kiss "before any of her had convened on the question" — and under her mouth, Randi's "catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long." *A beat too long.* Vee thinks Randi "must have forgotten" the ticklish spot; but the page also says Randi "tipped her head to settle the hug deeper and laid the whole of it open," and I am not at all sure *forgot* is the word. I'm leaving that ajar because the page does — but my pulse noticed.

**Erotic charge.** Working, absolutely — and interestingly, it's all *retold* heat that keeps going present-tense: Vee's mouth going "actually dry" at "didn't spill a drop," her body clenching in the booth at the memory of the wet kiss, the flush "gone down past her collar." The peak for me was the double structure of "It was powerful. It turned me on" landing on Vee mid-clench — Randi's confession and Vee's body agreeing before Vee's mouth can. The woman-to-woman simmer (knee, waist, neck, breath) runs under everything and never announces itself. That restraint is why it burns.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** One earned suspicion, stated carefully: "You picked well," Randi says — and Vee herself notices "the one word that wasn't quite true — she hadn't picked Pace so much as looked up... to find him already halfway across the floor at her, the whole thing decided before she'd known there was a thing to decide." Vee brushes it off as a technicality. I nearly said something out loud to the page. *Randi* picked. Randi said "Her" on a quad. That compliment is either Randi congratulating her own selection to Vee's face, or something stranger — an almost-confession, a woman handing her friend authorship as a gift. Either way it's the closest the secret has ever come to the table, and Vee walked right past it. The dramatic irony in this book has stopped being a hum and become a note I can hear.

**Friction.** Very little. One small thing: "The folder wouldn't sit flat in the drawer" — the narration naming Vee's own filing system a touch too neatly; I'd already felt the unease without the label. And I'll note, gently, that this is the second nearly-identical evening in a row — same class, same restaurant, same ten-o'clock shutdown, same sidewalk kiss — and while I believe the ritual is *forming on purpose* (Tuesday-and-Thursday, all spring, a done thing), a third debrief in this exact shape would start to sag. The escalation inside the ritual carried this one.

**The titles.** "clean-plate" / *A Clean Plate* — this one blooms after reading. Vee polishes her plate "without noticing she was doing it"; Randi takes "one small, exact bite" and leaves the rest — the two of them in one image, all appetite versus all control. And behind it: "didn't spill a drop," the swallowed everything, the clean version of a story versus the real one. The title didn't give anything away; it paid out afterward, which is my favorite kind. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* — keeps doing its slow work: sup with the devil with a long spoon, and here's another warm meal where the warmth is real *and* the spoon keeps shortening. No tagline in what I was given. "A Warm Reception" felt this chapter like it named the table itself: Randi receiving Vee's whole weekend, warmly, hungrily, without a scrap of mercy.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Thursday after Thursday of this and I know I shouldn't. Guesses from here, marked as guesses: Randi is normalizing the taste of a woman on a mouth — hers included in the precedent — and I think, whether she's steering or only wanting, that the road runs toward Vee's mouth and Randi's skin; the neck kiss is already halfway there, and neither of them has "convened on the question." And Dr. Marsh's course is going to keep captioning it: somebody in that room is going to say what we spend the grooming budget on now, and I suspect the answer is *this* — these dinners.
