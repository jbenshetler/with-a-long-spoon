## Reader reaction

This chapter had me grinning like an idiot for its whole first half and then quietly holding my breath for the last three pages, and I want to be exact about both.

The morning-after is pure swoon, and I refuse to apologize for it. Vee waking into "the acreage" of that bed, correcting "slept alone" to the past tense "with her whole body" — that's the happiness of the coat night still paying out, and after the month of silence and the wrecked Christmas I *earned* this morning right alongside her. The bread timed so she wakes inside the smell of it ("Even his absence had been cooked in advance") is exactly the Pace tenderness I half-trust, and the sweats left folded — which she looks at, smiles at, and leaves — is Vee's whole arc in one gesture. Six months ago this girl wore cardigans over cardigans. Now she stands at his counter in nothing but a green scrunchie and decides her nakedness is a throne, and the "pleasure princess" inversion she works out on her fingers in the hallway — not kept *for* a man's pleasure but kept *in* pleasure — made me laugh out loud with her. "I got mushrooms," delivered "level as a plank" by a man visibly picking his composure up off the floor like dropped change, is the funniest line in the book so far. And the pointing game — her finger commanding her own wrist, her nipple, "fair was fair; a princess keeps order in her realm" — is the most joyful sex this book has written. She's running the scene. After everything, after being steered at every fork by people she can't see, watching Vee point and be obeyed felt like justice, even though I know it's a justice inside a room two other people built.

The erotic charge is the strongest in the book, and I say that having read "Famished" and the coat. The orange is doing double work — "he was opening her as he had opened the orange, nothing hurried, nothing skipped" — and the sequence from the held-open breathing to the noisy, relishing drinking of her is graphic, sustained, and completely alive because her shame is in it, un-pardoned: "he did not skip past it or pardon it or wait for it to thin. He was drinking it." The cringe-and-heat "one arrival, indivisible" — that's the book's central wire, rendered at full voltage. I was gone.

And then the title lands. "Forbidden Kiss" — I'd assumed, going in, it meant Randi. It doesn't. It's Vee's own taste on his mouth, and the sequence of her response is devastatingly precise: the tongue goes first — "curious, unforbidden, hers" — and *then* the mind arrives, and behind it "the flat clean voice that had been installed in her before she could have argued with it." The mother's voice again, not saying floozie this time, just "that's not a thing you do." Her body said yes before her upbringing said no. The child's gesture of the hand across the mouth, "Ugh — gross," and then the legs clamping him in — "reading which no was which" — is consent writing at its best: he reads the two no's correctly, wipes his mouth once, "unbothered," and doesn't sulk or push or explain. And then the cost, which she doesn't register until afterward: his mouth never comes back to hers. Not once. That absence, felt only in retrospect — "somewhere in the pleasure she had missed it without knowing what she was missing" — hurt me more than the fight in chapter 45 did.

The afterglow is where the chapter earns its keep as more than heat. Her probing "the way you probe a place you've bitten" turns up the real question — *he liked it. Why does he like it so much* — and then her tongue goes out over her own lip, "looking for something," and she catches it doing it. That's the book's whole method in one image: her body already answering a question her mind won't ask. The tidy explanation she grabs ("he must have forgotten to wipe") is so obviously a comfort she's choosing, and the page says so — "it settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting." I'm leaving that ajar exactly as the page does. But I can't not hear the echo: the whole plot began with Pace asking Randi "Would you like to taste another woman?" Vee has just met the taste of a woman — herself — and her installed voice slammed the door on it while her tongue had already said yes. If the game is still running, this chapter is a hinge, and only I can see it turn.

Which brings me to the ending, the one place my stomach dropped. "She would tell her everything. Well. Nearly everything… Randi would know what to make of it. Randi always did." Vee is about to carry her tenderest, most unexamined question — the one about taste, about her own body's forbidden curiosity — straight to the woman who authored the plan and who is in love with her and who has every reason to know *exactly* what to make of it. That last line is warm on Vee's side and ice on mine. That's not suspicion of the text; that's the text using everything I know that she doesn't, cleanly, without a thumb on the scale.

Trust and suspicion, plainly: I have no new suspicion of Pace from this page — his behavior throughout is generous, patient, exact, and his handling of her recoil is genuinely lovely. What unsettles me is not on the page as menace at all; it's the dramatic irony the ending arms. Friction: almost none. If I quibble, the flavor-writing runs one course long — "a smell with shoulders on it" delighted me, but by the persimmon-and-salt paragraph I was ready to get back to the people. And "thorough as an honest tradesman" made me smile in a way I'm not sure was intended. Tiny things.

The book's title and tagline keep tightening. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is a yes given so freely she points with her own finger, and the one *no* she gives is the installed voice, not hers. *A Polite Invitation*: her tongue's reach toward the taste was, in miniature, an invitation her mind declined on old orders. And the long spoon hangs over every warm meal — this one had blood oranges.

What I want next: the Tuesday dinner. I dread it and I can't wait. My guess from here — and it's a guess — is that Randi will receive the "strange small thing" like a jeweler receiving a stone, and gently, laughingly, begin to reframe "gross" into "curious." That's what every brunch has done. I'll be watching her face the whole time.
