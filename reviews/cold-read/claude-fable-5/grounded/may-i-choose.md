# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

This chapter is a swoon, and I want to say that first and plainly, because I spent the whole read split in half: one half of me was Vee, grinning at a dark screen, and the other half was the reader the jacket made me — the one who knows this dinner exists because a man pointed at women on a quad until his girlfriend's body said "Her," and because that same girlfriend executed a shoulder-collision so clean it looked like the redhead's fault. The chapter itself never touches that. It doesn't have to. It just lets Vee stop bracing — "that low background readiness she carried everywhere, the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there, and she let it not be there" — and I sat there knowing there is a bill, of some kind, already running. That's not suspicion I manufactured; the book handed it to me chapters ago and then wrote the tenderest date I've read in a long time on top of it. The ache is the point, and it worked on me completely.

Where it landed: the text that "didn't say where, and he didn't ask her to answer that night." Vee's reading of it — "He just left it with me. Like it could keep" — undid me a little, because it's exactly the thing she's never had, and because I've watched Pace do this by design with Randi too: the room he leaves is his signature, and I no longer know how to feel about the room. With Randi I've seen what waits inside it (the bench, the "please" broken open). With Vee, so far, the room is only kind. The elevator beat — her forgetting to get off, throwing out a hand "begging the doors for mercy," him having "the grace not to show it" — was pure charm, and I laughed.

The meal is the heart of it, and it's doing the hunger work this book has been doing since the apple slices. Randi gets an apple sliced thin because thin is what her policing permits; Vee, who "hadn't eaten since breakfast," gets bread torn hot and two dips chosen for her, and "it kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line is the jacket's "quietly starving" made literal, and it's gorgeous and it hurt. She eats without watching herself eat. Nobody in her life has ever let her do that. The contrast between how Pace feeds these two women — portioning one, filling the other — is the most interesting thing in the book right now, and I don't know yet what it means about him. I'm leaving it ajar because the page does.

The title line: "May I choose?" I heard it the way Vee heard "May I call you?" — the same courtly asking — and the relief that goes through her at handing the choice across the table is real and lovely. And still. The book is called *A Polite Invitation*, and the blurb ends "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," and here is a chapter whose title is a request for permission to choose *for* her, granted gladly, over a menu she can't read. On the page it's only dinner. Under the page it's the whole architecture: he chooses, she says "Yes. Please," and it's the best thing that's ever happened to her. Nothing in the chapter is coercive — that's precisely what makes it merciless. The title illuminated the chapter and then recolored it on the walk home, and it gave nothing away in advance; I'd guessed it might be Vee's choosing, and in the end it is that too — "I'm making an exception" is her choosing, cleanly, hers. That last beat is perfect: her rule arriving "at the worst possible moment," him letting it close without argument, and her feet simply not going. He gave up the kiss and she closed the distance he'd surrendered. That's the mechanism the jacket promised — the open door as temptation — enacted in miniature, and as a piece of romance it is genuinely swoony even while I can see the shape of it.

One echo unsettled me more than anything overtly ominous: Vee can't keep the words. "She couldn't, later, have laid out what they talked about" — which is exactly, almost verbatim, what happened at Chi Latte with Randi ("the feeling was total and the transcript was blank"). Both of the people who came to her by arrangement produce the same amnesia in her: the words go, the feeling stays. I don't think Vee has noticed the rhyme. I have, and it's the one place this chapter's warmth carried a chill for me.

Cassie remains the person I trust most in this book, and her verdict — "That's not a boy. That's a man." — landed complicated. She's the one who said "Goodbye, Vee" like a longer parting; she reads people flat and true; and she approves of Pace. Either her radar has finally missed, or Pace is, in some real sense, exactly what he appears to be at this table — and I notice the book has never yet shown him being false, only selective. I'm holding that open. The right-hand thing — "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept" — I genuinely don't know what it is. A first sighting of something. I'm not going to guess; Vee files it as "one more thing he knew that she didn't," and so do I.

Randi is entirely absent from this chapter — not present, not even mentioned — and her absence is loud. The last interior I had from her was "she was going to have to figure out what she was going to do about this," and then a helpless "Yes" at a redhead on a quad. Somewhere offstage, she knows this date is happening. I felt her not-being-there the whole time.

Erotic charge: low and deliberately so — this is the courtship chapter, all its heat sublimated into food and hands. The hand-taking is the peak, and it's written like a sex scene in miniature: "like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly." That line got me.

Friction: almost none. If I reach — "one to wake you and one to fold you under," on the two dips, leans slightly toward telling me what the evening is doing to her; her own "imagined, dimly" softens it enough. And the blank-transcript beat, because it repeats Chi Latte so closely, flirted with feeling like a device — but I think the rhyme is the meaning, so I'll allow it.

What I want next: I want the second date and I dread the machinery resuming. Guesses from here: Randi will reappear in Vee's orbit soon and the "lunches too intimate to name" the jacket promised will begin; and at some point Vee's two amnesias — Randi's and Pace's — will be in the same room. *With a Long Spoon* keeps whispering its proverb at me: he who sups with the devil should use one. Vee just ate with her hands.
