# Cold read (grounded) — On Her Floor

## Reader reaction

So it finally happened, and the book made it happen in the one way Vee can't keep it. That's what I'm sitting with. Not the kiss itself — the kiss is glorious, I'll get there — but the frame around it: "You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it." I actually flinched. Randi built a room where Vee's realest response could occur in public, drew it out of her with both hands — literally, the hands on her hips, the arms lifted and held, the mouth at her ear all night — and then, the moment Vee stopped performing entirely ("She had not been performing — somewhere in there she had stopped entirely"), handed her a cover story that renames the truest thing Vee has ever felt about her as acting. That is the cruelest tenderness in the book so far, and I can't fully tell whether it's cruelty. From where I sit, knowing Gstaad — "let Vee arrive at it on her own" — this looks like the engineering of the arrival: give her the taste, deny her the name, let the want do the walking. But I also watched Randi come back from that kiss "a moment behind," not speaking, blinking, shaking her head, the grin arriving *after*. She got caught in her own show too. The page leaves that ajar and I'm leaving it ajar: I don't know how much of "I almost believed it" was management and how much was Randi slamming her own door.

The kiss itself worked on me completely. The held inch before it — "the held inch became its own long event" — is the sexiest thing in the chapter, sexier than the tongue, because it's the moment Vee's eyes finally do "as they had wanted to all autumn and never let themselves." All those months of goodbye kisses, the perfume she's "been quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping the file" — the book has been banking this since chapter 17 and it spent the account in one go. "Not a demand, a *please*" for Randi's tongue is a devastating little line: Randi asking, in the only language she'll permit herself. And Vee's hand going up into Randi's hair "without asking her" — Vee escalating, Vee wanting, Vee *giving it back*. Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over; the jacket line has never been truer or worse.

And the whole night was a machine and I watched every gear and still got swept. The skirt is Randi's — that's the psychological genius of it, and Vee even names the mechanism to herself: "in Randi's skirt the wanting wasn't hers to answer for." Borrowed permission. Then the margarita she didn't see ordered, the second one; the hands moving her hips "wider, looser, an arc Vee would never have given herself"; the wrists held up so "there was nothing Vee could do about it, because Randi had her hands" — and then "Your panties are showing," which lands shame and heat down the same wire on schedule, the mother's "floozie" arriving right on cue, and *underneath the shame* the other thing turning over. Vee "found out twice over: once to the room, and once, worse, to herself." That sentence is the chapter. The exposure isn't the underwear; it's the want.

How I feel about the people, moving: **Randi** — this is the most nakedly manipulative I've ever seen her with Vee on the page, and simultaneously the most nakedly wanting. Both readings sharpened at once, which is this book's whole trick. The Love bracelet exchange chilled me in a way the party couldn't warm: "Oh, you don't. It's a real commitment. Taking it off requires tools." Said lightly, about herself, to the girl who doesn't know who put it there — and the page doesn't say who put it there either, and I'm not deciding, but "locked on her wrist" from Gstaad is ringing loudly. **Vee** — my heart is in pieces for her. The final paragraph — "her body had not been told... She knew she had kissed Randi. She knew it had been good. She knew she wanted to do it again so badly her hands were not quite steady, and that there was no version of the night in which she could ask" — is Vee's interior at its finest: honest right up to the exact line she cannot cross. **Brooke** — the Chambord bit is a gorgeous double image ("She'll spend a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime" — Randi, describe yourself), and Brooke *not* mentioning the December crying visit read to Vee as kindness and read to me as a woman showing Randi, over Vee's shoulder, exactly how complete her file is. Vee felt Randi's shoulder arrive "warm against her arm — there, where a second before there had been nothing." Randi moved to guard her. Or to guard the file. Ajar. **Cassie** — four lines, all of them perfect. "That hem's not going to stay put" and the eyebrow at "Tonight I'm Randi's date," up a half-inch and held. She sees it. She's been seeing it since the first hello.

And **Pace**, absent, gets the chapter's quietest horror: "He gets me Friday. He gets me Sunday. You get me Saturday night." Vee says it glowing, the luckiest girl — and I hear a custody schedule drawn up by two people who confer about her. After three chapters of him being luminous (the roadhouse, Sheri, the wax she wore home for him), his absence here is its own presence.

Friction: almost none. If I reach — Harrison is a pure prop and the book knows it ("that was the whole use of him"), which is honest but makes the "show" excuse tissue-thin even inside the fiction; I half-wondered whether Vee would clock how thin it was. But then, that's the point: she takes the cover "because it was the only thing held out to her." I believed it. The unnamed beer boy's "Both of you... Neither one of you—" is a lovely small beat — the outside world reading the two of them correctly before Vee can.

The title: "On Her Floor" — I love how many ways it opens. The dance floor is Randi's floor, her house's party, her territory, and Vee spends the night being run on it. But it also whispers the other sense — where Vee ends up, figuratively: on Randi's ground, on her terms, floored. And there's a faint third: Vee's dorm floor, Cassie watching from the bunk, the world she came from. It didn't give anything away; it recolors afterward. The book's title keeps sharpening — *A Polite Invitation*: "Wanna go to a party with me?" and the yes "with no floor under it," out before the question finished. Every invitation in this book is polite and every one is load-bearing. *With a Long Spoon* — I thought of the proverb at the punch tub, all that hidden Chambord: sup with the devil; you won't even taste what you're drinking. And the jacket's "without quite letting herself know it, wanting the woman beside her" — this chapter is that sentence detonated. The tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" has never fit a chapter better.

What I want next: I want Vee at the debrief brunch — how do you debrief *this* to the person it happened with? I dread and want the next goodbye kiss, which can never again be "a French thing." Guesses from here: Randi will act as if nothing happened, and the acting will cost her; Vee will carry the kiss to no one — not Cassie, not Pace — and the first thing she edits out for Randi's sake instead of her own will be this. And somewhere the two "Pace" worlds are still on a fuse this chapter did nothing to defuse — Brooke now has Vee's face filed next to Randi's name.
