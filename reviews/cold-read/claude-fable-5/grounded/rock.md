# Cold read (grounded) — Rock

## Reader reaction

This is the chapter where I stopped being able to hold Pace at arm's length, and I'm not sure I forgive the book for how it did it.

The setting is almost nothing — a quilt, cold tea, the black ridge and hard stars on the other side of the glass, "the smallness of it was the point" — and inside that nothing the book quietly hands over more of Pace than the previous twenty-three chapters combined. He is twenty-two. His real name is Peter. His math teacher put his desk in the hall for a year because he asked questions she couldn't answer. And Daphne — the name that's been sitting sealed since the house tour — was the tutor they assigned him when he arrived at college at fifteen. She was nineteen. It went on two years.

Let me do the body first, as instructed. What I felt during the Daphne passage was a slow cold drop, the exact one Vee has: "She did the arithmetic without wanting to. It landed low and heavy in her stomach before it was a thought." And what I felt right after that was worse, because the machinery of the whole book turned over in my chest without anyone naming it: "Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong." That sentence is the jacket copy. The jacket says of Vee: "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over." Pace says of his own fifteen-year-old self, of the best two years he'd had, "It would have been stranger if she'd stayed." He hands Vee the thing that hurt him and names it luck — the page says exactly that — and I sat there realizing that the man is not running a cold game on Vee so much as living inside a grammar he was taught at fifteen, where the older lover who chooses you, tutors you, tends you, and never technically wrongs you is simply what love is. He met Vee by tutoring her. The Euler substitution, the pencil handed back. I don't think he knows he's Daphne now. I don't think the book said that. But my stomach said it, and I'm going to trust my stomach and mark the rest as a guess from here.

And — this is the part I called merciless — the chapter makes him more lovable at the exact moment it makes him more legible as damage. Twenty-two! I had him at late twenties, easy; the "young mathematician" of the jacket is barely a year older than Vee. The powerlifter body, the woodwork, the sewing, the cooking, the courtliness: I'd read all of it as a grown man's settled competence, and now it reads as a boy who was thrown out of childhood at fifteen and built a whole adulthood by hand, the way he builds everything, because no one issued him one. "I was not a good fit where I was." "Less than staying." The desk in the hall — "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" — Vee's line, and it's perfect, and his answer ("That is approximately what happened") is more heartbreaking for its precision. I like him more than ever and trust the situation less than ever, and both of those got stronger in the same twenty minutes. That's this book's signature move and it has never done it this hard.

The swoon is real, too, and I want to name it honestly. The hand settling over her breast "without pressing, without asking, as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm" — that's tenderness rendered as fact, not performance. The math aria genuinely lifted me: the shape you can fill with a cup of paint but never paint the outside of; the coastline that gets longer the shorter your ruler; Newton's three sentences and the ball and the leaf and the stars "all doing the one thing." "Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some" — I felt Vee's vertigo at the window, the stars turning from decoration into bodies, "not because she understood the mathematics, but because he did, and he had turned the understanding toward her like light." And Vee is so good in this chapter — the unopened saffron "old enough to vote," the trade she offers ("She had given him a whole marching season" is a line I'd frame), and above all the word she holds down under her tongue. The clean ugly grown-up word. She doesn't say it, and the not-saying is the most adult, most loving thing anyone has done in this book. Then the scar: "Not tonight," his hand pressing hers down onto it rather than moving it away — "no wall in it, only a door with a time on it that wasn't now" — and she turns her palm over so they're holding hands on top of his oldest wound. The doors motif has been running since Randi's deadbolt and Daphne's door, and this is its gentlest form yet. And the last "Peter," said "the way you say a word to keep it" — I melted, plainly.

Erotic charge: low flame by design. Skin under robe, the breast, the hip, and it stays warmth rather than heat; after the heat of the last several chapters, this restraint felt right, not slack.

Friction: almost none. The math speech flirts with going one beat long, but it's so exactly this man that I'll allow it. One thing I'm holding open rather than resolving: my memory has a Daphne fragment about a door he violated and paid dearly for, and tonight's Daphne story doesn't contain a door at all. Same Daphne, presumably — which means there's a second half of this story he didn't tell, maybe the half the scar belongs to. The chapter itself leaves it shut, so I do too.

The title, "Rock": oblique and rich. The black ridge rock outside the glass; the bent-steel stillness of the squat bar ("nothing in my mind but that moment") that Vee recognizes as how he is with her — "It's the lift. It's the same face"; the sense of him as her rock, steady and unmoving as the stars that only look unmoving — he just told her everything is moving, all one piece. It gave nothing away and it recolors the whole chapter afterward. As for *A Polite Invitation* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is the first time that tagline pointed backward at Pace instead of forward at Vee, and it made the series title's long spoon feel less like a warning about a devil and more like a question about who taught whom to dine this way.

What I want next: I dread and need the scar story, and I dread more the day Vee's held-down word about Daphne meets the plan she doesn't know she's inside. Guess from here: when Vee finally learns about Randi and Pace, this chapter is the one she'll replay.
