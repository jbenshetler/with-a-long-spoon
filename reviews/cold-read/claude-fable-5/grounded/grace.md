# Cold read (grounded) — Grace

## Reader reaction

Felt read

Oh, this one got me. I want to be honest about that first, before I say anything careful: the mirror-and-blindfold sequence is the most sustained, most tender, most fully *earned* erotic set piece the book has given me, and I read the back half of it with my breath actually short. Whatever else I say below, the swoon came first and it was total.

Start with the small stuff, because the small stuff is where Pace lives. Chicken tikka masala carried up a mountain in a thermos so it would still be hot on a freezing porch; a dry spiced cider he chose *because* it would cut the richness — "I wanted to please you," said the way he says everything, a fact. And Vee's read on it is the truest sentence in the chapter's first movement: "He carried her into rooms she wasn't even in." That is exactly what has always undone me about this man, and exactly what has always unnerved me, because I remember the heat turned up hours early for two different women. Here, alone with Vee, it read pure. I let it.

The key. A box he made with his hands, a brass key on pale silk — and I felt the cold finger before Vee did, because *she* half-recognizes the silk against her skin and lets it slide off her, and I sat there going: where have we seen oyster-pale silk, what was cut from what, what else has that bench made. The page leaves it open and I'll leave it open, but that little "the not-knowing slid off her as soon as it came" is the book doing its signature move — handing me a splinter Vee refuses to feel. And then the word-ledger, which broke my heart gently: he said *house*. "Some small exact part of her... waited half a beat for the other one, the one with a hearth in it. The other one didn't come." She is getting so practiced at letting it go. "She was good, now, at letting it go" — that sentence is doing quiet damage and knows it.

One more splinter, and it's the strangest in the chapter: at the mirror she describes *him* — "that little birthmark by his ear" — and "something crossed his face — a shadow, there and gone." I have no idea what that was. Being seen instead of seeing? Something about the birthmark itself? The page gives me nothing to resolve it with, so it just sits there ticking, and I notice the book has trained me to notice when Pace flinches, because he almost never does.

Now the long scene. What made it work on me is that it's the whole novel's argument run in miniature: every part of her she has spent her life hiding gets named, plainly, in full daylight, and named as treasure. "The spill is the part I want most" — the exact thing she hides, wanted, in one breath. "Peach gone into milk." The little toe that "had gone its whole life unadmired." The blindfold being *his flannel* — the shirt-motif sewn into a new object — got a genuine ache out of me. And the naming of her sex, the flinch-and-burn of the plain word, the wet framed as "the most honest thing I've ever stood in front of" — this is the shame-and-desire wire the book has been running since chapter one, and here Pace deliberately runs current down it in the direction of grace. Then the turn past the body: "Your body's only the part of you I get to put my hands on." The flannel over her eyes going damp and her not knowing when. Reader down.

"Go ahead." The refusal to touch her, the long silence, "Show me" — and her discovery that some of what he gives her she cannot give herself ("you can no more spank yourself than tickle yourself") is a genuinely brilliant piece of interiority, funny and sad and hot at once. "What are you thinking about?" — "You, you, you," the word breaking down into a keen. That is the closest thing to "I love you" this book has let anybody say, and she says it, blind, with her own hand, while he watches. It leveled me.

And then he takes her without asking — "the careful man who had asked her leave for every step of the whole long night had run clean out of asking" — and I want to be precise about how that landed: not as a violation, because the page has spent fifty chapters building the consent architecture that makes his losing it legible to her as *proof*, and she names it power, wants it, has wanted exactly this since the reconciliation sex in December. Within these two people it read as the gift she's been trying to wring out of him. I felt the heat of it, not the alarm.

Where the unease is earned — and it is, quietly, right at the end. Afterglow, his hand on her, and then: "Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?" And the narration flags it in Vee's own grammar: the yes comes out "easy and breathy and barely attended at all, the way you say yes to something small." I know that yes. It's the wax-chapter yes — "out ahead of her, her mouth answering before her mind had caught up" — the yes whose fear arrives after, once it's spent and can't be called back. The book has taught me its shape, and here it is again, closing the chapter, another door opened on a light word. That's not me manufacturing shadow; the page italicized it for me. What that door is *for* I don't know, and I note — with a small chill I can't fully justify — that this is the first chapter in a long while with no Randi in it at all, not even a mention, and somehow the machinery hums louder in her absence.

The titles. "Grace" is lovely and layered after the fact: grace as the unearned gift (the key, the litany — being given what she never asked for and couldn't earn); grace said over food, which this couple practically worships with; grace as what he keeps extending to her body where her mother extended judgment; maybe even a grace period — the word not said, the clock still running. It gave nothing away and paid out on every reading. *A Polite Invitation* keeps darkening by the chapter — every yes here was freely given, breathily given, barely attended — and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" was practically stage-directing that last page. *With a Long Spoon* hangs where it always hangs: over the warmest meal in the book, eaten on a porch at the edge of nowhere.

What I want next: I want this weekend to be real and unrevoked. What I dread (guesses from here): that the touch-and-tell promise is an instrument — that what she reports will travel, the way everything she tells travels; that the pale silk and the box mean the bench's hands have been in this room all along; and that the key to his house is being given to a woman who doesn't know who else has walked through that door. The happier guess: that "you, you, you" is the word arriving by another road, and he heard it.

**Symbolism:** The key on silk in a handmade box — belonging made object in place of belonging made word; she can close her fingers around a key, "and the word she wanted had only ever been air." The blindfold — she gives the looking over to him: sight itself as the thing consented away, in a book about who sees whom. The foxed mirror — the self she still can't hold her eyes on, described to her instead.

**Characterization:** Vee deepened — the self-touch discovery ("some of what he gave her she could not give herself") and her practiced letting-go are new, exact interior ground. Pace deepened at the edges — the litany shows a verbal, almost devotional register he's never spent at this length, and the run-out-of-asking break is the most human he's been; the birthmark shadow adds a hairline crack. No one flattened; no one else on stage.

**Pace — within the chapter:** The litany is very long and knows it — time "come loose" is the point — and for me it earned every inch; the only micro-drag was the second pass down her front (belly/hips) where the structure briefly shows. The porch and lake sections are perfectly weighted breathers.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** After the crowded roadhouse, the taproom, the party, and the gym, a two-person chapter at full intimacy is exactly the right rotation — the run's rhythm of rooms-full-of-people then rooms-of-two is holding. Momentum: building, but by deepening rather than event; the only plot-motion is the key and the closing promise, and both feel like fuses rather than payoffs.
