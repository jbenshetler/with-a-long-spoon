## Reader reaction

A quiet chapter, and I was glad of it — after five lit fuses (the sorority, MIRH, the bench standing in the bedroom), a Sunday-dinner debrief with Cassie felt like being handed a glass of water. And then the chapter turned out to be doing something that made my stomach dip anyway, without a single fuse burning.

Here's what got me: Vee spends the whole scene cataloguing her own edits. She cropped the photo before sending it home — "The version they have stops at the waist." She told her dad "the parts that fit." She withheld the cooking, the house, and above all the sewing. And I sat there realizing I was watching her do to her parents exactly what has been done to her all book — hand someone a version cropped at the waist, warm and true as far as it goes, with the real engine of the thing cut out of frame. She doesn't know that's the shape of her own life. I do. That's the ache of this chapter, and it arrives with no violence at all, just a girl turning her fork over at a cafeteria table.

The middle stretch is the best writing here — the dad "pricing" the dress, and then this: "Honestly, my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's exactly right about a certain kind of father, and it pulled the same short laugh out of me it pulled out of Cassie. And the wrong-versus-weird distinction — "Weird is something that doesn't fit anywhere in how he sees the world" — is Vee thinking well, which I love watching her do. Then she plants her flag: "I'm not ashamed of it. The opposite, if anything." From the girl whose mother's voice said "floozie" at every arousal, that sentence is a milestone she doesn't even notice she's passing. The shame wire really has been rewired. (By whom, and for what — that's the shadow I bring, not one the page waves at me.)

The mother detail wrecked me a little in both directions: she cried twice over the honor society, printed the photo, carried it in her purse, showed it to people who'd already seen it — and also "touched her own collarbone while she looked" at the shoulders of the dress. Pride and the old policing in the same purse. "You know how she does." "I know how she does." Cassie's echo there is the whole friendship in five words.

And Cassie. God, I trust her. "So what is he... Not to your dad. To you." Careful, exact, not prying — and it lands harder than any of Randi's silken extractions ever has, because Cassie wants nothing from the answer except Vee's good. And Vee can't answer. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" "Both. Probably both." That "both" is the truest thing Vee says in the chapter, and the closing line is the truest thing the chapter says about Vee: Cassie doesn't push, "Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not." Part of her wants to be pushed. Part of her wants someone to make her look. Cassie won't force the door — that's her ethic — and so the door stays shut, and I felt the cost of the courtesy. It's the gentlest possible rhyme with the whole book's mechanism: everyone leaves Vee the room to choose, and the room is where the danger lives.

My feeling about Vee moved a half-step: I noticed she now instinctively curates for everyone — Cassie gets the edited version, the parents get the cropped one, Randi gets the full nude. There's a ranking implied in who gets how much of her, and Randi is at the top of it. That chilled me a little, quietly.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and correctly none — one wry flash ("takes that dress off me") and the memory-heat of the dress itself. The chapter isn't reaching for heat and doesn't sag for lacking it.

Friction: almost none. If I squint, Vee's dad-analysis runs a touch essayistic for cafeteria speech — "An expensive gift from a man means his daughter is in deeper than he'd pictured" is very polished for a girl who "put a question mark on" the word boyfriend — but the chapter frames her as "finding the size of it" as she talks, so I mostly bought it.

The title: "Cropped" did quiet, excellent work. Before reading I guessed photo; after reading it's photo, story, self — the version that stops at the waist, told to everyone she loves. And it rhymes backward hard: two chapters ago Randi stung her with "you only gave him half?" and Vee proudly handed over the uncropped everything. Her parents get the crop; Randi gets it all. The title makes me see that asymmetry cold. The book's title keeps ripening the same way — "A Polite Invitation" is now also Cassie's polite non-invitation to confess, declined; and the jacket's "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" sits behind Vee's "I'm not ashamed of it" like a watermark.

What I want next: I dread the sorority collision more than anything — Vee saying "boyfriend" out loud for the first time here feels like the word going live, ready to be overheard. Guess from here: the anthro class "just us" or the next brunch escalates Vee-and-Randi past what Vee can crop out of her own account of herself. And I want one more scene of Cassie pushing, just once, before it's too late.
