# Cold read (grounded) — Another Round

## Reader reaction

The felt read

This one worked on me like the taproom worked on Vee: I came in braced and left disarmed, and I want to be honest about both halves.

The setup is a mirror of "Among Friends" and I felt the rhyme immediately — same little kitchen, same wine, same back at the sink, and then the same grocery-list detonation: last time "I want to meet your people," now "There's somebody I'd like you to meet." Except this time the gift has a blade in it, and the chapter knows it. The paragraph where the name lands is one of the best interior beats in a while: "the knowing turned out to be a coat of paint. Under it the cold arrived anyway, unscheduled, a train she'd been told was cancelled." Vee dismantled the imaginary blonde with Randi back in January and here is the book showing me — correctly, humanly — that insight doesn't kill jealousy, it just renames it. And Pace's list of facts — "Small." "Loud." "We cycle." "She dances." — read to me exactly as it read to Vee: a man handing over the one fact with an edge "like a dried spoon," with no idea it had an edge. Or with an idea. I genuinely can't tell, and I like that I can't.

The dressing scene hurt in the right way. "She dressed for the woman her jealousy had built, and she knew it while she was doing it, and she did it anyway" — that's the book's whole method in one sentence, self-awareness that changes nothing, and the induction heels coming out of the closet (Randi kneeling on a boutique floor, a card with nothing behind it — I remember every layer of those shoes) made her armor literally borrowed and literally Randi's. She goes to meet Pace's other woman wearing Randi's courtship on her feet. The page doesn't say that. I felt it anyway.

And then Sheri. God, I loved Sheri on the page. The checkpoint gave me her in outline; here she arrives at full volume and the chapter's best trick is the appraising look that Vee braces against — waiting for the pricing look, the placing look — and it turns out to be "enjoying her." The laugh that comes out of Vee "before she'd voted on it," a month leaving in one breath, nobody having said a word — that's the release valve of the whole chapter and it landed physically on me too. The motorboat line, and then Pace blushing — "past anything deniable" — was pure delight; Vee's right, it's the most disarming thing he's done, and it did more to humanize him than any of his competence ever has.

The coming-out story is the chapter's heavy center and it earns its stillness. "Whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish." "Easy words, for a hard thing. The ones who owed me words weren't spending any." I sat very still with Vee. And I could not miss — the book made sure I couldn't — that Sheri's grief runs on the same rail as Vee's wound: words withheld by the people who owe them. Pace's answer to Sheri's catastrophe was presence without one false word — coffee, donuts, the lock, the faucet, the bike — and Vee's "Bacon cheeseburgers," said to the window, told me she's re-filing the unsaid "I love you" under believe what I do all over again. It's moving. It's also the exact anesthetic that keeps her from pressing on the one thing he won't give her. I felt both at once and I think I'm meant to.

Two real drops of new information: "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen" — the parents door opens one inch, and Sheri, who owns "every word of it I've got," is the one holding it. And "he does go on about you" — Pace talking about Vee, gravely, somewhere she wasn't — warmed me clean through, same as her.

Now the unease, and it's earned, and it's specific: I know something Vee doesn't about the woman across the table. Sheri knows about Randi. "Two of them at once… complicated" — that was Sheri, at the diner. So when she says "You've got him happy. That's the whole book, far as I'm concerned," I heard the sentence do exactly what Vee herself admired minutes later: "there wasn't one uncalibrated thing about what Sheri chose to say and when." That line is the chapter quietly telling me the truth-teller is also keeping the cover. It doesn't make me dislike her — her loyalty is to Pace, and "far as I'm concerned" is doing honest work — but the one adult in this book with no angle turned out to have a small one after all, and the count of people managing what Vee gets to know just went up by one. Also: Sheri in person — bleached going dark, too much makeup, a hall out on 460 with live music Saturdays — sits closer than ever to the description of the malicious Rusty Nail blonde, and yet nothing in this warm, frank woman matches that sugared cruelty. The page still hasn't bound them and I'm still not binding them; but I notice the overlap got harder to ignore, not easier.

And the Saturday dance. "He's dancing, and if the redhead had opinions about it she could bring them to the hall." A country-western hall where Pace is a fixture, where the regulars know him — and I remember he took Randi dancing before Thanksgiving, and Sheri saw "the brunette, the sorority one." If Saturday's hall is that hall, Vee is about to walk into the one room in Virginia where both women have been seen on his arm. Guess from here, clearly marked: I think the book is walking her toward that fuse, cheerfully, in dancing shoes.

Erotic charge: deliberately banked this chapter, and rightly — after the roadhouse truck ride, a second scorcher would have been noise. What heat there is lives in the rig-and-mirror scene, in Sheri's slow appreciative descent of Vee's body (the frankest same-sex look Vee's ever received in public, and she liked it once she understood it), and in the ride home leaned on his shoulder. Simmer, not flame, and the simmer's good.

Friction: almost none. If I squint, Sheri's arrival is one more virtuoso performance in a row of them — the book has now given me three consecutive chapters of a social scene going beautifully (the coat night's afterglow, the roadhouse, the taproom), and some cussed part of me is doing what Vee did at the roadhouse: checking a thing that's going right for the trapdoor. But nothing on this page dragged or rang false; the drunk arithmetic ("the floor had gone approximate somewhere in the third cider") and the filthy song versions are lovely, precise comedy.

The titles. "Another Round" is doing at least three things and I enjoyed all of them: the literal rounds Sheri keeps ordering; the echo of the roadhouse — another round of introductions, another of Vee's worlds folded in; and, faintly, "round two" of a jealousy Vee thought she'd already fought and won. It gave nothing away; it recolored gently on finishing. The book title keeps tightening: this chapter was one long polite invitation — "There's somebody I'd like you to meet" — and every one so far has been real and warm and has also drawn Vee one room deeper into a house whose floor plan she doesn't have. With a Long Spoon hangs over the ciders that "kept arriving, that was the thing about Sheri ordering." And the tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — Vee said "I'd love to" with her voice gone small and bright, and meant it, and dressed herself for the war anyway. That's the tagline in miniature.

What I want next: Saturday at the hall, badly — and I dread it a little, which is the right mixture. I want to know "the rest another time" Sheri promised about why Pace is the way he is. And somewhere behind all of it, Randi got exactly one mention's worth of absence this chapter, and I felt the absence: Vee now has a second confidante, one who deals in true words, and I wonder what Randi makes of that when she finds out.
