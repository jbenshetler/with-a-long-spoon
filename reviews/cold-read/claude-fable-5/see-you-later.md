# Cold read (grounded) — See You Later

## Reader reaction

The book gave me Randi's seduction from the inside for three chapters, and now it hands me the mark's point of view — and it's devastating precisely because nothing bad happens. A girl gets a coffee and comes home warm. That's all. And I read the whole thing with my stomach slightly dropped, because I stood at the light pole with Pace two days ago and watched that "collision" get built across thirty feet of lawn, and here it is again through Vee's eyes: "one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it." Neither one did it. Oh, honey. One of you did it.

What wrecks me is how *good* the happiness is on the page. Vee's crush is rendered with such tenderness — writing the date at the top of a clean page "so that her hand would have somewhere to be," the three minutes of not looking at the door, the "yes" arriving in her chest fully formed and having to be held one full beat so it doesn't come out the size it actually is. I've been that person. Everyone has been that person. And the detail that the whole hour leaves no transcript — "the leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed" — is the truest thing in the chapter and the scariest, because a friendship you can't remember the words of is a feeling someone installed in you, not a conversation you had.

And I can see the machinery now because I watched it get built in "Standards." Randi is running Brooke's playbook, beat for beat: the warmth as a substance deployed into rooms, the "I'm not asking, I've decided," the clean release at the end, the exit that lands while walking backward. She was on the receiving end of that four-minute interrogation on Saturday morning and she took notes without knowing she was taking them — or maybe knowing exactly. What the page holds open, and I'm holding open with it, is how much of this is cold. Because I also watched her on the quad, and that "yes" when Pace pointed was bare and helpless and *hers*. She may genuinely want Vee more than she has wanted anything. Both can be true — the want real, the apparatus counterfeit — and after the bathroom mirror, the apparatus is all anyone gets. Vee is falling for the face.

Cassie. Four words a day and she spent three of them on "Goodbye, Vee." That landed on me like a hand on the back of the neck. No sulk, no scene — just a flat, finished word "for a longer parting than a Wednesday," and something passing over her face that Vee "did not try to" name. The text earned my suspicion here without telling me what Cassie saw; I don't know if she read Randi in ninety seconds or if she's grieving something about Vee I haven't been shown. But she declined the bit, declined the coffee, declined the whole warm machine, and she's the only person in three chapters who has. I trust her more than anyone in this book right now, and she's said maybe ten words.

The other thing under Vee's skin that the chapter refuses to look at straight — and I love that it refuses — is her own body. "Floozies," in her mother's voice, and under the word "a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for." The cardigan over the better shirt, on purpose, most mornings. Whatever Randi and Pace think they've picked out, there's a whole pressurized room in this girl that neither of them has seen yet, and I don't know if that makes what's coming better or so much worse.

Friction: one thumb on the scale. The professor packing up his slides: "*latent variables*. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can." That's the chapter tapping its own theme on the microphone, and I'd have felt the hidden-variables idea without the syllabus telling me. Small sin; the chapter is otherwise very clean.

The title: "See You Later" is Randi's warm certainty — "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see" — set directly against Cassie's "Goodbye," and the chapter makes you choose which parting to believe. I believe Cassie's, and that frightens me. The cover board: *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, bring a long spoon — and this chapter literally scored its seduction to spoons ringing against cups all over the yellow room, everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom. That got me. *A Polite Invitation*: "Come to Chi Latte with me. I'm not asking, I've decided." There it is. No tagline in what I was given.

What I dread, as a guess from here: that Vee gets brought to the bench — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is still hanging over everything — and that whatever is real in Randi's want won't survive contact with what she and Pace have planned it into. What I *want* is for Cassie to be wrong. I don't think she is.
