## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

So here's Vee from inside, finally — and it's worse than I expected, in the way I mean as a compliment. I spent the whole chapter split in two: one half of me was Vee, genuinely warmed, because the chapter renders being love-bombed *from the inside* and it works on you the way it works on her — "the *later* doing in her chest what the right pen had done" — and the other half of me knows the collision was choreographed, knows "we've got a stats class together" came home to Pace like a trophy, and so every warm beat lands twice: once as pleasure, once as machinery. "*There* you are" nearly knocked me over — that's Pace's line to Randi in the first chapter, "There you are, Randi," said after he'd broken her open on the bench. Randi is using her lover's most intimate sentence as an entrance line on a girl she's hunting. I don't know if she knows she's doing it. Either way it chilled me.

The line that will not leave me alone is Cassie's. "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word, flat and finished, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Cassie — four words a day, no feelings about the yellow place on Draper — is the only person in this book so far who looked at Randi and did not take the bit's open door. She "put her name down flat in the space" and Randi's charm just... hit air. I trust Cassie completely and instantly, precisely because Randi's warmth bounced off her, and that "something passed over her face, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name" reads to me like the first person in the novel who *saw something*. Whether she saw the truth or just felt the wattage as wrong, I can't say — the text doesn't tell me — but the chapter title being Randi's line ("See you later, okay?") set against Cassie's "Goodbye" is a hinge I felt physically. Two farewells, and I believe the wrong one is the honest one.

The professor's "latent variables — the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" made me laugh out loud and also wince — that's the closest this book has come to putting its thumb on the scale. Vee underlining it, "going nowhere," is a lovely touch, but the joke is a half-inch too on-the-nose for a book that's otherwise been so sly. Same with **Χ ΛΑΤΕ** at first — but no, actually, that one I loved: the joke arriving "only once you're allowed in on it" is exactly Vee's whole condition, standing outside jokes until somebody holds the door, and she doesn't know she's inside a much bigger one.

And the motifs are converging on her like weather. Vee has a mother's voice in her head about clothes ("floozies," "that's not the kind of attention a nice girl—") exactly as Randi has a mother's voice about food. The cardigan over the better shirt, worn "on purpose," "and the room did not know it was a choice" — that's a door, closed, in plain sight, and I know now what Pace does with doors he's seen. The blank transcript devastated me: "the feeling was total and the transcript was blank." She kept the feeling and lost every word — which is precisely what makes her unable to check any of it later.

Erotic charge: low and correct for the chapter — but not zero. Vee's long look at the underdressed girls, "riding under it where she didn't look," and her private, pleased knowledge that "she had more than they had" is the first flicker of Vee's own appetite, and it's the same shape as Randi's: real, disowned, filed under her mother's word for it.

Heat's not the engine here; dread is, and it's humming. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, I'm increasingly sure — and "every yes was freely given": Vee's "Yeah, sure, I could do coffee," eagerness "folded down," is a freely given yes to an invitation whose shape she cannot see. That's the tagline enacted in miniature.

What I want next: Pace and Vee in a room, and I dread it. Guess from here: Cassie becomes the person Vee stops listening to, and I will hate watching it.
