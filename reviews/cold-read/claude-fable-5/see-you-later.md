## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

First chapter in Vee's head, and it hurts exactly the way I was afraid it would. After three chapters of watching Pace and Randi build the machine, I'm now inside the person walking into it, and she's *lovely* — the three minutes of "not looking at the door," writing the date "so that her hand would have somewhere to be," the yes she has to hold back "so it would not come out the size it actually was." She's so hungry to be chosen, and so practiced at folding the hunger down, that every warm thing Randi does lands like rain on a drought. The line that got me hardest: "the leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed." She had the best hour in months and can't recover a sentence of it. That's a beautiful and frightening description of being charmed — and I know, and she doesn't, that the charm was engineered.

Cassie. Cassie is the chapter's quiet gift. She takes one look at Randi's practiced name-bit and puts "Cassie" down flat — refuses the door the bit was built to open — and then that "Goodbye, Vee." "The whole word, flat and finished, the word for a longer parting than a Monday." My skin prickled. The text earned that suspicion completely: someone in this book has instincts, and it's the roommate in yesterday's shirt with a lab at noon. Vee smooths the wrongness, of course. "Her face did the thing it did with wrongness, which was to smooth it" — that's her whole architecture in one clause.

And Randi from the outside is chilling *because nothing she does is visibly false*. "There you are" — Pace's exact phrase from the bench, now deployed on Vee. The forearm squeeze from the lawn, repeated. "I'm not asking, I've decided." "See you later — flat fact... the queen having decided there would be a later." Vee reads Randi's certainty as glamour; I read it as the schedule of a plan. What I can't tell — and this is the ache of the jacket copy working on me — is how much of Randi's warmth is real. Her joy at meeting Vee in ch.3 looked real. Maybe both things are true at once, which is worse.

The professor's "latent variables — the factors you can't measure directly... driving the whole thing from underneath" — okay, that one I felt the author's thumb on. It's a great joke, but it's *announced*, underlined once in her notebook, "going nowhere." A half-step too cute for this book's usual sleight of hand.

Erotic charge: low and clothed, but not zero — Vee's long look at the underdressed girls at the bar, the mother's "floozies" arriving in her mouth, and underneath it "she could wear what they were wearing... she had more than they had." The cardigan over the better shirt, worn *on purpose*, the room not knowing it's a choice. That's the jacket's "shames she was raised to bury" arriving on schedule, and it's genuinely charged — desire to be seen, policed by an inherited voice. And her "flat helpless want" looking at Randi is the most nakedly desirous moment in the chapter, and it isn't sexual yet, which is exactly right.

The title: "See You Later" is Randi's exit line, the queen's decree — but it's shadowed by Cassie's "Goodbye," the honest word next to the charming one. The chapter is those two farewells against each other, and the title picks the seductive one. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — keeps sharpening: Vee just had lavender coffee with her.

What I dread, as a guess from here: Cassie getting slowly displaced, and being right the whole time.
