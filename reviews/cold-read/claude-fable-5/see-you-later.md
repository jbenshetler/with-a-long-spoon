## Reader reaction

So this is the chapter where the jacket's promise — "lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does" — actually starts operating on me, and I have to report: it works, and it makes me feel slightly sick in the best way. I just spent a chapter inside Randi's collision from the other side. I watched Pace point and Randi engineer that "accident" with a dancer's precision — and now I get Vee's version: "one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it." Neither one did it. Oh, honey. I know exactly who did it, and reading Vee narrate it as luck, as being "let borrow inside a brighter life," put a genuine ache in my chest. Not dread exactly — the jacket told me nothing would be taken that she didn't hand over — but the specific vertigo of watching someone be *glad* about a thing I know the mechanics of.

And here's what's honest: the charm worked on me too. Randi's entrance — "*There* you are" — is the same line Pace gave her on the bench, *there you are, Randi*, and hearing it come out of her mouth as a social weapon, aimed, warm, perfectly deployed, made my skin prickle. She took the thing he found in her and she's using its phrasing. I don't know if she knows she's doing it; the page doesn't say, and I'm leaving it ajar. But the recurrence landed on me hard.

Vee herself — first extended time in her head, and I like her enormously. The class reading of the coffee shop is the best interior writing in the chapter: "Floozies, the word arriving whole and in her mother's voice," and then "behind the word, riding under it where she didn't look, a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for." That's the reader I am being fed exactly what I read for — the want she will not look at squarely, on the page, twice: once at the underdressed girls' bodies, once at Randi ("a flat helpless want... warm, unexamined, glad"). She's starving in a different register than Randi — Randi portions food, Vee portions *belonging* — the cardigan worn over "the better shirt on purpose," the coffee shop she never entered because it felt like a party she wasn't invited to. Quietly starving, the jacket said. Yes.

Cassie. Cassie is the moment the chapter goes cold in my stomach, and it earned it fair. She refuses the bit — "Cassie," flat, one word, and Randi's machine hitches for exactly one beat. And then: "Goodbye, Vee." Not *see you later* — the chapter's own title handed to Randi, and the *goodbye* handed to Cassie, the whole word, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Vee smooths it; I couldn't. Whatever Cassie saw or felt — the page doesn't say, "something passed over her face, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to" — I trust it. One person in this book looked at Randi's warmth and didn't buy it, and she said goodbye to her friend like Vee was leaving on a ship. That's my first genuinely earned unease with a bystander character and I'm holding onto her.

One thumb-on-the-scale moment, and I'll name it: the professor's "next time we get into the interesting part — *latent variables*. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can." Underlined once in Vee's notebook, going nowhere. It's a good joke and it's *right* — the whole chapter is Vee failing to infer the latent variable sitting at her elbow — but it winked at me a little hard. I smiled anyway. Same with the blank transcript ("the feeling was total and the transcript was blank") — that one I fully bought; that's exactly how charisma works on the hungry, and it doubles as the scariest line in the chapter if you think about what it means that Randi's *content* leaves no residue, only attachment.

Small pleasure I want on record: the spoons. "Spoons going against the insides of cups, all over the room, an irregular ringing, everyone stirring up the sweetness at the bottom." The series is called *With a Long Spoon* and I felt that image go through me like a signature — sup with the devil, the proverb goes, and Vee just walked into the sweet-smelling yellow room and drank what she was handed. "Trust me," Randi says, ordering for her. She does.

The chapter title: "See You Later" is Randi's exit line, and Vee's whole reading of it — "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see" — is more literally true than Vee can know. There *is* a calendar she can't see. The title does the innocuous-surface thing beautifully; against Cassie's "Goodbye" it makes a little diptych of partings, one warm and false-floored, one flat and true-feeling.

Erotic charge: low flame, but present and doing something new — it's all Vee's, and it's all unacknowledged. The looking at the bare midriffs, the "want had nowhere to put itself except *here, this, with her*." After the bench and the pointing game, this reads like the first bar of a long piece. I want more time in Vee's body; I suspect I'll get it.

What I want next: the pointing game's other shoe. Pace hasn't met Vee on the page. Guess from here: the machinery will keep working, Vee will keep being happy, and Cassie will be right — and I dread how long it'll take Vee to hear the word *goodbye* the way I did.
