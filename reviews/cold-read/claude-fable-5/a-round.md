## Reader reaction

Well. I had to put the book down for a second after "He hadn't looked down. The whole time she'd been dying he hadn't looked down once." That is the chapter — that one beat — and it worked on me completely.

Let me be honest about my body first: this is the most sustained erotic charge the book has managed yet, and there is not a single sex act in it. A fitting. A measuring tape. And I was holding my breath along with her. The rhythm the prose finds — "closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil" — did to me exactly what it does to Vee: it taught me the pattern and then made me feel the withdrawal ringing. The inside-seam measurement, "the back of his fingers against her where she was warm," her mother's voice arriving with "can he tell" — that whole run is the book at full power, shame and heat braided so tight you can't pull them apart, which I have understood since the truck scene is the point. Vee's arousal has never been despite the mother-voice; the voice IS the heat's fuel. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice" — that was the truck; this chapter is that sentence made into an hour.

The wet-satin sequence is the bravest erotica writing in the book so far. "The hope was visible. The hope was sized to be seen across the room." I winced for her, physically. She'd chosen those panties with hope, and her body turned the hope into evidence — that is the shame-machinery of her whole life rendered in one dark patch of fabric. And then the reversal: she opens her eyes braced for the grin she's "been bracing for her whole life," and it isn't the joke of her. It's a man with his chin craned all the way up, who has been waiting at her eye level for her eyes. Her posture uncoiling — the stoop coming undone, rising onto her toes without meaning to — I felt that in my own spine. "She had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." If the book had ended on that line I'd have believed it earned it.

And then — because the book is fair to both of them — Vee gets to spend the power. The breast against his cheek, the third time on purpose, "Oops. Sorry," and HIM blushing, fighting the grin, going still. I laughed out loud at her "pre-aroused every time I wore it" line, and so did he, and the fact that she stumps him — that her body becomes, in his hands, "something worth getting right" instead of something to apologize for — is the swoon underneath the swoon. The pin-prick and the kiss on her ribs, "before he'd decided to," and then "Keep still," a beat too late, with the cost audible in it. I wanted them to fall into it. I'm glad they didn't, because the restraint is the eroticism, and the chapter knows it.

Now the cold water I have to pour on myself, because I carry what Vee doesn't. Right there in her own head, undressing: a man "who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking." That exclamation is a knife the page put in my hand. He didn't walk past Randi. Randi chose her. Randi said "Yes" bare-voiced on the quad. Every ounce of Vee's triumph in that clause is built on the one thing she doesn't know, and the chapter lets it go by in her voice without a flicker — no authorial nudge, which is why it cut. And the riser. "Of course there was a riser in the corner of a woodworker's house, of course he'd built it" — Vee files it under craftsman's furniture, but I have seen this man's furniture before. The bench wore a table's lid. The riser puts her "at exactly the height it would put her if she had wanted it," his face level with where she is wettest. Vee reads coincidence; I read design, because the book taught me to. Same with the house "cranked up so a woman could stand half-bare in it and not be cold" — she experiences it as thoughtfulness, and it IS thoughtfulness, and it is also a man who planned hours ahead for her to be undressed. Both true at once. That doubleness — cherished AND arranged-for — is the whole book, and this chapter is its purest expression yet: nothing was taken from her; she took off every piece herself; and the room was built for it before she arrived.

Do I suspect Pace of anything in this hour beyond what I already know? Honestly — no new charge. His blush was real. The laugh surprised out of him was real. "I don't know. I've never thought about it" — that plain, self-surprised answer is the most trustworthy thing he's ever said. The suspicion I hold is the standing one, not a new one, and I want to be precise about that: within the four walls of this chapter, he is exactly what he appears to be, and that has never once been the same as the whole truth about him.

Friction, small: "His eyes dropped — just for a moment, just to the two points she couldn't do anything about… Moved on." fine — but the beat before it, "Except he'd thoughtfully turned up the heat. Wryly." — that dangling "Wryly." sits oddly, a stage direction where free indirect voice was already doing the job. And the long pin-by-pin middle flirts with one repetition too many of the smooth-press-pin figure before the pin-prick breaks it. That's the whole complaint list. The chapter earns every page otherwise.

The title: "A Round." I love how many ways it opens and refuses to close. The tape going around her — waist, bust, hip. His slow knee-walk around the base of her. Her body itself — the curves that ended her swimming, "too much woman for the sport," now measured and honored instead of disqualified. And, quieter, a round of a game — because I know, and she doesn't, that a game is being played, and this may be a round of it. The title gave nothing away going in; coming out, it's four meanings deep, and I notice the darkest reading is the one only I can make. That's this book's title-craft working. *A Polite Invitation* — "would you come to my house Saturday" was the politest invitation imaginable, and look what it opened; *With a Long Spoon* keeps whispering that someone at this table should be eating more carefully; the tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — has never fit a chapter better. Every yes here WAS freely given. The jeans, the shirt, the bra, the third press of her breast. All hers. That's the problem.

What I want next: the induction, the finished dress, her walking up to the front of a room in silk built on her body — and I dread it a little, because the dress is now the most loaded object in the book and Randi hasn't seen it yet. Guesses from here: the closed bedroom door will open soon, and I think Vee will be the one to open it; and some collision of the two halves of her life — Randi and the dress, or Randi and the house — is coming, and I don't think Vee survives it unchanged. Also still waiting, uneasily, on Cassie, who hasn't seen any of this hour and would have things to say.
