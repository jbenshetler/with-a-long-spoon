# Cold read (grounded) — A Round

*scene: scenes/a-round.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: ck-ch010 + raw ch011..ch014 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Well. I need a minute.

I've been waiting for this book to bring Vee and her body into the same room and make her stand there — and it did it with a measuring tape and a bolt of burgundy silk, and it is the hottest chapter of the book so far without a single act you could name in a report. She takes off her jeans "the undignified mechanics of taking off jeans in front of a man who was politely not watching her do it," and from that first fumble the chapter has me completely. The whole thing runs on the machine the measuring builds: "closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil" — that rhythm worked on me exactly the way it worked on her, and by the time "the pad was full of her" I was as stacked up with nowhere to go as she was.

How I feel about Vee: fierce, protective, delighted, a little in awe. This is the chapter where the girl from the pool bleachers — "too much woman for the sport" — gets a whole afternoon of being looked at and not once found wanting. The wet-satin sequence is the bravest writing in the book so far about a woman's shame: "The hope was visible. The hope was sized to be seen across the room." That sentence gutted me. Her mother's word — "stripper," "floozie" — arriving right on schedule, and her body answering the voice with heat, which is the same terrible knot the truck scene tied — the voice IS the heat for her, and the book keeps refusing to untangle it for her, which is honest. And then the eyes-shut beat: "That was the bravest thing she did all day, the not being able to stand it." When she opens them and he's been waiting, chin tipped all the way up, having never looked down — I actually put my hand to my own collarbone before I read that she did. And the physical answer — the stoop coming undone, the chin up, rising onto her toes toward "something she had no name for," and his little sideways mock-frown at her heel — that's the swoon of the whole volume so far. Her posture literally rearranged by being seen kindly. I believed every millimeter of it.

Pace. Here's my honest split. In the room, moment by moment, he is irresistible — the heat turned up hours ahead so she wouldn't be cold, the honest look that isn't ogling, the laugh she surprises out of him ("I don't know. I've never thought about it."), the kiss on the pin-stung rib "before he'd decided to," the "Keep still" that comes "a beat too late, after a silence she watched him spend deciding." The chapter lets me feel him almost fall, and the costliness of his not-falling reads as real. AND — I never once forgot the bench. This man has a measuring, patient, escalation-calibrated method, and I have watched him run it on Randi with restraints. "He started at the top, and he started gently, the way you'd gentle a horse" — the page says it itself. The chapter never winks at the plan, never mentions Randi except in Vee's own giddy aside ("who'd walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking" — oh, Vee, the dramatic irony in that one exclamation is almost cruel), and that restraint makes it worse and better at once: I get to feel exactly what she feels while knowing what she doesn't. The tenderness is real. The architecture is also real. Both. That's this book's whole trick and it's working.

Her turn to spend the power — the deliberate breast against his cheek, "Oops. Sorry," his blush climbing his neck — made me grin out loud. Her boldness is always like this: not schemed, "the wanting rising up and out before she could decide to want it," continuous with the truck window and the tree kiss. She escalates; he holds the line and lets her feel him holding it. The "plausible deniability" catalogue at the end — the palm down her spine, the finger at the back of her knee, the too-warm exhale below her navel — "He knew she knew. Neither of them said anything." That's a 3-heat register achieved with a fully pinned hem.

Friction: almost none. One line clanked slightly — "*Maybe he'll think it's the cold air.* ... Wryly." — that dangling "Wryly." reads like a stage direction that lost its sentence. And "the way you'd gentle a horse" is doing a lot; I noticed it as the author's hand more than Vee's thought, though I'll allow she's a girl who might think it. Otherwise the chapter earns every inch of its length; the pacing of the measuring section is the pacing of arousal itself and I wouldn't cut a number off the pad.

The title: "A Round" — the measurements are all "around": around the waist, under the bust, across the bustline. A round of drinks, maybe, in a book of rounds and games. And she is round — the body the sport threw out is exactly what the tape is honoring. It sat innocuous going in and came out warm. The book title keeps sharpening: this whole afternoon is a polite invitation — "I'd like to make it for you... If you'll let me" — every step hers, every yes freely given. Which, per the jacket's tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — is precisely what frightens me under all this melting. The long spoon: she's supping gladly.

What I want next: the dress finished, the induction, her in it. What I dread: that this dress — made by him, on her body, for her night — is also costume-making inside a plan, and that Randi is somewhere in the next room of this story. Guess from here: the bedroom door that stayed shut today gets opened soon, and I think she'll be the one to open it — and I think the dress arrives before the truth does.
