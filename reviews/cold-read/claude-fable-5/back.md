## Reader reaction

Oh, this one is quiet and it got me anyway — and then, in the last three words, it put a cold finger on the back of my neck.

The chapter is nothing but dishes and talk: Vee naked on the chair he made, running on at his back about Randi's sweater-return con ("She let him feel like it was his idea" — which, Vee, honey, look at that sentence for one more second), and him washing up. And out of the grocery-list voice comes "I want to meet your people." I felt Vee's gold-windows lift with her. This is the thing the whole fall has been missing — the relationship's namelessness "out there" — and here he is asking to walk into her world, not just keep her warm in his. When he pulled Meg's boots off the shelf — "Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off" — I melted right along with her. That's the move this book does best: love as retention, care as archive. He kept all of it. And "Bring Theo," with no question attached, no flicker of jealousy, while she's sputtering an explanation nobody asked for — that landed on me exactly as it landed on her, "warm side down." "Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one" is a lovely line, and it's him solving for Cassie's comfort, which is more social tenderness than I knew he had.

And yet. I cannot read this chapter with Vee's eyes only, because the very first name on the guest list is Randi. "You could meet Randi. Properly, I mean. You two said about nine words at the coffee shop and most of them were math." Reader, they have said considerably more than nine words. She is planning a dinner to introduce her boyfriend to his own lover, and she's delighted about it, and I sat there with my stomach dropping while she counted napkins. And the collision I've been braced for all book — the dorm that knows Pace, the house that knows Pace, the Rusty Nail regulars who've seen both women — he just proposed putting all of it in one room. Kayla, who can talk the paint off a wall, in the same room as Randi. Then the ending: "Will you pick the place?" — her tenderness about his ears, the Chili's picture arriving whole and unasked, is genuinely one of the best small beats here, her love doing quiet accommodation work — and he turns around with almost-happiness and says "Let me handle it."

That line. On Vee's channel it's pure warmth, the courtly man taking a task. On mine it is the exact cadence of the man who invented a pointing game, staged a coffee-shop rescue, and cooked her waking hours in advance ("nothing in this house happened at an accidental hour" — that was two chapters ago and it's still ringing). Did *he* propose this party, or is this the game's next move — Randi brought "all the way in," under cover of a floor party? I can't tell whether I just watched a man fall further in love or a man open a door he and Randi built. The chapter doesn't tell me, and I'm honestly a little in awe of how much dread it wrings out of a sentence that is, on its face, the sweetest thing he's ever offered. I also notice what nobody says: whether Randi knows this is coming. Tuesday's debrief dinner is already scheduled. She's about to hear about the coat AND the party in one sitting.

One more thing I loved: "She'd watched somebody be fine about it before. She wasn't going to look at when." Vee declining to open her own memory (the Rusty Nail? her mother's table? Chili's?) is exactly her — she protects people, including herself, by not looking. The book keeps letting her almost-see and choosing not to, and after last chapter's tongue going hunting for "salt or the memory of salt," her not-looking is starting to feel load-bearing.

**Friction:** almost none. If I squint, the Theo beat resolves a hair too frictionlessly — "Bring Theo, and reached for the next pan" is perfect for Pace, but I half-wanted one beat of something under it. That's a quibble.

**The titles.** "Back" is doing at least three jobs and I like all of them: his back as the listener she talks at (the chapter's literal geometry); she is *back* from the month of silence, the reunion consolidated into domesticity; and maybe the world coming back in — the relationship turning to face outward at last. Innocuous going in, resonant coming out; it gave nothing away. *A Polite Invitation* — good lord, this chapter IS one: "I want to meet your people" is the politest invitation in the book so far, and after fifty-two chapters of this series title I flinch at invitations on principle. *With a Long Spoon* keeps hanging over every warm meal, and this was a chapter made entirely of dish-washing after one.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here):** I want the party and I dread the party. Guess: Randi will hear of it Tuesday and have to manage her face in real time — or she'll co-produce the whole thing, which is worse. Kayla + Brooke's file + the "Pace" name in two girl-worlds is a lit fuse walking into one room. And Cassie, who has never trusted Randi, gets to watch her operate at close range for a whole evening. I would read that chapter tonight if I could.
