## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter is a gift-giving scene, and it worked on me almost embarrassingly well. The reveal — that the pull toward the sun porch isn't toward the bedroom ("Her body had assumed left. He didn't go left.") — is a lovely misdirect, and Pace "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth one more second" is the most disarming he's ever been on the page. That's the trouble with this book: I *know* from chapter one that this courtship was engineered, and the engineering has never once shown a seam — exactly like the dress. Vee even names the mechanism without knowing she's naming it: "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That sentence stopped me cold. It's about the dress. It is also, precisely, about her entire junior year. I don't think Vee will ever read it that way; I did, and it hurt.

The mirror sequence is the erotic and emotional peak, and it's earned. *Floozy* flares — the mother's voice, faithful to its whole history — and "she stayed anyway, made herself look, and somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame." Then the slit, the pale edge of her underwear, and the decision: "she was the one who'd taken the edge away." Her stripping herself for the mirror, alone, before walking to him — that's her agency doing exactly what the tagline promises. Every yes freely given. The heat here is real for me: the shimmy of the silk finding her, the bare leg "given and taken back and given," the hooked thigh in the den. The sex itself is elided ("nothing left for either of them to decide"), which after Famished's full explicitness felt right — the dress is the sex scene.

The pause to hang the dress mid-undressing — "the two-handed care you'd give something alive" — is the tenderest beat in the book so far, and I believe both of them in it.

The title: "Peekaboo" I first took as the slit — hidden, revealed, hidden. But it's also the underwear peeking at the slit's edge, and, darker, it's the whole book: what's hidden from Vee keeps almost showing and falling shut. The lining detail — a "soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" — Pace deciding what of her shows. Even his generosity is authorship.

Friction: almost none, but the closing phrase — "getting away with something" — is doing so much work it nearly tips into telegraphy. She means her own small transgressions (the bare leg, the daydream of the induction entrance). I hear the other meaning ringing under it: someone IS getting away with something, and it isn't her. That's the sentence the book has been building toward, and it lands like a bruise. And the induction daydream confirms my suspicion: November, the dress, the room turning — that's where this is converging.

One quiet ache: "she knew without being told that he'd been up the night before... while she slept across town, not knowing." *Not knowing.* The book keeps handing me these phrases with two floors.

What I dread, as a guess from here: Randi seeing the dress. Randi at the induction. The two of them comparing notes with the dress hanging between them.
