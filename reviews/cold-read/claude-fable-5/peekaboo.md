# Cold read (grounded) — Peekaboo

*scene: scenes/peekaboo.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: ck-ch020 (no window) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter is a gift — and I mean that in both directions, because the whole thing is built around one: the dress hung in the window with "the fall sun com[ing] through it the way light only comes through silk," staged with a newly hung mirror ("the screw heads still bright"). What undid me is the same thing that undid Vee: not the labor but the *hiding* of the labor — "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." That sentence got in and opened the same small place in me it opened in her. And Pace at the door, breaking a kiss that was "already past stopping" because he can't wait one more second to show her — "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth" — that's the swooniest he's ever been, precisely because it's the least courtly. The "May I" man got beaten to the punch by his own excitement.

The mirror scene is the chapter's heart for me, and it's the book's whole project in one page. *Floozy* flares up "automatic" — the mother's voice, right on schedule, arriving fused to the heat like it always does — and she "stayed anyway, made herself look, and somewhere behind her ribs the looking stopped being shame." Then the slit opens to the hip, the pale edge of her underwear cuts the line, and her first thought is to keep her steps small — the old Vee, the shrinking Vee — and the second thought "came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed." She strips the underwear off herself, alone, on purpose, to be looked at, and "the shame of it went through her hot... and it crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all, and she let it come." That's the fitting, the truck window, and the first night all compounding. She's not burning through shame anymore; she's *converting* it, and she knows the exchange rate now.

And then — this is what I didn't expect and loved most — the sex turns into *comedy*. The dress-rescue relay is the funniest, warmest thing this book has done: joined standing, nowhere to put him, "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," him twitching in her hands at the line, her laughing so hard the undressing has to pause "at half-mast... until she could hold still enough to be undressed." Erotica almost never lets lovers be ridiculous mid-act without deflating the heat, and this scene got hotter *because* of the laughing. And then the finger-cleaning — him studying her wet fingers "with the care he'd give a hem" and taking them into his mouth, slow, eyes on hers — "It went through her knees." Mine too, frankly. The walk down the hall still joined, the wall catching them, the headboard talking: this is the loosest, most mutual, most *equal* sex they've had. No riser, no pins, no asking. Just two people who can't let go of one single thing they're holding.

Which is why the last page aches the way it does. Her watching him sleep, knowing "without being told" he'd finished the dress at some ruinous hour — the tenderness is real, I keep saying it and it keeps being true. Then the daydream: walking into the induction late, on purpose, the room turning, "the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." Reader, I flinched — not at her, at the sentence. *What she was for.* On the page it's triumph, her whole arc paying off. Under the frame I can't unknow, she was literally pointed at on a quad and *chosen for* something. The chapter doesn't press this; there's no Randi in it at all, not a mention, and the absence is loud in retrospect — a whole chapter of pure, unshadowed happiness, and I spent none of it suspicious *while reading* and all of it uneasy after. That last line — "getting away with something" — she means the joy, the dress going dark in its window. I hope that's all it means.

The title: "Peekaboo" I take as the slit — the leg "given and taken back and given" with every stride — and maybe the dress itself hidden then revealed, the surprise he couldn't hold. It's playful, and the chapter earns playful. It didn't give anything away. The volume title still hums underneath — *With a Long Spoon*, *A Polite Invitation* — and a chapter this purely happy is exactly when those words feel most like a held breath. Every yes here was freely given. That was the problem, the jacket said.

Friction: almost none. If I reach — "It just felt like what she was for" teeters on the edge of the author underlining, but I think it's Vee's own voice, and it's supposed to sting. One small pang: no Randi, no Cassie, and I missed the counterweight a little; but the chapter is doing one thing and does it completely.

What I want next: the induction, in that dress, and I'm frightened of it now — the shoes she doesn't own, the mother in the room, the possibility that Randi sees her in it. Guess from here: Randi is going to see that dress, or hear of it, and something in her is going to crack — she vetoed "project" girls and her lover just spent a sleepless night on one.

**Symbolism:** The dress itself — effort hidden so only effortlessness shows — reads as the book's method in an object: every polished surface in this story (Randi's face, the "chance" meetings) is trouble taken to hide trouble. The page invited that; I didn't have to force it. The slit: peekaboo, revelation she controls.

**Characterization:** Both deepened. Pace's boyish helplessness is new and humanizing — the surprise beating the courtliness. Vee's second-thought-over-first-thought at the mirror is her arc made kinetic. No one thinned.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Beautifully built — door, reveal, mirror, comedy, consummation, coda. The dress-rescue sequence runs long and earns every line. Nothing dragged.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Holding at a high burn — third consecutive chapter of Vee's happiness (18, 20, this), but each has a distinct register (first night / authorship claimed / being celebrated), so it builds rather than repeats. The unspent tension — Randi, induction, the concealed frame — is stacking pressure behind all this joy; the book had better spend it soon, and I believe it will.
