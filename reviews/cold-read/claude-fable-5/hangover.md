# Cold read (grounded) — Hangover

## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter broke my heart in the gentlest possible way. After all the heat of the last few chapters — the coat, the truck, the taproom — this one is quiet as snow, and it landed harder than half the sex scenes have.

The morning-after care is where the swoon lives, and it's classic Pace: the ice water on a folded paper towel *so it wouldn't mark the wood*, the pills, his undershirt drawn over her head, "his mouth, once, on her forehead, in the dark, after he thought she was already gone." That last detail undid me — the tenderness he only spends when nobody's watching, which is exactly the shape of a man who can do everything except say the word. And then cinnamon raisin bread, baked from scratch while she slept, delivered with "Sit. You need to eat before you decide how you feel." The feeding-as-love motif is now so established that a loaf of bread reads like a love letter, and I received it as one.

But the chapter's real work is the friend conversation, and it's devastating in that flat, orderly Pace voice: "Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else." Vee's arithmetic — trying to lift Meg, Kayla, Cassie, Randi out of her own life and put one person in their place, and the sum won't run — is exactly the kind of interior math this book does best. And the ladder down: men bond by competing at fourteen, he had no arena; "Men don't start at twenty-two. That door's on a timer"; the ordinary questions — *what are you doing for Christmas* — each with "a gap under it." Then the degree-flatter line: the men around him "either weren't safe or weren't welcoming." *Weren't safe.* Set beside Sheri's "went bad with his daddy" and his old "less than staying," the shape of the father assembles itself in the reader's peripheral vision, and the book — rightly — refuses to turn and look at it directly. Neither does Vee, and I'm grateful she doesn't force it.

The Daphne passage is the bravest thing here. Vee finally holds both truths at once — that Daphne was all four friends in one, the only person he had, *and* that she had that much of him "only because of the thing about it that was wrong, the thing Vee still would not set a word to." "Both true at once, and it hurt to hold them both." That's exactly how I've been holding Daphne since chapter 25, and watching Vee arrive at the same unreconciled stack felt like the book trusting me. It doesn't resolve it. Good.

And then the ending: she crosses the kitchen and holds him *the way Sheri had held him*, whole weight committed — the hug she watched in the taproom, learned and repeated, tenderness passed hand to hand. And his response: "he wasn't happy and he wasn't sad. He was somewhere else with it, thinking." The chapter refuses to cash the hug into comfort. He receives it and goes somewhere she can't follow, and the last image is just bread cooling and wrong bright light off old snow. That withholding is what makes it land.

Two small pins I felt: "Women misunderstand it. The friendship. It gets awkward" — Vee fills in *his* friendship, and reads it through Theo, which is generous and plausible. But I have knowledge she doesn't: the last woman who was close to Pace didn't misunderstand a friendship — she's Randi, and she's his lover, and she was toasting Vee across a roadhouse table three nights ago. Whether he meant that sentence as cover, as history, or both, the page leaves it flat, and I felt the double floor under it without being told to. Second: "His one friend on this earth a five-foot woman who could never want him — the only kind, she understood now, the world had ever quite let him keep." Vee's conclusion, and it's *almost* true, and the almost is the whole book: he keeps women who want him too. Two of them. She's constructing a lonelier, safer Pace than the one that exists, and he lets her.

No friction to speak of — this chapter earned every beat. If I quibble: "and it was the best thing that had ever happened to her" about the water is a joke the chapter makes so early I briefly braced for a lighter chapter than I got, but that's the hangover voice working, not a miss.

The title: "Hangover" is literal — three ciders, the pressed light — but by the end it's the other kind: he is living the permanent hangover of a boyhood that never let him make a friend, and the chapter is her waking up to *that*. Nice, and it didn't give anything away. *With a Long Spoon / A Warm Reception* — this chapter is the warmest reception yet, bread and robe and held weight, and the spoon proverb still hangs over the warm kitchen; I felt it in the flat sentence about women and friendship. No tagline in my materials beyond the cover board line.

What I want next: Thursday's dinner with Randi is still pending, and Vee still hasn't told her about the "gross" kiss moment — that withheld story has a fuse on it. And I dread, as a guess from here, the day Vee learns that "women misunderstand the friendship" has a living counterexample she calls her best friend.
