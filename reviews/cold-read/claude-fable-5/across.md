## Reader reaction

This one is sunlight — and I sat in it the way Vee sits in that porch window, warm and a little braced, because I know what she has in her purse and who she doesn't know is coming to dinner.

The chapter is small on purpose: a locked dining hall, three girls, waffles, mimosas, the debrief. And it worked on me almost entirely through Kayla, who I would follow into any scene now — "This is the last morning of my life and it's going to be peanut butter crackers" is exactly the register of a college hunger crisis, and her shriek at "I flashed my boyfriend on his front porch" ("In front of *God*—") is the reaction the coat night deserved and never got until now. Vee finally gets to tell the story as triumph, out loud, to an audience that receives it as triumph. After forty chapters of her editing, cropping, keeping the middle back, there's something quietly moving in watching her hand over the *whole headline* — the flash itself, unashamed — and then notice what she still curates: "never had to say one word about the drive." The touching herself on the county road, the cruiser — that stays hers. I've come to read Vee by what she withholds, and this chapter knows I do.

What melted me, oddly, wasn't the sex talk — it was Vee picking up the check-adjacent role. "Get in my car. I know a place... It's a little expensive. Is that okay?" She's doing Randi's move — the collecting, the treating, the "let me give you a morning" — except Vee does it with Christmas money and a price warning, which is the whole difference between the two women in one gesture. And she reads the right side of the menu "out of old habit, and put it down; ...she didn't do any arithmetic at all." That's a motif being retired on the page and I felt it land. The split check at the end — "nobody letting anybody cover anybody" — is the anti-Randi economy, three phones tapping, and it made the friendship feel structurally different from the brunches in a way I trust.

Meg deepened nicely here. Her heating-pad confession ("I told him the cord was stupid") and "Sometimes you have to make it right in their language" is warm and funny and also — quietly — a slightly wrong frame laid over Vee's story. The coat wasn't an apology; Vee wasn't the one who owed one. Nobody at the table knows that, including maybe Vee, who lets "It was a big fight" stand. I don't think the page is being careless — it's showing me the story already calcifying into a tellable shape ("It worked") that leaves out the month, the word he still hasn't said, the drive. "Which was the size of it she'd brought, and it sat on the table looking exactly true." *Looking* exactly true. That verb is doing everything, and I appreciate that the chapter let it be that quiet.

And then Wednesday. Two days. Everybody. Cassie and Randi at one table with Pace, plus Meg, Kayla, Theo — and I felt the floor tilt under the sunshine, because I know what nobody at this table knows: Randi and Pace will be performing "nine words at the coffee shop" at each other across real napkins, in front of Cassie, who clocked something off about Randi on day one, and Kayla, who "can talk the paint off a wall." "He's handling it," and Vee "heard how much she liked saying it" — I liked hearing her say it too, that's the honest part; the dread is entirely mine, imported from the checkpoint, and the chapter never once nudges me toward it. It plays the dinner announcement as pure gift. That restraint is why the dread works.

No friction worth the name. If I squint, Kayla is one notch below her own top volume from the laundry chapter — but that's calibration, not a miss. Erotic charge is low and secondhand by design: the heat here is *narrated* heat, the coat story retold, and the small flush of Vee saying "Yeah..." with the huge smile. After "Forbidden Kiss" ran that hot, a sun-porch cooldown is the right breath.

**The titles.** "Across" — the table, first: three flutes coming together "over the middle of the table," the sun "came onto the cloth and sat down with them," Vee "had the middle of it all the whole time." But it's also *across* as in the crossing coming: worlds about to be walked across each other on Wednesday. It's an innocuous word that I suspect will look different in two days' reading time. *A Polite Invitation* — a dinner invitation, politely extended, to everybody, by the man who once extended a much less legible one; the volume title has never sat closer to the literal page. *With a Long Spoon* keeps its shadow over every warm meal, and this chapter is nothing but warm meals.

What I want next: the dinner, obviously — and I both want it and dread it. Guesses from here: Cassie watches Randi watch Pace and files something; some small tell (a look, a familiarity, the way Randi doesn't ask a question a stranger would ask) passes in front of Vee without registering — yet. And the mushroom-taste question from two chapters ago is still sitting minor-key under all this sunlight, unaddressed, waiting for Tuesday's Randi debrief that this chapter skipped past. I noticed it skipped it.
