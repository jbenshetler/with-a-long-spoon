# Cold read (grounded) — Strokes

## Reader reaction

Ah — a quiet one. After weeks of that house, the dresses, the bets, the brunches that leave me flushed, the book hands me seventy-five minutes of chlorine and a black line, and I'm surprised how grateful I am. This is the pool from the Cassie afternoon — "the one unstolen love," I remember thinking of it — and here it is again as the one place Vee's body has no opinions about itself: "her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here. It only worked." That line landed on me hard. For a girl whose whole arc has been shame and heat running on the same wire, a room where her body is just an engine is almost unbearably tender. Nobody gave her this. She took her lane her first week back and kept it. It's the only thing in her life right now that wasn't handed to her by Pace or Randi, and I felt the difference like fresh air.

The chapter's real work is what gets in the water with her. *Do you love him* — in Cassie's flat voice, off the third wall — and the thing that has been bothering me since "School Nights" bothers her too: the answer was "sitting plainly in her, easy to reach, easy to say, and no idea in the world why it had come out of her mouth as *maybe*." I love that the book doesn't answer why. And then the four strokes that are, for my money, the most important sentences in a while: she cannot tell "whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good, out of wanting, out of two months of being looked at like that." Four strokes is all it gets. The wall comes. She rolls. I actually said *oh* out loud. That's the first time Vee herself has brushed the question I've been carrying since the checkpoint began — not the plot's secret, she can't reach that, but its shadow: is this real or is it made? Her body files *did he mind* under nothing at all — the drawer answered that, and I believed the drawer too — but *do you love him* stays "a pressure under her ribs with a dread riding it." Dread. Not joy. The word snagged me and I trust it; the page put it there plainly and then let the water take it apart.

How I feel about everyone: Vee I love a little more for this — competent, unwatched, "not fast in years and did not care," legs "doing something honest." After so many chapters of her being looked at, a chapter of her not being seen by anyone felt like a gift the book gave her. Pace appears only as an ache and a question, and that's exactly right — a week of "Above Him" swooning, and now the counterweight, held gently, not cynically. Cassie isn't on the page and is all over it: her flat question is the thing swimming laps. She keeps turning out to be the person whose five words outlast everyone else's paragraphs. Randi is wholly absent — not even a flicker — and after "One Bite" left me rattled about her, the absence reads true to Vee: the practice-room rule again, put it down, don't look. No new suspicion earned here; this chapter has no one in it to suspect.

Heat: almost none, and honestly that's a relief and a virtue. The one faint charge is proprioceptive — water over the back of her neck, hot shower with eyes shut, arms "like they belonged to somebody generous" — sensual but not erotic, and the book knows the difference.

Friction: barely any. If I reach — "any more than she had ever once decided the second verse of a hymn" is lovely but the chapter says its own thesis twice ("It never answered anything, the swimming. That was the whole use of it") when once would have done. Tiny. The ending, though, is perfect: "Her head was as quiet as it had been all week, and she went up the hill in the dark with it that way, and did not go looking for it." She sandwiches the question. She doesn't answer it. The book leaves it ajar and so do I.

The title: "Strokes" is plain and triple at once — swim strokes, the strokes of the count, and, I couldn't help feeling, the strokes of a hand: his palm gentling her before each spank, his hand in her hair saying *wonderful*. The chapter itself stays chastely in the water, but the word carries the other meaning in from the recent chapters whether it wants to or not. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — hangs over even this quiet: sup with the devil with a long spoon, and here's Vee alone in a lane wondering, for exactly four strokes, whether the man is real or built. That's the long spoon question asked by the one person who doesn't know she's asking it. No tagline was shown me this time beyond the cover line, so I'll say only that.

What I want next: I'm braced, softly. A breath chapter this clean usually precedes weather. My guesses from here: Cassie keeps pulling threads — "you don't come home school nights" was an audit, and she audits; or the credit card comes due; or the pressure-with-dread-riding-it forces a saying-out-loud that Pace, who "says it in nothing," either meets or doesn't. And I still dread the day the third lane in from the wall learns who arranged the collision.
