## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

After the run of chapters this book just put me through — the bare walk down the hall, the brunch that nearly undid her, the drawer he emptied for her — this chapter is a glass of cold water, and I drank it gratefully. Vee alone in a pool on a Tuesday, nobody watching, nobody working on her. No Randi, no Pace, not even Cassie except as a voice carried in off the third wall. I hadn't realized how long it had been since Vee had a room entirely to herself, and the relief I felt at the empty bleachers told me something about how crowded her life has gotten — how many eyes are on her at all times, mine included.

The line that got me — quietly, the way this chapter does everything — is the body one: "her body, which had opinions all day long every day of her life, had none at all down here. It only worked." I've been reading this girl's war with her own body since the beginning: the cardigan, the mother's voice, the suit that "had nowhere left to go on her and hadn't for years" — the body that ended her sport now being the body everyone wants a piece of. And here is the one place where none of that runs. The pool was "the one unstolen love" back at the pool chapter with Cassie, and this chapter pays that off without ever pointing at it. She swims and the words come off things. It moved me more than half the love scenes have.

And then the four strokes. "She could not tell, for about four strokes, whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good." I actually stopped reading. This is the first time Vee herself has grazed the question I've been living inside since chapter three — and the terrible thing is that she's asking it slightly wrong, and I can't tell her. He *is* that good, scene by scene; I've watched the moleskin and the juice and the drawer. And he was also, literally, pointed at her, in a game she's never heard of. Both. The book gave her four strokes with the real question in her hands and then "the wall came, and she rolled, and the next hundred was already going" — and I don't read that as evasion by the author, I read it as exactly what swimming does, and exactly what Vee does. It's honest. It also made my chest hurt, because a smarter, colder girl might have hung on the gutter with that question a while longer.

Notice what dissolves and what doesn't: "*did he mind* was nothing at all" — the drawer already answered it, and her body knows it — but "*do you love him* was a pressure under her ribs with a dread riding it." Dread. Not joy — dread. She lay in his bed last week letting "I love you" be true where she couldn't take it back, happier than she'd known a person was let to be, and here in the water the same fact rides with dread on it. The chapter doesn't explain the dread and I'm glad; I can feel three or four sources for it (his silence, the speed, the "maybe" that came out of her mouth when the answer sat plainly in her) and the page leaves it in the water where it belongs.

No suspicion earned by anything on this page — there's nobody here to suspect. But the chapter sharpened my standing dread rather than lulling it. Watching her get her head "as quiet as it had been all week" felt like watching someone rest before something. That's my weather-sense, not the text; I'll mark it as a guess. Randi's total absence after "Sticky fingers?" is loud to me, though — Vee got soaked to the bone by a brunch conversation, nearly named a want in a bathroom stall, and this chapter she does the one thing that takes words off things instead of putting them on. It reads like recovery, and I don't think Vee knows what she's recovering from.

The title: "Strokes" is swimming strokes on the surface, and I took it that way for most of the read — but this book has spent thirty chapters teaching me what else a stroke is: his hand coming down, his hand soothing after, his hand in her hair saying *wonderful*. The pool version is the one kind of stroke that asks nothing of her and belongs to no one else. I like the title better the longer I sit with it. The cover board — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — hangs over even this quiet chapter: sup with the devil with a long spoon, and here's Vee at the one arm's length she has left, a striped lane with a fence of clock around each piece. No tagline appears in this packet's cover board, but I remember the jacket's line about every yes being freely given, and her four strokes of doubt are the closest she's come to auditing her own yeses. What I want next: I want her to get more than four strokes with that question. What I dread: that someone — Randi, Brooke, Cassie — closes the distance before she does.
