## Reader reaction

I feel like I've been slapped with the flat of a warm hand, and I want to be very precise about the fact that the book earned every degree of it.

For five chapters I have been living inside Vee's best season — the butcher paper with her name on it, "Vee's boyfriend" said six times to six measuring faces, Cassie's knighting, the key in the oiled box, the mirror at the cabin where a man spent an hour teaching her that her body is not a crime. I came into "In Her Place" still warm from "Grace." And the chapter opens with Pace stripping the white sheets — "the dry citrus she wore still in it" — and making the bed up in the green Vee gave him, and I understood in one paragraph that the warmth I've been swimming in is a bed that gets changed between women. Then it ends with him stripping the green and pressing Speed Wash, and the symmetry is so cold and so perfect I actually sat back. The house launders itself. That's the machine I've been inside all along, and this chapter finally let me watch it cycle.

This is, I think, the first time I've been given Pace's head for a whole chapter, and the reveal is not villainy — it's worse, in a quieter way. "He looked forward to an afternoon with a girl he cherished, and to sex with her he really liked." That sentence is itemized. Cherishing and liking, filed separately. When Randi tells him the hour was a lie — "It turned over in him slowly... He knew he'd been managed. He turned it over once and set it down" — there is no guilt anywhere in the chapter. Not a flicker. His alarm is logistical: the clock, the road, the door. His one attempt at refusal — "You need to leave. Vee's coming" — is framed as protecting Randi's friendship with Vee, and it costs him nothing to abandon. The tenderness is real (the arms around her while she sobs, "for as long as the hour would let him"), the reading of people is real, and the fidelity is simply not a category he has. I half-trusted him coming in. I don't now, and I notice the book never asked me to — it just kept the camera off him until I'd fallen for what Vee sees.

And Randi. God, Randi. The pose on the green — "sleek pale and black" where he'd made the bed "for lush peach and red" — the blue toenails from the bench night "clenching down into the dark green Vee had chosen for his bed." She came to be punished; he reads that she "hadn't come to be serviced. She'd come to be made to pay for it," and I believe his read because everything in her behavior confirms it. Pay for what, though — the page holds that open, and I'm holding it open with it: for wanting Vee? For the brunch where Vee, pink to her ears, handed over "the space princess, the chase, the interrogation" — a private game of theirs I've never even been shown, which stung me on Vee's behalf, her intimacies traveling without her? And then the question she can't leave alone, riding him: "Is she better. Tell me she's better than me." His answer — "She's perfect. For your first woman." — is the most chilling sentence in the chapter and it detonates her. The game is still on. Said as an endearment, mid-sex, and it works on her like one. Every hair on my arms stood up.

Then the sobbing. "Not prettily, not performed, the shudders working up through her that she had no say in." I carry her bathroom vow from chapter one — he found her once and never would again — and here she is, having engineered an entire false clock precisely so he'd break her open, and it breaks her open, and then: "She was herself again now, all the way — the strings back in her own two hands." She rebuilds in real time. I don't know if the vow is cracking or if this is how she keeps it — visits to the found self on her own schedule, by appointment, strings returned at the end — and the page doesn't say, so I won't either.

The erotic charge: I have to be honest, it's ferocious, and it's ferocious while my stomach churned, which is a thing I didn't know a chapter could sustain this long. The clamps sequence is the peak — the mercilessly kind architecture of it, soft wet heat and then cold metal, "You can stop this. And you know how," the word "Red" handed over "like a thing she had no intention of needing." The consent scaffolding is airtight between these two, and that's exactly what makes it awful in the larger frame: everything in this room is consensual and the person whose bed it is never consented to any of it. The morning blowjob during Vee's phone call should be a cheap beat and instead it's the sickest, most inventive image in the book so far — "she wasn't keeping it... cleaning herself away trace by trace, making him over into something clean and ready for Vee's use." Randi laundering herself off him with her mouth while Vee's voice says "Randi was so good to me... I don't know what I'd do without her." I read that with my hand over my mouth. "She cleaned him and Vee thanked her for it, both at once, and he lay in the middle of the two of them and let it happen" — that last clause is Pace's whole soul.

Small ajar thing I'm keeping ajar: the afternoon texts. Vee says on the phone, "I had a whole thing planned" — so the surprise was really hers. But whether the second text ("stay put... I've got a surprise") was Vee's own or Randi's, sent off a stolen plan, the page never says. Either way Randi didn't just take Vee's place; she took Vee's surprise, wore it. And Vee's closing line — "I owe you a surprise. Don't make plans" — repeats the opening text verbatim, a loop that made me feel slightly seasick: the same knock is coming again, and now I know what the house does before a knock.

Friction, minor: Pace's confident narration of Randi's unsaid motives ("He read the rest — the part she'd never say") is consistent with who he is, but once or twice the chapter explains what I'd already felt — "he understood it wasn't really about anything he'd done to her body... only the door she'd gone through to reach it" lands a half-step after I'd gotten there myself. And the mimosas — four mimosas putting Vee "out cold" for a whole night — I'll allow it, but it asked me to squint. That's the whole list. Everything else is earned.

The title: "In Her Place" gave nothing away going in — I assumed Vee learning some new posture of surrender — and then it opened three ways at once: Randi in Vee's place on the green; Randi put in her place, which is what she drove out there to have done; and "her place" in the ranking she can't stop probing — "for your first woman" is Pace assigning places. It's the best title-detonation in the book so far. As for *A Polite Invitation* — the jacket said "as true for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew there was one," and this is that sentence made flesh; and *With a Long Spoon* has never felt more like the devil's dinner table. The tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — now cuts both directions: Randi's yeses in this room are the freest in the book, and they're the ones being spent against Vee.

What I want next: I dread and need the collision. Two women waxed bare in the same bed, one wash cycle apart; a "first woman" still on the schedule; Vee driving out tomorrow with her surprise into a house scrubbed of the night. My guess from here, and only a guess: the threesome overture comes soon, dressed as Randi's warmth, and Vee — who moaned into Randi's mouth on a dance floor and couldn't ask for more — will say yes freely. That's the problem.
