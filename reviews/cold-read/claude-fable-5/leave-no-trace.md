# Cold read — Leave No Trace

*scene: scenes/leave-no-trace.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: dear*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This is the chapter I've been waiting for since "real shoes," and it delivered something better than the bed I half-expected. The dread I've been carrying since "Dear" got — not dissolved, but suspended. Randi isn't in this chapter at all, and without her the whole thing plays as pure courtship, and it worked on me completely, which is exactly the problem the jacket promised.

The moleskin scene got me first. Her mother's voice rising — "*what kind of girl lets a man*" — and then he's simply already done it, boot off, sock off, bare foot in his hand in the cold air, and "somewhere behind her the clench had let go without her catching it go." That's the whole pattern of this book in one beat: the shame arrives, and his competence walks straight through it before it can set. And then he *apologizes* — "I didn't think to ask about the boots." I noted it. He always asks, and when he fails to ask he names the failure. It reads as beautiful. Given what I know from chapter 3, it also reads as flawless technique, and I genuinely cannot tell where one ends and the other begins anymore. That's the state this book has put me in.

The cherry-pit correction — "We take nothing and leave nothing," then laying his own pit beside hers without looking — is the tenderest thing in the chapter and the title's literal source. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." Contrast her mother, whose entire pedagogy is making her small. I felt real warmth and real unease at once, because teaching someone without shaming them is also exactly how you make someone yours.

The dress. Oh, the dress. Russet silk, "the one," and she puts it back on the shelf herself — and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and *doesn't pick it up again*. Reader, I would bet the black dress never sees that induction dinner. That's a guess from here, but the author left the dress sitting on the rock between them so deliberately I could see it glowing.

Then the truck. The erotic peak of the book so far, and it's remarkable because he does nothing — he stands in the rain with his back turned, having given her his only dry shirt, and *she* summons him, *she* strips, eyes on his the whole way. "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice" — the mother's policing has fully inverted into fuel now, "floozy" landing as a thigh-press. That's been building since chapter 6 and here it completes. The tagline was standing in the cab: every gesture of hers was freely chosen, unprompted, hers — and it's still the endpoint of a plan she doesn't know exists. The button-by-button striptease with held eye contact genuinely worked on me, heat-wise; and then the swerve — his hand *shaking*, her arousal collapsing into care, "Pace. You're freezing" — moved me more than the striptease did. She brings his hand to her belly instead of her mouth. That's the chapter's real climax and it's tenderness, not sex.

One reader's stumble: "the old white pickup, the white gone dull in the wet" — I'd have sworn his truck was gray, back in "Off Six-Fourteen." Maybe I misremembered; it snagged me for a second. And the campus cop ending felt slightly convenient as a scene-ender — the siren eating her sentence so the big thing stays unsaid — though I'll admit the image of the two taillights going off together, him escorted away by law for a kindness, has stayed with me.

The title: "Leave No Trace" is the hiker's ethic, spoken on the page over cherry pits. But it recolors coldly the moment I let the jacket back in: this whole seduction is being conducted leave-no-trace. No witnesses, wax paper folded into the pack, Randi's fingerprints nowhere. Vee is being handled by someone who takes nothing and leaves nothing — except she's wearing his flannel home. He left a trace this time. Or she took one. I don't know which way that cuts and I like not knowing.

What I want next: the debrief. Does this reach Randi? Does Cassie see the flannel? And I dread the dress — if a russet silk dress appears, I'll know exactly what machinery bought it, and Vee won't.
