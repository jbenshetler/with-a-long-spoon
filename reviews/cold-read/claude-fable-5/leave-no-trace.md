# Cold read (grounded) — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

This one got me. I want to say that first and plainly: I spent most of this chapter melted, and I only noticed the cold spot in it afterward, the way you notice a bruise the day after the fall.

The whole hike is built out of small competences that land on Vee like tenderness, and on me too. The boot scene undid me — her stomach clenching because he's going to take her *sock* off, the mother's verdict already loading ("what kind of girl lets a man—"), and then the thing itself is so matter-of-fact it starves the shame of oxygen: moleskin cut, pressed with his thumb "to warm it," the sock "rolled gently up from her toes," the relacing, the pat on the toe "that meant only *done*." And then the line that is quietly the best in the scene: "Somewhere behind her the clench had let go without her catching it go." That's this book's whole method in one sentence — her defenses dissolving off-camera, even from her.

The cherry-pit beat is the same move and it worked on me again: "We take nothing and leave nothing," gentle, and then he lays his own pit beside hers "and didn't look at her doing it." She gets the rule without being made small. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." I believed every second of his kindness. I still do. That's the trouble.

Because there's one line I can't put down, and it's Vee's own: "The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." She says it about the sandwich — the hunger engineered so the food will land — and she says it with pleasure, and she does not take one more step with the thought. Reader, I took the step. That sentence is the entire courtship in miniature: build the hunger, then be the one holding the food. I don't think the text put its thumb on the scale here; it let her almost see it and look away, which is exactly her. But it chilled me, mid-swoon, and the chill was earned.

The summit passage is gorgeous and I want to flag it as gorgeous — the lake "holding the silver sky in it flat as a poured thing," and the valley's indifference: "it did not care that she was standing on the edge of it… and that not-caring was somehow the most beautiful part." For a girl who has been watched and judged her whole life — the bleachers, the mother, her own surveillance of her plate — a vastness that doesn't care is a mercy. And then Pace, caught looking "at her face in front of the valley," letting her catch him, then giving "her the rest of the looking alone." That's the most seductive thing he's done yet, and it cost nothing and asked nothing, and I swooned and noted the swoon.

The dress. She names the russet silk, hears "the wanting in it," and puts it back on the shelf herself — "You put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking." He says only, with "no weight on it at all": "That sounds like a beautiful dress." And doesn't pick it up again. I am now certain, as a guess from here, that this dress is going to come back — bought, produced, waiting in a box — and I both ache for the moment and mistrust it in advance, because I've watched how this machine works: it files her wants and returns them as gifts.

And then the truck. The flannel — his only dry shirt, packed for himself — handed over wordless, and then he steps back out into the rain and stands with his back to the cab so she can change. "His shirt, and then the truck. Both." The courtesy is total. And what it detonates in her is the chapter's erotic engine: she doesn't want the privacy. "*I want him in here with me.* And under it, slower — *I want him to see me.*" The striptease that follows is the hottest thing in the book since the bench, precisely because the mother's voice and the heat have fused: "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice," her thighs pressing "once, hard, on their own" at the word *floozy*. She keeps his eyes where she put them; she looks down at the fourth button *on purpose* so his eyes go with hers; she sees herself through him — the sheer bra, the gooseflesh, the raindrop running down between her breasts. And he doesn't move. "His face was want and it was gladness and it was a stillness she had never seen on anyone." I felt that in my body. This is her yes, wholly hers, performed as a gift — and yes, I know how much runway was built under it, and in the moment I didn't care, which I suspect is what the book wants me to notice about myself.

Then the turn that moved me most: reaching for his hand to start "the next thing," she finds it shaking. He's been freezing this whole time and was "a long way from the question of whether he was cold." She pulls his hand against her belly, under the flannel, into the warmth his shirt made on her — the first time in the whole book she has taken care of *him* — and says "We should go. Get warm. Please." Desire interrupted by tenderness rather than by shame. That's new for her, and it's the chapter's real summit.

The ending is a small perfect frustration. "The thing she wanted to say was bigger than any words she had for it" — and the campus cop's siren bark takes the sentence. "Pace—" and gone. I don't get to know what she was going to put where the big thing went, and neither does she, and I'm leaving that ajar because the page did. The last image — standing in his flannel that "smelled of soap and of a man and of the inside of a gym bag," the moleskin "still on her heel, holding" — is lovely. Something of his is on her, holding, invisible under the boot.

Which brings me to the title. On the page, "Leave No Trace" is the hiker's ethic, taught gently over a cherry pit. Read again, it goes cold in my hand. This whole enterprise — the collision, the summoned rescue, the runway — is a leave-no-trace operation: nothing taken, no fingerprints, every yes freely given, the wax paper folded into the pocket that "already had a sound of crumpled paper in it." He packs out everything. No evidence anyone was ever managed. The jacket's tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — has never fit a chapter more exactly: the truck striptease is the freest, most self-authored yes she's given yet, and I watched the trail get built under it switchback by switchback. *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon stays long, the invitation stays polite, and I keep accepting it right alongside her.

No Randi in this chapter at all, and I noticed her absence like a held breath — this is the first extended stretch that's purely Vee-and-Pace, no third presence, and it let me feel how good it would be if there were no plan. Cassie appears only in memory (the induction scream in the hall — and her "borrow good ones" advice produced Meg's ill-fitting boots, a funny, sad little chain). Friction: almost none. If I push, the sandwich tasting runs a touch long for what it does — but even there the sensory writing earned its keep, and her eyes running from horseradish and not caring that they ran is her arc in a mouthful.

What I want next: the dress, and I dread it. What I expect (guesses from here): the debrief with Randi — "You took your shirt off *for* him?" — where Randi will draw the truck out of her and I'll watch it become material; and consummation soon, likely at the house off six-fourteen, which the trail nearly touched. What I dread: that the next time Vee undresses, the room will have three people's intentions in it and only she will count two.
