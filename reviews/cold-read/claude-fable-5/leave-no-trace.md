# Cold read — Leave No Trace

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this one got me. After a chapter of Randi's smiling machinery, being back alone with Pace and Vee felt like coming up for air — and then the truck scene knocked the air right back out of me.

The hike itself is the Pace method in miniature, and I keep noticing that I can't tell anymore whether I'm being seduced along with Vee or whether he's just genuinely this good. The boot repair — "her bare foot was in his hand in the cold air" — the mother's voice rising (*what kind of girl lets a man*) and dissolving before it can finish, "the clench had let go without her catching it go." That's the whole book in one paragraph. Same with the cherry pit: "We take nothing and leave nothing," and then he lays his own pit beside hers and doesn't look. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." I felt actual gratitude toward him there, which is exactly the feeling the jacket keeps warning me to distrust.

The summit passage is the best pure writing in the book so far — "a lake holding the silver sky in it flat as a poured thing," and the not-caring of the world being "the most beautiful part." And then: he's not looking at the valley, he's looking at her face in front of the valley, "He let her catch him." A month ago I'd have swooned uncomplicatedly. Now I swoon and note it.

The russet dress. Vee names a wanting out loud, then shelves it herself — "You put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking" — and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and lets it lie. Reader, I know a Chekhov dress when one is set down on a rock between two people. The money question I've been carrying since the restaurant is now attached to a specific silk object, and I both want him to buy it and dread what it will mean.

Then the truck. Erotic charge: highest in the book, and — crucially — hers. He gives her his only dry shirt and stands in the rain with his back turned, and she reads it correctly ("His shirt, and then the truck. Both") and then *chooses* to undo that privacy. The striptease is extraordinary because the shame-voice and the arousal have fully fused now: "The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." Her mother's word — *floozy* — arriving at the same instant her thighs press. That's been building since the froyo chapter and here it's explicit. And his answer being stillness — "the gladness in him was the whole of the answer" — worked on me completely. Then the turn I didn't expect: his hand is *shaking*. He's been freezing this whole time and never said so. She pulls his hand against her warm belly and says "We should go. Get warm. Please" — and desire flips into care, hers for him, for the first time in the book. That moved me more than the buttons did.

The ending stung in a small precise way: the campus cop's siren bark amputating whatever big thing she was about to say. "Her body had already heard the siren and gone" — the trained girl bolting at authority before the wanted sentence can land. Both taillights swallowed by the rain. And she's standing there in his flannel, which she is going to walk into the dorm wearing, where Cassie lives.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the "Both" epiphany paragraph explains his gesture a half-beat more than it needed to — "for nothing she could see" then the deduction spelled out — but Vee working things out slowly is in character. The title bothered me in a good way: **Leave No Trace** is spoken on the page as trail ethics, but the chapter is *full* of traces — moleskin on her heel, his flannel on her body, wax paper of pits, the unsaid sentence. He leaves nothing on the mountain and everything on her. And of course the ugly second reading I can't unhear: the plan itself is designed to leave no trace — "They told her nothing." Randi never appears in this chapter and I still felt her, off-page, waiting to be handed all of this over a smoothie. Every yes here was freely given; that flannel is going to end up in a debrief.

Guesses from here: the russet dress arrives, somehow, for the induction — from him or (worse) via Randi. Cassie sees the flannel tonight. And the cop/fire-lane beat plus the old "off six-fourteen" trooper joke makes twice now that police have brushed the edges of Pace; I don't know what that thread is, but it's a thread.
