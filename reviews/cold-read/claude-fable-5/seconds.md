## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurt — and it hurt in the exact place the last two chapters had made soft. The shirt chapter ended with "He had heard all of it," and I cried a little at that, honestly; the book taught me that Pace hears everything, that hearing is his whole gift. So when Vee finally pushes her built sentence into the air — "I had been eating crackers my whole life. And you fed me a meal" — and he answers with "I love how good we are together," my stomach did the same small thing hers does in the laundry room. The two things had not been the same shape. She said *you*. He said *we*, and then narrowed even that to "what your body does." She offered him her whole interior and got back a compliment about their chemistry. I've been waiting chapters for one of them to say it, and instead I got the first scene in the whole book where his attention *fails* her — or refuses her — and I genuinely don't know which, and the page won't let me know, and I respect it for that even as I ache.

Because the page does show me something: the beat-too-long stillness, the hand that "had begun two different responses" and put both down, the two breaths she'd never heard him take. He *registered* it. My lean — marked as my lean, from here — is that it did not go past him. A man who heard the entire history of a dying flannel shirt in one confession does not miss "you fed me a meal" from a woman whose every appetite he has personally cultivated. Which means he swerved. And I hold what the page holds open: swerved because he can't say it while Randi exists? Because "I love you" is a word from the house he doesn't go back to? Because saying it would collapse whatever the game still is? I don't get to pick. Neither does Vee. That's the wound.

And here's the thing I know that she can't, the thing that made his answer land twice on me: "I never had this either. What you do — what your body does — I've never." Reader, he has a lover of years. Either that sentence is a lie, or — and this is the reading that actually chills me — it's *true*, because since the bench night Randi only gives him the face, and Vee is the first woman who's ever been fully present in his hands. If that's what he meant, then his answer was more honest than Vee could possibly parse, and still not the answer. The architecture was on that porch with them and only I could see it.

Vee broke my heart in the quiet ways this chapter. The rule she "did not know was a rule until she had bumped against it from the inside." The sentence carried for weeks like a memorized poem. And then the after: "*She had been received warmly,* she would remind herself, in the small private way you remind yourself of evidence whose weight you are not sure of" — that's the most precise sentence about being half-answered I've read in a long time. And the pile — "She put it where she put things she did not know what to do with. The place was not empty." I've watched her build that place all fall: the scar's "not tonight," the parents, the closed door. This is the first deposit that's about *her*, though. "The cracker-and-meal would not return to her speech" reads like a small funeral.

Erotic charge: barely any, and rightly — this is quilt-and-coffee intimacy, legs across his lap, the cool porch. What warmth there is (and it's real — the second cup already placed, cooled to drinking temperature, the choreography of reaching and not-moving) is care, not heat. No friction to speak of; if anything the middle section's "either end of it hurt" told me a half-step more than it needed to, but I'd felt it before the line said it, so it's a quibble.

The title did something cruel to me after the fact. "Seconds": the seconds of his too-long stillness; the second cup of coffee; last chapter she "held her plate out for another" — seconds of the meal, the greed he taught her. And the reading I wish I hadn't seen: *she is the second woman.* Sup-with-a-long-spoon indeed — "A Polite Invitation" keeps being exactly what this is, a warmth you're drawn into that was arranged before you arrived; "every yes was freely given" was the jacket's problem and here's the first yes that got no answer. What I dread next, guessed from here: that the pile behind her breastbone is where the book is going — that when the truth arrives it won't land on empty ground, and Randi's "just us" anthro class is standing right next to this new bruise.
