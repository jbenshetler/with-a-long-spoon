# Cold read (grounded) — Bare

## Reader reaction

This one hurt in a small, exact way, and it's barely two pages. After "Seconds" (the cracker-and-meal sentence set down and not answered) and "Under the Rug" (the fight, the nachos, the forgiveness that stopped feeling like plenty by midnight), "Bare" is the third night of the same wound — but stripped all the way down, alone, in the cold dorm room with Cassie breathing below her. And it's the one that got furthest into me, because there's no one else on the page to absorb the feeling. Just Vee, a shirt, and a phone she can't type into.

The phone beat undid me first. "She held her thumb over the little field where the words would go, and waited for the words. There weren't any." That's the truest rendering of that specific 2 a.m. paralysis I've read in a long time — the knowledge that every message you could send is "small, and would be answered small and warm, and would leave her exactly here." Small and warm. That's the whole problem of Pace in four words: he will always answer warm, and warm is not the name. She's diagnosed her own trap perfectly and can do nothing with the diagnosis.

Then the flannel. This shirt has been the book's warmest object since the rain on the hike — slept in, rationed, washed, ritually refilled with his smell two chapters ago ("wear it for me. The whole weekend"). And this chapter turns it over and shows me the underside: "the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." That line is quietly devastating. The book's central comfort-object exposed as a closed loop — her own warmth reflected off cloth, him across town asleep "easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." That last clause has a small blade in it, and I felt Vee's resentment in it even though the prose never names resentment. He sleeps easy. She doesn't. That asymmetry has been building since the dance-fight and it's now the shape of her nights.

And the ending — taking the shirt off and lying bare in the cold and *staying* in it, the shirt left "down in a heap where a dropped thing lands" — that's the first time Vee has ever refused his comfort. Every prior chapter she takes what he gives and converts it to heat or to sleep. Here she chooses the cold over the lie of the warmth, on purpose, and holds the choice till the window goes grey. It reads like penance and like protest at once, and I don't think even she knows which. The book leaves it open and I'll leave it open. Either way it's a genuinely new note for her: self-denial not from shame (her mother's old channel) but from clear-sightedness. The shirt promised him close and there was no one close — so she'd rather have the true cold than the false warm. That's growth, and it's grief, and it's both.

How I feel about the people: Vee — more than ever. This run of chapters has moved her from swept-up to awake, and awake hurts. Pace — my double feeling sharpens another notch. I hold the reader-only fact that he *can't* say the word cleanly, because there is a whole second life the word would have to be said across, and so his "I love how good we are together" and his "I'm listening now" land on me as both tender and evasive in the same breath. Whether he ducked her sentence deliberately or it "went past him entirely," the page (like Vee) can't resolve — but his not-saying now has weight I can't call innocent, knowing what I know. Cassie's sleeping presence — the two-inch window, faithful as ever — is a small comfort in the frame. Randi is nowhere in this chapter and I noticed her absence: Vee's crisis is running entirely without her confessor, and I wonder what the next brunch does with it.

Erotic charge: none, and rightly. There's skin — she's naked in the cold — but the nakedness is the opposite of erotic; it's exposure as fact. After the full-heat bedroom of "Under the Rug," this chapter's chill is the point.

Friction: almost none. If I reach, this is the third consecutive chapter circling the unsaid word, and I can feel the repetition — but each pass has contracted (a couple, a fight, now a solitary body), so it reads as a screw turning rather than a beat repeating. I'd want the next chapter to move, though. One sentence I'd flag as slightly over-neat: "and none of it told her what she was to him" — the chapter had already earned that without saying it. Tiny thumb on the scale.

The title: "Bare" is perfect and honest — bare skin, bare of the shirt, bare of the name, the ledger of what she has laid bare. It didn't give anything away; it deepened on the last paragraph. Against the book's title — *A Polite Invitation* — this chapter is what the politeness costs: every yes freely given, and no name attached to any of it. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — including, now, her own warmth, handed into a shirt that gives back only herself. The tagline's "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" has never fit a chapter better. *With a Long Spoon* still sits at arm's length for me, though "supping" with something you shouldn't get close to is starting to feel less like the jacket's metaphor and more like Vee's nights.

What I want next: I dread and want the same thing — this pressure has to go somewhere, and the two exits I can see are Randi's brunch table (where this ache will be extracted and worked on, and where Vee's other unsaid want is waiting) or a direct collision with the word itself. Guess from here: the withheld "I love you" is structurally tied to Randi and the plan — he can't say it because it isn't only hers to have — and Vee is going to carry this raw spot straight to the one person most equipped to press it.
