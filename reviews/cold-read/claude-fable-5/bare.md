# Cold read (grounded) — Bare

## Reader reaction

This chapter is barely two pages and it flattened me. After three chapters of the word circling — the cracker-and-meal sentence he didn't answer, the Chili's fight, the dark-room "did he belong to her" — this is the beat where nothing happens except a girl and a shirt, and it's the one that hurt most. The shirt has been mine as much as hers since the rain on the hike: I watched her sleep in it, sniff it stale, wash it in "The New Ordinary" and hand it back to be refilled with him, and that scene was one of the warmest in the book — "You'll have it back Sunday. Smelling like me." So watching her take it off and leave it "down in a heap where a dropped thing lands" felt like watching her drop something of mine.

The line that did the damage: "And it was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." That's a horribly precise discovery, and it goes past the shirt. Everything she listed a paragraph earlier — the rooms kept warm, the plate set down "once he'd built the hunger in her" (her own phrasing, and it stopped me cold, because that's exactly what he does and she said it without hearing it) — all of it is him doing, none of it is him saying, and she's just found out that a ritual of comfort can be a closed loop: your own heat handed back to you and called his. Knowing what I know — that this whole romance was pointed at her, built for her — the line lands twice. She's describing the shirt. I'm hearing the architecture.

How I feel about the people: Vee I ache for without reservation now. The detail of wearing his shirt *over* her own for the hallway, so Cassie "wouldn't have to say anything, a girl too far gone in a boy" — she's managing her own shame in her best friend's sightline, and it's so true to her. Pace — my trust took another quiet hit, and the text earned it. Not because he did anything here; because of what he keeps not doing, now three chapters running. "Gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him" is her bitterness, not fact, and I hold it as hers — but I notice I half believe it, and I notice I keep wondering whether the word he won't say is stuck behind the other woman I know about and Vee doesn't. That's a guess from here, and I'm marking it as one. Cassie, asleep two feet below with her window cracked its faithful two inches, is somehow the most comforting presence in the chapter without waking up once.

Swoon: none, and rightly none. What this chapter has instead is the ghost of every swoon before it — the whole catalogue of his care runs through her ("The things he made with his hands. The rooms he kept warm for her.") and each item is something that melted me in an earlier chapter, replayed now as insufficient. That's a cruel, effective use of my own reading history.

Erotic charge: a strange, real flicker. Pulling both shirts off and putting his back on with nothing under it, "the cloth straight onto her, the way she wore it when no one could see" — that's intimate and bodily and it worked on me for exactly as long as it worked on her. Then it curdles, deliberately. The final image — bare, cold, choosing the cold over the counterfeit warmth — isn't erotic at all, and the chapter knows it. Bareness has been her language of trust in this book (the truck, the riser, "May I look"); here she's bare for no one, in the dark, as refusal. That inversion is the chapter.

Friction: honestly none. It's spare and it earns every line. The phone beat — thumb over the empty field, "there was no text that made it come clear" — could have been mawkish and isn't, because she reasons her way out of sending anything and that's exactly her.

The title: "Bare" is perfect and it recolors backward. Every previous bareness was witnessed and cherished; this one is unwitnessed and cold, and the title makes me feel the difference. The volume board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* (no tagline supplied in this packet beyond that) — keeps sounding lower and lower under chapters like this. The polite invitation was accepted long ago; what I'm watching now is the bill arriving in installments, and she doesn't even know there's a bill.

What I want next: I want her to say the word out loud and make him answer, and I dread what his answer costs — and I dread more that Randi is the reason he can't give it (a guess from here, nothing on this page says so). I also can't stop thinking the shirt is going to stay in that heap.
