## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter is sly. "Covering" — I read the whole thing waiting for a cover to slip, and instead I watched Vee become the one doing the covering, and it landed like a small cold coin dropped in warm water.

**The felt read.** Start with what worked on me, because most of it did. The night class is lovely — Dr. Marsh arriving like weather, and that hau lecture went through me like one of the stats lectures used to, because of course it did: "the spirit of the one who gave it… the pull is why a gift can never really be free." I sat there doing the inventory before Vee could refuse to — the shirt, the gown, the drawer, the cashmere blanket that "simply appeared," the heels that are *Randi's*, the coat play that was Randi's idea, the whole relationship that was a gift Vee doesn't know she was given. Every object in Vee's life is inhabited by a giver, and half the givers are hidden. Vee "sat forward without noticing she'd done it" and I did too, and I notice the book has done this to me before — handed the heroine a lecture that captions her life while she takes notes and doesn't read them. I don't mind. It's one of my favorite things this book does.

Then Randi's story, which is the chapter's true center and knocked the wind out of me. The robin's-egg blue underwear, laundered, folded, set down beside her plate at Sunday dinner: "'They're not mine. They're your color.'" And the detail that ruins me — "She'd had them for *days.* She waited for him." That's the cruelest mother-move I've read in this book, crueler than Vee's "floozie," because it's patient. It's shame *staged*, with an audience booked in advance. And Vee's question — "How did they *tell* you?" — accidentally opening the door Randi hadn't meant to walk through, and Randi answering with "Nothing. One word, flat as a dropped card." For the first time I watched the face slip on the page in front of Vee, and Vee saw it — soft "What did you say?" — and then the brightness "came back up over her all at once like a light with a switch." Randi is never more heartbreaking than in the half-second before the switch. I understand her armor a little better every time, and I trust her not one inch more, and both of those are getting stronger together, which is exactly the corner this book keeps painting me into.

The neck kiss. Vee bent, "without any thought at all," and kissed the spot on Randi's neck where her own tears had been — and *Randi flinched.* The same verb from the bench, from "beautiful." Vee's body keeps doing things ahead of her mind with this woman — the tongue in the last chapter, the mouth now — and Randi, who wants exactly this and has resolved never to be found again, flinches and laughs it into "ticklish." I don't know which of them I ached for more under that streetlight. That little exchange — "you made my neck wet. Right here" — pointing at her own neck, elbow pinned in the hug — is as intimate as anything in the sex chapters, and it's fully clothed on a freezing sidewalk.

And then the ending, the title arriving: the restaurant closes and gets Vee "out of it," and she feels "a little unweighable stone of relief," and she carries the kiss story home untold "the way you carry something small in a full pocket." Vee — who tells Randi everything, who has been *trained* to tell Randi everything at these extraction lunches — has her first withheld thing. She's covering. Just a corner of herself, just till Thursday, she says. But I remember what she noticed under the cashmere: "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been—" That question, the one about what she might have been tasting all fall, is the thing in the pocket. And I — knowing what she can't — went cold at it, because the honest version of that question, asked out loud to the right person, is a fuse. She's planning to hand it to *Randi*. Randi will know what to make of it. God. Randi will know exactly what to make of it, and I have no idea what she'll do.

Suspicion, earned and precise: nothing new against anyone, but the machinery I already knew about ground audibly tonight. Randi "already there," the coat over the saved seat, the restaurant "Randi's find," the girl at the front who knows her — Randi is still always one move ahead, still receiving the full debrief of Vee's sex life over wine, "Good girl. Both of you" said like a handler's sign-off even when I believe the fondness is real. And her deflecting wink — "How long before Pace let you put clothes on again?" — deployed the instant her own mask slipped. She covered too. The chapter title is plural.

Friction: almost none. If I squint, the shore-house story is a very tidy thematic rhyme (underwear, a mother, shame at a table) landing exactly when the book needs it — but Randi trading a real wound for Vee's confessions is established behavior, and the wound felt true, so I bought it. Maybe the gentlest complaint is that nothing *happens* in the plot sense; but the not-happening — the untold story — is the event, and I felt it.

**Erotic charge:** low flame, but present — Vee's neckline "with somewhere to be," Randi's frank up-and-down, the retold morning warming the booth, and that neck kiss carrying more charge than it has any right to.

**The titles.** "Covering" is perfect and only readable at the end: Vee covering the kiss story, Randi covering the dining room with brightness, both of them buttoned to the collar over things underneath. The cover board — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* — keeps its teeth: another warm room, another meal, another spoonful taken gladly; the proverb hums under every one of these dinners now. No tagline in my packet beyond that.

**What I want next:** Thursday. Does Vee tell the kiss story, and what does Randi's face do when she hears "I tasted something in his mouth"? Guess from here: Randi will recognize it instantly — it may even be *hers* to recognize — and will steer, smooth as ever, and I dread how well it will work. And Wednesday's dinner — all of Vee's worlds in one room, with Randi in the middle of them and Cassie watching — is a lit fuse I can hear.
