# Cold read (grounded) — covering

## Reader reaction

Oh, this chapter is doing quiet, devastating work, and I want to talk about all of it.

**The felt read**

First, the pure pleasure: Vee retelling the coat night and the mushroom morning to Randi is one of the warmest scenes in a long while. I loved watching her *own* the story — "He isn't getting any of them. … I put it back and I shut the drawer on all of it" — the pleasure-princess bit performed for a second audience, the shimmy done right there in the booth. She's a different woman than the girl who read the right side of the menu first. And she still reads the right side of menus, but she picks the loft anyway. Her joy is infectious and I sat in it gladly.

But the chapter's real engine is Randi, and I felt my chest tighten twice.

First: the underwear story. Vee tells her fairy tale, and Randi — asked "what happened with him?" — answers, for once, with something true. And the truth is a horror rendered in one image: "Laundered. Folded. Robin's-egg blue." The mother who *waited for him*, who staged the cruelty at Sunday dinner, who said "They're not mine. They're your color." I have never been handed a cleaner explanation for the woman I've been watching for fifty chapters — the flawless face, the invented boys "safer than real ones," "eating her revulsion" at the family table. The boy who knelt in the grass quit that week and she never saw him again. That's where Randi learned that being found out ends things, that tenderness gets punished at a table. "Nothing. One word, flat as a dropped card." My whole read of her shifted a degree warmer and sadder at once. I don't think she planned to tell it — Vee's innocent "How did they *tell* you they'd found them?" walked her off the edge of her own performance, and I watched her voice "come down out of the story's key without her seeming to decide it." That's the realest Randi has been on the page since the bench.

Second: the bathroom scene, and what surfaces in it. Vee goes down to touch herself — third restroom, she counts, "of course she counted the practice room" — and at the top of the climb it isn't Pace's face that arrives. It's Randi's. "The tonight face, eyes dark and liquid, asking *how much could he see*." And then the page gives me one of its best flat lines: "Because that had been about Pace, obviously. All of it. About Pace." The lady doth protest. I'm not fixing what Vee won't fix — the book leaves her self-deception ajar and I'll leave it ajar — but the *reader* watched whose face did the work at the drop, and the reader noticed her tongue going hunting in Pace's mouth last chapter too. The wanting is accumulating faster than her permission.

And then Randi *names it*. "It's a good thing you got relief." That moment — "Vee's whole face stopped" — genuinely spiked my pulse, because for one beat I thought the whole cover was coming off. Then Randi hands her the exit: "After driving out to see Pace. He took care of you, but good." Watch what she did there: she saw through the floor, said so, and then *supplied the alibi herself*, pleasant as a hostess. That is either mercy or management or both at once, and with Randi I can no longer tell, and I think the book wants me not to be able to.

The goodbye lands the chapter's last small charge: Vee, "without any thought at all," kisses Randi's neck — and Randi *flinches*. The same flinch as "beautiful" at the bench. She laughs it off as ticklish, but I know that flinch. Unguarded tenderness is the one thing Randi's armor doesn't have a slot for. Vee's spontaneous mouth on her skin got further past the face in one second than Pace's whole bench did — and Randi turned her loose fast.

Dr. Marsh's opening lecture I read with a slow prickle. The *hau* — "the spirit of the one who gave it… the pull is why a gift can never really be free." I sat in a booth this chapter across from a woman wearing the scarf she once knotted at Vee's throat, in a book full of gowns and coats and heels and blankets given without cards, and a professor tells me every gift is inhabited and wants to go home. The stats course used to caption this book; anthro just took over the job, and I felt it in my stomach. Vee sat forward for eighty minutes and didn't hear a word of it about herself. She never does. It's the book's best trick and it still works on me.

And the small dread the chapter ends on: the closing time saved Vee from telling the forbidden-kiss story — and she felt *relief*, "a little unweighable stone," "which made no sense." It makes sense to me. Some instinct in her knows that story hands Randi something. She'll tell it Thursday. "Randi always did" know what to make of things — that line from two chapters ago is starting to sound less like comfort.

Friction: almost none. If anything, "Tomorrow… I can't wait to properly meet Pace" gave me a jolt of a different kind — wait, the group dinner is *tomorrow*, and Randi will be at a table with Pace, performing strangers, in front of Cassie who has never trusted her. My stomach pre-clenched. The chapter knows exactly what it's setting.

The title: "Covering." It's everywhere once you see it — the wrap blouse and its one controlling knot; the coat turned lining-to-the-wall to hide the bloom, then turned back out to *show* it; Randi covering her real story back up the moment the server appears; Randi covering *for* Vee with the Pace alibi; Vee's tidy "about Pace, obviously" covering her own face at the peak; the underwear washed and folded — a mother's obscene form of covering that uncovered everything. Even the book's cover-board — *A Warm Reception* — reads differently tonight: the loft was warm, the reception was warm, and warmth in this series is never free; the hau rides inside it. *With a Long Spoon* keeps whispering over every bottle Randi orders without looking at the price.

What I want next: Wednesday's dinner, with genuine dread and appetite in equal parts — every half-informed room in this book at one table. Guess from here: someone at that dinner catches something. My money's on Cassie's eyes, not anyone's words.
