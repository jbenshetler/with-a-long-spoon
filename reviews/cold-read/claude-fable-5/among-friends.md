## Reader reaction

This chapter did the thing I keep bracing against and keep falling for anyway: it made me deliriously happy for Vee while the floor of my stomach knew things she doesn't. "Among Friends" is the warmest chapter in a long while — maybe the warmest in the book — and I finished it grinning and a little wet-eyed and with a splinter under my thumbnail that I can't stop pressing.

Start with the swoon, because it's enormous. "I want to meet your people," said to a sink, in the grocery-list voice — and then the reveal that he has been *filing* them all fall: Meg's boots ("Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off"), Kayla who can "talk the paint off a wall" — Vee's own line handed back. I felt exactly what Vee felt: that being listened to that completely does something you'd be embarrassed to describe out loud. And then "Bring Theo," no question asked, no look around — the confidence of it, the refusal to make her explain a thing nobody asked her to explain. That whole kitchen scene is the best kind of intimacy this book does: nothing sexual in it and my chest went warm anyway.

The party itself is a small masterpiece of a man being good at people without ever being *on*. "How's your bench progressing?" — five words, and Danny talks for ten minutes and never gets a number. "Vee says you know jazz. Where does it start, for you?" — "a key go into a lock," and Theo's shoulders come down. He asked Kayla about the *dress* part of the wedding. He knew the Bricklayers' whole ridiculous name. "He walked around handing people the first line and then he stood back and laughed at their second one." And "Vee's boyfriend," six times, never varied, "a man with exactly one credential and no plans to produce another" — after forty-five chapters of the unsaid word, that word, spent in public, landed on me almost as hard as it landed on her. Cassie's knighting — "You can stay," like the results of an audit — genuinely moved me. Cassie glancing over mid-story and not stopping. "Something enormous and quiet go by underneath her, like a whale under a boat." That's the line of the chapter and I felt the whale.

And now the splinter. Because I know what Vee doesn't, this chapter is also quietly devastating about Randi, and the page never once says so — it just lets me watch. Randi found the place first, twenty minutes out on a road with no reason on it — Vee reads it as Randi's genius for arriving first at her things, and maybe it is, but I know Randi has been driving unmarked roads to Pace's world for a long time, and I can't rule out that she's been *to* this room before, or heard of it from him. The page doesn't bind it; I hold it open; it hummed either way. Then the handshake: "Vee's boyfriend" — and "Randi's hand closed hard around hers, hard enough to feel the bones." Vee reads solidarity. I read a woman hearing her own lover introduce himself, to her, as someone else's — and gripping down on it. "Her voice all warm coin." And the toast: "What I didn't tell him is that meeting Vee was the highlight of my year" — which is, God help me, the truth, the realest thing Randi says all book, delivered inside the performance where it's safest. Then the glazed quiet, the glass turning "a slow quarter at a time," and Vee — *proud* of it, proud she can move Randi, reaching over and covering the wrist and "the little cold line of the bracelet." Vee has no idea what she just touched. I did, and it ached.

The twinge scene is the chapter in miniature and it's honest in a way I want to salute: Vee sees Pace being wonderful at Randi, feels one bad half second "with a stinger on it," and talks herself down — correctly by everything she knows, wrongly by everything I know. "Some corner of her didn't know how to watch a thing go right without checking it for the trapdoor." The cruelty is that there *is* a trapdoor and her instrument found it and she filed the reading as a malfunction. Same with the parking-lot kiss — "what that looked like from over there. What he thought about it. If he thought about it" — the moth-light wondering that blows away. He knows exactly what it looked like. He half-authored it. And "What did you and Randi end up talking about?" — "They share an interest... You." I actually put my hand over my mouth. It is the sweetest true sentence in the chapter and the most double-edged one, both at full value, and Vee lets it "warm her from the middle out, too sweet to poke at." That's this book's whole method in one exchange, and it didn't feel like a thumb on the scale — it felt like a knife lying naturally on the table.

The erotic close worked on me completely. The truck blowjob is greedy and joyful and *hers* — "greedy with the whole night... 'Vee's boyfriend' said six times to six measuring faces, all of it going into this" — and the detail that undid me is the stillness: "the hand in her hair went still... every part of him pulled down to one place... She could live on that stillness — the man who did six things at once, down to zero." Then the drive: the flashback to the September vents, his hand trembling in hers then and nothing trembling now; her working her own jeans down "a graceless honest inch at a time"; and the consent grammar this book has trained me to love — "It was her that pulled it aside... an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second it went on." "The road itself was something he was doing to her" is gorgeous. Heat fully earned, fully explicit, fully hers. And then the last sentence turns the lamp back toward the splinter: "if some small sentence in her was still standing at a window somewhere, watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time, it couldn't have made itself heard tonight." So she *did* register Randi's quiet as something. It's in her, filed, drowned out. That ending is perfect and a little frightening.

Friction: almost none. Kayla, Meg, Danny, Theo are drawn fast and well; if anything the party's middle stretch is a touch long — one vignette (Meg's roommate story, maybe) could compress — but I didn't want to leave the room, so I can't call it a real complaint. Vee's "flat panic" at "brunches" — "*knows* was suddenly a live word" — I loved; guilt in rooms she hasn't entered yet is exactly her.

The title: "Among Friends" reads warm going in and double coming out. It's the true description of the best night of Vee's winter, and it's also what she is — among "friends," two of whom are lovers running a project on her, one of whom is in love with her. The series title has never sat heavier on a warm meal: brisket under heat lamps, her name on butcher paper, sup with a long spoon. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is fifty proofs of the first sentence.

What I want next: the debrief call Randi demanded — "I want the whole debrief of your own party" — which means Randi asking Vee to narrate a night Randi attended, and I dread and crave hearing how Randi holds her face through it. Guesses from here: the worlds touched tonight without colliding — Kayla, Cassie, and Randi were in one room and nothing snapped, which feels like the book cocking a hammer rather than lowering it. And Randi watching "Vee's boyfriend" get said six times has to cost something soon; her "not finished" thing with Pace just got a public counter-fact. I don't know which wire frays first. I turned the last page wanting the next one immediately.
