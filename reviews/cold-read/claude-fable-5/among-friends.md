## Reader reaction

## Felt read

Oh, this one got me. The dishes-and-wine opening is the coziest the book has ever been — "His back was a good listener" — and then "I want to meet your people," said in a grocery-list voice, and I sat up exactly when Vee did. This is Pace *initiating* the thing he's never done: coming out of the house, into her world. After forty-nine chapters of the drive, the wall, the withheld word, that sentence is enormous.

And then the reveal that he's kept everything — Meg's boots half a size big, Kayla talking paint off a wall, "Your heel came up out of the right one when you pushed off." That did to me what it did to Vee. It's the tender flip side of the man who engineered a collision: the surveillance-grade attention finally spent on pure love. "Tables have ends. Somebody ends up at one" — that's Pace's whole ethic in seven words, and I believed it. The Cassie logic — Vee refusing to build a dinner where one of her people has to be a good sport, "She'd watched somebody be fine about it before. She wasn't going to look at when" — quietly devastating; that's her mother, I think, or herself, and the book trusts me to feel it without naming it.

**"Vee's boyfriend."** He says it to Randi first — *first*, before any guest — and it went through me like it went through Vee, "the first swallow of something hot." The word that's been dormant on my ledger since forever, spent six times, flat, never varied, becoming the night's running joke. And Vee at the buffet trying it on with her own mouth. I was grinning.

But the chapter's real event is happening one layer down, and the text let me see it while keeping Vee blind. **Randi's hand closing hard around Vee's, "hard enough to feel the bones,"** at the exact moment "boyfriend" lands. Vee reads it as shared joy. I read it as a woman getting hit. Randi beat them to the restaurant — "We wanted to be here first." "You did. It didn't work" — and that little exchange has teeth I don't think Vee felt. Then the toast: "meeting Vee was the highlight of my year," the teasing draining out of her voice mid-sentence, and afterward the **glazed quiet, turning the glass a slow quarter at a time**, which Vee is "privately, foolishly proud" of causing. Foolishly is the author's one visible thumb, and I'll allow it, because it's Vee's own word for herself. I know from chapter 45 that Randi is in love with her. Vee puts her hand over the bracelet and Randi's smile "got loose from her a little, more of it arriving than she seemed to have planned" — I ached for Randi, genuinely, for the first time without a rider of dread. She sat at that table and watched the man she shares announce himself as the boyfriend of the woman she loves, and toasted them.

The twinge — Vee's jealousy stinger at Pace laughing at Randi — is beautifully misdirected irony: she checks the *right* pair of people for the trapdoor and talks herself out of it. "She had put these two people in one room herself" — no, love, they put themselves in a room long before you. And "What did you and Randi end up talking about?" / "They share an interest... You." — that answer is technically true and it made my skin prickle. He's telling her the truth in a shape she can't open. Ch.1 all over again.

The parking-lot kiss between the women, with Pace twelve feet away, "nothing on him to say what he made of it" — and Vee's moth-light wondering "what that looked like from over there" — the threesome plan breathing on the page and she almost, *almost* looks at it.

**The heat:** the truck. Yes, it worked on me — the blowjob especially, "the hand in her hair went still... every part of him pulled down to one place... She could live on that stillness — the man who did six things at once, down to zero." That's eros written through character and it's the best kind. The drive-home sequence pays off the ch.49 county-road denial explicitly — same road-in-the-dark, same circles, "her own hand had used on that other road and been denied, only it was his hand" — and her pulling the lace aside herself, "an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second," is the tagline made flesh. The kept coat with the stain — "She'd never tried. She'd kept the coat" — killed me.

And the last sentence: "if some small sentence in her was still standing at a window somewhere, watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time, it couldn't have made itself heard tonight." Vee *saw* Randi's grief. Some part of her filed it. It will come back.

**Titles.** "Among Friends" — warm on the surface, and barbed: what's said among friends, what's hidden among friends; Randi and Pace *are* friends, of a kind Vee doesn't know, sharing an interest. The book title's long spoon — supping with the devil at three pushed-together tables, everyone smiling. The tagline never louder: every yes tonight was freely, gloriously given, and the room it was given in was built by the two people who love her most.

**Friction:** almost none. Maybe the party set-pieces (Danny's bench, Theo's jazz, Kayla's wedding) run one beat long — each proves the same thing, that Pace makes people taller — but I enjoyed every one, so I can't complain hard.

**Want next:** dread and want in equal measure. The Feb dance is imminent. Guess from here: Randi's glazed quiet is the crack — either she tells Vee she loves her, or the threesome plan gets triggered as the only way she can have any of this. Pace saying "boyfriend" publicly may also force the Pace-Randi arrangement to a reckoning.
