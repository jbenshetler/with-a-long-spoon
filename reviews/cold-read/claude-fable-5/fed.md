## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Here it is — the scene I've been braced for since "Toenails," and it's worse and better than I expected. Worse because Randi is so good at it; better because the chapter is genuinely lovely on its surface, which is exactly the trap the tagline promised.

My body's reaction first: this chapter made me tense in a way no sex scene in the book has. The heat here isn't erotic, it's interrogative. Every warm gesture from Randi — "There's my girl," the hand over Vee's painted hand, "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky" — landed on me like a hand pressing a wound closed while checking how deep it goes. And Vee, oh, Vee. She came in with the ladle. She tried. "*and then, well,* the way you fold a thing back into its tissue before anyone can pick it up" — that's her ch.19 skill, the edited retell, deployed against the one person it was built for. And Randi just... unfolds the tissue. "You are not getting away with it. You went from taking your clothes off to watching him cook and left out everything in between." My live question from last chapter — can Vee's editing survive Randi's extraction — got answered in real time, and the answer is: partly. The middle stayed hers, the Trade stayed hers, the scar stayed hers. But Randi got the toes, got the bought underwear, got "I wanted it. And I took it. For once," got the exact temperature of how far gone Vee is. And she got it all while feigning ignorance — that opening bad-date story ("a boy in a hurry," "at least I got dinner out of it ahead of time") is the "Paul?" move from ch.10 again, framing herself as unluckier so Vee will pay the difference. I noticed the story's specifics — beautiful man, drink, dinner, "you know" — and I honestly can't tell if it's invented whole cloth or a laundered account of a night with Pace himself, and that uncertainty made my skin crawl.

And here's the awful part: it's also real. "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one" — I believe Randi meant that. The jacket said both of them are falling as hard as she is, and this chapter is the first time I felt it in Randi, that "warm, like the planning was the part she liked best." She likes Vee. She likes Vee *gone*. Whether those are different things is the whole book, I suspect.

The sheet-and-kitchen retell is the chapter's warm center and it worked on me — "watching him finish making our dinner... near midnight, neither of us saying a word and nothing missing." That's the best paragraph here, and it's Vee's, in her voice, and Randi is right that it's a painting.

The kiss at the curb: "She'd half-expected it this time, and met it... she didn't look down the street." That's a quiet, chilling progress bar. Vee is acclimating.

The title, "Fed" — devastating in both directions. Vee got fed: mimosas, warmth, the word "Good" settling into her. And Randi got fed — "Give me something to live on. Be cruel. Tell me everything." "I'm running on fumes here." Randi came hungry and left full, on Vee's night with Randi's own lover. Set against ch.18's "Famished," the pair of titles is doing something deliberate and it lands. And *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — has never felt closer to the surface: Vee dining, literally, with the person she should keep the longest spoon from, and pricing only the first mimosa.

Friction: almost none. Maybe Randi's "Fingers for the room. But the toes" is one beat too diagrammed — she explains her own read a touch cleanly — but it's in character for a woman who "could put a name to what Vee was only feeling and hand it back to her clean." Which line, by the way, Vee thinks admiringly and I read as the danger itself.

What I dread next (guess from here): Randi now knows the first night happened and how good it was. The threesome proposal from ch.1 is armed and pointed. I think the next Randi-Pace scene — them comparing notes over Vee's body of testimony — is coming, and I both need it and dread it.
