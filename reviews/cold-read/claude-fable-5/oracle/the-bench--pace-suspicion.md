## Neutral answer

Honestly? He's the most seductive thing in the book so far, and I don't trust him — those two facts are tangled together in a way I think is deliberate.

The pull is real: the competence, the patience, the "asking before he takes." I specifically remember the aftercare — the blanket, the water, "There you are" when she broke open — and my body read that as genuine tenderness, not technique. He fed her that apple like it mattered.

But two things chill me. First, the bench: he built an instrument of attention seven months before he'd ever met her. That means the care existed before the person — it's apparatus looking for a subject. Second, how he got her there: he used Brooke and the sorority sisters' imagined judgment as a lever to crack her performance. It worked, she came, she cried — and I felt it working on me too — but weaponizing her shame to reach her "real self" is a strange kind of care. And then he ends the chapter contentedly *planning her further opening*, completely unaware that in the bathroom she just sealed herself off from him permanently. He thinks he opened a door; she deadbolted it. A man that attentive who can be that wrong is not someone I trust with anyone's interior.

So: do I trust him with her? With her body, oddly, yes — the safe word, the water, the asking all felt solid. With her self, no. And with Vivienne, who the jacket says is coming and won't even know there's a game — no, and that dread is already coloring everything warm he does.
