## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one hurt in the good way. The dance lesson itself is the best extended metaphor the book has run — and the reason it works is that it's not decorative, it's literal. "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead. Being jelly doesn't work." That's the whole relationship in one correction. And "Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours" — the space you keep to protect yourself is what gets you stepped on. I felt my stomach do something complicated there, because it's true as dance advice and it's *exactly* the doctrine Pace has been teaching her all along: total surrender is safety. The chapter believes it. The pad scene is genuinely lovely — "There it is," quiet — and the moment the dance "took her and she stopped trying to guess" carried a real erotic charge without a single sexual act. Heat 1, but a strong 1; the frame-and-lead stuff is more charged than some of the actual sex chapters.

Then the blonde. "He's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night" — pure poison delivered as compliment, and I believed every beat of Vee's detonation because the book has spent twenty-five chapters building the *floozy* charge in her, the mother's voice, the "three dates and she'd spread her legs like a slut." That line landed hard — it's her mother's vocabulary erupting through her own mouth, and she even names the floozies on the floor. Her venom is ugly and completely earned by her architecture. The glass-past-catching image ("the way you hear a glass go past the point where you can still catch it") is perfect.

And Pace's exit wrecked me a little. No defense, no pursuit — beers set down untouched, out into the cold. That's either a man genuinely hurt or a man who knows the most devastating move is withdrawal, and my honest gut says *both are true at once*, which is this book's whole trick. The bartender's absolution — "Leaves her at the door. Doesn't take advantage... Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey" — I noticed something as a reader: every witness in Vee's world testifies for Pace. The girl at the till, the regulars, the bartender. And the testimony is *true* as far as it goes — he really doesn't take advantage of drunk girls. But I know what the bartender doesn't: the pointing game, the staged collision, Randi. Vee's instinct tonight — "I'm doing well. He's making me. I wonder how many of them he's taught" — was the closest she has ever come to the actual truth, and the chapter's machinery (the kind bartender, the sleepless shame spiral, "She'd done it to a good man") systematically dismantles her one correct suspicion. That's the most frightening thing the book has done quietly. She was wrong about *which* deception — but she wasn't wrong that there's one.

The washed flannel gutted me: "It came back smelling of fabric softener and no one. She took it down and put it on anyway."

Friction: mild. The blonde is a touch convenient as a grenade, and the three italicized thoughts ("*I'm doing well. He's making me.*") are slightly more legible than this book usually allows — a thumb on the scale, but a light one.

And the ending — "go to Randi's." Oh no. She's taking her repentance to the other half of the trap. "Cool embarrassment was just information" is such a Vee sentence and I dread where it's carrying her.

**Title:** "Lesson" is doing triple work — the dance lesson, the lesson she thinks she learned (don't punish a good man), and the lesson I'm afraid she actually absorbed: distrust your own alarm. That last one recolors the whole chapter darkly. *With a Long Spoon* — the frame is the spoon, isn't it: the thing that keeps you exactly close enough to be fed. "Every yes was freely given" — she fought the frame, then gave it her weight freely, and it held her "in the one place the next step could come from, so there was nothing to do but take it." That sentence *is* the tagline.

**Guess from here:** she goes to Randi to confess/repair; Randi extracts the whole night, files it, and engineers the reconciliation — possibly steering it toward the threesome. The cab fare came out of money "meant for other things" — the debt noose tightens.
