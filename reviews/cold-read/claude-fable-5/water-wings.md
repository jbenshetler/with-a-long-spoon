# Cold read — Water Wings

*scene: scenes/water-wings.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: long-way*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the chapter that made me realize how much I'd missed Vee existing outside the mechanism. No Pace, no Randi, no staged anything — just Vee and Cassie and a rec pool, and I exhaled for fifty pages' worth of held breath right along with her. The chapter even names the feeling: "fifty yards of held breath going out at once."

The synchronized swimming reveal landed hard. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken" — that's the saddest, cleanest line in the book so far, and it recolors everything: the mother's "that's plenty" voice, the overbuilt froyo cup, the suit from sixteen "when there'd been less of her to fill it." The underwater passage — the scull her hands remember, "Nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything" — is the first time in six chapters that Vee has been somewhere no one wants anything from her, and the book knows it and so do I. "Grieved, a little" nearly did me in.

Cassie is now fully my favorite character and I'm slightly in love with her. The splash instead of the question — "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger" — is the exact inverse of everyone else in this book. Pace asks. Randi draws out shames. Cassie *splashes*, and it works better than either. "They will never know the love of a woman," kept low, kept for Vee — she could've played it to the bleachers and didn't. That's real love, the kind with no operation under it, and having it on the page makes the Pace/Randi warmth look different by contrast. Which unsettles me: the chapter where nobody wants anything from Vee is the tenderest one yet.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and rightly. Bodies everywhere — the suit that "gave her nowhere to hide," the bleacher catcalls — but it's all about the body as problem and audience, not desire. The catcalls ("You won't need floaties with *those!*") gave me the flinch they should; Vee's banked eyeroll broke my heart a little because it's a *practiced* face, the same face-on entrance-walk performance the book keeps showing me.

Friction: almost none. Maybe "and the water knew it" is a hair cute. The last line — the body "stayed up on the deck with the rolled towel, where it could wait" — flirts with over-neat but earned it.

Title: "Water Wings" — the floatie joke from the bleachers, cruelly; but also the thing you don't need once you can swim, and Vee can swim, beautifully, in the one element that was never taken from her. Sweet and sad both ways. Against the book title and tagline: no yeses in this chapter at all — which may be exactly why it's here. This is what Vee has that's hers, before. I dread the "after" reading of this chapter.

What I want next: I dread Pace or Randi finding out about the pool — the one untaken room. *Guess from here:* the water will come back — Pace's house, a lake, something — and the mechanism will enter the last place that was hers.
