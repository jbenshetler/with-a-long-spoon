## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, I loved this one. I loved it in the uncomplicated way this book almost never lets me love anything, and I spent half the chapter waiting for the trap and it never sprang — which, given the jacket in my other hand, is its own kind of suspense.

The setup made my stomach clench right along with Vee's. "The name went through her before she could put anything in front of it" — yes, mine too, because Sheri has been a live wire since chapter 47, "real Sheri" was literally on my worry list. And Pace's delivery is so perfectly, maddeningly *him*: "Small," he said. "Loud." He rinsed. "We cycle." A plate went into the rack. "She dances." Handing her the one fact "with a blade in it... like a dried spoon." I genuinely couldn't tell if he knew what he was doing there — whether he's oblivious to jealousy or administering exposure therapy — and that uncertainty is the residue of everything I know that Vee doesn't.

Then the reveal, and it's one of my favorite reversals in the book so far: the midnight blonde "didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand. There was no room for her." Five-foot-nothing, crooked makeup, a hug "with its whole weight committed" — and Vee's dread persists one confused beat ("the body was all wrong and the hug was not") before Sheri's slow appreciative once-over lands not as pricing but as *enjoying* her, and Vee's laugh breaks out "before she'd voted on it." That laugh released a month of tension in ME. And "And you get to motorboat them things?" — Pace *blushing*, past anything deniable — is the most human I've ever seen him. Vee's right: most disarming thing in fifty-one chapters.

Sheri herself is an instant top-tier character. The coming-out story wrecked me — "Whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish," "Strangers will say that for free. Easy words, for a hard thing." And the payload dropped so quietly I almost missed its weight: "Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen." SIXTEEN. My carry-forward says "parents cut off" — this is the first number, and it's worse than I imagined. It also recolors ch.25's tutor backstory ("Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong") — sixteen, gone from home. Something happened. And Sheri: "I could tell he understood it, and I didn't ask how." She's the one person who takes nothing off him, and he's *off duty* with her — a lean Vee had never gotten to see.

Also crucial, and I noticed it hard: Sheri's dance-hall testimony. "Hands stay where the dance puts them. Every time. The whole hall's given up on him." Independent character witness for Pace's restraint — the same restraint the jacket tells me is the mechanism of a trap. The book keeps stacking evidence that he is genuinely good, from sources with no stake. Either the trap isn't what I think, or it's going to hurt more than I can currently price.

Erotic charge: essentially nil, and correctly so — Vee's whole armor-dressing sequence (Randi's induction heels against a woman she invented) is charged in a different register, dread-as-eros. The nearest heat is the arm at the end, "It read as affection. It worked as ballast... those had never been two different things."

Friction: almost none. Maybe "Fear needs a face, so you give it one" lands a half-inch too aphoristic, the author explaining Vee's own insight back to me. Tiny.

One cold thread I refuse to drop: no Randi in this chapter at all. Two nights after the toast, after "call me tomorrow, whole debrief" — and we hear nothing of that debrief. The absence is loud to me even if it isn't to Vee.

Titles: "Another Round" — literal (the taproom rounds, Sheri sending him back for the porter) and an echo of the early chapter "A Round." It also feels like the book saying: another circuit, another person folded into the table. Round like the tables Pace insisted on — no ends, nobody lesser; Sheri belongs at one. The book title darkens strangely against a chapter this warm: sup with the devil with a long spoon — but everyone at this table used their bare hands and nothing bit. Yet.

What I want next: the hall, Saturday, Brooks and Dunn, "leave them shoes home." Guess from here: the dance chapter is where Randi's grief or the Feb dance thread finally collides with Vee's new world — and I half-dread Randi showing up at that hall.
