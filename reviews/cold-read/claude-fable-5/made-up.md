## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is a small chapter and I loved it, and the loving was uncomplicated in a way that made me nervous the whole time — I've been trained by thirty-one chapters to check the corners of any room this warm.

What lands first is Vee. She's the engine of the entire scene: she made the plan, she staged it, she directed him — "Get your wine and go to the den. Wait for me in the recliner." — and he *obeyed*. After the bet and the spanking last chapter, this is the counterweight, and it's gorgeous: she puts out one flat hand and he sits back down, and "she watched him make himself do it." The girl who couldn't put her own arms down in the fitting-room mirror is now choreographing a private runway in nothing but heels. The blush line at the end — "the blush went down and down, and she did not cover one inch of it" — is the chapter's whole argument in one sentence, and it pays off "shame wants a witness" from last chapter by inverting it: here shame *has* a witness and stands there anyway, and it turns into pride. "Knowing that, and going anyway, was the entire costume." That's the best line in the chapter and I felt it in my chest.

The erotic charge is real and it's all clothed-in-nothing tension — no sex, and it doesn't need any. It peaks twice: the slit "given and taken back and given" on the long stride, and then the second walk down the hall, "the house's warmth moving over every part of her at once so that there was no forgetting, anywhere, what she wasn't wearing." His restraint — the hand that "came partway up and he put it away again, and the putting-away cost him something she could see" — is doing the erotic work. Looking-as-touch. It worked on me.

And here's my unease, which the text earned. The dress. Randi commissioned that dress; Pace *made* it; Randi buckled those heels onto her feet in a boutique, on credit. Vee thinks she's giving him a gift he's never seen finished — and she is — but every article on her body in this scene was placed there by the two people running the game. "It was out there now, in the cold and the dark, fifty feet from the man who'd made it" gave me a genuine chill in the middle of a tender paragraph. She smuggles the dress in her car like contraband, "a night she wasn't letting herself plan on," and the whole apparatus was planned for her months ago. Even her triumph is wearing their costume. Also: "The other is for the world. This is just for me." Lovely line, and — the world included Randi's induction night, which he engineered. "Just for me" is a claim he shares with a partner Vee doesn't know exists. I don't think he's lying, exactly. That's the horrible part.

Friction: almost none. Halloween as the frame ("Tonight they're costumes") is a touch neat — the one-night-a-year-nobody-has-to-explain-it line points at its own theme a little — but I'll allow it because Vee's wit sells it. The chapter is short and knows it.

The title: **Made-Up** is doing three things at once and I noticed all three with pleasure — made up as in costume/pretend (Halloween), made *up* as in invented (she invented this reveal herself, her first authored performance), and the dress literally *made* for her by his hands. Maybe a fourth: made-up as in reconciled, the couple fully at ease post-Rusty-Nail. The book title keeps tightening: this chapter is Vee issuing the invitation for once — every yes here is *his*, freely given — and that's exactly why the tagline hums underneath. She thinks she's the host now. She's still the guest.

What I want next: the retelling. She debuted the second costume for him; will she debut the *story* for Randi? And I keep waiting for the bench. Guess from here: the "bringing a bag" thread plus the heels living at his house means the threesome on-ramp is close — the two worlds can't stay fifty feet apart much longer.
