## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this chapter is a mercy and a knife at once. After two chapters of frozen diptych — Randi in Gstaad, Vee in her childhood bed — the book finally lets me at Pace, and it does it in the one place I'd never have guessed: a county-road diner with Sheri, and the first line already tells me he's carrying something he's decided "could keep." Which means the silence Vee has been reading as absence is a man white-knuckling through the break too. "I want Vee. I miss her." I actually exhaled when he said it. Forty-six chapters and the most direct thing he's ever said about her comes to a five-foot woman over cheeseburgers.

Sheri. I've been dreading Sheri because Vee's jealousy made her a blonde-adjacent rival in my head, and the book just quietly demolished that. "There was somebody... Paige... Liked her." Sheri likes women. The rival Vee invented is a gay friend who thinks about *Randi's* backside "more than's decent" and is squarely, bluntly on Vee's side: "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her... from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." She's wonderful — the one person in this whole novel who wants nothing off Pace, and he "come in tonight needing it more than he'd have said." Her estrangement is a gut-punch mirror too: Paige left her over "not talking to my people," and Pace was cut off by his parents — "Ironic. Isn't it." The line "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice" landed hard, and Sheri swatting it away ("Don't waste the good stuff on me") landed harder.

But the chapter's real detonation is casual, mid-fries: "She find out about the brunette, then." *Sheri knows about Randi.* Knows Pace has both. Has met Randi — "Y'all came in dancing... She looked at me like I was one more of your harem applicants." So the dance-hall night — the one Vee's imagination has been poisoning — was Pace and *Randi*, with Sheri hugging him hello. And Sheri asks it like it's the obvious failure mode, and Pace's "No. Not like that" is chilling in its calm. The one honest friend he has knows he's running two women and gives him hell about the *declaration*, not the deception. I don't know what to do with that. It made me trust Sheri completely and trust Pace a notch less, even while my sympathy for him climbed.

And then his defense — "I told her she was the most important thing in my life... And she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something" — bricks, "and the true things were supposed to come to something." He *knows*. "Is that so hard?" / silence. "So say it." / "I can't." Not *won't* — can't. After ch.25 I believe the can't is real, tutor-shaped, and the book still refuses to explain it, which is right.

Erotic charge: essentially none, and correctly — though Sheri's raunch ("Bet those long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you," Pace blushing and answering anyway) keeps Vee's body warm in the room. His "The air's different when she's in the room. It — crackles" is the closest thing to a love declaration he's ever produced, and Sheri's "Lord almighty. Get over yourself" at "the philosophy of your food" gave me the only real laugh in five chapters. Friction: almost none. Maybe the Chili's recap paragraph over-explains a fight I already lived through — "every sound in the room arguing with him at once" retells ch.42 for anyone who forgot; I hadn't.

The title: "The Usual" — the order she writes without asking, "yes like people saying yes to their own names." A yes that isn't even a choice anymore. Against the tagline that's almost sinister on second look, but on the page it's the tenderest thing here: the one room where being known requires no words at all. Which is exactly the room Pace can't build for Vee, because she's asked for the words.

What I want next: February. "Brooks and Dunn cover band... You're coming" with "the redhead" invoked — that's a planted date and I want Vee at that dance hall meeting Sheri, the imagined rival turned ally. Guess from here: Sheri learns the two women know each other as best friends and that's when her "pretty shitty" verdict gets teeth. And I still dread the reveal more than the rupture — Vee now has three people who love her and one truth none of them will hand over.
