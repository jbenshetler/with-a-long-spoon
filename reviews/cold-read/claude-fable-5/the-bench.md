# Cold read (grounded) — The Bench

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came in expecting Vee — the jacket promised me a starving college junior — and instead spent the whole chapter with an unnamed woman in a warm house, and when he finally said *"There you are, Randi,"* I actually made a sound. So this is Randi. The glamorous friend, the one who will steer. Book One opens not with the girl who doesn't know there's a plan, but with one of the planners — facedown, red, begging, and crying without knowing it. If the jacket is to be believed, I've just watched the seducer get taken apart first, and that recontextualizes everything the blurb promised. I'm holding it loosely, like I was told to. But my skin isn't holding anything loosely. This chapter worked on me completely.

The heat is real and it's built almost entirely out of patience. The apple sequence — sliced thin because she won't eat thick slices, cut sides up because he *noticed she eats them faster that way* — did more to me than most full sex scenes do, because it's desire expressed as attention. Bite, kiss, bite, his hands turning back "a little higher... each time leaving again before it arrived." The undressing from one knee, folding each garment. And then the bench itself: her reading its purpose piece by piece — face cradle, familiar; handholds, "a massage table didn't have them"; the open middle where "her hands closed on nothing." The peak for me was the *please* — the distinction between her waiter's-voice *please* and the one "from below," and his refusal to accept the counterfeit. "It was still her *please*... She was about to know." I was fully swept up. I wanted her to get there. When she did — "The orgasm was *hers* in a way nothing he had done with her had ever been" — I believed it in my body.

And then the bathroom scene took all of that and quietly turned the deadbolt on it, and I have to say: it wrecked me, and I'm grateful for it. "He could have the face. The face was very good. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." That's the coldest sentence in the chapter, and it arrives right after the warmest scene, and the chapter *earns* both. The flinch at "You're beautiful." The bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail "where it would hurt," which she doesn't seem to know she's doing — that detail scared me more than anything else on the page. And the terrible symmetry of the final image: he holds her, "quietly happy about her," having no idea the door just closed. She locked the front door behind her first thing when she arrived; she locked the bathroom door, "which was not a thing she had ever done before in his house"; and the hum in her ribs is "the size of a key turning in a lock." I noticed the locks stacking up as I read, and it felt like watching weather come in.

How I feel about them: Randi, I ache for. The mother on egg whites "a thing she had told him with a knife in it," the appetite trimmed to almost nothing, the face that "took the being-looked-at... because the face was not her." Pace — this is the honest, complicated part — I like him. The page gives me no consent worry at all: safeword offered kindly ("Tell me anytime"), check-ins, aftercare, the water left in advance, the Daphne lesson about closed doors. My unease with him is subtler and I think the text *did* earn it: he is so sure of his reading of her. "All he had done was find it, and answer it" — and he's wrong, or at least he's about to be, and he doesn't know it, and there's something in his connoisseurship ("he heard himself want to hear it again") that treats her interior like a craft project he's finishing. I don't distrust his warmth. I distrust his confidence.

Friction: small. The paragraph where she anatomizes the bench made me work to assemble the object in space — I reread it twice. And "years of cheer written into the muscle" bumped against "the long dancer's legs" a page earlier; tiny blip. The title gave nothing away — "The Bench" is flat until the top comes off the table, and then the last paragraph hands it its full weight: the bench "full of what it was... waiting for the next time," a disguised thing in plain sight, which is also exactly what Randi just decided to become. *A Polite Invitation* — this chapter is made of polite invitations: the unlocked front door, "Come with me," "Show me," every door held open. And *With a Long Spoon* — I hear the proverb about supping with the devil in it, and the jacket's closing line, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," now reads like a caption for this exact chapter. Randi's yes was total. That's what broke her open, and that's what she's sealing shut.

What I want next: I want to meet Vee, and I dread it now in a way the jacket alone didn't make me dread it — because I've just watched what being truly *seen* did to Randi, and (guess from here) I suspect whatever game she brings to Vee will be built out of tonight: either handing Vee to Pace to get his gaze off herself, or wanting to watch someone else fail to survive it. I'm turning the page.
