# Cold read (grounded) — The Bench

## Reader reaction

The chapter has arrived inline, so here is my reader reaction.

---

I have to start with the trick the name played on me, because it's the biggest thing that happened to me in this chapter. The jacket gave me Vee — twenty-one, starving, waiting — and so for pages I let myself half-assume the black-haired girl locking the deadbolt behind her was her. Sorority, twenty-one, a body policed by a mother of egg whites, a man who slices the apple thin because he's *noticed* — it all fit the girl I was promised. Then, at the absolute nadir of her, tears pooling on the leather: "There you are, Randi." I actually sat back. This is the glamorous friend. This is the other half of the "game" the jacket says is coming — and I've just watched her get taken apart, before Vee has appeared at all. The jacket said the book would put me ahead of Vee from the start; I didn't expect being ahead to feel like *this*, like being handed something intimate and terrible about the woman who will later be doing the steering.

The scene itself worked on me completely, and I want to be honest about that before anything else. The apple game — bite, kiss, bite, his hands turning back "a little nearer... and each time leaving again before it arrived" — is some of the best slow heat I've read in a long time. The edging on the bench, the anatomy of her *please* — "It was still being said in the voice she said *please* in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water" — that line is doing real erotic and psychological work at once. And the consent architecture isn't decoration: "Tell me your word." "Red." "Tell me anytime." He checks her hands, her shoulders, takes her heels off tenderly mid-scene. I trusted him with her body the entire time, and that trust is exactly what let the heat run as hot as it did. When the real begging came — "lower, broken, almost ugly... the voice of a woman who was not performing because she had run out of the apparatus of performance" — I was fully swept. That was the peak, more than the orgasm itself.

And then the bathroom, which broke my heart in a way I wasn't braced for. Pace thinks he gave her the best night of her life, and in one sense he did — "she had been more alive on that bench than she could remember being anywhere, ever." But the mirror scene is her sealing the crypt: "He could have the face. The face was very good. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." The tragedy is precise: he can hold her *or* see her, and being held *while* seen is the one thing she cannot survive. The flinch at "You're beautiful" — landing "somewhere it was not braced for" — hurt more than any strike in the scene. And the small horror of the bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail, "where it would hurt," which she doesn't know she's doing — the page didn't comment on it and neither did he, and it's the detail I can't shake.

How I feel about them: Pace fascinates and slightly unnerves me. His attentiveness is genuinely erotic — the apple cut-sides-up, the water bottle left in advance, the Daphne lesson about closed doors — but there's an engineer's satisfaction in him I noticed: "the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go," the bench "his own design." He built a device that hides as furniture and let her lay her underwear on it for weeks. He's honest when caught — "you said it was a table. I didn't correct you" — but that's an honesty that operates by omission, and the book let me feel the sliver of cold in it without insisting. Randi I ache for. The face-versus-her split, the appetite "trimmed to almost nothing," the girl who begged her mother in tears and got nothing — she went from the jacket's glamorous accomplice to the most wounded person on the page in one chapter.

Suspicion: the text itself gave me one earned chill, which is her closing vow paired with the jacket. She's decided he gets the face forever — and the jacket tells me a game about another girl begins between these two. My guess from here, marked as a guess: whatever they do to Vee grows out of *this* night — out of what Randi cannot afford to feel and Pace's appetite for finding what's behind faces. The final image did the chilling work: the bench becomes a table again, "full of what it was... waiting for the next time." Friction is minor: the mirror monologue over-explains its thesis once ("because the face was not her, and what was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing" — I'd already understood), and the Daphne paragraph is slightly tidy backstory. Neither broke the spell.

The titles: "The Bench" is perfect in retrospect — an object that is more than it says it is, in a house where a table is not only a table, owned by a man who lets you believe your own descriptions. *A Polite Invitation* — everything here was invited, asked, worded, safeworded; "Show me," she said. And that was still, somehow, the trap. The series title *With a Long Spoon* calls up the old proverb about supping with the devil — I don't know yet who the devil is here, and that's exactly the question this chapter plants. I want the next chapter now; I both want and dread meeting Vee, knowing what I know about the two people waiting for her.
