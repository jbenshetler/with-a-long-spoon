## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Well. That's an opening. I came in expecting Vivienne from the jacket, and instead I got Randi — the game-runner — before the game exists, and I'm glad the book did that, because now I know something the jacket only hinted at: what Randi looks like from the inside, and what Pace can do to a person.

Pace first, because he's the one my body responded to and my head kept side-eyeing. The attentiveness is genuinely seductive — the apple sliced thin, cut sides up, "because he had noticed that she ate them faster when the cut sides showed." That line did two things at once to me: it's tender and it's surveillance. He watches her the way a naturalist watches an animal. The safeword care, "Tell me again if you forget. I won't be upset" — I believed the safety, I felt the warmth. And then: "He had begun building it seven months ago. He had met her three weeks ago." That sentence dropped through me like a stone. The bench predates her. She thinks it was made *for* her — "For you. Tonight." — and that's technically true and deeply not true, and he knows the difference and lets her have the flattering version. That's my first real, text-earned suspicion of him. Also "You said it was a table. I didn't correct you" — the man's honesty runs on a lawyer's clock.

The erotic charge: high, and honest about it. The undressing sequence — folding each garment, the button-by-button silk — worked on me completely; the slow pass of hands turning back "before it arrived" in the kitchen is the hottest thing in the chapter for me, hotter than the bench itself. The bench section runs long and I felt the length, but the length is the point — the edging is done *to the reader* too. Peak: the shift in the *please*. "It was still being said in the voice she said *please* in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water." When the real one arrives, I felt it land. The Brooke gambit — invoking her sorority audience mid-scene — made me flinch; it worked erotically and it felt like a scalpel, and I noticed I didn't fully trust him for having it in his kit.

Then the bathroom mirror section, and the chapter turned into something else — the best thing in it. Randi's face-as-armor logic ("what was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing") is devastating, and her decision — "He could have the face... she was not coming out again" — arriving "quiet as a deadbolt going over" — chilled me. She flinched at "You're beautiful." She pressed the bottle cap under her thumbnail until it hurt. This is a woman in real trouble, and Pace, holding her, thinks it was "a remarkably good evening." That dramatic irony in the final pages is brutal and I loved it: he sees so much and misses the one thing that matters. He found her and thereby lost her, in ninety minutes, and doesn't know it.

The jacket tells me these two will later run a game on Vivienne. Reading this chapter, I now suspect the game may partly be Randi's solution to what happened tonight — a guess from here, nothing more. But "with a long spoon" — sup with the devil, right? — and "every yes was freely given": tonight every yes *was* freely given, and Randi still walked out of that bathroom feeling she'd crossed something she couldn't uncross. The tagline just got its first demonstration, on the schemer herself.

The title "The Bench" — plain, furniture-shop flat, and that flatness is right: the bench pretending to be a table, "full of what it was," is the whole chapter. The closing image — it will be a table again tomorrow, "waiting for the next time" — put a hook in me.

Friction: minor. The eating-disorder material ("her mother lived on egg whites and tofu, a thing she said with a knife in it") is deftly done, though Pace's closing meditation on the apple — "a girl who had been portioning herself for years... All he had done was find it, and answer it" — edged toward self-congratulation that I *think* the book knows is self-congratulation. If it doesn't know, that's a problem; the mirror scene suggests it knows.

Want next: to see how Randi is with him now that the face is back on — and to meet Vivienne, knowing what I now know about who's picking her.
