**Felt read**

I spent most of this chapter half-certain I was watching Vee — the jacket's girl, twenty-one, sorority, starving in the quiet way — and then "There you are, Randi" dropped through me like a trapdoor. I actually stopped and reread it. This is *Randi* — the glamorous friend, the one the jacket says starts the game — and she's the one facedown on the bench, begged open, crying without knowing it. Whatever I thought the shape of this book was, the first chapter just turned it a quarter-rotation: before Randi is anyone's conspirator, she is this. A girl who eats thin apple slices only, whose mother "lived on egg whites and tofu, a thing she had told him with a knife in it," whose face has done all her living for her. I came in braced to distrust her and I left the chapter aching for her instead. That's a real move and it worked on me completely.

Pace. God. I want to be careful here, because the swoon is enormous and I want to name it honestly: the apple sliced thin because she won't eat thick slices, cut sides up because she eats them faster that way; the water bottle left in advance; taking off her heels mid-scene and holding her bare foot a moment; "Tell me your word... Tell me anytime." This is the courtliness the jacket promised, and on the page it is genuinely, weak-kneed romantic — the undressing sequence, him on one knee folding her clothes, is one of the most tender stripteases I've ever read. And the Daphne paragraph — he once went through a door he wasn't invited past and "it had cost him more than he had known, then, that anything could cost" — made his restraint a scar, not a technique. And yet. "He heard himself want to hear it again, and he soothed her and waited and hit her again to hear her make it again." "Building in her the kind of need that could not be denied. Could not be bargained down." He is a man who *produces* outcomes in people, exactly, patiently, and he's proud of the production — "the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go." I don't distrust him yet. But I noticed that he thinks of the evening as something he made, and the jacket has already told me what he and Randi will someday make together. The text earned a hum of watchfulness, not suspicion.

The erotic charge is the hottest sustained thing I've read in a long while. It peaks not at the orgasm but at the *please* — the anatomy of her three pleases, "It was still being said in the voice she said *please* in when she asked a waiter for a glass of water. She did not yet know she was saying *please* like that. She was about to know" — I was holding my breath. The edging, the spanking rhythm, the wet described frankly and warmly — nothing coy, nothing clinical. Every yes on this page is real and freely given, and the chapter is scorching *because* of that, not despite it.

And then the bathroom, which is the best writing in the chapter and the saddest. "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." She was more alive on that bench than she has ever been, it came from being *seen* — and her answer, arriving "quiet as a deadbolt going over," is *not ever. He could have the face.* The flinch at "You're beautiful" broke my heart; the bottle cap pressed slow into her own nail bed frightened me. She locks his front door behind her first thing, every time; she locks the bathroom door for the first time ever; the hum in her ribs is "the size of a key turning in a lock." I don't think she's lying to him yet so much as sealing herself — but the smile she rebuilds in the mirror "was lying. The face was good at lying." The chapter ends with him quietly happy and completely wrong about what just happened, and I know it and he doesn't, and that gap hurts.

Titles: "The Bench" is perfect in retrospect — a table that was never only a table, a purpose hidden under a lid, which is exactly what Randi decides to become in the bathroom: a surface with the top back on. *A Polite Invitation* and the jacket's closing line — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — now read as the book's whole nervous system: this chapter is nothing but freely given yeses, and the problem is already visible. *With a Long Spoon* — I hear the proverb about supping with the devil, and I don't yet know who's the devil at this table. The jacket put me "ahead of her from the start," and yes: I've now met the two planners in bed together before Vee has drawn a single breath on the page.

What I want next: Vee, obviously — but also I dread, as a pure guess from here, that what Randi "does about this" is the plan itself: that rather than be seen again, she'll hand Pace someone else to see. If that's where this goes, this chapter just made it tragic in advance. I'd turn the page right now.
