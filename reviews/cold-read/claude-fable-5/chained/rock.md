# Cold read — Rock

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the chapter I've been waiting for since the flinch in "Two Towels," and it delivered more than I braced for. The porch, the quilt, the cold "on its own side of the glass" — the frame is so tender that when the trapdoors open they open silently. And they open three times: the parents ("My mother stays home. My father works in a chemical plant" — nothing else, "It was not withheld. It was only the size of it"), the hallway ("They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment" — Vee's line, and it's perfect), and then the tutor. That last one flattened me. "She was nineteen." "It went on two years." I did the arithmetic exactly when Vee did, and the book knew I would: "The word for it started to form in her, the clean ugly grown-up word, and she held it down under her tongue." I felt her holding it. And the worst line — the one that reorganizes Pace for me — is "he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck."

Because now the whole architecture rhymes and I can't unhear it. "Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong" — that is almost word-for-word Vee's own "Nobody did anything to me... on purpose" from Off Six-Fourteen. The boy who was seduced at fifteen by the older tutor assigned to him, who experienced it as the best two years of his life, is now the man engineering a seduction of a lonely girl and insisting "I want her to get there on her own." He's not a predator to himself. He's repeating the thing that was done to him and calling it tenderness — and the horror is that on the page it *is* tender. My sympathy for him went up and my fear of him went up in the same breath. That's a rare thing for a book to do to me.

Vee is magnificent here. The unopened saffron ("old enough to vote" — the joke lands and then the phrase curdles when *voting age* comes back in the tutor story, twenty-one, and I don't think that's an accident), her refusing to say sorry twice because sorry would be a demand, her palm flat over "the place the careful voice had come out of." She's a better listener than either of her handlers, and she doesn't know she's being handled.

The scar returns and softens: not moved away this time — pressed *down*, held. "Not tonight" with "no wall in it, only a door with a time on it." Palms together over it. That's the erotic peak of the chapter for me, more than the breast under the quilt at the top, which was lovely but ambient. Heat here is low and glowing; this is an intimacy chapter, not a sex chapter, and it's right that it is.

The math aria — paint you can't cover the outside of, coastlines, Newton's three sentences, "most people walk around inside the poem" — is the most alive he's ever been, and Vee watching him more than listening is exactly how I read it too. One small friction: "It became something else," he said — a dialogue beat that repeats the tag oddly after the previous exchange, a tiny seam. And the correction of my own memory: I'd half-carried "left home at fifteen" as mysterious; now it's fully accounted — SAT at fourteen, college at fifteen, "Less than staying."

The title: **Rock**. Peter means rock — Petros. The chapter hands me his real name and titles itself the translation. It's also the squat bar, the steadiness she names in him ("You're right here"), the ridge holding its black. That's a beautiful title, and it recolors nothing away in advance — I only got it at "My real name is Peter." And Randi's crowd never got this name. Vee has something Randi doesn't. That matters.

*With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — keeps sharpening: now I wonder who the devil is. The tutor? Pace, formed by her? The tagline sits under his "Nobody did anything wrong" like a caption.

What I dread, marked as guess from here: that Pace's model of love — engineered, asymmetric, experienced by the younger party as luck — is exactly what he's building for Vee, and that the reveal of the pointing game will force her to choose between the arithmetic and the luck, as he never did. Also: the scar's "later" is now promised twice. It had better come.
