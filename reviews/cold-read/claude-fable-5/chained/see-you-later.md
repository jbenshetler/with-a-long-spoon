## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the first chapter from inside Vee's head, and it did exactly what I was afraid it would do: it made the seduction feel wonderful from the inside while I sat there knowing the whole thing is choreography. I read the collision scene in the last chapter from Randi's side — the staged stumble, the "flawless accidental" — and now I get Vee's version: "one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it." Neither one did it. Vee, honey. Somebody did it.

The chapter is warm and it hurt. Vee's interiority is the best thing on the page so far — the three minutes of not looking at the door, writing the date "so that her hand would have somewhere to be," the yes arriving "fully formed, ahead of everything" and her folding it down to a socially survivable size. She's starving for exactly what Randi is serving, and the book let me feel how *good* it tastes: "the transcript was blank... she had been keeping the feeling." That line is devastating precisely because it's an honest description of any great first hang with a charismatic person — and also a description of a mark leaving a con with empty pockets and a warm chest.

Cassie is the chapter's conscience and I love her instantly. Four words a day, declines the bit's open door with a flat "Cassie," declines Chi Latte, and then — the chill of the chapter — "Goodbye, Vee." The whole word, "the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." The text earned my shiver there; Cassie clocked something in Randi that Vee's hunger won't let her see, and she said goodbye to the Vee who existed before. And Vee "half forgetting the word had been odd" as she turns back to the better thing — that's the trap of the tagline working in real time.

Randi from the outside is dazzling and I now know too much. "Yes, like the adjective" — the bit with a shape, an engineered opening. The warmth that "swung over and landed" on Cassie, "total, undimmed" — I know from three chapters of her interior that Randi's warmth is a managed instrument, and watching it fail on Cassie was quietly satisfying. And "Love you, girl" from the woman who is scouting Vee for her lover's bed made my stomach drop.

The mother's voice colonizing Vee — "*floozies*," "that's not the kind of attention a nice girl" — and under it the "longer beat of looking than the word accounted for." So Vee has her own policed appetite, her own covered-on-purpose body, her own buried wanting. The jacket said Randi would draw "the shames she was raised to bury" out of her, and here's the shame, laid in one paragraph, cardigan over the better shirt.

One thumb-on-the-scale moment: the professor announcing "*latent variables* — the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can," underlined once in Vee's notebook. It's clever, but it winked at me a little hard. I forgive it because Vee letting the actual math — "the pleasure of the problem opening" — slide by like scenery for Randi's elbow is such a good, sad detail.

Erotic charge: low and diffuse but real — the shoulder against shoulder, the whisper, Vee's long look at the underdressed girls, the "flat helpless want" for Randi. It's pre-erotic, appetite forming, and it simmers correctly.

The title: "See You Later" — Randi's exit line, "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see." That's the whole book in a phrase: Vee reads it as being chosen; I read it as the calendar Pace and Randi keep. And it's set directly against Cassie's "Goodbye" — the two farewells are the chapter's hinge. A Polite Invitation gets more literal every chapter: "you have to come, I'm not asking, I've decided" — and every step is hers anyway.

What I want next: more Cassie, dreadfully. I'm guessing (guess from here) she'll be the one voice warning Vee, and Vee will cost herself that friendship. And I want to know when Pace enters Vee's frame — whether Randi hands her to him or he "happens" to appear.
