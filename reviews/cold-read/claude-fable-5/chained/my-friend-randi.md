# Cold read — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this chapter hurt in the exact way this book has trained me to hurt: everything on the page is warm, and everything I know underneath it is ice. Vee crying the whole drive from Roanoke, lying on the made bed in her plane clothes all day — that opening is bleak and true, and then she goes where she goes "when it got like this," and it's Randi, and my stomach did the thing it always does now when Vee runs toward Randi for shelter.

And here's what's so awful and so good: **Randi's counsel is genuinely excellent.** Every line of the dismantling — "the fear's a liar, and you haven't slept," "How do you know any of that?" / "I've seen her... In my mind," "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one" — is exactly what a wrecked girl needs, delivered better than any real friend I've had could deliver it. She reads Pace *perfectly* ("That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — which I know from the diner chapter is precisely true). She restores Vee's best memory, kills the spite-blonde, hands her back her own wanting. If I knew nothing, this would be the best-friend chapter of the year. But I know Randi is in love with her and hiding it, I know Randi and Pace started this as a game, and I know Randi *doesn't know about the fight* — she's learning of the December walkout right here, in real time, and steering it back together without once flinching. Watch her work. It's dazzling and it's dreadful.

The peak of the dread, for me, is small: the shoes. "She crossed to the closet and came back with a pair of black heels hooked on two fingers... *Randi always knew the shoes.*" Randi dressing Vee for Pace's door. The coat plan itself — "nothing under it" — Randi doesn't even say it; she stops at "You've got that good long coat" and *waits for Vee to find it*, which is the pointing game in miniature, the yes manufactured so it arrives as Vee's own. "Vee felt it settle over her like a thing being decided for her and by her at once" — that sentence is the whole cover tagline in one clause, and I felt it land.

The shame beat worked on me erotically more than anything explicit has in a while: making Vee *say* "It's such a slutty thing to do," the word costing her, and then "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's this book's signature move — shame lit, then handed back as a gift — and here I watched Randi do it deliberately, the jacket copy performed live.

And then the fairytale retold — and Randi *lies about the one true thing*. From her own chapter I remember John left her feeling nothing; here she stops at "we went up," looks down at her hands, and Vee "read the quiet as discretion." Then the cover story: "perfect everywhere but the bedroom." That's... almost the truth, told as a joke, and Vee laughs. But the real quiet — the looking down at her hands — I think that was Randi touching the actual hollow of it, in front of the person the hollow is about, and swerving. That moment moved me toward pity for Randi even inside the dread. Also Vee's aside gutted me: John's easy naming of imaginary children is "the exact thing she didn't have... a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it." The fairytale was built to comfort and it wounds every time.

One revelation I have to log: the spite-blonde has been **Sheri** all along — "this girl, Sheri, she was alone." Vee knew the name (there was a Sheri chapter way back) and built the blonde model on it. And I've *met* Sheri — five feet, permed two-tone hair, wants nothing from Pace, the kindest read in the book. Randi demolishes the fantasy without ever knowing how right she is. Delicious irony, cleanly earned.

Friction: almost none. Maybe "raccoon who lost a fight" is a hair broad, but it earned Vee's first laugh, so I'll allow it. Brooke's walk-through warmth ("She always seemed to be passing through") is a nice texture beat.

The title: "My Friend Randi" — it's Vee's phrase, the possessive of trust, and it's a knife. This is exactly what Vee would call this chapter, and exactly what I can't. And *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — has never been more literal: the devil just talked her down, dried her tears, told her the truth about her fear, and put heels in her hands.

What I expect next (guess from here): the coat scene. Vee at Pace's door, bare under the long coat, in Randi's heels. I think he opens it and I think it works — the jacket promises full heat — and I dread that the reunion will be engineered-real, both at once, like everything else. I also can't stop wondering whether Randi's heels will ever be *recognized* by Pace at the door. I'm turning the page fast.
