## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this chapter is doing something to me and I'm not entirely comfortable with how much of it is working. The opening in bed is genuinely hot — the mid-orgasm question ("Would you like to taste another woman?") landing in her body before her mind, making her come "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — that's the erotic peak of the book so far, and it's peak precisely because it's also the most disturbing thing Pace has done. He explicitly reasons: "A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was, by some old architecture, a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there." Reader, I shivered. After the bathroom chapter and "no door," here he is — the man who can't leave a closed door alone. He doesn't know she re-sealed herself. He thinks he's giving gifts. He *frames* everything as gift: "what he was bringing her was good... She deserved it, and he wanted to be the one who gave it to her." I half believe him. That's the problem with him. His care is real and it's also a crowbar.

Randi broke my heart again, in miniature: "Only in a threesome. With you." The narration spells out exactly what she did — "The same act. A different owner. She could live inside the second version. She could not live inside the first." That's the one place I felt the author's thumb: the paragraph explains her maneuver so thoroughly ("which was unsurvivable... which was merely sexy") that I wasn't allowed to work it out myself. It's a beautiful insight slightly over-served.

The pointing game itself — I read the whole quad sequence grinning and uneasy at once. "I point. You pick" is genuinely charming as a couple's game, and the parade of rejected types is sharp social comedy (the matched pair, "she'd be a project," the room-runner: "God, no"). And I noticed what Pace noticed: Randi's taste is *not* her own grammar. She wants the opposite of herself. Then Vivienne — because that has to be Vivienne, "Her name is Vee," the jacket finally walking onto the page — arrives laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Gorgeous entrance. Randi's bare "Yes," before the costume comes back up, is the chapter's emotional peak for me.

And then the engineered collision. This is where my stomach turned properly. Watching Randi deploy "a decade of dance" to stage an accident so clean "the redhead had walked into *her*" — I felt the jacket's trap click shut in real time. "They told her nothing." I just watched the nothing begin. Vee's hand flying to her own mouth, apologizing for a collision that was done *to* her — that's the whole book in one image, I suspect. Also: "I'm hungry" / "I can't eat that" walking past the cheeseburger, then the closing line "Let's go feed you" — the eating thread is being played very deliberately and it lands cold. He's going to feed her. With what?

The title gave nothing away going in — "The Pointing Game" sounded whimsical — and now it's exact: he points, she picks, and the game's cheer is the wrapper on an acquisition. *A Polite Invitation* now looks like it names what just happened on that lawn: nothing was invited yet, but the mechanism of polite entrapment just switched on.

What I dread, marked as guess: Vee is warm and unguarded (the beanie-girl "maybe" taught him the type — open, takes up room), and these two hyper-controlled people are about to close on her like a pincer. The blurb says both fall for real. I believe it already, faintly, in how long Randi couldn't look away.
