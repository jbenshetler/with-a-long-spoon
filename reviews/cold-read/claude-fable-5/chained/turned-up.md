# Cold read — Turned Up

*scene: scenes/turned-up.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: a-round*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the exhale chapter, and I needed it. After the held breath of the fitting, watching Vee come home and *tell* it — the book has trained me to love these retell chapters (this is the fourth one), and this is the best of them, because for once I got to watch Vee discover her own chapter alongside Cassie. The shirt reveal landing on Cassie first — "He *sews*," set down like a fact, then "Vee. The shirt." — was genuinely delightful; Cassie connecting the no-tag shirt before Vee volunteers it is exactly the honest-sensor work I trust her for.

But the line that got me, and it got me in a complicated way, is the heat: "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold." Cassie goes very still, her face goes soft — "Oh, Vee" — and I felt the softness too, and *at the same time* my stomach dropped, because I know from chapter one that the too-warm house is the standing condition of Randi's nakedness. It predates Vee. She's building her tenderest proof of being thought-of out of a thermostat that was never set for her — and Cassie, working with only Vee's data, ratifies it. That's the cruelest thing this book does: every wrong conclusion is reasoned honestly from true observations. Nobody lies to her on the page and she ends up wrong anyway.

The comedy works. "I was on a box" as a repeated defense made me laugh out loud, and the "Are you sure he's not gay?" bit is exactly the friend-logic a real Cassie would run — and then Vee's answer, patting her own ass "brisk and clinical, like she was indicating it on a diagram," is the funniest beat in the book so far. And then the reversal I didn't see coming even though I *watched* the fitting: "*I* was grinding on *him.*" She's reframed the breast press — the thing she did once, deliberately, and apologized for — into a boast. The stoop that came undone last chapter is staying undone. She's proud. She's *wicked*. Watching Vee narrate herself as the aggressor, hands over her mouth, rocking with the pillow — that's the girl from "Nobody did anything to me... on purpose" learning to say *I did something to him, on purpose*, and it moved me more than the joke.

Erotic charge: secondhand but real — retold heat, warmed rather than hot. "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" and the on-his-knees geometry, "Let the geometry say itself," carry a low hum. Peak is emotional, not sexual: "Like I was supposed to be there."

Friction: almost none. Maybe "Cassie said nothing, which was the right thing" edges toward telling me how good a listener Cassie is, but it's in Vee's voice and I'll allow it. No suspicion of Cassie — she's the one person in this book with no game running.

**The titles.** "Turned Up" — the heat turned up before she arrived, and Vee turned up at home lit like a lamp, and the volume of her own wanting turned up. It points straight at the chapter's most double-edged line, and only I know it's double-edged. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — keeps darkening: the warmth Vee is feeding on was set for someone else, and Cassie just co-signed the misreading. "Every yes was freely given" — this whole chapter is Vee cataloguing the freely-given yeses with joy.

What I want next: Randi. Six chapters absent. Vee is going to give *this* debrief to someone, and it won't only be Cassie — and my dread is Randi hearing the thermostat story and knowing exactly whose warmth it was. Guess from here: the dress-ready phone call is the next hinge, and the finished dress or the induction is where Randi steps back in.
