# Cold read — Off Six-Fourteen

## Reader reaction

This is a small chapter and it gutted me anyway. The whole thing is two girls in a dorm room, and the line that did it is the one Vee is proudest of: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That's the cover tagline walking around on the page in a girl's mouth. Every word of it is true — and I know about the pointing game, the staged collision, the whole architecture, so hearing her offer her own agency as the proof of the thing's cleanness was almost unbearable. She's right. That's the trap. The book didn't wink at me once; it just let her say it, glowing, and let me carry the rest.

Cassie earned her keep again. Her "flat dry floor" under the gladness — "How much did he give you to drink?" — is exactly the right question asked of exactly the wrong evening. He engineered it so the honest answer exonerates him: tea, no wine, no pressure, her kiss. Cassie runs every reasonable safety check a friend should run and every one comes back green. Then the state-troopers joke, which is funny until Vee "counts it off like a dossier, like plenty" — and the dossier is *nothing*. "Off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." Cassie's deadpan "That's the whole address" is the chapter title, and it landed on me colder than the window. The one person built to catch this is being handed a scent trail with nothing at the end of it — and she caps the highlighter and says "Borrow good ones." My last skeptic just signed the permission slip, on evidence that was all true.

The hiking invitation is the plot moving under the warmth: she's being drawn out west of town, past where it goes to gravel, toward the isolated house I've known about since chapter one. "He'll have water, he's not going to walk me off a cliff" — I flinched. And "I know where he lives and everything," when she knows a road number. The spin in the too-small room was lovely — clumsy, over before it was a dance, exactly her — and the bean-bag collapse, and Cassie's "I'm looking" answering "Look at me," which is this book's chosen-and-seen ache in four words. Erotic charge is near zero here and correctly so; this is the afterglow chapter, the joy chapter, and the joy is real, which is the horror.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the "rich people hike, poor people walk" bit is a beat of Cassie-shtick, but I laughed, so I can't complain. The closing image — the cold thin line over both of them, and Vee "warm and did not feel it" — is the author's thumb, lightly, but it's earned: she has stopped feeling the cold coming in.

Title, before and after: "Off Six-Fourteen" meant nothing going in; now it's the non-address, the whole of what anyone will know about where she went. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — keeps tightening; the spoon is getting shorter, and next it apparently goes to gravel.

What I want next: Randi. Three chapters absent now; the kiss and the hike have both happened without her on the page, and I'm starting to suspect (guess from here) that her half of "falling as hard" is curdling into something the plan didn't budget for. What I dread: the hike, the house, and the moment Vee's "I did it on purpose" gets tested against what she wasn't told.
