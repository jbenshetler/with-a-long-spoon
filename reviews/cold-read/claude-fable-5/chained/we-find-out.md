# Cold read — We Find Out

*scene: scenes/we-find-out.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: practice-room*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is the chapter the whole book has been walking toward, and my body knew it before my head did — the moment his hand came down "once, open, across the seat of her underwear" and she went still, I actually said *there it is* a half-beat before he did. And then he said it. "There it is." That line is doing double duty and it went through me like ice water in the middle of a hot scene, because that's the sister of "There you are" — Randi to Vee, Pace to Randi on the bench in chapter one — and it landed here like a technician confirming a signal. The kitchen spank in "A Recognized Method," the counting story in "The Practice Room," and now this. Three chapters, one funnel. And Vee even *feels* the story working on her: "Randi's story rose up in her like something she'd swallowed: would she have to count out loud?" Reader, I wanted to reach into the page. The story was placed there. She braced against theatre and got tenderness instead — "What came was nothing like the story" — and that swerve is the most sinister-beautiful thing this book has done, because the gentleness reads as proof he's *not* the story, when the gentleness is the method.

And yet. My honest, uncomfortable report: it's gorgeous and it worked on me. The bet is coercion dressed as play — "Either you admit you lost, or we find out," his hand already on her knee, "slow enough that she could have stepped out of it the whole way and didn't" — and I felt the heat of it anyway. The finger held up in the lamplight, "the shameful plenty of the wet... held up now in the soft light where they could both see exactly how much of it there was," and then his face reading it back to her: "Like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." That's the tagline in one image. Every yes freely given. Her body giving the yes her mouth kept refusing — "she could not have said which one of them she was lying to." Peak heat of the whole book for me, and peak dread, in the same paragraph, which I think is exactly what this novel is.

New data I didn't expect: *he flinched.* She hits his thigh and "she had just watched it flinch" — the wall showed a seam. And she's faster than him, and she flashed him, "She had never in her life done such a thing." I loved her in this chapter — playful, bratty, alive, the girl the floozy-voice never let exist. Which makes the funnel worse. The aftercare wrecked me: the juice, "wonderful, wonderful," the scrunchies in "the plenty of them" saying he means to stay — except this man ordered scrunchies in bulk, which is also *planning*, and I can no longer read his tenderness without seeing inventory. She touches the scar again and "didn't ask now either... the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep." She's stopped asking questions. That's the trap's quietest click.

The title — "We Find Out" — is his line verbatim, the bet's ultimatum, and it recolors as I sit with it: *we* find out, plural, and the "we" who set out to find out what Vee is includes a woman not in the room. What was found out will be reported. "No Randi waiting to be told" — except there is, and Vee knows it enough to name it.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the curry coda runs a touch long after that bedroom, though the domestic landing is the point. "Victorian LARPing" was a delight — her voice getting funnier as she gets freer.

What I dread, marked as guess: this gets retold to Randi — possibly by *him* this time — and the properly-equipped version, the bench, is next. The bathroom monologue ("I'm going to start bringing a bag") says she's moving in emotionally. The fall will be from height.
