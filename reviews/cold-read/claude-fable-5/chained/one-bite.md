## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this chapter is cruel in the softest way, and I mean that as a compliment to my own pulse. The retelling I've been waiting for since "We Find Out" happens — and it's Vee doing it, eagerly, in full sun, and the extraction machinery I've watched Randi run since "Fed" barely has to work anymore. Look at the trigger: "I had him filed under spicy. And this is all very... Vanilla." One goad and Vee spends everything — "He spanked me. It was out before Vee decided to spend it." That "decided to spend it" is exactly the money-language this girl thinks in, and it broke my heart a little: she's paying for intimacy with her own privacy and doesn't know the exchange rate is rigged.

The erotic charge here is genuinely high and it's all secondhand-firsthand — Vee re-inhabiting the spanking as she narrates it, "the print of his hand arrive again where the seat met her," her body "kept faith with every word." Telling as a second act of sex. And then Randi's Radford story — which I flatly do not believe is true. The "young Robert Redford" who conveniently leaves, the stranger's bed, "knees wide, and me singing the whole song of self-pleasure" — it's too perfectly aimed. It lands the exact image needed to push Vee over, at the exact moment. After the counting story in "The Practice Room" did the same job, this reads to me as a planted munition, and the text earned that suspicion: Randi tells it right when Vee is visibly at the top, and then — "Do what you need to," said with "no curiosity anywhere in it, as if she had said nothing at all." No curiosity. Randi is *always* curious. That absence is the loudest thing in the chapter.

The bathroom scene wrecked me. In "The Practice Room" Vee refused to touch herself over Randi's story — "would not say yes to it." Here she gets further: hand on herself, "barely breached," one inch off — and Randi's face arrives behind her eyes and *stops* her. "She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was, and the two would not hold in one body." That's the most psychologically precise sentence in the book so far. Vee's shame witness has changed. It used to be her mother's floozy voice; now it's Randi's fond face. That's a takeover, and Vee thinks it's love.

Then "Sticky fingers?" — I gasped, honestly, right along with Vee. And the resolution — pastries — is perfect and merciless: "she could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." That's the whole book in a sentence.

And the last image. The kiss, "See you soon, gorgeous," and then — glaze on Vee's lip, "the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." Randi took her bite, closed the subject, and left the sugar on Vee's mouth. I sat with that for a good minute. Randi is *feeding* her, marking her, and Vee tastes it after the car is gone. Whether Randi tasted anything of Vee in return I refuse to think about too directly, and that refusal is doing something to me.

Friction: almost none. The title is flat-footed — "Vee Tells Randi About We Find Out" is a log line, not a title, and it told me exactly what the chapter was before I read it. After thirty-one chapters of titles that recolor on landing ("The Practice Room," "We Find Out"), this one gave the game away and gave nothing back. It's my one real complaint.

*With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, yes, and here's Vee licking the devil's sugar off her own lip. The tagline gets sharper every chapter: Vee's yes in that stall was freely withheld, even — and the withholding itself was engineered.

Guesses from here: the Radford story was fabricated (or a Pace night wearing a costume), Randi will retell all of this to Pace, and the "dark corners" language plus the kiss are the threesome on-ramp warming.
