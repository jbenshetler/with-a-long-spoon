## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this one hurt in the good way. I've been waiting for Pace to walk into Vee's frame since the collision, and the book made me watch it happen from inside the one head that doesn't know it's happening. The bell over the door tinkled and I knew before Vee did — "Vee did not look; people came in and out of the coffee shop all afternoon" — and that gap between what I know and what she knows is the whole engine of the chapter. Cassie's "That cute guy in line is looking at you" landed on me like a trap springing softly.

And here's what unsettles me most: everything Pace does is genuinely *good*. The tutoring sequence is the best thing in the chapter — reading the problem twice, asking what her professor said instead of showing off, the margin note not a rewrite, and above all "He had given her the question and given her back the pencil." If I didn't know this man had planned this room, this would be the most swoon-worthy meet-cute I've read in years. Because I do know, every kindness reads double. "He had kept himself out of it so the solving stayed hers" — that's exactly what he does in bed with Randi, and Vee is reading his technique correctly and drawing the wrong conclusion from it: that it's spontaneous. Her econ eye — "how a smart person handles a problem they could handle in any of several ways" — is *so close* to the truth and files it as charm.

The devastating detail is Randi's cup. Lifted "without a new sip happening," held both hands "a second longer than the sip required," and Vee's brain "helpfully provided the explanation *she's bored*." Randi's jealousy — or something — is leaking through her hands while her face runs "go on, this is nice," and only Cassie "watched the cup without watching it." Cassie again. Cassie sees everything: "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." That line stopped me cold, because it's the one true forensic observation in the room — a man meeting a stranger doesn't look at a beautiful woman like Randi like a great-aunt *unless he already knows her and is overcorrecting*. Cassie has, without knowing it, put her finger directly on the evidence. And Randi immediately smothers it under the coffee-he-forgot bit — which is itself staged, of course he didn't want coffee, he came for Vee — and I watched Randi convert Cassie's near-miss into more seduction. That's the scariest beat in the chapter.

Vee borrowing Randi's name-bit — "V.T. Yes, like the university... Yes, they find it funnier than I do" — made me wince with tenderness. She's already wearing Randi's clothes, socially. And then "Vee," quickly, "suddenly wanting her own name back" — she feels the borrowed skin and steps out of it. That little recoil is why I love her. (Also: the jacket's "Vivienne Thorne" finally lands on the page, and it lands as a *performance*.)

Erotic charge: low-explicit, high-simmer, and it works. The forearms, the cuffs rolled "two easy turns," "what he did with his hands besides math," "may I call you" hitting her body before her mouth. This is chapter four of foreplay and I'm fully in it.

Friction: almost none. If anything, Vee's long paragraph decoding Pace's tutoring method ("The way he had... The way he had...") runs one beat past its welcome — but the interruption ("The thought did not finish") saves it, twice, and the not-finishing is doing character work. Randi's Bessel's-correction question is a lovely piece of stagecraft — feeding him a one-sentence layup so he registers as helpful-to-everyone — though I can't prove it was scripted, and that uncertainty is the point.

The title: "Substitution" is perfect and multiple. The calculus move — turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know. But the whole *chapter* is a substitution: Pace substituted into Vee's afternoon as a stranger; Vee substituting Randi's bit for her own voice; and — from the pointing game — Vee being substituted into a bed she doesn't know exists. "Turn it into something you already know how to do" is Pace's whole method with women, and Vee wrote it in her own margin. The tagline keeps tightening: her "Yes. Please. Thank you," her "Yes" to *may I call you* — every one freely given, every one engineered upstream.

What I dread: that Cassie is going to keep being right and keep being managed. What I want: the first phone call, Vee-POV, and to see whether Randi's cup-hands mean what I think they mean — that this is already realer for her than the game allowed for. *Guess from here:* the plan's first crack is Randi's jealousy, not Vee's discovery.
