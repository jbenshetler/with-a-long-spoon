## Reader reaction

I spent this whole chapter in a state I can only call split-screen. On one side of the glass: the most charming meet-cute the book has given anyone — a man crossing a coffee shop with his eyes only on her, the rolled cuffs, the margin note instead of the rewrite, the pencil handed back "at the exact line where she could solve it herself." On the other side: I know. I was there for the Pointing Game. I watched Randi engineer a shoulder-collision so clean it looked like the other girl's fault, and now I watch her suggest "the place on Wilson" — the *exact café she named in her alibi to Brooke* — and text under the table, and I understand that the bell over the door didn't tinkle by accident. Vee is the only person at that table who thinks this is a Tuesday. Reading her fall for it felt like watching someone be handed a beautiful gift with a wire running out the back of it, and the terrible thing — the thing that kept me leaning in — is that the gift is *also real*. The math help is honest. The listening is honest. "May I call you?" made even me sit up. That's what's so destabilizing: I couldn't find the fake part, only the withheld part.

Randi undid me here in small ways. "Randi," said Randi — one bare word, deliberately not doing her name-bit, because the bit is for people she's charming and Pace is not someone she is meeting. And then Vee *does* the bit — "V.T. Yes, like the university" — wearing Randi's borrowed poise to impress Randi's boyfriend, and neither the borrower nor the lender says a word. That's the chapter's cruelest, most delicate moment for me, and nobody on the page flinches. And Randi's hands: the cup that comes up to her mouth twice "and no sip happened," the both-hands hold "a second longer than the sip required" after he answers her stats question flatly, like a stranger would. Vee reads boredom. I read a woman running hot and holding still — watching the man who put her on the bench turn that attention on the girl *she chose*, playing great-aunt while it happens. Whether that's arousal, jealousy, or both braided together, the page doesn't say, and I'm not going to say either. But it's not boredom.

Cassie. Oh, Cassie. "That cute guy in line is looking at you" — she's the one who points Vee toward him, unknowing, the honest instrument of the whole setup. And then she's the only one who *names* the geometry out loud: "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She's describing the performance of strangerhood with perfect accuracy and no idea it's a performance. After her flat "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter — the whole word, "the word for a longer parting" — I'm starting to trust Cassie's instruments more than anyone's in this book, and it aches that she can read the look and not the lie.

Pace himself: my feeling genuinely moved. Through the bench chapter I trusted him — the water bottle, the closed door he wouldn't knock on, the care that felt like care. Here, everything I admired is still present and now it's *deployed*. Keeping himself out of the solving so it stays hers — that was exactly his method with Randi on the bench, the waiting, the not-directing, and watching the same craftsmanship aimed at a woman who doesn't know there's a craftsman is where my stomach tips. The page hasn't shown him do one unkind thing. That's the point, I think, and it's working on me as low continuous dread under real charm.

The heat is quiet but present — Vee's eye caught on his forearms "a half-second past where she would have chosen," her unfinished "what he did with his hands besides math," her chest taking the stairs too fast, and Randi's sipless cup humming beside it all. Nothing peaks; everything hums. And the title did something to me: "Substitution" is the calculus move — turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do — and then it kept going. Vee substituting Randi's bit for her own voice. Randi and Pace substituting "strangers" for what they are. Maybe Vee being substituted *into* something whose shape I can't see yet. The book title finally clicked for me too — *With a Long Spoon*, as in supping with the devil, and *A Polite Invitation* is exactly what "May I call you?" was. The politeness is the invitation. That reframe went cold down my back.

One small friction: Vee's paragraph anatomizing Pace's tutoring method ("the path of a person who wasn't performing… who had kept himself out of it") runs a beat longer than her dazzlement needs — the author making sure I clocked the craft. Her "econ eye" mostly earns it, but I felt the thumb. What I want next is almost unbearable in both directions: I want the phone to light up for her, because her wanting is so open and so earned — and I dread the moment she learns Randi and Pace lock each other's doors behind them. Guess from here: the "later" Randi promised and the call Pace promised are the same event, and Vee will be the last to know it.
