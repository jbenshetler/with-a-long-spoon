# Cold read (grounded) — Substitution

## Reader reaction

So this is the meet-cute the jacket promised — "steers her, lightly, laughing, as if by accident, into the path of a young mathematician" — and I got to watch it happen knowing exactly what it was, and the terrible thing is it still worked on me. That's the book's whole wager, isn't it — "lets the reader, ahead of her from the start, fall for it exactly as she does" — and this chapter is the proof of concept. I knew Randi's texting ("Some boy, Vee thought, amused") was almost certainly the summons. I knew Randi suggested "the place on Wilson" — the very café she used as her alibi to Brooke in "Standards," which landed on me like a small cold coin: she already had this place in her pocket. And I still felt Vee's chest do the stairs-too-fast thing right along with her when Pace handed the pencil back.

Because the pencil is the swoon. Not the forearms (though — "They were not a math tutor's forearms" — noted, Vee, noted). The seduction here is pedagogical, and it is devastating precisely because Vee is smart enough to see it and name it: "He had given her the question and given her back the pencil." He performs not-performing — reads the problem like he's never seen it, writes the ugly formula only to dismiss it ("Nobody should have to carry that around"), leaves a margin note instead of a rewrite. Every move engineered so that "the solving stayed hers." And here's where the chapters rhyme in a way that made my skin prickle: this is exactly the bench. On the bench he built Randi's begging so it would be hers; here he builds Vee's solution so it will be hers. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — the jacket line keeps getting more precise and more sinister. He gives people themselves, and that's the hook that goes deepest.

How I feel about everyone right now. Vee I am fully inside of and rooting for, which is uncomfortable, because I know the room is rigged. Her borrowing Randi's name-bit — "V.T. Yes, like the university" — broke my heart a little: she's already assembling herself out of Randi-parts, and then "she added, quickly, suddenly wanting her own name back" — that flicker of self-rescue is the most hopeful thing on the page. Her econ answer is the chapter's quiet dagger, and I don't think she hears herself: "the gap between what they say they're doing and what they're doing... if you understood it well, you'd understand a lot." Oh, honey. You are the data set.

Pace — my feeling has genuinely moved. After "The Bench" I found him almost unbearably attentive, and the tenderness read true. Here, watching the identical attentiveness deployed on a stranger as an opening move, cold from a standing start ("Pardon me. It sounds like you're having trouble with math"), the same behaviors read as technique. The "may I call you" that Vee hears as a word from her mother's vocabulary — I heard it as the lights-up speech from the first night with Randi: courtliness as method. I don't think he's lying, exactly, and that's what unsettles me. The page doesn't let me decide whether the warmth is real or is the tool. I'm leaving that ajar because the book does.

Randi is the one I can't stop watching, and the chapter knows it — it keeps cutting to her hands. The cup that comes up to her mouth twice "and no sip happened." The both-hands hold "a second longer than the sip required." And the bare "Randi," one word, no adjective bit — the exact flat name-drop she deployed nothing like when she met Cassie. She's performing minimally for Pace because Pace doesn't need the performance; he's already had all of her, including the part she locked away. But what is the cup-business? Jealousy watching him do to Vee what he did to her? Arousal at the game going live? The nail-bed press from "The Bench" was self-harm-adjacent; this fidgeting sits in the same family and the page won't name it, so neither will I. Then her closing sales pitch — "A man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call" — delivered with such warm sisterly authority that I felt the manipulation and the genuine generosity in the same sentence and couldn't peel them apart.

Cassie. Cassie is becoming my dread and my favorite. "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter; now "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" — she's the only person at the table doing accurate instrumentation, and Vee's narration even hands us the ache underneath: "It was the one that slid off Cassie every day of her life, and Cassie had named it without a flicker." Cassie watching Randi's cup "without watching it." She sees. She says four words a day and every one of them is a reading.

Erotic charge: this chapter runs on a low current rather than a burn — deliberately, I think, after "The Bench" nearly took the paint off the walls. The heat is all in Vee's small slippages: the eye that stays on his forearms "a half-second past where she would have chosen," the wondering "what he did with his hands besides math" (reader, I know what he does with his hands, and the dramatic irony there is genuinely hot in a way I feel slightly implicated by). It simmers; it never means to boil.

Friction: almost none. The one thumb-on-scale moment for me is the professor's line from "See You Later" still ringing — "latent variables... the factors you can't measure directly" — and now this chapter's econ-trick "turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do" doing double duty so neatly that I can feel the authorial pleasure. It stays just this side of cute because Vee's math genuinely needs it. And Pace's "More or less" about finishing the PhD, plus Vee noticing he "seemed young to be finishing" and setting the thought down — twice now the book has flagged something soft-edged about his credentials/age, and I'm holding it as a live question, not a verdict.

The title: "Substitution" is doing shameless, gorgeous triple duty. The calculus move itself — turn what you have into what you already know. Pace substituting himself into Vee's day as "a stranger." And the one I can't unsee: Vee being substituted into the space Randi vacated in the bathroom mirror — Randi sent the face back out and is now furnishing him a whole new girl to be found the way she couldn't bear to stay found. Euler's identity as the mechanism — "two are a struggle... put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain" — is practically a diagram of the three of them, and I refuse to decide yet who the two struggling terms are. *A Polite Invitation* — the "may I" IS the polite invitation; the whole chapter is one. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, and the spoons were literally ringing in Chi Latte last chapter. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." Vee's "Yes. Please. Thank you." Freely given. That's the problem.

What I want next: the phone to light up, and I hate that I want it. Guesses from here: the "lunches too intimate to name" with Randi begin soon; Cassie keeps registering true and gets ignored; and at some point Vee learns Randi already knows what Pace's hands do, and I dread that page more than any other.
