## Reader reaction

The deadbolt again — first sound of the chapter, and it did something to me it didn't do in "The Bench," because since then I've heard that lock from the inside: *quiet as a deadbolt going over*, her decision in the mirror that he gets the face and nothing else, not ever. So when she comes through the door with no hello in it and her mouth already on his, I read it two ways at once and couldn't stop. The heat is real — she's been holding that kiss the whole walk over, and I felt it land. And also: this is the face's finest work, isn't it? Or is it? The chapter won't tell me, and I genuinely don't know, and that not-knowing is the most alive thing on the page.

"Tell me." God, that's good. She came to his house hungry to hear about his date with the girl *she* picked, and she straddles him and feeds him wine out of her own mouth to get the story out of him — and when he gives her "she built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop," he feels her answer in her thighs before her face admits anything. That detail wrecked me a little, because it's Vee's appetite that arouses her — Vee eating without her mother's voice, without the apple-slice arithmetic Randi lives inside. She wants the girl who can *want*. The charge in this chapter runs entirely on that, and it's hot in a way that also aches.

Then the turn, and it's the best beat here: "She's easy to listen to," and Randi goes quiet under his hands — thighs not tightening but *setting*, the smile "put on a beat after it was called for." Pace reads it as wanting held, more want than the face can carry, and he's *moved* by it. But I've been in the bathroom with her. I've watched the face manufacture exactly this kind of lateness and lie beautifully afterward. Is he right — is it desire too big for her? Or did something in "she's easy to listen to" land somewhere else — that he *listens* to Vee, gives Vee the thing that undid Randi on the bench? The page holds it shut and I'm holding it shut too. What I'll say is: Pace's total confidence in his reading — "he read it as he read everything on her" — chills me a degree now, because I know one enormous thing he has misread completely. He thinks he found her. She decided he never would again.

The grace note I want to mark: he keeps Vee's confession. "It was hers. He did not hand it over." After a chapter of two people consuming a third girl in absentia — and my low-grade discomfort about that is real; Vee is a story being drunk with wine, and she doesn't know the collision was choreographed — that one withholding is the decent thing on the page, and it mattered to me that he did it. Though even that cuts both ways: he's keeping a private channel with Vee that Randi can't see, in the same minute Randi keeps hers.

The last line is the whole chapter: "He had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else." He takes the wine only through her mouth; she takes the date only through his. Everyone in this book is tasting everything secondhand now, and the title names it — tannin, the dry catch of the wine, poured an hour early and *left to open*. That's the want itself: it's been left alone to open, "he had done nothing to grow it but leave it alone." As a title it recolored the chapter after I finished — astringent, mouth-drying, the thing that lingers. The cover board — *With a Long Spoon*, *A Polite Invitation* — is starting to hum for me. He that sups with the devil should have a long spoon, and every invitation in this book has been immaculately polite: *may I call you*, *go meet her*, *tell me*. I still can't tell who's supping with whom, and I notice I no longer assume it's Pace holding the spoon.

Do I want to keep reading? Badly. What I dread, as a guess from here: Vee, warm and unarmored and grateful for a right pen, walking into whatever this couple is building — and I don't yet know whether it will be a gift or a consumption, because the two people building it don't know either.
