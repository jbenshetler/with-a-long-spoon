# Cold read — Dear

*scene: scenes/dear.md · model: claude-fable-5 · read after: off-six-fourteen*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Randi's back, and the first thing she does is put a knife in me that Vee can't see: "How's it going with the, the hottie. What is his name." Randi snapped her fingers twice. "Paul?" — She's his *lover*. She's been in his bed for six weeks. Watching her pretend to fish for "Pace" while Vee, glowing, hands over the entire dinner — "May I choose?", the kiss, "Because of him" — is the queasiest thing this book has done to me since "He wants to see you naked." Every earlier chapter of Randi-charm I could half-enjoy; this one I read through my fingers, because now Vee is delivering the debrief I've been dreading for three chapters *directly to the person who ordered it*, and she thinks it's the best afternoon of her year. And Randi is so good at it. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it" — she's holding Vee's submission up to the light, cataloguing it, and Vee experiences the cataloguing as being *heard*. That's the grandfather-shaped wound being worked with a jeweler's tools.

The Randi "catastrophe" story — the lacrosse date who wouldn't listen — landed on me two ways at once. As comedy it's genuinely funny ("I could have sewn my own mouth shut"). As machinery it's exquisitely dishonest: "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else" is a fake dating life deployed as a frame to make Pace shine brighter. And her Friday — I know where her Fridays go. The gravel drive. The lie has a body under it.

The Cassie beat at the top hurt more than anything at the Smoothie Bird. Cassie's look going past Vee to Randi and being "folded away" — and then Vee, unprompted, gatekeeping: "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one... Not her thing." Vee excluding *Cassie*, the one honest person, and rationalizing it — "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That line is the chapter's true cold thin line off the window. The sensor is being routed around, and Vee is doing the routing herself.

Brooke. So Brooke *is* real trouble — she walks in with an older, silent, coffee-drinking woman whose eyes do "a slow, easy circuit... of Vee," "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over." Who is that woman? Alumna? Advisor? She's new, unnamed, and she read as genuinely dangerous — appraisal without appetite. And "We missed you Thursday" is Brooke doing exactly what she did in the Standards chapter: laying a fact down warmly and watching what it does. Where was Randi Thursday? I can guess.

Then the beat the chapter is named for: "Vee's my dear friend," the cool damp hand closing over hers in public. And here's my problem as a reader — I *felt* it. I felt Vee's flood of being chosen out loud, "no one had chosen her out loud like that in a long time," and I believed Randi meant something by it, maybe even something real (the jacket did promise Randi falls too). But I also watched Vee construct a whole economy around it — "it had cost something... she had spent it on her without appearing to count it" — with zero evidence of the cost. Vee is doing the accounting *for* Randi, awarding her generosity on credit. That's the trap in miniature: the yes freely given, the price invisibly imagined by the one paying it.

Erotic charge: essentially none in the physical sense, but the Randi-Vee current — hand on hand, "You little minx," the hug, "Love you, girl" — hums at seduction frequency. This is courtship. The jacket told me Vee is meant for their *bed*, and this chapter reads differently with that in mind than Vee can possibly read it.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the wrong-size feeling was named a few times more than it needed ("went very slightly the wrong size for the room" / "The wrong-size feeling did not go away" / "the wrong size forgotten" / "The wrong size was gone") — the fourth iteration is the author making sure I got it.

The title, "Dear": before reading, it looked like a letter's salutation. After, it's "Vee's my dear friend" — and the other sense, *dear* as in *costly*, is sitting right there in Vee's own paragraph about what it "cost" Randi to say it. Dear = expensive. That's exactly the book's tagline geometry: the gift and the price are the same word.

What I want next: I dread Randi carrying this whole debrief home to the gravel drive — "she let him choose for you, and she liked it" repeated across a pillow. Guesses from here: the older woman matters (Standards has a hierarchy above Brooke?); the hike chapter is next or soon; Cassie's exclusion will widen before it breaks open.
