## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Well. Eighteen chapters and the book finally opens the bedroom door, and it was worth every deferral. My body noticed before my brain did — the kitchen prep laid out and *stopped* ("The pan was dry and clean on the burner. He'd done all the preparation and stopped.") did something to me before anyone touched anyone. He knew. He set the meal down mid-motion because he knew what would happen first, and he still let her make the move. That's the whole Pace method in one cutting board.

And she does make the move. Every step of this is hers — the green set bought "for the color," the frosted plum "chosen without thinking too hard about why" (I laughed out loud; the toenail polish is Randi's motif, and Vee doesn't know she's rhyming), "I never thanked you for the shirt," the apron strap pulled, "Will you show me your bedroom?" The jacket tagline has been humming under this book for seventeen chapters and here it's almost unbearable: every yes freely given, and I watched a man engineer the conditions under which she'd give them, and I *still* wanted her to give them. That's what this book does to me.

"Famished." She says the book's hunger-word out loud and "heard it a beat after he did." The chapter title gave nothing away and then detonated in dialogue — perfect.

The mother's voice thread pays off exactly as I hoped and feared. "*Floozie*, said the voice. That was the worst it had." And then the sentence I think the whole book has been building toward: "she waited for the shame to crest and close her, the way it always had, and it didn't. The shame was still running and she was burning through it." Not cured — *outrun*. "It was the first time that had been true" landed like a bell. The erotic charge is the highest in the book: his question — "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — is the fitting scaled up, her wetness reframed as tribute rather than evidence, and the standing orgasm with his hand taking her weight is graphic and sustained and never clinical.

The undelivered secret finally gets delivered — and not to Randi, who tried to extract it. She *folds it*, "the spot centered," and says "Trade." The shirt for the panties. She turned her shame into a gift, in his light, naked, sheet on the floor and neither of them looking at it. That exchange choked me up a little, honestly. "Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private" — I don't know what that privacy is (trophy for Randi? feeling? both?) and the not-knowing is the first cold draft in a hot chapter.

New fact I'm holding carefully: the surgical scar, inner thigh near the hip. He moved her hand away without speaking. "She filed the scar in the place where she kept facts without frames." So did I. That's a chamber loaded for later.

Friction: almost none. The mirror line ("Like a short story…") is a hair cute, but she calls it "a nervous thing to say to an empty room," so the book already knows. No suspicion earned against Pace *in this scene* — which is itself the trap; nothing on this page is anything but tender, and I know what I know.

Guesses from here: those folded panties end up where Randi can see them, or Randi extracts this night and something in Vee finally resists the extraction. The threesome proposal from chapter 1 is now armed. Dread and appetite in equal measure.
