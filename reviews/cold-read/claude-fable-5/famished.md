Ten-plus chapters of "May I" and moleskin and mint tea, and when it finally happens it isn't him who opens the door — it's her, with her hand on his apron strap: "Will you show me your bedroom?" I actually put my hand over my mouth at "Famished," because she hears it a beat after he does and so did I — the jacket called her "quietly starving" on page one of my acquaintance with this book, and here she is answering "Are you hungry?" with the whole novel in one word. That's the title doing its work in the scene itself, and it earned the shiver.

The felt read, honestly: this chapter worked on me almost without interruption. The lead-in is exactly right — the week of his silence "gave her nothing to read," the flannel gone thin so she has to chase the smell of him, the mall trip where she buys the green set "for the color" and the frosted plum polish "without thinking too hard about why." And then the sex. This is the most explicit the book has gone with Vee and it is genuinely hot — not clinical for a sentence. The peak for me is the wetness, of all things, because the book has spent two chapters teaching me that her body's evidence is her deepest shame — the thumbprint on the couch, the "big and dark and stupid" patch at the fitting — and here she walks in *already ashamed of what he hadn't seen yet*, "The anticipation sat on top of the fact of it, which made it worse, which made more anticipation." Then he stays in it. "He did not move past it… nothing in how he held her wanted her to be any less than she was." And the line that made my chest go tight: "she waited for the shame to crest and close her, the way it always had, and it didn't. The shame was still running and she was burning through it." The book has never pretended the shame goes away — *floozie* runs on its wire through the whole scene, "the heat and the shame… ran on the same wire, and she had stopped looking for one" — it just stops being able to close her.
