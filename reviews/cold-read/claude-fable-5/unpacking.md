# Cold read (grounded) — unpacking

*scene: scenes/unpacking.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: ck-ch050 + raw ch051..ch052 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

What a gentle landing this is, after the coat and the cashmere and the taste she wouldn't name. "Unpacking" is a dorm-room chapter, one scene, two girls, two lamps — and it did something to me that the last three erotic chapters, for all their heat, couldn't: it made me a little sad in the exact middle of my happiness for Vee.

The room itself got me first. Cassie's window open its two inches — "defended territory, all winter, every winter" — and Vee standing in the cold line *on purpose*, "clean and boiled pink," because she'd missed a draft. That's this book at its best: a body's small weather doing the emotional work. She's just come from a house where "the water came down like it meant it," and she can stand in the dorm's polite trickle and its microwave-popcorn heat and be "happy in it anyway, happy the whole time." I believe that happiness, and it moved me — she isn't a girl who can only live in his kingdom now. Both rooms hold her.

And then the lie. Because that's what it is, and the chapter knows it and doesn't flinch: the shrunken cranberry sweater, "the dye went into my good pants," a whole invented laundry accident to cover the plain fact that she drove out there in a coat and nothing else. This is new. Vee has always *cropped* — "she edits the middle out of every story" is practically her signature with Cassie — but cropping is omission. This is fabrication, delivered fluently, "her mouth set off without her." And Cassie — oh, Cassie. "Pace. Shrank your sweater." "To a doll size." That held-breath silence, and then the rare full laugh, "and the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." That sentence is the whole chapter. Cassie doesn't believe a word of it. Cassie *knows*, has probably known since the Sunday-in-his-sweats-and-Randi's-heels eyebrow, and she chooses to laugh with her friend instead of at the story. It's the most loving act of non-belief I've read in this book, and it also quietly files Vee, for the first time, on the same shelf as the book's other performers. Randi builds surfaces for her parents; Vee just built one for Cassie. I felt the chill of that under the laughing, and I don't think I'm inventing it — the page put "corrected by nobody" right there.

The beat that actually got me behind the eyes: "My mother says thank you for the card." Vee's hand stopping partway to the towel and going "to the collar of her robe instead." "She wants to know when she gets to see you again." And Vee's "Oh" coming out small, and "Something had gone through her middle that she had no intention of examining tonight, warm and specific, like a hand cupped briefly at the back of her neck." Vee has spent the whole book being mothered badly at long distance — the "floozie" voice, the cropped photo in the purse — and here is a mother, offstage, in a house of saved boxes and Play-Doh cookies, simply wanting her. Cassie delivering it in the snowshoe register and then recapping the highlighter "done with the subject the exact moment before it would have needed anything from either of them" — that's four-words-a-day love, and it's the warmest thing in the chapter. Warmer than the mushrooms.

Cassie herself is a delight all through. The break monologue is perfect deadpan — the box closet, "I had a civic duty," the brother-in-law "holding them like she'd towed his car," "The baby is fine. The baby is a baby." Turning the whole chair around to give Vee "the entire front of her attention." I trust her completely, same as ever, and this chapter deepened her without spending a single earnest word.

The ending is a lovely minor-key rhyme with the opening of "Forbidden Kiss": there her hand went out across the wide bed and found cold cotton; here her arm goes out across the one-girl bunk and finds "the cold painted cinderblock a hand's width off." She leaves the hand on the wall — "and the wall was only a wall." No ache spiraling, just orientation: he's west of town, Friday will come. And the flannel — collar up over her nose, "him still, but going faint," and her decision that "he would just have to wear it for her this weekend" — the shirt ritual resuming exactly where it dropped in the heap in December. That made me smile.

Erotic charge: barely any, and rightly — this is the exhale chapter. What charge there is lives in residue: the weekend "still on her like sun she'd been out in," the flannel, the retold "I got mushrooms."

Friction: almost none. If I squint, Vee's mimicry of Pace ("Three grains. 'They do it that way in Japan.'") retells a bit much of the breakfast I just read — but that's what a girl bursting with a weekend does, and Cassie's flat echoes ("He salts the persimmons") make the repetition the joke, so it earned itself.

Suspicion: nothing new against anyone here — the room is clean of the book's machinery, which is part of its sweetness. But the chapter did add one thing to my unease ledger, and it's about *Vee*: she can lie now, easily, to the person who loves her most plainly. The book has been teaching her appetite all along; this is the first time I've watched it teach her cover.

Titles: "Unpacking" is doing three jobs and I like all of them — Cassie's duffel suspended mid-unpack; Vee unpacking the weekend for her audience of one (selectively — some items stay in the bag, like the bag itself, left in a car that never existed in that role); and the thing she declines to unpack at all, the warm specific something from Cassie's mother "she had no intention of examining tonight." Innocuous on the shelf, loaded in the hand. *A Polite Invitation* — Pace's guest-list chapter just before this ("Bring Cassie, too... Bring Theo") is literally an invitation being assembled, and this chapter shows me why it's dangerous: Vee's worlds in one room means Vee's *versions* in one room, and she's now running at least two. *With a Long Spoon* keeps hanging over every warm meal, and this chapter served the meal secondhand and still made me check the spoon.

What I want next: the party Pace proposed, obviously — Randi and Cassie at the same gathering is the fuse I've been watching all book, and "Let me handle it" from the previous chapter now reads like a lit match. Guesses from here: the Tuesday dinner with Randi ("she would tell her everything... nearly everything") comes first, and Randi will pull the taste-question out of Vee like a splinter and do something with it; and Cassie, at whatever gathering happens, will finally look at Randi in front of witnesses. I dread it a little. I'll be there.
