# Cold read (grounded) — unpacking

*scene: scenes/unpacking.md · model: claude-fable-5 · memory: ck-ch050 + raw ch051..ch052 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

After the coat, the kiss, the party planning at his sink — this is the exhale, and I loved it more than I expected to love a chapter with zero skin in it. Vee coming in from the showers and standing on purpose in Cassie's two inches of cold — "she had missed a draft" — undid me a little. That window has been the dorm's signature since forever, and the book knows it: home is the specific discomforts you'd defend.

Cassie is the star here and my heart just goes out to her, whole. "Anything interesting happen this weekend?" said *to the book* — and then she turns the whole chair around, feet folded up, "the entire front of her attention." That's Cassie's love language rendered exactly: she never spends words, so she spends posture. And the interrogation-by-echo — "Mushrooms." "He salts the persimmons." "He burned the toast." — is the funniest run the book has given her, each flat repetition both applause and audit.

Because it IS an audit, and here's where the chapter got its teeth into me. Vee lies. Smoothly, elaborately — the shrunken cranberry sweater, the dye in the good pants, the t-shirt "down to about here" — a whole fabricated laundry accident to cover *I drove out there in a coat and nothing else*. And Cassie, who catches everything, catches this: "Pace. Shrank your sweater." "To a doll size." That held-breath silence, then the rare full laugh — and the devastating line, "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." Cassie knows. Cassie is laughing *at the lie*, with her, and choosing to let it stand. It's the tenderest possible form of I-see-you, and it also quietly extends the book's saddest pattern: Vee edits the middle out of every story, even for the person who'd never judge her. She'll tell Randi "nearly everything" and tell Cassie the mushrooms. The woman who engineered her gets the confessions; the woman who loves her without an angle gets the cover story. That asymmetry has been building for fifty chapters and this scene made me feel its cost in the middle of a laugh.

And then the beat that got me most: "My mother says thank you for the card." Vee's hand stopping partway to the towel and going, without her, to the collar of her robe — "warm and specific, like a hand set flat between her shoulder blades." Cassie's mother wants to see her again. After a whole book of Vee cropping herself for her own family, here's a family asking for the whole of her, relayed in Cassie's driest register, dropped and closed "the exact moment before it would have needed anything from either of them." Vee declines to examine it — "which was not the size of it at all" — and the page lets that stay unexamined, and so do I. But something in me flagged it: this is the belonging Randi's world keeps promising and never delivers, offered free, in a snowshoe anecdote.

Cassie's break monologue — the box closet, the Play-Doh cookies, "The baby is fine. The baby is a baby" — is perfect four-words-a-day comedy. I laughed out loud at the brother-in-law "holding them like she'd towed his car."

**Trust vs. suspicion:** none earned here, and that's the point — this is the one room in the book with no game running. Which is exactly why Vee's lie in it stung. The only shadow is imported: I know Tuesday brings Randi wanting "every last detail," and I know which friend gets the truth.

**Erotic charge:** essentially none, by design — the weekend rides on Vee "like sun she'd been out in," a lovely afterglow line, but this chapter's warmth is all fraternal.

**Friction:** none, honestly. It earns its smallness. If anything, the sweater lie is so fluent it made me blink — Vee inventing at Randi-level speed — but I think that's deliberate, not a wobble.

**The titles.** "Unpacking" — Cassie's duffel suspended mid-unpack, sure, but really it's what *doesn't* get unpacked: the weekend's true middle, the something warm through Vee's middle she "had no intention of examining tonight," the lie left folded. A quiet, perfect title that recolors on the last page. *A Polite Invitation* — Cassie's mother's message is the politest, most genuine invitation the book has issued, and *With a Long Spoon* keeps humming under every fed meal; the mushrooms-on-toast rave, retold secondhand, sits right under the proverb.

**What I want next:** the Tuesday debrief with Randi — what Vee says about the wet kiss, and how Randi's face handles it. And the everybody-party Pace is planning: too many rooms know half of this, and he just proposed putting all the rooms in one.
