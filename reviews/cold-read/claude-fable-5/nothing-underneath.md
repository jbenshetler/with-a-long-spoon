# Cold read (grounded) — Nothing Underneath

## Reader reaction

The plan Randi handed her last chapter is the plan she carries out, and I want to say up front that it *worked on me* — the whole long ritual of it, the eyeliner as courage applied by degrees, the drawer of underwear "not invited tonight," the giggle at "last thing, only thing." This is Vee's best chapter as a self. The girl who twice fled to a bathroom and twice stopped her own hand because of an imagined witness stands in a lit room, watches herself in the glass, takes herself nearly over — and stops *on purpose*, for a reason she chose: "It was not hers to finish... it left her feeling, of all things, powerful." That's not the shame stopping her anymore. That's appetite with a will. Her mother's word "came up out of old habit and found nothing to hold to" — I actually exhaled at that line. The wire that has run shame and heat together all book finally carried only heat.

And the doorstep. God. "The porch light was off and the door was shut, because she was not expected, because no one had left it open for her" — the warm-house motif inverted into the exact cost of December, and then the coat opens and the wall comes down "all at once," and he looks at her "like a starving man... like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look." He goes to his knees in the January cold. The door bangs open behind them and neither cares. The sex is graceless and desperate and completely earned — six weeks of two people wrecked in parallel discharging in one standing stroke. And then pancakes, "made and not poured," and a grown man on one knee with his voice cracking: "I missed you so much." That's the swoon, whole and real, and I was in it.

But I have to be honest about what I noticed while I was melting. The fight was *"what am I to you?"* and the fight is over — and the question was never answered. He said her name, he cracked open, he missed her so much — and the word she drove out into the cold about is still nowhere on the page. The chapter knows it: "tonight there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." Tonight. Vee has traded the question for the reunion, and I don't blame her for one second, and I also know the question is still sitting on that table in the middle of the bedroom, full of what it is, waiting. Randi's prescription — "You don't do it with words. Words are the whole war" — got exactly what it promised: peace without terms. The body succeeded where the sentence failed, which is beautiful, and is also a truce, not a treaty.

And there's the other shadow I carry that Vee can't: this triumph ran on Randi's script and Randi's shoes. Even the line Vee steadies herself with at the mirror is Randi's, said "first in Randi's warm certainty and then in her own less certain voice." The page works hard — and mostly succeeds — at making it Vee's own act ("she had decided this, in her right mind"), and I believe the mirror scene and the unfinished wanting are nobody's but hers. But the reconciliation of the two people Randi loves was, once again, engineered by Randi, and Vee will thank her for it. The imagined blonde dissolving at the sound of the sad horn ("no man put on music like that for a woman") was a lovely touch — the fear-built Sheri simply couldn't get a grip on the real house.

Erotic charge: this is the hottest chapter in the book, and not because of the acts — because of the *withholding*. The mirror edge-and-stop, the satin lining on bare skin at every stoplight, the hand leaving the wheel, the cop lights turning her guts to ice while she's slick to the fingers — the near-catch scared me and heated the page at once, exactly the shame-as-fuel move this book does better than anything, and this time she reads the mark on the lining as *proof she means it* rather than evidence against her. The doorstep consummation, doubled cold-and-heat on her body, "strung the length of her between the two" — it never went clinical once.

Friction: almost none. Kayla's laundry monologue while Vee stands there bare under wool is a comic set piece that could have been cut by half, but "smiling and dying by inches" bought it back. If I squint, the cop passing without stopping is a freebie — but the fright did real work on her body, so I'll allow it.

The chapter title gave itself away — deliberately. Last chapter ended with Vee saying "And nothing under it," so "Nothing Underneath" wasn't a spoiler, it was a promise, and I turned the page wanting it kept. But I can't stop hearing the second meaning: nothing underneath the reunion either — no word, no name, no answer under all that warmth. The volume title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the series, *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil with a long spoon, surely — keep whispering that everything freely given here was invited by someone with a plan, and this chapter is the politest invitation yet: a woman handed a coat-trick by her best friend and applauded for her bravery. No tagline in my packet beyond the cover board.

What I want next: for Vee to ask the question again someday, in daylight, fed and warm, and for him to answer it. What I dread, as a guess from here: that this peace holds all spring on exactly these terms — everything given, nothing named — until the two secrets under the floorboards (his and Randi's) come up through the part that carries forward. Cassie's "Hmm" was the right last word: one eyebrow from the only person in the book with nothing hidden.
