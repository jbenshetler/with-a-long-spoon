## Reader reaction

Felt read

I read this chapter with my heart going. After four chapters of ache — the cracker-and-meal sentence set down and not picked up, the shirt taken off in the cold, the door walked out of, the beige Ohio midnight — the book finally let the pressure blow, and it blew exactly the way I wanted it to and exactly the way I'm afraid of, at the same time, and I want to be honest about both.

The swoon first, because it's enormous. The whole preparation sequence is one of the best things the book has done with Vee's body and Vee's nerve at once. The drawer of underwear "not invited tonight" and the sound of it closing being "funnier and worse than she expected"; "last thing, only thing" with the heels; the giggle about whether his eyes will ever get down to the plum toes — she's funny in this chapter, alone in a room, which is when you find out who a person is. And the mirror scene is the hottest thing in a while precisely because of where it stops: "She turned from the glass unfinished on purpose, and it was harder than anything the day had asked of her yet, and it left her feeling, of all things, powerful." That's Vee, three hundred pages grown. The girl who wore flats to church is now curating her own denial like he taught her — and choosing it, which is the difference the book has been building the whole time. Her mother's word coming up "out of old habit" and finding "nothing to hold to" nearly made me cheer.

The car, the hand leaving the wheel, the cop lights — I gasped and then laughed out loud when the cruiser blew past "after something that had nothing to do with her at all." The mark on the lining that "only told her she meant it" is the cardigan-and-shame motif turned fully inside out.

And the doorstep. "He looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — that's the sentence of the chapter. The sex is graceless in the exact right way — the door banging in the wind, the three hobbling steps, jeans around his thighs, "which was the truth of them tonight." The cold-on-wet-nipples doubling, his mouth doing on the doorstep what his voice wouldn't do in December — the chapter says that out loud, "the mouth that had spent no word she needed on her in December giving her everything it had now," and it's devastating because it's true in both directions. Then the pancakes, made and not poured, and his voice cracking on "I missed you so much" — a grown man on one knee at midnight with his face open. I melted. I'm not going to pretend I didn't.

Now the other thing, which the chapter itself keeps flagging so I'm allowed to. She came in December for a word. She came back in January with her body, because "the words had failed her and this would not" — and it worked, and the word still was not said. "I missed you so much" cracks his voice and it is still not the word; "Vee" in his mouth makes her cry because it's her name and not the name of what they are. The last line of the reunion — "tonight there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given" — has "tonight" doing a mule's load of work, and the title stands over the whole thing like a verdict: Nothing Underneath. Under the coat, gloriously, nothing. Under the reconciliation — also nothing new. The question she asked in "Believe Me" is not answered; it is fed, warmed, made love to, and put to bed. The laundry-room pile of things she doesn't look at just got one more soft heavy item.

And Randi is in every seam of this chapter without appearing in it. The plan is hers, word for word — "take this into your own hands," "you've got the goods" running under the shower — and the heels are hers, on Vee's feet the entire time, through the mirror, through the orgasm on the doorstep, through "Those stay on." I felt that. Vee reconciles with Pace wearing Randi's shoes, executing Randi's script, and the fix Randi engineered is precisely the fix that resolves the fight without the declaration. I can't tell — the page doesn't tell me — how much of that is Randi loving Vee and giving her the bravest true advice she has, and how much is Randi, half a player in this from the beginning, steering Vee back into the arrangement on terms that cost Pace nothing. After "Fairytale" I believe Randi's love completely and her disclosures not at all, and this chapter lets both readings stand. I'm leaving it open because the book does.

Small perfect things: Kayla's laundry monologue while Vee stands there "wearing not one stitch of the underwear Kayla was mourning" — comedy the book has earned. The music through the door — "no man put on music like that for a woman" — killing the blonde phantom in one line. Cassie's whole scene: the coat, the man's sweats, the going-out heels, "Hmm," back to her book. Four words a day, and one of them does the work of a page.

Friction: almost none. If I press, the imagined coat-opening ("the kind of reveal she'd only ever seen in a movie") slightly pre-plays the real one, so the doorstep had to beat its own trailer — it did, barely, because his face is what the trailer couldn't show. And I noticed the chapter is careful never to let Pace speak about December — "It's late. Is everything alright?" and then no words at all until the tease. That's true to him, and it's also convenient for him, and I felt the convenience.

Titles: "Nothing Underneath" is the rare title that works three ways — the literal coat, the shame stripped of anything to hold to, and the hollow under the sweetness where the word still isn't. It didn't spoil; the jacket's own logic (A Polite Invitation, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.") has never fit a chapter better — this yes was the most freely given of the book, delivered naked through January cold, and she was talked into giving it by the woman running the other half of the game. With a Long Spoon keeps its distance and keeps being right.

What I want next: I want the term to start. I want the brunch where Randi hears how the heels performed. Guesses from here: Vee's next collision is with Randi, not Pace — the "Just us" anthro class, the goodbye kisses, the mirror-refusals; something there gives before Pace's word does. And Sheri's February dance invitation is sitting there like a lit fuse — "the redhead" and possibly the brunette in one hall.
