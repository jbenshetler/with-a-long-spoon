# Cold read — Sorority

*scene: scenes/sorority.md · read after: all-told*

## Reader reaction

This one made me sick with how well it worked, which is exactly the problem I keep having with Randi and can't seem to hold onto in the moment.

The dress-showing at the top is genuinely lovely. Vee putting it *on* instead of holding it up on the hanger — "the wanting got there before the plan" — that's her whole nature in one gesture, the exposure-hunger she half-hides. And "He didn't make this *for* you… He made it *to* you" is a knockout line that pays off the sun-porch tears I remembered from before. It lands *to* like a stone in water. I felt Vee's throat do the thing. I felt it too.

But here's what I can't unsee, and it's the reason I trust nothing: Randi's hands. She reads the cut "as her eyes had," fingers up the bias seam to the bare shoulder — "the silk cool there and Vee warm under it." That's not a friend admiring a dress. That's someone appraising merchandise, and the merchandise is Vee. "Charmeuse… And a good weight. A verdict." I've been told these two want Vee's body for something Vee doesn't know about, and this whole chapter is Randi's hands learning Vee's body under cover of shoes and silk. The one honest tell the text gives me is buried and quiet: "one faint thing under it she didn't stop to name." Vee felt it. She didn't name it. I named it.

The shoe-store slapstick genuinely disarmed me — the yeti canoe shoe, the pink bedazzled platforms, "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal," Randi folding over the bench with the real laugh "that costs her something to give." I laughed. And that's the trap, isn't it — I *like* them together, I want Vee to have exactly this friend, and the chapter knows I do and closes on it: "she had never once in her life had a friend like this." The dread is that the friend is a harvester and the laugh is real anyway. Both. The book keeps refusing to let me have the comfort clean.

The "seven — that's me exactly, same feet, same everything, we can share, it's practically a marriage" beat is the machinery humming right out in the open, and I felt it. Interchangeable feet. Interchangeable bodies. For a threesome. Randi establishing that she and Vee are *the same size* — I don't think that's innocent and I don't think I'm reaching.

Erotic charge: the peak is the kneeling. Randi down on one knee on the pale floor, holding Vee's bare foot in both hands, the frosted-plum polish Vee painted for *Pace* — and the thumb across the big-toe nail, "Vee felt it go all the way up the length of her… her hand shot out and found Randi's shoulder to stop it." That's the goodbye-kiss staircase advancing into full-body arousal, and it's dressed as a shoe fitting. Randi looks up and "smiled and said nothing at all." The withholding. It worked on me — it's the most charged thing in the chapter and it's transacted entirely through a *foot*, which is exactly this book's move. The polish being the one Vee chose to catch light "as the silk did" ties her want for Pace and her response to Randi into one nerve.

Where I felt the author's thumb, mildly: the credit-card passage over-narrates the wound a touch. "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it — that she did not have the next part, the paying-it-back part, that it would be sitting there next month with nothing behind it but next month." I got it at "there was nothing under it." The rest is the book making very sure I feel the class-cliff, and I did feel it, but I felt the *hand* making sure. Still — "less like wearing a shoe than being stood up by one" is a beautiful sentence and it earned the money-shame it sits next to.

The two-stores structure is the cleanest class-mirror the book has done: Vee's store where "the ground was hers," where she knows which rack lies about a size — and then the cream-and-brass boutique where the price is written tiny on the sole and "the number on that sole started where her most ended and kept on going." And Randi buys loafers "several times" the price without looking, mid-anecdote, to walk out in. That contrast did more to me than any line about money could.

What I want and dread next: the induction. The dress shown now to Randi (still not Cassie — I notice, and I mind, that Cassie's tidy empty side of the room got a cold little sweep from Randi's eyes and nothing more). Vee has now gone into unrepayable debt to be *stopped-the-room* beautiful, at Randi's steering, and she thinks it was her own idea because Randi said her private thought out loud. *Guess from here:* Pace will be at that induction, or the shoes/dress will be for a night Randi and Pace have already arranged. And I dread that the "same size, we can share, practically a marriage" line is a seed that gets literalized — that somewhere ahead Vee and Randi are made interchangeable for real, in the dark, and Vee is the one who can't see it.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Randi came to Vee's dorm; Vee, unable to just hold it on a hanger, *put on* the burgundy dress and let Randi look. Randi appraised it with her hands — reading the bias cut up to the bare shoulder — and named it: "He made it *to* you," landing the word that Vee's sun-porch tears had been about. Then the shoe date: first Vee's cheap bypass store, where the ground was hers and they collapsed into real slapstick laughter (yeti shoes, bedazzled platforms). Vee found a sensible tan heel her debit could afford; Randi discovered they're the *exact same size* — seven, "same everything, we can share, practically a marriage" — and steered Vee out with "a dress a man made *to* you doesn't want to go with everything." At a cream-and-brass boutique with prices hidden on the soles, the right shoes cost more than Vee's whole ceiling. Randi knelt, held Vee's bare foot (frosted-plum polish, the one Vee painted for Pace), thumbed the big-toe nail — and Vee felt it surge up her whole body and grabbed Randi's shoulder. Vee bought the barely-there black heels on her *emergency credit card* with nothing behind it, refusing to flinch. Randi bought loafers several times pricier without a glance, to walk out in. Closed on the two of them laughing on a gold sidewalk, Vee clutching her box, thinking she'd never had a friend like this.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — exposure-hungry (put the dress on rather than hold it up), proud, broke; just went into unrepayable debt to be room-stopping beautiful, believing it was her own idea. Responds bodily to Randi's touch now, not just Pace's. Frosted-plum toenails chosen for Pace.
- **Pace** — offstage; named "lover" by Randi this chapter, which recolored him in Vee's chest as "adult and lit." Still the maker; still Randi's secret co-orchestrator.
- **Randi** — dazzling confidante and harvester; this chapter her hands learned Vee's body and her feet under cover of dress/shoes, established they're identical size ("we can share"), and spoke Vee's private want aloud so Vee would own it as her own. The big laugh she gave "costs her something." Casual bottomless wealth beside Vee's cliff.
- **Cassie** — roommate, moral floor, trusted; absent, her tidy empty bedside given a cold sweep by Randi. Still hasn't seen the dress.

**How I feel.** The warmth is real *and* it's a harvest, and the chapter proved I can't hold both at once in the moment — I laughed with them and it worked on me. Deep tenderness and mounting dread for Vee, who is being steered into debt and arousal at once. Randi's foot-touch is the erotic peak and the clearest advance of the physical track yet; "same size, we can share" reads to me as the threesome machinery humming in plain sight. Cassie remains the warmth I trust and I mind that she keeps being kept out.

**Open questions.** The induction (dress + these shoes) looms — will Pace be there? Is this night already arranged by Pace and Randi? When does Vee learn they know each other? Will Cassie ever see the dress, or catch Randi? What does the "same size / interchangeable / we can share" seed pay off into? Still open: "Peter," the scar, the "parents" wound, and where the Randi touch-staircase goes now that it's reaching full-body response.
