# Cold read — Off Six-Fourteen

*scene: scenes/off-six-fourteen.md · read after: may-i-choose*

## Reader reaction

This is a small, warm chapter and it broke my heart a little, which is exactly the trap of this book.

**Vee.** I love her here and I'm scared for her here in equal measure. The spin — arms up, hair everywhere, nearly taking out the closet door, collapsing into the bean bag — is the most nakedly joyful I've seen her, and it's *earned* joy, joy over a thing she genuinely did herself. What guts me is the exact shape of what she needs Cassie to believe: "*Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose.*" That's the whole engine, right there, spoken as triumph. She's proud of the one thing that makes her uncatchable. From my seat, knowing what I know, "I did it on purpose" is the sound of the hook setting — he built a room in which she'd choose, and now she's defending the choice as proof she's free. And she's not wrong that she chose. That's what makes it awful.

**Cassie.** She's still the moral floor and she still can't hold. But watch how close she gets. She raises, in order: *how much did he give you to drink* (the date-rape question, dismissed instantly and truly — no wine, tea), *the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop*, and then the one that made the back of my neck go cold — "That's the whole address. *Off six-fourteen.*" Cassie's dry flat floor caught the real thing: Vee is about to go out *west of town, past where it goes to gravel*, alone, to a house she's never seen, belonging to a man she's known days, and the sum of what she can tell a rescuer is a road number. Cassie *names* it. And then — because it's one instance, because there's nothing actually visible — she caps the highlighter and says "Borrow good ones." She hands over the alarm and picks her reading back up. That's the second time Cassie's radar has fired correctly and then stood down, and it's more frightening than if she'd noticed nothing.

**Pace.** Offstage but everywhere. The tell that lands hardest: he "let it go" at the tree. He didn't push for the kiss — "Smiled like I'd told him something nice and started walking us on, like it was fine." Of course he did. He knows she has to be the one to close the gap; her doing it herself is the entire point. His not-taking is the most efficient taking. And now the hiking date — his ground, his water, his trail, his "the one thing he said. Real shoes." Him setting the terms again, small and caring, and her saying yes "so fast it was —." I dreaded the move to his territory and here it comes.

**Erotic charge.** Not a sex chapter and it doesn't pretend to be — the charge is emotional, not bodily, and it works on that register. The peak for me is the spin and "*Look at me,*" and it's not arousal, it's tenderness curdled with dread. Nothing went slack.

**Friction.** Almost none — this is tight and well-heard, the dialogue rings true. If I'm honest, the title plus Cassie's repetition ("Off six-fourteen." / "Off six-fourteen." / "That's the whole address") is the closest the chapter comes to nudging me — the point (she knows nothing, and that's the danger) gets said three times and titled on top. But it's Cassie *saying* it in character, not the narrator editorializing, so I'll allow it; the repetition reads as Cassie worrying a loose tooth, which is what she'd do.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread the hike. *Guess from here:* the next real scene is Vee going out off six-fourteen to his house, isolated, and either that's where the next physical riser happens or it's where she first sees his world — and I keep waiting for the moment his and Randi's prior knowledge of each other becomes visible on his ground. I want Cassie to have written the road number on her hand. I want, and don't expect, the shame-voice to come back and warn her.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Vee came home glowing and told Cassie about the dinner and the kiss — insisting hard that *she* chose it, sober, on purpose, that nobody talked her into it. Pace hadn't pushed at the tree; he let it go and she pulled him back and kissed him. He then asked her hiking; she said yes instantly. His one condition: real shoes. Cassie, glad for her, still fired her dry skepticism — did he get you drunk (no), you'll go missing on a mountain, and the address is *nothing*: he lives "off six-fourteen," west of town past where the road goes to gravel, a house Vee has never seen. Cassie named the danger, then set it down: "Borrow good ones."

**Who's who.**
- **Pace** — orchestrator courting Vee; here, offstage but steering. Didn't take the kiss so she'd have to give it; sets the hiking terms. Lives isolated off Rte 614, past gravel; house unseen by Vee. Still utterly distrusted by me; still working. Right-hand-only quirk still unexplained.
- **Randi** — Pace's sub/girlfriend and Vee's co-opted friend, co-running the op. Offstage this chapter.
- **Vee** — radiant, proud of choosing him herself ("I did it on purpose"); the pride is exactly her disarming. Never hiked, doesn't own real shoes, about to go to his remote house. Wide open, aching to be right about him.
- **Cassie** — roommate, moral floor. Twice now her radar has fired true (the drink question, the non-address) and twice she's stood down because one instance shows nothing. Approves enough to say "borrow good ones."
- **Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey** — sorority machine, offstage.

**How I feel.** Tender-sick. This chapter was pure joy on Vee's face and pure dread on mine. The seduction's masterstroke is now spoken aloud by the victim as freedom: *I chose, on purpose, nobody made me.* Cassie can hear the alarm and can't hold it. Mood: warm, closing, moving onto his ground.

**Open questions.** What's out at the house off six-fourteen — is he rich, and what does the isolation mean for what happens on the hike? Will the move to Pace's territory be the next physical riser? When does Vee learn Pace and Randi already know each other? Why the right hand only? Will Cassie's radar ever fire loud enough to actually stop her — and does anyone but me hear "off six-fourteen" as a warning?
