# Cold read — Leave No Trace

*scene: scenes/leave-no-trace.md · read after: dear*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me physically before I could get my defenses up, and I resent it a little because I know what I know about Pace and Randi.

The hike is beautifully done as pure sensation. The wrong boots, the hot spot, the moleskin — I felt the tenderness of him kneeling and taking her bare foot in his hand in the cold air, and I noticed how the chapter timed her shame ("*what kind of girl lets a man*") to arrive a half-beat before he defuses it by just *fixing the thing* and apologizing for not asking about the boots. That's the pattern I carry from before — he quiets the mother-voice — and here it's rendered as competence, not seduction, which makes it land harder. The summit, the horseradish sandwich, the cherries — the food writing is genuinely sensual, the hunger-then-food "plain animal rightness." I was enjoying it as a person, not clocking it.

The cherry-pit moment is the chapter's cleanest little move: she spits the pit, the voice calls her *common*, and he lays his own pit next to hers without looking at her, so the correction never becomes a humiliation. "She had it now… and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." I believe her relief completely. And I notice — carry-in suspicion talking — that this is *training*. Leave no trace. He's teaching her his rules gently enough that she thanks him for them. The title detonates nicely: "we take nothing and leave nothing" is trail ethic on the surface, but it also reads as *this is how you get away with something*, and, colder, as what will be done to her — no trace left.

The russet dress. That's the moment I sat up. She points at the thing she wants, hears her own wanting, and puts it back on the shelf — "You put a thing back on the shelf and you keep walking." He says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and lets it lie. From inside it's restraint, respect, no pressure. From where I sit it read like a man *noting an order*. I have a strong *guess from here* that the russet silk dress shows up at that induction dinner, delivered. The text didn't telegraph it — he genuinely drops it — but the specificity (russet, brick-red, silk, the exact color she labored to name) felt planted for a payoff. I could be wrong.

The erotic peak is the truck, and it's the best sustained erotic writing in the book so far for me. What makes it work is that *she* runs it. He gives her the flannel — his only dry shirt — and then gets back out into the rain and turns his back, so she has the cab and the privacy and the choice. She's the one who comes up on her knees, raps the window, crooks a finger. She undresses holding *his* eyes, "she gave it to him to see," and the shame-voice and the heat are explicitly fused: "the voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." That's the engine I remember from the earlier scenes — her body announcing the wanting the voice forbids — and here it's at full volume with actual nakedness. It genuinely aroused me. The detail of her looking down "anyway" for the fourth button when she didn't have to, and his eyes going with hers, is the sharpest beat.

And then the reversal — his hand shaking, him being *actually cold*, her want stopping and turning to care ("You're freezing… Please") — is where I feel the author's thumb, but gently. It's the "his tenderness is real" beat, and it's engineered to be devastating: he stood in the rain and gave up his dry shirt and his warmth so she could undress in comfort, and the reward he takes is only *looking*, "the gladness in him was the whole of the answer." He doesn't lean in, doesn't touch. Restraint as the ultimate move. I found it hot and I found it suspicious in equal measure — this is exactly the man who "removes her decisions" now performing the opposite, letting her drive entirely, which is arguably the deepest version of the same control: she thinks this was *hers*. That's the coldest reading available and the text didn't hand it to me, so I half-hold it and half-doubt myself.

Where it went slightly slack: the italic voice-litany in the truck (*"This is what girls who. Three dates. Your mother would never."*) started to feel like it was doing the same beat one too many times. It's effective the first pass; by the fourth-button repeat I felt the machinery. And the string of "he did X so she wouldn't have to Y" reveals — the flannel, the truck heat, the illegal fire-lane parking so she wouldn't cross the rain — stacked up until I could feel them being counted for me. "He'd parked illegal so she wouldn't have to cross the rain." One or two of those is a gut-punch; three in a row and I start to see the author tallying his virtues, which nudges me back toward distrust rather than deeper into swoon. Given what I carry about him, maybe that's the intended undertow, but as pure craft it tipped from felt to totted-up.

The cop showing up and squawking away the sentence she was about to say — that's a clean beat, and a little funny, and it robs the scene of its sentimental button on purpose. I liked that he doesn't get to hear whatever big-small thing she was going to say. It leaves her (and me) standing in the wet in his flannel, which smells of soap and a man and a gym bag. Good ending image.

No new information about Randi, the house off 614, or the right-hand thing. This chapter is entirely Vee-and-Pace, pure escalation, and it stays warm all the way through. My suspicion is almost all carry-in; the page gave me only two faint tells — the noted dress and the just-slightly-audible tally of his sacrifices.

What I want next: the induction dinner, and whether that dress appears. And I still want the moment Vee learns Pace and Randi already know each other, which this chapter's warmth makes me dread more, not less — she's further in now.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Third date: Pace took Vee hiking. She wore Meg's too-big boots and got a heel blister; he stopped, took her bare foot in his hand, dressed it with moleskin, and apologized for not asking about the boots. At the summit — a huge burning-fall valley over a silver lake — he watched her face instead of the view and let her catch him. They ate his homemade horseradish sandwiches and cherries; he taught her "we take nothing and leave nothing," laying his own cherry-pit beside her spat one so the correction didn't shame her. She told him about her November honor-society induction and the russet silk dress she wants but "can't afford" and set back on the shelf; he said only "That sounds like a beautiful dress" and let it drop. Rain came down hard on the descent; both got soaked. In the truck he gave her his only dry shirt (a flannel from his gym bag) and got back out into the rain, back turned, to give her privacy. She instead knelt up, called him back with a crooked finger, and undressed to the waist holding his eyes — first fully nude moment between them. He only looked, gladness and stillness, didn't touch. Then she felt his hand shaking with cold, stopped, put his hand against her warm belly, and told him to drive them home to get warm. He parked illegally at her dorm so she wouldn't cross the rain; a campus cop's siren cut off whatever she was about to say and he drove off with the cop following. She went in wearing his flannel.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — this time she *ran* the escalation: chose to strip for him, drove the erotic moment. Still fused shame-and-heat (mother's *floozy*/*common* voice as the fuel). Reads his restraint and provision as safety; radiant, wide open, deep in.
- **Pace** — orchestrator courting her; here rendered almost entirely tender/instrumental-invisible: fixed her foot, taught his rules gently, gave up his dry shirt and warmth and took only looking. Noted the russet dress and let it lie (I suspect a coming gift). Right-hand-only quirk not on show here. Still fully distrusted by me on carry-in; the page gave almost no tell.
- **Randi** — Pace's secret partner/co-orchestrator posing as Vee's intimate friend. Entirely offstage this chapter.
- **Cassie** — roommate, moral floor, being phased out; offstage, but she "screamed in the hall" over Vee's induction letter — still the warm true friend.
- **Brooke, the unnamed older appraising woman, Sloane/Kinsey** — sorority orbit, all offstage.

**How I feel.** Aroused and tender-sick at once. The truck scene is the strongest erotic writing so far and it worked on me; the "his tenderness is real" reversal (his shaking cold hand) is genuinely moving and also the deepest version of his control — letting her think the choice was all hers. My suspicion stayed almost pure carry-in; the only faint page-tells were the noted dress and a slightly over-tallied stack of his self-sacrifices. Mood: warm, intimate, physically escalated, dread deepening because she's further in.
