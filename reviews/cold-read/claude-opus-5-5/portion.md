## Reader reaction

Pace wakes me up at the end of this chapter, but it's Randi's bowl I can't stop thinking about. He cooked all afternoon: chicken in cream, peas and carrot, wine in the pot, bread from the machine. He ladles her share into "the small one, the one he sliced her apples into," and he thinks that's tenderness. In his mind it *is* tenderness. Then she draws the spoon "not down into the bowl, along the top of it," tastes what it brings up, sets it down and says "It's wonderful." I went cold. From the start I've carried the thin apple slices, the mother on egg whites and tofu, and the rule about thin slices and thick ones. A cream stew, served in the apple bowl, on a night she arrives already hurting. He has no idea what he's put in front of her. She eats the salad down to the dressed leaves at the bottom, with a second glass she poured herself, and leaves the bowl with its one line through the skin. I ached for her and couldn't do anything with it, and neither can he.

My feeling for Pace didn't drop, but it changed. I believe his care: "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything." I've watched him earn that on a mountain and on a riser, and I watched what it gave Vee. He's right about Vee. He's also completely blind to the woman across the table. "Good. She's happy," she says, pressing the glass rim under her thumbnail. That's the same pressing I remember from the bottle cap in the first chapter and the water glass at the lunch with Vee. He watches her hand pour the wine and it "went in somewhere and stayed," but he can't follow it inward. He keeps reading her as "the want, with an edge on it tonight," the same explanation he gave himself the night she left after sex. The narration is careful never to tell me he's right. When he turns the evening over "from the gravel forward" looking for what he did, the answer isn't anywhere in the evening, and that's exactly why he can't find it.

Randi is the one I'm leaning toward. What is "It's selfish, Pace" actually about? He decides she's stung because Friday happened without her, the two of them having picked Vee together. That may be part of it. But I've been at those lunches with her. I've seen the "There's my girl," the second press of the goodbye kiss, the accent thickening while one mimosa sat almost untouched, the *"You don't have to tell me"* that shut a door softly. So I can't let his reading be the whole of it, and I won't decide it for her either. Whatever Vee's Friday did to her, the page leaves the reason open, and I'm leaving it there. "Or you're not," flat, a voice he'd never heard her use. That line was the hardest moment of the chapter for me.

The sex works as a scene and gives no heat, and I think that's how it's meant. She drops the inch-and-stop and the eyes. And she never says "tell me," the word that has been their ritual. "Whatever this was, it was not for him, and he gave it to her anyway." Then the cashmere blanket, her back to him, and she stays this time but turned away. The wanting is all there, pointed somewhere neither of them names. It didn't arouse me. It made me sad and a little afraid.

Some friction. The music cue (*Violator*, "every sound in it polished") felt a touch arranged, the album title doing a bit too much work. And "He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house" is pointed, but it earns its place. Everything else is held so tightly that I trusted it.

The title, **Portion**, hits after the fact. It's her portion in the small bowl, her portion of Vee, her share of the weekend ("You've got her Friday to Sunday"), the portion she won't take. It didn't give anything away going in, and it hurts going out. The cover, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now reads differently to me. She eats with the tip of the spoon, skimming the top, at a polite dinner she didn't want. It feels like a book about who gets fed and who keeps their distance from it. The covered bowl in the fridge, "for her," is the most tender and saddest image yet.

Next, I dread Randi pushing the timetable with Vee herself, without him. That's a guess from here. I want someone to notice she didn't eat. I want to know whether Vee sees what Pace can't.
