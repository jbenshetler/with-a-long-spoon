## Reader reaction: "The Pointing Game"

The first page had me before I'd caught up with it. *Would you like to taste another woman?* and she comes, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That got me too. After the bench, where her body kept getting ahead of her, it's a strong move to have one sentence do what his hands did there. I believed the astonishment on her face. I also liked that Pace "knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." On the page, that's the right instinct.

Then I started reading him more carefully, and I think the page earned that. "He had decided this beforehand": he goes into her "undefended" and asks "into the warm disorder of her." He says he wants her "gathered back into herself… free to weigh it and choose." Then he hopes the truth will come out "dressed as play," and when it does, "He let the camouflage stand." He sees exactly what "Only in a threesome. With you" is doing. The line "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift" is his reading, and it's sharp. Then he takes the gift anyway, gladly: "It was the best idea he'd had in a month." My problem is what I know and he doesn't. I watched her in his mirror decide "she was not coming out again," and in her cold room I watched her not-cry. He thinks the bench was her meeting herself. I know she went back behind the face. So everything he reads as her being "game" now has a second layer for me. I don't think he's a villain. He's attentive, patient, tender ("kissed her, slow and tender, for no reason but that he wanted to"). But he's also running his own film: "two of her in his mind," the sprinter undressing her, "her first woman, and him there to watch her find out." He even wants the blowout girl "himself." Is he opening a door for her or walking her through one he built? The page leaves that open, and I'm leaving it open too.

Randi is the one I ache for, and the one who swept me up. The walk is lovely and charged. Her "Too expected" comes in a voice gone "lower, considering." "Is that a yes or a no for you?" tests the edges of the game. I loved the library-steps girl with the beanie getting a real "Maybe." Then the kiss on the path with her own taste still on his mouth, which is filthy and gorgeous in daylight. "Oh. You dirty boy" is the kitten voice covering real heat, and I could hear the seam. And then the redhead. The stopped stride, the bare "Yes" "with nothing around it," then the costume going back on: "Uh — yeah. I like her." That's the whole woman in three words. The staged collision is a thrill to watch. Ten years of dance turned into a pickup technique, and the redhead ends up apologizing to *her*. Then she comes back glowing, does involuntary happy footwork, and gives the breathy bedroom "*Yes*" in daylight. I melted. Whatever this costs later, she's lit up right now, and I wanted that for her.

The last two lines did the most to me. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" This is the woman who wouldn't look at the table on Saturday. Now she's the one bringing it up, so she's carrying it forward herself. It's not quite her own want yet, but it isn't only his either. And "Let's go feed you," after her automatic "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger. That's the apple again: Pace as the man who feeds a woman who rations herself. It's tender, and it's a little possessive, and I can't separate the two.

Friction: the redhead's entrance runs long and gets leery in places. "Clothes chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task" is a wink I didn't need. The dance-follow metaphor gets explained twice. "Which was unsurvivable" and "because it was beautiful" spell out Randi's move when I'd already felt it. And the opening line, "Let's do something different… Today," said while she looks at the table, puzzled me in a good way. Did she want the bench, or anything but the bench? He "said nothing to it" and took her to bed. I'm keeping that question.

The chapter title is literal and fair: "I point. You pick." It didn't give anything away. On a second look, though, the pointing seems to go both ways: who is actually choosing? The cover board says WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION. No tagline was given. The long spoon makes me think of the old saying about supping with the devil: you need distance from whoever you're eating with. It sits uneasily next to all this feeding. "A polite invitation" now has two candidates on the page: "Go get her," and whatever Randi is about to offer Vee. Both are polite on the surface. *Guess from here:* Vee is going to be more than an instrument. Her laugh, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten… that she could be seen," is the exact opposite of Randi's face. I dread Randi wanting her for herself, outside the "for us," and having nowhere to put it.
