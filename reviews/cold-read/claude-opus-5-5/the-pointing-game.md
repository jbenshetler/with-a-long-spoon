# Cold read (grounded) — The Pointing Game

## Reader reaction

I came out of "Standards" with Randi on her back under a synthetic duvet, shoes on, not crying. Then this chapter opens on her slowing at the table with its top back on and saying *Let's do something different. Today.* That lifted me right away. She's the one who looked at it. Whatever she decided in that bathroom mirror, her body keeps walking her back to this house.

The bed scene took me fast. Him staying with her "longer than she needed." The kiss "with his mouth still wet from her," and her kissing back "harder for it, ardent before she could think better of it." Then the question comes "into the warm disorder of her" and she comes on the words alone, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That's the hottest beat in the book for me so far. It's hot because the wanting outruns her, not because of anything mechanical. I felt it hit her.

Then I read *He had decided this beforehand*, and something cooled in me. It was earned, not reflexive. He planned to slip in while she was "undefended" and to ask the question there. He's careful about consent in the literal sense: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." He waits until she's "gathered back into herself, free to weigh it and choose." I believe he means that. But I know what he doesn't. Last time she promised herself "she was not coming out again." Now I watch him see straight through her "Only in a threesome… With you," call the move "beautiful," and accept the costume anyway because it gets him what he's after. He even registers that wanting another woman is "unsurvivable" for her, and goes ahead and builds a game to take her there. I don't think he's a villain. I think he's a man who loves finding her and hasn't noticed that being found is exactly what she can't bear. "He was glad of it" is doing a lot of quiet work.

The walk itself was a pleasure, and I let it carry me. The burning sugar maple, the leaves showing their pale undersides. Then the cheeseburger: "No. I can't eat that." It went straight back to the apple slices and landed like a small bruise. "Let's go feed you" at the end closes it like a hand on her back. It's tender and a little possessive at once, and I can't pull the two apart. The game is cruel to strangers in a way that thrilled me and made me wince together. Point, judge, dismiss. The "too expected" ponytail who "could have been Randi." I loved Randi learning the rules with "I point. You pick." I loved her moving from good sport to "shopping," and her body leaning forward like a follow before the lead calls the turn. That dance image is lovely and exact. The "God, no" at the blowout girl who "chaired things" has real feeling in it. That one is Brooke, or near enough, and I laughed.

And then Vee. The writing goes lush here, and it should. Red hair "sparked to copper," freckles, a body "the year's clothes were not cut for," laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten… that she could be seen." That last phrase is the key in the lock. It's the thing Randi can't do and wants most. Her bare "Yes," "with nothing around it," was the most naked moment the book has given her outside the bench. The staged collision is a showpiece of her competence. Making it look like the redhead walked into *her* is so precisely Randi. Then the grin she can't hide, the "happy footwork." And the second "*Yes*" in her bedroom register, in daylight. I swooned. I wanted it for her, against every caution.

Then she says "Do you think she'll like the bench?" and my stomach dropped. The bench was where she broke open and swore never again. Now she's offering it to a stranger. Maybe she's handing the exposure on to someone else. Maybe she's finding a way back to it through someone else. Or maybe it's just appetite. The page doesn't say, and I'm leaving it open. Vee herself is a stranger, warm and kind on sight, and so far all I have of her is the surface Pace and Randi see.

My friction: Pace's narration sometimes explains Randi's moves to me after I've already seen them ("not a woman who hungered for another woman… but a girlfriend offering her man a gift"). The fantasy flashes ("the sprinter was getting her out of her clothes from behind") pulled me a little too far into his head as he cast strangers.

"The Pointing Game" is literal, and it gave nothing away. Afterward it darkens a little, though: pointing is what you do at a menu. The cover, WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION, now reads to me like the old warning about supping with the devil. I can't yet tell who the devil is, or whether it's a person at all. "A Polite Invitation" now feels like Randi's staged little collision, and like whatever she'll offer Vee next.

From here, my guess is a study-group pretext in stats. What I'm afraid of is Randi using Vee as a place to put what she won't keep in herself, and Vee getting hurt for it. Or Pace finding that what he unlocked doesn't stay "with you."
