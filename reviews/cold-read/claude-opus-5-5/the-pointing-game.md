So when Pace slides into her while she's "still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended," I was hot and uneasy at once. The want shows up in her before she can stop it, and I felt it the same way.

The line I keep turning over is "He had decided this beforehand." He timed the question for the moment she'd be most open, then tells himself "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." I believe he means that. He waits until the heat has left the room and asks again plainly, and he wants her "free to weigh it and choose." That's real care. But he also reads her answer, "Only in a threesome. With you," as a gorgeous sleight of hand, "The same act. A different owner.", and decides it's sweet and workable. I know something he doesn't: she isn't moving toward him, she's hiding behind the face. He's sure she's opening. I don't think he's predatory. I think he's very good at reading her body and overconfident about reading her mind. I can't tell how much of her yes is "He's hard to say no to," and the page doesn't settle it, so neither will I.

Then the walk, and I was swept up. The burning maple, the wind in the copper hair, "I point. You pick." Randi learning she gets to choose, "shopping," the follow weighting forward before the lead moves. The "Maybe" for the girl on the library steps with the beanie and wire glasses. The kiss where he hasn't wiped his face and "her breath caught against his lips." Then the redhead, laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten... that she could be seen." That's exactly what Randi can't do and, I think, what she aches for. Her bare "Yes," "with nothing around it," then the kitten costume hurrying back over it. Whatever else is going on, she wants Vee, and she's lit up in a way she can't manage.

The unease I can actually point to on the page: his little fantasy inserts. "The sprinter was getting her out of her clothes from behind." "He wanted her himself." And her "Do you think she'll like the bench?" gave me a jolt: thrilling, and a bit chilling. Vee is a stranger who just apologized for a collision she didn't cause. "Let's go feed you" closes it tenderly and in the same key as the apple and "I can't eat that," the hunger he wants to feed. I find that moving, and I'm wary of it.

Friction: the fantasy flashes run straight into real time with no break, so "This one would not have waited to be asked. She brought Randi in and kissed her" took me a reread to see as his imagining. The narration over-explains Pace's read now and then ("which was merely sexy"). "Tuesday-afternoon current" quietly told me days had passed. Fine, but I noticed.

Titles: *The Pointing Game* is literal and fun, but by the end it's pointing back at him. Who's pointing whom? Randi was pointed at Vee as surely as she picked her. The cover board, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, brings the old proverb to mind: you need a long spoon to sup with the devil. So someone here needs distance from someone, and I don't yet know who's the devil. Maybe nobody, maybe desire itself. "A Polite Invitation" now reads like the bumped shoulder, the hand on the arm a half-beat too long. No tagline supplied.

What I want next: Vee, on her own terms, with a mind of her own. I'd love her not to be a gift moving "toward her hands." *Guess from here:* stats class becomes the ground where Randi and Vee happen without Pace, and that's where it gets complicated. I dread the bench being introduced to someone who hasn't chosen it yet.
