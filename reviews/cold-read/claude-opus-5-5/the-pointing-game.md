## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction: "The Pointing Game"**

The opening is the hottest thing I've read in this book since the bench, and it got me in a different way. The bench was slow and withholding. This one hit fast: "Would you like to taste another woman?" reaches her body "before it reached her," and she comes "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." I felt that jolt in my own body. It's the best kind of erotic surprise, a want with no name yet setting off the orgasm all by itself. The kiss "with his mouth still wet from her," and her kissing him "harder for it, ardent before she could think better of it," is filthy and tender in the same breath. I melted a little at "kissed her, slow and tender, for no reason but that he wanted to."

My feelings about Pace are more tangled now, and the page earned that. He really is careful. He waits to ask the real question until she's "gathered back into herself," free to choose, and he says that matters more to him than the answer. I believe him. But I've been inside Randi's bathroom. I know she decided that "the one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." So when he watches her turn the want into "Only in a threesome. With you," and thinks it's beautiful, I'm reading two things at once. He does see the move. He calls it moving the appetite, "the same act. A different owner." Then he decides it's a gentle wrapper she needs, and he hands it back as a game. What I can't tell is whether he's giving her air or being given the face and calling it air. The book leaves that ajar and so will I. "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it": he's so sure. On the bench his sureness felt like care. Here it starts to sound like a man who believes he can always tell.

And his own wanting sits right on the surface of the walk: "two of her… and his hands full of both," the sprinter undressing her from behind, the girl in the blazer "he wanted… himself." He keeps calling it her gift, but it's plainly his too. That's not sinister on its own. Lots of us want things for our lovers that are also for us. Still, "I point. You pick" is a lovely rule laid over a game he invented, with targets he chose. I stayed swept up, a little uneasy, and I think the chapter wants both.

Randi is wonderful here. The "too expected" verdicts, the dry "Is that a yes or a no for you?" The way she shifts from being a good sport to "shopping." Her long look at the girl on the library steps, the bare "Yes" with nothing around it, and then the visible grab for the costume: "Uh — yeah. Yeah. I like her." The meet-cute collision she stages so the redhead apologizes is delicious, a trained social instrument aimed at a person for the first time. Then the happy footwork "she didn't decide to do," and the squeaking *Yes* sliding down into her bedroom *Yes* in daylight. That was the swoon for me: the face cracking from joy, not from begging.

Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" That stopped me cold, in a good way. She's the one who brings the bench into it. Is that her own want, the bench and Vee folded together, or her filing this under *theirs*, under him? I truly don't know. And "Let's go feed you," right after "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger, brings back the apple and all his tender thoughts about her hunger. Him wanting to feed her starts to feel like a theme, and I'm not sure it's entirely innocent.

Friction: the tour of women goes on a beat too long, sorted by type like a catalog, though I suppose the sorting is the point. The fantasy lines ("The sprinter was getting her out of her clothes from behind… Randi let her.") drop into plain past tense with no frame, and for half a second I thought it was happening. "It was the best idea he'd had in a month" is a touch smug. The "follow breaks frame before the lead calls the step" image is lovely once and gets used twice.

**Titles.** "The Pointing Game" is literal. It names the game Pace "had no name for yet," and it didn't spoil anything for me. On second thought, the pointing goes one direction, his finger, and that colors it. The cover board reads *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. The long spoon calls up the old saying about supping with the devil, and "A Polite Invitation" now feels aimed straight at Vee: a staged bump, a stats class, a girl who forgot she could be seen. Guess from here: the invitation is coming, wrapped in politeness, and somebody will need that long spoon. I can't tell yet who's the devil and who's the diner.

Next, I want Vee on the page, her own interior, and whether Randi lets herself want her or keeps routing it through "with you." I'm dreading the moment the game stops being a game for one of them before the other notices.
