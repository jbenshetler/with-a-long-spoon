**Felt read**

The chapter opens on her slowing at the table with its top back on, and I felt it in my chest. I watched her decide two chapters ago, at the mirror, that she was "not coming out again," and here she is looking right at the thing and saying *Let's do something different.* Today. Is that a door opening, or the face choosing what it will let him see? The page doesn't say, and I'm leaving it open.

The bed scene is very hot. When she comes the moment he asks the question, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it," it went through me too. Her body answers before her mind can make the wanting into something safer. What moved me was the line after it: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." That is the consent I want, and he means it. He waits for the heat to leave the room so she can choose. Then she pulls her move, "Only in a threesome. With you," and his reading of it is gorgeous and sad: "The same act. A different owner." She's done exactly what she did in the mirror. The want goes behind a face, and this time the face is the generous girlfriend.

This is where my trust in Pace gets more complicated, and I think the page earned it. He sees straight through the costume, admires it, and says "Okay," easy. He accepts the cover story while knowing it's a cover story, and builds the whole afternoon on it. I don't think he's wrong about what she wants. Her bare "Yes" at the redhead is the most naked thing she's said in daylight. But last time his "remarkably good evening" sat right beside her vow to hide, so I no longer fully trust his confident narration of her inner life: "She wanted this. She had not let herself hold that she wanted it." Maybe that's true. He's also clearly wanting it for himself ("He wanted her himself," "him there to watch her find out"). I like him, I'm aroused by his patience, and I'm watching him. All three are true at once.

Randi is wonderful in this chapter. I loved the bump, the decade of dance folded into three seconds so that the redhead apologizes. I loved the involuntary happy footwork on the brick, and the two *yes*es: the squeaky daylight one, then the breathy bedroom one in public. That's a crack in the managed surface, and I wanted it for her. Then comes "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger, said without a glance. Then "Let's go feed you," which closes the chapter on the apple again. He's still trying to feed a hunger she keeps trimmed. That landed tender and a little ominous.

Vee is the swoon. The head thrown back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." I fell for her too. But that line has a shadow, and the page put it there, not my wariness: she's the one woman who forgot she was being watched, and she's being watched, pointed at and picked by a couple for "the bench." "Do you think she'll like the bench?" gave me a real chill. Randi asks it, low and wanting. A stranger who only bumped into someone is already being measured against furniture built for something else.

Friction: the pointing game is uncomfortable to read (rating strangers like dishes), and the text knows it, giving Randi that first recoil of propriety. Some of Pace's narration over-explains ("She was shopping"; "It was the best idea he'd had in a month"). And "Clothes chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task" leers a bit for me. The public kiss with her taste still on his mouth is very hot, and slightly unasked-for in a way I noticed.

On the titles: "The Pointing Game" is literal. It doesn't give anything away; it names the game Pace couldn't name yet. The cover reads "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION," and no tagline was supplied. "With a long spoon" makes me think of the old saying about supping with the devil, and after this chapter I'm wondering who's at whose table. "A Polite Invitation" now seems to point straight at Vee: a bump on a walkway, *no, my fault*, a stats class. A guess from here: the invitation will be very polite, and what it invites her into will not be.

What I want next is Vee in her own right, not as a pick. I dread Randi using her as the generous gift so she never has to own the hunger. I'm also afraid of what happens when the hunger refuses to stay owned by "us."
