**Famished: Reader reaction**

This one swept me under, and I let it. The first time the book let Vee have sex, it didn't cheat on the shame or tidy it away. The shame is there all the way through, and the chapter's point is that it stays: *"the shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her."* That line got me in the chest. Her mother's *floozie* has been sitting in her head since the stats classroom. Here it doesn't win, and it doesn't vanish. It turns into fuel she burns through. *"She could not find the seam between the heat and the shame because there was no seam, they ran on the same wire, and she had stopped looking for one."* That's the honest version of this, and I felt it physically before I thought about it at all.

**The lead-up.** The opening is quietly lovely. She has worn the flannel so long his smell has gone thin and she has to chase it. She buys the green set "for the color." She paints the frosted plum on her toes and hides it inside ballet flats. I loved her line to the mirror, *"long enough to cover the subject and short enough to keep you reading,"* because it's so Vee: a nervous joke told to an empty room. Then *"Famished," she said, and heard it a beat after he did.* That's flirtation done right. The word goes out before she does.

**Pace.** My trust in him holds and probably deepens. The clean dry pan and everything chopped and waiting tell me he planned to cook, not seduce, and he lets her change the plan. She has to pull the apron strap because his hands had already started *"the beginning of stepping back."* He waits inside her until her hips tell him. Consent here isn't paperwork. The page says it's "a real question" and "something else as well," but the wording felt clumsy in a scene that was otherwise exact. I didn't fully buy the "crystal strings" either. It's a touch ornamental at the one moment I wanted it plain.

**The heat.** It peaks twice. First comes the oral, standing, which echoes the sun porch with him on his knees and her above him. When the careful thing in him "let go," it landed as a release for both of them, not as something taken from her.

The page doesn't say, and I won't settle it. He did once withhold something Vee told him. I'm holding both.

**The scar and the green.** A large scar high on his inner thigh is a first sighting, and then the page moves on. I noticed it and filed it. I also noticed the green. Randi's car is green, and after that kiss Vee caught a smell on herself that was "bright and green." This week Vee walks into a store and "the green called to her." Randi is never mentioned in this chapter, and I felt her absence anyway. I won't push that any further than the page does.

**The titles.** *Famished* is her word and the chapter's whole appetite. It also fits the feeding thread: the apple slices, Christine's food, and now the fra diavolo after. This time she's genuinely hungry and eats until she's full. That's a quiet triumph for a woman who used to watch herself eat. *With a Long Spoon* calls up the old saying for me: sup with the devil with a long spoon. That sits darkly over a chapter this warm, and it's why I dread the panties' next stop. *A Polite Invitation* sounds like *"would you come to my house Saturday."* Every door in this book gets opened politely. No tagline was supplied.

**What's next.** This is a guess from here. I expect Pace to go to Randi and be asked to "tell," and I want to know whether he keeps this one. I want the dress. And I dread the moment Vee learns the two of them talk.
