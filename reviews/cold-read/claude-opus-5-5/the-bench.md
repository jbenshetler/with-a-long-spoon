## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

I opened this expecting Vee, since the jacket is built around her. She isn't here. For most of the chapter I didn't know who the woman was: just "she," black hair, startling blue eyes, a sorority, Kentucky until she was nine. Then the line *"There you are, Randi"* landed and I had to rethink everything I'd read. So this is Randi, the "glamorous new friend" from the jacket, with Pace, before Vee has arrived. The surprise worked on me. The jacket made Randi sound like the one in control, and the first thing the book shows me is Randi taken completely apart.

The heat is real and it built slowly, which is what I want. The apple scene in the kitchen got me before anything explicit happened: "Bite, kiss, bite," his hands going up her sides and turning back "before it arrived," and her catching him with *"You're doing that on purpose,"* Kentucky slipping into the vowels. That's flirting with real play in it. On the bench, what kept me in it was the care: "Tell me your word," "Hi" said to her upside-down face, the twenty or thirty seconds with his palm on her cheek, taking off her heels in the middle of the scene. The consent felt real to me. She says *"Show me"* with her nose crinkled, and she sets his hands on her own breasts. The heat peaks at the second *please*, "the please of a woman who needed something." It broke me open a little. Then the crying, "just leaking," and the blanket she'd brought because she gets cold after sex. I melted.

Then the bathroom turned it. I was ready for her to feel freed. Instead the chapter goes into the mirror, *"The face had just begged,"* and arrives at what I'll be thinking about for a while: she was more alive than she's ever been, and the tenderness waiting in the next room "could not happen." "He could have the face... she was not coming out again." That's interior life done properly: shame, hunger and self-protection knotted into one decision. The resolve sounds firm, but the page itself follows it with "She did not know yet" and "the size of a key turning in a lock." So I'm holding her as someone who has just bolted a door and may not be able to keep it bolted. The detail that hurt most was small: the water-bottle cap pressed into the nail bed "where it would hurt." It was never commented on, and it scared me a little for her.

Pace I like very much, and I trust him in the moment. He waits, he checks in, he holds the Daphne lesson ("a closed door was hers, not his"), and he sets his own erection aside: *"That's not what this night was for."* Some unease crept in, and I think the page earned it rather than my being on guard. *"He heard himself want to hear it again."* His little speech, "Facedown on a bench in a man's house... Begging," which is exact and a bit cruel. And mostly his serene misreading at the end: "a remarkably good evening," "finally let someone feed her," while she lies there deciding never to be found again. The dramatic irony is strong. He thinks he's answered her hunger, and she has just decided to starve it. I don't think he's sinister. I think he's sure of himself, and that may be worse.

Friction: the *please* passage over-explains ("The please was different. The please came from below.") I'd already heard the difference without being told. "The face" repeated in the mirror section leans hard too, though it mostly earns that. "It was the script for the *wanted* position" felt like the author stepping in to label things. Seeing Randi's sorority-dinner morning ("laid out on her bed that morning") from inside Pace's close POV was a slight wobble. The anatomical detail on the bench runs long and turns briefly into a fittings catalogue before her body takes over again.

Titles. *The Bench* is literal at first. By the last paragraph it reads as the object that holds what happened, "full of what it was... waiting for the next time." Pace's "Not only a table" sounds like a description of himself, and maybe of her. *A Polite Invitation* already fits: everything tonight was asked for and agreed to, and it still cost her something she didn't agree to pay. I know the old saying behind *With a Long Spoon*, about needing one to sup with the devil. That makes me ask who the devil is meant to be, and I truly can't tell yet. The jacket line "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" now has a body attached to it.

What I want: Randi again, because the jacket says she's the one who will draw Vee open, and now I've seen why she might need someone else to do the opening. My guess from here is that the game the jacket mentions grows out of this closed door. I dread the moment Pace realizes she's gone back behind the face.
