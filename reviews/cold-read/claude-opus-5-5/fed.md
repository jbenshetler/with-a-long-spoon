## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I opened this chapter grinning along with Vee. "The grin was the entire story." The detail that got me first was that she brought no bag, because packing one "would have been to say the thing to herself." That's so exactly her: she wants something completely and still won't say it out loud. Waking with her hair "its own wild country" and laughing at herself in his mirror is happiness without anything put on for show. I was glad for her all the way down.

Then the zipper moment, which is the best thing in the chapter. She's three feet from Cassie with the skirt half down, there's nothing under it, and she zips it back up. It's funny, and it's hot in a quiet, private way. The green satin is in Pace's hand and she drove home bare. What stopped her wasn't her mother's word ("she found she didn't much mind it this morning"). It was that undressing would be the one part of the story she couldn't edit. Cassie got to me too. Something crosses her face "for half a second" and is gone "before it found a name," and she doesn't pull at the seam. She says "Get your brain back from your boyfriend," which is "kindly, certainly, wrong." I trust Cassie completely, and I ached a little at how much Vee is keeping from her now: the middle of the night, lifted out "the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole." Vee calls it "a kindness" and believes it "the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep." That line knows her.

The brunch is where my stomach turned, even though nothing happens on the surface. Vee does the Thursday arithmetic, orders the mimosa anyway, and "did not think about the number again." I loved that for her. Then Randi gives her "sad little story" about an old boyfriend who was beautiful and in a hurry, and that "lowered my expectations" pun. It's charming. It also sets up a comparison for Vee to win, and I couldn't shake that. Pace was "not in a hurry. At all." I can't tell if the boyfriend is real. Like the Hermès-scarf man, he might just be a story built to get Vee talking. "Give me something to live on." "I'm running on fumes here." Then the title lands: *Fed.* Randi is being fed. I know Pace reports to her, and here she's going straight to the source for the part he might keep back.

Still, the page won't let me write Randi off as cold, and I won't either. When Vee tells the sheet story, Randi "got there first" before the image could turn cheap and gave it back as "a painting. Keep that one." It's lovely and protective, and she "meant it." Her accent thickens and her eyes go "liquid at the edges." Vee guesses she's drunk, checks, and finds one mimosa, mostly untouched. That's the moment that earned my unease, and my sympathy too. Something is coming loose in Randi while she listens to Vee talk about being wanted by the man Randi sleeps with, and I can't tell if it's jealousy, longing, or her want for Vee. I'm leaving it open. One small thing caught me: Randi says "a beautiful man at the stove." In this chapter Vee never calls him that, and I remember him as having an ordinary face. Maybe it's just a figure of speech. I noticed it anyway.

The frosted-plum moment made me hot. Randi takes it slowly: fingers "for the room," toes for "a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands." Vee's hands press flat on her thighs under the table. The real reason for the color is the silk, the dress he's making, and it rises into her face "before she had decided to show it." Vee is being undressed in public by someone who isn't touching her, and it works on me. Then "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky," and I winced, because she *is* caught, in a way she can't see. But "I wanted it. And I took it. For once" is Vee's own, and Randi's "Good," with her hand left there warm, sounded sincere to me.

The goodbye kiss: this time Vee "half-expected it, and met it," and didn't look down the street. That's a real shift. She's settling into Randi the way she settled into Pace. "The friend she'd wanted at fifteen" broke my heart a little, given what I know about how this friendship started. The chapter ends on "the day was good," and I believe Vee believes it.

On the titles: *Fed* recolored the whole chapter for me on second thought. The ladle, the brown milk, "something to live on," the dinner Randi "only got," Pace cooking at midnight. Everyone is feeding or being fed, and the hungriest person at the table is the one who barely touches her drink. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* fits Randi's brunch exactly: gracious and warm, and it gets everything out of Vee. The cover line *With a Long Spoon* reminds me of the proverb about the devil, and I'm still holding that loosely. Nobody here is a devil yet, but Vee is eating with no spoon at all.

What I want next, and these are guesses from here: Randi coming apart after this, maybe back at Pace's. Cassie's half-second look turning into words. And I dread the day Vee learns who she was "telling."
