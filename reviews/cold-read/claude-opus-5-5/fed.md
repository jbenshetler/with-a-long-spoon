## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction: "Fed"**

The last line of the morning section caught me off guard: "She had never in her life been so happy." I believed it, all of it. The chapter opens in the cold dorm room, cereal milk gone brown, Vee with no bag because packing one "would have been to say the thing to herself." That detail made me love her. She drove out sure and still couldn't tell herself so. Then she wakes with her hair "its own wild country," laughs at herself in his mirror, and grins at every red light on the way home. It's the clean, sunny morning after the night in "Famished," and I didn't want anything to cloud it.

The zipper moment, though, is the heat of this chapter for me. She's three feet from Cassie with the zipper half down, nothing on under the skirt because she traded the green panties to Pace, and she zips it back up. "The next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing." That line worked on me. Last chapter's shame-heat comes back in a quieter key: the bare body is the one thing she can't edit, and she chooses to keep it to herself. Cassie's "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend," said "kindly, certainly, wrong," is lovely and a little sad. Then there's the flicker at the very start: "for half a second something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name." I noticed it and I'm leaving it unnamed, as the page does. Cassie doesn't pull at the seam. Whether she feels it is left open, and so do I. The ladle image, where she lifts the middle out "the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole," is gorgeous, and I believed her self-deception ("It was a kindness") exactly as far as the narration does, which isn't far.

Then the brunch, and my body changed its mind. I've liked Randi's warmth, and I still do in places. "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky" is a genuinely generous thing to say to a frightened girl. "You drove over there sure. So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" pushes Vee toward owning her own wanting, and Vee does own it: "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." I was glad for her. But I know things Vee doesn't. Randi's "sad little story" has an old boyfriend, a dinner, and "a boy in a hurry," and she says "I only got dinner that night." I can't tell which night or whose dinner, and I won't pretend I can. Still, I remember her at Pace's asking "How was it?" and never waiting for the answer. Here it's "How was he?", and she does wait. "Give me something to live on." "I'm running on fumes." The page earned my unease. Randi is being fed, and Vee is feeding her.

The detail that stopped me cold was the accent. "The esses running long, the a's lifting." I first heard that back in "See You Later," and here it gets thicker the longer Randi listens, with her eyes "gone liquid at the edges." Vee checks the glass and it isn't the mimosa, "nothing came along to take its place," and she lets it go. I can't let it go. Something in Randi comes loose when Vee talks about her own wanting, and the polished voice slips. I don't know what it is. Longing, envy, grief, all three? I'm not going to decide. But it felt more naked than anything Randi has said out loud.

The goodbye kiss repeats exactly, hand at the hinge of the jaw, the second press, and this time Vee meets it and doesn't scan the windows. That small change landed on me as tenderness and as something sliding into place, both at once. Vee calls Randi "the friend she'd wanted at fifteen," who "liked her, some days, better than she managed to like herself." It aches, because it may be true and it may not be the whole truth.

Friction: not much. Randi's toe-paint reasoning ("Fingers for the room… the toes… for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands") was a touch too neat and knowing, though maybe it's meant to be: she's an expert. The brunch also repeats last chapter's lunch beat for beat: prices read right side first, Thursday arithmetic, Randi's sex story, extraction, kiss. The repetition is pointed, and it holds because Vee changes (she orders the mimosa, she doesn't look around). Still, I felt the pattern.

**The titles.** "Fed" is Vee fed in every sense: the dinner, the night, the mimosas, being liked. Then it turns on itself. Randi is the one eating, living off Vee's stories. The cover board reads *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. The spoon sits right next to the ladle in this chapter. The phrase brings the old saying to mind, about keeping your distance when you sup with someone dangerous, and I can't yet say who's at that table or who's holding the spoon. "A Polite Invitation" fits every warm, gracious, steered brunch. No tagline in what I was handed. No jacket copy shown.

**What I want and dread:** I want the dress, and Vee wearing it. I dread the moment Vee learns that Randi and Pace know each other, and how much of what she's told at these tables has travelled. *Guess from here:* the next time Randi is with Pace, she'll ask for what Vee wouldn't give her.
