## Reader reaction

The first scene made me grin. Vee comes in "still warm," already talking, and it fills the little dorm room. Cassie gets pulled out of her chair "the way good news pulls you to your feet," and the clumsy spin that nearly takes out the closet door is pure joy. I believed every bit of "I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." After the restaurant chapter that mattered to me. Vee's yes at the tree was hers, and the page lets her say so out loud. Cassie's "How much did he give you to drink?" didn't read as a downer. It's the friend who checks, then believes her ("this time the okay had her believing it"), then grins. I love Cassie more every chapter. She's the one person here I fully trust.

Under the joy, a small cold thread. "He lives out west of town, off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." Vee counts it off "like a dossier, like plenty," and Cassie answers, "That's the whole address." I know about the bench. Vee doesn't. I don't think Pace means her harm. On his own pages he's been careful about yes, almost to a fault. But she's agreeing to things inside a picture she can't see, and that unease is earned. The page put it there, not my nerves.

The Smoothie Bird scene is where I sat up, because I know things Vee doesn't. "What is his name. Paul?" Randi snapped her fingers to fetch a name she has said in bed. Watching her play dumb about the man she was straddling on a couch a week ago made my skin prickle. Then the "catastrophe" date: tall, dressed like money, a man who talked about himself all night. Is it real? I can't tell. Maybe it's a story built to make Vee feel lucky, maybe something true wearing a costume. I'm leaving it open. But it fits too neatly as the opposite of Pace. And Randi's smile goes "a whole notch" brighter when Vee says she felt good just listening to him. Two chapters ago, "She's easy to listen to" made Randi go still on Pace's lap. I can't read that brighter smile, and I don't think the page wants me to yet. Delight, hunger and something tighter could all be in it.

Then the line that undid me: "Ask and then ravish," said "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That's the one moment Randi drops the wattage, and it's the hottest beat in the chapter. Vee's hands run down her thighs while she pictures the trail, and Randi joins her inside that picture instead of hosting it. I believed it. The want leaking out of Randi there feels real to me, and it isn't only for Pace's sake. On Vee's side the pull toward Randi keeps surfacing and getting filed away: "that side of Vee's face was hot... This had color in it," and later the long look at Randi's brows and lit-blue eye "until she caught herself doing it." Between the two women it's quietly hot in a way the Pace thread isn't yet.

Brooke's arrival is very good and very tense. Brooke grilled Randi in the common room about Pace's name. Now she finds Randi holding hands with a girl from stats. "Vee's my dear friend," and the cool, damp hand stays on the table "in plain view." I don't know why Randi does it. It might be generosity, or cover, or a claim, or a dare to Brooke. Vee reads it as Randi spending something on her, and she's probably right that it cost something. The silent taller girl's last look, "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over," chilled me.

My friction is mostly sameness. The Smoothie Bird replays Chi Latte almost beat for beat: the matched girls, the cardigan "like an apology," the high table that feels like a shelf, the quick hug, "Love you, girl." Some of that echo works, since the ritual is becoming "a thing they did." But I felt the rerun, and the descriptions of the girls are starting to blur together. "Two small skies where her eyes should have been" was a reach.

On Cassie: the look that passes over Vee to Randi and gets "folded away," then Vee's comfortable story about having your own people, with "something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That hurt more than anything at the smoothie bar. Vee is starting to leave Cassie behind and calling it healthy.

**The title:** "Dear" opens up after reading. It's "dear friend," the thing said aloud in front of the girls who decide what things are worth. But dear also means costly, and the chapter says outright that Randi "spent something." It also points at how dear Vee is becoming to more than one person at once. Then the last line: "She felt picked." I know she was literally picked, pointed at across a lawn in a game. Vee felt it as the best feeling in the world, and I felt it as a gut punch. The cover board, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, hits harder now. The polite invitation has been made in four voices: "May I," "Love you, girl," hiking "Sunday," "dear friend." No jacket or tagline was supplied, only the cover board. "Long spoon" makes me think of the saying about needing one to sup with the devil. I'm not putting horns on anyone. The phrase just reads to me as a warning about keeping your distance, and nobody here is keeping any.

**What I want next:** I want the hike. I want Vee to see the house and the "long table." My guess from here: the gap between what Vee thinks is happening and what is happening will close somewhere on that gravel road, and I can't tell whether that will feel like being let in or being caught. I'm dreading Cassie being left at the dorm. And I want more of the low-voiced Randi.
