## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came out of this chapter happy and uneasy at the same time. The happiness belongs to Vee. The opening dorm scene is the most purely joyful thing I've read in a while. She spins in a room too small for it and nearly takes out the closet door, then lands in the bean bag laughing "*Look at me*." I adored her. The detail I keep thinking about is what she most wants Cassie to have: "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." That's the consent I read for, said out loud and owned. Cassie is wonderful in that scene too. Her "How much did he give you to drink?" is dry, but under it she's checking, and she believes Vee once she has the answer. "Borrow good ones" is love said in three words.

Then the second half got under my skin. I know things Vee doesn't. I watched Randi send Vee across the lawn, and I watched her ask Pace "*Tell*" with her thighs tight. So when she snaps her fingers and comes up with "Paul?" I don't buy the forgetting for a second. It's a costume, and I flinched at how easily she wears it. The "catastrophe" date story told "like a story she had told before and would tell again" reads to me as cover. It may be true, but it's a tool either way. The cruelest part was the line "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you… is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else." She's describing Pace by contrast. She's selling him while sounding like she's warning against someone else.

What I won't do is make Randi only a schemer, because the page won't let me. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." She says it "slowly, holding it up to the light." That's the woman from the bench recognizing herself. It isn't just reconnaissance. Then there's "Ask and then ravish": "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That line got me. Something real slipped out of her there, and I can't tell how much of the afternoon was for Pace and how much was her own want with nowhere to go. I'm leaving it open, because the page does. The same goes for "dear friend" in front of Brooke. Vee feels it cost Randi something. I believe it did. Claiming a stats-class girl with a pilled cardigan in front of the president who stays seated is a risk. Whether she spent it on Vee or on the plan, I don't know. I suspect both, and that's what hurts.

The swoon here is the Vee/Randi current, and it works. "Two small skies where her eyes should have been, and by the second block that side of Vee's face was hot… This was not fine. This had color in it." Vee doesn't know what she's feeling and I do, and that gap is delicious and sad. The erotic peak is quiet: Vee's hands running down her thighs while she pictures the trail, and Randi dropping her voice to meet her. Vee lifting her breast to make the no-bra case ("I'd black my own eye") is funny and frank. It also put Vee's body right in front of Randi, which I suspect Randi will carry back to Pace. The heat here is in the wanting, not in anything happening.

I'm a little worried about Cassie. Her look "go past her, to Randi… whatever had been in them was folded away." And Vee's comfortable thought, "it was fine to have your own people," with "something turned over, small" underneath. I felt Vee drifting away from her best friend, and I felt a small dread that the book wants me to notice it. Vee slighting Cassie's world as "not her thing" stung a little.

Friction: hardly any. Brooke's companion, "the taller one," stays nameless and silent. That works as menace, but I wanted to know who she is. The brow passage ("the deciding had cost money") nudges the class point a bit hard after the shelf, the cardigan "like an apology," and the oat milk. And "You little minx" sounded like stock Randi-voice to me.

The title, "Dear," means *dear friend* to me now. It's tender and it's a claim of ownership, said in public with a cool damp hand. It's also the word you open a letter with, an address before the real message. The cover board says *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. The proverb I hear in it is the one about needing a long spoon to sup with the devil. This chapter is all polite invitations: Chi Latte, the Smoothie Bird, hiking, "dinner sometime." Everyone is being fed. Randi drinks something "the color of a bruise." The title makes me feel that every sweet thing handed to Vee comes on a long handle. No tagline or jacket copy was supplied.

What I want next is the hike. I want Vee seeing the house off 614 and Pace asking before he does anything. What I dread is the bench, and the moment Vee learns who Pace's girlfriend is. My guess from here is that Randi turns up somewhere Vee doesn't expect her.
