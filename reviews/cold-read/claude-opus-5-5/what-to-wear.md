## Reader reaction

Funny thing: this is the lightest, sunniest chapter in a while, and I read the whole of it with a weight in my stomach. That weight comes from what I know. Nothing on the page itself puts it there.

On the surface, nothing could be warmer. A gold dining hall, girls stealing fries off each other's trays, Kayla's chatter "you climbed onto rather than followed," and Meg, who "followed the Pace situation in installments like her shows." I liked being here. Vee at this table is the Vee I've wanted to see. She's "laughing with her whole body, loud and easy, the kind that made the next table look over and then want in." This isn't the girl in the cardigan hearing her mother say *floozies*. She has friends who aren't Randi and aren't Cassie, and with them she's funny and brags a little ("Almost finished already, which is obnoxious"). When she can't hide that she counted the days, "Twelve," I laughed with them. Her giddiness is catching. "It's the least information a human being has ever sent me and I have read it a thousand times." I felt her heart "doing something quick and light and stupid, and she let it." I let mine do it too, for a second.

Then the last chapter catches up with me. Two nights ago Pace was underneath Randi saying "Her stomach is soft," and Randi came on it. Tonight Vee is telling the shirt story to her friends and quietly leaves out the middle. Meg even says it: "You keep getting to it and going soft and skipping the middle." The middle is that she took her wet shirt off in front of him on purpose and gave him her body to see. She keeps that to herself. Pace kept part of it to himself too, how he knew. But he still handed Randi the fact of it. So Vee's most private moment has already been passed on, and she's guarding it in a place where it no longer needs guarding. That's what gives this chapter its ache for me.

Then there's "He asks me first." Vee is "climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner." I believe her, and I believe him. He does ask. Everything I've seen of him tells me the asking is real. That's why the house frightens me. It has the bench in it, sitting under a tabletop that looks like a long table. And there's a girl who locks the deadbolt first thing, who said "I want us" and then didn't stay. Vee doesn't know Randi has ever been through that door. Meg's "Everybody's different at the start… Sometimes they even are" lands like a quiet warning. Vee "let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." It caught on me.

How I feel about Pace here, seen only through Vee's telling: still warm, still trusting what he does, but uneasy about the plan he's part of. "Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." Kayla calls it "a ransom note," and that's a joke. But he sent no "what for" and no "what to wear," and for this man, who always gives the choice back, that reads as either very shy or very deliberate. I honestly can't tell which. Randi is absent, never even mentioned, and her absence is loud. Vee talks about her love life for pages and never brings up the friend who told her to go out with him. Cassie isn't here either. I noticed that.

The erotic charge is low, but it's there. "He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes." Vee runs her palms down the tops of her thighs. Her hand holds her own arm up near the shoulder without her knowing. The flannel sits folded on her pillow. It's wanting in the waiting room.

Friction: not much. One moment made me trip. "'So what's Pace do, then?' Kayla said, Theo already forgotten" comes right after a line that is also Kayla's, and it reads like a seam. The Theo bit is charming, though it may just be texture. "They're all nice until you don't want them and then they're wonderful" is a great line.

The chapter title, "What to Wear," started as the girls' question. After reading, it points at something darker. At Pace's house, what she wears may not matter long. And he didn't tell her, the way he picks her food but didn't pick this. There's also the russet dress she gave up on, which keeps hovering in my mind. The cover reads *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, and this chapter is the polite invitation, almost word for word: a courteous text, a time, nothing more. "With a long spoon" calls up the old saying about supping with the devil. With "there's no meal at two" and all the feeding in this book, that pricks. A guess from here: Saturday is the bench, or Randi, or both. I dread Vee walking in alone, unknowing, in whatever Kayla curls her hair for. I'm completely hooked.
