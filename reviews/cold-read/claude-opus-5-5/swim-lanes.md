## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one went straight into my body and then left me cold in an open doorway, which may be the point. The early part is so physical: Pace stiff from the rain, the shower "until the cold came out of his back," the open refrigerator. Then the car comes "low and expensive" over the gravel, the deadbolt goes over, and she's on him before he gets past the kitchen. I didn't need a name. The citrus "and the cold thing under it," the black hair falling around his face, the lock: it's Randi. The freckles across her chest snagged me for half a second, because I've had freckles filed under Vee, but nothing on the page says Randi can't have them. I'll leave it there.

The undressing is the best of its kind I've read in a while: "Every garment was an interruption." His hands keep getting knocked off her by sleeves and straps and keep going back. She laughs through her nose at him. Then she takes control. She looks at his face, not at what she's holding, and lets herself down an inch at a time with stops, so he has to watch. "She had earned it and she knew it." I liked her so much there: confident and greedy, not hiding behind the face.

Then "Tell me," and he gives her Vee by smell. Gardenia and musk, "on her throat, and lower," the shampoo, "a hint of chlorine. Like a pool." That landed hard on me. I'd carried the pool chapter as the one place Vee's body wasn't a problem, and now that smell is being delivered into a bed she doesn't know exists. It's the hottest moment in the chapter, and part of why is that it feels slightly stolen. Then "Her stomach is soft," and Randi clamps down, eyes shut, not moving, long enough that he says her name. Then she rides him with her belly "tight as a board." Pace takes it for want. The page lets him take it that way, adds "If it was something else he could not have said what," and so I can't say either. It could be want, grief, jealousy, or all of them fused. Her laugh "came out of her thin, not her laugh." I felt that in my stomach.

"I want us." It went through me the way it went through him, "like the first swallow of something," and I truly don't know what it means. Us, the two of them, against Vee? Us, the three? Us, as something she's afraid of losing? I'm leaving it open, and it's the most romantic and the most unsettling line here.

Then she leaves. No lying loose on his chest. She says no to food, brightly. "See you." The empty chair by the door, set aside at the start, comes back and stings: no monogrammed bag, so she'd planned not to stay. Pace's explanation ("It was the want... more than she could lie still under") is exactly as far as he can see, and the chapter admits that. The "low and plain" hurt that she hadn't wanted to stay made me feel for him more than I have before. He also never got to answer "How was it?", and she never asked again. That told me she didn't want the trail. She wanted only the parts that went into her.

On trust: Pace keeps the truck moment for himself ("he kept it") while telling Randi Vee's stomach is soft. That's an odd split. He thinks he's guarding Vee while giving Randi her body piece by piece. I'm not suspicious of him exactly, but my unease about Vee as the one being talked about went up, and the page earned it. Randi I trust less as a narrator of herself than ever, but I ache for her. The ending, with him eating at the table and still hungry so he makes more while she wouldn't eat at all, is quiet and devastating.

About the titles: "Swim Lanes" points back to the chlorine and to parallel tracks that never cross. Pace in his lane, Randi in hers, Vee in a pool she thinks is private. Everyone keeps something ("he kept it"; she kept her after-time for herself and then skipped it). The cover gives no tagline, only *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. The long spoon reads more pointedly after this: you keep your distance from what you're eating with. Randi is feeding on Vee at arm's length, through Pace. "A Polite Invitation" still feels like what's being extended to Vee, very nicely. My guess from here is that Randi's composure cracks before the invitation is ever delivered, and that her leaving tonight was the first crack.
