## Reader reaction

**Water Wings — Reader reaction**

This chapter let me breathe, and I didn't know I'd been holding my breath. After the bench, the mirror, Randi finishing the wine in one swallow, I'm suddenly in a rec-pool locker room with Cassie fighting her suit "like it had insulted her family." "Who designed this? Genuinely. What man?" made me laugh out loud. I've liked Cassie since that flat "Goodbye, Vee," but there I mostly trusted her. Here I love her. "Tossers. They will never know the love of a woman," said low and kept for Vee alone, when she "could have pitched it up into the bleachers," is the most purely kind moment the book has given me. Then there's the water she drives at Vee to break the mood. She doesn't ask, because "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger." She just starts a splash fight. And she takes the wave full in the chest "and did not so much as change expression." I wanted to be in the pool with them.

Vee moved me more here than at Chi Latte. There she was all dazzled wanting, and I liked her and worried for her. Here I got her body from the inside. She's wearing the suit from sixteen "when there'd been less of her to fill it," and taking the bleachers "like an entrance." Then she goes under, "The room switched off," and the line that really got me: "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." Her grief there is small and exact, and it isn't explained. I felt it in my chest. The catcalls are rendered without fuss: one long blink, one eyeroll, and the cost only shows when the "fifty yards of held breath" go out on Cassie's joke. That cost felt earned.

The shower section is where the heat comes, quietly. Her shoulders are cold where the towel stops while the rest of her holds the shower's heat, and she's thinking of "His hands, the knuckles thick on them... which was really not the place." It's a simmer, not a burn, but it's real and it's hers, and "She got her sweats on pretty fast after that" is charming. She wants him, and she's honest about it while still being fair: "He wasn't gorgeous." I like her for being fair.

That brings me to the unease, which I think the text has fully earned. I know things Vee doesn't. I know Randi steered that collision on the lawn. I know Pace went home and told Randi about the froyo cup while she sat in his lap with his hands on her breasts. So when Vee says to herself, "He'd helped her with her calc problem, gently, and just enough," the "just enough" goes cold in my hands. Then there's "Don't answer tonight." To Vee it's endearingly odd, and she liked it "more than she'd have admitted to anybody." To me it reads like the bench work: the waiting, the not-yet, making her hold her yes in her mouth. I don't know that it's cruel. Pace has been careful about consent in every scene I've seen, and his "I won't be upset" on the bench was sincere. But what he's doing to her is a technique, and she can't know that. Watching her wind the wet curl around her finger over and over, I felt protective and a little complicit at once. I'm not suspicious of Cassie at all. If anything she's the only person around Vee who isn't arranging her.

Some friction. The ending confused me: the thread holds "just his one text, still asking whether she liked frozen yogurt." So she never texted back? Then how did they set up the froyo, by calling? Or is the point that she's never actually typed a word to him? I tripped on it. It's a small thing, but it pulled me out right at the last image. And "the splashing won. It always did, back before any of it" is lovely, but "before any of it" waves at a history I don't have. I'll leave that open.

**The titles.** "Water Wings" points straight at the catcall about floaties, and the chapter quietly turns it around: in the water Vee needs nothing to hold her up. On land, Cassie is the thing keeping her afloat. On second thought it also puts a shadow over the dinner: Vee is a strong swimmer about to go somewhere deeper than she knows, and she thinks she's fine. The cover reads *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. The long spoon calls up the old saying about who you'd need one to sup with, and this chapter is literally a polite invitation: "would she have dinner with him." After "May I call you?" and now "Don't answer tonight," the politeness itself is starting to feel like the long spoon. Who is supping with whom, I can't say yet.

**What I want next.** I want to stay with Vee and Cassie. I dread the Saturday dinner and want it anyway. My guess from here is that she'll send the yes before she's supposed to, or hold it exactly as long as he wants and not know he wanted that. I also want to know what Cassie saw that day when she watched Randi texting.
