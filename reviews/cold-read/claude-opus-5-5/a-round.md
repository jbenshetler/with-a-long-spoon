## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter swept me up, and I want to say that before anything else. It's long, and it keeps returning to the same rhythm of closeness, two touches, withdrawal, pencil. It earned every bit of that length, because the repetition is how it works on her, and it worked on me the same way. By the inside seam I was holding my breath along with her. "*Can he tell?*" in her mother's register made me feel the shame in my own stomach, and it arrived *as* heat, not instead of it. The writing is very good on the soaked satin: "the hope was visible. The hope was sized to be seen across the room." That stung, and it was hot. I didn't expect those two things to sit so close together.

The turn is what undid me: she opens her eyes and he's craned all the way up, waiting for her face, and "He hadn't looked down." I melted. The physical rearranging after that is gorgeous, the stoop coming undone and her rising onto her toes without meaning to. Then his "*hmm*" and the sideways look at her heel, a tiny private joke between them. Then she starts spending her new power, and "Oops. Sorry," with her breast held against his cheek, made me grin out loud. I loved watching Vee find out she's a person who can make a careful man go red. Her "pre-aroused every time I wore it" line was the funniest thing in the book so far, and it's what makes her feel whole: funny and brave right in the middle of her shame.

Pace, on the page, is close to ideal. He's patient, delighted by her, and he struggles visibly over "Keep still," which comes "a beat too late," with the cost showing. The pin-prick kiss is lovely. The small touches after it, like the finger at the back of her knee and the palm down her spine, are deniable and both of them know it. That's where the erotic charge peaks for me: the restraint becoming a game they're both in.

But I can't read it innocently, and the book itself took that innocence from me two chapters ago. In "Swim Lanes" he lay under Randi and gave her "gardenia and musk" and "Her stomach is soft." So when Vee thinks he "walked right past Randi, Randi!, without even looking," I winced at her. I know he didn't walk past Randi at all. I also can't help wondering what tonight's report will be, and whether the wet satin becomes Randi's next "*Tell.*" To be fair to him, the page has also shown him keeping things back from Randi, like the private thing on the walk and how he knew about her stomach. So I don't know what he'll hand over, and I'm leaving that open. My unease isn't about anything in this room. It's about everything around it, and I think the earlier chapter earned it. Within this chapter, I trust him with her body completely. What I don't trust is the arrangement she can't see.

Randi is absent, and I felt that absence. Her leaving his bed early, and "I want us," sit under this chapter like a bruise.

A few points of friction. "Wryly." on its own as a sentence is the author nudging me. "The whole furniture of shame" works, but the floozie and stripper beats, plus the swimsuit backstory, are starting to repeat what earlier chapters already established. The braced-for-ridicule paragraph ("She knew that grin... No, not grinning. Smiling.") felt like a feint the author built just to correct it. And the last lines spell out the lesson ("had not, once, been found wanting") a little more than they needed to after that eyes-opening moment.

**Titles.** "A Round" first made me think of the tape going *around* her waist, then the roundness of her body, which the chapter refuses to treat as a problem. On second thought, it also reads like a round in music, a melody repeated and overlapping, or a round of drinks passed along. The rhythm of the chapter is a round. And if this afternoon is going to be sung again later in Randi's ear, that's a darker kind of round. The cover's *With a Long Spoon* still sounds like a warning: you sup with something dangerous and keep your distance. *A Polite Invitation* has never fit better than it does here, with "would you come to my house Saturday," the tea, the turned-up heat. Everything about it is courteous, and I can't yet tell what the courtesy is for.

**What I want, expect, and dread next:** I want the dress finished and her wearing it. I dread the scene where Pace goes home to Randi, or Randi comes to him. My guess from here is that the closed bedroom door, and whatever sits under a tabletop in this house, are still waiting. I can't stop reading.
