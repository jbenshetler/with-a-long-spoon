## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I'm grinning through the first stretch. Vee is underslept, sore and "fit to burst," and she drops into the seat between her two girls. Cassie's opener, "Your bed's made. It's the same bed you made," is the funniest and most loving line in the chapter. Then comes the forty seconds where Vee looks up from the baseball numbers and realizes Cassie never looked at the board at all. She was watching what the dress had done to her friend. That got me. Cassie keeps quietly becoming the person in this book I trust most, and her "Was it a good night?", answered with "Oh, yes," then the highlighter going back up, is all the tenderness she lets out. That small ease in her face moved me more than the boutique did.

Randi is harder, and better. Every warm beat she gives Vee lands on me twice, because I remember her at Pace's table asking "How long, Pace?" and calling his patience "selfish." So when "for once there was nothing at all on her face" in the lecture hall, and later when her hand rests on the shoulder seam and "the breath ran long," I feel the pressure behind it. Vee reads it as Randi being jealous *of him*, jealous of a man who sits up nights over her. That guess is so generous and so lonely that it hurt to read. I don't know what Randi's quiet is. It could be envy, longing, impatience, or grief at being left out of a Friday she helped set in motion. The page doesn't settle it and I won't either. What I'm sure of is that "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you" is a true, perceptive gift. Whatever else she's doing, she sees Vee.

The heat in this chapter comes from the women, and it got me. "You lucky slut" lands "lower than Randi meant it." Vee's body keeps "the night's account" in her thighs and nipples, and she's sure the room can read it. The shoulder seam. And then the thumb: Randi kneeling on the pale floor in her beautiful clothes, holding Vee's foot to the light, looking at the frosted plum, "Mm," one thumb across the nail of the big toe, and "the floor of her tip." Vee's hand shoots out to Randi's shoulder to steady herself. That's the peak, and I felt it in my own stomach. The plum polish was chosen for Pace, for the silk, and Randi has now noticed it at two tables and touched it on her knees. Randi's quiet "That's me exactly. Same feet, baby… it's practically a marriage" is played for comedy, and it's also exactly what it says.

The shoe-store sequence wins me over as plain friendship too. There's the yeti canoe, the pink rhinestone six-inch platforms ("Do these go with burgundy?") and the flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." Vee feeling "like the funniest woman in Virginia" is a joy I don't want to spoil. The couple by the boots watching them, "a little flush, there and gone," is a nice catch.

The unease is real, though, and the page earns it with money rather than menace. I winced when the debit card went back into the wallet and the emergency credit card came out, "There was nothing under it." Randi's loafers ring up at a multiple of Vee's heels and she never glances at the number. Her line "It's not going to *spoil*" makes Vee laugh, and it also stings a little, because Vee really is holding the box like it might leave her. I don't read malice in it. I do read a girl being walked, warmly, one *yeah* at a time, past her own limits. That rhymes with the lunch where Randi led Vee down the dress fitting and then shut the door on her. Vee's reasoning, "They were what the dress was owed," is self-deception I recognize and like her for, and I'm scared for her Thursday.

Friction: Simpson's paradox is heavy-handed. "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them" is the author tapping the glass. Each pairing taken on its own reads one way, and added together they flip. I got it the first time, and the red dot on the column made me roll my eyes a little. The Mercedes window detail being replayed also felt like filler.

On the titles: "Sorority" first reads as Randi's world, her habit of being early, her "four years." By the end it means something closer to sisterhood made by choice: Vee between two women, Randi's "practically a marriage," and the cover's promise doubling back on itself. Nothing was given away. The cover board, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, keeps echoing in me. To me a long spoon means supping with someone dangerous, and every invitation in this book has been very polite: "Come on. I know just the place." There's no tagline in my packet. The title tells me the dinner is being served and nobody has mentioned the spoon yet.

What I want next: Vee walking into the induction late in the dress and these heels. What I dread: the credit card bill, and Randi's patience running out before Pace's does. A guess from here: the shoe date is Randi's way around Pace's "on her own."
