## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter got to me, and the last page hurt. Most of it is two people under a quilt with cold tea, talking, and I was fully inside it. The opening is a warm room facing a cold window: "the cold stood on the other side of the glass and leaned in." I settled in right away. Then Pace's hand rests on her breast "without pressing, without asking, as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm." It's affectionate rather than sexual, and it charged me more than some of the explicit chapters have.

Vee is wonderful here. She's funny without performing it: "Whatever was in it may have been old enough to vote." The unopened spice-rack seals keeping "the stubborn dignity of things no one in the house meant to discover" are beautifully done. Her mother defends herself before anyone accuses her of anything, and the line hits because the floozie voice still sits in my memory. I loved that Pace "went first to the hand underneath the thing." He calls the casseroles "affectionate," and that one word won me over to him again, which surprised me. His quick read of her name, "the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other," shows his kind of attention working on her past instead of her body.

Then the softness turns. "My real name is Peter." He leaves his mother out of the answer, and Vee lets it go. The detail of his desk being moved into the hall turned my stomach. Vee says, "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment," and he looks at her "as if she had put her hand on a place he had not offered her." She stays quiet afterward instead of saying sorry, and that restraint is what made me love her in this chapter. His math speech is a little long, and the coastline and Newton parts drift toward lecture. But the closing line, "Most people walk around inside the poem," pulled me back, and so did her moment of seeing the stars as moving bodies. "It was almost grief" was the line that made the speech work for me.

Daphne is the center of the chapter, and it landed in my body before my mind, the same way it did for Vee. She was nineteen and he was fifteen, for two years. "Nobody did anything wrong." He speaks in that careful voice, like someone carrying a full glass. Vee holds back "the clean ugly grown-up word," and I held it back with her, though I was also saying it in my head. Then the line I'll carry forward: "he had handed her the thing that had hurt him and named it luck." That rearranges what I remember from the first chapter, where Daphne "had built a door" that he "had not respected." His version tonight is that she left for sensible reasons and he did nothing wrong. Those two accounts don't fit together neatly, and I'm leaving the gap open. I don't know whose door was crossed, or by whom. The scar's "Not tonight" is a third door. I liked that it came as "a door with a time on it," not a wall. He keeps her hand on the scar, and that's tender.

The lifting is the closest thing to heat in the chapter. "Only this lift, this heartbeat." Vee realizes "that's how you are with me… It's the same face." I felt the warmth of that. Later I felt a faint chill: being completely present in the moment is also how a man might keep the future out of view. That's my unease, not anything the page says.

The last page is where my unease comes from, and the page earns it. Vee lies awake counting her luck: "I had stumbled into her," the man who "crossed a coffee shop for it," "for no reason she had done anything to deserve," "all of a piece, the one and then the other." I know the collision was staged, and I watched Randi text right before Pace showed up. Hearing Pace's "one piece" line about Newton turned into Vee's belief that both people came to her by chance was painful. Even so, it doesn't make the chapter's tenderness false. He seems truly present with her, and she's truly happy. Both things are true, and that's the sadness of it.

On the titles: I can't settle "Rock." It could be the solid ground he seems to her. It could be the iron bar in the squat, or rocking someone to sleep, or the ridge outside the glass. It feels oblique to me, and it gave nothing away. The book's title, *With a Long Spoon*, has always made me think of the proverb about supping with the devil. Tonight Vee is fed with the shortest spoon possible: cumin, his kitchen, his robe. *A Polite Invitation* sits on top of Vee's sense that she simply stumbled into all of it. I dread what comes next, and I want it. My guess from here is that Randi's pressure for the threesome will push in soon, and Vee will meet it with all this trust in place.
