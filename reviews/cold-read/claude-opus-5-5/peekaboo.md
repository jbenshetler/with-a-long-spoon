## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction: "Peekaboo"**

I came into this straight from "Portion": Randi's untouched bowl with one line drawn through the sauce, *It's selfish, Pace*, her back turned to him in the cashmere. Then this chapter opens on Vee driving out midweek with a "low warm hum," and none of that reaches her. That gap was the first thing I felt, before any of the heat. It's a weekday afternoon, and Randi had just complained that he has Vee "Friday to Sunday." Vee doesn't know any of it, so the whole chapter glows on her side and feels faintly exposed on mine. The last line, "getting away with something," landed hard because of that. She means the dress, the slit, the bare leg. I heard Randi's empty bowl in the fridge.

Inside the chapter, though, I was swept along, and I won't pretend I wasn't. The dress reveal got me. Silk lit from behind in the window, the pedestal moved to face a mirror with "the screw heads still bright," and Vee with her hand over her mouth. The line that opened me up was her realizing "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble." It's the same thing as the shirt with no tag, and now I understand it as something about him: care that erases its own seams. Her crying, "the dumb happy kind that always beat her to it," felt true. So did his "Yes," with no shrug in it.

The mirror scene is where Vee keeps growing, and I love watching it. *Floozie* flares up, "and she stayed anyway, made herself look," and the looking turns into "a lot of woman who knew exactly how much she was." Then comes the slit and the pale edge of her underwear crossing it. Her first instinct is to take small steps so it won't show. Her second is to take the underwear off. That's the title, I think: the peek of fabric, then the leg given and taken back with every stride, peekaboo. I liked that she's the one who "took the edge away," and that the shame "crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all." Over these chapters the shame hasn't vanished. It has turned into fuel, and that feels earned.

The erotic charge is very high, and what surprised me is how much of it is comic. The standing sex in the den, then "Wait. The dress." The whole relay after that: "Ow. Don't point down." "There's a lot of you covered in a lot of me," and his twitch at it, her laughing until her leg slides off his hip. It's warm, silly sex between two people who like each other, and I believed every awkward second. Then it snaps back into real heat. She wipes her hands down his flanks. "Are they clean enough?" "Closer." He puts her fingers in his mouth, and that went through my knees too. In the bedroom she holds him off with her plum-painted feet on his chest, then "Well?" "I'm looking." "I noticed." He goes slow on purpose, "and she felt what the holding was costing him." Consent here isn't a checkpoint. It's built into how they play. She sets the pace and he takes hers. The one place it went slack for me was the climax paragraph. "The headboard talking" is a bit stock after everything before it was so specific.

On Pace, I'm warmer than ever toward the man Vee sees. He was up all night finishing the dress, he goes down "hard and fast" afterward, and his arm comes around her in his sleep. I don't distrust his tenderness with her. What I've carried in from "Portion" is that he truly can't see what he's doing to Randi, and this afternoon is that blindness in action. He set up a mirror and a pedestal for Vee and doesn't seem to wonder how this lands with the woman at the other end of the game. That's not suspicion of Vee's scene. It's dramatic irony the earlier chapters set up. Randi isn't in this chapter at all, and her absence is loud.

On friction: the shoe paragraph runs a little long. The pumps "would have read like an apology," and "that woman could wait" feels like it's planting something. My guess from here is that shoes are coming, from him or from Randi. The closing induction daydream ("the way the room would turn. She let it turn") is lovely but a bit tidy, a bow on top. The oyster lining as "a soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" is gorgeous and I want to hold on to it.

The cover still reads *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation.* "He'd asked her over for the afternoon and left the rest of it to her" is literally a polite invitation, and so was "would you come to my house Saturday." The long spoon, the old saying about supping with the devil, keeps sitting over these scenes of being fed, dressed and made. Nothing on this page earns that shadow by itself. It comes from "Portion" and from what Vee doesn't know. I want the induction. I dread the next lunch with Randi, now that Randi is tired of waiting.
