## Reader reaction

I came into this one still raw from the bench, so the cold of the sorority house hit me hard. His house is warm, "the even heat he kept the whole house at." Hers "did not hold its heat; the corners were always cold." She's under a "light and synthetic and adequate" duvet when she owns a cashmere blanket that lives at his place. That small "It was not — it was a duvet" got to me. She almost lets herself want something and then pulls back.

Brooke scared me, and the chapter earned it. Nothing she says is unkind. She's warm, "a voice the way other people had a perfume," and every question is a little cut. "Pace. That's actually his name?" "Engineering, isn't it" puts him a notch down. "He doesn't *teach*, does he" is a tripwire, and Brooke lays it with a laugh. Then "I'm sure you want to shower" sends Randi upstairs before she can use the three lines she'd put together. Brooke never stands up. I believe she runs that house. I can't tell what she knows or what she wants with it. Maybe she's just protecting the chapter, maybe it's more personal, and the page doesn't say. What I'm sure of is that she was taking notes: "Pace," said Brooke, "like she was filing it." Sloane's compliment, "I love that you don't care about cars," takes the white pickup and the man who drives it down a notch while sounding like praise. Kinsey's "short-cute" stung me for Pace. I've watched this man build furniture and lean his whole attention toward her, and here he gets shrunk to a cheap truck and a height, and Randi's laugh matches Kinsey's pitch before she's had time to choose it. I didn't hate her for that. I felt for her. The whole chapter is that reflex, the face doing its work.

And Randi herself moved me more here than she did in the mirror scene, because now we see the face at work in daylight and it's running a beat late. "Randi's smile arranged. The arrangement was a fraction late." That phrase keeps coming back, with the gap a little wider each time, and I felt my own breath catch in those gaps. Then the lie about the paper and the place on Wilson. Brooke "basically lived there sophomore year," which might just be chat or might be a hint that she can check. I'm leaving that open. Then there's her own title. She sits on the board as Standards, so she's the one who judges the other girls, and she's standing in last night's clothes being judged. I don't need the irony spelled out and the page doesn't spell it out.

Upstairs is where the chapter really got me. She puts the phone facedown without reading the two notifications. She goes to the closet with her hand half raised and can't remember what she came for. She lies down in her shoes. "*He's hard to say no to,* she heard, in her room, in a different key. It was not a key she could sing in." That line went through me. At first she said it as a cute line, and now it sounds like something closer to fear. Fear of him? I don't think so. Fear of what she turned into when she said yes to him, maybe. The page stops "where the words stopped," and I'll stop there too. The last paragraph, the not-crying that "did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door," is heartbreaking. Last night she locked a door for the first time. Now she's learned to need no door at all. It feels like the vow from the mirror coming true.

Pace isn't on the page, and I missed him the way you miss heat in a cold room. My trust in him hasn't moved. If anything, watching the girls make him small made me protective of him. I'm suspicious of Brooke and wary of the house. I'm not suspicious of him.

There's no erotic charge here, really, and it isn't trying for any. The only heat is a leftover: the wrinkled cardigan, silk showing on the second step, a body that was somewhere else last night. Whatever is warm here is remembered, and it makes the cold sharper.

One bit of friction: "a woman who would one day be some CEO's third wife" felt like the narrator sneering at Brooke rather than Randi seeing her. It's funny, but I felt the author's thumb on it. The repeated "fraction-of-a-second" also comes close to being a device, though I think it earns its place by widening each time.

The title, "Standards," works three ways for me. It's her board seat, it's the scale the house measures by (cars, height, engineering versus math, the girls "built to the one scale"), and it's the standard she's failing by being seen. It didn't give anything away. It made the chapter darker once I'd finished it. The cover gives me *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. "A polite invitation" now fits Brooke's warm grilling as well as Pace's open door, since both are polite and both come with a price. "Long spoon" suggests eating with something dangerous at a safe distance, which is what Randi has just decided to do with Pace, maybe, or what the house does with her. That's my guess from here. There's no tagline in what I was given.

What do I want? I want her to read those notifications. I dread her going back to Pace's house wearing the perfect face while he, warm and unsuspecting, meets only the surface. I'm also afraid of Brooke's next move, now that she's filed his name. Guess from here: the teaching question comes back.
