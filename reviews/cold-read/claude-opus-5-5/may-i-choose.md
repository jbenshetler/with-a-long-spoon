# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-opus-5-5 · memory: raw ch001..ch007 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter took me in completely, and I let it. The early beats are lovely. *I'd love to.* is the shortest of her drafts and the one she sends. His reply comes back flat and simple, and she clutches the phone to her chest anyway. Cassie's "That's a whole face" made me laugh. My favourite moment is the elevator doors timing out on Vee while she stands there looking at him, with her hand thrown out "begging the doors for mercy." It's the most purely charming thing in the book so far. I'm completely on Vee's side. She's funny about the menu ("beautiful nonsense. I have no map"), she's honest that she likes Carole King, and she has a hunger that "had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line hurt. So did the readiness she drops at the table, "the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due." I wanted her to have this night.

Here's my problem. I know things Vee doesn't. I watched Pace point her out on the quad and picture "that mouth at Randi's breast." I watched Randi drive up to his gravel already wet, rehearsing Vee's cardigan buttons. So every gentle thing he does here reads two ways for me. "May I choose?" is a beautiful, courteous question, and it's also exactly the gesture Randi uses to hand him her evenings. Vee feels "the relief of handing the choice across the table... more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would," and I felt it with her and flinched at the same time. The book earned that unease; I didn't go looking for it. Then he feeds her. On Randi's first night he fed her apple slices and later thought of her as "a girl who had been portioning herself... who tonight had finally let someone feed her." Now there's Vee, with her mother's "that's plenty," and the hollow opening wider. He seems to find the hungry women and answer the hunger. Whether that's tenderness, technique, or both, the page doesn't say, and I'm leaving it open. I don't think he's faking it. In "Tannin" he held back Vee's embarrassing true thing from Randi ("It was hers"), and Christine's warmth toward him reads as real. What troubles me is the setup around him: Vee doesn't know she was chosen on a lawn for someone else's bed.

Randi is missing from the page, and I could feel her absence the whole time. I kept wondering where she was tonight and what she'd ask him when he got home. Cassie's "That's not a boy. That's a man" is warm and correct, and it also made me ache a little. Cassie, who said "Goodbye, Vee" in the lecture hall, is the one person close to this who isn't playing.

The heat here is slow and hand-centred, and it worked on me. Vee watches his right hand all night: the bread torn "with care for the heat," the drip of baba ghanoush licked off the side of his hand in mid-sentence. "She lost the thread of what he was saying" landed hard. That's desire as a loss of attention, which is exactly how it feels. The kiss is very well judged. First she states her rule. Then he smiles and lets it close, she stays planted, and the held hand pulls taut between them. "But I'm making an exception." He answers "no more than she'd brought" and leaves the last inch to her, and then she takes it. As consent, it's impeccable. Her choice is clean, and I believe it. I just also know whose game she's choosing inside. Feeling him smile into the kiss melted me.

Some friction. The right-hand passage over-explains ("a pattern now, enough times over to be one: always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept"). I got the point the first time. The walk-and-talk section ("the walk and the talk the same thing") goes soft on purpose, which mostly works, but the middle of dinner sags with abstraction ("not, for once, anywhere except exactly where she was"). And "something that was only him and that she had no name for either" repeats the book's no-name-for-it move once too often. Randi had it, Vee had it at Chi Latte, and now Vee has it again.

On the titles: "May I Choose" is gorgeous and a little frightening once you've read the chapter. It's a request to take her choice, asked so politely that handing it over feels like relief. Then the chapter turns it around: "Whatever came next was hers too." The cover makes sense now. *A Polite Invitation* is "May I call you?" and "May I choose?" *With a Long Spoon* points me straight at the old saying about supping with the devil, and this chapter is literally a supper. He feeds her and she eats from his hand's example. The title gave nothing specific away, but it made the warmth feel dangerous in hindsight. I'm not calling Pace the devil. The cover is the one making that suggestion, and the page hasn't confirmed it.

What I want next: more of Vee getting fed in every sense. What I dread: the moment Randi's interest and Pace's tenderness meet in one room, and whether anyone tells Vee what she walked into. *Guess from here:* Pace goes home and reports to Randi, and Randi's held-level face comes back.
