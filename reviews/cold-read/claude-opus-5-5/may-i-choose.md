# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

The first half of this chapter made me grin. The whole of it frightened me a little. And for long stretches I forgot to be frightened, which may be the most honest thing I can say about it.

I loved the opening. Vee types the long text over and over, cuts it down each time, and sends *I'd love to.* His answer, *I'll meet you in your lobby at seven*, is so plain that it eases "something behind her ribs." Then she says of him, "He just left it with me. Like it could keep," and Cassie, who teases her about everything, doesn't tease her about this. "That's not a boy. That's a man." Cassie's earlier "Goodbye, Vee" is still in my ear, and I couldn't tell whether this was a blessing or a quiet sizing-up. I'm leaving that open, because the page does.

The lobby scene got me. Vee forgets to step out of the elevator, the doors start to close, and she throws out a hand "begging the doors for mercy." It's funny and true, and I felt warmly toward both of them for it. He's "somewhere in himself" until she appears. He's showered, he's still pink from the razor, he's wearing the boots he actually looks after. I believed in his care.

Then the restaurant, which is the real heart of this chapter. Vee hasn't eaten since breakfast because the dress and the other dress ate her afternoon. The hunger "kept going down and down into something that had been empty a lot longer than since noon." That line landed in my body. I thought straight back to the apple, sliced thin and laid cut-side up for Randi, the appetite kept "trimmed to almost nothing." I thought of Vee's froyo cup, with her mother's *that's plenty* gone quiet for once. Here she eats "and didn't notice she'd stopped" watching herself. It was a deeply sensual pleasure to read: the bread steaming as it tears, sharp lemon and then smoke, the drip of dip he licks off the side of his hand while she loses the thread of what he's saying. That was the erotic peak for me. It wasn't a kiss. It was her watching his hand go to his mouth and letting the conversation fall without scrambling to pick it up.

This is where my unease comes from, and I think the book has earned it. "May I choose?" She feels "the relief of handing the choice across the table," and the narration calls it a surrender. "She let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could." I've watched Randi hand him "the evening," "the day," hand him everything, and I know where that went for her: a bench, begging, the face going back on for good. I also know something Vee doesn't. Two nights ago Randi sat astride him with a glass of wine saying *Tell*, and he told her about the froyo cup. So when Vee feels her "low background readiness" for the bill switch off, I felt it switch on in me. The bill exists. She just can't see the table it's sitting on.

And yet I can't call him false. He kept her "true thing" from Randi. He says "Christine" like a name he's glad to say. His joke that he keeps going back to get even and is "losing" is charming and sounds real. "Third, badly" made me like him. I don't think he's lying to Vee. What sits under the date is the point-and-pick on the quad, not any falseness in him. That doubleness is what's keeping me in the book.

The kiss was lovely. "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days." He accepts that without leaning in, just a smile, and starts walking her on. Her feet stay put, the joined hands pull tight, and she says "But... I'm making an exception." He leaves "the last inch where it was," she takes it, and he only meets her fully on the second kiss. The consent is impeccable, and that's exactly why it's unsettling: he's very good at leaving room, and Randi walked into that room too. Feeling him "smile inside the kiss" with mint still in both their mouths made me want this for her. I wanted it for both of them, against what I know.

Some friction. "The care ran past anything the room could see, down to the part of him she'd only get close enough to smell" pushes too hard. The hand that "had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly" leans on the scale; I felt the author's thumb there. "A rule that had never been breached before" is flat. The right-hand-only detail is nice, but it gets underlined twice, "a pattern now, enough times over to be one." I understood the first time.

The chapter title gave nothing away beforehand. Afterward it rings: *May I call you* in the coffee shop, now *May I choose*. His courtesy is a question every time, and every time she says "Yes. Please," the same words she used at the coffee shop. The cover, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, hits harder after this chapter. Tonight was nothing but a polite invitation, and she sat down to sup. My guess from here is that Randi hears every bit of this, maybe even the kiss under the tree, and that what Vee was told she could leave with him will be taken out and handled by someone else. I dread that and I want to read it.
