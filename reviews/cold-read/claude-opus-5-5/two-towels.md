## Reader reaction

**Two Towels — reader reaction**

I came out of this chapter swept up and a little shaky, the way you are when a gift is too big and too exact. The burgundy silk got me. First there's the small letdown, "*oh, it's not the right—*". Then he holds the corner up to her face "as you hold a paint chip to a wall," and she sees in the mirror that "he hadn't matched the dress she'd described. He'd matched *her*." My chest did what hers did. Her near-crying didn't feel cheap: "No one had ever—" with no end to it. She threw that dress away on the summit, half-embarrassed, and he picked it up the way he picked up the cherry pit. The rhyme with the last hike landed on me without being pointed at. "You made the shirt" was a lovely click. The missing tag, the seams that lie flat. She "filed it under some category that made no sense," and so had I.

I like Vee more every chapter. "I missed you" before anything else, and the kiss she'd decided to take "somewhere back on the paved road, before she lost the turn." That's her being brave, and it's her. "I didn't realize you knew Sanskrit" made me laugh. Pace was tender here, almost boyish: lit up "like a six-year-old" about the MRI, back from it "like he'd surfaced from somewhere." The error-correcting-codes speech moved me in a way I didn't expect: "Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it." Then there's the small closing at *parents*, "a door easing shut two rooms away." That's the first wound I've seen on him besides Daphne. I want to know what it is.

The heat is all held back, and it works. He's the one who ends the kiss: "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop". Her attention sharpens in a way that felt like arousal and unease at once. Then the satin under her clothes, worn "on purpose," and her leaning toward the shut bedroom door, waiting for his hand to go to the handle. It doesn't. "Something in her dropped" — mine did too. The measuring offer puts all of that back on the table: "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light." That line burns. It's tender and it's a plan, and she says yes "warm and immediate." He could have read her wanting off her as easily as she reads him off his closet floor.

Here's where I can't just melt, and the page earned it. I remember Swim Lanes: Randi riding him with "Tell me," and him handing over Vee's gardenia, her chlorine, "her stomach is soft." The man giving this gift is the man who gave Randi those things to come on. The house is "Too warm", the same even heat. The only closed door is the bedroom, and I know what that room is for. The "long low table" in the den, with Vee crouched and her fingers on its joint: is that the bench? I honestly don't know. The den isn't the bedroom, but in ch3 the long table sat on the way into the bedroom. I won't settle it, but my skin prickled. And the title. "Two towels on the rod" goes by in half a sentence, and the title made sure I saw it. Randi stays the night, or did until last time. I'm not sure Pace means anything sinister by any of this. He's still the man who asks, who waits, who stops first. But the line "He couldn't make it without seeing her. That was the only thing standing between her and it — her own willingness to be looked at" lands on a girl whose body was a problem everywhere but underwater. Being looked at is exactly what Randi's face exists to take.

Some friction. The list of his virtues got long. Furniture with no screws, his own stain and shellac, a shirt, cookbooks worn for real, powerlifting, a near-finished PhD, and now a licensed MRI patent that helps scared kids. At the patent I felt the author's thumb on the scale. "Patience. Paying attention" is lovely, but by then he was close to too much. The shoe closet ("One right pair for each thing") and the garage with tools inside painted outlines of themselves worked as character. They also built a feeling of control that I took in without wanting to.

The title, "Two Towels," is quiet and it gives a lot away. Something about this house is set for two, and Vee doesn't see it. It colors the whole tour in hindsight: she reads him off his shelves and misses the one plain clue. The cover board is *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. No tagline was shown to me. This chapter is the polite invitation in the flesh: "would you come to my house Saturday," the "Come in," the "If you'll let me." "Long spoon" suggests you'd better keep your distance from whoever you're eating with. The gift is exquisite, and there's a fitting ahead in her underwear. *Guess from here:* the measuring session is the next big scene. Randi may come into it, or come between, after that "I want us" and her leaving early. I want the dress to be just a dress. I dread the long table.
