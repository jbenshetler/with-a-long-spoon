### Impression
Vee is easy to love: funny, generous, starved, opening up. I'm tender toward her and worried for her, because her happiness sits on a setup she can't see. Pace reads as sincerely good: patient, attentive, scrupulous about her choosing, and wounded himself (the hall, Daphne, the scar, his mother). I trust his tenderness. But he is still half of an arrangement made about her, and he misreads Randi badly. That blindness is what unsettles me about him, not any malice. Randi is the most complicated: manipulative in practice (the staging, the reporting, leading Vee's confessions), yet visibly in pain, falling for Vee, and jealous. I feel real sympathy for the girl in the bathroom mirror and wariness about what her hurt might make her do. Cassie I trust completely; her quiet watchfulness and that "Goodbye" feel like the book's conscience. The pull of the Vee–Randi current is strong and charged.
