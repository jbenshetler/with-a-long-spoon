### Impression
- **Vee**: deep sympathy and tenderness. She is warm, funny, self-deprecating and hungry, and coming alive in a way that's lovely to watch. I'm afraid for her: she's the one person in the triangle who doesn't know what she's in, and her happiness is built on things she can't see. She's also starting to spend past her means.
- **Pace**: I largely trust his intent. He is patient and exact, he truly cares, he refuses to rush Vee, and his tenderness toward Randi is real. Unease: he took part in choosing Vee, reports her intimacies to Randi, and doesn't seem to see the ethics of the setup. He also misreads Randi badly, and his own past (Daphne at 15, the hallway, the scar, his parents) suggests damage he names as luck. Strong pull toward him.
- **Randi**: the most charged and least trustworthy. She is a performer who engineered the meeting, steers Vee's confessions, and probably invents men to tell stories about. Yet she is also clearly wounded (the face, the food, the thumbnail) and really falling for Vee, which makes her both dangerous and sympathetic. I feel suspicion, pity, and a strong pull, and I watch her closely.
- **Cassie**: full trust. She's the steady, clear-eyed friend, and I worry she's being edged out right when Vee might need her. Her early "Goodbye" and her watchfulness feel like the book's conscience.
- **Brooke / the sorority**: wary. A quiet threat to Randi's cover.
