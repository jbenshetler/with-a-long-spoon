## Reader reaction

**Reader reaction: "Leave No Trace"**

I spent this whole chapter in two places at once, and that's what makes it hurt. Up on the trail I'm with Vee: the heel slipping in Meg's boots, thighs burning in a way the pool never asked of them, the summit opening so suddenly that "her body stopped her." That view, with its lake "holding the silver sky in it flat as a poured thing" and the ridges burning, swept me right up. I gasped at the horseradish along with her. But I also remember Randi on the quad saying *go get her*, and Randi on Pace's couch saying *Tell.* I know this hike will be told back, maybe tonight, over a glass of red, probably by him. Vee doesn't know that. So every tender thing he does lands on me twice: once as tenderness and once as something that's going to be passed on.

And the tenderness is real on the page. I can't call any of it fake. He kneels and peels off her sock while her mother's voice is already lining up its verdict, and all he says is "*hmm.* Hot spot. We can fix that." Then he apologizes: "I didn't think to ask about the boots." He picks up the cherry pit without shaming her and sets his own pit beside it without looking at her. He hands her the flannel and walks out into the rain. I like him. My body likes him. I just can't tell what his attention is *for*, and the book won't tell me either. Is he courting her, or is he working the long project he started on the quad? Maybe both, and maybe he couldn't separate them himself. I'm leaving that open, because the page does.

The truck scene burned. What got me wasn't the stripping. It was "the voice was the heat and the heat was the voice." Her mother's *floozie* lands and her thighs press together on their own. That's shame turning into want, right in front of me, and it's the first time Vee goes looking for her own transgression instead of shrinking from it. Her two thoughts, *I want him in here with me* and then, slower, *I want him to see me*, are the truest lines in the chapter. The consent is clean and almost ceremonial. She knocks on the window, crooks a finger and holds his eyes there. He doesn't lean in or lift a hand. "The gladness in him was the whole of the answer." Then comes the turn I loved: she reaches for his hand to start the next thing and finds it shaking. He's been shivering the whole time. She tucks it against her belly under the flannel and says "You're freezing." That gave me the ache. Her want turns into care in a single breath. And his stillness unsettles me a little: "a long way from the question of whether he was cold." Where was he? Was he thinking of Randi, of the bench, of what he'd tell her? I can't know. I noticed the thought and set it down.

The dress worries me in a quieter way. She names the russet silk, puts it back on the shelf, and he says only "That sounds like a beautiful dress," and then "didn't pick it up again." My guess from here: he will. A man who notices which way a girl likes her apple slices doesn't forget a russet dress. I can't tell yet whether that will feel like a gift or a hook.

The friction is minor. The campus cop's siren cutting off "Pace—" felt convenient, the author's hand closing the door before Vee could say the big thing. "The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." That over-explains the feeding motif I'd already felt on my own. The clipped "Push and lift. Push and lift." rhythm worked on me for the climb, though.

The Cassie scene at the end was lovely. She's rapt, she says just "Vee," "like setting something down where it wouldn't break," and she ends on "Keep the shirt." Cassie's warmth here makes her "Goodbye, Vee" from the lecture hall echo louder. I notice that Vee tells Cassie everything and that I don't know yet what she'll tell Randi.

**The titles.** "Leave No Trace" starts as trail ethics: the pit on the wax paper, carrying out what you carried in. Then it sours. Pace leaves no trace in the sense that none of his touches leave a mark, and everything gets carried back out and reported. Vee, meanwhile, keeps the shirt. She's the one keeping a trace. The cover board, *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*, brings the old proverb to mind: you need a long spoon to sup with the devil. This chapter is all eating, and someone is always feeding her, from the baba ghanoush to the sandwich to the cherries. "A Polite Invitation" makes me think of *May I* and of the cherry pit picked up so gently. Every invitation in this book is beautifully polite. That's the unease.

**What I want, expect and dread:** I want Vee to have this, and I want her to be warm in the flannel. I dread the next Randi-and-Pace chapter, where the shirt becomes a story he tells. Guess from here: the bench is coming for Vee, and the dress comes first.
