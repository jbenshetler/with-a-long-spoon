## Reader reaction

I felt almost breathless through this. Vee arrives ready to take the kiss she was denied by rain and police lights, and she does; the opening kiss works because it is so plainly hers: “I missed you,” then she leans in. Pace stopping it is unexpectedly affecting. It leaves her desire lit up and unsettled, but it also sharpens the pattern she has begun to trust in him: he can want her without treating her stopping as the only brake. That is intensely sexy to me—more than if he had simply swept her into the bedroom.

The house makes Pace both more attractive and more unnerving. It is so orderly, capable, and private: the paired shoes, used kitchen, wild paper-filled office, handmade furniture, immaculate workshop. I love Vee’s attentive reading of him, especially “one right pair for each thing and no more.” But I also feel the scale of how much he contains. He has an MRI-related patent; he builds furniture, makes shirts, cooks, does advanced mathematics, hikes, powerlifts. His competence is almost overwhelming. The page earns Vee’s awe, and I share it, but because I still know the beginning was arranged behind her back, I cannot wholly relax into the fantasy of him as simply miraculous.

The dress hit me hard. Pace remembered a wish Vee had already relinquished and apparently chose silk not to reproduce the magazine image but to suit *her*. “He’d matched her.” That is romantic in a way that made my chest squeeze. Yet it is also an enormous gift, and he needs to see and measure her in her underwear to make it. The chapter makes clear that she says yes eagerly and freely, and I believe that she wants this. Still, the cover tagline is ringing very loudly now: every yes is hers, but the conditions around these astonishing, tailored acts of care feel almost too perfectly fitted to make saying yes easy.

I am especially moved by Pace’s answer about error-correcting codes: “Say it more than once so the noise can’t kill it.” It feels like a real glimpse of what he loves—not merely being impressive, but making something useful for scared, sick people. The small close at “parents,” though, leaves a new shut door in him. His bedroom is another. Vee dressed for it, leaned toward it, and he simply did not open it. I felt her little drop of disappointment. I also admired that she did not punish him for it or turn it into a scene.

The erotic charge is a strong 2 for me: no explicit sex, but the kiss, the unopened bedroom, Vee’s satin underthings, and Pace’s calm invitation to measure her “down to what you’d wear under it” are all hot. It peaks at the opening kiss and again at the final “Yes.” The measuring is now loaded with anticipation. I want Vee to be delighted and seen, and I dread the possibility that the dress becomes another beautiful thing she feels she must repay.

“Two Towels” initially seemed almost comically mundane when Vee sees “two towels on the rod,” but now it feels pointed: one house, one man, yet two towels already waiting in the bathroom. It made me wonder whether he had prepared for her visit, though the text does not prove that. It also sits beside the later invitation to be measured: the whole house seems to have room made for her before she has decided what she wants. *A Polite Invitation* feels increasingly exact—Pace does not force doors, but his offerings are so exquisite that entering becomes its own temptation. *With a Long Spoon* still means little to me beyond the uneasy “devil” implication; the warmth of this house makes that unease sharper, not weaker.

My one friction point is not disbelief exactly, but accumulation: Pace’s handmade furniture, advanced research, patent, woodworking shop, clothing-making, cooking, lifting, hiking competence, and remarkably tailored emotional restraint make him feel almost impossibly complete. Vee’s awe carries me through it because it is her first encounter with his private life, but I am now waiting to see a limit, mess, cost, or ordinary human failure in him. The chapter does give a little mess in the office and that quick closed expression around his parents.

I very much want to read the measuring scene next. My guess from here is that Vee will choose how much to reveal and that Pace will continue to ask rather than presume—but I am also waiting for the strange pressure of such generosity to become harder for her to name.
