## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel physically cold, hungry, and then intensely keyed up. The hike itself is lovely because Vee is allowed to be inexperienced without being humiliated. Pace fixes her heel, shows her where pits go, gives her food, and never turns any of those moments into evidence that she is foolish. “She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her” hit me hard. That is exactly the kind of care Vee is starved for.

The summit genuinely opens the book up for me. “The world swallowed everything that was in it and went on being the world” is beautiful, and Pace watching Vee see it, then giving her “the rest of the looking alone,” is one of his most attractive gestures yet. He still frightens me in the broader sense because I know he and Randi started all this under false pretenses, but nothing he does here feels coercive on the page. If anything, he is almost startlingly restrained: he waits outside in the rain while she changes, he does not open the truck door when she calls him back, and when she undresses, he looks only because she has put his eyes on her.

The truck scene is where the erotic charge peaks, and it works. Vee choosing to expose herself while holding his gaze is very hot, but it is also moving because she is fighting with her mother’s voice in real time. Her desire is not purified of shame; it is tangled up in the thrill of becoming “the very thing the voice said.” Her reaching for his hand to start “the next thing,” then realizing he is shaking and putting his hand beneath the flannel against her own warm belly, changes the temperature of the whole scene. I felt protective of both of them there. He gave her his only dry shirt and stood outside getting colder; she sees him, stops herself, and insists they go. That is intimacy, not just heat.

I’m also pleased that Vee tells him about the russet silk dress instead of entirely swallowing the want. Pace’s “That sounds like a beautiful dress” is so light it could be nothing, but I felt the dress remain present between them. I want him not to turn that into a grand gesture that makes her indebted. At this point, I want Vee to get the dress because she wants it, whether or not he helps.

The ending jolted me. The campus cop’s lights feel like a hard intrusion into the warm sealed space of the truck, and Vee’s unfinished “Pace—” leaves me anxious. I do not know whether the officer will merely move him along, issue a ticket, or create some larger problem. The repeated image of the foam on her heel “still… holding” is tender: Pace’s care remains on her body after he has been driven away.

The title, **“Leave No Trace,”** first belongs to the literal hiking rule and the cherry pits. But by the end it feels more charged: Pace and Vee have left a great deal on each other—his flannel on her, her wet shirt in her hand, the unfinished thing in the cab—while trying to get safely back to ordinary life. The title did not give anything away; it makes the cherry correction and the ending linger. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, continues to feel unnervingly exact: Pace’s conduct is full of invitations rather than demands, and Vee keeps stepping through them willingly. The tagline remains the ache of the book. Nothing in this chapter falsifies Vee’s yes; she is unmistakably choosing. But I cannot forget that she was chosen first without her knowledge. *With a Long Spoon* still means little literally, though this chapter’s careful food, shared bottle, and the danger of closeness make me feel the series title hovering.

My immediate guess from here is that the cop interruption will delay or complicate whatever Vee was trying to tell Pace, rather than ending their closeness. I dread the eventual collision between how safe and freely chosen this feels to Vee and what I know about its beginning. I also want to know whether Randi will hear about this encounter—and what she will do with Vee having crossed another threshold with Pace.

#### Running memory — How I feel

- **Vee:** I ache for her and am happy with her. Her joy remains real: she feels newly entitled to want, choose, kiss, say yes, be publicly claimed as important, and now make herself sexually visible on her own terms. Ch.11 adds bravery, hunger, and tenderness toward Pace: she is not merely receiving care but notices his cold and chooses his wellbeing over immediate gratification. Her certainty that she chose Pace is real, which makes the hidden circumstances more troubling rather than less. Her growing belief that her place is beside Randi is beautiful and alarming. I especially want her to have the russet dress without having to surrender her agency to get it.
- **Pace:** Highly attractive, increasingly tender-seeming, and still frightening because he knows and controls so much that Vee does not. Nothing in ch.11 directly makes him unsafe; he is careful, prepared, attentive, restrained, and willing to stand in cold rain so Vee can have privacy. His patient response to her inexperience makes me trust his immediate conduct. Still, his isolated home and trail off six-fourteen gave me small apprehension, and the campus cop following him now creates a fresh unease. Christine’s affection and Cassie’s approval remain meaningful counterweights to my distrust.
- **Randi:** I am drawn to her warmth and social confidence, and ch.10 makes her public defense/claim of Vee feel deeply affecting. But I cannot forget she helped engineer Vee’s connection to Pace. Her absence during the date felt loud; now that she knows about it and the kiss, her immediate delight feels both loving and potentially controlling. I trust the reality of Vee’s response to her more than I trust Randi’s transparency. I am waiting to see how she receives this new escalation with Pace.
- **Cassie:** Still fully trusted and loved. She celebrates Vee without shaming her, checks for alcohol and coercion, and has a protective floor beneath her warmth. In ch.10, her brief unreadable look at Randi before leaving gives me concern, but not distrust. Her advice about real shoes led Vee to Meg’s boots, though the boots themselves were wrong.
- **Brooke:** I feel wary of her polished friendliness. She may simply be socially fluent, but Vee’s felt experience of being measured makes me alert around her.
- **Campus cop:** No personal impression yet, only immediate anxiety because the officer’s lights and siren severed an intimate moment and followed Pace away.
- **Overall mood:** Warm, seduced, buoyant, and guilty about that warmth, now washed through with rain-cold vulnerability and a sharp police-light interruption. Vee’s freely given happiness and desire are beautiful; the reader’s knowledge of the hidden setup keeps a shadow at its edge.
