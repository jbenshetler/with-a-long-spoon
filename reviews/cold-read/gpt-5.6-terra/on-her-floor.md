## Reader reaction

I’m shaking a little with Vee at the end of this. The kiss landed as a genuine crossing-over: not a coy brush she can file under friendship, not just a joke that got too close, but Randi’s mouth and hands and Vee wanting her with her whole body before she has language to manage it. I felt the full swoon of it. The held inch before the kiss, Vee finally getting to look at Randi’s face “as they had wanted to all autumn,” and then the particular taste of her—those were devastating. It is hot because it is so specifically Randi, not because there is a boy watching.

And then Randi calls it a show. That hurt. Not because I think the kiss was false on Randi’s side—the moment afterward, when she has to blink and draw breath before the grin comes, makes that impossible for me to believe—but because she snaps the cover story back into place precisely when Vee has been opened past it. Vee takes the only explanation offered because she has nowhere else to put what happened. I feel enormously tender toward her there, and angry on her behalf without feeling simple anger at Randi. Randi seems to have wanted it desperately too; that is what makes her retreat feel so painful rather than merely cruel.

Vee’s excitement about being Randi’s plus-one is lovely and sad in a way I couldn’t separate. “Tonight I’m Randi’s date” is clearly a joke she can say safely, and Cassie’s lifted eyebrow lets the line hang with more charge than Vee is ready to touch. I like Cassie more for not making Vee explain herself. The borrowed skirt works on Vee at first as borrowed permission: if it is Randi’s, the looking is somehow not her responsibility. But on the floor it becomes Vee’s appetite as much as Randi’s garment. She does want to be looked at; she wants Randi to move her, hold her up, choose the size of her dancing. That mixture of shame and wanting is doing real work on me, especially when Vee realizes she has been exposed and finds the feeling “not only awful.”

Randi is almost unbearably magnetic here. Her pleasure in Vee is so clear—“I’m going to stand next to you all night and live off what spills”—and I melted at the two of them walking arm in arm through the cold. The bracelet moment is beautiful but gives me a little chill on second thought: “Love,” close around Randi’s wrist, removable only with tools. It may only be one more glamorous fact Vee collects about her, but it sits oddly against this chapter’s mix of commitment, performance, and what cannot be said plainly.

Pace’s absence mattered. Vee says he gets Friday and Sunday while Randi gets Saturday, and it feels playful, fortunate, almost domestic in its scheduling. Yet this chapter gives Randi and Vee a space where Vee’s want can finally surface without Pace’s gaze organizing it. Nothing went slack for me. Even the party-punch and margaritas sharpen the sense of Vee being carried along by Randi’s assuredness, though the second margarita appearing without Vee seeing it made me briefly uneasy—not as a claim that anything is wrong with the scene, but because Vee is already so surrendering of her own footing.
