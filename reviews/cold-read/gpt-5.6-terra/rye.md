## Reader reaction

I melted a little at Vee coming home in Pace’s flannel and telling Cassie everything—not ashamed, not minimizing, just astonished by herself. “I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain” landed hard. Cassie’s dry jokes make room for Vee’s joy, and then her quiet “Look at you” gives it weight. I like Cassie more here: she is protective without taking Vee’s experience away from her.

The heat is mostly in Vee’s retelling: Pace kneeling to tend her foot, “these forearms,” and especially her saying she took off her wet shirt “on purpose.” It still works because she owns it so plainly, and because his looking is held as something careful rather than grabbed. The softness peaks for me at “like I’d handed him something,” and at Cassie’s final permission: “Keep the shirt.” I have no new suspicion of Pace from this page; Vee’s account makes his restraint and care feel even more real. The larger unease remains only what I already know and Vee does not.

“Rye” is the sandwich, of course, but it now feels like the solid, sharp, sustaining part of the day Vee is allowed to remember and want. *A Polite Invitation* still promises doors opened carefully—Pace waiting outside the truck, Vee knocking him back in—but I’m increasingly aware that an invitation can be freely accepted and still come from a situation Vee cannot yet see. *With a Long Spoon* and the packet’s “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem” still hover, though this chapter itself is warm rather than ominous. I want Vee to keep telling Cassie the truth, keep the shirt, and see Pace again. My guess from here: the next meeting may make that interrupted truck moment harder to leave unfinished.

### Chapter record

- **Vee (Vivienne) Thorne:** arrives in Cassie’s room wearing Pace’s flannel, three sizes too big; hair dried in every direction; sore legs and a blister “like a second toe.” She lowers herself into her desk chair with both hands bracing. She recounts the Sunday hike: tree tunnel, steep ascent, lungs quitting, borrowed boots that did not fit, the summit’s silver lake far below and almost-black rock, and getting there on her own legs. She says Pace used moleskin and little scissors from a trail kit to treat her blister. She describes him as having notable hands, forearms, and possibly being built under his jacket. They ate rye bread with horseradish, cherries, and shared water; carried out crumbs and cherry pits. It rained freezingly on the descent. In Pace’s truck, wet through, she changed into his dry flannel while he stood outside in rain with his back to the cab. She knocked on the glass and called him back in, took off her wet shirt before him on purpose; he looked but did not touch her, shook with cold, turned on the heat, and drove her home. She kept his shirt without asking.
- **Cassie (Cassandra):** in sweats, warm and dry, with brown shag hair untangled; sits in a swiveling chair. Jokes about a “windowless van,” having a witness statement and Vee’s blood type; asks whether Pace carried Vee. She listens raptly as Vee describes the truck, says “Vee” softly, advises borrowing real boots next time, and tells her, “Keep the shirt.”
- **Pace (Peter):** mentioned only. Took Vee hiking on Sunday; had a first-aid kit with moleskin and little scissors; treated Vee’s blister without making her feel she had ruined the day.
