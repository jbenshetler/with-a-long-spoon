## Reader reaction

This is such a warm, giddy chapter that I felt myself grinning with Vee before the invitation even arrived. She is suddenly loud, social, funny, eating fries, telling a story she knows gets better in the telling and enjoying that fact. After the intimate safety of Cassie’s room, it is a pleasure to see her take up space among Meg and Kayla. “Vee sat between them, laughing with her whole body, loud and easy” feels like a real change in how she inhabits herself.

I like Meg immediately: she has the dry, grounded response to everything, and her “Sometimes they even are” lands as the only little check on Vee’s incandescent certainty without deflating her. Kayla is a lot, but in a fun, affectionate way; she makes the invitation feel like an event. Neither woman knows what the reader knows about Randi and Pace’s beginning, so their excitement also gives me a faint ache. They see the romance Vee sees. I can’t wholly join them, even though I want to.

Vee’s account of Pace makes him more attractive again on the surface. The dry shirt he packed for himself, standing in the rain with his back turned, pulling under the dorm overhang: it is all care, and her frustration that he was too cold to kiss is funny and genuinely hot. The erotic charge peaks when she admits, “It’s making me lose my mind,” and explains that his asking, waiting, and doing “exactly what happens, the way I said it” is what has her climbing the walls. That works on me more than Kayla’s breezy assumption that two o’clock means sex. The invitation itself is charged precisely because it is so spare: “would you come to my house Saturday. Two o’clock.”

But the house gives me a little stomach-drop. Not because Pace has done anything on this page to earn immediate distrust—his actual words are polite, an invitation rather than a command—but because Vee has never been through his door and because I know the connection began with a hidden design. The passage that gets me is: “there was a door, and Saturday behind it, and not one word about what it was for.” It makes the title feel wonderfully exact. **“What to Wear”** is Kayla’s practical panic, but it is also Vee trying to prepare herself for an unknown threshold. The title did not give away the invitation; it sharpened the delicious anxiety once it came.

*A Polite Invitation* is now very literally arriving: Pace’s restrained text is polite almost to the point of opacity. The cover tagline is getting harder to ignore, because Vee is so clearly thrilled to say yes, and that freedom is real—yet I know how much she was guided toward this man before she knew him. *With a Long Spoon* still means little to me in the immediate scene, except that the book keeps making ordinary domestic things—food, shirts, doors, now getting dressed—feel risky and intimate.

I absolutely want to read Saturday. My guess from here is that Vee will go, and that the preparations with Meg and Kayla will be both fun and painful because they are preparing her for a house neither they nor she understands. I dread any moment when the hidden beginning collides with the happiness she is carrying into that door.

#### Running memory — How I feel

- **Vee:** I ache for her and am happy with her. Her happiness, hunger, pride, and erotic initiative are real. Ch.12 strengthened my feeling that she is finding a language for her body that is not her mother’s: she can say she ate, climbed, wanted to be looked at, and kept the shirt. Ch.13 expands that freedom into public life: she laughs loudly, eats fries, talks openly about wanting Pace, and lets herself be excited rather than apologetic. I especially want her to have the russet dress and real boots without either becoming a debt, and now I want Saturday to remain truly hers.
- **Pace:** Highly attractive and increasingly tender-seeming, but still frightening because he knows and controls so much that Vee does not, and because the origin of their connection was secretly engineered. His conduct on the hike and in the truck, as Vee recounts it, gives me no direct reason to think he endangered her: he is prepared, attentive, restrained, and protective of her privacy.
