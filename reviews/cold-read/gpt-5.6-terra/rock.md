Her father works on jet engines for a major airline; he calls her Vee except when she is in trouble, when he calls her Vivienne. Her parents met at church and are from the same city. She has a brother three years older, in the Army, unmarried; they are fine but distant, and Vee does not know his phone number. Her family had a Corolla, church, plentiful casserole-like food, and a spice rack with mostly unopened jars.
- **Vee’s adolescent relationship:** At fifteen she dated a trumpet player while she played clarinet; they sat three rows apart in band. The relationship lasted one marching season. He began seeing a girl from the Methodist church over the summer. Vee listened sadly to the same four songs until her mother removed her headphones.
- **Pace’s family background:** His mother stays home. His father works in a chemical plant. His real name is Peter.
- **Pace’s education:** He is twenty-two. At fourteen he took the SAT for a summer program, performed exceptionally well, and through scholarships and adult intervention went to college at fifteen. In seventh grade he had taught himself trigonometry to write a graphics program; because his teacher could not answer his questions, she moved his desk into the hall during math and assigned long-division worksheets for the school year. College introduced him to people who found mathematics beautiful.
- **Pace’s mathematics explanation:** He describes a shape that can hold a finite cup of paint but has an infinitely extensive surface; a coastline whose measured length grows without limit as the ruler gets shorter; and Newton’s three laws as applying alike to a thrown ball, a falling leaf, and the stars. As a child he wanted to make graphics using edges inside edges forever.
- **Pace’s former tutor relationship:** On entering college at fifteen, Pace was assigned a nineteen-year-old female tutor, ostensibly to help him find his feet. The relationship lasted two years. She left when she turned twenty-one and he was seventeen. Pace says nothing happened and nobody did anything wrong; he calls it the best two years he had had and says he did not feel young with her.
- **Pace and lifting:** He began lifting partly because he was a fifteen-year-old in a dormitory full of men. At a squat bar carrying enough weight to bend one-inch steel “like dry spaghetti,” he experiences freedom in having no thought but the lift and heartbeat.
- **Scar boundary:** Vee touches Pace’s old raised scar high on his inner thigh. Pace places his hand over hers, keeps it against the scar, and says, “Not tonight.” Vee turns her palm beneath his and they leave their hands together over the scar.
- **End image:** Vee says “Peter” softly without a question. Pace closes his hand slowly around hers over the scar. The fire continues behind them while the cold remains outside the glass.
