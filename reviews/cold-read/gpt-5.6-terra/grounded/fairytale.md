Randi gets the full fairytale—snow, champagne, white tie, a beautiful man who can dance and make a sleigh appear—and I could feel the seduction of it. The blue dress, his face when she comes down the stairs, the marble floor and the old dances: I understood why she lets herself think, “this is the one.” For a while I wanted to believe in it with her.

But the chapter makes the emptiness afterward land hard. John is charming, capable, attentive in the broad social sense, and Randi is plainly choosing him at every turn; she even finds the lack of asking exhilarating for one night. I felt deeply for her in the mirror. “This was the best the acceptable world could do for her.” That is brutal, especially because she cannot locate the failure in John. Her decision to let Vee “arrive at it on her own” makes me tender toward her and uneasy at once. That is a very lonely ending: Randi going back to the warmth she can borrow from a man she does not want, while the person she wants is far away and being carefully kept from the truth.
