## Reader reaction

This got under my skin in a colder, quieter way than the last chapter. I felt Randi trying to get upstairs before anyone could see that anything had happened to her, and then Brooke’s soft, immaculate little interrogation made my whole body tighten. The cruelty, if it is cruelty, is in how little Brooke has to do: “*Pace.* That’s actually his name?”; “He doesn’t *teach*, does he.” Nothing is said outright, yet Randi is made to account for herself in front of three people who are all listening.

I do not trust Brooke’s warmth here. The page earns that distrust through her timing, her authority, and the way she “typed something” after extracting each detail. Sloane and Kinsey feel less dangerous but not kind exactly; their laughter lets the room close around Randi. I feel enormous sympathy for Randi, especially when she cannot make herself take off her shoes or read her phone. The line “The duvet was the duvet” is devastating: Pace’s held warmth is so present by absence that this ordinary cover cannot reach her.

There is no erotic charge on the page itself, but the aftermath is intensely bodily. “He’s hard to say no to” changes key in her mind, and I feel the chapter refusing to let me settle into a simple version of last night. I still believe she wanted Pace; I also feel newly alarmed by the phrase and by how unavailable her own crying is to her. Her face is working again, but it does not protect her from what she is carrying.

“Standards” lands sharply: Randi holds that board position, yet she is being quietly assessed by Brooke, who has more authority. I badly want the next chapter, though my guess from here is that Randi will try to return to normal rather than tell Pace what has shifted.
