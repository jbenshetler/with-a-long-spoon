## Reader reaction

This made me feel cold almost immediately. Randi’s walk into the house is all practiced control under pressure, and I felt for her so sharply when Brooke’s friendliness became a neat little interrogation. Nothing Brooke says is openly cruel, which makes the way she holds the room worse: “*Pace.* That’s actually his name?” and then the relieved “Good” about him not teaching her. Randi gets through it brilliantly, and that competence feels lonely rather than triumphant.

I don’t distrust Pace more from this chapter; he is absent, and the warmth of his house is almost painfully present by contrast with Randi’s room and “adequate” duvet. I do feel Brooke’s warmth has edges now. She may genuinely be friendly, but she is also collecting and sorting information, and Randi can feel exactly how much power Brooke has to do that. Sloane and Kinsey feel like an audience Randi has to manage.

There is no erotic action here, but the charge of the last night is still alive in the sentence Randi cannot let herself finish thinking: “He’s hard to say no to.” It lands differently now because we know she did say yes, explicitly and repeatedly—and because she cannot bear to follow that truth into feeling. The ache peaks with her lying down in her shoes, under a duvet she can only call a duvet. I wanted someone to give her warmth, but not demand anything from her in return.

“Standards” is cruelly apt: Randi is on the board called Standards, and the chapter puts her back among the social rules she knows how to enforce and survive. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, and series title, *With a Long Spoon*, still point toward civility with something difficult underneath it; I have no cover tagline in this memory to react to. I badly want the next chapter to let Randi breathe—or to show whether Pace recognizes how far away she has gone. My guess from here is that maintaining the polished version of herself will get harder, not easier.
