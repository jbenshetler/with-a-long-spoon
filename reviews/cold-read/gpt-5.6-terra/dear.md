## Reader reaction

I felt the air change the moment Cassie saw Randi and left. Cassie’s “whatever had been in [her eyes] was folded away” gives me a small pang: she noticed something, or at least saw Vee getting pulled into a different orbit, and chose not to interfere. I still trust Cassie completely. Her easy departure makes Vee’s immediate availability to Randi feel lonelier.

Randi is intoxicating here. She listens to Vee’s date story exactly where Vee most wants to be heard, catches the importance of “May I choose?” and makes Vee feel glamorous rather than foolish for wanting to let go. I felt Vee’s happiness in that exchange—especially her proud “I kissed him”—and I liked Randi for giving her somewhere to put it. But I am uneasy too, because I know Randi has helped build the circumstances Vee is calling spontaneous. When she turns Vee’s “I made an exception” into “Because you couldn’t not. Because of him,” it feels gently leading, even if Vee accepts it as true. The text earns my suspicion because I have already seen Randi arrange things and guide Vee’s choices; on the surface of this scene, though, she is warm, protective, and genuinely magnetic.

The smoothie shop made me physically shrink with Vee. “It covered her like an apology” is brutal. The high chair, dangling feet, pink drink, the room of polished matching women: I could feel her being displayed and judged before anybody openly judged her. Brooke’s smile is chillingly polite. Nothing she says is cruel, but Vee feels “taken measure” of, and I felt that too. The taller unnamed woman’s quiet scrutiny added to it. Brooke’s “We should all get dinner sometime” landing as “lovely, and meant nothing” is exactly the kind of exclusion that stings because it cannot be challenged.

Then Randi takes Vee’s hand and calls her “my dear friend,” and I felt the relief with Vee. It is emotionally potent, maybe the strongest charge in the chapter: not erotic exactly, but intimate, public, and loaded. Randi spends social standing—or at least Vee believes she does—to choose her aloud. I understand why that overwhelms Vee. The final hug and “Love you, girl” are tender enough that I wanted to believe them. Yet the fact that Vee walks away thinking her place is “here, next to Randi” makes my stomach tighten. Randi’s affection may be real; that does not erase that Vee has been drawn into this without knowing the first move was a game.

Pace is absent but somehow everywhere in Vee’s telling. Her description of him becoming “about six years old” over bridges is lovely and makes him more human to her—and, honestly, to me. The erotic charge is mostly simmering rather than explicit: “May I choose?” has its own charge, as does Vee admitting she liked surrendering that small control. The heat peaks when she says, “Not six years old when I kissed him,” and when Randi presses the meaning of the kiss. It never becomes clinical.

The chapter title, **“Dear,”** lands directly in “Vee’s my dear friend,” but it also hangs over the whole chapter as a question: what does it mean to be called dear, loved, picked, publicly claimed? Vee receives that word as rescue. I do too, briefly. Then I remember the cover: **A Polite Invitation** still feels like the soft, impeccable language around doors Vee is being invited through—“May I,” “dear friend,” “Love you, girl.” **With a Long Spoon** still points toward something dangerous beneath a civilized surface; I do not know what it means yet, but the smoothness of this social world makes me more alert to it. And “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap” feels especially sharp here, because Vee’s yeses and feelings are plainly real. Her being genuinely happy does not make the hidden setup less consequential.

I want to keep reading because Vee is coming alive, and because I now dread the eventual collision between her joy in Pace, her attachment to Randi, and what I know. My guess from here is that Randi’s private connection to Pace cannot stay absent forever, especially now that Vee has told Randi about the kiss and the date in such detail. I also want Cassie back in the room with all this; she is still the person whose instincts I most trust.
