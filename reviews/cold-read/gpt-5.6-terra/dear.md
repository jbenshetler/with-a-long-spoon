## Reader reaction

I felt Vee’s happiness first, then the little bodily collapse of it at the Smoothie Bird: cardigan as “an apology,” feet dangling from a chair that makes her feel displayed. Randi’s attention brings it back so quickly that I felt relieved with Vee—and uneasy about how much power that relief gives Randi. Her public “Vee’s my dear friend,” hand held on the table, then the hug and “Love you, girl” are intensely effective. I wanted to believe them. I also couldn’t forget that Vee has no idea Randi chose her within an arrangement.

Randi lands even more vividly here: warm, funny, generous with attention, and capable of making Vee feel singled out in exactly the way Vee aches for. Her reframing of the kiss catches me: Vee begins with “I made an exception,” but Randi guides her to “Because of him.” That feels subtly off, earned by the dialogue itself, though I can’t say whether Randi means harm or simply sees Vee’s feeling differently. Pace remains warmly present through Vee’s account; the phrase “May I choose?” still has a charge, but now Vee’s relief at handing responsibility over makes me nervous too.

The erotic charge is a 1: mostly in Vee’s proud “I kissed him,” and in the surrendering warmth of being chosen and held. It’s more emotionally charged than sexual here. The high-chair scrutiny is uncomfortable in a useful way. I did feel the text press a little hard at “which was lovely, and meant nothing,” since Brooke’s invitation could plausibly mean more than Vee can know; but the scene otherwise let Vee’s feelings do the work.

“Dear” now seems to be about the dangerous sweetness of being named as someone’s chosen person. It fits Randi’s public claim on Vee, which feels both nourishing and weighted. *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, and “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” keep making me feel that Vee’s yeses are real—and that the invitations around them may not be as innocent as they feel. I want the next chapter to let Vee keep this joy, but my guess from here is that the comfort she finds beside Randi will make any later revelation hurt worse.
