## Reader reaction

This made me feel both warmed and sickened in the particular way the book has taught me to feel around Randi and Vee: Randi gives Vee exactly what Vee most aches for—attention, celebration, a public place beside her—and Vee blooms under it. The hand on the table, “Vee’s my dear friend,” and the goodbye hug are intensely effective. I felt Vee’s relief in my body. I also felt the trap tighten, because Randi knows this connection began in a private arrangement Vee cannot see.

Randi is more vivid to me here: playful, socially brilliant, genuinely delighted by Vee’s happiness, and frighteningly able to turn warmth into an atmosphere Vee wants to live inside. Her listening to Pace’s dinner story, especially “You let him choose for you. And you liked it,” feels intimate and a little too knowing. I don’t think the text asks me to believe her care is fake; that is what hurts. Her affection for Vee seems real. So does the hidden power she has over what Vee thinks their friendship means.

Vee’s happiness is beautiful and precarious. She begins the chapter glowing about Pace, then shrinks in the Smoothie Bird, then is restored by Randi choosing her aloud. I especially felt for her at “the whole room remembered she was sitting in it.” The erotic charge is only a simmer here—mostly in Vee’s proud “I kissed him” and Randi’s close attention to her giving herself over to Pace’s choice—but it is charged by intimacy rather than sex.

Brooke earns my unease again. Her warmth does not crack, which makes it harder to trust. The line “We missed you Thursday” lands like a polite reminder that Vee has stepped into a world with rules and history she does not possess. The taller woman’s silent appraisal also made me tense; I do not know who she is, and Vee does not either.

“Dear” points directly to “Vee’s my dear friend”: a word of affection that gives Vee belonging and weight at once. It is also unnerving because it is said before an audience, and because I know how little Vee knows. *A Polite Invitation* now feels less like one invitation than a succession of gracious doors: coffee, dinner, friendship, a hand held in public. The tagline remains painfully exact: Vee’s responses are freely felt, yet the conditions around them are hidden. *With a Long Spoon* still means nothing definite to me.

I want to see whether Randi can sustain this closeness without either confessing or recoiling. My guess from here: Vee will carry “dear friend” as a foundational promise, while Randi may be feeling something much less simple than friendship.
