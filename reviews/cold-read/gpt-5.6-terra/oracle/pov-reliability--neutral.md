**Question asked (verbatim):** When you're inside a character's head and they're describing someone they love or want — Vee on Pace, or on Randi — do you take that as how the person really is, or as colored by what the narrator feels? Walk me through how much you trust each viewpoint's read of the others.

---

I feel the attraction first, and then the gap. Vee’s read of Pace is deeply trustworthy about what it feels like to be with him: his attention, food, warmth, restraint, and practical care repeatedly land as real on the page. But she keeps trying to make those acts answer questions he has not answered in words, so I trust her experience of being cherished more than her conclusions about what place she has in his life.

Her read of Randi is likewise true in the body—Randi is magnetic, funny, affirming, and makes Vee feel chosen—but Vee cannot yet fully see how much she wants her or how expertly Randi directs the terms of closeness. I trust Vee’s heat and relief around Randi; I don’t trust her “just friendship” explanations.

Randi sees Vee unusually clearly: her hunger, shame, humor, aliveness. Her affection feels real to me. But her viewpoint is the hardest to trust as an account of what is fair to Vee, because she knows the hidden arrangement and keeps deciding what Vee can know, including offering interpretations of Pace that soothe Vee into action. Pace also sees Vee’s wounds and wants with remarkable accuracy, yet his confidence that care should speak for itself leaves him blind to the specific naming she needs. So none of them feels false to me; each is most reliable about the tenderness they feel, and least reliable where that tenderness protects them from saying the whole truth.
