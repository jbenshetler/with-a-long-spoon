They feel very alive to me—especially Vee, Randi, Cassie, and Pace—though I remain aware that Pace and Randi are arranging something around Vee. Vee is the most fully lived-in: her hunger, shame, jokes, small pride, and terrible midnight conclusions all feel bodily and specific. Randi is dazzling and frighteningly real because her warmth never cancels her control; the hollow fairytale with John and her private tenderness toward Vee made her more than a schemer to me.

Cassie rings truest as a friend: dry, protective, and careful not to demand disclosure. Pace feels real in his precise care, his difficulty with words, and the unresolved hurt around Daphne and his family—though his confidence that he can guide Vee toward herself is exactly what makes me uneasy. No one feels simply false; if anyone risks feeling over-perfect, it is Pace’s attentiveness early on, but the later failures of naming and silence give that perfection a painful human limit.
