## Reader reaction

This lands warm and buoyant after the date: Vee comes through the door practically incandescent, and I’m smiling with her. The little rush of “*Me*” when she says she said yes to hiking is lovely—she is not merely pleased to be wanted; she is thrilled to discover herself as someone who wants things and does them.

Cassie remains deeply endearing. Her first instinct is to celebrate, but she also asks, “How much did he give you to drink?” It is a blunt, necessary check, and Vee’s answer—“Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose”—hits hard under the cover tagline. It’s genuinely joyful on the page, but I can’t forget that Pace and Randi set the whole meeting in motion without her knowing. Cassie believes her, and I do too, about the kiss being Vee’s choice. That does not make the larger situation feel clean.

Pace is still almost entirely seen through Vee’s glow here: “lovely,” prepared with water, insisting on “real shoes.” Nothing in this chapter itself makes him seem threatening. Yet “off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel” gives me a small bodily prickle. It is a very isolated-sounding place, and Cassie’s joking state-trooper line lets that prickle exist without making Vee’s happiness ridiculous. I’m not expecting him to harm her from this page; the jacket and what I know of the hidden setup make me wary of how safely he is becoming installed in her imagination.

The erotic charge is low but alive in the retelling of the kiss: Vee looking at her hands, speaking “smaller and one at a time,” then admitting that she stopped him and pulled him back. The real peak is emotional and physical at once: her spinning in the tiny room, hair everywhere, unable to contain herself. It feels more intimate than explicit.

“Off Six-Fourteen” initially sounds like a location, and that is exactly what it becomes: Vee’s small proud dossier of a man she scarcely knows. But the title also makes the remoteness stand out. It does not give away anything, though it makes the hiking invitation feel like a threshold. *A Polite Invitation* continues to feel almost painfully apt: Pace has invited, asked, waited; Vee has answered freely and fast. *With a Long Spoon* still means little to me beyond the restaurant’s tea glasses and the broader sense that something tempting may need distance. The tagline is loudest in Vee’s insistence that she chose: she did. That is what makes me both happy for her and uneasy.

I want the hiking chapter badly. I want Vee to see Pace’s house, learn something real and ordinary about him, and keep this new access to her own appetite and desire. My guess from here is that the isolation of “off six-fourteen” will make the next meeting more intimate—and that Randi’s absence cannot last much longer.
