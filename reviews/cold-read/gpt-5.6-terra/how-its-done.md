This chapter made me feel bright and then abruptly chilled. I was pulled into Vee’s pleasure at being chosen by Randi—the car, the restaurant, Randi’s particular attention, the dangerous relief of being told that what happened with Pace was “just how it’s done.” But I felt increasingly trapped alongside her as Randi drew the story out, one approving little question at a time. The laughter is sexy and intimate at first; then Randi’s “You don’t have to tell me” lands like a small, precise refusal after she has led Vee right to the edge of saying the thing she most needed to say. I felt Vee’s secret stranded in the silence.

Vee remains enormously sympathetic to me. She is hungry for Randi’s approval in a way that is understandable and painful: Randi can make her chest, her embarrassment, even her desire feel like luck rather than a fault. I loved Vee’s raucous surrender to “actually it was really *hot*.” She gets a little freedom there. But I hated watching her pay for lunch she clearly cannot afford, while insisting it is “nothing,” because she will not let herself be the broke girl at Randi’s table. By the end, her attempt to explain the kiss away as “a French thing” feels both innocent and desperate.

Randi has become much more troubling to me. I am still attracted to her glamour, warmth, confidence, and the real-seeming softness of that first smile. Her story about being tied up and tickled has erotic charge because Vee’s response is so immediate and unprepared; the image of Randi losing her polished control is potent. But Randi’s control comes roaring back in how she guides Vee: “Which hip?” “And what did he *see*?” The text absolutely earns my suspicion here. Randi appears intensely interested, even keyed-up, and then closes the door exactly when Vee begins to confess. I cannot tell whether she is jealous, protecting herself, protecting Vee, or simply deciding she has heard enough—but the abrupt switch to “Big day” feels cold. Then the mouth kiss is not framed as something Vee understands or actively chooses. I felt a jolt at it, not romantic certainty.

Pace is absent, but his presence fills the lunch. Randi makes the fitting sound almost like a performance she can narrate better than Vee can live it. That sharpened my protective feeling toward Vee: Pace’s actual waiting during the fitting had left room for Vee; Randi’s questions feel, at least in this moment, like they are taking room away.

Randi may be extrapolating from dressmaking, but it made me stop. The emotional logic otherwise held me tightly.

“How It’s Done” is chillingly apt. On one level, it is Randi normalizing being measured without a bra: “it’s just how it’s done.” On another, it describes her own method of getting Vee to disclose: establish safety, flatter, ask one tiny question at a time, and let Vee walk herself somewhere exposed. The tagline is getting more frightening: Vee says yes constantly, but the “trap” may be that every person around her knows how to make a yes feel like the only warm, flattering, reasonable thing to say.
