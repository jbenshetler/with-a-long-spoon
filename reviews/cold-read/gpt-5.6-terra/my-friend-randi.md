**Symbolism noticed**

- The disguised bench suggests an ordinary surface concealing a more exposing reality.

- Randi’s face and practiced smile suggest a self-protective role she can give others while keeping herself unreachable. - The mirror remains a place of exposed self-recognition for Randi and Vee.

- The pointing game suggests a socially acceptable route for a desire Randi cannot name as hers; its rules appear to give her choice while Pace determines the frame. The photograph and its later sharing continue the tension between choosing and being arranged or appraised.

- Pace’s house door, clothing, towels, and prepared drawer make intimacy feel warmly offered while remaining shaped by Pace’s home. His statement that the house is his home, followed by his refusal to return to his parents, makes that welcome feel chosen against an unseen past. The unlocked door and deliberately heated rooms become a private world Vee is asked to trust, while her departure makes clear that welcome is not the same as a publicly nameable bond.

- The washed flannel symbolizes a version of intimacy that survives through absence only by being renewed through Pace’s body; Vee experiences that renewal as a freely granted promise of return. In “Bare,” it becomes insufficient evidence: scent and retained warmth cannot supply presence, mutual definition, or love. The house’s food, heat, wine, and music fail by the same measure when Vee refuses them rather than let care stand in for language.

- Randi’s white-gold bracelet, impossible to slide off in the ballroom, reads as the beautiful inherited life that holds her in place. John and the mountain fairytale expose that life at its most flawless—and therefore show most clearly that it cannot reach her underneath.

- Vee’s childhood bedroom, filled with awards and faces for Vivienne, reads as a preserved identity that once held her whole world and now confines her. - Vee’s long coat now reads as a portable door: it hides her, then is meant to open at Pace’s threshold and turn her body into an answer. The black heels Randi lends her make that answer partly Randi’s arrangement as well as Vee’s choice.

**How I feel**

- **Randi:** Strong attraction and sympathy, with heightened active unease. Her recognition that John’s perfect life leaves her cold makes her more exposed and human; her choice to let Vee hear only friendship makes her warmth newly frightening. Her New Year’s text is genuinely tender, but I now feel its affectionate surface as a painful partial truth. - **Pace:** Attraction to his composure, craft, intellect, humor, and non-humiliating care remains, but anger and distrust are now acute. His statement that Vee is the most important person in his life seems sincere; his refusal or inability to name the relationship, followed by an attempt to soothe with touch, makes the private refuge feel painfully insufficient. Sheri’s challenge confirms that his account of care does not answer Vee’s request. His silent absence over the holiday leaves me wary, though I have no evidence he has done anything wrong. I dread the possibility that his warm welcome tonight will become another substitute for an answer.

- **Vee:** Strong tenderness, attraction, and protectiveness. Her directness—especially refusing the offered hand—is brave, devastating, and a real movement toward protecting her own need rather than translating it into gratitude. Her gentle New Year’s message makes me ache because she does not know what Randi has chosen. Her midnight jealousy feels unfair only in the way fear often is: I do not believe it as fact, but I feel how little she has been given to stand on. Her confession, “I want him back,” is painfully honest; I want her to have care that does not require her to become an answer with her body.

- **Cassie:** Strong trusting affection and curiosity; Vee’s most grounding relationship. I wish Vee had her present.

- **Sheri:** Stronger trust, affection, and interest. Her grief makes her bluntness toward Pace feel compassionate rather than merely combative; she is the first person to name the actual choice he is making. Vee’s fear of her is still not proof about Sheri.

- **Overall mood:** The refuge has broken open, while Randi discovers the opposite world is no refuge either. Vee has found warmth again in Randi’s room, but it has become a launch toward a private encounter that may feel like rescue and still leave the essential question unanswered.
