I feel tenderness for Randi here, and much more understanding. John seems charming, capable, and attentive enough in the ways she invites, but I don’t feel much attachment to him because the page makes clear that neither of them has actually offered the other a self. Pace’s presence in her mind moves me too. She sees that his lightness of lead asks more of her, while John’s certainty lets her rest—and then realizes that the effortful thing was also the thing that reached her.

The Vee material is the sharpest pull. Randi knows she loves Vee, knows what she wants, and chooses concealment because friendship is too precious to risk. That is sad, but not sinister to me. Her text is affectionate and false at once: she gives Vee the fairytale while withholding the loneliness at its center. I ache for Vee receiving it from Ohio.

Heat is explicit but deliberately hollow after midnight. The peak is earlier—the dancing, especially “this is the one”—because Randi is so willingly inside the dream. I want to see Randi and Vee together next, especially whether Randi can keep calling Vee “gorgeous” without finally saying what she means. My guess from here: the cover-word will not stay safe forever.
