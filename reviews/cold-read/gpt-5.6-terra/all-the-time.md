## Reader reaction

This was deliciously warm and dangerous to me at once. Vee is so openly happy that I felt happy for her—her relief at being “finally hungry out loud,” her pleasure in the food, the quiet, and Pace’s attention all feel earned in her body. But Randi is what unsettles me. Her tenderness is real on the page, especially when she lets Vee talk about sleeping safely and being wanted “with the body attached,” yet her bright, briefly unfixed reaction makes me feel she is taking in far more than a friend ordinarily would. I trust that she cares; I do not trust that I understand what kind of care it is.

The erotic charge is strong, though more in confession and looking than action. It peaks for me at Vee admitting the “amount” of sex, then again when she gives Randi the uncropped photograph and waits under her gaze. The eggs Benedict—“The middle, when it goes everywhere”—feels pleasurably loaded without becoming clinical.

“Course it was” after Vee says the photograph was her idea pricked me. It is admiring, but it also seems to press Vee onward. So does “and you only gave him half?” Vee’s decision to show the full image feels like hers, but it comes with that small ache of wanting not to be thought timid.

“All the Time” means the new continuity of Vee’s wanting: sex, food, quiet, sleep, photographs, Saturdays with Randi, and the pull back toward Pace’s house. It makes the book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feel less like one invitation than a life Vee is increasingly being welcomed into. The series title and tagline still leave a chill beneath that welcome: every choice Vee makes feels freely made to her, but I keep wondering what she cannot yet see.

I badly want the Anthro class and the newly deliberate Randi time to arrive. My guess from here is that Vee’s relationship with Randi is becoming impossible for her to keep treating as merely a particularly close friendship.
