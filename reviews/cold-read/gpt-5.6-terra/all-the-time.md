## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel almost drunk on Vee’s happiness, and then afraid of how entirely she is giving herself over to it. The opening is gorgeous and immediately intimate: Randi appearing first out of the dark, “cream coat and a top the deep red-purple of mulberries,” the booth, the mimosas already waiting. Vee’s warmth that Randi “had even thought to make joining her easy” got me. She experiences even being expected as a gift.

I feel very tender toward Vee here, and also alarmed on her behalf. She is not merely reporting sex to Randi; she is trying to understand the scale of her new life. “The amount of it.” She is hungry, sleeping deeply, eating richly, looking forward to Friday while sitting in the library on Tuesday. Her happiness feels physical and real, not decorative. When she asks whether she is “getting greedy” or becoming “some kind of — I don’t know. Sex pervert?” I felt the old mother-voice still there, ready to turn abundance into defect. Randi’s answer — “You’re just finally hungry out loud” — is exactly the thing Vee needs to hear, and I felt the relief of it with her.

But Randi’s warmth has a peculiar force now. I believe she means what she says in the moment; “if you’re a sex pervert, you’re *my* sex pervert” is funny, affectionate, and also possessive enough to prickle. The chapter lets me see Randi get genuinely struck by Vee’s description of Pace: her eyes go “bright and a little unfixed,” she has to shake herself clear, and her voice isn’t even when she says, “It doesn’t.” I felt a real jolt there. Vee reads that response as pride — that her ordinary Saturday can move someone like Randi — while I feel the dangerous extra knowledge pressing behind it. Randi is hearing Vee describe a love affair Randi helped arrange and is secretly bound into. Her silence at “Like he wants *me*. With the body attached” is the most unsettling moment in the chapter for me, because the text earns it: she is visibly affected, and we know enough to understand why.

The erotic charge is strong, though less in direct sex than in confession, appetite, and being watched. It peaks for me when Vee turns over the photographs. The progression from the “safe” cropped image to “I gave him *all* of it” is hot because Vee chooses to show Randi, feels exposed, and wants not to seem like “a girl who flinched.” That last motivation makes the heat uneasy. Randi does say, “You don’t have to show me,” which matters; Vee’s choice is hers. But Randi’s “Legs like yours, though — and you only gave him *half*?” lands exactly where Vee is vulnerable. I felt Vee’s foot moving under the table and wanted her to be able to keep the photo private without fearing she’d become smaller in Randi’s eyes.

The food is intensely erotic too, in the best way. The Benedict’s “thick and gold” yolk and hollandaise running “everywhere,” Vee’s involuntary sound, her hand going back for more before she decides: it’s appetite without apology. It is especially moving that she can order “the good one, the one that cost,” not soup “so you could afford the chair.” She is practicing abundance in public now, not just in Pace’s kitchen. And her description of Pace’s cooking — “Like my mouth had been asleep since I was born and nobody told me. Like I’d been eating with the sound off” — landed as one of the chapter’s most romantic lines. Randi’s “He woke you up” feels both true and troublingly complete as a statement of what Pace has become to Vee.

I still feel strongly pulled toward Pace through Vee’s account. She describes his attention as total, sexual and otherwise; he cooks, listens, lets silence be comfortable, and makes her feel wanted as a person “with the body attached.” That is devastatingly romantic. But the chapter also makes the dependence more visible: Friday through Sunday are saturated color, while “the weeks in between ran gray.” Vee is happy, but she is beginning to frame ordinary life as waiting for Pace and Randi. The final sentence, pointing her Corolla back toward “the house in the trees, and dinner, and the long slow thing that came before dinner,” is sensuous and satisfying, but it also feels like she is being drawn back into the enclosed world where she has less access to perspective.

Cassie’s absence is felt. Vee calls Randi “the one friend she trusted with all of it,” and it aches, because Cassie is still her safest friend but is receiving only the emotional outline. Randi gets the photographs, the repeated sex, the wanting, and the plan for a class together. “Just us” is innocently thrilling to Vee, but Randi’s small roughness on the phrase made me wary. It is a new private space for the two women, and I want it for Vee — I want her friendship and possible desire for Randi to have room — but I dread the hidden structure Randi is carrying into it.

The chapter title, **“All the Time,”** initially sounds sexual: Vee and Pace have sex “two, three” times at night, then mornings and afternoons; she has “sex brain” all week. Vee freely orders Benedict, freely shows Randi the photograph, freely leans up for the second kiss, freely drives back to Pace. Her yeses are real.
