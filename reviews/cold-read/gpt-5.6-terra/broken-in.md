## Reader reaction

I felt such immediate, bleak tenderness for Vee waking in Pace’s shirt after barely sleeping, trying to turn herself from “crime scene” into “long night.” The cold water “like a verdict,” the bare legs in October, the apple: she is punishing and remaking herself at once. But when she decides, *I’m going to fix this,* I felt her gathering herself. I wanted badly for that resolve to stay hers.

Brooke at the door gives me a jolt. Vee calls her Randi’s sister, though I only knew her as the poised coffee-carrying woman with Brooke at Smoothie Bird; either way, Brooke knowing Vee is “the stats friend” is chilling. “The house had a folder on her anyway” is exactly how it feels. Brooke’s warmth remains measuring, and now the sorority house itself feels like a place where information travels without Vee’s consent.

Randi is enormously effective here. At first I was relieved alongside Vee: she gets her upstairs, listens, holds her hand, and does not pretend Vee’s accusation was fine. Her “That *bitch*” is almost comically satisfying, even though Vee herself knows the blonde did not actually deserve it. Randi helps Vee see that jealousy means she cares, which is a real and needed correction to Vee’s shame. The chapter’s emotional relief works on me because it works on Vee: “the thing Vee had been holding clenched … let go, because Randi had said so.”

But that relief turns uneasy very quickly. Randi does not just comfort Vee; she takes charge of what Vee will do, when she will do it, how it will look, and what it will mean. “Not today” lands as an instruction, not advice. The line “he’ll smell it on you” makes Pace feel almost like a creature Randi knows how to manage, and “he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing” is seductive but unsettling. Vee has come for help making amends, and Randi has converted it into an entrance.

The erotic charge is extremely strong in the dressing sequence—stronger for me because Vee initially experiences it as harmless, friendly dress-up. “Standing in her bra in front of Randi was no event”; then Randi looks, circles her, and says, “He doesn’t stand a chance.” That lands hard. The peak is unquestionably the shoes: Vee unable to see under her skirt, Randi’s “cool quick hands,” the hand holding her thigh while she balances, the thumb in “the soft hollow behind her knee,” then the cognac heels and Vee’s thought that surrendering the placement of her foot makes her steadier. It is hot, but it is also increasingly disquieting because Vee explicitly turns lack of control into security: “Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less.” Given everything I know about how Vee entered Pace and Randi’s orbit, I cannot take that innocently.

The kiss at the end changes something for me. Vee realizes she “hadn’t braced. Hadn’t tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi’s. It was only what they did.” I feel both warmth and alarm. Vee is no longer merely explaining kisses away as French or accidental. She is accepting them as part of the relationship. Randi’s affection is real on the page; I do not doubt that. But Randi is also shaping Vee into an image, putting her in her own shoes and silk, giving her “marching orders,” and aiming her toward Pace while privately being Pace’s lover herself. The title *Broken In* is sexy, funny, and ominous: shoes must be broken in; Vee is being dressed, coached, and made ready; and she may be getting accustomed to forms of handling that feel like care.

Pace is absent, but I feel more protective of him than I expected after ch.26. Vee understands that she hurt him and wants to repair it. Randi’s insistence that she wait one day may be sensible—an apology made in panic can be more about relieving oneself—but I dread how fully Randi now gets to author the repair. My guess from here is that Vee will arrive at Pace’s door tomorrow looking radiant and certain, but with words partly supplied by Randi rather than fully discovered by herself.

The book title *A Polite Invitation* now feels more dangerous than ever. Nobody is forcing Vee across a threshold. Brooke welcomes her in; Randi says “Come up”; Randi tells her to stand here, take off her top, walk, look; Vee complies and genuinely enjoys it. The tagline—“Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.”—is sitting right inside the shoe scene. *With a Long Spoon* still points me toward intimacy with something dangerous: Vee is taking comfort from Randi precisely when Randi is the least neutral person she could consult. I absolutely want to keep reading, but I dread tomorrow night’s polished apology and whatever Vee still does not know she is walking into.
