Yes, they find it funnier than I do,” consciously borrowing Randi’s introduction manner from their first statistics-class meeting. She then says people call her Vee.

- Randi introduces herself only as “Randi.” Cassie introduces herself from the next table with a wave. Pace repeats, “Cassie. Vee. Randi,” and nods.

- Pace asks with his eyes before taking Vee’s pencil and reads the problem twice. He says Vee’s work is right but that the problem is built to send her in circles. He writes a complicated formula in the margin, says she could memorize it, then agrees when Vee visibly dislikes it that nobody should carry it around.

- Pace makes a small right-angle crossed mark beside the formula and asks whether Vee has done Euler’s *e* to the *i x*. He explains that two difficult parts, joined “at right angles,” become a single thing that is easy to name and use. He returns Vee’s pencil.

- Vee applies the substitution in several clean lines, reaches a plain answer, checks it, and finds it holds. She recognizes that Pace chose a tutor’s route that left the solution hers instead of demonstrating his own ability.

- Randi asks Pace why sample standard deviation uses *n*-minus-one rather than *n*. He identifies Bessel’s correction and explains that estimating the mean uses up a degree of freedom, so division by *n* underestimates variance on average. Randi says, “Right. Right, I knew that. Thanks.”

- Pace asks Vee what she studies apart from statistics. She says econ and explains that she likes how economics addresses how people behave when they believe they are rational, the gap between stated behavior and actual behavior, and applied math’s use of structure in real situations.

- Vee talks for approximately four or five minutes about a research project she has thought about but not begun. Pace says “yeah” and “really,” asks a follow-up about her professor’s work, listens without checking his phone or interrupting, and laughs warmly at an unintended joke about her statistics class.

- Vee sees Randi smiling encouragingly. Randi has one hand around her coffee cup and the other in her lap. Twice she lifts the cup to her mouth and lowers it without sipping.

- Pace says he is a grad student in math, “Finishing a PhD. More or less.” Vee is embarrassed that she had warned him calculus was hard. He replies, “You gave fair notice. I took it seriously.”

- Pace checks his watch, says he has to run, then tells Vee, “But I’d love to see you sometime. May I call you?” Vee says yes. He asks for her number, types it in, repeats it to confirm, pockets his phone, nods to Randi and Cassie, tells Vee “talk soon,” and leaves without obtaining the coffee for which he had been waiting in line.

- Vee keeps one hand on her page and the clean lines below Pace’s crossed mark. She feels as though she has climbed stairs too quickly.

- Randi says, “He was cute,” then, “You should go out with him.” Vee says he has not actually asked her out and worries he may never call.

- Randi tells Vee, “It was a very cute thing, watching you not understand what was happening.”

- Cassie says Pace looked at Vee as if he had forgotten anyone else was present, but looked at Randi “the way you’d look at somebody’s great-aunt.” Vee admonishes her; Cassie says, “I’m only saying.”

- Randi says Cassie is right about Pace’s look and points out that he left the coffee shop without his coffee after giving up his place in line and spending the entire time with Vee. She says, “A man doesn’t forget his coffee over a girl he isn’t going to call.”

- Vee looks at Pace’s crossed mark and solution, then at her facedown phone. She does not turn it over, but lets herself want it to light up.
