## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Oh, this one hurt in the good, awful way. I finally meet Pace through Vee's eyes — and the vertigo is that I know everything and she knows nothing. The whole chapter is a magic trick I've been shown the wiring for, and it *still worked on me*. When he crossed the room "his eyes on her face the whole way, not on the table, not on Randi, on her," I felt Vee's chest go quiet along with her — and then, one beat later, felt sick, because "not on Randi" isn't gallantry, it's discipline. He's performing not-knowing his own lover, and he's flawless at it. "Randi," said Randi — one bare word, no adjective bit — and I nearly laughed at the nerve of it, these two doing improv over the head of the girl they picked.

The tutoring scene is the erotic center even though nobody touches anybody. The rolled cuffs, the forearms Vee's eye stays on "a half-second past where she would have chosen," the pencil handed back "at the exact line where she could solve it herself" — the book has taught me that Pace's seduction *is* attention, and here it is in broad daylight, fully clothed, and it's genuinely hot. What's devastating is that Vee, with her "econ eye," almost catches it: "The way he had—" and the thought doesn't finish. Twice. The author is letting her get within one clause of the truth and then letting the room swallow it. That's exactly what happened to her at Chi Latte — "blank transcript, kept feeling" — and it chilled me to see the pattern repeat with the other half of the pair.

And Randi. God. "Twice the cup came up to her mouth and went back down, and no sip happened." Randi is watching her lover seduce the girl she chose, and something is happening in her body she can't put anywhere — arousal? jealousy? both? Vee's brain "helpfully provided the explanation *she's bored*," and I wanted to shout at the page. Cassie "watched the cup without watching it." Cassie again. Cassie who clocks that he looked at Randi "the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" — and *says it out loud*, and neither Vee nor I can tell if she's just naming the flattering fact or filing the wrong note. I think she's filing. Cassie is the only instrument in this book that reads true.

Suspicion: fully earned, fully mine, none of it Vee's — that gap is the whole experience now. The one moment of real ache outside the machinery: Vee borrowing Randi's name-bit ("Yes, like the university") and then "suddenly wanting her own name back." She's already wearing their clothes.

The title — "Substitution." A calculus pun, obviously: the integral solved by substitution, turning the thing in front of you into a thing you already know. But it recolors instantly — Pace substituted himself into the scene; Randi's *n*-minus-one question was pure substitution-of-purpose; and Euler's identity, the true thing Vee has "known for years and never once used" — her buried capacities called up and put to work — feels like the book winking at everything Randi's been excavating. Two things at right angles becoming "a single thing that's suddenly plain": that's the threesome math, and I don't think I'm reaching. "You already know every piece of this" is the most double-edged line of kindness in the book so far. And "may I call you" — a polite invitation, verbatim. The jacket's trap is now fully assembled and Vee is holding the coffee, wanting the phone to light up.

Friction: almost none. Randi's closing sales pitch ("A man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call") is a shade slick, but that's Randi, not the author. If anything the chapter is *too* smooth — I kept waiting for one seam to show and the only seams were the ones I brought.

What I want next: the call, the first date, and — dread — how long they can run this before something (Cassie, I'd guess from here) tears the paper. I also want to know what Randi's cup-hand meant, because I don't think even Randi knows.
