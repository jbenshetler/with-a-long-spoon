## Reader reaction

This hurt. I came in braced for her to flinch, or retreat into the warmth again, and instead Vee holds herself there in the cold and asks the question cleanly: “What am I to you?” I’m proud of her for not letting Pace’s abundance answer it for her this time. The house is almost cruelly seductive—the low lamps, food, wine already poured, piano, the heat he turned up for her—and it all still works on me. I believe he loves caring for her. I believe his face opening at the door. But I also feel the trap Vee feels: everything real between them seems to have to remain inside that unlocked door.

Pace’s “You’re the most important person in my life” landed with a jolt of warmth, because it is enormous. Then “Words are cheap. Believe what I do” made my stomach drop. It isn’t nothing; it is plainly his sincere conviction. But he has been asked for words because Vee needs a way to live this relationship in the rest of her life. His silence after “Words are cheap, and you still won’t spend them on me” earns the devastation. I don’t suddenly read him as false or uncaring. I read him as someone whose care is real and whose limit is now hurting her badly.

The erotic charge is mostly memory and atmosphere rather than active heat: “the best of my life,” his careful desire, the body that comes “with me attached.” What makes it ache is that Vee explicitly refuses to call that sex dirty or wrong. She wants it. She just cannot let it be the entire language of their bond.

“Believe Me” initially sounds like Pace’s argument, but by the end it feels painfully double: he asks her to believe his actions; she has to ask whether he will believe that words are not a frivolous demand. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* now feel less like promises of a pleasant welcome than signals about the danger of accepting a beautiful arrangement whose terms cannot be spoken. I badly want Vee to be warm and safe tonight, but I’m relieved she left when his hand reached for repair instead of an answer. My guess from here: Pace may finally have to confront why saying the name of what Vee is to him feels impossible.
