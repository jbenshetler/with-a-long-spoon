The heat from last chapter is still in my body, but this chapter turns it into a chill: she comes home carrying something raw and private, and Brooke’s smiling questions make every answer feel like a tiny examination. Brooke is not openly cruel, and that matters—she is warm, organized, even apologetic—but she has the authority to make Randi account for herself. I felt Randi’s relief when Brooke says, “Go,” as sharply as a physical release.

Pace is absent, but Randi’s remembered line—“He’s hard to say no to”—landed horribly differently here. The page has not made me doubt his care or Randi’s consent; what unsettles me is that Randi herself cannot bear to follow the thought through. She said yes, asked, begged, and wanted him, and now she is alone with language that could make that wanting sound unlike hers. That is sadder and more frightening than a simple reversal.

There is no erotic activity here, but the erotic charge remains in the contrast between Pace’s warmth and this “light and synthetic and adequate” duvet. The cold room, her shoes still on, and her inability to cry made me ache for her. I trust the page’s tenderness toward Randi completely. Brooke feels socially dangerous, though not malicious; Sloane and Kinsey feel like a watching chorus more than full people yet.

The packet’s “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem” feels newly alive here—not because the yes has been undone, but because Randi cannot yet make room for what it meant. I want the next chapter badly, and my guess from here is that Randi will try to restore her immaculate ordinary life before she can face what happened.
