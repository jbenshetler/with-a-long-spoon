## Reader reaction

This left me cold in the body. Vee taking Pace’s shirt off because even his scent becomes a reminder that he is not there—and that she cannot name what she is to him—hurts. I feel especially protective of her here. Nothing has happened between them that cancels his care, but the care has become almost unbearable precisely because it is so specific and still will not answer her simplest need.

Cassie’s sleeping presence is oddly tender: she is there, steady and unintrusive, while Vee is stranded above her. Pace feels absent rather than newly suspect. I do not think the page gives me reason to doubt that the shirt, the warm rooms, the food, and his attention are real. But Vee’s thought that he may sleep “easy as a man with nothing grinding in him” makes the imbalance ache. She is carrying the question alone.

There is no erotic charge in the sexual sense, but the shirt on bare skin has intimate force. It peaks at “the clean warm of him” and then turns cruelly sad: the warmth is only Vee’s body returning heat to itself. That lands hard. The title, “Bare,” means Vee literally lying naked in the cold, but also without the cover story of wearing his shirt over her own sleep shirt. She cannot make the garment mean more than it does. *A Polite Invitation* still feels like it promises doors opened gently and willingly; this chapter makes me feel how politeness can leave a person waiting outside the one answer she needs. *With a Long Spoon* remains ominous to me, though I cannot yet say why from the pages alone.

I want Vee to sleep, first of all. Then I want her to ask Pace plainly—not because I think she owes the book a confrontation, but because this silence is costing her. My guess from here is that she will keep trying to make herself bearable to the uncertainty before she asks for a name.
