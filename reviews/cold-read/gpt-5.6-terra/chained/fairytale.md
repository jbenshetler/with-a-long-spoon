## Reader reaction

This chapter left me with a hollow, almost physical cold. I was swept up at first: Randi meeting John in the lift queue, their ridiculous instant family, her competence on the black run, the champagne, the blue dress, the white-tie room, the sleigh. I believed in the seduction of it right alongside her. “He could have left her behind whenever he wanted, and didn’t” is a genuinely attractive detail; so is the way he dances without making her guess. For a while, I wanted her to have the gorgeous night she so clearly knows how to make.

And then the chapter turns that beauty into something unbearably empty. The sex is consensual, wanted in the moment, and apparently good—but “a good sandwich eaten fast” is devastating. The peak of the erotic charge is the waltz: Randi letting John lead her with absolute clarity, enjoying not having to work. The hotel scene is explicit but emotionally remote on purpose; I felt her distance from herself more than heat. Her body is there, but she is watching it happen “from a long way off.”

I feel sympathy for Randi, not distrust. She is not being tricked by John; the page is clear that he is polished, generous, skilled, and safe. She realizes that safety is not the same as being known. Her thought of Pace is complicated because she has just privately measured him against this ideal man and found Pace reaching further, even “on his worst night.” And Vee arrives with even more force: Randi understands what she wants with frightening clarity, then chooses not to tell Vee because she cannot bear risking the friendship. “Vee was her gorgeous” landed hard. It is tender, possessive, frightened, and not yet an action.

John does assume sex, and Randi explicitly finds she likes not being asked that night; I don’t read that as a consent failure because she chooses him and follows him upstairs. But it throws Pace’s asking into sharp relief. With John, she gets ease, spectacle, and a safe script; with Pace, she gets being seen. With Vee, she gets a possibility she cannot safely name. That is a painful three-way truth, not a simple ranking.

“Fairytale” gave the game away a little in the best possible way: I expected an alluring fantasy, but its real meaning is the approved life Randi can perform perfectly and cannot inhabit. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, now feels newly pointed—Randi has made a polite, covered invitation to Vee in “miss your face most though, gorgeous,” while refusing to put the real question on the table. *With a Long Spoon* still feels like a warning about closeness that may be dangerous, managed, or both. The remembered packet’s line about freely given yeses now feels especially heavy: Randi’s yes to John is free, and still it gives her none of what she needs.

I badly want Randi and Vee to get one honest conversation, though I dread how much Randi has already decided to preserve the friendship by keeping Vee’s understanding partial. My guess from here is that the cover-word will hold only as long as Vee can keep reading it as friendship.
