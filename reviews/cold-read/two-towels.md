# Cold read — Two Towels

*scene: scenes/two-towels.md · read after: what-to-wear*

## Reader reaction

This chapter did to me almost exactly what I dreaded and wanted it to: it made me fall a little, right alongside her, and then the title sat in my stomach like a stone.

**The house and the reveal.** The slow tour is lovely and I let it work on me — the squared shoes, one right pair per thing; the used-not-shown kitchen; the office that "had thrown over order," green graph paper in square handwriting. It reads as a real person, built and self-contained. Then the escalation of gifts: he made the furniture, the joints with no screws, mixed his own stain and shellac; the patent (MRI, "somebody sick, somebody scared, a kid who can't lie still… out of the tube in a fraction of it"); and the detonation — *he made the shirt*, and now he wants to make the dress. I felt her chest not knowing what to do with itself, and honestly mine didn't either. It's a genuinely swooning sequence. The dress beat is beautifully turned: the flip from disappointment (*oh, it's not the right—*) into "He'd matched *her.*" That landed clean and hard.

**Pace.** Everything the page shows is disarming, and the "warming to it" — the error-correcting-codes riff, lit up "like a six-year-old who'd just found out everything there was to know about dinosaurs" — is the most human and likable he's been. And yet my carry-in dread turned this warmth *up*, not down, because I know what I know. Two specific page-level things caught, though, on their own terms:

- The house was **"too warm — a close, even heat with no draft in it,"** and she almost asked and didn't, "the way you walk past a wrapped box on the counter without asking who it's for." That simile is doing double duty and I felt it: something is prepared here. A house heated evenly, in advance, for an afternoon of being looked at, of being measured "down to what you'd wear under it."
- **The one closed door.** "My bedroom," said flat, left shut. She'd worn the satin for exactly that door and something in her dropped. On the page it plays as his restraint — his signature move of *not* taking. But the single closed door in an otherwise open house is a held card.
- The flinch at **"parents"** — "a small closing, there and gone, a door easing shut two rooms away." That's the second time the hidden-self thing has flickered. I still don't know what's behind it, and the text let it pass without explaining, which I liked.

**Two towels.** The title. In a bathroom, "plain, clean, two towels on the rod," in a house owned by a man whose whole grammar is *one right thing for each thing he does* — one pair of boots, the knives he actually reaches for, nothing redundant. Two towels breaks his own rule. It slid past *her* completely, and it nearly slid past me, which is exactly why the title is pointing at it. I don't know what it means yet, but a man who keeps exactly one of everything keeps *two* towels — that's either habit, or it's a place set for someone. Given everything I carry about Randi, that towel is the coldest object in the chapter and no one in the room noticed it.

**The measuring.** "To do it right I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it'll fall." Read as courtship it's exquisite restraint; read against my carry-in it's the same engine — he takes *looking*, he removes her decision to hide, and the only thing between her and the gift is "her own willingness to be looked at." That last line — "her own willingness to be looked at" — is the whole seduction in miniature and I felt the trap and the tenderness of it at once, unresolved, which is the mode this book keeps promising.

**Where it's slack.** Nowhere much. The prose is dense with lovely observation and I mostly rode it. If anything, the shoe-closet and kitchen inventories run a beat long — "one right thing for each thing he did" is stated, then restated, then restated ("the same logic as the shoes"). I got it the first time. Minor.

**Friction / thumb on the scale.** Very light. The one place I felt a nudge was the reveal being spelled out a touch: "Counterfeit. Sample. Off-brand. She'd never landed on the answer because the answer was absurd, and the absurd answer was standing in front of her." That's the text walking me through her deduction a half-step more than I needed — I'd already gotten there from "no tag in it." But it's in her voice and I forgave it.

**Erotic charge.** The heat here is almost entirely displaced into objects and provision — it works on me *anticipatorily* rather than in the body. The peak is the kiss at the door, and specifically **he** stops it: "the stopping was his; he put the brakes on… no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop." That reversal is the erotic core of the chapter — he withholds, and the withholding is the pull. The measuring offer is charged the same way: nothing happens, and the not-happening is the whole thing. It's arousing precisely because it's deferred.

**What I want / dread next.** I want — and dread — the measuring itself. Guess from here: the next scene is her being measured "in good light," in the satin she wore on purpose, and it will be the most erotic and most quietly ownership-shaped thing yet, all under the cover of a favor. I also want to know about that second towel and whether the closed bedroom door and the flinch-at-"parents" are the same secret. And Randi is *still* gone; the longer she's offstage the more I feel her, and now there's a spare towel in the house.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Vee drove out to Pace's small, isolated house off the gravel past Rte 614. At the door she took the kiss she'd been wanting since the mountain — and *he* was the one to stop it, which she'd never had a boy do. He gave her the full tour: squared shoes (one pair per purpose), a used working kitchen, a chaotic paper-covered math office, one **closed bedroom door** he left shut, a den full of **furniture he built himself** (screwless joints, homemade stain), a wall of books (fantasy paperbacks, cookbooks, high math), a spotless woodshop in the converted garage, and a glassed-in porch with a mountain view. He casually revealed he holds a **patent** (an MRI signal/denoising method) that funds the house, and flinched slightly at the word "parents." On the porch: a bolt of **burgundy silk** — he'd secretly bought cloth to make her the honor-society dress she'd named and let go on the hike, matched not to the magazine shade but to *her* coloring. The last piece landed: **he made the shirt** she'd worn to death (no tag). He offered to **make the dress**, which means measuring her "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light." She said yes — warm, immediate, no hedge.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — wide open and now overwhelmed; performs romance less here, feels more; wore satin under her clothes hoping he'd give her a reason to show it; her one guard is "her own willingness to be looked at," and it's dropping.
- **Pace** — maker of everything (furniture, shirt, soon a dress), patent-holder, self-contained, genuinely likable and lit-up about his work; his signature is *withholding/stopping*, taking only looking; flinches at "parents"; the tenderness reads real and I distrust it on carry-in, not from the page.
- **Randi** — Pace's secret co-orchestrator posing as Vee's friend; still conspicuously offstage. The house now has an unexplained **second towel** and a **closed bedroom door**, which I connect to her.
- **Cassie** — converted skeptic roommate. **Kayla / Meg** — Vee's dining-hall friends. **Theo** — turned-down nice guy. Sorority orbit offstage.

**How I feel.** More seduced than ever and correspondingly more uneasy — the gifts are magnificent and the whole scene is warm; my cold comes from three quiet objects/beats the room ignored: the pre-warmed house ("a wrapped box… without asking who it's for"), the **two towels**, the **one closed door**. The measuring offer reads to me as the seduction engine dressed as a favor — decision removed, looking taken, hiding disallowed. Mood: swooning with a stone in the stomach.

**Open questions.** What is the measuring scene going to be? What's behind the closed bedroom door, and why **two towels** in a one-of-everything house? What's the wound under "parents" / the hidden self? Where is Randi, and does the spare towel mean she's been here / comes here? Will Vee ever learn Pace and Randi already know each other — and will any warning catch before the dress is finished?
