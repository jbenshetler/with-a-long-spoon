## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter still lying on that cold bed with Randi, shoes on, deadbolt turned over inside her — *not ever* — and the first thing the page does is put her back in Pace's bed, slowing at the table "with its top back on," and I don't get to hear a word of what she's thinking. Everything here is his. That gap is the chapter for me: I know what he doesn't, that the woman he's reading so confidently decided in his bathroom that he could "have the face," and now I'm watching him read the face and the body and be certain he's got her.

And the thing is — the body does tell on her. The question, "Would you like to taste another woman?", asked while she's "still pulsing around nothing," and she comes *on the question*. That wrecked me a little, in the good way. "The question reached her body before it reached her." I believed it completely, and it was the hottest beat in the chapter, hotter than anything in the walk. Then his restraint afterward — waiting for "the afternoon to go ordinary around them," wanting her "free to weigh it and choose" — is the Pace I trusted on the bench, and I liked him for the hope that she could answer "dressed as play." But "He had decided this beforehand" sits in my chest. He chose the undefended moment on purpose. He says "He did not take it for her answer," and then he spends the whole afternoon acting as if it were.

Her answer — "Only in a threesome. With you." — I hold half-open where the page half-opens it. Pace reads it as a brilliant transfer, hunger laundered into a gift, "which he could see was unsurvivable." Maybe. Having watched her in the mirror two chapters ago, I think he's probably right, and it's a beautiful piece of watching. But it's *his* reading, and the page never lets Randi confirm it, and I notice how much the chapter wants me to take his seeing as the truth of her. That's where I feel a thumb on the scale: his near-omniscience about her interior, when I just read a whole chapter proving her interior is the one room he can't enter.

The walk. The "handful of times a year" afternoon, the single maple "burning orange in a field of things that had not yet decided to change" — gorgeous, and it landed before I noticed it was about her. "I can't eat that," to the cheeseburger, automatic — the apple all over again, the portioning. Then "What about her?" at the shell of her ear, and my own propriety flinched exactly where Randi's did: *pointing*, in daylight. The chapter knows it's doing something socially wrong and makes the wrongness part of the charge. "I point. You pick" — I laughed with her, and I felt the game's rules click into something that genuinely does give her the choosing. "She was shopping" — the follow leaning into a turn not yet called. That dance image got me; it recurs from the dance-trained line of her on the bench and it makes her wanting legible without a word from her.

The kiss on the library walk, his face unwiped since the bed, "the moment her own taste reached her" — yes. That's the second peak. "You dirty boy," sex-kitten, and I could hear the seam too.

Then Vee. I felt her arrive the way Pace did — head thrown all the way back, laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten... that she could be seen." After an afternoon of mirrors and matched pairs, after two chapters of a face arranged in bathrooms and common rooms, a woman who forgets she can be seen is the opposite of everything Randi has built, and I understood on sight why Randi "did not look away." The bare "Yes" — "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — and then the costume coming back up, "Yeah. I like her." That's the same disjunction she saw in the mirror, the smile sitting on top of the face that begged. It swooned me and it hurt.

And then the unease, which the page earned without my going looking for it. "Go get her." The engineered collision — Randi's decade of dance folded into "three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing," so that *Vee* apologizes. Vee's hand flies to her own mouth for a thing done to her. A stranger being chosen and maneuvered by two people who've already imagined her mouth at Randi's breast and "all that loose red spilled down over her." I'm aroused by the image and I'm uneasy for Vee, and both are true. Her name, a stats class — real girl, real schedule. Randi's half-second of happy footwork, her two different "yes"es, daylight and bed — that was pure delight, and then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

I went cold. Not at the heat of it — at where it comes from. The bench is where Randi cried on the leather and locked a bathroom door and decided never to come out again. And her first instinct toward the woman she wants is to picture her *there*. I don't know what that is — appetite, or wanting someone else to be undone where she was, or wanting to watch instead of be watched — and the page doesn't say. Pace kisses her head and says "Let's go feed you," and the apple bowl and the cheeseburger she couldn't eat make that line both the warmest and the most loaded in the chapter.

The title, "The Pointing Game," named the game before Pace had a name for it ("He had no name for the game yet") — so it gave the shape away, mildly, but not the Vee of it. The cover board — *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation* — recolors badly and well: a staged collision and two minutes of warm talk are exactly a polite invitation, and the long spoon is for supping with someone you shouldn't sit too close to. I don't yet know who's holding the spoon.

I want Vee's own chapter. I dread, as a guess from here, that she'll be offered the bench by two people who are each lying to the other about why.
