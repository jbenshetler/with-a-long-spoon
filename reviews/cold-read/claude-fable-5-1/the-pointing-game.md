## Reader reaction

I came off "Standards" cold — Randi under a synthetic duvet in her shoes, deciding the one he'd found was going back behind the face — and this chapter opens with her slowing at the table with its top back on and saying *Let's do something different. Today.* I felt my stomach drop a little. Not dread exactly. More the feeling of watching someone walk back into a room they'd locked, carrying the key like it's nothing.

And then the bed, and I'll be honest: the question took me the way it took her. "Would you like to taste another woman?" asked into "the warm disorder of her," and her body answering before she could — "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That's the hottest beat in the book so far for me, hotter than the bench, because the bench was Pace building something and this was one sentence knocking out the floor. His own heat at it — "It had gone through him too, that a single question had taken all that control out from under her" — I believed, and liked him for not pretending otherwise. The slow tender kiss "for no reason but that he wanted to" melted me outright.

But I know something Pace doesn't, and it colored everything after. When she comes back with "Only in a threesome. With you," he hears it as *beautiful* — "She had not taken back the wanting. She had moved it." I heard the bathroom mirror. I heard the face, very good at lying, doing exactly what it promised to do. He's not wrong that the want is real; her body said so twice, and he's careful to say "He did not take it for her answer." And then two paragraphs later: "So. There it was." And on the quad: "the clean line of what he already knew about her, what her body had told him an hour ago and her mouth was still busy correcting." So he does take it. That's the first time I've felt the ground shift under my trust in him. Not that he's cruel — he isn't, every move is gentle, "I point. You pick," the choosing hers — but he is so *pleased* with his reading of her, and he has no idea she went behind the door Saturday night and shot the bolt. "The gift was moving toward her hands." He thinks it's a gift. I don't know what she thinks it is. The page doesn't let me know, and I notice I'm grateful for that.

The pointing game itself swept me up almost against my will. The social wrongness of it — two people on a brick path discussing a stranger — and then her going still, the three beats, the "Too expected," the voice gone "lower, considering." Watching her stop being a good sport "a quarter mile back" and start *shopping*, "a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves." The kiss with his unwiped mouth on the library steps, her composure getting "a crack in it." The whole stretch reads like a dance, and I was in it. And then Vee: head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That line stopped me. It is the exact opposite of everything Randi is. Of course that's the one. The bare "Yes" — "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — is Randi's real voice, the voice from the bench, out loud in daylight, and then I watched the costume come up over it: "Uh — yeah. I like her." Both are on the page. I don't get to decide which one is her.

The collision choreography was pure delight — the redhead's hand flying to her own mouth, the half-beat Randi's hand stays on her arm — and I rooted for her, grinning, "a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do." The two *yeses*, the squeaky one and the breathy one. I wanted it for her. Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" and my skin went cold. The bench she wouldn't look at Saturday. The bench she begged on. Is she offering Vee the thing that undid her — as his thing, handed over, kept at arm's length? Or does she want to see it done to someone who can laugh with her throat bared? I don't know. The line is spoken low, "without lifting her head," and he just kisses her hair and says "Let's go feed you" — which, after "I can't eat that" and the thin apple slices, lands like a man who thinks he's answering her hunger and may be answering a different one than he thinks. The chapter title gives the game its name before Pace has one — "He had no name for the game yet" — so I came in knowing it was a game, and a little braced, which I think cost me the first beat of innocent confusion Randi had. Minor.

Friction: Pace's interior occasionally explains what the scene already showed — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner." I'd worked that out from her kiss on his jaw; the gloss is the author's thumb. Also "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer" followed by "So. There it was" — if that contradiction is Pace's and not the author's, it's the most interesting thing about him right now, but I can't tell yet.

The cover board: *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. No jacket, no tagline supplied. "Sup with the devil with a long spoon" — and here is Randi executing the politest invitation imaginable, a staged shoulder-bump and a stats class. Who is holding the long spoon, and who is the devil, I genuinely don't know, and it's unsettling me more than I'd expected after a chapter that was mostly joyful. Vee is the fourth person in this book to have two names, so I suspect — guess from here — she's not a one-chapter redhead. I expect the three of them in a room, and I dread, a little, whether Randi brings the face or the one from the bench, and whether Pace will know the difference when the third person in the room is someone who's never learned to hide.
