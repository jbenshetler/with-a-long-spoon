## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The first page took me straight back under. His mouth on her, her hand "not pushing, just holding, as if to confirm he was there" — and then him coming up into her before she's finished, "still pulsing around nothing." I felt that in my body before I had a thought about it. And the question — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — making her come, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That is one of the hottest things this book has done, and it isn't an act at all; it's a sentence landing in a body that answers before the woman can. I believed it completely. "On her face was astonishment, and under it the helplessness, and under that what the question had been for."

Then the move I can't stop turning over: "Only in a threesome. With you." I read it the way he read it — "She had moved it. She had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument" — and it made me ache, because it's the bathroom mirror again. Saturday she decided the one he'd found "was going back behind" the face. Here is the face, doing exactly its job, finding a way to say yes "without making the wanting stand naked between them." It's brilliant and it's sad and I love her for it and I wanted to shake her.

On Pace my feeling has moved, and I want to be exact about why. I still trust his hands. He waited for "the heat to leave the room" before asking plain; he "did not take it for her answer"; "I point. You pick" is a genuinely swoony line — the game is built so the choosing is hers, and watching her discover "that she could" was my favorite thing on the quad. But. "What her body had told him an hour ago and her mouth was still busy correcting" — he has decided which of her is the real one, and he's steering the afternoon toward the one he picked. Every woman he points at, he fantasizes first: "two sleek tails swinging... his hands full of both," "the sprinter was getting her out of her clothes from behind," "that mouth at Randi's breast, all that loose red spilled down over her." I don't begrudge him the heat — it's honest and it's hot — but it means the gift has a lot of him in it. And Vee: a stranger laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten... that she could be seen," now selected, approached by engineered collision, with Pace at a light pole "far enough back to be nobody." The page earned my unease there. Nothing here is unkind. All of it is one-sided.

And yet — the *yes*. "The word came out alone, with nothing around it." I stopped reading for a second. The whole afternoon of verdicts — "too expected," "you'd get both or neither," "God, no" — had taught me her taste by elimination, and then the redhead came out with her head thrown back and I knew before Pace said "Her." The collision was a pleasure to watch, "so clean it looked like nothing," and when she came back "failing to hide the grin" and her feet did "a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do" — that's the woman from the bench, out in daylight, and I was lit up with her. Then the second *yes*, "the register she only used in his bed and never in daylight." Swoon, full stop.

Then the last line of hers chilled me on the spot. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" The table she wouldn't look at Saturday, that she slowed at today. The bench broke her open and she decided never again — and here she is proposing to put someone else on it. I don't know what that is. Deflection? Appetite? A way to stand beside the bench instead of on it? The page leaves it open and so do I. And "Let's go feed you" — after "I can't eat that" to a cheeseburger — puts the apple back in my mouth. He's still feeding her thin slices of the thing she wants.

Friction: the imagined tableaux pile up a bit, and the dance metaphor comes twice ("a follow gathering herself," then "the follow had felt the music and broken frame") where once would have done. "The maple was burning" started to feel like a tic by its third appearance. Small things against a chapter that mostly had me.

The title named the shape before I got there — I knew a "pointing game" was coming once I hit the walk — but not the stakes, and not Vee, so it took little. *With a Long Spoon*: you sup with the devil with a long spoon. I keep asking who's the devil in this and I can't settle it — Pace steering, Randi managing, or the appetite itself. *A Polite Invitation*: the shoulder-bump *was* one, exactly. No tagline in my packet.

I want to read on badly. Guess from here: Vee's stats class gives Randi a reason; whatever Randi's wanting is, it's hers now and not Pace's, and I suspect he's going to find that out.
