**Felt read**

I came into this chapter still lying under that synthetic duvet with Randi, shoes on, waiting for nothing — and the first paragraph tells me she went back. She slowed at the table with its top on, looked at it this time, and said *Let's do something different. Today.* That landed on me before anything else did: the deadbolt she turned in the bathroom mirror didn't hold her away from him, it just changed what she brings through his door. I don't know whether that's a betrayal of her bathroom resolve or exactly what she meant by "he could have the face" — the page doesn't settle it, and I'm leaving it open. But I felt a kind of relief and a kind of dread at once, seeing her there.

The bed scene is hot in a way that's almost unfair. Him coming up into her "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing," the kiss with his mouth wet from her, *you like the taste of yourself* — yes. And then the question asked into "the warm disorder of her," and her body answering before she can: "she came, hard... far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." I believed that completely. It's the hottest beat in the chapter and it's also the one that made my chest tight, because it's the bench again — he's found a place in her she hasn't looked at, and she's been found before she can decide to be. "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." I want to trust that line. I mostly do.

The afterglow re-ask is where my feeling for Randi deepened past sympathy into something like protectiveness. The flinch. The tongue touching her lip. Then the recovery — "Only in a threesome. With you." — and Pace's reading of it: "She had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument: not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy." That's devastatingly exact and I think it's *true*, and the word "unsurvivable" is the book being honest about what the sorority house and the egg-white mother have built in her. I ache for her here. And yet she's "plainly excited," and I believe that too.

The walk. God, the walk worked on me. The burning maple, the colonnade, the cheeseburger she can't eat (that hurt — "I can't eat that," automatic, right after we've watched her eat apple off his hand). And then the game. My first reaction to "What about her?" at the shell of her ear was Randi's own — the "bald social wrongness of it" — and I felt that recoil as my own propriety. But I turned the way she turned. "Too expected." "You'd get both or neither." Watching her go from good sport to *shopping*, "a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves" — that's the swoon of this chapter, Randi discovering she has a taste and that it's hers. "I point. You pick." I grinned. Her strong *no* to the blazer girl who ran the room told me more about her than any of the yeses.

And then Vee. The page held me where it held Randi — "she did not look away." The bare "Yes" before the costume, then "Yeah. I like her" dressing it up. The engineered collision, so clean the redhead apologizes — I was delighted and a little chilled, because that's the same instrument that smiles at Brooke across the common room. The grin breaking through, the happy footwork, the two *yeses* — the squeaky one and then the bedroom one in daylight. That's the most unguarded I've seen her outside the bench. I melted. Then: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" and the floor dropped. She's already put Vee on it in her head. And I felt a flicker for Vee herself, who laughed with her throat bare to the sky and has no idea she's just been *picked*. That's not suspicion of Randi exactly, but it's unease the page earned: a stranger "chosen" by two people conspiring on a wall, and the whole book's first "we" being built on her not knowing.

Trust in Pace: holding, with a new grain in it. His care is real on the page — the waiting, the re-ask when she's "gathered back into herself." But this chapter lets me see more of his own wanting than before: "He wanted her himself," the picture of "that mouth at Randi's breast... and him there to watch." "It was the best idea he'd had in a month." He frames it as *for her*, and it's also for him. Not off, but not nothing.

Friction: "If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her" — a bit of thesis in Pace's head that I'd rather have felt than been told. The pointing sequence runs a touch long by the third candidate before it pays. Minor.

The title: *The Pointing Game* gave nothing away and then named exactly what it was — and it's the right word, because what's unsettling is the *game* of it, the lightness. *With a Long Spoon* I keep hearing as "sup with the devil" and I'm still not sure who the devil is; *A Polite Invitation* — Vee just got one, and the politeness is the point. No tagline in my packet.

Next: I want Vee's eyes, her own page. I dread — a guess from here — that the thing Randi locked away on the bench is about to be asked for again, by a woman this time, and I don't know whether the face can hold.
