## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter still lying under that synthetic duvet with Randi, shoes on, the lock turned over inside her — *the one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again.* So when she slowed at the table with its top back on and said *Let's do something different. Today,* I felt a small cold drop. Something different. From the bench. She's steering away from the place where she got found, and he doesn't hear it that way: "He said nothing to it. He took her to bed."

And then the bed took me anyway. I'll be honest: the first half of this chapter is the hottest the book has been for me, hotter than the bench, because the heat is *want* rather than process. His mouth still wet from her, "she took herself off his lips and kissed him back harder for it" — yes. Then the question dropped into her while she's "still pulsing around nothing," and she comes *on the question*, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That landed in my body before my head caught up, which is exactly what it did to her. The line I keep rereading is "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." That's the Pace I trust — the one who waited outside the bathroom door. He waits for the heat to leave the room before asking plainly, so she can "weigh it and choose." Good. That is what a yes being really a yes looks like.

But the reader I am now, after the mirror scene, can't read his certainty the way I did two chapters ago. "She wanted this. She had not let herself hold that she wanted it." "If pleasure was also a bridge, then let the wanting simply feel good in her." He's building toward a conclusion he's already reached — he "decided this beforehand," it's "the best idea he'd had in a month." Last time he thought *this had been a remarkably good evening* while she stood in his bathroom deciding never to come out again. He's a brilliant reader of her body and I no longer believe he's reading *her*. The text earned that for me; I'm not inventing it.

Randi's move — "Only in a threesome. With you" — I watched with something like admiration and grief at once. He names it and the naming is exact: "She had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument… The same act. A different owner." That's the face, doing what the face does. And then the pointing game. God. The first point, the propriety recoiling in her hand, then "Too expected" in a voice "lower, considering" — I grinned. "I point. You pick." She laughs "before she could decide whether to." I was swept, fully, by her shopping down the library walk, the follow gathering before the lead. The kiss with her own taste still on him, "You dirty boy" — the sex-kitten register with "the seam in it." I felt the seam too.

Then Vee. The description of her — head "thrown all the way back, laughing… like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen" — that is the single most desirable image in the book so far, for me, and I notice it's desirable precisely because it's the thing Randi has never once done on the page. And Randi's "Yes," "the word came out alone, with nothing around it." I believe that yes. Her stride dropped. She didn't look away. I melted at her feet doing "a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do," and the two *Yes*es — one squeaking, one breathy, the bed register in daylight. For one paragraph I wanted this for her more than I've wanted anything in this book.

And then the unease, which the page earned twice over. First: that collision. "A decade of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually taught" folded into an ambush "so clean it looked like nothing," engineered so *the redhead* apologizes. The woman who forgot she could be seen is being hunted by a woman who has never forgotten, with a man at a light pole watching "far enough back to be nobody." Vee doesn't know she was pointed at. Second: "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I went cold. Randi left the bench without looking at it, locked that door, asked for *different* — and the first thing she does with her real yes is wonder about putting another woman where she was. I don't know what that is. Displacement, sharing, revenge, curiosity — the page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but it's the darkest line yet, delivered as pillow talk. Pace just kisses her head and says "Let's go feed you," and *feed* goes straight back to the apple slices and "I can't eat that" at the cheeseburger. He still thinks he's answering her hunger.

Friction: small. The catalogue of pointed women ran a hair long, and "which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy" explained a move I'd already understood. Otherwise I bought it all.

Titles. **The Pointing Game** — I read it before the chapter and it meant nothing; now it names the whole second half and quietly indicts it: a *game* with a real stranger as the piece. It gave nothing away that I minded. The cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — no tagline or jacket supplied. "A polite invitation" just happened on that lawn: a stats-class acquaintance, names exchanged, hands lifted. And a long spoon is what you sup with the devil with. For the first time I feel the series title pointing not at Randi but at Vee — a *guess from here* — the one about to be politely invited to a house with a table that isn't a table.

What I want next: Vee on the page in her own words, not Pace's lens. What I dread: that the yes I believed will be managed into something else before she gets there.
