## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I came into this chapter braced, because I was still carrying Randi's bathroom mirror — *the one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again* — and the first thing the page does is put her in his bed with her hand flat on his head, *not pushing, just holding, as if to confirm he was there.* That undid some of the brace. And then the question. *Would you like to taste another woman?* — asked into her while he's inside her, and she comes on it, *far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it.* I felt that one in my own body before I could do anything with it. It's the hottest single beat the book has had since the bench: the want arriving as an event, before she can "make the wanting mean anything safer." That phrase lands because it's exactly what I watched her do in the mirror last chapter. The page knows her, and now so does Pace, and for a moment I was just swept.

But I want to be honest about the shape of that. *He had decided this beforehand.* He times the question to the one state in which she can't manage her answer. That is an ambush, and he knows it is, and the page doesn't pretend otherwise. What rescues it for me — and it does rescue it — is that he refuses to let the body's answer stand as consent: *He knew her body's answer. He still meant to ask her.* He waits for *the afternoon to go ordinary*, and asks again, and gives her room to answer *laughing, dressed as play.* That's the care I came to this book for. And her answer — *Only in a threesome. With you* — is the smartest thing she's done. Pace reads it precisely: *The same act. A different owner.* She hasn't taken back the wanting; she's handed him the deed to it. He lets the costume stand. I'm of two minds about that, and I think the page wants me to be: it's tender, and it's also a man deciding what her disguise is for.

The walk is where the chapter opens its hands. *I point. You pick.* I liked him for that line — he isn't shopping for himself, he's teaching her she gets to. And watching her go from *a very good sport about your little game* to *shopping* a quarter mile later, the follow's coil he knows from the dance floor, is genuinely delicious. The kiss outside the library — *he had not wiped his face since the bed* — made me catch my breath along with her. Then Vee. Head all the way back, *throat bare to the sky*, laughing *like a person who had genuinely forgotten… that she could be seen* — and Randi's bare *Yes*, *with nothing around it*, before she can find the kitten again. I believed it completely. It's the same thing I watched happen on the bench: the real voice getting out ahead of the arranged one. And then the engineered collision, so clean the redhead apologizes — I grinned at my own admiration of it, and then felt the small chill of what I was admiring. Randi can aim that instrument at a person. She just did.

The last exchange is where the chapter turns over in my hands. *Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together.* The happy footwork she didn't decide to do — that's the most unguarded thing she's done in daylight, and it melted me. Then, low, without lifting her head: *Do you think she'll like the bench?* She slowed at that table on her way in. She walked past it Saturday without looking. Now she's offering it — the place she was found — to a stranger, and the page doesn't tell me whether that's desire, or a way to put someone else on the bench so she doesn't have to be the one seen. I don't know. I'm holding it open; the page does. And Pace answers with *Let's go feed you*, which, after *I can't eat that* at the cheeseburger, after the apple slices, hit me somewhere under the ribs. He keeps trying to feed her. She keeps portioning.

Where I'm suspicious: not of anyone's warmth — of Pace's certainty. *What her body had told him an hour ago and her mouth was still busy correcting* — he's right about the want, the page shows me he's right, but he's a man who doesn't know a deadbolt went over in his bathroom four nights ago, and I do. He thinks *the gift was moving toward her hands.* I think it is, and I think it's also moving toward the last thing she decided to let him have. Friction, small: the imagined tableaux dropped in without a frame — *Both, then. The sprinter was getting her out of her clothes from behind* — stopped me for a beat before I understood I was in Pace's head. And *The follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step* explains a thing the bare *Yes* had already done better.

The title gave me nothing going in — I didn't know what the game was until his mouth was at her ear — and now it's exact, down to the rudeness Randi registers first: *he was pointing, in daylight.* The cover board reads *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*; no tagline, no jacket supplied. *A polite invitation* just got very literal: a shoulder-bump so courteous the other woman apologizes. *With a long spoon* I still can't place, and I'm keeping my distance from it. What I want next: Vee, in a room, in her own words. What I dread: that Randi has found someone to put on the bench in her place, and that Pace will be too happy to see it.
