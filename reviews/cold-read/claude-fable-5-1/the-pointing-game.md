## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

The first page took me completely. He has decided beforehand to slide into her while she is still pulsing — and I liked that he's decided it, liked the deliberateness, because by now I trust his hands. The kiss with her own taste on his mouth, her "ardent before she could think better of it," the "Yes. Warm, and a little caught by it." And then the question — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and she comes *on the question*, "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That is one of the hottest things in this book so far, hotter for me than anything on the bench, because it's a want she didn't know was there arriving through her body with no apparatus to intercept it. I believed it. I felt it in my own stomach.

And then the afterglow negotiation, which I found beautiful and sad in the same breath. The flinch, the tongue at her lip, the recovery — "Only in a threesome. With you." He reads it exactly as I read it: she "moved" the appetite, "handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument." A different owner. I know, from the bathroom mirror two chapters ago, what she decided: he could have the face; the one he'd found "was not coming out again." So I'm watching her do that *while he thinks he's seeing through it* — he sees the costume, but he reads it as a sweet detour toward a shared thing, "the best idea he'd had in a month." That line made me wince a little. Not because he's cruel — he is still the man who left the water bottle — but because he's so pleased, and I know something he doesn't.

The walk: the maple going over "all at once," the wind, her hair in one scrunchie "giving it nothing when she didn't" — I loved the page's eye here. The cheeseburger beat landed hard: "I can't eat that," automatic, and I thought instantly of the apple sliced thin. Then the pointing. My own propriety recoiled the way Randi's hand did — pointing at strangers in daylight — and then I got pulled right along with her. "Too expected." "You'd get both or neither." "Is that a yes or a no for you?" / "I point. You pick." That exchange is where I grinned out loud; it's the lightest, most *playful* the two of them have been. And the kiss on the library walk with his unwiped face — "the moment her own taste reached her because her breath caught" — pure heat, and her "*Oh*. You dirty boy" with "the seam in it." The page keeps letting me hear the seam. I'm grateful for that.

Then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her whole throat "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That sentence stopped me, because *being seen* is the exact thing that undid Randi on the bench and the exact thing she fled into the bathroom from. And Randi's response is the barest thing in the chapter: she stops, she doesn't look away, "Yes," alone. I swooned at that. Then she hears herself and puts the girlfriend-gift words around it. I ached.

Here is where the unease is earned, and I want to be precise about it. Not the fantasy — a woman wanting a woman is nothing to flinch at, and the page is right that it's *her* want. The unease is the collision. "A decade of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually taught" folded into three seconds "so clean it looked like nothing," engineered so the redhead apologizes, so Vee's hand flies to her own mouth. Pace stands at a pole and finds it "a thing to watch," and I was dazzled too — and then I thought of Brooke in the common room, the seated authority, and I realized I had just watched Randi do *Brooke's* kind of thing to a girl who laughs with her head back. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" went down cold. A week ago she couldn't look at the bench. Now she's planning to put a stranger on it. The page doesn't tell me whether that's appetite finally allowed, or the face finding a safer place to point the hunger. I'm holding it open. But I don't fully trust either of them with Vee right now, and that's new.

Friction, small: this chapter is all Pace's eye, and after the mirror scene I badly wanted one line from inside Randi and got none — the hum, the key in the lock, is she still hearing it? "Her body wanted this; her mind had not yet caught up to it" is *his* verdict, and I don't accept it as the book's; I think her mind caught up in a bathroom and locked the door. Also "He did not take it for her answer" sits oddly against an afternoon run entirely on "what her body had told him an hour ago and her mouth was still busy correcting." The page lets him contradict himself, and I noticed. The girl catalog went a shade clinical in the middle stretch — lavender, sage, the blazer — though each verdict of hers earned its place.

The title gave me the game but not the question, so it didn't spoil the thing that mattered. After reading, "pointing" means more: who gets to point, and the moment it turns from his finger to her stride. *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*: the "polite invitation" has just happened on a lawn, and it was polite in exactly the way Brooke was polite. No tagline in my packet. Next: I want Vee's face from her own side. I dread, as a guess from here, that she's warm enough to walk in without a spoon at all. "Let's go feed you" — he thinks he's answering her hunger. I'm not sure whose hunger just got fed.
