## Reader reaction

Oh — *this* chapter. The one I've been braced for since chapter 1, and I didn't know it until it was on me. We're finally in the room I've only ever seen through cracked doors and afterward-stories: Pace and Randi, in bed, together, in full. And it's not the scene I expected. It's not the game at all. Or rather — it's the game with the lights on, and what's under it is worse and warmer than I feared.

Let me start with the shock of the reader-position: for the whole book I have watched these two through Vee's eyes or through Randi's armored interiority, and here I'm suddenly just *with* them, present tense, no frame. And the first thing that hit me was how *easy* they are together. That banter — "Showing off." "You asked me to come here." — has the shorthand of people who've been at this a long time. There's real fluency there, real pleasure, and I felt the pull of it before I felt anything else. Then the whole scene pivots, and I realized what it actually is: **a confession conducted as sex.** "I miss her during the day. More at night." "She kisses me first now." "She kissed my neck again." They are using each other's bodies as the confessional for the woman neither of them can say these things to. And the honesty between them — that's the thing I wasn't ready for. Pace and Randi never lie to each other on this page. The two people lying to everyone else in the book are the only two people telling each other the whole truth. I had to put the book down at "I want her to know exactly what she does to me."

The erotic charge is genuine and sustained — this is the hottest stretch of pages in a while for me, and it's hot *because* of what's being said, not despite it. Randi describing the neck kiss while Pace moves her — "I wanted to turn around. Put my hand on her. Here." — her own hand closing on her own breast as a stand-in for Vee's, and Pace's mouth taking what's offered: that's devastating, the wanting literally routed through a third body. And when he asks "Why?" and answers himself "She makes me happy," and Randi's rhythm *falters inside the one he held* — I felt that falter in my own chest. Because there it is: he loves Vee. It's on the page now, in his mouth's shape if not the word, and Randi watches it land on his face and then says the unbearable thing: "She's not the only one in love." And he can't tell her she's wrong. No rescue. Her thumb moving once over his palm. That's a romantic beat as much as an erotic one, and it's aimed — wherever it's aimed — at *him*, and I genuinely don't know what she means by it and the chapter doesn't let me know. Is it Vee? Is it him? It sits there open and I'm leaving it open.

And then the last third — the cleanup, "I did my part. Now stick that inside me" — swings back into playfulness, and I noticed my own unease arriving late, under the humor: the mirror of Vee's Tuesday. Randi licking herself off him, kissing him after with "the faint salt of both of them" — the exact act Vee flinched from, that Randi said turned her on. It's a triangulated rhyme and I don't think it's an accident on the book's part. Everything Vee did on the county road, everything Vee confessed over the braise — these two have already done it, and done it *with her name in the room.*

My feelings have moved. Pace: I've trusted his care and distrusted his architecture all book, and here he is leading, in control, "You're still leading." "Yes." — but the confession cracks him twice, once at "she makes me happy," once at Randi's love-sentence, and I watched the composure reassemble by hand just like it did in his doorway over the mushrooms. He's a man who tells the truth in increments to the one person it's safe with. Randi: I keep thinking I've settled her and I haven't. The polished answer gone from her face — twice. "She makes me happy" undoes her, and "I want her to know exactly what she does to me" — spoken without humor, stripped — is the realest sentence she's said in the whole book, and it's not about Pace at all. What terrifies me is that the reader's dread and the lovers' appetite are converging: the triangle isn't a secret design anymore, it's the actual shape of all three of their wanting, and Vee is the only one still outside the room.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no suspicion of anyone *in this chapter* — no one is performing here, which is itself unsettling after chapters of everyone performing everywhere else. The performance happens in Vee's scenes; this is the back room where it comes off. That's what makes it hurt.

Friction: very little. The doll line — "using me like your fuck doll" — landed slightly more scripting than I needed from Randi, whose register is usually finer than that; it read a half-step b-way and then the scene corrected itself. Otherwise the chapter earns every one of its minutes, and the pacing — confession, climax, cleanup, second act — is the cleanest architecture in recent memory.

The title: "My Pleasure." On the page it's Pace's answer-shaped life, and it's Randi's command-shaped mouth — but after reading it, I think it's about who pleasure *belongs to* here. Vee discovered hers as a princess with a slave; Randi, the woman who "runs rooms for a living," spends this chapter having hers held, led, emptied, and confessed out of her. The one who does the working is the one being worked. That recolors the whole book's engine for me.

What I want next: Thursday. Vee still holding that stone of relief about the untold part of Saturday; Randi carrying "she's not the only one in love" around like the bracelet on her wrist. Somebody in this triangle is going to run out of rooms to lock. I dread it and I'm three pages from turning it.
