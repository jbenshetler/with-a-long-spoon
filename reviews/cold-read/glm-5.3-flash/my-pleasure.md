## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter straight off "The Reach" — Vee alone in the dorm, tongue a breath from her own fingers, unable to close the distance — and the title on this one made my stomach drop before I'd read a line. *My Pleasure.* Whose pleasure? I knew. I knew and I read it anyway.

And the book just sets it down in front of you, no wind-up: Randi and Pace, in bed, the affair I've known about since chapter one rendered at last in full on the page. The mechanics are gorgeous — her on top, owning every fraction of the distance, his strength "useless in it" — and I was hot, genuinely hot, for most of this. She's a magnificent erotic creature here: the comic post-clean-up gravity ("I did my part. Now stick that inside me"), the leading/following argument, the cleaning of him with her mouth as pure appetite. And then the book does the thing it's been building toward since chapter one and has Vee's Saturday morning arrive *inside their bed as their sex talk*. They debrief her. The parking lot. The hand in his hair going still. "She left you with zero thoughts." It's the brunch in the darkest mirror possible — everything Vee gave Randi in confidence, given back to Pace as foreplay. I felt the floor tilt under the heat. I stayed hot anyway. Both things at once, which is this book's whole method.

The neck-kiss report gutted me. "She kissed my neck again." / "She's coming along." Coming along — like a project with a schedule. And then Randi naming what she thought about — the breast in her hand, the nipple in her mouth — and coming with it. So Vee's want, the one Vee can't name, is being spent in this bed too, as fuel for the two people using her. I ached. Vee was in the room the whole time without being in the room, and the chapter knows it.

But the sentence that actually broke me was Randi's, flat, no rescue in it: "She's not the only one in love." And Pace's face softening before he could do anything about it — about Vee, from Randi's mouth. Not Randi's own confession (though I genuinely cannot tell if that's what it is — the page leaves it beautifully, terribly ajar; her thumb moving once over his palm, and that's all we get). It's the cruelest and most tender thing in the chapter at once: the word he can't say to Vee arrives here, via the woman he's lying to her with. I wanted to put the book down. I kept reading.

What I want next: Thursday. Vee's stone of relief, the story she keeps not telling. And I'm dreading the moment this bed and that brunch table touch — because they already have, and Vee tasted it on his mouth without knowing what she was tasting.
