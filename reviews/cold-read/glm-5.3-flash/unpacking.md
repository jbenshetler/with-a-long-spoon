## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This one is a slow exhale, and I loved it for that. After two chapters of body and heat, the book sits me down in an overheated dorm room with an open window and lets the weekend come out of Vee sideways, and it turns out the most intimate thing in the chapter isn't sex — it's the laundry lie.

Because that's what it is, and I felt it land with a small cold weight under all the warmth. "Pace shrank your sweater." He didn't. The record stood exactly where she left it, corrected by nobody. And here's the part that got me: it's a *kind* lie. She's doing the thing she's always done with Cassie — editing the sexual middle out — but this time the edit is doing more work than covering a spanking or a bet. It has to explain an entire naked weekend, a girl who left her bag in the car because there was nothing important in it, and the story she builds is almost too neat: one sweater, ruined by accident, and the toast burn as the only casualty. And Cassie — Cassie who reads Vee whole without asking — laughs the rare full laugh and lets it stand. I couldn't tell, and I don't think I'm meant to, whether Cassie buys it or is simply spending nothing. That's the good kind of open. It put a thin unease in me I didn't want there, right in the middle of a chapter that made me happy, and I think that's the point: Vee's new life is wonderful and she still can't carry all of it into the room where she's most herself.

And the warmth is real. I melted at the card from Cassie's mother — "she wants to know when she gets to see you again" — and at Vee's hand going to the collar of her robe instead of the towel, and her not fixing how small her voice came out. That's a hand cupped at the back of her neck, exactly as written; Vee has a family at school and she's only now feeling the shape of it. The shower that runs "simply less" and her being happy in it anyway — that's the chapter's whole thesis, that she can go back to the small life and it doesn't shrink her anymore. The hand flat against the cold cinderblock a hand's width from where acreage used to be, and the wall "was only a wall" — I ached at that, the good ache, the homesick-for-a-person ache. Friday will come.

I also keep feeling the momentum of last chapter's dinner plan sitting under this one — Pace wanting to meet her people, all of them, "let me handle it" — and this chapter quietly raises the stakes on it without mentioning it. Randi will be in that room. Cassie will be in that room. Vee has just shown me exactly which stories she's told each of them, and they don't match. I'm already bracing and delighted about it.

The title: on the surface it's Cassie's duffel, socks in one stack, tees in another, unpacking suspended mid-way. But it recolored for me as I read — it's Vee, unpacking her weekend for Cassie and packing the true thing back in the bag. Everything important stays folded. The book's whole silence, in a dorm-room image.

**Structured block**

**Cast present (in person):** Vee, Cassie. Mentioned-only: Pace (in the stories and the shirt), Randi (in the sweater-counter story), Meg, Kayla, Cassie's mother and family.

**Heat:** 0 — deliberately; this is afterglow and friendship, the weekend's heat only echo.

**Romance:** 2 — not between lovers on the page, but the tenderness is everywhere: the card, the shirt collar, the hand on the cold wall, "he would just have to wear it for her this weekend."

**Motifs & images:** the flannel shirt worn and smelled ("him still, but going faint" — repeat since ch 11/27/40); the open window against the heat (Cassie's signature); the bunk one girl wide vs. the king bed's acreage; the lie as packing — new motif, and a dark cousin of the cropped photo.

**Symbolism:** the suspended unpacking — Cassie's duffel half-done, Vee's truth never fully unpacked for anyone.

**Characterization:** Vee deepens — her happiness now includes being able to return to smallness without losing herself, and her lying has grown more practiced, which worried me. Cassie deepens by spending almost nothing; the full laugh and the precision of "done with the subject the exact moment before it would have needed anything" are pure her. Pace, offstage, keeps landing as the man who remembers heel sizes.

**Pace — within the chapter:** perfectly judged length; a quiet chapter that earns its quiet, never drags.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** a true deceleration after two hot chapters, and the right one — the book breathing before the dinner it loaded last chapter. Building, not sagging.

**What I want / dread next:** the dinner. Every thread points at it — Pace "handling" the venue, Randi's appetites, Cassie's flat eyes, two incompatible sets of stories in one room. My guess from here: something cracks there, or nearly does.
