## Reader reaction

This chapter gutted me, and I mean that as the highest compliment.

The walk home is where it starts — she can't leave the night at the party, and neither can I. The thong coming off and her fingertip finding the new bare of herself, and the picture arriving unbidden — *Randi's mouth would do what to this* — is one of the most honest moments of want the whole book has given me. Not performed, not staged. Just a body and a thought she didn't authorize. And then the hand against her own breastbone "like something she'd caught stealing," and the reach for *boyfriend* the way you reach for a rail — god, that word. She built the whole chapter's title out of that rail, and the title is doing exactly what the book does: naming the thing she's holding onto while she slides.

Because she doesn't stay. She puts the skirt back on. *He'd want to see me in this,* and "did not look at the thought too hard" — I felt that refusal of attention like a draft through the room. And she leaves the thong half-on and won't ask herself why, and I'm sitting there knowing exactly why and watching her not know, which is the position this book has kept me in since chapter one and it has never once gotten old.

The drive out and the dark house — she rings twice, he opens in boxers, and she's on him before his sentence finishes. And the sex is *wrong* in a way that's the truest sex writing in the book so far. "She was not trying to have him. She was trying to get free of something, and her body went about it like a fight." Coming with her face turned into his neck so she wouldn't have to see him see her. I ached for both of them. And then — he knows. Of course he knows. "That was angry, Vee." His stillness, his hand just resting, the question with no push in it.

The confession machinery is where the chapter became, for me, the best thing in it. She lies by telling him Harrison — a true-shaped lie that she can't bear to leave inside him because it's *false* and would hurt him — and so the truth tears loose. That's her whole moral character in one gesture: she cannot stand a lie *sitting in someone else's chest*, even when the truth costs her everything. And "I kissed Randi... and I kissed her back" — I felt my own breath catch. "It didn't stay a joke" is as much of the true thing as she can say, and it's enormous.

And then he plays her. That's the part I have to hold honestly, because it's both unbearably hot and quietly frightening, and the page keeps both fully lit. The stalling, the rationing — *tell me the real story and I'll give you what you want* — each truth bought back an inch at a time. He makes her say "my ass," makes her say "I didn't try that hard to get my hands back," makes her say "I wanted it." And she loves it. She *says* she should feel rationed and doesn't — "it felt right" — and my swoon and my unease ran on the same wire, exactly like her shame and heat do. Because here's what only I know that she doesn't: Pace has been *wanting this*. The man who engineered her into his life is now, in the dark, extracting from her a confession that opens a door he and Randi built together — and when she throws "you want Randi" at him, that flicker crosses his face, and he defends himself for the only time I can remember. "You keep saying her name, Vee. Not me." And she finds it *sweet*. Finds his self-defense, the one crack in his habit of leaving her the whole room, the sweetest thing he's ever done. I wanted to put the book down and put my head in my hands.

Then the two collapses at the end, one after another. "Anything else you want to tell me about Randi?" — and she says "No, nothing," level as a countertop, having just told him the whole kiss, while the goodbye kisses and her own hand in her dark room sit right there unsaid. She's not even lying, exactly; her rule sorts them before they reach her mouth, and I could feel how airtight it feels from inside and how damning it looks from out. And then the question that isn't about Randi: "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" And her reflex-no collapsing into nothing, and "I'm not gay" / "I didn't say gay" — that exchange is perfect. He never once pushed past what she offered; that's been true of him since the first chapter. It's also how a patient man farms.

The last pages are the loneliest and the most tender: the relief of *I got away with it*, followed immediately by Tuesday sitting at the end of the dark, circled. Her mouth still tasting of salt and lime even after everything he put inside her — "she could not have told, even now, which of them she'd been chasing at the end" — and I can't tell either, and the book doesn't tell me, and that's where it means to leave me. *What is wrong with me.* No answer. Outside the trees the cold went on being kept out. That last line — *whatever had hold of her was not, even now, anywhere near done* — is the chapter admitting it's a door and not a landing, and I walked through it willingly.

Where does this leave my feelings? Vee has never been more sympathetic to me — her honesty-within-evasion, the way she cannot leave a false thing in his chest even to save herself, is genuinely noble and genuinely doomed. Pace I trust as a lover and no longer pretend not to fear as an architect; the extraction scene is the clearest evidence yet that his patience is also appetite, and I notice that when she accused him of wanting Randi, he never actually said no — he said *you keep saying her name.* That's a fact about her, not a denial, and I'm holding it open. Randi is offstage the whole chapter and still its gravity. Cassie asleep below, silent, a good cold draft of a conscience in the room.

The title, having read it: "Boyfriend" is the word she reaches for as a rail, the plain straight fact she clings to while everything else gives way — and it's also the word Pace spent six times in a roadhouse without ever once needing it before, and the word that never arrives as *love*. The book titles the chapter with the thing she's hiding behind, and I think that's exactly right and exactly cruel. Volume title, *With a Long Spoon — Book Two: A Warm Reception*: the spoon keeps earning its proverb, and the reception could not have been warmer or more compromised.
