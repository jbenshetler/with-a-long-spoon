## Reader reaction

I came into this chapter expecting a first-day-of-class chapter, something to breathe between the coat and the dinner, and instead the book quietly handed me one of its most charged hours yet — and almost none of the charge happens in a bedroom.

The Dr. Marsh lecture stopped me cold. *Hau* — the spirit of the giver riding inside the gift, the pull of a thing back toward the hand that gave it, why you can't regift your grandmother's sweater. I sat there knowing exactly what I was reading about. Every good thing in Vee's life arrived as a gift from two people she can't see giving — the shoes, the heels, the play itself, "Randi, whose mad gorgeous idea it had been, who had handed her the play like a queen lending a sword." The gifts are *inhabited*. Something is riding inside the objects. The book has been running this thesis through food and flannel and burgundy silk for fifty chapters and now a professor said it out loud in front of both of them, and Randi's pen moved and stopped and moved. I don't think I was meant to miss that, and I didn't.

Then dinner, and the retelling — which is the oldest and best trick this book has, because Vee telling the coat story is itself a seduction, staged on both sides of the table. I melted at "Your shoes," and Randi's hands flat on the table, quietly triumphant, saying nothing at all. The wickedness of that scene is real and I loved it: Vee painting the foot on the chair, "And behind me the skillet goes quiet," and the heat stopping being remembered and simply being there. Then the bathroom. I'll say it plainly: the stall scene is explicit and hot, and what makes it hot is that it's not about Pace — the page makes that undeniable at the top of the drop, when Randi's face arrives behind Vee's eyes and the question stops being a question: *there's no way he couldn't.* And then Vee stands there and reassures herself — "that had been about Pace, obviously" — and I wanted to put my hand over hers. Obviously. The third restroom, she counts; of course she counts the practice room. The self-deception is getting thinner and she's still not looking at it, and I ached for her the whole walk home.

But the thing that actually scared me: "Things were really heated. It's a good thing you got relief." I felt the floor tilt with Vee. Randi read her through a floor — or knew, or — I don't know how. And what Vee does with being caught is drink the wine and stay, which is its own terrifying intimacy. This is the woman who can read her whole, and who is steering her, and the surveillance and the cherishing are the same hand on the same table.

And then Randi gave me the robin's-egg blue. The mother setting the laundered underwear beside her plate — "They're not mine. They're your color" — she'd had them washed, she'd waited for the boy, she *knew her color*. The fun going out of the brightness, the nail working the rim of the glass. That is Randi's shame-voice origin story, delivered to the one girl whose shame-voice I already know by heart, and the symmetry nearly knocked me over: two girls taught by their mothers that being known is the punishment. No wonder Randi flinched at the neck kiss. And no wonder Vee kissed it — unasked, unthought, bending to the spot where her tears had landed. That little kiss is the furthest Vee's unnamed want has ever gone with its body, and Randi's "ticklish" was the fastest door-close I've seen her do. I'm holding that flinch open, the way the page left it.

The stone under Vee's breastbone at the end — relief that the "part after breakfast" went untold — makes no sense to her and perfect sense to me. The kiss that went sideways is the one story that touches Randi's mouth. She'll tell it Thursday. She won't, is my guess from here — or she'll tell a version of it, and Randi will be waiting.

The title, *covering*: the coat decent to the collar over nothing; Vee turning the coat around to show the lining (covering turned inside out); Randi covering the story's escape routes; Vee covering the bathroom trip; the wrap blouse "doing a better job of showing her breasts off than of controlling them." Everything in this chapter is a covering over a telling, and the title names the whole mechanism. And *A Warm Reception* — the dinner, the meeting with Pace tomorrow, the reception Randi is preparing for — reads, after this, like a promise with an edge on it.
