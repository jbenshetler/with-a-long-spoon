### Impression

Vee has my whole heart, and this stretch mostly gladdened me — she is eating, swimming, striding, pointing, taking; the porch-flash, the truck, the road, the pointing finger in his bedroom are all *hers*, and the book is honest enough to keep the shame and the heat fused while the shame slowly loses its grip. But ch 58 is the saddest thing in the book so far: a woman one breath from her own hand's taste who cannot close the distance, lying down in her own mess because it's hers. She is so close to something and the door in her won't open, and I don't know whether to ache or cheer.

I trust Pace's care more every chapter — the headlights, the ten and two, the "boyfriend" said like a fact, the blush Sheri pulls — and I still don't trust his architecture, which this span made worse and sweeter at once: the man who hosts her friends flawlessly is the man who was in bed with her best friend the same week, being told she loves him, and saying nothing. The empty word is no longer just a wound; it's becoming a choice. Sheri, though — Sheri I trust completely. Five feet of exactly-what-she-chooses-to-say; the month nobody showed up for; she's the best new person in the book and I wanted to sit at that taproom table.

And Randi. Ch 59 is the most tender and the most damning scene yet — she and Pace making love inside their shared, confessed want of Vee, "she's not the only one in love" hanging in the room over both of them — and I still cannot file her as villain or guardian. She loves Vee without currency. She is also, right now, the only one of the three who knows the whole shape of the triangle and is using the knowing. The MIRH point still sits in my chest. The table is set for three, the reader has been seated since the first course, and nobody at that table has said the one true thing out loud — while Vee, glowing, tells herself she picked well.
