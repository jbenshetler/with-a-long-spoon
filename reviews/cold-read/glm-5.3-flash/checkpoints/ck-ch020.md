### Story so far
The book opens behind Vee's back. Pace and Randi have been lovers for about four weeks (ch 1): at his house off 6-414 she locks the deadbolt, he feeds her apple slices cut thin, and he introduces the bench he built for her — head-down, open — and takes her through a slow spanking-and-edging scene until she begs in a broken voice she has never used, comes harder than she knows how to be, cries, and locks herself in his bathroom, where she decides that the self he found behind her face is going back behind it and he can never know. (He, meanwhile, thinks of Daphne and the cost of opening closed doors, and holds her while she leaks.) The next morning (ch 2) she walks into her sorority in last night's clothes and Brooke, the chapter president, pleasantly interrogates her — his name really "Pace"? grad student? math? not your section? — while Randi lies smoothly and Sloane and Kinsey supply commentary. Then (ch 3) Pace takes Randi walking on campus and plays the pointing game: *I point, you pick.* She rejects the girl from inside her own grammar, a matched pair, the room-running blonde — then Vee comes out of the dining hall, tall, copper-haired, laughing with her whole throat, and Randi says "Yes" before she can dress it, engineers a collision, and comes back glowing: "Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together."

From there the book moves inside Vee's life (ch 4 onward). Randi appears at stats, sweeps Vee and silent Cassie into her orbit, takes Vee to Chi Latte, and gives her the best hour of her months; Vee keeps the feeling and loses the words. At Wilson's coffee shop (ch 5), stuck on a calculus problem, Vee is approached by a stranger who asks before he helps, hands the problem back so she solves it herself (Euler's formula), and turns out to be a finishing math PhD named Pace; he asks *May I call you* and leaves without his coffee. He texts within twenty minutes (ch 6): frozen yogurt, the long way, and Vee confessing it is the nicest anyone has been to her in years. A pool chapter (ch 7) shows her history: synchronized swimming, the body that ended it, her mother's voice, and Cassie's wordless protection. He asks her to dinner without demanding an answer (ch 8) — Cassie: "That's not a boy, that's a man" — and at Christine's Middle Eastern restaurant he says *May I choose* and orders for her in a language she can't read; the evening goes soft; under a tree she refuses the kiss, he lets it stand, and she pulls him back and kisses him. ### Impression
Vee: I am entirely on her side and slightly heartsick for her — the want has been so starved that being fed looks like being loved, and right now, page by page, it mostly *is*; the shame loop breaking open in the fitting and the bedroom is the best thing in the book. Pace: I can't decide how afraid of him to be. The craft is undeniable and a little chilling — the pointing game, the calibrated patience — yet the tenderness never reads as false; he protected Randi's locked door and never once took what Vee didn't hand over. I trust his *wanting* her; I don't yet trust the plan around her. Randi: the most wounded and the most dangerous person on the page — I believed her cold performance until the water glass trembled, and now I think the plan is starting to eat its own architects. Cassie is the only person I'm not the least bit worried about; if this goes wrong for Vee, Cassie will be the one standing there with a witness statement. My unease lives entirely in what Vee doesn't know — and in how long the book can keep her happy before she finds out she was invited.
