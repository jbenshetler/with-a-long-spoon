### Relationships

**Vee ↔ Pace** (core)
- **State:** involved · surface: "boyfriend" (the word she put a question mark on, to her parents) / true: something unnamed by him — she is "the most important person in my life," but the word *love* has never crossed from him to her.
- **Flags:** consummated: y (first in ch 18). She loves him: y (internal, ch 35, unspoken). Reconciled after December rupture: y (ch 50, the coat). He has kept her nude photograph (she sent it herself; he promised never to share it). Spanking/consensual power-play established and escalating (ch 29, 32, 50).
- **Axes:** warmth ↔ cold: warm, both poles tested (his December wall vs. his relenting warmth). isolation ↔ belonging: she has crossed from isolation into belonging in his house (her own bathroom drawer, the shirt, the warm house). cherished ↔ used: she feels cherished — the pages show him genuinely cherishing; the reader knows she was chosen by design. desire-worked-on-her ↔ desire-hers: he works her desire with endless patience; the desire is also hers — she claims it ("I wanted it, and I took it"), and the book keeps both true.
- Where they stand: reunited, happy, full of sex and pancakes and midnight pancakes-adjacent tenderness — and the center of it still has a silence in it where his word should be.

**Vee ↔ Randi** (protagonist's other central pair)
- **State:** friends · surface: best friends, "dear friend," "love you girl," mouth-kiss goodbyes now unremarkable · true (Vee's side): a want she won't name (the flat helpless want at Chi Latte, the prolonged kiss she chose to prolong, the stopping hand in the bathroom stall); true (Randi's side, shown to reader): love without currency for the first time in her life — and a deliberate decision not to tell Vee anything that would let Vee decide.
- **Flags:** mouth-kissing: ongoing (since ch 17). Vee's desire for Randi: unadmitted but on the record (body answered every telling). Randi's withholding of the game: y (ch 46).
- **Axes:** warmth ↔ cold: radiant warmth on the surface; the reader holds both the genuine cherishing and the steering. isolation ↔ belonging: Randi is the door into the world Vee watched from outside (Chi Latte, the boutique, the Mercedes). cherished ↔ used: Vee feels cherished; the reader has seen the game. desire-worked-on-her ↔ desire-hers: Randi deliberately works desire in Vee (the brunch, the practice room, the coat); Vee's own want is real and increasingly hers, though she still can't name it.
- Where they stand: the most intimate relationship in Vee's life, and the one the reader watches with both tenderness and dread.

**Pace ↔ Randi** (non-protagonist but core to the machinery as the reader sees it)

### Story so far

Randi comes to Pace's house; he reveals the bench he built her; a long consensual spanking scene takes her somewhere she has never been, and afterward she locks herself in his bathroom and decides — silently, permanently — that he can have the face and never the person behind it. (That decision is the checkpoint the book keeps testing.) At his suggestion they play the pointing game on campus: he points, she picks, and the red-haired girl coming out of the dining hall breaks her frame — "Her." Randi stages a collision, learns the girl's name is Vee, and that they share a stats class. Vee is lit up by the meeting; coffee at Chi Latte; the right pen; "love you, girl." Vee, stuck on a calculus integral, is helped by a pleasant, ordinary-looking stranger — Pace — who gives her Euler's identity and gives back the pencil; he takes her number and forgets his coffee. Froyo, then dinner at Christine's ("May I choose?"), then the first kiss — Vee's exception, her doing. The mountain hike: moleskin, horseradish, cherries, leave-no-trace, the dress she can't afford named and let go; rain; his only dry shirt; she undresses for him in the truck cab and a cop's siren ends the night. First visit to his house: furniture, patent, workshop, and the burgundy silk — he offers to make the dress; the measuring; the fitting; the soaked satin and his upturned, delighted face. Randi extracts the fitting story over Sunday lunch in the Mercedes and closes the door gently when Vee nearly tells all; the first mouth-kiss goodbye, explained away as French. The Friday dinner becomes the first night (ch 18): shame and heat "on the same wire," the scar glimpsed, the folded green panties traded into his hand. The finished burgundy gown hangs in his window; she wears it bare and strides to him; they pause mid-act to hang it properly. The induction: she wears the gown with nothing under it and gives the room her leg on purpose; the photo goes whole to Pace, cropped for her parents. The lost-bet spanking (ch 32) — "he checked" — lands as the first fully-given night; the "trade" of the panties becomes the shirt-trading economy; the bathroom drawer becomes hers. Randi's brunches become the confessional: the fitting, the swat, the bet, "he checked" — each telling working desire in both of them; Randi's stories (the scarf, the Radford boy) work on Vee in return, and the goodbye kisses stop being braced against. Pace's histories surface by firelight: Peter, the hall at fifteen, Daphne, the scar ("not tonight"). The Rusty Nail fight: Vee punishes him for the man she wanted; the bartender's verdict; the pie-labeled apology in Randi's heels ("leave them on"); the dishwater swat that lights her up; the lost bet and the quiet ceremony of ch 32. Brunch (ch 34): Vee confesses the bet in daylight, excuses herself, stops herself at the edge in the bathroom stall, returns to "Sticky fingers?" — and a kiss that leaves the taste of glaze she never ate. The nights multiply; she sleeps whole nights for the first time; she loves him in her chest and won't say it because he hasn't. He gives her the top drawer, the scrunchies, the photo taken of her by her own phone at his direction. Her pre-dawn sentence ("crackers... a meal") is met with "I love how good we are together" — near enough to hear, not the word; she retires the sentence into her body. Chili's proves he can't live in her old fine; the fight about the name ends with her walking out into the cold. Gstaad: Randi meets John on a lift, plays at marriage and children, buys the story-dress, dances a better lead than Pace's, goes up without being asked — and lies beside a faultless man feeling nothing, deciding over Vee's midnight text to keep the brunches exactly as they are and let Vee arrive at the truth on her own. Vee spends the break in a room that no longer knows her, building a blonde from spite, unable to keep even her own first night's memory from turning blonde at the table. Pace, at the diner, tells Sheri the truth sideways — "she wants a declaration... I can't" — and Sheri tells him plainly that not saying it matters more to him than having her. Home again, wrecked, Vee goes to Randi's and is walked off the ledge and into a plan: show up sure, nothing under the coat, Randi's black heels. She does it — winds herself up at the mirror and on the road and leaves it unfinished on purpose, knocks on the shut door, opens the coat, and the wall in him comes down all at once; he takes her against the open door and the couch, then feeds her pancakes at midnight and says "I missed you so much" with his voice cracking. Sunday she walks into the dorm in his sweats and Randi's heels, and Cassie's eyebrow says the outfit already told it.

### Impression

Vee has my whole heart and most of my worry. She walked into this starving and every open door fed her, and watching her take her own pleasure, her own shoes, her own stride — "I wanted it, and I took it" — is genuinely moving; the coat scene is the bravest thing in the book and I believed every button of it. I trust Pace's care completely and I don't trust his architecture: the care is real, the withheld word is a locked room, and the reader knows what Vee doesn't — that the room she was welcomed into had her name on the guest list before she knew there was one. I keep turning over whether the design corrupts what grew in it, because the book keeps insisting both are true. Randi is the one I can't look away from and can't settle about: her love for Vee is, for the first time in her life, not currency — and she is steering the girl she loves for sport and appetite both, and chose the friendship over the truth, and I do not know whether that is protection or the deepest use of all. The MIRH point sits in my chest the way it sat in that glass room. Cassie I trust without reservation; she sees everything and spends nothing. Right now the book has me exactly where its jacket promised: ahead of Vee from the start, and falling for it anyway.
