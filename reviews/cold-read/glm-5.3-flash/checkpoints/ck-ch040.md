### Story so far
The book opens inside the secret: Randi comes to Pace's house off 614; he puts her on a bench he built and spanks and edges her until she begs in a voice that isn't her performing one — "There you are, Randi" — and afterward she locks the breakthrough back behind her face, deciding he can have the face but never the woman behind it, and that she must hide him from her sorority. In bed days later, Pace asks if she'd like to taste another woman; her body answers before she can. On a campus walk they play the pointing game — he points at passing women, she picks — and when a laughing copper-haired girl comes out of the dining hall, Randi says "Yes" before she can dress it, then engineers a collision. The girl is Vee; they have stats together.

Randi closes on Vee: stats class, Chi Latte, "dear friend" declared in front of Brooke. Randi then engineers a second meeting — a stranger "overhears" Vee stuck on an integral; it's Pace, math PhD, who gives her back the pencil and the trick (Euler's formula), takes her number, and asks "May I call you?" Froyo, then a proper dinner: he says "May I choose?" and orders for her; under a tree she breaks her own rule and kisses him. He takes her hiking; fixes her boot with moleskin; carries out every cherry pit; at the summit she confesses the russet dress she can't afford ("That sounds like a beautiful dress"). In a cold rain he gives her his only dry shirt and stands in the rain while she changes; she strips her wet shirt off for him in the cab; a cop's siren ends the night before a kiss. He makes her a dress from burgundy silk, measured on a riser in his overheated house — the fitting floods her with shame-want; he kneels, sees everything, and only smiles up at her face. Lunch with Randi pulls the secret out of her; Randi kisses her on the mouth in the street.

His house, dinner, first sex: green lingerie bought as a "campaign," the shy heat breaking open, the trade of the damp panties. Randi debriefs her over mimosas — "You're not greedy, you're finally hungry out loud" — and extracts everything. The dress is finished; they ruin the afternoon with it. In bed Pace and Randi compare notes: he wants Vee to arrive on her own; Randi admits she's in past her own depth and describes Vee's body with open want. Fireside, Pace gives Vee his history: college at fifteen, the desk in the hall, Daphne, the scar he won't open yet — she calls him Peter to his sleeping ear and keeps the name. Country dancing; a blonde poisons her with "he's so good about getting a girl home safe"; Vee stings him publicly and badly, then, coached by Randi (robed, barefoot, dressing Vee in her own shoes and scarf, another mouth kiss), returns with a HUMBLE PIE label and a clean apology. At the sink a wet-handed swat lands and lights something neither names; they go to bed on it, and she stays two nights. In a music-practice room she tells Randi about the spanking and Randi answers with her own over-the-knee story until Vee has to flee to a bathroom and stop herself short. The induction: she walks the slit open on purpose, bare under the burgundy, and sends Pace the photo from Berkeley. She proposes the pinup photo herself; he poses her like a calendar girl and she sends it. Brunch with Randi: the bet, the checking, the bare-bottom spanking — Vee tells it all in daylight and has to leave the table; Randi's "Sticky fingers?" hangs unanswered; the glaze on Vee's lip is from a pastry she never ate. Vee loves him, unsaid; he gives her the bathroom drawer. She stops coming home school nights; Cassie asks if she loves him — "Maybe. I think so." Photos migrate from her phone to Randi's eyes at Saturday brunch; Randi's reactions grow oddly raw; they sign up for anthro together, just them. The stats project on PPP loans turns up Miranda Holdings LLC — Randi's family — as the biggest outlier; Randi says "Cheating bastards" once, ragged, and Vee quietly covers her and tells no one. Then five nights apart for school; she lasts five, comes back, and asks him to wear the flannel all weekend so it will smell like him again. He puts it on over his shirt without making her ask twice.

### Impression
Vee is the easiest person in this book to love and the easiest to worry about, and both feelings are getting louder. Her appetite waking up is genuinely moving — the girl who portioned apple slices learning to eat like a field hand — and her courage (the slit, the photo, the apology, the shirt) keeps paying off in dignity rather than humiliation. Pace: I trust him almost without reservation, and I keep noticing that my trust is exactly what the book is built to hold — he is tender, exact, never takes what isn't handed to him, and every good thing he does is also the mechanism. The reader's knowledge sits on my chest: every lunch Vee calls the best of her week is an extraction. Randi has become the one I watch sideways — magnetic, generous, funny, and lately too interested, her appetite leaking out around the edges of the friendship while she plays the safe, warm confessor. Cassie I trust completely; she's the only person in the book with no angle, and her flinty non-warmth toward Randi feels like the healthiest instinct on the page. Sheri intrigues me — the one character with full information and no stake in the performance. Nobody has yet been cruel to Vee except the blonde and her own mother's voice; the danger so far is all tenderness, and the dread is that the tenderness is real on every side at once.
