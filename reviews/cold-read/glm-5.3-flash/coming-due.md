## Reader reaction

**Coming due** — and the title finally cashed in. This is the chapter where the door Saturday cracked open stops being a door and becomes a draft she can't close, and I read the whole thing with my chest tight.

What gets me is how precisely the book renders the wanting as *unownable*. Vee doesn't decide anything here; she endures. Monday night, hand flat on her stomach, "a lid held down on a pot" — because "as long as she didn't give it the thing that would make it true, it could stay only wanting." That's the whole tragedy of her in one sentence, and it's the same grammar as the shame-heat loop from way back: the wire that runs wanting and wrong through the same filament, except now the wrong isn't her mother's voice about her body — it's the friendship itself. Randi is the one person she can't charge this to. There's no Pace-shaped object to pour it into this time, and the book knows it: "In his bed there had been him for it... Here there was no him."

And Randi. God, Randi. Rereading her through what I now know — "she's not the only one in love," said flat in Pace's bed with no rescue in it — every gesture here reads doubled. The Lifesaver, the finger along the inside of Vee's wrist ("static," Vee tells herself, and it goes on not being static), the neck kiss at the door. That neck kiss wrecked me. It's the exact spot, the weaponized spot, and Randi does it *while holding her*, laughing "See? How do you like it?" — playful on the surface, and underneath it either the cruelest tease or the truest thing she's ever let Vee feel, and I genuinely cannot tell which, and I think Vee can't either. Then "I've wanted to do that for the longest time" — *having* — and Vee catches the possessive half a beat late, the same lag as in his bed. The word wants to mean something and Vee won't let it finish arriving.

The kiss she turned away from is the bravest and saddest beat. She takes the miss *on purpose* — the first truly deliberate refusal she's made in this whole book, the girl who has never once managed to stop a thing before it started — and Randi just... absorbs it, reads her, and escalates anyway. "I'll walk you to your dorm. We can chat, and I'll make sure you get home safe." Vee tries to buy her way out of Randi for the night and buys more Randi. That's the pattern of her whole life with both of them: every exit door she finds opens onto more of the thing she's exiting.

The small moments landed hardest for me, actually. Sunday: "she drove home in the early dark full of him and not, somehow, filled." One clause and the whole marriage of Pace-and-Randi wanting is stated. She starts sex with him twice and it doesn't hold. The plum sweater at the closet — unable to say whether she's dressing to be looked at or covered — that's Vee's entire architecture in one hanger.

And then Cassie. The last three lines are the best ending the book has done. Cassie, cold still coming off her coat, four words a day Cassie, asks the plain question with worry actually in her voice — and Vee, who has an answer for everything, an edit for every audience, stands in the doorway with nothing. "Her oldest friend had asked her the plainest question there was, and she could not answer it." I sat with that a second. Cassie sees it. Cassie has always seen it — the eyebrow, the flat patient look she's never spent on Randi — and now the book has finally let her spend words on it, and it scared me more than anything Randi did.

Trust and suspicion: I trust Vee completely, I ache for her. I don't trust Randi at all and I love her anyway — the brunch request lands like a trap being accepted by the person it's set for. Vee proposed it herself, out of the ringing in her neck, which is the most Randi outcome possible: the wanting does the recruiting. And there's a Thursday-sized gap now — "I've got to go discipline a bad girl" — Standards committee, Brooke's house, and I don't know what to do with that except file it with a small cold feeling I can't justify yet. Marked as a guess from here: Thursday is going to matter.

Heat: the chapter is almost entirely chaste on the page — a wrist graze, a knee, a neck kiss — and it ran hotter on me than half the explicit chapters. The wanting does all the work. The class hour with her leg held still is unbearable in the best way.

Friction: almost none. Marsh's reciprocity lecture sliding off Vee was exactly right — the one night the book's little lecture-craft doesn't reach her, and the book admits it. If anything I wanted more of the Monday insomnia, not less.

The title: "coming-due." A debt arriving. Marsh's reciprocity lecture is sitting right there — the gift that is a debt, the hau that wants to go home — and now I see it: every gift Randi has given, skirt, shoes, advice, the coat plan, is coming due, and what's being called in isn't money. It's the wanting, or the truth, or both. The book's been running a tab since October.

Cast present: Vee, Randi, Cassie (end), Marsh in person at class. Pace present only as absence and Sunday. Mentioned-only: Brooke (via Standards), Pace.
