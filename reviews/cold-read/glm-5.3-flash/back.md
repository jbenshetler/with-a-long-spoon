## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This one got under my skin in a way I didn't expect from a chapter where, on the surface, absolutely nothing happens. A woman talks to a man's back while he does dishes. And it's one of the most loaded chapters in the book.

The warm part first, because the warm part is real. He's been *keeping* her. Not the polite listening — the shelf. The boots being Meg's, said once on a mountain in October in half a sentence. Kayla talking the paint off walls, her own line handed back to her in his flat certain voice. When Vee felt "something she couldn't have named settle in her, warm side down," I felt it too — that specific pleasure of discovering you've been heard when you'd braced for being merely tolerated. For a girl who was eating crackers her whole life, being *remembered* is its own kind of feeding. And "Bring Theo," no flinch, no question — after she braced for the question — that landed on me the same way it landed on her. That's a man secure enough not to need the reassurance. Or a man performing security. I genuinely don't know which, and the chapter doesn't tell me, and I sat with that.

Because here's where my stomach went, and I have to be honest that it's the reader's knowledge and not Vee's that put it there: he says "I want to meet your people" and it *arrives as Randi*. Of course it does. And I know — Vee doesn't — that the man volunteering to meet her best friend properly is that best friend's secret lover. "Let me handle it," he says at the end, mild as milk, and to Vee that's a gift, the man taking the burden of planning off her. To me it rang a bell I couldn't unring: this is what he *does*. He handles things. He points; someone picks. He sets the table before you arrive. The whole book's central dread — was the room she was welcomed into built in advance? — just quietly extended its perimeter to include her entire found family, at his suggestion, over dishwater. I'm not saying anything bad is going to happen at that party. I'm saying I read "Let me handle it" with a cold finger down my spine, and the chapter earned it from me, because the last time he handled something, I was in on the blueprints.

And then the fork. The picture that surfaces unbidden — Pace across the nachos at the Rusty Nail, squaring his fork to the table edge, *I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you* — that did something tender to me. That's the fight she's still carrying, the man she punished for not being the man she wanted, and here he is asking, unprompted, to be let into every part of her life she's never shown him. Her people. Her floor. He's asking for the thing she was starving for him to ask for, without her ever having said so — which is either growth or him having read her perfectly again, and once more the book lets both be true.

The title, "Back" — I read it three ways and I think it means all three. His back, the good listener, the thing that makes her run on longer than she means to. Giving back — her own lines, her own details, returned to her like proof of account. And the sense of a book turned around, facing the other direction: after a chapter ("Missed a Spot") that was all body and heat and her reigning over him, this one is all interior and quiet and him holding the reins without either of them noticing. The volume's title, *A Warm Reception*, now looks almost savage to me — a reception is literally what they're planning, her two worlds at one table, and I know things about two of the guests that the guest of honor doesn't. Warm is doing a lot of work in that phrase.

Small friction: almost none. If anything, the chapter is *short* on the table — I wanted more of the roster-building, more Kayla comedy — but that's greed, not a flaw. The "warm side down" construction is odd enough that I paused, but I think it's deliberate: the settled feeling has an underside, and Vee declines to look at it, and so does the prose.

What I want next: that party. God, that party. I want it and I dread it — Randi and Pace in one room with nine words of math between them and a whole history, Cassie's flat patient eyes doing their audit, and Vee radiant in the middle of a table that was set before she knew there was one. That's my guess from here, not knowledge: the party is where something cracks. I'll be there for it.

Pace both deepens and thins by design: more generous than ever, and deliberately unopenable — I know his memory is an archive and I still can't see into the room where it's kept.
- **Pace — within the chapter:** compact and seamless; one scene, one turn, earns its length. The roster riffing could have run longer without hurting it.
