## Reader reaction

The felt read:

I went into this chapter braced exactly the way Vee was braced — I've been carrying that built blonde since Christmas too, the tan legs in the wine light, and when Pace said "There's somebody I'd like you to meet" my stomach did the same drop hers did. *Sheri.* And then the book did the cruelest, kindest thing it could have: the blonde died on contact. Five foot nothing, two-tone hair, crooked makeup, a longneck and a grin — and the heat in Vee's chest *stayed*, because it had never needed the blonde. I felt that land in my own ribs. A month of dread and the rival turns out to be the person I immediately want in my life.

And Sheri is wonderful. I loved her instantly and I don't trust my own instant with anyone in this book, which is maybe the saddest thing about where I am as a reader now — but this one I think I can trust, because she costs Pace nothing to be around and everything he gives her is unengineered. The hug with its whole weight committed. The *you get to motorboat them things* and Pace BLUSHING — I sat up. He blushes. Two years of this book and I have never once seen color climb that man's neck, and Vee noticing it is the same jolt I had. He's different in her company — off duty, the one company that never wanted a thing off him — and that phrase does quiet work, because everyone else in his life has wanted something off him, including me.

Her story wrecked me. Coming out and losing the whole tablecloth — mother, Marine father, brother, church, every girl she'd driven to Roanoke — and crying in a two-step and the one man who showed up with coffee and donuts and a fixed lock and no false words. *He knows how it goes. Went bad with his daddy. He hasn't been home since he was sixteen.* Sixteen — that's a new number, and it settles next to the scar and Daphne and the hall like another room in the locked wing of that house. And the horror underneath, the one Vee doesn't have: the man who can hold a stranger through the loss of everyone she loves — that man engineered the loss Vee would suffer if she knew. I held that while Vee held her cider and didn't drink it, and it hurt more than anything on the page, because the page was so warm.

Also — Sheri is gay. The book just set a woman down in front of Vee who lost everything for loving women, who looked at Vee's keyhole and bare January legs with that slow *enjoying* look and the soundless *oh* she kept for later, and Vee laughed her month of dread out in one breath. I don't know if the page is doing what I think it's doing, holding a mirror up — Sheri as the honest version of a life Vee keeps almost-touching with Randi — but the resonance was loud in my chest. "Bring your opinions." I underlined that with my whole heart.

The ending undid me gently: the headlight vigil on the duplex door, the middle of the bench conceded to the smallest by ancient law, the filthy song parodies I'll be ruined for in checkout lines too, and *bacon cheeseburgers* said to the window. "Like something full he was carrying, and had no intention of spilling." That's him. That's the whole problem and the whole beauty of him in one line.

Heat 1 — the charge is all in Sheri's appraisal and Vee's flustered glory in the rig she built for a rival; nobody's touching anybody, and it still hums. No swoon-quibble: this chapter simply worked on me, start to finish.
