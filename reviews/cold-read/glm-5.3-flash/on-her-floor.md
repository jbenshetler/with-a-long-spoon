## Reader reaction

Okay. That kiss. Let me start there because everything else in this chapter is runway for it, and the landing is enormous.

The whole first half disarmed me. The Chi Latte opener — "You I just get to have" — is Randi at her most bewitching, and I felt Vee's yes come out "with no floor under it" before Vee did. The dressing montage got me: Randi's room, the three dresses, the iridescent skirt handed over like it was nothing — and I know from the fitting and the boutique that nothing Randi hands Vee is ever nothing, but Vee doesn't, and the chapter lets me sit with both. "Being included was worth the risk." God. That's the whole girl.

And the detail that broke me was almost nothing: Vee in her room with no mirror big enough to see the whole outfit at once. She gets herself "in pieces" — face and shoulders here, mid-thigh there, never the whole. That's her entire life in one image, and the book just set it down and walked on. Cassie seeing it whole — "On Randi that's short. On you—" — and not reaching for her sorority speech, that's Cassie spending exactly the right amount.

The party itself ran hot on me in a way I didn't expect. The skirt is doing the coat's work in reverse — the coat was decent over nothing chosen on purpose; this is too-much showing that keeps happening to her — and the exposure scenes genuinely burned. The hem climbing song by song, the arithmetic of "how high was still a joke, how high stopped being one," the green thong chosen the night before when it was nobody's business: that's the shame-heat loop running in public, and when the panties-showing lands I felt the flush climb with her — and then the turn, "it was not only awful," and I recognized the wire from ch 18 and ch 50. The page has earned that fusion so many times now that it lands like a chord.

Then the dance. Randi's hands on her hips, up her sides, lifting her wrists, running her whole body off a grip — I was half-floating in the sensuality of it, and half-sick, because I'm ahead of Vee and I could see the choreography. "Let's put on a show — make him wish he were me." Harrison is a prop. And then the held inch — Randi pausing a breath short of her mouth, letting Vee want it, letting Vee *watch herself* want it — that is the cruelest, most expert beat in the book so far, and it is also, on the page, dead sexy. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. The perfume "catalogue she'd been keeping for months without admitting she was keeping the file," the taste "salt and lime and under it Randi, particular" — I was swept up. The want Vee finally feels all the way — "she wanted Randi's mouth lower" — is the named-adjacent thing the whole book has been rationing, and it arrived in a roomful of people with a boy watching, which is somehow both the hottest and loneliest possible place for it.

And then: "I almost believed it." I actually put the book down. Because Vee takes it — "the only thing held out to her" — and folds herself back into the show version, says *god, his face* in the voice that knows how, while her body is still ringing with nowhere to put any of it. That last paragraph is devastating. She "knew almost nothing... not what she'd done, or what it had been." She was not performing. Randi framed it as performance and Vee had to live inside the frame. And the reader sits there knowing what the frame is for.

Do I trust anyone right now? Randi has never felt more off to me — and the text earned every degree of it: "She's here with me," the hand at the small of her back before the boy even asked, the second margarita Vee never saw ordered, the kiss staged for Harrison and then carried ten seconds past any bit's stopping point, the flinch when Vee kissed her neck Tuesday now reframed here as ticklish. And yet — "came back from it a moment behind... drew a breath, and shook her head, blinking." That's on the page too, and I don't know what it is. I'm leaving it open, because the page does. Brooke's warmth I now half-distrust on pure instinct — the folder, the perfected switch-on smile, "she'll spend a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime" — Randi describes Brooke and describes herself. I don't think the book meant me to miss that. And the Love bracelet: *you don't take it off, it requires tools.* Named Love. Welded on. I nearly gasped. The book just told me something about Randi through a piece of jewelry and let Vee laugh and tuck it away.

Pace is absent and everywhere — "He gets me Friday. He gets me Sunday. You get me Saturday night," said like the luckiest arrangement in the world, and I know where the Saturday girl sleeps. Brooke's recitation, gentle and complete, was a small gift — Randi's shoulder "coming warm against her arm" right at the wrecked-December mention: Randi arriving at the wound she caused, standing beside it. I don't know if that's comfort or cover and the chapter doesn't say.

What I want next: I want the fallout, and I dread it. Vee's body is "still sure there was more coming." She can't ask. She'll go to Pace tomorrow with Randi's taste still on her — and I remember the truck, the taste-hunting tongue, "he likes it... he's enthusiastic." The ingredients for a collision are all on the table and none of the people holding them can see each other's hands. Guess from here, and only a guess: this doesn't go back in the box. The kiss was real for Vee and Randi knows it was real, and "I almost believed it" is either mercy or the deepest use of all, and I genuinely cannot tell which — which is exactly where this book has always wanted me.
