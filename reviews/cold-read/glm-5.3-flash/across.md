## Reader reaction

The felt read:

This is a breather chapter, and I didn't mind for a second — after the coat and the naked morning and the whole charged weekend, the book sits me down in a sun porch with Vee's floor-mates and lets me watch her be *happy*, and it turns out I needed that as much as she needed the waffles. Kayla is exactly as advertised — "I planned my *morning* around it" — and I laughed at her more than once, honestly laughed, the mourning over the locked dining hall doors, the orange juice in a flute "which repaired her feelings completely." Meg is the dry fulcrum as always: "Okay. This is a place." These two are a real floor, a real friend-group texture the book has only sketched before, and giving them a whole scene makes Vee's world wider right before Pace walks into it.

The retelling is the chapter's spine, and it's doing something sly I only caught on the second beat: Vee tells Kayla and Meg the coat story *edited*. "I flashed him on his front porch" gets the full production — Kayla's shriek, three heads turning — but the drive, the winding herself up, the deliberate unfinished want, none of that. She gives them the scandal and keeps the interior. And I noticed *how* she edits, because the recent chapters taught me the gesture: she cropped herself at the waist for her parents, and now she crops the wanting out for the girls. Same skill, kinder audience. It made me fond of her and a little wistful at once.

Meg's Matrix-movies apology theory — "sometimes you have to make it right in their language" — is funny and warm, and also quietly wrong about what Friday was, and Vee lets it stand: "It was a big fight." Which is true and not true, and the toast that follows — "We're better than we were" — landed on me as both entirely felt and slightly curated, the size of it she *brought*. I held that loosely; the page lets it be sincere too.

The swoon, for me, was small and specific: Vee reading the right side of the menu "out of old habit" and then *not doing any arithmetic at all*. Christmas money waiting for an occasion. After a book where every cost has been weighed — the dress she couldn't afford, the split checks — that's a whole character arc in one sentence. And then the check *does* get split three ways, "nobody letting anybody cover anybody," and that's its own lovely counter-note: she spends on her people, but she will not be kept by anyone but him. The chapter knows the difference.

What actually has me leaning forward is Wednesday. Pace wants to meet her people — all of them, Theo included, which he took without a flicker ("Bring Theo," and she felt something settle "warm side down" — so did I). But I know what she doesn't, and I sat through this entire sunny chapter with Randi's name sitting at the end of that dinner table like a loaded thing. Vee's two worlds in one room: the woman who is being seduced by her best friend without knowing it's a seduction, and the man who built the seduction with that same friend, in secret, in bed. The chapter is pure warmth and I still felt the floor a little loose under it. "He's handling it," she says, and loves saying it — and I remember who else handles things.

The title, "across" — I think it's the tapping of the three phones on the terminal, nobody letting anybody cover anybody, each in her own lane of the same warm table; or the light lying across everything "without asking." It sat oblique for me, honestly. Didn't give anything away, didn't unlock much either. The book title — *With a Long Spoon* — keeps whispering its proverb at me: he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon. Vee is supping at tables everyone else chose, and Wednesday she'll seat the two of them on either side of her at once.

What I want next: Wednesday. I'm dreading and craving it in equal measure — Kayla's hair summit, Cassie's eyebrow meeting Pace, Randi and Pace in the same room with Vee beaming between them. I have no idea whether the book intends the dinner to crack anything, and I refuse to guess harder than that. This chapter earned its gentleness; I'll take it.
