## Reader reaction

**Among Friends** — the felt read.

This chapter did something I didn't expect it to do: it gave me the "meet the friends" dinner and made it *hot*, and then it made me ache in a place I wasn't watching.

First, the dinner. "Vee's boyfriend." He says it six times, flat, like a fact, and every single time it landed low in me too — because this is the man who cannot say *love*, who has never needed the word "boyfriend" anywhere, and he spends it here, first, out loud, in front of Randi of all people. Vee hears it "like the first swallow of something hot," and so did I. And there's a cruel irony underneath I could feel but she can't: the two people in that room who know the relationship was engineered are the two people hearing him claim her publicly. Randi's hand crushing Vee's — I read it both ways at once, celebration and something tighter.

The dinner itself won me completely. Pace working the room — never his own numbers, always the first line handed to someone else — Danny talked into believing he'd had a conversation, Theo unlocked with "*for you*," and then Cassie. Cassie *talking*, whole paragraphs, telling their story to him. Vee standing at the slaw end feeling "something enormous and quiet go by underneath her, like a whale under a boat" — that's exactly the size of it. Cassie has been the goalkeeper for two years and she just let him through, and Vee had the wisdom not to go over and disturb the view.

The twinge — Pace laughing at Randi's elbow, the half-second stinger, the hand at her collarbone — the text earned that, from Christmas, and I felt it too. And then the private scene at the end: the three of them, and Randi saying the brunches are the highlight of her week, then "meeting Vee was the highlight of my year," and the toast, and Randi going to that glazed quiet turning her glass a slow quarter at a time — and Vee *knowing that quiet* and being proud of producing it. God. Vee touching the bracelet on her wrist. I know what the bracelet is. I know what the quiet is. Vee reads it as her own power and it's the truest thing and the most damning thing in the chapter at once.

The truck. That's the heat of the book, and it peaked here for me. She takes him in her mouth out of pure greedy joy — "the man who did six things at once, down to zero. She could live on that stillness" — and then the drive, and the memory of September (the cold vents, his shivering hand he never mentioned), and she undoes her own jeans in a moving truck with nothing accidental anywhere in it, pulls the lace aside herself, "an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second it went on." That's the book's whole thesis in one gesture: desire worked on her, desire *hers*, both true. The car scene ran hot on me the whole way — 3, easy.

And the endings. Two endings, actually. The roadhouse quiet: "if some small sentence in her was still standing at a window somewhere, watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time, it couldn't have made itself heard tonight." That's Randi's glass-turning gesture *in Vee's own body*, laid in her like a splinter, and I don't think Vee will ever find it. I did. It stayed with me longer than the orgasm did.

And "We get along... You." — Randi and Pace, asked directly what they talk about, and his true answer hiding inside a sweet one. Vee takes it as the plain and obvious warmth of being loved by her people. I take it as the two lovers standing in plain sight with "no plans to produce another" credential. And the parking-lot kiss between the girls, twelve feet from him, "nothing in it a parking lot couldn't hold" — except he knows exactly what those brunches hold, and I held my breath for a second there.

Dread and glow in equal measure. This is the best the book has felt and the most compromised, simultaneously, which is where it lives.
