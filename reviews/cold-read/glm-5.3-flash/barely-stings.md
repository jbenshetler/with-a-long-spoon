# Reader Reaction — "Barely Stings"

Okay. I need to sit with this one for a second, because my hands are actually a little unsteady and I want to be honest about why before I get tidy about it.

This chapter hurt me, and I don't mean that as a complaint — I mean it landed in the exact place this book has been building toward for fifty-some chapters: Vee's yes, and what it costs her, and what gets said *before* she understands what she's saying yes to. The whole thing is one scene, one table, one bright room, and it had me clenching through every strip like it was happening to me.

Let me start with the part that hit hardest, because it's the part I didn't expect: **Sondra's hand on her thigh.** "Don't move." And Vee's body *knowing that grammar* — knowing a hand pressing it down and holding still, learned in a warm dark room from a man who means it — and here it is with everything true scooped out of the middle. The wrong hand, holding her exactly the way she wants to be held, wanting nothing from her. God. That's the shame-heat loop from ch 18 and ch 50 transported into a fluorescent-wax room and stripped of the love, and what's left is the ache itself, visible, exposed. She lies there "aching for the right one" and gives the woman nothing — keeps "the whole lit ruin of herself back behind her teeth where it belonged to him." That is the most Vee sentence in the book. The shame and the wanting running on the same wire, except this time the heat has nowhere legal to go, so it just burns her from the inside while a stranger works.

And the arousal *is* there, unbidden, appalling to her — the thought of him "lit and wrong" under the bored hands of a woman with a clock running. I felt that. The erotic charge in this chapter doesn't come from anything sexual happening; it comes from Vee being aroused at the worst possible moment and not being able to stop it, and from the anticipation pooling at the end — her own hand on the bare smoothness, "the sound she made was not one of pain," and then immediately his hand, the weekend, the right hand learning what she'd done. That final page is genuinely hot in a way that's all *forecast* — the wanting is entirely forward-pointing and it still lit me up. The wanting is the whole engine and it's running full.

But now the part that has my stomach actually knotted, and I have to name it plainly: **Randi lied to her.** "Barely stings." The glass of wine. "You'll love it." And not one word about rolling over. Not one word about *that*. Vee lies on the table realizing "the part Randi hadn't said" and I sat there with my chest tight, because this is the pattern of the entire book rendered in miniature: Randi sends her somewhere, sands the dread off it first, and lets Vee walk in unprepared. Vee "believed her as she believed everything Randi told her about the country they both lived in that Randi had a passport for." *It was restful, being told.* That line is doing so much work and it broke my heart a little. Vee's trust in Randi is so total and so convenient — for Randi — and the reader is sitting in the same chair Vee sat in at that glass room over MIRH, knowing things, carrying them silently. This time Vee even *notices* the gap ("The part Randi hadn't said") and — this is the part that made me put the book down for a second — she doesn't blame Randi. She files it nowhere. She absorbs the betrayal as her own failure of knowledge. "A thing only Vee had somehow failed to understand she'd been agreeing to." That's the tragedy of her, right there. She audits herself, never the people who steer her.

And Pace. Let me talk about Pace, because my feelings about him are getting more complicated and I think the book wants that. He asked in "the plain voice he used for facts about the world," looking straight at her, and *he asked* — "Will you?" — and that asking undoes her, and I felt it undo her. He never tells her why. "He asked for the thing itself, clean, and left the place where the reason should be for her to fill, and she filled it, every time, with *yes, yes, tell me when.*" I read that and something cold went down my spine right next to the warmth, because that is the architecture of the game, isn't it — the space left open for her to fill, and she fills it, every time. Except now the reader has seen that space left open *deliberately* twice — by Pace, and by Randi with the waxing — and the two of them are the same two people who chose her before she knew there was a choice. He asks, and the asking is real, and the asking is also a design. I don't know how to hold both of those and the book isn't asking me to. It's just asking me to feel both, and I do. The yes is hers — "her mouth answering before her mind had caught up" — the book has been careful, over and over, to make her desire hers. But her *yes arrives before her information does*, and that is becoming the sentence the whole book is written in. The fear "arriving too late to be any use — that was nearly the point of it." *Nearly the point.* Whose point, Vee? I ached.

The mother's voice — "the kind of girl who" — arriving in the bright room and staying "where it always did." That's the shame-voice motif from ch 18 and the induction, and it doesn't close her here, and that's growth, and the growth hurts. She says "everything, all of it, bare" out loud to a stranger and it costs her everything and costs the woman nothing, and that asymmetry — "the nod you'd give a coffee order" — is one of the truest things about shame I've ever read on a page.

Also, and I want to say this because it surprised me: the two girls on the waiting-room couch. "One had come to be looked at... the other had come to be touched... Vee sat down between them and understood which one she was." First sighting of this image and it's a whole thesis of the book in three sentences. Vee has spent her life dressing against being looked at; she's spent this year learning to be touched, and wanted, and looked at — and here she names herself as the second kind and the chapter then makes her endure the first kind at its most impersonal. The grooming motif from Marsh's lecture — the comb, hands in hair, one-fifth of the day — hums underneath this whole chapter without the book pointing at it. Being maintained by someone's hands. Baboons and Brazilians and Pace at the sink. I noticed; I don't think I was meant to miss it.

What did I feel about the ending? She touches herself, alone, and the sound "was not one of pain," and then it's *his* hand she wants, the reveal of the weekend, and she walks out past the girl waiting "to be looked at" as "the most expensive thing in the room, and no one in it knew but her." That last line should be triumphant and it almost is — and the "almost" is the chapter. The secret-in-public thing again, like the gown at induction, like the coat: wearing nothing underneath the world's ordinary surface. She's carrying the soreness and the smoothness and the wanting into her weekend like a hidden garment. I swooned and I grieved in the same breath and I think that's exactly what this chapter is.

No swoon-quibble: the one moment I'd flag as slightly author-thumb-on-scale is "she filled it, every time, with *yes, yes, tell me when*" — it's a little too neatly thesis-shaped, a little too aware of the pattern, for a girl who "did not let herself look at" her own knowledge elsewhere. It's a small thing in a chapter that otherwise never once put its thumb down.

Where does the title point? "Barely Stings" is Randi's lie, and the chapter is the correction of it — the title *is* the false floor, the friendliness that sands the dread off a thing so you'll walk into it unprepared. Every time I see it now I'll hear Randi's warm voice over Vee's tears at the corners. It gave nothing away before I read it and it means twice as much after. As for the volume — *A Warm Reception*, Book Two of *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon has always been Pace's feeding, the hospitality as seduction, and "a warm reception" now reads twice too: the warm welcome and the waxing room's own warm reception, Sondra's "how're we doing, first-timer?" warmth with the clock running under it. Warmth as the book's whole method, and this chapter is the first time the warmth is *professional* — warmth that wants nothing — and it turns out that's the cruelest warmth in the book so far.

What I want next: the weekend. I want his hand finding her bare and the scene the chapter has been building toward — and I want to watch whether Vee tells Randi how much it hurt, and whether Randi's face does anything. I dread, a little, that Vee will say "barely stings" back to her, laughing, and hand the lie back as truth. Guesses from here, clearly marked as guesses: I suspect the waxing is also going to matter at the dinner party or with Sheri somehow — Sheri's blunt eye down Vee's body in the taproom is still fresh in me, and I keep wondering what that look would find now. And I am holding, carefully, the thought that Vee's "yes before information" is going to meet, one day, the biggest withheld information of all — the game — and that the collision will be catastrophic precisely because she trained herself to fill the reason-gap with yes.
