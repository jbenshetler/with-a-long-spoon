## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I opened this cold and the first thing that got me was the intimacy of the hello — not the sex, the *deadbolt*. She locks it behind her before anything else, he doesn't call out, "the small economy of two people who did not perform for each other." That line settled over me like the sheet it describes. And then the apple: sliced thin, cut sides up, because he noticed she eats faster that way. That detail did something to my chest before anything explicit happened. It's tender and it's surveillance at the same time, and the book knows it — "he was sorry about it" — and I was already in.

The seduction itself is extraordinarily well-rendered. The lap, the apple game, his hands turning back before they arrive, her finally *placing* his hands on her breasts — the whole grammar of this is consent as erotic material, and it worked on me completely. The bench reveal is genuinely tense: "No, you said it was a table. I didn't correct you" is a chilling, gorgeous little beat, and her reading the bench's purpose part by part, hands closing on nothing, throat baring — I felt her nervousness as my own. "Show me," she says. Perfect.

The spanking and edging sequence runs hot and long and mostly earned it for me. The checks ("Tell me your word," "You with me?") never felt like compliance theater; they felt like craft. The real climax isn't the orgasm, it's the begging — the moment her sorority *please* breaks into the raw one, and "There you are, Randi" landed on me like a bell. I teared up at the crying-into-the-leather. This is the best of what the jacket promised: explicit, warm, consensual, psychologically exact.

Then the bathroom, and the book turned, and I went with it. Her mirror monologue — the face that takes being-wanted and lets none of it reach her, the aliveness that came "from being seen" and therefore cannot be allowed again — is devastating. She decides to go back behind the face, permanently, while he sits outside the locked door honoring it because of a woman named Daphne and a door he once didn't respect. That backstory drop, one paragraph, no explanation, is the kind of thing I love being trusted with.

What unsettles me — and the page earns this — is Pace's own accounting. He "felt the small warm pleasure of having taken her further than he had thought she would go." He reads her entirely and gets her entirely wrong in the very act: "All he had done was find it, and answer it," he thinks, while the hum in her ribs is "the size of a key turning in a lock." He's not a villain on the page; he's patient, careful, genuinely deferential — and he engineered this, built the bench with a false top, and watches her with the satisfaction of a craftsman. The tenderness and the design are the same gesture. I can't decide how afraid of him to be, and I think that's the point.

The last image — the bench waiting, "full of what it was" — left me aching and slightly cold at once. She's going to lie to him with her best smile and he's going to be quietly happy, and neither of them knows yet what tonight actually did. I want the next chapter badly. Also — where are Vee and Cassie? The jacket made me expect a Vee; I got Randi and Pace and a name I don't know yet, Daphne. I'm holding that open.

Series line "With a Long Spoon" means nothing to me yet; I'll wait for the book to feed it to me.
