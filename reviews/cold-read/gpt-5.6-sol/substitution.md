## Reader reaction

I’m swooning and uneasy at exactly the same time. Pace’s attention works on me because it works on Vee: the steady look, the rolled cuffs, the “May I call you?”, yes—but most of all the pencil. Him giving it back “at the exact line where she could solve it herself” is genuinely intimate. He sees not just that she is smart, but the particular way she wants to be smart. I felt the pull hard when he asked, “What’s drawing you to it?” and then actually listened to the answer. Vee isn’t merely being admired; she is being invited to become more articulate, more capable, more herself. That is seductive as hell.

And because I know what Vee does not, I can’t experience the apparent chance meeting as innocent. Pace walking in just after Randi’s absorbed texting, Cassie announcing that “cute guy” is looking at Vee, Randi supplying the easy social endorsement afterward—it is too cleanly the plan from “The Pointing Game” coming to life. Pace may be sincerely delighted by Vee; I believe that he is. But his opening, “It sounds like you’re having trouble with math. Can I help?”, wears the costume of spontaneity. The tenderness is real and the setup is still a setup. That combination is much more disturbing—and much more compelling—than either simple manipulation or simple romance would be.

Vee gets me completely here. Her borrowing Randi’s practiced introduction and then quickly reclaiming “Vee” is touching: she tries on Randi’s ease because she wants to meet Pace at the altitude where he seems to see her, but she still wants him to know her own name. I also ache for how thoroughly being chosen governs her. Pace’s smile makes her feel “absurdly, chosen,” echoing how Randi’s attention made her feel chosen at Chi Latte. She is starving for focused recognition, and these two know how to feed that hunger—even if Pace’s exact understanding of its depth is still open from Vee’s side.

My feelings about Randi sharpened. Her performance is excellent, but the chapter gives me enough physical tells to see the strain beneath it: the coffee lifted without drinking, the cup held too long, the question about Bessel’s correction that creates a little interruption and receives a much shorter answer. I can’t settle what she is feeling. It could be nerves that the plan is underway, jealousy at Pace’s immediate absorption in Vee, discomfort at watching herself become socially invisible, or several of those at once. The page leaves it ajar, so I do too. But after the rawness of “The Bench” and her decision to retreat behind her face, seeing her now manage this encounter makes me feel protective of her and wary of her simultaneously.

Cassie remains the person I trust most instinctively. Her flatness doesn’t feel cruel; it feels observant. She notices Randi’s texting, the restless cup, and the hierarchy of Pace’s attention. Her great-aunt line is funny because it is brutally accurate. After her strange “Goodbye, Vee” in the previous chapter, I’m even more alert to the possibility that she senses a structure Vee cannot see, though that is only my guess from here. At minimum, she is not swept up by Randi’s presentation.

The erotic charge is a strong simmer rather than explicit heat. It peaks around Pace’s hands: rolling his cuffs, the forearms that “were not a math tutor’s forearms,” the thick knuckles, Vee wondering what else he does with them, and the tiny consent embedded in how he “asked for” her pencil with his eyes. The mathematical attention becomes erotic because he never overwhelms her with what he knows. Even his competence leaves room for her. After the sustained explicitness of “The Bench” and the opening sex of “The Pointing Game,” this is quieter, but it doesn’t feel slack; it feels like first-contact electricity.

The one place I feel the page pressing its meaning rather hard is the explanation of Pace’s tutoring choice: “The way he had given her back the pencil at the exact line where she could solve it herself.” I already felt that from the action, and the extended unpacking nearly over-explains the swoon. Still, Vee’s recognition of it is psychologically persuasive enough that it doesn’t break the scene for me.

“Substitution” lands in several places at once. The literal mathematical breakthrough is substitution: turning the impossible integral into something Vee already knows. But Randi has also supplied Vee with a borrowed social script, which Vee substitutes for her own uncertainty. Most powerfully, the encounter substitutes design for chance: Vee experiences Pace as the man who spontaneously crossed a coffee shop for her, while I know Randi and Pace selected her before this moment. Pace’s description—two difficult things placed “at right angles” becoming one plain, solvable thing—also makes me think of him and Randi joining their different abilities around Vee. That may be my guess from here, but the image is too pointed not to tug at me.

*A Polite Invitation* now feels almost painfully exact. Pace asks permission at every visible threshold, including the beautifully old-fashioned “May I call you?”, while the largest invitation—the arranged meeting itself—remains invisible to Vee. *With a Long Spoon* still suggests appetite approached with elegant distance or careful handling: desire fed deliberately, perhaps by people who know it may burn. And the tagline, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.”, is fully alive here. Vee’s yes is real. She wants him to call. What troubles me is not her consent to the date; it is that she cannot consent to the hidden frame around how the choice reached her.

I want the phone to light up because Pace and Vee already have chemistry, and I want more of the way he makes her intelligence feel desirable. I also dread how perfectly he and Randi can reinforce each other: he gives Vee depth and attention, while Randi tells her what the attention means and encourages her toward it. My guess from here is that Vee will read their coordination as two separate forms of being chosen until those forms have become indispensable. I’m very ready to keep reading, because the seduction has stopped being an advertised plan and become something I can feel working.
