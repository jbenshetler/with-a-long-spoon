## Reader reaction

I came out of this chapter flushed, delighted, and then quietly sick with dread. The first half gave me almost everything I wanted for Vee: Pace publicly naming himself as “Vee’s boyfriend,” saying it over and over, and welcoming her friends by remembering what matters to each of them. I melted at the simplicity of the declaration. After all the pain he caused by refusing that exact kind of word, “a man with exactly one credential and no plans to produce another” landed enormously. It does not explain why he could not say it before, but it is real movement, public and voluntary. Vee hearing it “somewhere low every single time” was exactly right; I felt it there too.

Pace was tremendously attractive to me through the party. His gift is not merely listening but making people more fully themselves. “Where does it start, for you?” opens Theo without testing him. Asking Danny about his progress lets Danny keep his dignity rather than turning the conversation into a display of Pace’s strength. Watching Cassie give him “the best of their two years” was the chapter’s most tender social beat. Cassie’s “You can stay” is tiny, dry, and practically a benediction. I trust her judgment more than anyone’s, so her acceptance swept me up.

Vee made me ache with happiness. She has wanted her private life to become publicly real, and here Pace does not merely endure her people: he learns them, delights in them, and builds a room where none of them has to be “a good sport.” Even the absence of screens matters after Chili’s. He chose a place where he could actually hear everyone. The night answers that earlier failure without making a speech about it.

Randi broke my heart. Her fierce grip when Pace says “boyfriend” cannot be only the happiness Vee assigns to it; I know too much for that. Then she raises the glass “To my beautiful best friend,” admits that meeting Vee was “the highlight of my year,” and goes into that “glazed sort of quiet,” turning the glass by quarters. Vee reaches across the bracelet and thinks she has simply moved her friend. She has—but she cannot understand what she has moved or how painfully. I still love Randi’s magnetism and tenderness, but the secrecy felt especially cruel tonight because she and Pace are sitting together inside the truth while Vee glows at having finally brought them together.

The line that dropped the floor out from under me was Pace’s answer about what he and Randi discussed:

> “We get along,” he said. “They share an interest.”

Then:

> “Randi. Your friends. All of them.” A mile marker went by. “You.”

That is exquisitely double. Vee receives the sweet, obvious meaning: everyone loves her. I hear the concealed one too: Randi and Pace literally share their desire for Vee. I cannot tell whether Pace intends Vee to hear only the innocent meaning, but he knows the other meaning is there. That made his warmth feel off in a way the page fully earned. The chapter gives Vee the relationship-name she needed while showing me that the deepest naming problem remains untouched: she still does not know what the three of them are.

The erotic charge worked strongly on me. Vee’s desire grows directly out of watching Pace cherish her people, so the sex does not feel pasted onto the party; it is gratitude, pride, possession, and relief becoming bodily. Her taking him into her mouth in the truck is hot because she is so decisively hungry: “greedy with the whole night.” His hand holding her hair “not steering, just holding” preserves the sense that she is directing what happens. His collapse from doing six things at once “down to zero” is a gorgeous erotic image.

Her later choice to undo her jeans, pull aside her own thong, and place his hand where she wants it is equally clear and active. The repeated emphasis that she keeps choosing—“an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second it went on”—made the consent vivid rather than procedural. But the moving truck caused genuine reader friction. Pace is driving one-handed on a dark county road while sexually stimulating her, and she physically takes his hand off the wheel. I could feel the heat, but I could not forget the danger. “The truck ran on steady under his one wrist” seems meant to make his control reassuring; it did not entirely reassure me. This is the one place where their private conviction that Pace can handle everything pushed against my ability to surrender to the scene.

The chapter title, “Among Friends,” first means exactly the warm communal night Vee wanted: her worlds meeting, mixing, and approving one another. By the end it feels sharper. Vee is among friends, but two of those friends share a secret life and a secret understanding of her. The title did not give away an event, but it made me alert to how dangerously incomplete the word “friends” is here.

The cover-board line, “WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION,” feels fully alive now. “A WARM RECEPTION” promises welcome, and this chapter delivers it literally: warm pine, shared food, Vee’s name on the table, Pace received by her people and receiving them beautifully. “WITH A LONG SPOON” feels warier beside that warmth—the sense of sharing a table while needing distance from something dangerous or morally complicated. That is where I feel the book taking me: deeper into an arrangement that can be genuinely nourishing and genuinely unsafe because Vee does not know who else is already eating from it.
