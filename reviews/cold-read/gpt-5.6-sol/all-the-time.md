The deeper charge is watching her life divide into these intensely colored weekends: Pace’s house, Randi’s booth, then Pace again. “The man wanting all of her and the friend wanting her piece” lands deliciously, but it also makes me ache because Vee experiences the arrangement as abundance while I know the two people dividing her time already belong to each other. Vee makes me tender and happy here. She is more alive, more candid, and less apologetic than she has ever been. “We have so much sex, Randi” made me laugh with her; it is wonderfully unguarded. Sex pervert?” I feel the old shame tightening around her even while she is flourishing, and I love the relief when Randi tells her, “You’re just finally hungry out loud.” That line genuinely got me. Pace is absent in person but powerfully present in everything Vee says.
