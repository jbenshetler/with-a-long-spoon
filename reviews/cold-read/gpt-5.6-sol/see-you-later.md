## Reader reaction

I feel giddy for Vee and sick for her at the same time. Her anticipation in the lecture hall got me immediately: three minutes of determinedly not looking at the door, then Randi appearing behind her with “*There* you are.” That lands like wish fulfillment. Vee is so hungry to be chosen that every scrap becomes enormous—the right pen, the touching bags, the invitation delivered as a decision. Her happiness is tender and painfully exposed. I like her vanity less—the reflexive “*Floozies*,” the conviction that she has “more than they had, and better”—but the chapter lets me feel the mother’s voice inside those judgments and the desire running beneath them. That makes her deeper rather than merely snobbish.

Randi’s warmth still works on me even though I know this meeting was engineered. She is socially dazzling: she catches Cassie’s dropped response without punishing her, makes Vee feel clever and useful, and gives attention with an almost physical intensity. I understand completely why Vee floats through the hour. But now the generosity feels dangerous because Vee believes she has been spontaneously chosen, while Randi approached her after selecting her with Pace. I don’t yet know how much of Randi’s delight is real. It may be; she was genuinely lit up after meeting Vee. But “*Love you, girl*” costs Randi almost nothing and means everything to Vee. That imbalance unsettles me more than any single lie.

Cassie is the person I trust most. Her refusal is clean, and “*Goodbye, Vee*” chilled me because the page makes it unmistakably deliberate: she “let it stand” and did not soften it into ordinary leave-taking. I don’t know what Cassie sees. Maybe she simply recognizes Vee’s capacity to disappear into someone else’s approval; maybe she distrusts Randi specifically. The chapter holds that open, and so do I. Her brief look at Vee and that full word feel protective, sad, and powerless rather than jealous.

The erotic charge is quiet but real. It peaks in Vee’s longer-than-accounted-for gaze at the underdressed women, then becomes unmistakable in her “flat helpless want” for Randi. She does not name that want as sexual, and I won’t force it further than she does, but her attention keeps finding women’s bodies and then losing all its defenses around Randi. The high stools, nearly touching knees, citrus, and Randi’s hand between her shoulder blades create a low bodily current beneath the social swoon. It isn’t hot like the bench chapter; it is the first warm pull of someone being led toward a want she cannot yet look at.

The café hour swept me up because the prose reproduces Vee’s intoxication: afterward she cannot retain the conversation, only what it felt like to be included. That is lovely and alarming. The one moment where I felt the page’s hand was “*latent variables*.” It is apt—Randi’s hidden purpose, Vee’s hidden desire, everything shaping the scene without being spoken—but underlining it makes the signal conspicuous. Otherwise the repeated grammar of belonging—sign, room, clothes, high table—earns its length because this is not merely coffee to Vee. It is admission into a life.

“See You Later” becomes two opposed promises: Cassie’s pointed refusal to say it and Randi’s radiant certainty that there will be a next time. To Vee, the title is almost a benediction; to me, knowing how deliberately Randi found her, it also sounds like the next step in a recruitment. *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* is beginning to feel literal in its politeness: nobody has forced Vee anywhere. She has been warmly invited, fed something sweet, and made eager to return. The spoons ringing throughout Chi Latte give the series title its first direct tug for me, though I still do not know what the “long” spoon finally promises. No cover tagline was supplied.

I want Randi to want Vee as a person, not merely as the woman she and Pace selected, and I dread how badly Vee could be hurt by learning the coffee was never accidental. My guess from here is that Cassie will try, in her spare four words, to warn her—or that Vee will hear warning as resentment because being chosen feels too good to surrender. I absolutely want to keep reading. The swoon is real; so is the trap-shaped space beneath it.
