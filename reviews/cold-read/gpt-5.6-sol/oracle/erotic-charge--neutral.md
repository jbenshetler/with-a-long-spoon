**Question asked (verbatim):** Is the book working on you — where does it run hot, where does it go slack?

Yes—powerfully. It runs hottest when care, appetite, and agency become inseparable: Pace feeding Vee, returning the pencil where she can finish the problem, making the dress to her actual body, or receiving an exposure she has deliberately staged; and Randi drawing Vee open through attention, confession, and those increasingly mutual kisses. “The Bench,” “Famished,” “A Round,” “The Practice Room,” “One Bite,” and “Nothing Underneath” all got me bodily while keeping tenderness, shame, and concealed knowledge alive inside the heat. The book’s strongest ache is that the local yeses feel real even as Randi and Pace secretly shape the route toward them.

It goes slack mainly when it explains a meaning I have already felt—the repeated claims that a response is someone’s “real” self, the conspicuously thematic statistics lessons, or lines that neatly summarize the emotional mechanism. Pace’s near-mythic accumulation of talents occasionally strains belief, and a few continuity or logic catches jarred me, especially Randi’s rewritten shoe-store story. Some later sex and domestic-feeding rhythms also risk repetition, but changes in Vee’s agency usually keep them alive. The current romance conflict is working because the repetition has become the point: Pace keeps answering with hands, food, warmth, and sex, while Vee increasingly needs words. “Nothing Underneath” gives ecstatic release, but it does not resolve that wound—and I remain urgently invested.
