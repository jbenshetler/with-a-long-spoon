**Question asked (verbatim):** The statistics professor and his lectures — the paradox, the error-correcting codes, the outliers. How did he land the FIRST time, and had that changed by his third or fourth lecture? A straight teaching device, or a joke/wink shared with you — and if a joke, did you enjoy him or did he wear thin? 0-5 on how much you enjoyed him by the end (0 = groaned, too on-the-nose; 5 = delighted, wanted more), with what changed your read.

---

2.5/5. The first time, with “latent variables” in “See You Later,” he landed as a slightly conspicuous wink: the concept fit the class naturally, but I could feel the novel arranging the sign. By “All Told,” Simpson’s paradox—and especially Vee writing that “short stretches can lie against the whole”—felt too insistently explanatory. “The Practice Room” and “The Outlier” gave the lectures more emotional force because the ideas carried forward into Vee’s desire and Randi’s family shock, but by then he was still primarily a teaching device, not a personality I enjoyed or wanted more of.
