My first feeling is relief on Vee’s behalf: she finally goes to someone, gets held, cries, laughs, and leaves with purpose instead of lying alone under Pace’s silence. But that relief curdles badly because the person holding her knows far more than Vee understands. Randi’s care feels real—she comes down fast, removes Vee from the public hall, forgets her own mess, and holds her “past where a hug stops.” I believe she missed Vee and loves having her there. I also feel intensely suspicious of what she does with that trust.

The most troubling moment is Randi’s absolute “I know so.” She knows Sheri is not Pace’s lover, knows Pace’s habits, and can reconstruct why he went “behind a wall,” but lets Vee believe this is merely brilliant intuition. Then she steers Vee away from the exact thing Vee asked Pace for: “You don’t do it with words. Words are the whole war.” Vee wanted a declaration and left because acts could not replace one; Randi persuades her to answer that refusal with the most exposed act possible. “You just show up already being the answer” feels seductive and frightening. Randi is helping Pace get Vee back without requiring him to answer her.

The erotic charge works strongly once Randi introduces the coat. The long hugs, Vee breathing Randi’s bitter-orange skin, and the ordinary goodbye kiss add a quieter charge because neither woman names what is happening between them.

I ache for Vee, but I’m also frustrated that she lets Randi recast the confrontation so completely. “You backed him into a corner in his own house” puts the moral weight on Vee for asking a fair question. I don’t buy the leap from Pace’s focused attention to “That is not a man with another woman”; Randi knows it happens to be true about Sheri, but the argument itself proves nothing.
