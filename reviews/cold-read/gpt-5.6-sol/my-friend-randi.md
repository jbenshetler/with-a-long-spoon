## Reader reaction

I hurt for Vee from the first page: that drive toward a town containing Pace but “not one inch of him for her” lands like grief already settled into geography. Her loneliness feels bodily—the silent car, Cassie’s stripped side of the room, the radiator ticking while she turns the last night over and over. I understand why she goes to Randi, and the first long hug absolutely works on me. Vee breathing in the bitter-orange smell of Randi’s neck, both women holding past “where a hug stops,” is intimate enough to make me ache. Randi sweeping expensive new things aside because Vee matters more is lovely. Brooke also moves me with her uncomplicated “Oh, honey”; I trust that warmth because she asks what Vee needs and does not pry.

Then my trust in Randi drops sharply—not because her love is false, but because she uses privileged knowledge while pretending to reason only from Vee’s story. “I know so” is true in a way Vee cannot understand. Randi knows Pace is not finished with Vee; she also knows Sheri is not the imagined blonde rival, yet lets Vee believe she has arrived at reassurance by examining evidence. Worse, she recasts Vee’s legitimate need for a named relationship as an ambush: “You backed him into a corner in his own house.” Vee did deliver an ultimatum, but Pace really did refuse the word she needed. Randi’s version makes his silence wounded male helplessness and Vee’s asking the offense. I felt the author’s thumb slightly in “That’s a boy holding himself still so it won’t show how much you got him,” because it is so perfectly tailored to dissolve Vee’s anger—and because Randi knows exactly what she is steering her back toward.

The coat plan is hot. It catches in precisely the place Vee’s desire and shame meet: nothing underneath, black heels, crossing the cold to present herself at Pace’s door. Her wanting is real; I believe “She was going to do it” as her own answer, not merely Randi’s command. The charge peaks when Randi reframes the act as courage: “You’d be walking through the cold to hand him everything, no words, no fight, nothing kept back.” But that line also frightens me. Words are not merely “the whole war”; words are what Vee asked for, and Randi is persuading her to answer Pace’s refusal by making her body the peace offering. Vee is exhausted, heartbroken, and desperate, while Randi promises Pace cannot say no. This remains consensual as Vee’s choice, but it does not feel emotionally safe or equal. I want Pace and Vee to have each other again; I do not want Vee to surrender the question in order to get him back.

The second half makes Randi’s concealment hurt more. Vee hears John’s invented family and effortless future as everything Pace would not say aloud. Randi gives her the glittering version while withholding that the “masterpiece” left her cold, then turns emotional emptiness into a joke about bedroom failure. I understand why—she cannot tell Vee that Pace and Vee are the people who actually reach her—but Vee is trusting her with the raw center of her life while Randi keeps editing the truth. Their second lingering hug and routine kiss still pull me strongly. Randi has plainly missed her, and “My Friend Randi” feels full of painful double meaning: Vee experiences her as the friend who knows her, rescues her, and always knows the shoes, while I know that “friend” is both true and radically incomplete. The chapter title does not give away an event, but it puts pressure on the category Vee trusts most.

The cover board, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, now feels pointedly uneasy. The long spoon still promises appetite and feeding, but also distance and the reach required when intimacy may be dangerous. “A Polite Invitation” fits the coat plan’s surface—Vee arriving as an unmistakable invitation without speaking—yet very little beneath it feels merely polite. No cover tagline was supplied. I badly want Pace to open that door and finally say the missing words before accepting what Vee offers. My guess from here is that the gesture will reunite them physically, perhaps spectacularly, while leaving the original wound untouched. What I dread is Vee mistaking his desire for the answer she asked for—and later discovering that her trusted friend knew far more than she admitted.
