This is quiet, but it gets under my skin. I feel close to Vee here: her Thanksgiving was genuinely good, and yet almost every truth she told her parents had to be cropped to fit what they could recognize. The cropped photograph becomes the model for the whole visit. Her line that she told her father “the parts that fit” hurts because that is also how she understands Pace herself: in true parts, without the concealed whole.

Cassie remains the person I trust most. “So what is he… Not to your dad. To you” is exactly the question I want asked, and she asks without cornering Vee. Then she makes the kind choice when Vee says she both cannot and does not want to answer. The final turn is excellent and painful: “Vee was grateful. My feelings about Vee’s father warm slightly. He seems limited rather than cruel: burning the rolls, feeling proud of her, and silently calculating the cost of the dress all feel loving. Vee’s judgment that he could understand Pace undressing her more easily than Pace sewing for her is funny, sad, and believable. I feel more apprehensive about her mother. The repeated collarbone gesture makes Vee’s old shame vivid even though her mother says nothing.

There is no active erotic heat, but Pace remains erotically present through the dress and the sentence about his taking it off her. More strongly, there is domestic and romantic charge in Vee finally grasping the magnitude of his having made it because she once mentioned something unaffordable. That lands more powerfully than the undressing. It also sharpens my unease: Pace’s attentiveness is real, lavish, and deeply binding, while Vee still cannot say what he is to her and does not know one of the largest facts about him.

Nothing here makes me newly suspicious of Cassie or Vee’s parents. Pace’s absence does not create new evidence against him, but the chapter makes the asymmetry around him feel worse. Vee tells her parents a cropped truth, yet Pace is withholding far more from her. The line “Too good is its own kind of suspicious” lands with extra force because I already am suspicious—not of his cooking or sewing, but of the secret relationship and game surrounding all that tenderness.

The title, **“Cropped,”** initially points directly to the waist-up induction photograph Vee sent her parents. By the end it means the cropped account of Pace, the cropped version of Vee’s adult and sexual life, and perhaps the cropped relationship-label “boyfriend,” spoken with a question mark. It does not give the chapter away; it gathers meaning as Vee talks. *With a Long Spoon* still has no settled meaning for me, though it continues to suggest approaching something desirable and dangerous while trying to preserve distance. The tagline feels painfully active here: Vee freely chooses every truthful fragment she gives her parents and freely chooses not to answer Cassie, but all those chosen omissions leave her alone inside an understanding already distorted by other people’s larger concealment.

I want Cassie to ask again, though I respect her for stopping. I want Vee to ask Pace directly what they are and to say she loves him. My guess from here is that this unnamed relationship will become harder to sustain now that she has called him her boyfriend at home. I also expect the Thanksgiving return to bring her rapidly back toward Pace and Randi, while the Miranda Holdings discovery remains waiting underneath everything.
