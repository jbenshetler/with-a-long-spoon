This chapter swept me up so thoroughly that the cold afterward landed almost physically. I wanted the mountain, the champagne, the impossible dress, John’s hands crossing over his heart, the sleigh bells in the snow. Randi’s happiness while skiing felt especially good because it escaped her management: she is “grinning without deciding to.” John’s refusal to prove he is better than her—having “the run to spare” and spending none of it—made him enormously attractive. For most of the chapter I understood exactly why she wanted to call him the beginning of her life. Then the hotel room emptied the fantasy so completely that I felt the chill with her.

Randi deepens painfully here. Breakfast explains her polish without excusing what she does with it: her father enjoys the invented boys, her mother offers a beautiful, bony performance of affection, and Randi knows how to feed them a safe version of herself. I felt revulsion toward both parents’ world, especially the way her father’s joking instruction to find an “eligible bachelor” makes desire another family errand. Her thought that “The flaw was hers” hurt because I do not believe she is defective—but I believe she experiences the faultless emptiness as evidence that she is.

John worked on me until he didn’t. His playful entrance, easy quiet, competence, direct invitation, and spectacular orchestration all have real pull. John reads her social performance perfectly and her interior hardly at all. Nothing makes him sinister. His light lead requires Randi’s listening and participation; John’s easier lead lets her surrender without revealing anything. Pace frightens her because he finds her. My guess from here is that Randi will return from this perfect failure more certain of whom she wants, but not more willing to risk asking openly.
