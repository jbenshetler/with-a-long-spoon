## Reader reaction

This chapter makes my whole body unclench. After the exquisite manipulation and charged attention of the recent chapters, being in the pool with Vee and Cassie feels like taking a clean breath. I love Cassie here. Her care has no agenda and barely even announces itself as care: she saves “They will never know the love of a woman” for Vee instead of performing it for the bleachers; she notices that Vee surfaces “with her face on wrong” but does not demand an explanation; then she sends that deliberately inadequate sheet of water across the gap. She knows exactly how to reach her friend without making Vee’s sadness into an event. That moves Cassie for me from watchful, dry, and possibly wary into someone deeply trustworthy.

Vee makes me ache. The men in the bleachers turn her body into public property for a moment, and her practiced responses — “the long blink,” the eyeroll — show how fluent she has had to become at pretending this costs her nothing. The line that really lands is: “She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem.” That hurts because the problem is plainly not her body; it is everyone’s claim on it, plus the judging voice she now carries inside herself. Underwater, she gets briefly back to a self that precedes being watched.

There is no erotic charge for me here, despite the swimsuit and the comments about Vee’s breasts and legs. The exposure is rendered as exhausting, not inviting. What does have a physical pull is the underwater passage: the cold rising, light falling “in slow sheets,” her hands remembering the scull. It is sensual in the broad bodily sense, but peaceful rather than sexual. The chapter’s real swoon is friendship. “Whatever she’d carried up from the bottom didn’t survive it” gives me a rush of affection for both women, especially because Cassie doesn’t rescue Vee through insight or interrogation; she simply starts a water fight and lets joy be enough for the moment.

I have no suspicion of Cassie. Her attention feels clean in a way almost nobody else’s has lately. Nor do I read Vee as hiding something sinister from herself here. She is carrying grief she cannot or does not name, but the chapter lets that remain open. The ending does not claim she is healed: the troublesome body can wait “up on the deck” only for the duration of play. That limitation makes the relief more believable.

I had almost no friction. The chapter is short, exact, and earns its lyricism. She keeps Vee from remaining submerged in grief without dragging her out or demanding that she explain why she went under.
