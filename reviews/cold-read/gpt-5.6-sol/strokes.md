The first thing I feel is relief in my own body. After the escalating heat of Vee’s encounters with Pace and Randi, the pool gives her—and me—a place where nothing has to be confessed, performed, or decided. I loved the blunt physical competence of “Her legs were doing something honest. Her hands knew where to be.” This is still the Vee whose body so often speaks before her mind, but here the body is not exposing or embarrassing her. It simply works. I feel tender toward her wet-haired, pleasantly exhausted quiet, even though I know she has not resolved anything.

Her “maybe” to Cassie now feels less like uncertainty about loving him than fear of looking directly at what loving him has already changed. I also ache over “Did he mind,” because Vee does not know he gave her the top drawer. She is swimming with an anxiety already answered by an act of tenderness she has not yet seen.

There is almost no erotic charge here, and that feels right. The prose is intensely bodily—the damp skin, water over her neck, loose legs, generous arms—but not sexually charged for me. Its pull is the pleasure of exertion and the return of an old self. The swimming does not produce an answer; it strips language away until she can carry the remaining shape without being pursued by it. That landed beautifully.
