This made me ache before it turned me on. Pace’s helpless grin and the dress glowing in the window are almost unbearably lovely. Vee crying because “he’d taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble” feels exactly right for her: she is overwhelmed not merely by beauty or expense, but by labor shaped specifically around her. His simple “Yes,” which “didn’t make it smaller,” moved me. My attraction to Pace deepens again here because his making, patience, and delight all feel genuine—even while the enormous concealed betrayal remains untouched.

The strongest erotic turn is Vee alone at the mirror. The dress makes her look at herself without apology, but the decisive moment is hers: “she was the one who’d taken the edge away.” Removing her underwear, striding out with the slit opening to her hip, and wrapping that bare leg around Pace is very hot because it grows directly from being seen by herself first. The actual sex is elided, but that works for me; the charge peaks before it, in her walk toward him. The afterglow then becomes tender rather than slack. Their stopping to save the dress is funny, intimate, and believable.

Vee’s happiness frightens me more now. She imagines entering the induction in something “fitted to her by a man who’d thought about nothing else,” but I know he has also been thinking about Randi and their hidden game. That sentence hurts precisely because Vee’s belief is emotionally sincere and factually incomplete. The ending—“getting away with something”—lands as both delighted and ominous. She thinks she is getting away with visibility, sexuality, and pleasure; the cover makes me fear that someone else has defined the larger terms.

I trust Pace’s tenderness and workmanship, but not his honesty. Nothing here makes the care look false. If anything, his exhausted sleep and instinctive arm around Vee make it harder to dismiss him as simply manipulative. That deepens rather than resolves my suspicion: he appears genuinely attached while still withholding the fact that could reorganize everything. Vee I love and want to protect. Her choice to stay before the mirror when *Floozy* appears feels like real movement since the last chapter’s verbal claim, “I wanted it. And I took it.” Here she enacts that claim bodily.

My one point of friction is the summary “after that there was nothing left for either of them to decide.” Emotionally it fits the rush, but in a book so attentive to choosing, it briefly blurs what specifically happens and what each chooses. I did not need graphic detail, but the phrasing feels more fated than the surrounding scene’s careful agency.

“Peekaboo” first feels playful—the slit gives and takes back her bare leg with every stride—but it also names the structure of concealment and revelation. The dress shows her while selectively covering her; Pace waits out of sight and then sees her; Vee imagines entering public view while the truth of who made the gown remains hidden. The title did give away that some revealing would occur, but not the emotional form of it.

*With a Long Spoon* still has no single settled meaning for me. Feeding, appetite, and dangerous intimacy remain active, but this chapter adds another object made with long, patient care and then offered as though effortless. The tagline is much sharper now: Vee freely chooses the visit, the bare dress, the removal of her underwear, the walk to Pace, and the sex. Every yes feels intensely hers. The trap is that those choices are being made inside a relationship whose founding truth she still does not have.

I want the induction next—or at least soon. I want to see Randi’s face when Vee arrives in Pace’s gown, especially if Randi knows he made it. My guess from here is that the dress will make Vee publicly impossible to overlook and may bring the hidden worlds dangerously close together. I dread the gown becoming evidence: to Brooke, to Randi, or eventually to Vee herself.
