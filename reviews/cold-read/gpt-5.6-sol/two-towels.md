- **Origin of the dress idea:** On the summit days earlier, while holding cherries under a silvering sky, Vee mentioned an honor-society induction, a silk dress she could not afford, and its color. Pace replied that it sounded beautiful. Vee had abandoned the idea and planned to wear a serviceable black dress she wears to everything.
- **Flannel revelation:** Vee realizes Pace made the good shirt he lent her—the one she wore heavily, with flat seams, a well-sitting collar, and no label. She had previously turned it inside out searching for a brand. Pace says he made it because he could not find a shirt he liked.
- **Dress offer:** Pace picks up a coiled measuring tape and offers to make Vee’s dress. To do it properly, he would measure her down to what she would wear underneath, in good light, assess the fall, then cut and sew it. He estimates a couple of weeks and says it would be ready before induction.
- **Consent and emotion:** Pace leaves the decision open with “If you want it.” Vee understands he remembered her discarded wish, obtained silk, reserved weeks of labor, and intends to return the wish made real. Her eyes become hot and full, but she does not cry. She understands the dress requires her willingness to be looked at and answers, “Yes. Yes—of course, yes,” warmly and without qualification.
