## Reader reaction

A quieter chapter — a chaser after the hike. It's Vee telling Cassie everything, and it works on me because I *have* the source scene fresh; watching her narrate it, giddy and sore, lets me feel how far in she is without the author saying so.

**Vee.** I like her here. The physical comedy — "My legs are going to file a complaint," lowering herself "like a much older woman," the blister "like a second toe" — is genuinely charming and grounds all the swoon. What lands hardest is the tell she doesn't hide: "She said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed.*" The eating is the erotic peak *for her.* That's true to what I've been carrying — shame and appetite fused, food as the charged thing — and it's rendered here without a thumb on the scale. And "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain" — that's the real gift he gave her, and she names it herself. She's radiant and I'm fond of her and a little sick for her.

**Cassie.** This is the chapter's spine for me. She comes in armed — "I had your blood type," "a whole witness statement ready," "revising a file." Her radar is fully up, playing the joke but meaning it: *no windowless van, brought you back, with your shoes on.* And then I watched it stand down in real time — "Cassie, who had been doing the dry thing, the file-revising thing, went still and stayed that way." Rapt. Disarmed by exactly the provision-details (he got out into the rain, back turned) that I read as the seduction's whole method. That's the ache: the smartest, most protective voice in Vee's life gets talked out of her own alarm by the very evidence that should sharpen it. And her final counsel is *boots,* not *him* — "Borrow real boots next time." She critiques the footwear and blesses the shirt. That quietly gutted me.

**Pace.** Entirely offstage, present only as Vee's telling — "of course he had a kit," the forearms, "he looked at me like I'd handed him something." My distrust is all carry-in; nothing on *this* page earns it. Which is exactly the machine working: secondhand and adoring, he's spotless.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** The text itself gives me nothing new to doubt. The dread is structural — it comes from me knowing Randi exists and Cassie doesn't. The chapter's real move is watching the one skeptic get converted. That's more chilling to me than any tell would be.

**Erotic charge.** Low and warm rather than hot — appropriate, it's a debrief. The charge is displaced onto the sandwich, deliberately, and that "the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" line is the erotic center. Not aroused here; tender, and a little uneasy at how good the story sounds when she tells it.

**Friction.** Very little. The one place I felt the author's presence was "she slowed then, as she'd slowed at the tree the other night" — a callback I only half-hold, and it reads slightly like a note to me to notice a rhythm. Minor. Otherwise the dialogue is clean and does its own work.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to know if Cassie ever gets a fact that *doesn't* dissolve on contact with Vee's happiness. I dread the induction — the russet dress question is still live, and this domestic warmth feels like the calm before the next escalation. *Guess from here:* the dress arrives (from him, or engineered via Randi) at the November induction, and it'll be received as another perfect gift. And I keep waiting for Randi to surface in Vee's actual life — she's been offstage too long for someone supposedly this close.
