## Reader reaction

Pace is the one I feel for here, and I feel for him mostly because he can't see the room he's in. Randi arrives on a Friday with no warning, "her whole face opened," pleased in a way the page says she has never let show at that door. It warmed me, and then it made me wary. She edits Vee's morning for him: "She was a wreck. She cried." Vee never said she cried. When Randi tells him "Give her a day," she is steering him with the same timing she gave Vee, who was told "He won't text you." Both of them now act out Randi's prediction. Pace calls it "only what he was," and I read that as being played without knowing it. The text earned that suspicion. I don't think she's cold, though. The tenderness reads as real, and the managing seems to go on alongside it.

The sex is where this chapter peaks, and it works on me. Randi pulls his head up before she comes, "not like that," then sits on him and narrates Vee in her bra, the hands up the long skirt, the thumb behind the knee: "she made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it." The charge comes from Randi enjoying the telling and from her body answering every word. Her want for Vee is nearly out in the open, and Pace takes it as a gift. "I haven't stopped thinking about her" is the line that burns. The blue toenails clawing the sheet are a good last image. It's hot, and it's also sad, because she's staging her hunger as an offering again. She also stays overnight, which is a change from the early chapters.

Friction is small. The Sheri ride is a pleasant cooldown but a little expository. She says the novel's stakes aloud: "If that's not already complicated, it will be." I liked her bluntness and the way Pace gushes about Vee until she says "Listen to you." His own enthusiasm gives him away. "She couldn't take your heat" is a nice line. "She knows. She likes her." / "Not yet." compresses the whole situation neatly, and I wish it had been left for me to infer.

Pace's "I'm glad it was you" and "it warmed him that the two of them had that between them without him" are kind and also blind. He still reads bodies better than hearts. I'm fond of him and I'm a little afraid for him.

**Title:** "Space" works several ways. It's the day Randi tells everyone to leave Vee. It's the room Pace leaves her. It echoes the dance lesson, where the "space you're keeping" is what gets you stepped on, so the space may be less kind than it sounds. "Give her a day" and "He leaves you the room" both point at it. The book title and series title, "With a Long Spoon" and "A Warm Reception," still promise warmth with a caution about sup­ping with something. I read the long spoon as the distance Randi keeps even in her warmth. No tagline was supplied.
