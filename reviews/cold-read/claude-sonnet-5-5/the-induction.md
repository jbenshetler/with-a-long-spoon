## Reader reaction

I'm reading this chapter as a reader who has just come out of "The Practice Room," and the two chapters feel linked.

**Felt read**

This is the chapter the book has been building toward since the burgundy silk, and it lands. The turn I felt in my body is the second step. "She took a breath, and made the second step long." Vee has spent the whole book hearing her mother say *floozie*, and now she chooses to show her leg on purpose to a room full of somebody's parents. "the heat came up — her body agreeing, low and certain" is the best swoon in the chapter. It's shame turned into something she owns, and the page lets her have it without lecturing. I was rooting hard for her. The "nothing at all under the silk" detail is hot in a quiet way. Her hand at her hip, holding the slit shut, is the clothed charge I like most.

The chapter also holds back, and that earned some unease. Vee wants to stop their hearts, and she does. But the pleasure sits in a room where she's alone with it. Pace is in Berkeley and Randi is at "an officers' meeting." The dress, the heels, the plum nails and the debt-shaped extravagance all trace back to the two of them, and neither is there to see it. I can't say Randi's absence is suspicious. The text gives a flat, plausible reason. But I noticed it, and I noticed that Vee sent the photo to Pace and not to Randi.

Cassie is the character who moved me most. "You look—" and not finishing is "a standing ovation," and her eyes are bright "which she'd deny to the grave." She holds Vee's phone and steers her out to a terrible sandwich. She's plainly the one who loves Vee for herself. Vee can't give her the real thing, though. "Which was kind, and was not it." Vee keeps the true part to herself again, and that has been the drift for several chapters. I felt a small ache over Cassie.

Vee's own interior is the richest it's been. The line about "nobody in this room who had ever taught her what to be ashamed of" is a sharp reversal, and it's quietly sad. Her mother's bright phone voice is the most human thing here. The last sentence declines to settle whether the walk was pride or heat: "whether, by now, the two came to the same thing." I'll hold it open too. It could be liberation, or the shame wire still running with a new current on it.

Friction is small. The prose gets knotty in a few places: "the same three as the induction, and neither would move" and the tangled opening of the Pace paragraph. The "navy girls" imagery repeats a little. I also felt the author's hand in the "dread of it was behind her" close, which tidies the ending a bit. The chapter is short on actual sex, and its charge is all anticipation and display. That's fine, but I missed another body in the room.

**The titles.** "The Induction" is plain and gives nothing away. It names the ceremony, but it also reads as Vee being inducted into something else, a way of being looked at. "Induction" has a mathematical ring too, which suits this book. The volume title "A Warm Reception," under the series "With a Long Spoon," now sounds ominous to me. The long spoon is for supping with the devil. The warmth Vee keeps being given feels like the thing the title warns about. Nothing on the page proves it, but the dress, shoes and debt do pile up. The packet showed no tagline.

**What next.** I'm pulled hard to keep reading. I expect, as a guess from here, that Vee will want to show Randi what the dress did, and that the two of them and Pace will have to come into the open. I dread the moment Vee learns she was picked together.
