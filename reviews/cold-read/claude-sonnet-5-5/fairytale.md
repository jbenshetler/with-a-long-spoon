## Reader reaction

The chapter swept me up, and then it left me cold on purpose. It's Randi, alone, over New Year's in Gstaad. That's a long way from Vee's cold doorstep, and I felt the cut. I missed Vee and Pace for a beat. Then the chapter showed me why it had moved me away from them.

**Randi.** My feeling for her shifted most here. The breakfast with her parents hurt in small, exact ways. She hugs her father "eating her revulsion." She keeps her plain white-gold bracelet "out of the light." She invents the hearts she's broken because "the invented ones were safer than the real ones." I ached for her. Then the ski day and the ball worked on me the way they worked on her. She laughs on the lift, he skis better than her and spends none of it proving it, and the dress is that pale blue that wakes her eyes. I let myself believe the fairytale, and the book made me pay for it. "Nothing developed" is the chapter's best line. The sandwich comparison, "the pleasure real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared," is cruel and funny, and I believed it. I now trust her self-knowledge more than I did, and I'm sorrier for her. The line "Her body had spent itself on him and she remained unmoved" got me.

**Swoon and heat.** The swoon is the ball: the bubble that breaks into scented smoke, the waltz where she finally lets someone steer, the sleigh. The sex itself is skipped, and only the aftermath is on the page. The lipstick ring on his body, "felt her own mouth do it from a long way off," is cold, not hot, and it's meant to be. The heat that's in this chapter is borrowed. "For one second, from nowhere, Pace" is the only moment that charged me. The chapter's real thesis is that this faultless man can't touch what Pace's worst night could. I felt that.

**Vee.** Vee is on the page only as a text, and she's the center of it anyway. I'd been reading Randi's kisses as a game or as jealousy. Here she says outright that she loves Vee "without some private qualifier," and that Vee is "her gorgeous." I believe it. The reasoning for staying quiet feels earned: she can't picture Vee's face, and she isn't brave. Her plan is "as long as the word kept its cover." That's a loaded sentence, and I can't tell whether she means to deceive Vee or just to protect herself. The text exchange hurt. Vee offers "i'm here if you want to talk. or not," and Randi sends back a true-but-empty "fairytale." The Vee who wrote that text is also the Vee who ended the last chapter crying in the cold, and I feel the weight of that.

**Pace.** He comes in as contrast: "Pace had never assumed anything in his life. Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." That quietly confirms his consent habit from Randi's side, and it makes his refusal to say a word for Vee sting more. I'm unsettled by how little Randi knows about Vee's breakup. She doesn't seem to know it happened, and that gap hangs over the whole chapter.

**Trust and suspicion.** John earned no suspicion. He's a bit of a template, "cotillion-good," and he's meant to be. What's off is Randi's resolve to "let Vee arrive at it on her own." That's a decision to steer someone without telling her, and it pulls against the love she describes. The page doesn't settle whether it's tenderness or control, and I'm leaving it open.

**Friction.** The ski and ball sections run long and glossy, and a few lines are on-the-nose. "Nothing had happened to her eyes" lands, but "The flaw was hers, carried into the room the same as the cold" then explains it. The cold-versus-warmth image is stated three times. Her announcement to herself that she's "not brave enough" is a bit tidy.

**Titles.** "Fairytale" is Randi's own word for the lie she texts Vee, and it recolors the chapter. The whole thing is a fairytale performed for a mirror, and the real fairy-tale-shaped thing is Vee, whom she can't have. The book title, *With a Long Spoon* (Book Two, *A Warm Reception*), now reads to me as the warning about sitting down to eat with the devil. "A Warm Reception" is ironic: the warmth here is borrowed, "in the warmth she could borrow and could not make." I have no cover tagline to go on.

**Next.** My guess from here is that Randi comes back and, at brunch, doesn't know Vee and Pace have fractured. I dread her using the "cover" of friendship at exactly the wrong moment. I want her to be brave, and I expect she won't be yet. I'm hooked.
