## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is a quiet chapter that did a lot of damage, and I felt it in my stomach. Vee is wonderful here, glowing and generous, handing Randi the whole Sheri story as a gift. The Sheri laugh, "armed to the teeth for a woman who does not exist", is lovely. But the chapter's real event is a thumb that stops. Vee says Sheri would have "unwrapped" her on the floor, and Randi's thumb freezes on her hand. Then comes the flat "And you liked it." Vee says "No," too fast, and patches it: "Not my thing." I didn't believe her, and I don't think Vee does either. She has just spent two weeks learning that "gross" and "enthusiastic" can sit in one body. The "No" lands right beside the earlier sentence she can't shake: "He likes it. Actually — he's enthusiastic."

Randi is the one I feel most for, and my feeling shifted. Before, she was the gifted guide. Now she looks like a woman visibly gripping the table. The page shows her go still, go quiet, and "didn't reach, didn't laugh, didn't fill it." Vee reads it as Randi still guarding her from the rival. That's the sweetest wrong reading in the book so far, and I ached at how kindly it's meant. "I know she isn't, gorgeous" is a line with something else under it. Randi has told the truth about the threat all night and can't say what she means by it. After the previous chapter, where she told Pace "She's not the only one in love," I read this as jealousy of a different kind. She isn't jealous of Sheri taking Pace. She's jealous of Sheri's open appreciation of Vee, which Randi can't voice.

Vee also turns Sheri's story into something she carries to Randi: "Saying it out loud did something to her that hearing it hadn't." That's tender. She quietly leaves out the part she'll keep for herself ("the other part she didn't write down. She had that one cold"). I can't tell whether that's the Sheri look or the kiss at the end. The page leaves it open, and I'll leave it open too.

The waxing sequence is where I felt the pull and the unease together. Pace asks for "bare" as "part of your surprise," and Vee says yes before he finishes. That's consent, but she's also half-offering it. Then Randi supplies the salon, "Sondra, Draper Street, say my name in the first sentence," with the appointment already made. Randi is authoring the preparation for Pace's surprise, which is quietly delicious and a little controlling. The plan to make Pace come first with her mouth is hot and funny, and Randi's "Nothing I love more than a date who takes care of me first" is a slip you can nearly hear.

The goodbye kiss is the real heat. Vee turns her face up before Randi starts, closes her eyes, and waits for the hand. Randi's mouth "eased back without ever leaving Vee's, and then she took more of it, and this time it stayed." Vee's mouth goes to Randi's neck "and neither of them said a word about it." That's the peak. The thing neither of them says is the whole chapter.

Friction: the title *Between* is oblique at first and then lands. Vee is between Pace and Randi. The thumb stops between two sentences. The kiss lingers between friendly and not. It isn't spoiling anything. It sits on the seam the chapter walks. The book's title, A Place at the Table, fits the booth dinners and the table of friends in the preceding chapters. *With a Long Spoon*, the series title, feels like warning me about supping with the devil. I don't see a devil here, just two women eating carefully around each other. I have no tagline in my packet.

Smaller friction: the telling is a bit long before anything turns. The repeat of the booth, the jukebox and the "ticklish" kiss pattern is familiar. I dread that Vee will tell Randi about the waxing results, and that Randi has now been handed Vee's bare body as a new item to hear about. I want Vee to say the unwritten thing. I expect she won't yet. My guess from here: the next brunch gets very hot, and the "surprise" for Pace brings the Randi question back around.
