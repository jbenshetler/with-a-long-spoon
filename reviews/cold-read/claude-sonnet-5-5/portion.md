## Reader reaction

Pace is the one I felt most for here, and Randi is the one I'm now worried about. This is a small chapter with a lot of pain in it.

Pace cooks all afternoon, and the detail that undid me was "Hers was the small one, the one he sliced her apples into." The house smells of the pot, the wine is open an hour, and he's glad all the way down when he hears about Friday. He is happy for Vee, he's tender about it, and he's sure it's shared. It isn't. Randi's "It's wonderful. You shouldn't have" landed like a stranger's thanks, and he notices that she never says things like that to him. I ached for him not knowing where to put it.

Randi is the chapter's center, and she's hard to watch. She drags the spoon along the top of the sauce and never eats. She presses the glass rim under her thumbnail, which echoes the bottle cap in chapter 1 and the water glass at lunch. Her voice goes flat: "She's not ready. Or you're not." "It's selfish, Pace." Her anger sounds like jealousy, but it's dressed up as impatience for the game. She wants Vee, and she wants Pace to have given up the weekend with Vee. She also can't say either thing. I believe that reading, but the page doesn't confirm it. She never says it, and I'm holding it a little open.

The sex is the chapter's sharpest turn. She skips the inch-and-stop and the eyes. She says no "tell me," and she rides him with her eyes shut. "whatever this was, it was not for him, and he gave it to her anyway." That line got me. It's charged, and I felt it as grief more than heat. The wanting is real, but it's aimed at someone who isn't there. Then she goes straight into the cashmere blanket, back turned. He thinks "it was the want... with an edge on it tonight." He's wrong, and I know it, and I can't tell him.

He also does something that stung. "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything." He's right, and it's cruel to her in the way a correct answer can be. He doesn't know what he did, and he looks "back through the evening from the gravel forward" and finds nothing. The covered bowl in the fridge, "for her," is the most tender and most helpless image in the book so far.

Trust and suspicion: I don't suspect Pace of anything new. I do feel dread for how this plan, which was Randi's condition, is eating her. I don't know whether she's jealous of Pace, of Vee, or of the whole arrangement. She still keeps her feelings walled off from Pace. The two scenes of her calm and the two of her cracked feel like the same woman.

Erotic charge: low-moderate. The sex scene is short and bleak by design. It works as a wound, not as arousal. I wanted more of the old rhythm and felt its absence, which I think is the point.

Friction: Pace's interpretation is a little too neatly wrong ("it was the want... from somewhere"). He is good at reading people, so his blindness is partly the author's thumb. I'd also like a touch more Randi interiority. We get none, and the chapter holds back from her entirely, which works but frustrates me.

Titles: "Portion" is a very good fit. It's the small bowl, the one line drawn through the sauce, the thin apple slices from chapter 1, and Randi rationing herself. It also asks how much of Vee each of them is portioned, and how much "as long as she needs" is a portion of Pace. The volume title, "A Polite Invitation," sits in tension with this. Nothing here is polite; everyone is being polite and starving. The series title, "With a Long Spoon," I now read as the distance you keep from something dangerous or hungry. Here Randi holds the spoon and doesn't eat. I have no tagline in my packet.

Next: I'm afraid of Randi forcing the pace on Vee, something past the kiss in the car. I'd guess (from here) that she either goes to Vee herself or breaks with Pace. I'm very much pulled forward.
