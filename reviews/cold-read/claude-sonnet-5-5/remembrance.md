I'm not sure how I feel about Pace anymore, and this chapter is why. He is back at the center, and the book now lets me see how far he has gone into the plan. He's tender with Vee, but he also wants to be in the room when she comes for the first time with Randi's mouth on her, and he wants "to be the reason she was in the room." "And she would not know, until she did." That line chilled me. In the same chapter he keeps Saturday from Randi, "It was not his to hand across," and he snaps "Don't" when her teeth close on his shoulder. He's protective of Vee and of himself, and both sit beside a scheme built on her not knowing. I trust his tenderness and distrust his method. I can't make those two feelings cancel, and I think the book wants that.

Randi is the one who ached for me. She is the first to say "It's her, isn't it," smiling a beat too long. She reads his silence. She goes still under him at the idea of telling Vee, then comes alive at "give it to her." She also asks "How are you going to tell her about us?", which sets the trap and the question together. It reads as a woman steering a man toward a plan that keeps her hidden from Vee, and from herself. The Kentucky comes up in "*me*" drawn long and soft, and I believe that moment. "She'll remember me." She stays after for the first time in two visits, then leaves anyway "so you can sleep." Her tenderness looks like retreat.
