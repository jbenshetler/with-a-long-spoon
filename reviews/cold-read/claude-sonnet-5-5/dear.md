## Reader reaction

Vee is soaring, and I'm soaring a little with her. The opening is lovely: the cracked window, the spill of news, the clumsy spin that nearly takes out the closet door, and "Look at me." The line I keep returning to is "I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose." It's a real yes, and it lands, but I know things she doesn't. I watched Pace plan her from a retaining wall. I watched Randi pick her. Her proud consent is true on the page, and it's also being steered by two people I've seen at work. That doubleness is what makes the scene ache instead of just glow.

Cassie is the one I feel most for. She's warm and quick ("How much did he give you to drink?"), and she's the only person watching out for Vee. Then the scene breaks the other way. Cassie leaves class with something folded away in her look, and Vee doesn't watch her go. Vee also tells Randi that the smoothie place "isn't really her place," meaning Cassie's. Vee reads this as healthy, and then something "turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the cruelest beat of the chapter. She's already trimming her friend to fit the new room, and she doesn't see it. The earlier "Goodbye, Vee" sits very differently now.

Randi unsettles me, and I'm unsure how much. At the smoothie place she is radiant and funny, and she's also a feeding hand. She says, "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Then she says, "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Vee drops the dignified "exception" and takes the smaller, truer line. The French-date catastrophe reads like a prop: I know Randi was with Pace, or at least that her weekend wasn't that. Her "Ask and then ravish" comes "a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That is the only moment the performance slips, and it's the one that hooked me. She wants something, and the wanting is aimed at Vee while she hands Vee to the man. I feel real tenderness for both of them and real dread. The cool, damp hand held "in plain view" says "dear friend" like a spent coin, and Vee is overwhelmed to be chosen. I believe the choosing is partly true. That makes it worse.

Pace is offstage, but his fingerprints are everywhere in Vee's retelling: "May I choose?", the restful softness, and the hike, "real shoes." She's quoting his script back, and Randi enjoys it.

Erotic charge is low-simmer here. The peak is Vee's hands sliding down her thighs while she imagines the trail ("Before he ravished me"), and Randi's low echo. The Brooke interruption is cold and well done. Vee shrinks in the high chair, and "Vee's my dear friend" is the one good snap of social power in the chapter.

Friction: the chapter is a little repetitive. The high table, the "dear friend" moment and the hug all echo Chi Latte almost word for word. I think that's deliberate, but it nearly stalls. Brooke's taller companion is a bit vague. The line "Rich people hike. Poor people walk." is funny, but Cassie shows no real worry beyond jokes.

"Dear" is the chapter's pivot word, and it's quietly menacing now. It's also affectionate, and also what you write above a letter. It gave nothing away; it recolored the hug. The series title, With a Long Spoon, and Book One, "A Polite Invitation," fit what I'm watching: everyone is so courteous and so careful about distance. The invitation is the hike, and "May I" is its politeness. The long spoon implies someone supping with a danger they think they can handle. I don't know who that is yet. I guess Randi, maybe Vee.

Next: the hike, and Vee in "real shoes" at Pace's house. I dread the first time Vee sees the bench. I also want Cassie to push back, and I want Randi to crack.
