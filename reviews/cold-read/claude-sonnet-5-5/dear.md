## Reader reaction

Vee carries this chapter, and I'm fond of her. The spin into the bean bag, "Look at me," and "I did it on purpose" all worked on me. Cassie's "How much did he give you to drink?" is a good, protective beat, and Vee's answer, "nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it," is the line I trust least. She believes it, and she may be right. But I just read the evening from the other side. A man who sliced apple thin because she ate it faster that way, and who heard "It's for this" about a math trick, knows exactly how to make a yes feel like her own idea. I don't doubt her wanting. I doubt how little she knows about who built the evening.

Randi is the one that unsettled me. In the Smoothie Bird she tells an invented catastrophe date, the lacrosse boy and the French place, to a girl whose boyfriend-to-be is, as far as I know, hers. "Paul?" is a faked forgetting, and she snaps her fingers for it. The whole scene is Randi harvesting. She repeats back "You let him choose for you. And you liked it," and "Because of him," and she's gentle each time. She feeds Vee the hiking picture ("let your mountain man ravish you against a tree"). Then comes the low, late "Ask and then ravish," said at Vee's own volume. That line gave me a small cold shiver. It could be her own hunger leaking out, or her sorting through what Pace does. I don't know which, and the page doesn't say. I can't settle her motive, and I won't. But the sorority-president hand on the table, "Vee's my dear friend," feels bought for a reason, and Brooke earlier was grilling Randi about Pace. Vee reads it as being chosen. I read a woman spending Vee as currency.

The swoon is real, and it's Vee's. The shelf of the high chair, the "wrong size" feeling, then "She felt picked. She carried it the whole way home" is a bittersweet ending, because "picked" is exactly what I fear she is. Cassie is the ache. Her look at Randi goes "folded away," and Vee's "Not her thing" about Cassie, with "Something turned over, small, and she let it lie," is Vee quietly shedding the one person who asks the real question. Earlier Cassie said "Goodbye, Vee" in that flat finished way, and this chapter echoes it. I want Cassie to be wrong about nothing and ignored anyway.

Erotic charge is low here and mostly sideways: Vee's hands running down her thighs, the blush on the sun side of her face while Randi watches, the lift-a-breast joke. The flicker works because I know what Randi wants. The pool chapter and the last kiss carried more heat. This one is a slow-burn conversation chapter, and it's good at that. Friction: Brooke's tall friend scanning Vee felt a bit staged. The "Look at me" scene runs a touch long.

Title: "Dear." It's a plain word that this chapter turns edged. "My dear friend" is affection and ownership at once, and it works as a salutation to a letter that may be a long way from sincere. It also fits the book's cover, "With a Long Spoon · Book One: A Polite Invitation." "Long spoon" suggests supping with the devil and keeping your distance. This chapter shows me the invitation: polite, warm, "may I choose." I don't know yet who holds the spoon. The series promises courtesy as the way in, and I now feel the dread of courtesy. The jacket and tagline aren't in my packet, so I can't weigh them.

Next: the hike on Sunday, and I dread the moment Vee finds out whose man he is. I'm guessing Randi will be there, or Pace will tell. I want to keep reading badly.
