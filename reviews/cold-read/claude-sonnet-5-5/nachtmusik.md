## Reader reaction

Randi has stopped being a character I was waiting on and become the one I'm afraid for. The second half hurt. It's very good, and I felt sick reading it. She slides his arm off "a little at a time" and takes the phone off the magnet. Then she tries his code with her own birthday, carefully, and it fails. Vee's birthday opens it. That detail landed like a hand on my sternum, and the book doesn't comment on it. Then she writes to Vee as him: *"makes me wish I was a dress."* She reads Vee's delight, *"who are you and what did you do with him??"*, and her eyes sting and the Kentucky comes up: "Oh, honey." Then she deletes all of it, including the sweetest reply Vee has sent in the whole book. She puts the phone back so exactly that the magnet clicks the same way.

The unease is earned, and it's specific. This is a book that cares about a clean yes, and here she borrows his voice and Vee's trust without either of them knowing. I don't know yet whether I read her as predatory or drowning, and the page leaves that open. It shows the pull plainly. She zooms in on the picture and sees what Pace "looked at on a bed in Berkeley and seen a dress." She knows Vee's face from across a table, "the moment before Vee leaned in." Her nipples tighten under the cashmere, and she thinks *Damn Brooke.* I believe her hunger. The part that aches is that the only way she can voice it is through his mouth.

Pace is still tender and still a bit blind. He sees the flicker cross her face when he refuses to show the photo, "It's hers, she sent it to me", and sets it down. I admire the refusal, and it costs Randi something. He also notices she steers every conversation, and he was "restless since Monday," and he sleeps. I'm fond of him and I'm bracing for him.

The first half is a different chapter. Her arrival is glad and has real heat: the kiss at the airport, the Mercedes taken fast on the curves, "Take me hard and quick," the bite. But I could feel the performance. The sex runs on Vee. She is feeding him the fantasy of the dress with "almost nothing between my hand and her skin," and it works on him, and on me, because she is working so hard at it. The heat peaks there, and it burns because I can see her at the center of it, arranging it so she never has to ask for anything herself. It doesn't go slack, but it's the tightest, saddest kind of heat.

On friction, I was lightly irritated by "feline flourish" and "tits out." The latter, though, may be her armor talking. I don't want the chapter to resolve her. The ending leaves her awake with her eyes open and the phone "keeping" something, and I'm glad it stops there.

The title *Nachtmusik* is night music, and it fits better than I expected. The low music plays all through the house, Pet Shop Boys, "the same cool voice," and she lets it cover her. It's the soundtrack for a theft done quietly. The series, *With a Long Spoon*, and the book title, *A Polite Invitation*, sit uneasily with this chapter: she is sitting very close to the fire, with the long spoon in her hand. No tagline was shown. I keep wondering who is supping with whom.

As a guess from here, she'll keep the picture, and maybe the "keeping" matters more than the deletion. I want her to be seen, and I dread what happens to Vee if Vee learns who answered her.
