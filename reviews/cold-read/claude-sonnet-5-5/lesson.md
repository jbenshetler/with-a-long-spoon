## Reader reaction

The first thing I felt was ache, and it stayed. Vee has had a beautiful run with Pace, so watching her wreck it in one sentence hurt. The dance lesson works because nothing in it is sexual and it still charges the air. "You have a job here. Give me something to push back against." The hand at her shoulder blade, the lead arriving before her feet know it, and "There it is," quiet: I felt that more than most of the explicit scenes. It's courtship by instruction. After the Peter and Daphne chapter, Pace is dearer to me than he was. His dressing for the dancing and not the weather ("he'd dressed for the dancing, not the weather") made me soft toward him.

Then the Rusty Nail turns. I believed Vee's slide completely. She looks for the join between Pace and the Pace she's built, the hugging women make a count she didn't mean to start, and her mother's line arrives flat and on time. The blonde's "so good about getting a girl home safe" is poison with a smile. Her "Does your *service* include…" is cringe-perfect, the glass going past catching. I want to shake her and I understand her. "Floozies. She watched them." The shame and the jealousy run on the same wire, as before. The bartender's "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey" was the chapter's warm center. It recolors the blonde as a rival, not as testimony, and I'm not sure the blonde was lying or just territorial.

Trust and suspicion: I distrust no one new. My unease is about what I know and Vee doesn't. Pace is plainly good in this chapter, and he left because she wounded him. He didn't leave out of any guilt. Randi is the person Vee runs to at dawn, and I'm a step ahead there. Randi's smile will arrange itself over whatever Vee confesses, and I dread what it travels into. I don't know whether Randi will comfort her or something sharper. Vee also never wonders why the bartender knew who she was, "the one the girls were all talking about." I held that open.

Erotic charge is low-simmer here, mostly in the dance frame and the sleepless shirt. The line about the sensation under the shame ("his hand at her shoulder blade… the moment the dance took her") is the hottest beat. The shirt now smelling of "fabric softener and no one" is a fine small grief. The middle bar section is slack on heat but purposeful.

Friction: the section breaks are choppy, and the chapter is a bit scene-list. "She'd spread her legs like a slut" felt blunt even for her mother's voice, though it's earned. The cab details (the belching engine, the fare) are one beat too many. Her not calling Cassie is believable but I'll miss Cassie.

Titles: "Lesson" is neat. The dance lesson is the obvious one. The other lessons are what the girls teach her, what Vee learns about her own cruelty, and possibly what Pace's women have all learned. The title doesn't give anything away. The volume title, *A Warm Reception*, and the series line *With a Long Spoon* both sit in my mind with the Rusty Nail: a warm room, everyone glad to see him, and a fire under it. No tagline was supplied. The jacket's "walking into fire" is closer to what I felt.

What I want next: Vee to apologize to Pace and be met with grace, and I dread her confiding in Randi, because the jacket says everything travels. Guess from here: Randi will take this and use it, maybe gently.
