## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I felt a dread I couldn't quite pin down, and I never fully trusted the glow.

Vee is the happiest we've seen her, and I'm glad for her. The made bed as a "tattletale," the account her body keeps ("a good soreness no gym had ever given her"), and "Oh, yes" all landed warmly. Cassie is the one I ache for. She sits turned toward Vee through the whole lecture and doesn't care about the dress. She cares about what the dress did to her friend. Then Vee says "Cassie had never once in her life cared what was on anyone's feet," and goes shoe shopping with Randi anyway. Cassie goes to a lab and walks off alone. Vee barely notices.

Randi I now read two ways at once. In the room she's a delight: the yeti shoe, the rhinestone platforms, the real laugh. But the dread is hers, and I think the page earned it. Last chapter she pressed her thumbnail into a glass, called Pace "selfish," and rode him with her eyes shut. Here she says "You lucky slut," then "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." She fingers the silk, kneels on the boutique floor, and strokes Vee's plum toenail until Vee's floor tips. Then she spends effortlessly, in a way that makes Vee's credit card look like a drowning. I read her warmth as real, and also as hunger and management. Vee reads her pause as jealousy of Pace's attention. That's closer than Vee knows, and Vee still gets it wrong. I find that moving and a little frightening.

Pace is offstage, but he hangs over everything. Vee tells Randi "He was up all night finishing it. I watched him sleep." That sentence is the book's quiet knife for me. Randi's hand stays on the shoulder seam.

**Swoon / pull:** The dress scene in the dorm room worked best. Randi's eyes travel the bias line, then the fingers go up the seam and rest at the bare shoulder. It's heat without sex, and Vee holding still under the hand is the charge. The toe-thumb in the shop is the peak. It's a tiny touch that goes "all the way up the length of her."

**Suspicion:** Mine is earned by the page. Randi steers the shoe choice away from "the sensible one" toward the unaffordable. Vee has put money on the credit card and "there was nothing under it." Randi says "Love you, girl," and Vee thinks she's never had a friend like this. I can't tell if that's tenderness or capture, and I'm leaving it open.

**Friction:** "Randi looked up and smiled and said nothing at all" is slightly on the nose. Vee's narration also explains Randi's sadness in a tidy, pitying way ("nobody who had ever sat up a night over anything of hers"). It's dramatic irony, but it leans on me. The Simpson's paradox lecture is a bit signposted: same numbers, different answer depending on how you group. I noticed it and mostly forgave it.

**Titles:** "Sorority" first suggested the house, Brooke, the board. It's really about Vee being drawn into Randi's world, the shopping and the sisterhood as an economy of belonging. It also recolors "Love you, girl." The cover says *With a Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation*. Right now the invitation feels very polite, which is what makes it uneasy. I haven't seen the spoon yet.

**Next:** I want to know whether Cassie will say something. I dread the credit card bill and the induction night. My guess from here is that Randi will be there and Pace will see something he didn't expect.
