## Reader reaction

The chapter swept me up, but under the sweetness I felt a chill I couldn't entirely justify.

I'm moved by Vee, and I love Pace more than I expected to. The dress scene lands hard. She named a dress offhand, "said it the way you say things you've already let go of," and he kept it. He matched the cloth to *her*, not to the magazine photo. "He'd matched *her*" made my throat tighten. The shirt with no tag was a lovely reveal, and it explains why the flannel felt so right on her. His modesty also works on me: "I couldn't find a shirt I liked, so I made one." The error-correcting codes speech, with its "Say it more than once so the noise can't kill it," is the warmest he's been on the page. Vee's tears at "No one had ever—" got me.

Then the last beat turned it. "He couldn't make it without seeing her. That was the only thing standing between her and it — her own willingness to be looked at." The page has her treat this as a gift and a test at once. Her "Yes — of course, yes" is eager and unhedged, and I believe it's hers. But the offer is structured so the generosity and the undressing arrive together, and he phrases it as "If you want it." I'm still on his side. I don't think he's cynical. But I know things Vee doesn't. He's been collecting this girl for Randi too, and she's still unknowing inside a lovely trap. Every kindness now has two possible meanings, and I hold both open.

The house is full of small wrongnesses I noticed because of what I remember. It's too warm, "a close, even heat," and Vee files it under mysteries. I know he keeps it warm for Randi. The piano replaced Steely Dan and Blondie. The bathroom has "two towels on the rod." The bedroom door stays shut, "nothing withheld and nothing offered," and her sinking disappointment there is delicious. He stopped the kiss himself, which she says no boy has done. I read the house as tidied and edited for her, with Randi scrubbed out of it. When he flinched at "parents," a door eased shut, which fits the Daphne wound I still can't see. When she asks about money, the patent answer feels like a plausible fix for the "cheap counters, built furniture" puzzle. I accept it but don't fully trust it. I did notice his lie by omission: he keeps Randi's existence behind that closed door.

Erotic charge: low-to-medium. It peaks in the opening kiss and its aftermath. The dress-measuring promise, "down to what you'd wear under it, in good light," is a slow burn, and it's all anticipation. Her satin under everything, waiting for a door that doesn't open, is the best heat in the chapter. The tour sags a little. The shoe closet, the kitchen and the shelves are pleasing but long, and the "read half of him off that closet floor" lines over-explain.

Titles: "Two Towels" paid off only obliquely. The towels get one line in a hallway, which made me stop and count: two on the rod for one man who lives alone. That pairs with Randi and Vee, or with the three-way he's building. I read it as the chapter showing me its hand without Vee seeing. Book One, "A Polite Invitation," fits this exactly. "May I" and "if you want it" are polite, and they're also the machinery. "With a Long Spoon" suggests dining with the devil, and I feel that more now. I'm not convinced yet that anyone is the devil.

I want the measuring scene badly. I dread Vee finding the second towel. Guess from here: Randi shows up in this house, or Vee spots a trace of her, before the dress is finished.
