## Reader reaction

Pace is the only POV in this chapter, and it left me quietly aching for him. He drives home cold and hungry, and he's still hungry at the end. I'm moved by how plainly he registers the one thing he can't solve: "that she had not wanted to stay." He reads her leaving as appetite. I don't think that's the whole truth, and I don't think he believes it either. It's the second time he's kept something of Vee's from Randi ("That she had given him herself, in the truck with the rain coming down, and he kept it"), and my sense of him as a man with a divided loyalty has hardened. He is also withholding from the woman in his bed while she's the one asking him to give.

Randi hurt me most. The sex is a hunger she can't carry. Her laugh comes out "thin, not her laugh." Her belly is "tight as a board with the work of it." She says "I want us," "torn out of her," and I couldn't tell whether "us" means Pace, Vee, or all three. The page leaves it open, and I'm leaving it open. I won't say she's jealous or aching for Vee, though I feel both. She skips the part she keeps for herself, the lying loose on his chest, and she bolts. The unasked question matters too: "How was it?" and she never lets him answer. She wants the scent and the body but not the story. That's desire she can only take secondhand.

The erotic charge works, and it peaks in the slow descent, "An inch, and a stop," while she makes him watch her. Then comes "Tell me," and he feeds her Vee in details: gardenia and musk, chlorine in the hair, the soft stomach. The charge is that Vee is in the bed without being there. It's very hot and also a little sad. "Every garment was an interruption" is lovely. It dips only slightly when the logistics run long, as with the cardigan, the silk and the clasp. Then the aftermath, the shivering and the blanket and "See you," goes cold in a way I felt physically.

Trust and suspicion: I distrust nobody's tenderness, but the arrangement is clearly fraying. I notice that Pace is sharing Vee's body as a story, in a bed where Vee has no say. That unsettles me more than anything Randi did. The text earned it by showing him keep the truck confession hidden while giving up the smell of her skin. The blanket echoes the cashmere one from chapter 1, which makes Randi's retreat feel like a repeat of that bathroom lock.

Friction was minimal. "He took it for the want... If it was something else he could not have said what" is a bit of a nudge, but it's honest to his blindness, and I didn't mind.

The title: "Swim Lanes" lands for me as the chlorine, Vee's pool, but also as separate parallel lines that aren't supposed to cross. Randi, Pace and Vee each move in their own lane here, and this chapter is where the lanes show as separate. The book's title, *With a Long Spoon* (Book One, *A Polite Invitation*), now feels more pointed. The spoon is the long reach you need to sup with the devil, and I'm unsure who is the devil here. "Polite invitation" is the ironic frame: so far everyone has been courteous, and that's where the harm sits. No cover tagline was supplied.

What I want next: Randi and Vee in a room together with the want out in the open. I dread the moment Vee finds out she was picked, and I'm afraid Pace's hidden loyalty will be what breaks the arrangement. A guess from here: Randi's departure foreshadows her pulling away from Pace rather than from the plan.
