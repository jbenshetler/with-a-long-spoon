## Reader reaction

Vee is still the easiest person in the book to love. She is giddy, sated and sure of herself, and she thinks "*He's all mine*" with no guard up. The opening works on me as warmth first. I like the red hair as a curtain around their faces, and I like her noticing that he takes comfort at her breast and that it isn't only sex. When he's hard again and she says "Again" as a statement, not a question, I feel her delight in being wanted. "A girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it" is the line that moved me most. It's the cure for her mother's *floozie*, and it makes me want her to keep it.

The heat is real but kind. It peaks in the first scene, in the hands that arrange her limb by limb, with a kiss at every place he sets her. I like how he grins "like a boy who'd gotten away with something." That's hot because it's tender and lets her pose herself inside his looking. The photo works for me as erotic and consensual. She brings it up herself, she asks for a promise, and she sends it "before the nerve could leave her," which reads as bravery, not pressure.

The unease comes from the page, not from me being on guard. His promise is "Never. I'd never share your photograph." That is exactly worded. I know he passes Vee along to Randi in other ways, by talk, and I know about the earlier chapters where he "kept" some things back. So the sentence reads as true and also not quite the sentence she asked for. She asked "never share me with anyone," and he answered about the photograph only. I'm not saying he'll send it. I'm saying I noticed the gap, and the page gave me it. I hold it open. It may just be a man being precise.

I like him here. The cooking is lovely and sensory: the lemon rain, the garlic smashed three times, the butter turning the sauce "from bright to silk." Then "I don't go back" lands hard. His face goes "level and exact," and she takes her hand off it and lets "the quiet be kind." That's the Peter-with-no-door again, and I'm intrigued, not suspicious. The Sheri flicker of jealousy is small and human, and her relief at "She's alone" is a bit sad. I'd guess Sheri isn't the threat she fears.

The drawer scene is the chapter's best move. Vee reads the bare counter as "he minded," and it turns out he's given her the top drawer and crushed his own things into the bottom one. It is a quiet, almost wordless "yes, stay." It echoes the green scrunchies he left earlier, and the promise of duration is stronger now. "There was never a note" is a good line, because his love lives in objects and not in saying it. I felt a real ache for her, since she's content to live "inside the everything-he-did" while the word he hasn't said stays unsaid. And I know she whispered "Peter" to a sleeping man. That is tender and a little sad.

Friction is minor. The chapter has no Randi in it at all, and that absence is loud after the brunch chapter. Vee is happily telling Pace about Randi, and I know what Randi and Pace are doing with her. The dramatic irony does the work. The wine and cooking run a touch long as procedure, though the sensory detail mostly earns it.

The title "Above Him" first reads as the literal opening, her over his chest, her hair a curtain. On second thought it recolors. She is above him in the picture, and the drawer is arranged as top and bottom, with her given the top one. It also hints at a power reversal, since she is the one he is kneeling to. I like it, and it gives nothing away. The series title *With a Long Spoon* and the book's "A Warm Reception" now feel pointed. The reception is warm, and the spoon is for supping with someone you shouldn't trust. The long spoon is the line I'd keep half an eye on. This chapter feeds her by hand and by spoon, tasting first.

I want to keep reading. I dread the day she learns about the planning, and I expect the photo to matter somewhere (a guess from here). I also want to know what "I don't go back" costs him.
