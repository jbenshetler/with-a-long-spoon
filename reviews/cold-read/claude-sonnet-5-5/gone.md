## Reader reaction

Randi is the one I feel for in this chapter. She's lying on Pace's chest talking about Vee, and it isn't the avid, demanding debrief from earlier. It's fond and unguarded. "I didn't think I'd like her this much... She got past me before I could help it." Then she says "God, she's lovely," and it reads as plain confession. Pace takes all of it as erotic material. He pictures the two women together, then Vee alone in his sheet. He can't see that the woman on top of him is falling in love.

The best moment is small. Pace says Vee makes a room go brighter, "like somebody found the lamp." Randi goes still, "the kind that takes holding," then says "Yeah. She is." and finds him hard. "So soon?" is a deflection, and I felt it. The sex that follows is hot, with her slow, coming apart by degrees, and it also hurts a little. She's using his body to put something down. Pace keeps being tender and almost wholly blind. "She's ashamed" is a good read of Vee. He has no idea he's lying under a second person who is ashamed, and what she's ashamed of is right there in her own mouth.

I trust Pace's goodness here and don't suspect him of malice. I do feel the gap between his skill with bodies and his deafness to hearts, which the chapter keeps pressing. Vee is offstage but warm. The shoe-store anecdote, the bigfoot mime and the red-faced embarrassment, made me like her more.

The food thread is what moved me most. Randi asks to be fed, asks for more wine when her glass is nearly half full, and then eats everything. She even drags the fork around the plate's edge. "That was lovely." She has refused his food before, the salad only, the untouched stew and the sauce-only taste. Now she takes it. I don't know whether it means she's letting him in or something has broken open. It reads like a small yes after the closed bowl last time, and I'd leave it ajar. She stays overnight in his undershirt, without a bag. The blanket, the chill, the hand on his wrist, and "Pace, darling" all feel like intimacy she's allowing herself. The page doesn't tell me she's decided anything.

Friction is light. Randi's "I knew you were different. I wasn't going to wait" lands as tender, but it's slightly on the nose. The chapter is quiet and domestic after a long run of Vee-centered build-up, and I liked the quiet.

The title "Gone" works on a few levels. It's Randi's phrase for Vee, "so gone over you." It's also Randi herself, gone on Vee, and gone in the sense of lost. It doesn't give anything away and it colors the chapter well. The volume and series line, "With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation," keeps nagging at me. Here the spoon is a fork, a feeding scene, and the invitation has become something none of them can manage from a safe distance. No tagline was supplied.

I want Randi to say it out loud, to Pace or to Vee, and I dread her not doing it. A guess from here: this tenderness is the calm before something sharper, since Pace still doesn't see her and Vee doesn't know the three of them are connected.
