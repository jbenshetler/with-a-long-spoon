## Reader reaction

I'm not sure I can call this a good chapter. It's the one I've been waiting for, and it hurt, which seems to be what it was for.

**Vee** is the one I'm with, closer than I've been in a while. The opening holds her outside the door, "she hated that it did," and the warmth she has worked out for herself ("so she would never once be cold under his roof") is turned against her. I ached for her at the line "Words are cheap, and you still won't spend them on me." She doesn't shout it. She hands his sentence back to him, nearly steady. Her speech about the house is the best thing she's said in the book. "Like my body comes with me attached" was already hers, and here it's an accusation. She gets one ugly line and lets it stay ugly: "that he feeds me and we have a lot of sex."

**Pace** I feel torn about, and I think that's the point. He asks "Did something happen?" and sets out the whole good evening, the chicken and the porch and the clear sky. That's heartbreaking, because it's exactly the answer-by-doing she's refusing. "Do any of your friends have a relationship you'd rather have than this one?" is a dodge dressed as honesty, and I wanted to shake him. Then the arms fold, "the still of a man holding a line at cost," and I believe it costs him. I can't tell what the line protects. It could be fear, or a principle, or something older, maybe his family and Daphne. I won't decide. When the cold goes out of him and his hand lifts, "not the hand in place of the word" is the narration naming the trap, and I'm glad Vee goes.

**Swoon and pull:** The only warmth is in the memory of the house, and the chapter uses it against her. The smell, the piano, the two poured glasses, all of it gets through her guard "and made it worse." That is the erotic charge here, wanting that's already known and aching. It peaks at the threshold, in the space of floor she won't cross.

**Trust and suspicion:** Nothing new about Pace's intent, but the text makes me feel the gap. Vee still doesn't know about Randi and the arrangement, and I do. So "What am I to you?" lands doubly. Part of me thinks the honest answer is tangled up with something he can't say to her. That's a guess from here, but the page earned it: he reached for the answer "and not find it anywhere in himself."

**Friction:** The chapter has no Randi at all, and I noticed. Vee came to the house, not to her. A smaller point: the "most important person in my life" line is a real offering that gets steamrolled. I wish she'd stopped on it for a breath. But she says "But what am I?" and I buy that she's too far in to hear it.

**Titles:** "Believe Me" turns on his "Believe what I do." It's his plea and her demand turned inside out. It reads a little like it gives away the argument, but it recolors the title as something he asks and she can't give. The book is "With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception." Here the warm reception is the trap she's leaving. The long-spoon idea, dining at a safe distance from the devil, feels closer than ever, though I don't know what it means yet. The jacket tagline wasn't given to me.

**Next:** I dread and want a reconciliation that skips the word again. I'd like him to say something, even badly. She has a flight in the morning and a suitcase, so there's a separation built in. I guess Randi gets Vee's call or tears next, and that scares me. Heat is low because the want is all held back, but the pull to continue is high.
