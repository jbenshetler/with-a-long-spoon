Honestly, I loved the first two-thirds of this, and the end made my stomach drop in a good way.

**Felt read**

Vee is the center, and I'm with her completely. She dresses and redresses, ties the sash until the knot sits right, and feels "absurdly glad" at the sight of the scarf. Her eagerness is a little painful because Randi is so unhurried. The "Hmm. Hmm." that answers her saved line, *you could watch it go*, got me. "Vee's smile stayed up. It just stopped being attached to anything." Then Randi lays a palm on her chest and says "This did it," and the whole mood reverses. That is Randi's steering in miniature: a small withholding, then a touch that repairs it.

Randi's story about the underwear was the best thing here. It starts as a bright costume-drama bit, and then the brightness leaks out. "They're not mine. They're your color." And then: "My color. She knew my color." The glass rim goes under her thumbnail, the flush climbs her neck, and she says "Nothing" as flat as a dropped card. I ached for her, and the Kentucky-adjacent rawness came back without the accent. The detail that the boy "barely ate" and quit that week is cruel in a quiet way. She recovers within a breath and aims her warmth back at Vee, and I read the recovery as part of the wound.

The erotic charge is strong, and it comes from the telling. Vee recounts the coat and the porch while Randi's eyes go "dark and liquid," her foot slips out of its shoe against Vee's ankle, and Randi asks "How much could he see?" and "Could he see you were ready?" The bathroom stall peaks it. Vee comes on Randi's face and Randi's question, and the line changes under her hands: "*there's no way he couldn't.*" Then the narration insists "that had been about Pace, obviously. All of it." I don't believe that, and I don't think the page does either. The "third restroom" count, including the practice room, is a lovely self-incriminating detail. The scene is hot and a bit sad, because she's lying to herself with such tidy bookkeeping.

"It's a good thing you got relief" made my own face go still along with Vee's. Randi says it pleasantly, "pleasant as a hostess," and I can't tell what she means by it. It could be tease, possession, or hunger. I'm leaving it open. She also flinches when Vee kisses her neck, then covers it with "ticklish... don't you dare weaponize that." I don't buy that as ticklishness, but the page doesn't say what it is, so I'll hold it ajar. The ending, with Vee relieved the closing interrupted her story and planning to "tell it Thursday," reads as dread dressed up as cheer.
