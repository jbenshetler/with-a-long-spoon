## Reader reaction

Vee is the only POV here, and she's circling. I'm with her completely. I feel tender and a little frightened for her. The chapter is almost all rumination, and the ache comes from how well it traps her.

**Felt read**

What got me most was the kisses turning over in her head. "*What if she meant them?*" She goes back through the gearshift, the curb, the foyer, and every one has changed in hindsight. Then she argues herself out of it with "Vee was the one who'd leaned up after her", which is self-blame doing the work of denial. I believe her shame. She'd sooner be the guilty party than risk the idea that Randi wanted it. That's the interior wanting I came for.

The Monday-night bunk scene is where the heat peaks. It's quiet and clothed. Her nipples are "standing up in the dark with nothing on the other side of the cloth now, no one pressed to her to want it gone." She holds her hand flat on her stomach "a lid held down on a pot." Her logic is that it can stay "only wanting" if she doesn't give it the thing that would make it true. I felt that. Her refusal to touch herself is more charged than touching would have been.

The Lifesaver moment works the same way. A fingertip drags across her wrist, "her pulse leaping up under it to be found," and she tells herself "*Static.*" I did notice that Randi's touches are all deniable. The knee against hers stays "the whole hour," and the bite on the neck comes with "See? How do you like it?" Randi seems to enjoy it, and I can't tell whether she's innocent. I'm not suspicious of her yet, just watchful. The line "Having you at the dance was so great. I've wanted to do that for the longest time" could mean a lot of things. Vee's reading of "having" is what makes it land. Randi got the word out and moved on, and the word still reached everything Vee had.

Pace is barely on the page, only reading to her on the couch, and I felt his absence in a specific way. "In his bed there had been him for it, to pour the heat into." The line shows she's been using him as a place to put Randi-wanting, and he hasn't minded because he asked for it. I know things she doesn't, like his arrangement with Randi, and that sits uneasily under her guilt. She's being tender-hearted about a lie she told him, and I don't know what he's holding. I'm not sure he's an innocent here. That's me carrying dread forward, not something this chapter earned.

Friction: the middle runs long. The "dread of the end of the evening" and the "apprehension holding its hand" passages repeat the same beat. Some phrases, like "nothing anyone could have taken to a priest," are sharp, but the walk home mostly circles. She also calls it "the first lie" in the previous chapter and doesn't mention it here, which feels like a small hole.

The ending with Cassie is the cleanest beat. Cassie's question has "a thread of disbelief she didn't trouble to hide," and Vee has no answer. Cassie is the one person who has read this correctly all along, and I trust her more than ever.

On the title, "Coming Due" reads for me as the bill for the weekend arriving, with Marsh's reciprocity lecture ("a gift was a debt") sitting right beside it. Debts and gifts have been building since the dress and the heels. It also puns, and I'm not sure the pun was needed, because the sexual double meaning is already there. The volume title, *A Place at the Table*, now seems to be about the table she can't sit at, the dinner she refuses. The series title, *With a Long Spoon*, I only half understand. It suggests keeping a safe distance from something dangerous, and she's currently failing at that. The book has no tagline in my packet.

I want to know what Brooke's Standards committee does Thursday. "Somebody's been a bad girl" feels loaded, and it pairs with the earlier choose-between-us warning. My guess from here is that the Thursday committee puts pressure on Randi. I also expect Saturday's brunch to be the confrontation, or the next retreat.
