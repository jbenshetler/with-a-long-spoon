## Reader reaction

I'm a little let down, and it's a gentle letdown. This chapter is almost all talk, and it's the first one in a while where nothing is sexual. After the pool of heat across the last several chapters, the hangover morning feels like a deliberate exhale. It's also tender in a way I trust. Pace's care shows in the details: the water glass on a folded paper towel "so it wouldn't mark the wood," the undershirt, the forehead kiss "after he thought she was already gone." Vee's reconstruction of the night, where she realizes how much of it "must have been just him," is the chapter's best swoon for me. I wanted to melt, and I did, a little.

The center is Pace saying "Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else." That hit me. It's quiet and plainly delivered, and he doesn't ask for pity. His explanation (the timer on male friendship, the gap under every ordinary question about Christmas and family) feels earned. It matches what Sheri said and what he told Vee in the quilt chapter. "It isn't tragic... and the good order was the tell" is Vee reading him well. My feeling for Pace deepened, and I ache for him more than I did.

Vee's long interior stretch is where I slow down. She spends a lot of paragraphs building his eighteen-year-old self and the "ugly grown-up word" about Daphne, then withholds the word: "the thing Vee still would not set a word to." I like that she holds both truths at once. But it runs long and explains what the dialogue had already landed, so it reads a bit over-illustrated. The moment in the kitchen worked better. She crosses to him, hugs him the way Sheri did, and doesn't say sorry. I'd have cut more of the reverie to get there sooner.

Vee's own self-deception is the thing I keep watching. She's relieved that Sheri is gay ("I don't have to be jealous"), and she plans to hand Randi "the win with both hands." She's proud, and she's happy with her own telling. But she's walking toward Tuesday with that unfinished kiss and the "enthusiastic" sentence still unspoken to Randi. Pace's "Sheri is my friend" also dodges her question about the guys, and she notices the pause. The ease of the morning feels slightly like cover for what's unsaid on every side. That's my suspicion, and the text earned it only mildly: the pause in him, and Vee's list of things she hasn't told Randi. I don't distrust Pace here. If anything I distrust how easily Vee is lining up the story she'll tell.

Heat is low. There's a faint warmth in the robe and the shirt, but this chapter isn't about wanting. I'm fine with that after chapter 4's explicit scene and the lingering "mouth still empty."
