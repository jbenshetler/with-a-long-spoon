## Reader reaction

Honestly, this one hit me hard. I was swept up first, then sick about it, and the book earned both.

**Felt read**

Vee is the one I feel most for right now. She reaches for "*boyfriend*… the way you reach for the rail on a stair," and I believed the grab. It's a plain, straight word for a girl who is slipping. The walk home is the best passage for me. Her body "had given her away in that room… and it had not taken it back." Then the bare skin under her fingertip, and the thought of what Randi's mouth would do to it, "an offering with the wrong name on it." That landed low in me. She says "I'm just horny" to herself, and the chapter lets me watch her not believe it.

The drive and the doorbell, then the angry sex, are charged and honest. "Not once… had she needed him to drive someone else out of her head" is the line that told me she isn't fooling anyone, herself included.

The long confession scene is the heat peak, and it's very hot. Pace rations her: he lets her down an inch for each truth and stops when she tidies. I felt her shame turn into wanting in real time. "I wanted it" and "I wanted her mouth on me" come out of her only because the denial leaves her nowhere else to go. The structure works, and it's explicit and consensual in feel. She laughs at "that's *cheating*," and she isn't offended. I can still hold some unease. He goes hard off her shame and she's glad of it. He also makes her pay for her own pleasure with the most exposed thing she has, and the page lets her call that right. I'm not sure the page agrees with her.

Pace is warm, but my feeling about him is more tangled now. He seems to want the story more than he wants to comfort her. He never says Randi's name and says "I don't mind." Then he asks "Anything else you want to tell me? About Randi," and "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" I can't tell whether he's fishing, steering, or just kind. The text leaves it open, so I hold it open. I do read a flicker crossing his face when she says "you want Randi." His defensive "You keep saying her name. Not me" is the first time he explains himself, and Vee reads it as sweet. I'm less sure it's innocent.

The lie is the chapter's hinge. "No. Nothing." is her first lie to him. She weighs it as "it wasn't the doing that made you a cheat. It was the keeping of it," and I felt that cold drop with her. The roadhouse-lot memory adds a nice twist: Pace saw a kiss, she just told him nothing happened, and a small bright thrill sits inside the guilt. I find that thrill honest and creepy at once.

Randi is offstage, but she colors everything. I notice Vee never lets herself ask whether the kiss was a performance. Randi's "I almost believed it" is still sitting in me like a splinter. That is the earned suspicion here: Randi's cheerful retreat to the "bit" and Vee's silence about what it cost her.

Friction: a few spots over-explain her state ("the caught part of her and the dying-to-tell part…"). I'd have been fine with less of it. The ending, "*What is wrong with me?*", is a bit on the nose, though it did work on me as ache.

**Titles.** "Boyfriend" is the word she reaches for as a rail and doesn't trust. It's ironic and sad. It also recalls the earlier fight over what to call them, and now she uses the label to defend against a woman. "A Place at the Table" and "With a Long Spoon" suggest a bargain with something that's feeding on her. The long spoon fits: she's eating with two people at arm's length. I have no tagline in the packet.
