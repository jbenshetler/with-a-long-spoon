## Reader reaction

Randi is the one who moved me here. I felt for Vee, but Randi is what the chapter is about. She asks small questions ("Did you take it all?" "Didn't spill a drop?") and Vee's body answers before her mouth can. "You *did* it. And you can't even say it" is delicious and a little merciless. I trust her less than I did, and I don't think the page forced that. She orders "the special" a half-beat before Vee does. She eats half the meat and none of the polenta, and her plate stays "clean" only in the sense that she leaves it. She goes quiet and winds her ponytail around her fingers while Vee describes the wrist and the toes. Then comes the line that landed on me hardest: "I do." Quiet, no smile, "filled to the brim." After the pointing game, the toast and the kisses at the goodbye, I read this as Randi's want being let out for a second and then covered over again.

Vee I love and ache for. She is so happy and so ready to give. She sands the story down for the white cloth, and her body won't let her keep it sanded. Her mouth goes dry and the bit "fell out of her hands" mid-punchline. The line that undid me was "Who would ever actually *want* something that gross?" Then she says "he's enthusiastic" and can't take it back. Her shame is doing what the book has always done with it. She lays the dirty-bright thing on the table, and someone she trusts quietly says it's fine, and then she keeps carrying it. I like that she gets to be wrong and funny and warm all at once. I also like that she doesn't get a verdict. Randi gives none ("Mm," the sound of a woman "finding everything in order"). That absence is why the sentence follows Vee home.

The erotic charge is strong. It peaks in the telling of the wrist and the toes, "He opened me like the orange," and the taste arriving on her tongue in the booth. The heat comes from Vee narrating while Randi listens, still. "I do" is a tiny peak of its own. The kiss on the neck is real swoon. Vee goes back to the spot on purpose, and Randi's body "did a small quick thing, a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long." Then "*Ticklish*," laughed off. I could feel both of them there, and only Vee doesn't know it. The earlier truck scene was more graphic, but this one made me feel more.

Unease I'll name only where the page earned it. Randi says "You picked well" while knowing she picked, and Vee swallows the technicality. That is a small, quiet lie of kindness and it stung. The coat's dark stain also keeps showing up. Vee shuts it "like a drawer closing on a photograph," and that did what the book wants, tying her shame to her bodily truth.

Friction is light. The lecture on grooming, the comb and the cleaner fish is a little on the nose about touch and exchange, and I felt the author lean in. The restaurant business repeats Tuesday on purpose: the same booth, the same wine, the same chairs stacking. That's fine, but I felt the sameness.

Titles: "A Clean Plate" works on first read through Vee's polished-clean plate and Randi's mostly clean one. On second thought it means something sharper. It also means a wiped mouth, "wipe your mouth," and everything the two of them clean up and don't say. The series line, "With a Long Spoon," and "A Place at the Table" read differently now that I've watched two women eat across from each other, one holding the spoon. The book's promises about appetite and about being fed feel less sunny than the jacket-level warmth suggests. I have no tagline to report.

What I want next: the Tuesday brunch or the dinner with Pace and Randi at the table. I guess, from here, that Randi will take the taste and the sentence "he's enthusiastic" somewhere private. I dread Vee not seeing it, and I want Vee to see it.
