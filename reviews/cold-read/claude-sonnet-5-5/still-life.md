Right now I'm swept up in Vee, and under that a low dread. The scene is warm and I'm in it. She goes into him hard on the stoop, and the champagne, the scar she kisses past, and the "pleasure princess" pride all land as pure delight. The painting is the real swoon. It's blindfold-adjacent, with the walnut and parsley as a ritual, the grapevine arriving through touch, and the stray breast she turns into his face. He wears the orange smear on his cheek the whole time, and that detail got me. I root for them completely in that bed.

Pace is at his most tender and most playful. He's asking, waiting, and making "too much" the whole point. Then the gesso. He paints her mons with her own wetness, and she realizes it with shame that turns to pulse. I was fully with it, and it's the hottest beat so far. Then I got uneasy at "He did not kiss her mouth." That is the one flagged omission in a ritual where every color came with a kiss. The chapter teases it, and she wants the taste. Afterward he *does* kiss her, and she searches his mouth and finds only "a trace." Whether he's withholding, or just cleaned her away thoroughly, is left open. I won't fix it either way.

The ending is the real ache. She keeps the photo secret and tells herself she'll never show Randi, "This one was too much." Last chapter she hid the salon from Randi's mouth. Here she hides the best night of her life from the one person she tells everything to, and she can't even imagine Randi's voice reacting. Her imagination hits a wall there, and it reads as desire she won't look at. "She would not be telling her about this" has weight. The earlier "Barely stings" omission by Randi still sits strangely with me. I don't suspect Pace of anything, only Vee's hiding, which the page earned.

Randi isn't present, but her fingerprints are everywhere. Sondra, the wax, the "most expensive thing in the room," and the quoted "Showing him your toes, eh?" Friction: the explicit passages are long, and the middle stretch gets a little inventory-like, brush after brush. The "Keep still" call-and-response repeats. Some lines explain too much, like "The gesso. The primer. The brush thick with what it drew off her. Herself." The pride-in-the-photo beat is nearly a thesis statement. I could also feel the author aiming the reader at the missing kiss.

Titles: "Still Life" is a nice double meaning. It means painting fruit, and "keep still" is the refrain. It also sits against a life that is anything but still. The title doesn't give anything away, and it recolors the scene on a second look. The volume title "A Place at the Table" and the series title "With a Long Spoon" both read here as hunger, feeding, and who gets seated. The long spoon makes me think of eating with something dangerous. The title fruit feeding fits that, and the secret photo suggests a place at the table she's not offering Randi. That's a guess from here.

I expect the next chapter to be Randi, the skirt, and the mixer. I want to see whether Vee lies by omission and whether Randi senses the withheld one. I dread that taste is going to become an event, and I'm very much pulled onward.
