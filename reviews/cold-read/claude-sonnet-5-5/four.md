## Reader reaction

Pace has felt like the book's careful man. This chapter showed me what he doesn't see, or won't, and I was swept up and uneasy together.

**Felt read**

This is Pace's POV, and the chapter belongs to Randi. I was with her the whole way. She narrates Vee into the room: the wet hair, the steel cup, "still full of your come." She makes Pace hard with it. Then she puts herself on her knees at the couch rail, face turned from him, and tells him about the goodbye kiss while he takes her from behind. The mirror gives him her face the whole time, and that detail does real work. The slowing-down questions ("Do you like it?" / "I didn't ask who it was for") are the hottest thing I've read in a while. The wanting is plainly on the page and she keeps swearing it's work. "I'm doing the work," she says, and comes on that line. I'd call it a swoon, and a little heartbreaking. "God, I want her" slipped out into the wood, and her scramble after it ("For us. When she's ready") is the book's central crack made audible.

Randi hurt me more than anyone. The morning scene is the quiet peak. She comes back with Vee's green panties in her fist, kisses him hard, and puts his hand where he's never been allowed. She won't let him do anything and won't take off the shirt ("she would have had to open her hand"). Then she cries. "It's hormones." That is the first time she has let her wanting for Vee run through her own body with the lights on, and she still can't name it. I believe Pace sees only part of it. His reading, that the grief is hers over not having something of Vee's to keep, is tender and probably wrong, or at least not the whole truth. I'd leave that open, and the page does too.

Pace is more complicated for me now. "She consents — every time — or I'm done" is exactly the kind of thing a decent man says. Yet he is sitting on a plan Vee doesn't know about, and he keeps her "Saturdays" and her confidences as raw material. His one honest question, "Is she all right?", got brushed off. "Then I'll be careful with you" is sincere, and sincerity is what makes this hard to watch. He folds the panties into his drawer under the undershirts, squared, as Vee folded them. The last beat is the drawer: he pushes Vee's drawer shut with two fingers and blames the house settling. It's a tidy small image of Pace smoothing over a door that won't latch. I read it as a man not looking, and I'm not sure he's hiding anything.

Vee isn't here, only narrated, and she comes through vivid and loved. Randi telling Pace about Vee's pride, "she put her hands down in her lap like she was hiding something," made me tender toward her and uneasy for her. Vee thinks she's the one chosen. In the room that decides things, she's the subject of the conversation.

Friction: the opening reaches a little for plot recap ("September. Second week of term"). Pace's interior sentence about the lawn, "he had never once pointed at a woman he did not want," edges toward telling me what to think. One small thing: "the white oak under their weight" and "the white gold on her wrist… against the wood," the repeated wood-on-body motif, is nicely handled. I did stumble on "still full of your come" as Randi's line. It lands hot, but it's the rare line where she sounds written rather than spoken.

Titles. "Four" reads as a count: the fourth time he comes, and "That's more than three." It reads too as four people and four weeks, and maybe the fourth thing, the panties. It's oblique, and I like it more for that. The book's title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps pointing at supping with the devil, and this chapter makes Pace and Randi the ones holding the spoons. "A Warm Reception" has turned ironic: the warmth is real, and it is also the welcome. I have no tagline in the packet.

Next: I want Randi to break and say it to someone, and I dread her saying it to Vee, in a booth, with Vee believing it's friendship. Guess from here: the threesome gets close, and it's Pace's morning drawer, not a fight, that matters.
