## Reader reaction

Vee aches for me in this chapter, and I also wanted to shake her a little. She lies awake with the word "right there, simple, wanting out." Then she blurts "Is this what we are now?" at the wrong volume. I felt that hard. "She listened to herself not having whispered" is exactly how a person hears their own bad timing. Pace I trust and like more than ever. He doesn't flinch. He asks "Where do you want to go tonight?" and gets up already glad to solve it. But I know things she doesn't. I know about the arrangement with Randi. I know about the panties in his drawer. So his goodness now has something under it for me. I can't call it hypocrisy, because the page doesn't. It's only that she's asking for a name from a man who is hiding something large from her.

The chili's scene worked on me. His eyes keep slipping to the TV, his hand squares the fork, and a flash of irritation crosses his face. She reads all of it as him not wanting to be seen with her. Later he says rooms like that are "too loud... I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you." That reframes the whole table, and I believed him. It's also the most honest thing he says anywhere. She half-takes it as comfort, but she forgives him for something she invented ("You went cold on me after the Rusty Nail"), and she knows it's false as she says it. That unflattering self-deception is the kind of interior life I read for. "It wasn't the word. It would never be the word" stings.

The sex is the strongest heat. The slow breast worship, "as though he were taking something there for himself," is tender and specific. Then her sharp, startling wish for him to lose control, and him actually losing it. I felt the swing from care to urgency. The tension is that she gets her wish and it still isn't the word. The drowning-the-name-at-the-peak beat, "She came with his name in her mouth and not the other thing," is a good, quiet gut-punch. It does go a bit expository near the end, though. The last page restates "no name" four or five times, and I'd have trusted a leaner version.

Suspicion: I don't suspect Pace of cruelty. The page gives me a man who really can't hear in loud rooms. What I do feel is dread, because "boyfriend" is the very word she can't say to her parents. She's trying to name something that, from where I sit, is partly built on a secret. The pantry-drawer detail and the hairbrush earlier made me think he was making her a home. That makes this worse.

Friction: "A billboard is to a painting" for the nachos is clever, but the whole food-ruined-by-him conceit ("He had ruined it for her") is a little pat. I also notice she pins her uncertainty only on his silence. She, too, hasn't said it, a pattern the last chapter set up.

Title: "Under the Rug" fits what she sweeps down: the name, the fear, and the false forgiveness. It also hints at things hidden in the house that she hasn't seen. Book Two's "A Warm Reception" and "With a Long Spoon" ring ironically now. She's being so warmly received, and the long spoon suggests she's being fed from a distance. No cover tagline was supplied.

I'll keep reading. I want him to say it, and I dread what it will cost when she learns the rest. (Guess: the TV, the hearing detail, and the secret will collide.)
