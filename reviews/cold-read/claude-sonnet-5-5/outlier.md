## Reader reaction

The chapter sets up a classroom group project, and then the floor drops out. The first half is nearly domestic: Cassie flat and certain, Randi with her ease, Vee in the middle "loving them both." Then the point on the graph goes up and to the right, alone in the white, and the tag reads MIRA. I felt the room change before Vee did. The best moment is the withdrawal of motion: "Not a motion — the withdrawal of motion, the sudden absence of all the small unconscious movements a person makes just by sitting in a chair." It reads as hurt that has nowhere to go. "Cheating bastards," ragged and said for the first time, is the first time Randi has come off as a daughter rather than a performer. I felt for her sharply.

Vee is the one I trust most here, and she moved me. She looks at the person, not the page. Her tact is a small act of love: she slides the laptop over and gives Randi "somewhere to put her hands." She decides not to say the name, "then or after." That decision is the chapter's hinge. Vee protects Randi by keeping a secret, and secrecy is what this whole book runs on. Her kind lie sits on top of the others. She also builds a kind story for Randi, "dealt a rotten thing by a man she'd had the luck to be the daughter of." That is the same move as her invented Swiss school. I can see her filling the gaps in Randi with mercy again, and I'm not sure that's safe.

Cassie is the quiet pivot. She can't see the screen, so she takes Randi's two words as agreement and pronounces proof. Her father was laid off, and she says it flat, "no handle left out." The irony is cruel and clean. Randi's family sits on the wrong side of Cassie's whole thesis, and neither of them knows. I feel more for Cassie too, and more worry about what happens when she finds out.

Randi's own fear lands hardest. The money behind her sorority life, the Mercedes, and the Rombauer without a glance at the menu is suddenly visible as something other people paid for. I read her as ashamed, not guilty. I still hold open how much she knew. Her "Her father had taken the loans… kept people on with them" was said with certainty. Her ragged reaction suggests she didn't know the numbers, or hadn't let herself.
