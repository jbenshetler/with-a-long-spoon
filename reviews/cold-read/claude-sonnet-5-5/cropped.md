## Reader reaction

Mostly I ached for Vee, and I was a little frustrated with the prose.

**Felt read.** The Sunday dinner with Cassie is the part that worked best. Vee is exact about her parents: her mother touching her collarbone, her father pricing the dress, "Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." I laughed at the line about weird being worse than wrong, and it felt true. Cassie is her steadiest self here: flat, kind, and not pushing. Her question, "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" is the sharpest in the chapter. Vee's "Both. Probably both." made me trust her more. I felt close to Cassie again, and Vee drifting from her hurt a little.

The porch scene hurt in the way I think it was meant to. Vee builds a sentence to carry "I love you" without breaking her own rule, and the sentence is lovely: "I had been eating crackers my whole life… you fed me a meal." Pace answers "I love how good we are together," and it is about their bodies. I know things Vee doesn't. I know he has a drawer of her photos and a lie running under all of this. So I read his two held breaths and his stopped hand as a man who couldn't say it, and I won't call it more than that. I didn't see coldness. I saw him handling something he can't hold. I don't read him as cruel, but I feel the cost landing on her. She keeps saying "received warmly" to herself like evidence she doesn't trust, and that is the sorrow in it.

The chapter's weak patch is the laundry room. The porch scene's time and place are loose. It sits oddly with dinner at Sunday's end, and I was briefly unsure when it happened. The prose also turns abstract and repetitive: "She did not know what it was. She did not know yet." "Her body did a small thing it did not have a name for." It tells me the hurt after I had already felt it. The last paragraph says "retired into her body" and "the cracker-and-meal would not return," which is neater than the scene needed. I'd have stopped at the dryer buzzing.

Randi is only present as an absence. Vee told her about the photos and the uncropped picture in an earlier chapter, and now the parents get the cropped one. The title lands on that: the photo cropped at the waist, and the sentence cropped when he answers only the half he can take. I think it works. It doesn't give anything away. It recolors the dress photo and the exchange on the porch together.

The cover board reads "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION." I take "long spoon" as a warning about eating with something dangerous, and "warm reception" as ironic, since Vee is received warmly and still unanswered. No tagline was supplied.

I want the next chapter to cut back to Pace or Randi, because Vee's silence is now loaded. My guess is the gap between what Vee says and what he can say will break open. I dread the moment she finds out about the choosing.
