The chapter is warm, and I mostly gave in to it. Vee doesn't know what I know, and that sits under the whole thing. The title reads as a plain description of the chapter at first. Five nights apart, a drawer of her things in his house, and her saying "Her space." After the last few chapters, the Randi scene and the Daphne door, it recolors. The new ordinary is a life where Vee is happy and every comfort in it is partly staged. The book titles ("With a Long Spoon," "A Warm Reception") point the same way. Warmth is offered, and the spoon is long because the table is dangerous. I'm only following signals here, but the chapter keeps tugging at them.

Vee moved me most. Her deciding to call the five nights "her own doing" while some corner of her wanted him to fight for them is a sharp, true detail. "He never said so. He only opened the door and waited." I know why he doesn't push, and she doesn't. I'm not sure his gentleness isn't also a kind of withholding. The scene in the hall is funny and hot, and I enjoyed it. She hops out of her jeans with "no dignity anywhere," he catches her bra and just holds it, and she says "I'd have burned the house down for him." Her body "published the number" she'd kept private, which I liked. That's the swoon, and the next part is where the chapter earns its keep.

The flannel scene is the center. She washed away his smell, and she's embarrassed to say it. He first takes it as a return and a rejection. She says "I need you to wear it for me," and he puts it on over his own shirt. It's tender, specific and slightly absurd, and I felt it. It does read as the sweetest thing in the book so far. The sweetness also has a flaw, because I know Pace reports Vee to Randi, and I can't now read his "heard exactly how many of her nights" without that knowledge.

The long table in the bedroom is the one detail that chilled me. "The long, unusually proportioned table that stood in the middle of his bedroom." She throws her thong on it and never looks where it landed. I know what's under that table's top. She doesn't, and she's tossing underwear onto it. It's the best use of what I know in the chapter, and the text didn't have to say anything to do it. I still trust Pace's tenderness here as real. I distrust the setup around it. I have no new suspicion of him in this chapter. The dread comes from the last chapters.

The food passage is lovely and slightly long. Tasting every flavor "separately" is the "eating with the sound off" thread from the brunch, and the cotton candy and strawberries line is a bit neat. Erotic charge peaks in the hall undressing and fades into the cooking. The oral and sex are brisk and summarized compared with that hall. Randi's absence made me think of her anyway.

I want more of Vee finding out. I dread it and want it. A guess from here: the flannel and the drawers get weaponized by the truth later, and the drawer Pace gave her reads very differently once she learns who watched her arrive. I'll keep reading.
