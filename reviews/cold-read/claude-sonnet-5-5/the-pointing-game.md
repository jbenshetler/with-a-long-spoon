## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

I'm uneasy, and the page earned it. Pace has gone from tender to something else. In "The Bench" Randi decided in the bathroom that the self he found was "going back behind" the face "not ever." Here she hands him "the day" again, and he reads her slack, contented body as an opening. I was with him for the first stretch. The oral, the slide into her while she's still pulsing, the kiss with her own taste on his mouth: that was warm and specific, and it worked on me. Then "Would you like to taste another woman?" lands, her body answers before she can, and he explicitly "did not take it for her answer." I liked that care. But then he plans the walk, plants the pointing game, and decides the hunger is something he can steer: "He thought it was the best idea he'd had in a month." That line made my stomach dip. He calls it play, but it's a trap he built out of her tenderest tell.

Randi moves me most. Her "Only in a threesome. With you" is a quick, brilliant swerve, and I ached for it. It's the face doing its job again, turning a want she can't survive into a gift for her man. It also feels like the book's real subject. On the walk she goes from "very good sport" to "shopping," and I believed every beat. Her Kentucky vowels slip. Her "No" to the redhead-adjacent types is too fast, and "Maybe" to the beanie girl is real. Then the redhead stops her mid-step and the "Yes" comes out "alone, with nothing around it." That was the swoon of the chapter for me. It's a woman seeing something she wants, with no costume on yet. I wanted her to have it, and I dreaded what it will cost her.

Her approach is also lovely to watch. The collision, the hand lingering half a beat, and the footwork on the brick she didn't decide to do. "Her name is Vee" hit as a named person arriving. I'm holding Vee open: all I know is warm, loose, unpolished, laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten... that she could be seen." That's exactly what Randi can't do, so I read the attraction as partly longing to be that.

Trust and suspicion: I distrust Pace's certainty, not his kindness. He's attentive and he means well, yet he's hunting for "a way for her to say something true" disguised as play, and he picks the redhead himself ("I point. You pick" isn't quite true; his pointing steers). I don't think he's malicious. But the last line is Randi's, "Do you think she'll like the bench?" and it chills me. She's offering Vee up to the bench, to the thing that broke her open, and I can't tell whether that's desire, deflection, or a way to put something between herself and being seen. I'll leave that open.

Erotic charge: the opening bed scene runs hot, the question coming mid-thrust is a real peak, and the walk is a slow-burn sort of heat built on her gaze. Nothing sags. The nature description is a bit padded, and the long cataloguing of each girl's athleisure sometimes slows the charge. "Greek pastel," "the grammar" repeated: a bit much.

Friction: "Tuesday-afternoon current" after "Saturday morning" confuses the timeline. I can't tell how much time has passed since "Standards." Pace's inner sentences sometimes announce a bit too neatly: "And he understood what she had done, because it was beautiful." The dance lead/follow metaphor is vivid but pressed hard.

Titles: "The Pointing Game" gave me the shape in advance, and it carries the menace the chapter has. Pointing is selection, being looked at, and being picked, all things Randi is used to being on the other end of. The book title "A Polite Invitation" and series "With a Long Spoon" (the proverb about supping with the devil) now read darker to me, with Pace as the host and Randi as the guest. I'm not sure who the devil is yet, and I like not knowing. No tagline is shown.

Next: I dread and want the bench scene with Vee. Guess from here: Randi will keep performing while the real want pulls her somewhere she can't dress up, and Pace's gentleness won't be enough, because the thing hurting her is being seen.
