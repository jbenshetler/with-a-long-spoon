## Reader reaction

I'm the reader, so this is my reaction to "Missed a Spot."

I came out of this chapter happy, and the book has me worried about that happiness. Vee is the warmest she has been since the porch. Waking in the big bed is the joy of the first stretch: the bread timed to her waking, "Even his absence had been cooked in advance," and her nakedness turning from terror into play. I laughed at the pleasure slave becoming a "pleasure princess." Pace is still the most attentive man in the book. He says "I got mushrooms," burns the toast, and says "No" when she offers the sweats. He answers her "gross" by easing back and asking nothing. I love that he doesn't push, doesn't sulk and doesn't explain. My feeling for him is tender and a little pained, since I know what he sobbed over alone in the last chapters and she doesn't.

The erotic charge is high. The kitchen tease works best for me: the half-beat clockwork slipping, "Something's burning," the finger wag, the walk down the hall where she can no longer tell the performance from the wanting. The pointing is hot, and "Bad slave" made me grin. The long mouth sequence is sustained and sensual. The breath with no touch, the slurping, and his eyes opening to watch her are where it peaks. It slackens slightly in the last stretch, where the sentences turn into stacked sensory lists.

Then the turn, which is where the chapter earns its weight. The taste of herself reaches her tongue first, with "curious, unforbidden, hers." Then the mother's voice shuts it down: "Ugh — gross… Wrong." What made me ache was her legs locking around him ("Don't you dare stop"), and him reading "which no was which." After that she hunts his mouth for "a faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning." Her tidy explanation, "he must have forgotten to wipe," is the self-deception I'm here for. "Why does he like it so much?" and "how many times, and how carefully" sit under the affection like a minor chord. The page earned that unease. I don't suspect Pace of anything. I suspect her reading, because the book has shown me a wider arrangement she can't see. I also notice she plans to tell Randi "the strange small thing." That seems both natural and unsettling given Randi's role.

Randi is offstage here, but she's everywhere. The coat, the heels and "Randi always did" all run through her, and Vee's trust is total. After the last chapter, where Randi's hand went to her mouth and Brooke said "You need to choose," that trust hurts to read. I want to know what Randi does when Vee brings her this.

Friction: the "pleasure slave" riff is a lot of words for one joke, and "reigned, barely" and "honest tradesman" are a bit cute. Vee has also slipped fully back into cozy domestic bliss. The December question, about what she is to him, is untouched, and the chapter knows it: "Provided for." That gap is the book's real tension.

Titles: "Missed a Spot" is the kitchen joke, the dish she points at. Afterward it recolors as what she's missing: a spot she can't see, a spot in the account. It didn't give anything away. "A Place at the Table," with the jacket's "She thinks she's a guest," is coming true. She is fed, kept and placed, and still unnamed. The series title, "With a Long Spoon," promises someone feeding with distance, and that feels apt after a breakfast made entirely of care he can't say aloud.

Next, as a guess from here: she tells Randi, and Randi's reaction gives something away. I dread Vee's happiness being used.
