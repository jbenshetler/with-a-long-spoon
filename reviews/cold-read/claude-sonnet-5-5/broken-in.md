**The felt read**

I love Vee here and I'm uneasy about her. She makes her own recovery out of an apple, bare legs and cold water, and "the cold went through her like a verdict" is a lovely line. Her walk to the sorority house, "a person carrying something she didn't want to spill," is tender. Last night's cruelty, that she punished him "for being the man she wanted him to be," earned my sympathy, and she's brave to tell it straight. Then she hands the repair to Randi, and that's where I bristle.

Randi is the chapter's real subject, and she's hard to read. The scene is warm. She's sleep-rumpled and barefaced, she takes Vee's hand, and the Edna Mode laugh feels truly shared. I believe her warmth. But everything she does with it steers. "Not today." "You go in there sure." "He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing." She predicts Pace's behavior with a confidence a friend wouldn't have. We know she's his lover, so that confidence reads as inside information. Vee doesn't know, and the page lets it sit there. She also turns Vee's jealousy into proof of love. That's kind, and it also keeps Vee on the hook.

The dressing scene is the heat, and it works on me. Vee stands in her bra in a long skirt, unable to see anything below the hem. She feels only "the cool quick hands," a strap closing at the ankle, and a thumb "pressing once into the soft hollow behind her knee." "Somebody kneeling to *tend* to her" is the best swoon in the chapter. Randi's flicker when she looks at Vee's bare feet is the best hint. The shoe fitting also echoes the last chapter. Randi knelt to buckle the strappy heel there too, and I noticed it. I can feel Randi wanting, and Vee reading it as friendship.

The shoes undercut the swoon. "A structure that took her foot and *held* it… Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it." That could be a gorgeous line about surrender, or a warning, and the page doesn't say which. Vee also knows what they cost, "decided not to finish the math," and she's already carrying credit-card debt. "Evil Queen" and "stepsisters" made me flinch. The scarf lesson, hands over hands, "Tension. It has to hold its shape," is intimate and also a handoff. Randi's accent comes up in "bath" and "sleep," Vee files it under Switzerland, and the invented grandmother with the espresso is now a whole story. That's the sharpest dramatic irony here, and it's quiet.

Trust and suspicion: I don't distrust Randi's affection. I distrust where she's pointing it. The advice sounds sensible and shapes Pace's reception. The word "Standards" in her own mouth is a small laugh. Brooke's "folder on her anyway" is a good chill.

Friction: "shoe whore" and the Edna Mode laugh slide by pleasantly, but the pile of consumer detail slows the middle. The wardrobe talk is also a little long. The chapter has no Cassie beyond her alarm, and Vee's skipped class and "ignoring unease" is easy to forget.

Titles: "broken-in" fits the boots and shoes, and Vee's being broken in to Randi's hands and Randi's world. It also recolors last night's fight. I read it with a slight bruise, since "broken in" is also something done to a person. The series, "With a Long Spoon," and the book title "A Warm Reception" now read as pointed. A warm reception is exactly what she gets, from a house that has a folder on her. I take the long spoon as sup-with-the-devil wariness, and Randi's "Evil Queen" joke nudges that. I only half trust that reading. The packet shows no tagline.

Next: I want Vee at Pace's door in the black dress. I dread that Randi has scripted it. I guess, from here, that Pace's reaction to Vee's apology will be gentle and a little puzzled, and that Randi will pull more from Vee afterward. I'm also curious whether Vee will ever learn the two know each other.
