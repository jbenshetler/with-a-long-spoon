## Reader reaction

Vee is the warmest thing in this book so far, and I'm afraid for her. This chapter let me sink into her pleasure, so the fear isn't free. The hunger carried me. She's starving, and she doesn't know she's been starving for longer than since noon. The bread arrives "blistered and steaming," and the bright sour dish "went down into the hollow and the hollow only opened wider." Then she stops watching herself eat, which she says is something "some part of her almost always was" doing. That's a tender image, and the apple scene with Randi is still in my head: thin slices, cut side up, a girl portioning her wants. Here the appetite is let off the leash, and it moved me. I like Vee's small rebellions too, like not hearing her mother in the froyo cup and the borrowed shoes. I also like her stopping the walk with her feet: "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days… But I'm making an exception." That yes is hers. He waits, leaves "the last inch where it was," and doesn't lean.

My feelings about Pace are split, and the page earned that. In his own chapters he's careful and consent-minded. Here, through Vee's eyes, every kindness fits something I've already watched him plan. He told her "Don't answer tonight," and she reads it as respect. He sat her somewhere the staff know him. Vee notes that he always eats with his right hand, "some consistent rule." He asks "May I choose?", and she feels relief at handing the choice across. The chapter's own title is that sentence, and it means something different here than at the bench, where he chose everything for Randi. He ordered food in words she couldn't read, and she was "content for once not to know." I can't call it sinister, because it's so gentle and she's so happy. But I know he pointed her out on a path, so I read the whole evening with a second pair of eyes. He is still the same man who got a girl he knew only from a quad to a table. The unease isn't unearned. Nothing on the page contradicts his kindness, and that's what makes it creep.

The Randi cut-in mattered. Randi sat there at the coffee table gripping her cup, and now she's absent. Vee has no idea. When Cassie said "That's not a boy. That's a man," I felt dread. Cassie's "Goodbye, Vee" from the lecture hall still sits wrong with me, and Cassie is the only one who seems to see Pace's gaze clearly.

Erotic charge is low here: a simmer. His hands, the forearms, the mint in the shared kiss. The slow kiss is sweet more than hot. The wanting is real but gentle, and it works by anticipation, since I know what the bench and the bed hold.

Friction: some narration is a bit knowing. "He'd showered and shaved and dressed and come to get her, and the care ran past anything the room could see" over-tells. The food paragraphs are lovely but long, and a few "she let it not be there" repetitions pile up.

Titles: "May I Choose" is, for me, both his question at the menu and the whole book's question. Who chooses, and who thinks they do? "A Polite Invitation" fits this chapter's manners exactly, and "With a Long Spoon" suddenly sounds like a warning about sitting down to eat with someone. No tagline was supplied, so I have nothing to add there.

Next, I want the call or the dinner Saturday, and I dread the moment Vee learns about Randi, or that she never does. Guess from here: Pace is steering toward the threesome without telling either of them.
