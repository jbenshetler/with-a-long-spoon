**The felt read**

I ached for Vee here, and the ache was specific. The cold room that "did not know her," the gold swimmers, the fabric softener and lavender: all of it is exact. The line that got me was "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone." The chapter is quiet and nearly plotless. It still worked on me because I had just watched Randi's side of the same midnight. I know what Randi was lying on when that text came: a sleeping stranger, a cold she carried in with her, and a deliberately curated fairytale. Vee reads "i'm never coming home" and envies it. She builds a "devastatingly handsome billionaire" and a "weekend so wild" out of the text. The real night was a good sandwich eaten fast. That gap is cruel in a tender way. Each woman is lonely and envies the other's imaginary life. Neither will say the true thing, and Vee's "not the thing… never once let on she'd seen" mirrors Randi's "keep the word under cover."

Vee has moved for me. In "Believe Me" she was hard and brave. Here she is small, and it shows how thoroughly she has put her trust in two people who are each withholding something. I have no suspicion of Vee. The page earns my worry for her. I do feel a prickle about Randi's text, since Vee takes it at face value so completely. That's dramatic irony working, not a new doubt.

Pace is absent, and the absence is the chapter's real subject. Her jealous fantasy is the part that stung and also turned me on a little, in a rueful way. She watches him "at the stove… the garlic going into the oil… the plate set down only once she was aching for it — the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark." That connection of food to sex is the book's best trick, and here it turns on her. The turn is when she replays the sheet-and-fra-diavolo night and the blonde slides into her own seat. "She could not keep her own night." That's the sharpest moment in the chapter. It's jealousy as erasure, and it landed in my gut. Heat is low, but the wanting is vivid: "arms around her, his arms… whose he had them around instead."

Friction: the "stripper's body" self-description comes out harsh, though it plausibly fits her shame. The recap of the bartender's and Randi's reassurances ("leaves you the room") is slightly list-like. Her spiral is a little repetitive, though midnight loops are like that. Calling Gstaad "poisoned" money is knowing, and it stays tied to what she saw in the glass room.

Titles: "Old Acquaintances" is the Auld Lang Syne line, "should old acquaintance be forgot," and it fits midnight exactly. It colors the chapter wryly. The old acquaintances are the room, the girls who stopped texting, and the self she's outgrown. Maybe it also points at Randi and Pace, who are no longer new. I read "Book Two — A Warm Reception" as promising welcome and warmth. Here the reception is the cold room, and I suspect irony is coming. "With a Long Spoon" suggests supping with the devil, and it makes me watch Randi and Pace with more caution than Vee does. The packet gave no tagline.

What I want next: the term starting, Vee and Randi face to face, and whether Vee says anything. I dread Randi's cheerful performance meeting Vee's open hand. A guess from here: Pace is the one who has to break the silence, or he doesn't, and that tells her everything.
