## Reader reaction

Pulled in, and a little stunned by the pivot. The first section is Vee's hug with Randi. She works hard at not remembering the dance floor, then gets Randi's head heavy on her chest and a lime Lifesaver in her palm. The shame-heat of "A girl needs a spanking!" lands as real charge, and it works because Vee tries to file it as friendship. She never opens her own wintergreen roll. Randi feeding her lime is a small, hot, deniable gesture. I'm still with Vee, and I ache a bit at how tidy her story is: "It would be a tragedy… if her sex brain went and wrecked something that good."

Then the book flips, and the chapter earns its name. Randi walks into her own Standards room and the chair is taken. The scene is cold and precise, and I was gripped by the correctness of the machinery. The half-second of quiet is skipped, and the folder tab is handwritten and crooked. Brooke "looked at the page… the tell of a woman who did not quite have it by heart." Randi grading the label while being tried is the best detail in the chapter. I feel for her more than I have so far. Her silence over Kinsey's lie ("the thin truth cost more") is the book's central evasion turned on her. She lets a false account of the bra stand because the true one would show her wanting.

The interior material is the richest I've had from Randi. The August common and the man who "could not be laid a hand on" explain why she hurried. She also regrets taking the first night fast, having watched Pace be unhurried with Vee. Most important, she planned to tell Vee at the mixer, and the real kiss emptied her. Her line "I almost believed it" is now doubled in meaning. I hold one thing ajar: she says she'd give up the house before Vee, "she knew that the way she knew her own name," and then immediately argues it won't come to that. The refusal reads as love, but also as a wager she hasn't priced. Her calm ("It had held for twenty-one years") is both armor and sad.

Trust and suspicion: Brooke is hard to read. Her offer, "just stand up, just briefly," is generous on its face and also a trap. Randi "could not read" her last look. I'm uneasy but not certain of anything. The complaint's "loose. And sloppy" and the "what goes around" gossip about Lauren Pike earn some sympathy for Brooke being cornered by her own rules, but I don't trust her warmth.

Friction: the rule-machine passages run slightly long and explain themselves ("Correct was the whole of what it was for"). The sentence structure in the middle stumbles in places ("Randi couldn't say where the sorority ended and the girl began in Brooke"). The lipstick on the carpet is a touch symbolic, though I like her leaving it there.

Erotic charge peaks in the first scene, and again in the memory of the kiss, which is only a flicker. The Standards section is cold by design, though shame-and-exposure runs through it. The dread is that Randi has now bought Thursdays and Vee's ignorance together.

Titles: "Inversion" fits well. Randi is judged from the chair she's always judged from, and the Vee-as-seducer and Vee-as-desired positions flip too. "A Proper Serving" reads differently now, as justice dished out and as the portion Randi rations. I'd guess from here that Vee will soon notice the changed Thursdays and that Brooke will move on Vee.
