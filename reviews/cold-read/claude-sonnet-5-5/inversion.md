## Reader reaction

Honestly, this one split me. The first half is a small, aching Vee scene, and the second is a cold procedural. After four chapters of Vee drowning in her own wanting, I got Randi's POV and it hit hard.

**Felt read.** The Vee opening is tight and charged. Randi presses a lime Lifesaver into her palm and sends her straight back to the dance floor. Randi's head rests on Vee's chest in the hug. Then "A girl needs a spanking!" and Vee's body runs the whole kitchen, bet and "did you deserve it" in the time it takes to laugh. The self-deception is fully on display. She calls the dance floor "Randi's joke" and herself the one who "took it too far," and she does it in the same breath as noticing the fit of Randi's body. She keeps the untouched wintergreen in her pocket. That small detail landed with a pang for me, because it says Randi stopped needing her ritual.

Then the switch to Randi, and my feeling for her moved a lot. She has been warm, manipulative, jealous and polished. Here she is stripped. She sits in the chair "pulled out from the wall with nothing beside it" and grades Brooke's crooked handwritten tab even while being undone. The Kinsey and Sloane testimony is excruciating. She lets the bra and up-the-skirt lie stand because "the thin truth cost more." "The wanting was the real crime. Just not the one they would name." That line got me. The memory of the August common and Pace walking in "wearing the wrong everything" was lovely too. Her regret over taking him fast and not letting the want ache is a new, tender note. She is still guarded and still lying, but I ache for her now. I trust her no more than before, since she is still hiding her love from Vee and was steering things, but she is human to me in a way she wasn't.

I read Randi's inner life as ajar where it should be. She thinks she'll tell Vee, then "Not my thing" kills it. She picks Vee over the house "the way she knew her own name." But she also tells herself it's only Thursdays that she loses, and I don't fully believe that arithmetic. It sounds like someone talking herself into it. I'm not sure Brooke's "I can't read it" look is a verdict either. I'd call it unease Brooke has earned, not a plot.

**Friction.** The Standards procedure runs long and somewhat static. I wanted it to burn hotter than it did. The cold-room detail is good, but the "machine, a system, something older and bigger than Randi" paragraph tells me what the scene already shows. One thing confused me. Randi says she'd lose only Thursdays, yet the "outside things" demand covers seeing Vee at all. Was I supposed to catch her fooling herself? I think so, but it's quiet.

**Erotic charge.** It peaks in the first section, mostly through restraint: the lime, the chest, the spanking joke. Randi's half is wanting without touch, a different, sadder heat. It goes cold and clinical in the middle.

**Titles.** "Inversion" fits well. The Standards chair becomes the accused seat, and Vee, the one who was always chosen, becomes the unwitting center of someone else's wanting. The book is "With a Long Spoon · Book Three — A Place at the Table." That feels right now. A table and a seat, "the one open chair," and "dinners gone" all point at who gets to sit where. The long spoon promises supping with something dangerous. No tagline was shown.

**Next.** I dread Brooke's follow-through and the Saturday skirt and mixer plan. I want Randi to tell Vee. My guess is she won't, and that she'll get found out publicly before she speaks.
