## Reader reaction

I felt this as a long exhale after the last few chapters, and it worked on me.

**Characters.** Vee is the warmest she's been, and I love her for it. She trades her Ohio for his small, flat "My father works in a chemical plant." She catches that he "had taken his mother out of the answer" and lets him. She declines to say sorry because "sorry would have asked him to accept comfort for a thing he had not meant to open." Her tact here is real. Pace is at his most human and most disquieting. The hallway desk, the trig, and the paint-and-surface speech made me want him. The Daphne passage turned my stomach. Daphne was nineteen, Pace fifteen, and he says "Nothing happened. Nobody did anything wrong." He "named it luck." Vee holds the word "under her tongue" and I did too. The page doesn't tell me whether he's wrong or merely loyal to the only tenderness he had. It leaves that open, and I'll leave it open. His defending it "and he did not hear himself doing it" hurt. It also recolors his obsessive consent talk: maybe he's building the door Daphne never built.

**Swoon.** The quilt, the black glass, his palm on her breast "without pressing, without asking" all landed. The best moment was "That's how you are with me… It's the lift. It's the same face." Then his hand came to her hip and said everything. The scar beat ("Not tonight," with "a door with a time on it that wasn't now") is the book's trust in miniature. Her whispered "Peter," "the way you say a word to keep it," moved me.

**Suspicion and dread.** The dread comes from the closing paragraphs. Vee's gratitude is complete and wrong. "She had stumbled into her" and "a man had crossed a coffee shop for it" are the exact collision and substitution I watched being staged. I'm shown a woman calling herself fortunate "for no reason she had done anything to deserve." I read that as devastating dramatic irony, not authorial thumb-pressing. The text earns my unease about Pace and Randi's design. It doesn't earn any about Vee. I also notice that Pace's talk of fitting a room "brighter" and of Vee's shame sits oddly beside his own Daphne blind spot.

**Erotic charge.** Low. It's a simmer, with warm skin, the robe, the hand on her breast, and the scar under both palms. The charge is intimacy, and it's tender, not hot. Nothing goes clinical, but nothing peaks.

**Friction.** The Ohio and casserole bit is a little long and cute ("old enough to vote"). The math monologue is lovely but a bit lecture-shaped. "Twenty-two" and the prodigy backstory arrive neatly. The closing recap of "six weeks" is slightly on the nose. Small quibble: "tea gone cool" shows up more than once.

**Titles.** "Rock" gave nothing away beforehand. Now it could be the hard fact under his life (the squat bar, Newton, the proven shape), or the ground she's standing on and thinks is solid. The "Rock" on its own is oblique. The series "With a Long Spoon" (the old line about supping with the devil) and "A Polite Invitation" now feel pointed. The invitation was polite, and she's being fed on schedule. There's no cover tagline in the packet.
