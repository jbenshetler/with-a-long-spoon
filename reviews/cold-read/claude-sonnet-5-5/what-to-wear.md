## Reader reaction

The chapter is warm and funny, and I enjoyed it. It's a low-stakes breather, though, and I knew things Vee doesn't.

**Vee.** I'm fond of her here. Her laugh makes the next table "want in," and she steals Kayla's fries. She tells the shirt story with real showmanship. What got me is what she edits out. In her telling the truck is all shivering and sirens. The shirt coming off and Pace's hand on her belly aren't there. She keeps those, the way Pace keeps what she gave him. I liked that she's protecting them. Her mother's voice is absent this whole chapter, which feels like a change. "Once. One time, the whole time" is a lovely, unembarrassed ache. The line about being undone by "a man asking me what I want for dinner" is the best thing in the chapter.

**Pace and Randi.** Pace is only reported here, but it stung that Vee's pride in him ("powerlifter. An actual one") is built on a man who is also, offstage, telling Randi how Vee smells. The last chapter was Randi riding him through Vee's gardenia and "her stomach is soft." Then she left. Now this innocent table is delighted that he "asks me first." I feel dread, not suspicion of Vee. The text invites his house at two o'clock, and I can't tell whose invitation it is. Randi told him "I want us," and she bolted afterward. Kayla's "He wants you for the afternoon" lands with a chill I'd call earned. The page doesn't say who is steering.

**Kayla and Meg.** Kayla is a delight, all curls and ransom-note outrage. Meg is dry and level, and her "Sometimes they even are [different]" is the only caution in the room. Vee lets it "go by," and "it didn't catch on anything." I noticed Cassie is missing. Vee's people are now the athletic-dorm table. This follows the quiet drift away from Cassie. I felt a small sorrow about it.

**Heat.** It's low, mostly anticipation. "Her heart was doing something quick and light and stupid" has a flutter in it. The charge comes from the unexplained door and from what I know is behind it. I wanted more body than gossip. The retelling is more charming than hot.

**Friction.** "Twelve days" is cute but slightly arranged. Vee says the one kiss was "a couple weeks ago," then twelve days, which is fine. "I told him flat out I don't kiss a man I've known two days" conflicts a little with the earlier kiss logic, though I may be misremembering it. The Kayla and Meg scene is standard dorm-girl banter, a bit stock. The ending also over-explains by having Kayla spell out "He wants you for the afternoon."

**Titles.** "What to Wear" is the practical question Vee can't answer, but it also recalls the russet dress she can't afford and the black dress that's "good enough." I suspect clothes are about to matter. It also hints at what's expected of her. It doesn't give anything away. The book title *With a Long Spoon* (the saying is about supping with the devil) and "A Polite Invitation" fit this chapter well. This is the invitation, and it's polite to the point of saying nothing. The ransom-note joke is almost the series' tagline. No cover tagline appeared in my packet.

**Next.** Guess from here: the Saturday house visit is where the bench and Randi come in. I'm braced for Vee walking in tender and trusting. I want her to get her yes, not have it arranged. I'm very much pulled forward.
