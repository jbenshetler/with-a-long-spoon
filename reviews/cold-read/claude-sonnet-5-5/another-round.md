## Reader reaction

Sheri was the cold train the chapter spent its first scene building. Then she walked in and wasn't the blonde, and I felt that fall out of me along with Vee.

**Felt read**

I loved this chapter. It's a release. The cold arrives as "a train she'd been told was cancelled," and Vee armors herself against the woman she built: borrowed keyhole shirt, short skirt, the induction heels. Then Sheri turns out to be five foot nothing, bleached and permed, with makeup worn "like she'd dare you to mention it." My laugh went off with Vee's. "The blonde didn't die so much as fail to find anywhere to stand" is a great line. The best beat is Sheri's slow look, which isn't pricing Vee. It's *enjoying* her. That's the first time anyone has looked at Vee that way on the page without wanting something off her.

I like Pace more here. The blush is lovely. "And you get to motorboat them things?" turns him red to the ears, and Vee has never seen it before. The headlights held on Sheri's door until the porch light comes on is a small, quiet tenderness. The donuts and the lock fixed on the morning after are the same man who showed up for Vee. Sheri's account of him is the first outside testimony I've had that his care is not aimed at anyone. "Hands stay where the dance puts them." That reads as true to me. I trust him a little more than I did.

Sheri's coming-out story stopped me. "Whole tablecloth come off the table and nobody caught a dish." Her line about strangers saying it'll be all right ("Easy words, for a hard thing") is the best thing in the chapter. It's a quiet rebuke of Pace's "words are cheap," and it leaves me wondering whether Vee hears that. I notice Sheri half-mentions he hasn't been home since sixteen. That is a new fact about his father, and I'll hold it loosely.

Friction: the Pace-from-Sheri's-mouth rehabilitation is a bit tidy. Everyone Vee meets vouches for him, and I'd like one thing to resist. Sheri's chat about Vee's legs feeling "like Christmas" is also a little broad. But it's warm, so I forgive it.

One thing I can't un-notice: Vee spent a month sure about a rival, and the scene lets her laugh it off. Last chapters had her hiding a story from Randi and a sentence ("he's enthusiastic") riding her home. None of that surfaces here, and I'm aware of the hush. Nobody mentions Randi at all except Randi's heels on Vee's feet. I wonder if the relief is slightly too clean. That's a guess from here, not something the page earned.

Erotic charge is low, but there's warmth and a little jealousy turning into pleasure. The Sheri look carries a faint charge. Nothing explicit, and it didn't need to be.

**Titles.** "Another Round" is the drinks, but it also reads as another round of introductions, with Vee meeting a friend after Pace met hers. It's gentle and slightly ironic against the prizefight image. The book title "A Place at the Table" fits well: Vee has a place at a table and is learning she's allowed one. The series line "With a Long Spoon" still points ahead to caution about supping with something dangerous, and this chapter is the opposite, so I'm keeping the sense of dread loosely held. No tagline in my packet.

**What next.** The Valentine's dance at the hall with Sheri and Vee both there. I expect Vee to get to see the thing Sheri described. I dread Randi and Pace sharing a room again, and what Vee eventually learns about the game.
