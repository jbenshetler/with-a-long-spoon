## Reader reaction

Wow, this one worked on me. It's the first chapter in a while where Vee is simply happy, and I let myself be happy with her. The cabin, the tikka masala carried up a mountain in a thermos, and the dry cider that "should not work" all read as Pace loving her through logistics. "I wanted to please you" melted me. So did her cold hands going under his shirt, and "Not a popsicle."

The key in the oak box is a real gift, and she clearly feels it. I noticed she also waits half a beat for the other word and lets it go again. She's "good, now, at letting it go." That ache is the chapter's undertow. She takes the key as an answer to a question it doesn't quite answer, and I understood her taking it.

The blindfold scene is the heat. He names her body part by part, and each description turns what she's ashamed of into what he wants most: "the spill is the part I want most," "peach gone into milk," the morning walk to the bathroom. "A beautiful body is just a fact… It's whose this one is" is the tenderest thing he's said to her. The flannel over her eyes is his own shirt. The line "the old voice came scrabbling back up… and neither one won" felt true. The consent is clean and constant: "May I cover your eyes?", "Do you want relief?", "Show me." Making her touch herself while he watches, with her hand "fluttering" because there's no body to follow, is hot and exposed in equal measure.

Then the unease, which the page earned. When he asks "What are you thinking about?", "a dark curtain of hair falling forward, and under it a mouth" comes up, and she flees to "You." She says it three times, "you, you, you," and that line is stunning. But it's also a lie by substitution, her second to him. The first was "No. Nothing." Now it's a bigger one, and he's hungry for it. His closing request is that she tell him when she touches herself thinking of him, and she says yes "barely attended." I'm not sure he's naive. He asked about Randi after the mixer and stepped around the name, and the shadow crossing his face at "the birthmark by his ear" is odd. I won't claim to know what he knows.

Friction: a few places lean on the word "power" and on "traitor, truth-teller" a little hard. The sustained naming section runs long, though it earned most of it. The oyster-pale silk she half-recognizes, "felt against her skin somewhere before," is a nudge I can't place. It might be Randi's cashmere or the dress, but I won't guess further. I also doubt that bare gladness. The chapter is lovely, but the book keeps showing me the floor under her is thinner than she thinks.

The title is "Grace." It fits the chapter's gift-giving: the key, the meal, a body forgiven. It also reads as the stretch of grace before a fall. The volume title, "A Place at the Table," pairs with the key and the "house" he offers. "With a Long Spoon" suggests dining with something dangerous, and it makes me watch what's being fed to whom. No tagline was supplied.

I dread Randi's mixer plan, and the secrets Vee is keeping from both of them. I want Pace to finally say the word. Guess from here: the "you" lie will come due.
