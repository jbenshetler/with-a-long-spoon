The chapter works on me, and mostly it makes me uneasy. Vee is easy to love here. She's stubborn with the problem ("I want to do it"). She gives up the full-name bit to get her own name back ("People call me Vee"). She gets a little drunk on being asked a real question. I ache for her a bit because I know what she can't. Pace walks in as a stranger, and I know he's Randi's boyfriend, that Randi pointed him at Vee, and that Randi is sitting right there. Every warm beat has a second floor under it. His "Pardon me" and "May I call you?" land as charm for Vee. For me they land as craft.

Pace is the one who shifted for me. In the earlier chapters his attentiveness read as tender, and the bench scene had real care in it. Here the same attentiveness is aimed at a girl who doesn't know she's being aimed at. The teaching is lovely: he asks what her professor said, he hands the pencil back "at the exact line where she could solve it herself." It's also a technique, and I just watched him use it. He rolls his cuffs, he takes her number, and he doesn't give his own. He nods at Randi "the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt," and I can't tell whether the great-aunt look is acting or something worse. I don't suspect him of cruelty. I suspect him of being very good at this, and not knowing what it costs.

Randi is the quiet ache of the chapter. She's the one who got Vee here, and the page keeps showing her hands doing something her face won't. The cup comes up and goes down with no sip. She asks about Bessel's correction, a question she plainly doesn't need answered, and he answers her "the way you'd answer anybody." Vee reads all of it as boredom. I read it as Randi watching what she built. Her "He wants to see you naked" and "You're not unsure" are her pushing the thing forward while it hurts her. The *Cassie* beats are good too. She's the one person in the room who says the true thing flatly ("He looked at you like he'd forgotten there was anyone else"), and nobody hears what it implies.

She presses her palms to her thighs when he checks his watch. That's wanting that doesn't know its own name yet. It's sweet, and for me it's tilted by what I know of his hands.

And there's Vee standing in for Randi's choice, maybe for Randi herself, since Vee does Randi's name bit and Randi's *beat*. It gave nothing away. It recolors the scene after the fact, which I like. The volume title, "A Polite Invitation," fits the chapter's *May I call you?* and that small politeness feels like a trap with a bow on it. "With a Long Spoon" suggests the old proverb about supping with the devil. A spoon is also what Vee holds at the end, in the froyo scene. So I'm braced, with no evidence of a devil yet. There's no tagline in the packet.

I want to keep going. I dread Vee learning. I'm guessing Randi will get hurt by being right about what she set in motion.
