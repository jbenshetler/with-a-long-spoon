## Reader reaction

The chapter opened a door I'd wanted open. We finally get Pace from inside, and he's wrecked. I felt a lot for him, and it was sympathy more than swoon. The diner is lovely: Elvis under the tabletop glass, the waitress saying "The usual?", the griddle as the only sound. Sheri is the best thing in it. She says "Get over yourself" about the philosophy of his food, and then "from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." I trusted her completely. She's the one person who tells him the plain thing. His answer, "I can't," made me want to shake him, and it also hurt.

The back half hit hardest. The whiskey, the phone photos, and the horn record are all handled quietly. Then there's the hangover, with the mop and bucket, the freezer rows of chicken and broccoli, and the "prison food." After that comes the engine on the highway that isn't her, and the sobbing. "He ate anyway" is the right last line. I felt it in my chest. He is grieving in the one language he's got, which is food. When he finally says it to himself, "He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him", it explains what the earlier chapters only showed. I didn't need him to spell it out, and the page does lean on it a bit. It still landed, because the cost is so concrete.

My feeling about him moved a lot. Before, I read him as withholding. Now he's a man who said the word once, lost everything, and decided the word was the danger. "He had taught himself that the words were the difference." That is sympathetic. It also doesn't excuse him: he heard her crackers-and-a-meal speech, felt the true answer rise, and "chose the smaller truth." The page holds both of those and I'm leaving it that way. There's also a smaller uneasy note. He still pictures the three of them at the kitchen table, "nobody hurrying anywhere." Vee doesn't know she's part of that picture, and he never asks whether she'd want it. And the "hormones" explanation for Randi's crying is, for the second time, too easy. He misses her, and he has no idea what he's missing.

Trust and suspicion: I'm not suspicious of Pace's feeling. It reads as real. What the text earned is worry about his blindness, to Randi and to the way Vee is kept out of the plan. Randi is only offstage here, a blanket and a "say something nice to me," but she's there. I feel for her too.

Erotic charge is low. Sheri's "long legs feel like Christmas" banter is bawdy and a little funny, and Pace's face going hot at it is sweet. The real charge is longing, with the porch and the stoop and the first bite reaching her face. That's a simmer and nothing more. It runs on missing, not wanting.

Friction: the brown-haired girl with glasses "further down than he had been in years" is dangled and dropped. That's a deliberate tease, so I'll hold it open. I'd already guessed there was more under Daphne, but I don't know who this girl is. A couple of lines explain him a little too neatly ("Whiskey River was a liar"). The Daphne recap repeats what I already know.

Titles: "The Usual" is the waitress's line, and it also names what he can't have. His usual is Vee on the stoop. It's a quiet, good title, and it gave nothing away. "With a Long Spoon · Book Two — A Warm Reception" now reads differently, because the warm house has been cold and Pace keeps its heat to himself. A long spoon suggests a dangerous meal and a bargain. There was no tagline in my packet. I now suspect the warm reception is the thing at risk.

Next: I badly want them back in a room, and I want him to say it. I dread him holding out and Vee giving up. My guess from here is that the three-way picture comes back before he finds his nerve.
