That ache is real, and so is the tenderness I feel for her.

Randi is where my feelings moved. Until now I read her as warm and in love with Vee, and she still reads that way. But this time I watched her work the table. She asks "Walk me." Randi may be teasing, or hungry, or steering. The text doesn't settle it.

The pastry detail is what unsettled me. Randi takes "one neat bite," says "Delicious," and never touches it again. That matches the food-policing I've seen in her. Her mouth keeps the corner up, "in no hurry to come back down." At the end there's glaze on Vee's lip "from a pastry she had never once touched." So the sweetness came from Randi's mouth, from the kiss that "took a little more." That's a lovely, charged last image. It also says Randi ate her only bite through Vee. It's one of the better closing beats in the book.
