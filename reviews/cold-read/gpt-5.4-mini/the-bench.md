I did feel the author’s hand in the long stretch where Pace carefully interprets her reactions and her “voice,” but the page earns most of it.

The chapter title, “The Bench,” becomes literal and then something more: a surface, a device, a place where she is made to be seen and changed. It also feels like the chapter’s center of gravity, the thing everything else was leading toward. The book title and tagline now read as warning as much as tease: a spoon is for measured feeding, not force; “every yes was freely given” feels newly ominous because this chapter is all about how a yes can still become a trap when someone is very good at guiding it. My guess from here is that what happened on the bench will matter more to her than to him, and that she’s not going to be able to keep pretending it was only a beautiful night.
