## Reader reaction

God, this one worked on me. It's a single scene — two women at brunch — and it might be the most quietly devastating chapter yet precisely *because* nothing violent or overtly manipulative happens on its surface. It's just a girl telling her best friend about a good night and getting talked, one small confession at a time, into aroused helplessness across a tablecloth. And I could feel the whole machine humming under the sunlight.

Let me start with the body, because the chapter is almost entirely body. It's hot — but it's a horrible, exposed heat, not a comforting one. Randi runs Vee up a staircase of confessions ("Walk me," "What was the bet," "How was it settled?") and Vee's arousal climbs each step in the *retelling*, so that by the time she gets to "He checked" and "Not at all" she's pressing her thighs together under the cloth in an eleven-a.m. glass room full of brunchers. The bathroom scene is the peak, and it's the cruelest thing in the book so far: she's soaked, of course, "always," her body "so far out ahead of her permission that it shamed her even here, alone, with no one to give the shame to" — and then, right at the give, *Randi's face arrives behind her eyes*, fond and near, "as though Vee were the best thing in the room," and she cannot come. "She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was, and the two would not hold in one body." She stops one "unfair inch off," curdled into "something low and ashamed and obedient. Frustrated and cowed in the same breath." That's the whole chapter in a sentence. Denied by an internalized witness she installed herself.

And then the title lands like a trap door. **"Sticky fingers?"** I actually flinched. Randi says it, then immediately tips her head at the pastries — "sticky fingers" from the *glaze* — and Vee "could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." That ambiguity is the engine of my dread. Because *I* am fairly sure. Randi knew. The whole brunch was a build; she paced it, she goaded it ("Walk me"), she delivered the Radford-boy story — "panties to the side, knees wide, doing a private dance" — as the deliberate final push that sent Vee to the bathroom; and then "Do what you need to," light, "no curiosity anywhere in it." That's not innocence. That's the operator watching the machine finish.

The last image is the kill. **The pastry she never touched.** Randi took "one neat bite," said "Delicious" in a way that "closed the subject," and never touched it again. Vee ate hers in a blur she doesn't remember. And on the sidewalk, tongue going to her lip "without being told," there's "a thread of sweetness, sugar and butter and something warm under it, the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." From the title, **One Bite** — that's Randi. One neat bite and set it down. She barely tastes it; she's not even hungry. Vee is the one left with sugar on her mouth she can't account for, wanting, blurred, consumed. The controlled operator takes a single bite for form and leaves the plate; the mark eats without knowing she ate. It recolors the whole book's warmth-as-medium into something predatory that doesn't even need appetite to feed.

My feeling about **Randi** moved hard this chapter, and *downward*. Before, she was offstage, coldest, present only in buckled heels. Here she's fully on the page and she is *magnificent* and appalling. Every warm beat is a tool and I watched each one work: "There's my girl," the hand caught and let go, ordering the second mimosa to make them "the same, the same drink, the same easy morning" so "something small in Vee sat up and warmed itself at it." The "vanilla" gambit that provokes Vee into confessing everything — classic. And the closing "You're my best friend in the whole world," which drops Vee's floor out "kindly this time, sweetly, in a direction she had no guard up against at all." That's the masterstroke: after an hour of erotic exposure, the finishing move isn't sex, it's *belonging*. She picks careful, unassuming Vee "out of everyone, for the closest place there was," and the cold morning goes "briefly luminous." I wanted to shout. The intimacy is the hook, and the harvest — because everything Vee just spilled goes to Pace — is disguised as the deepest friendship Vee's ever had.

The goodbye kiss confirmed what the ledger flagged: "her mouth staying on Vee's a beat past what a friend's would, then the small drawing-off, the held half-breath, then her lips once more with a little more in them." The worn-in kiss plus the lighter second touch, exactly as before. Practiced. And Vee's want-for-women, barely defended, is now being actively cultivated.

**Vee** — the protective ache is close to unbearable now. What kills me is that last chapter she was *in command*, running his moves; here she's reduced to a body climbing a wall she doesn't control, unable to even finish alone because a face she loves is watching from inside her skull. Her command was borrowed after all, and it evaporated the second a better operator sat across from her. The line "with no one to give the shame to" is devastating — it pays off the whole "shame needs a witness" thread, except now the witness is *installed*, permanent, unbanishable. She's been colonized. And she still doesn't know Randi and Pace are one hand.

Trust/suspicion: I have zero trust left in Randi and the text fully earned it this chapter — the pacing of the confessions, "no curiosity anywhere in it," the "sticky fingers," the untouched pastry. This isn't me pattern-hunting; it's on the page.

Friction: almost none. This is the tightest chapter in the run. If I reach, "the same drink, the same easy morning, and something small in Vee sat up and warmed itself at it" leans a hair explanatory, but it's Vee's felt naivety, so it earns itself. The Radford-boy story is arguably a shade too perfectly engineered as an arousal-delivery device — a young Robert Redford who dances *and* knows "when to ease off and leave you the room to come after him" — but I think that's the point: it's a *constructed* story, possibly invented on the spot as a tool, and its too-good smoothness is a tell, not a flaw.

The book's title and tagline — **A Polite Invitation**, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — have never been more literal. Vee ordered the mimosa freely (for her *own* budget reasons). She told every story freely, delighting in the telling. She went to the bathroom freely. She ate the pastry — well, she doesn't even remember eating it, which is the tagline's dark edge: the yeses accumulate past where she can track them. **With a Long Spoon** — sup with the devil. The spoon here is the tablecloth, the daylight, the "girls' brunch" frame that keeps it all deniable; distance as the tool that lets Randi feed without ever seeming to reach.

What I want/dread next: I dread the debrief — everything Vee just gave up flowing to Pace, "just for me" meeting this. I want, badly, for Vee to catch the thread — the "sticky fingers," the untouched pastry, the too-smooth story — and I fear she's too far gone; "she could not tell" and "she did not find out" says she's already choosing not to see, the same way she chose not to ask about the scar. *Guess from here:* the two-hand reveal (Randi + Pace as lovers/conspirators) is being loaded; when it drops it'll break Vee on the "best friend in the whole world" line specifically.
