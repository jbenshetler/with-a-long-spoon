## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This chapter sat me down at a bright brunch table and slowly, deliberately cooked me, and I came away as flushed as Vee did. The whole thing is one long escalation across a tablecloth, and it worked on me — the mechanism is the same one Randi always uses, drawing the weekend out of Vee one confession at a time ("He checked," "He made me strip. Slowly."), except this time the chapter made me feel exactly what Vee's body is doing while she talks: the slow give, the pressing of thighs together under the cloth, the arrival "a third time." The erotic charge is almost entirely *re-telling* — nobody touches anybody except a goodbye kiss — and it still ran hot, because the heat is in being drawn open in a too-bright room where there's nowhere to hide a body. That sun-porch glass, "a light that found everything and forgave none of it," is the fitting riser and the lights-on pedestal all over again: worship and exposure, except the worshipper here is Randi.

And here's where my wariness, which the checkpoint had sitting squarely on Randi, got *sharper* and also complicated. Two things I can't un-see. First: Randi's "spicy / vanilla" gambit is nakedly a *technique* — she files him under a verdict, baits Vee into defending him, and Vee spends herself ("He spanked me") before she decides to. I've watched Randi mine Vee for Pace before, but this is the first time I felt the needle go in and *saw the thread pulling*. Second, and worse: Randi's own Radford story. A blond boy, "a young Robert Redford," who "could dance, actually dance," whose phone rings twice with a friend-in-trouble so he has to leave her with "the engine running." That is built, beat for beat, to end with Randi masturbating in a strange bed with her knees wide — a picture dropped into Vee precisely when Vee is already climbing. It's a mirror of the practice-room chair story. I don't believe it's idle. It's a key turned in a lock.

Then the bathroom, which is the ache of the chapter. Vee gets herself nearly there and *stops* — because Randi's face arrives, fond and near, "as though Vee were the best thing in the room," and "she could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was." That undid me. The desire for Randi has finally surfaced in Vee's own body in a way she can't file under "still so full of him," and it's so forbidden to her that it kills the orgasm rather than feed it. "Frustrated and cowed in the same breath." The shame-on-the-same-wire-as-heat thing, which I've watched all book, just found its sharpest point yet, and it's pointed at a *woman*.

"Sticky fingers?" — God. That line put the floor out from under me the way it did under Vee, and the genius-cruelty of the chapter is that it, like Vee, *refuses to let me know* whether Randi saw clean through her or innocently meant the pastry glaze. "She could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one. She sat down. She did not find out." I didn't find out either, and I love that the page held it open instead of winking at me. The final image seals it and chills it at once: the thread of sweetness on Vee's lip from "a pastry she had never once touched." Randi took one bite, set it down, said "Delicious," and closed the subject — and the sweetness ends up in *Vee's* mouth by way of the goodbye kiss. One bite. The title is doing a lot: the apple-bite-and-kiss from the very first chapter, the hook Vee "bit" at the Rusty Nail, and now a taste transferred mouth-to-mouth from a thing Vee swears she didn't touch. It lands as *the lie her body tells* — she didn't touch it and her lip is sweet anyway. I find that genuinely a little sinister and I can't tell if the page earned the shiver or if I'm bringing it; I think the page earned it, through the pastry Randi deliberately didn't finish.

The volume signals — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**, chapter **One Bite** — now feel pointed. "He that sups with the devil needs a long spoon": the reception is warm, the brunch is warm, Randi is warm all the way through, and that's exactly the warning. I didn't read it that cleanly before this chapter; the sweetness-on-the-lip ending made the spoon proverb click.

My feeling about Randi *moved*: from pity-tinged wariness toward something closer to dread. The bench-broken woman is still in there, but this chapter showed me the operator at full craft, aimed at drawing Vee's desire *for Randi* to the surface — and the Redford story makes me suspect she's planting, not just harvesting. Vee I ache for more than ever; she's radiant and starving and being handed exactly the intimacy she can't refuse. Pace is offstage, spoken of fondly, "definitely-not-vanilla" — oddly, keeping him out sharpened how much the real current now runs Vee↔Randi.

Friction: almost none. One faint thumb-feel in Randi's Redford monologue being *so* perfectly engineered ("the engine running and nobody left to drive it") — but it's in-character for her to perform, so I bought it as her craft rather than the author's.

What I want next: for Vee to *find out* — about the door, about the kiss, about being chosen. And I dread it in equal measure, because the sweetness is already in her mouth.
