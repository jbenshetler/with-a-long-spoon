## Reader reaction

Oh, this is a relief and a gut-punch at once. After ch.43–45 — Vee walking out, then two isolated NYE chapters — I finally get *Pace's* head again, and the first thing he does is decide "he would not bring her down today." That opening line landed strange: "he would not bring her down today" — bring *whom* down? Sheri? Vee? It reads at first like he's protecting Sheri from his own darkness, and there's a private "whatever he'd carried in from the truck could keep." That's him referring to the rupture with Vee, I think — the freight he's driving around with. So even alone with a friend, he's *managing*, laundering, deciding what "can keep." That's the man I've come to know: he pre-decides everything.

And then the room does the thing. "the warm griddle-smelling dark," the dark jukebox, "grateful for that silence like a man come out of a crowded room into an empty one." This is the payoff of the Chili's from ch.41 — and the text *explicitly* rhymes them: "Vee had tried once to take him to the other kind — the Chili's out by 460... something in him had come apart very quietly over the hour... every sound in the room arguing with him at once." I've had this behavior for chapters — the loud bright room that costs him, never named — and here it's rendered from *inside*. It didn't feel like over-explaining because it's his felt experience, not a diagnosis. "Here he could have heard her whisper. He wondered whether she'd let him." That ache — he wants to be in a room quiet enough to hear Vee, and she's gone. That got me.

**Sheri.** She's wonderful, and she reorganizes a lot for me. I've been half-suspicious of Sheri for chapters — "another woman he keeps fed," Thanksgiving, "she's alone," Vee's jealousy at the bar. This chapter cashes it: she's a friend, full stop, and the text *earns* it rather than asserting it. "She didn't want him, she wasn't impressed by him... she just liked him and gave him hell." The detail that undoes my suspicion cleanly is her own heartbreak — Paige, the design-school junior, birds, "seven at the table, everybody hollering." Sheri's estranged from her people, and Pace's "I don't go back" clicks against hers: two people who won't go home. "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice." That's Pace being genuinely kind and it's about *both* of them. I trust Sheri completely, and I trust that trust because she's the one person who talks *to* him instead of wanting something off him.

Which is exactly why the middle of the chapter is devastating. Sheri is the reader's proxy. She asks the question I've been screaming: "Is that so hard?... So say it." And Pace does the brick-laying — "every brick was true and the true things were supposed to come to something" — and Sheri cuts straight through it: "She didn't ask you for reasons... What is it you want." "I want Vee. I miss her." "So say it." "I can't." "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her... from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." **And he doesn't argue.** "There was nothing in it to argue with."

This is the most exposed I've seen him and it *deepens* the mystery instead of solving it. He confirms it's not inability — it's a chosen "I can't" that outranks having her. But *why* is still a sealed box. The chapter is honest that even Pace lays it out as reasons-instead-of-the-word — the same engine as "words are cheap." He does to Sheri, a little, what he does to Vee: answers "the one next to it, the one he had answers to." So the pattern is confirmed as bone-deep, not situational. I still don't know what he's guarding, and the text didn't cheat by telling me.

**Erotic charge:** low and that's right — this is a warm, aching, funny chapter, not a hot one. But there's real heat in *how he talks about Vee*, laundered through Sheri's teasing. "The air's different when she's in the room. It — crackles." "Bet those long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you." His face going hot. And the tenderest tell: "she gets what it's for. The whole idea under it. The philosophy of it." He's most turned on by being *seen* — she reads the intent under his cooking. Which is the cruelest irony, because the whole rupture is that he won't let himself be pinned by the word she needs, yet he prizes her for reading him. Sheri's "The philosophy of your food. Get over yourself" is a perfect deflating laugh, and I loved that it gave him "the first full one of the night."

**Trust/suspicion:** My suspicion this chapter is *reduced*, oddly — Sheri clears, and Pace is rendered sympathetically enough that I ache for him. But my larger dread from the carry-forward is untouched: I still know the threesome engine, I still know Randi's cowardly warmth is aimed at Vee, and none of that is in this room. This chapter is a pocket of genuine warmth — which, per everything I've learned about this book, is often where the cold hides. But I can't manufacture a tell I didn't feel. On the page, this is just two lonely people eating cheeseburgers.

**Friction:** Very little. The Chili's callback is the closest thing to the author's thumb, but it read as *his* memory, not a footnote, so it stayed inside. One tiny beat — "she waved a fry, closing the folder" — pinged against Brooke's "folder" motif; probably just an idiom here, but I noticed the word.

**What I want / dread:** I want Vee to have a Sheri. She has Cassie (absent, badly wanted) — and Sheri is the anti-Randi: the friend who tells you the shitty truth instead of the survivable frame. The structural rhyme is loud now — *Randi produces with words a comfortable frame; Sheri produces the uncomfortable true thing.* Guess from here: this chapter exists to show me what an honest confidante sounds like right before I go back to Vee walking into Randi's dishonest one. I dread the brunch. I also, newly, want to know what Pace *does* with "that feels pretty shitty" — Sheri just told him, plainly, that he's choosing wrong, and he didn't argue. Does that move him toward saying it? Or does he just drive the freight around a little longer?
