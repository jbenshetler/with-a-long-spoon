## Reader reaction

# The Usual — felt read

This is the first time I've been alone in a room with Pace and *liked* him without reservation, and it unsettles me a little how much the chapter earned that. Take him out of the bedroom and the provision-machinery and put him across a Formica table from someone who wants absolutely nothing off him, and he softens into something I can just sit with. The whole opening — the double-wide-turned-diner, the dark jukebox, "grateful for that silence like a man come out of a crowded room into an empty one" — is a direct answer to the Chili's disaster, and the book knows it: it *tells* me it's an answer ("Her voice was down under all of it. He couldn't pull it clear... Here he could have heard her whisper. He wondered whether she'd let him"). That's Pace at his most sympathetic — a man who ruined an ordinary date because his nervous system couldn't survive it, sitting in the one room where he could hear the woman he misses. I felt the ache of that.

**Sheri** is the gift of the chapter. She was a name and a shrug before — "she's alone," the blunt riding friend — and now she's a whole person, and a sharp one. Five feet of motion, too much makeup worn "like she'd dare you to mention it," part-time in school "now that there was nobody behind her writing checks." And then the real thing: she's estranged from her family, and it cost her a girlfriend — Paige, the bird prints, the house of seven hollering over each other that Sheri *liked* and couldn't stay near. "Couldn't get right with me not talking to my people... call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas." That landed hard, and Pace's one gift to her — "People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice" — is the best thing I've seen him do, because for once he's *giving* something without it being a hook. She won't let him: "Don't waste the good stuff on me. Save it for your redhead." I trust Sheri completely. She's the second clean witness in the book after Cassie, except she's a witness who's actually *in* it.

Because — and this is the chapter's quiet detonation for me — **Sheri knows about both women.** "She find out about the brunette, then." She knows Vee (the redhead, the curvy one, "that backside") *and* she met Randi ("that brunette you brought dancing before Thanksgiving — the sorority one"), and she knows Randi is a secret Vee hasn't cracked. But she reads it as ordinary two-timing — a man with a harem of applicants — she has no idea Vee and Randi are entangled with *each other*, no idea there was a game. So now there's a person in the world holding half the secret and misreading it as garden-variety cheating. That tightened something in my chest. The worlds still haven't touched, but the number of people who could accidentally *make* them touch just went up.

And Sheri asks my exact question and gets my exact non-answer. "She wants me to say it." / "Is that so hard?" He won't answer that one — he answers "the one next to it, the one he had answers to," and lays up his bricks: every weekend, nothing else in the room, *I told her she was the most important thing in my life.* "And she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something." That line is devastating from the inside — he genuinely can't see why the ledger of true deeds doesn't add up to the one word. Sheri, God bless her, refuses the bricks: "She didn't ask you for reasons... What is it you want." — "I want Vee. I miss her." — "So say it." — "I can't." — "Then not saying it matters more to you than having her... from this seat, that feels pretty shitty." And **he doesn't argue.** That's the tell. He knows she's right and he still can't. Which means the block isn't stubbornness or philosophy — it's a *can't*, a wall he won't explain even to the one person he can't con. I still read Randi behind it. The word already belongs somewhere it can't be spent on Vee.

**Erotic charge:** low and all secondhand — Sheri doing the leering *for* him. "Bet those long legs feel like Christmas wrapped around you." "The one with the big tits." It's funny more than hot, and it works as comedy — Pace's face going hot, "She sees things nobody else does... the philosophy of it," and Sheri flattening him: "The philosophy of your food. Lord almighty. Get over yourself." I laughed out loud there. It's the most *human* Pace has been, getting mocked and taking it. The chapter isn't trying to be hot and doesn't suffer for it.

**Friction:** almost none. The bricklaying passage risks over-explaining Pace's self-justification, but it's dramatized as *his* compulsion — "he could hear himself laying it up brick on brick and couldn't stop, because every brick was true and the true things were supposed to come to something" — so the over-explaining is characterization, not the author's thumb. If anything nudged me it was the neatness of Sheri arriving as the perfect truth-teller exactly when Pace needed one, but she's grounded enough in her own grief that I forgave it instantly.

**The title.** "The Usual" — the waitress's line, the standing order, "they told her yes like people saying yes to their own names." On its face it's warmth: the comfort of the known, the one place that doesn't come apart on him. Reread against the book, it curls: *the usual* is exactly what Pace has made of provision — a fixed order, a thing arranged in advance so nothing has to be asked. And there's a colder read hiding in Sheri's jab about "your harem applicants": maybe two women, kept in separate rooms, is *his* usual. I can't tell yet whether the title is tender or indicting. Probably both.

**The book's title / tagline** still sits where it sat — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter doesn't touch Vee's yes directly, but it shows me the man who built the trap can't say the one word that would make it not a trap, and doesn't fully know why. That's the engine humming under the diner warmth.

**What I want next:** Vee. We've been away from her POV for a chapter and the break has passed — I need to know if the silence holds or breaks when term starts. *Guess from here:* they reconcile without the word, because he shows up and she can't not let him, and the not-saying keeps festering. And I'm still bracing for the moment Sheri, who now holds half the secret and misreads it, says the wrong true thing in front of the wrong person.
