## Reader reaction

## The felt read

This is the first time I've been inside Pace's head, and it moved me — genuinely, warmly, against my own guard. After a whole book of Pace-as-blank, Pace-as-sealed-door, here he is missing Vee out loud: *"I want Vee. I miss her."* And the thing Vee has been aching over from the outside — the word he won't spend — is rendered here from the inside as something he *can't* spend. Sheri lays the knife right on the table: *"Then not saying it matters more to you than having her,"* and he doesn't argue, *"because there was nothing in it to argue with."* That's the whole withheld-word motif turned around and shown from the other side, and it's the saddest version of it yet. He even *did* say a big thing — *"I told her she was the most important thing in my life… And she looked at me like I'd handed her half of something."* Half of something. That's exactly Vee's "an open hand offered. Not the thing." They are doing the identical thing to each other and neither can close the gap.

So my read on the Pace/Vee question moved hard toward **real limitation**. Not design, not coldness — a man laying up *"brick on brick"* of true reasons *"because every brick was true and the true things were supposed to come to something,"* and they don't come to the word. That anguish reads real to me. And when Sheri asks what Vee's got and he reaches for it — *"The air's different when she's in the room. It — crackles,"* and then the tender one, *"She sees things nobody else does… she gets what it's for. The whole idea under it"* — I believed him. He loves her *mind*, her palate, the thing Vee herself named as the loneliest true thing about her. That's the warmest Pace has ever read.

**And yet.** The chapter opens on a note I can't unhear: *"he would not bring her down today… he had long practice at deciding a thing could keep."* Whatever he carried in from the truck could bring Sheri down, and he's *choosing* to hold it — a cool competence at keeping things in reserve, even with the one friend who wants nothing off him. And Sheri, a neutral mouth, confirms flat-out that he's got two women and Vee doesn't know: *"She find out about the brunette, then?" "No. Not like that."* So the tenderness and the two-timing sit on the table together. I'm not going to let either one erase the other. He can miss Vee this much and still be keeping Randi in the dark. Both are true.

**Sheri** is a delight and I trust her completely — she's the un-currency friend, five feet of country dry-wit who *"didn't want him, wasn't impressed by him, and she'd say so to his face."* She's the truth-teller: *"Don't bullshit me, Pace,"* *"from this seat, that feels pretty shitty."* And she's got her own grief that rhymes the whole chapter — estranged from her people by choice-and-wound, dumped by Paige over exactly that: *"call your mama, Sheri, it's Christmas… Ironic. Isn't it."* The "I'll Be Home for Christmas" on the jukebox pressing on both of them, and *"People who've never lost anything think grief is a choice"* — a genuinely good line that earns itself. Two estranged-from-home people in a warm booth while the song plays. It answers Vee's Ohio chapter beautifully.

Here's the thing that clicked with a chill: Sheri saw Vee *"at the bar before she started throwing things,"* and Sheri is bleached-blonde. I've been carrying an open question about a "blonde at the bar" who once told Vee something true about Pace — and this looks like her. If Sheri is that blonde, then whatever "poison" reached Vee came through Pace's own no-agenda friend. I'm holding that as strongly indicated, not certain; the page doesn't spell out what passed between them.

And a small cold beat for **Randi**: she met Sheri once — *"looked at me like I was one more of your harem applicants. Never did learn my name."* Randi cold and dismissive to a working-class woman she read as competition. And to Sheri's outside eye, Randi's just "the sorority one," a fling — *"I have never seen you gone on a girl like you're gone on this redhead."* To Pace, Vee outranks Randi. That genuinely complicates my con-frame: if Randi engineered all this, she's invisible even to Sheri, and Pace's own heart puts Vee first. I'll hold the architecture ajar rather than treat it as settled.

**Erotic charge:** low and warm, comic — Sheri ribbing him about *"those long legs,"* *"that backside,"* *"the one with the big tits,"* Pace's face going hot and going on anyway. Not hot, but it lets his wanting show without a bedroom in sight. Peaks at *"it crackles"* — desire rendered as air.

**Friction:** almost none. *"The philosophy of your food. Lord almighty. Get over yourself"* — Sheri puncturing his self-seriousness — is the funniest and most human the book's been, and it saved the food-philosophy stuff from preciousness by naming it precious. If I reach: *"like people saying yes to their own names"* is lovely but the second image (Elvis/family/car under glass) runs a touch long before it pays off.

**The title — "The Usual":** the waitress's *"The usual?"* — *"there was no question and never had been, and they told her yes like people saying yes to their own names."* The usual is the thing you can say *yes* to without declaring anything — the exact opposite of what Vee is asking Pace for. He can order the usual; he can't say the word. And Sheri's *"What always happens"* is her own usual heartbreak. Refuge in the known versus terror of the declaration — that's the chapter. Innocuous on the surface (a diner order), charged underneath. Good title.

**The book titles:** *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, keep your distance while you eat with danger. Pace suppers with Sheri, no danger there, all safety. *A Polite Invitation* still points somewhere I can't see yet. (No tagline in my memory to lean on.)

**What I want / dread next:** I want the term to start and Vee to get Randi back — and I dread it, because the person she'll pour her Pace-heartbreak into is the one who must never hear it. *Guess from here:* the word Pace can't say is tied to whatever's behind the sealed parents-door, "I don't go back," the scar. And I want to know what he carried in from the truck that could "bring her down" — that reserve note is the one cold thing in a warm chapter.

**How I feel — overall**
Moved, and re-weighed. This chapter did the thing I didn't expect — it made Pace *human* and made me ache for him, a man who misses Vee badly and genuinely cannot say the one word that would keep her, laying up true brick after true brick that never reaches the thing. It's the same disease Vee has, from the other side, and the two of them handing each other "half of something" is heartbreaking. My con-dread loosened: to his own heart Vee comes first, Randi reads as a fling even to a sharp neutral witness, and the coldness looks more like a sealed wound than a scheme. **But** the two-timing is confirmed by a mouth with no reason to lie, and the chapter opens on a cool note — a thing kept in reserve, even with the friend he loves — that I won't let the warmth erase. Sheri I trust and love outright: dry, wounded, no-currency, the truth-teller, and quietly the mirror of the whole book — estranged from home while the homecoming song plays. Warm-and-aching toward Pace now where I was wary; delighted by Sheri; still warm-and-uneasy toward Randi; and the old irony sits quiet — the person Vee will run to is the one who must never hear it.
