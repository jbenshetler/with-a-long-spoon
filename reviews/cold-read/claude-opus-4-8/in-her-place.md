# Cold read — In Her Place

*scene: scenes/in-her-place.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: grace*

## Reader reaction

This is the chapter I've been dreading since the first hint of a machine, and it arrived by doing the one thing I didn't see coming: it left Vee's head. Every chapter until now has been hers. This one is **Pace's** — and the very first thing his POV hands me, no coyness, no deniability, is the sheets. *"The white was his and Randi's — the dry citrus she wore still in it."* There it is, confirmed from the inside: Pace and Randi share that bed. The thing I've carried as suspicion for the whole book is now just a fact I'm standing inside, and it's worse to know it from within his tenderness than it would have been to catch them.

And then the substitution, which is one of the cruelest, most beautifully executed scenes I've read in this book. He strips the white, makes the bed up in *the green — the sheets Vee had given him, her color and no one else's* — for an afternoon with "a girl he cherished." He locks in on the surprise, waits for heels that don't come, opens the door on **Randi** laid out on Vee's green "like something off a calendar." His body drinks her in before his mind can object. And I did too, honestly — the writing of her is gorgeous and I was swept before the cold came. Then the cold comes to him and to me at once: *this is Vee's bed, Vee's afternoon, Vee's place.* The title lands right there. Randi came to be **put in her place** — and to put herself **in Vee's place** — and to be **made to pay** for doing it. Pace reads it exactly: *"she hadn't come to be serviced. She'd come to be made to pay for it."*

What this chapter does to **Randi** is enormous. The suspicion I've held is still all true — she got Vee drunk on "four mimosas" and drove her home unconscious specifically to clear the field, engineered the whole afternoon, invented the clock and the racing hour to torment him — and yet I have never felt her so much. The jealousy is naked: *"Is she better. Tell me she's better than me."* And the crack I flagged chapters ago — *what does she do with a success that hurts?* — cracks open here, literally, into sobbing that *"wasn't really about anything he'd done to her body… only the door she'd gone through to reach it."* She built a punishment scene to have somewhere to put the pain of Vee taking (or being) her place. The bracelet named Love — I'm now almost certain it's for him, and it's a locked, toolless, tool-required thing, which is exactly what this looks like: love she can't get off. I ache for her and I'm frightened of her in the same breath, and the two-valence holds perfectly — the smile that comes up "soft and almost tender" when she hears Vee's voice on the phone is *real*, right next to what she just did to her.

Pace's line — *"She's perfect. For your first woman."* — is the coldest, most precisely engineered thing in the chapter, and it's what makes Randi come. It reduces Vee to a phase, an experience Randi is *having* rather than a rival, and it also drops something new on me: is Vee **Randi's first woman**? That recolors the mixer kiss she filed as "a show." I'll hold it ajar — it might be canon, it might be a cruelty tuned to land. Either way, he plays her body with a diagnostic exactness that unnerves me *because* it's the same reading-the-present-tense attention he gives Vee.

And here's the turn I didn't expect: **Pace is managed too.** He tries to send her away — *"You need to leave. Vee's coming"* — worries the affair will take Vee's friendship down. He's not the sole puppetmaster I half-pictured; Randi out-plays him with the invented hour and the drugged girl asleep across town. He's culpable and out-maneuvered at once, and that makes him more human and more damning together.

The ending is a gut-punch built entirely out of a laundry machine. The chapter *opens* with him hiding the white (Randi's) to make room for the green (Vee's); it *closes* with him stripping the green — the night, the scent, "all of it" — so *"there was nothing left in the house for Vee at all, clean long before she ever reached the door."* The bookend is the machine itself: the apparatus that erases traces so the beloved walks into a clean room and never knows. And Randi does it to herself first, going down under him on the phone to *clean herself off him with her mouth* — "putting herself under the other girl, handing him back to her washed" — while Vee's voice says *"I don't know what I'd do without her."* Both at once, and Pace *"held the phone away from his mouth and breathed very carefully."* That's the whole book in one image.

Swoon and dread are the same substance here and I couldn't pull them apart. The erotic charge is very high and sustained — the clamps, the reading of fear and want "braided so close," the spanking that walks lower and lower and then *goes the other way* — it worked on me even as the frame around it (a girl put to sleep so this could happen) sat like a stone. Nothing felt like the author's thumb; the one place I had to slow down was the texting logistics — I couldn't fully reconstruct whose phone sent "wait till I call," whether Randi spoofed Vee or Pace just assumed — but that fog is arguably Pace's fog, so I let it ride.

The book's frame — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon* — reads darker than ever. You sup with the devil with a long spoon; Randi is the long-spoon guest at Pace's table, and Vee is the dish. "In Her Place" told me nothing before I read it and now won't stop turning over: Vee's place, Randi put in her place, Randi placed beneath Vee.

What I want next: Vee's POV back, and the terror of watching her arrive to "make it up properly" in a house scrubbed clean, thanking Randi. I dread the debrief being *this* — the overdue Pace–Randi reckoning was this whole chapter. And I'm now watching for the one loose thread: Vee told Randi *everything*. That's kindling.
