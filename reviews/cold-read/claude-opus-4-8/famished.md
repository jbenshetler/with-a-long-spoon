Probably I'm over-reading; it reads on the page as pure Vee, buying the color for the color, buying the matching bra because it'd be "silly" to leave it, choosing the frosted plum polish "without thinking too hard about why." On first feel: unbelievably attractive, the competence, the readiness, a man who prepared and then waited on her. On the second beat, knowing what I know: he *staged* the pause. He knew dinner would come after. The clean dry pan is a held breath. It's the pre-warmed house again — conditions arranged in advance, laundered as care. And yet, damn it, it still works as seduction. That's the thing this book keeps doing to me.

"Famished," she says, "and heard it a beat after he did." The title detonating in her own mouth. Good.

**Where it went slack:** almost nowhere, honestly. If anything the debrief-and-eat coda ran a touch long after the emotional peak, but the quiet was the point — "The evening had used her well" — and the "Trade" earned the extra minute. The one line that came closest to the author's thumb was "and never closed her... It was the first time that had been true," repeated twice (once mid-scene, once as the paragraph button). It's telling me the milestone rather than fully trusting me to feel it. But it's a milestone I *did* feel, so I forgave it.

**What I want / dread next:** I want to know what that scar is, and I dread it a little. I want Randi back and I dread her too — the double-press kiss and the withheld secret are unpaid. And I keep waiting for the bench, which was *not* in that bare white room. Tonight was warm and real and I let it be — but the ledger keeps totaling.
