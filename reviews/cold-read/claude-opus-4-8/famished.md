# Cold read (grounded) — Famished

## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This is the one the whole book has been walking toward, and it did not disappoint me — it wrecked me a little, actually, in the good way and then in the uneasy way, and I want to be honest that the good way came first and lasted longest.

Let me start with the body, because that's where this chapter lives. The heat is the highest it's been for Vee — this is the consummation, and it earned every page of restraint that came before it. What got me wasn't the sex acts themselves (though the going-down is graphic and unhurried and good); it was the through-line the whole book has been building: **the shame and the heat running "on the same wire," with no seam.** "She could not find the seam between the heat and the shame because there was no seam, they ran on the same wire, and she had stopped looking for one." That sentence is the thesis of Vee as a person, and to watch her finally *burn through the shame instead of being closed by it* — "the shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her" — I felt that as a genuine release. I've been aching for her since the pool, since the froyo cup getting away from her, since the wet spot on the satin at the fitting. This is the first time her body being loud in front of someone didn't end with her locking a door. "It was the first time that had been true." I believe it, and I'm glad for her, and that gladness is uncomplicated in a way almost nothing else in this book is.

Pace. God, the question. "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" I actually made a sound. It's the exact inversion of every shame she carries — her wetness, the crystal-string thong she's mortified by, all of it reframed by him not as her being *too much* but as **proof being the good thing.** "Her body producing proof that she wanted him, and that being the good thing, the right thing." He keeps doing the one thing that undoes her: meeting the evidence of her appetite and being *glad* of it. When his palm presses the damp satin back against her *before* he's bared her — "so he met it before he had bared her at all" — that's the whole seduction in a gesture. He goes to the thing she's most ashamed of and greets it. I am, as a reader, as disarmed as she is. That's the swoon, full-strength, and I'm not going to pretend I was on guard through it. I wasn't. I was in the bed with them.

And then — the panties. That last movement is where the swoon and the dread finally shook hands, and I sat with it a long time. She goes back for the ruined green satin, folds it "in thirds, the spot centered," and *trades* it to him. "Trade." That's her, spending the power she found on the fitting box, handing him her most-shamed thing as a gift, on purpose, staged, the spot centered so it will be seen. It's erotic and it's tender and it's also — I can't help it — it's her handing herself over, literally, an artifact of her surrender, and the book has been telling me since the jacket that handing herself over freely is *the problem*. "Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private." That privacy of his is the only cold spot in the chapter, and I noticed it. What is he feeling that he turns away with? The book left it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar, but it pricked me. This is the man who is running a game with Randi. A trophy just changed hands, and I don't fully know what it means to him.

Which is the ache under all of it: everything that swept me up in this chapter is real *for Vee* and, I still believe, at least partly real for him — his care reads genuine, the scar on his thigh that she finds and he draws her away from, the way "nothing in how he held her wanted her to be any less than she was." But I know what she doesn't. I know there's a plan. So the more perfectly he meets her, the more the exactness of it looks like *knowing her file* — and I can't tell anymore, and I don't think I'm supposed to be able to tell, where the tenderness stops and the operation begins. The chapter didn't manufacture that dread; the whole book earned it, and this chapter is warm enough that the dread had to sneak in past the warmth. That's the achievement.

The opening domestic stuff — the mall, the green silk-and-bra set bought "for the color," the frosted plum toenails hidden in the ballet flats, the bed filling up with tried-and-discarded shoes — I loved all of it. It's Vee lying to herself in real time, "told herself none of this meant anything in particular," while assembling, garment by garment, the outfit of a woman who has decided to sleep with a man and won't say so even to her own mirror. "Long enough to cover the subject and short enough to keep you reading" — she's *narrating herself as a short story*, and it's charming and a little heartbreaking because she's the only one who doesn't know the ending. The self-deception is rendered so cleanly I never felt the author's thumb; it's all just her.

Randi is not on the page here, and I felt her absence like weather, because the previous chapter ("How It's Done") left me rattled — that kiss in the car, the second press "that had something in it the first one hadn't," the cold thought about who might have *seen*. So coming into a chapter that's all Vee-and-Pace, I kept a small part of me braced for Randi to matter, and she doesn't, and the not-mattering is its own tension. What Vee will *tell* Randi about tonight — and what she'll fold up and keep — is the ghost sitting in the empty side of the booth.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** I have no new suspicion of Vee — she's transparent, hungry, decent, and finally fed. My suspicion of Pace didn't *spike* here so much as go quiet and patient underneath the warmth, which is almost worse. The single earned flicker is that "private" turn of his shoulders at the very end — the page gave me that, I didn't invent it. Everything else about him on the page is care. I hold both.

**Where it went slack:** honestly, nowhere badly. If anything the post-sex fra diavolo scene is deliberately quiet — "she did not feel like talking and he did not appear to feel like talking and this was fine" — and I found the quiet earned rather than flat. The one thing I'll name as friction: "The evening had used her well." I sat on that verb. *Used.* In a book whose jacket promise is literally "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over," a line that says the evening *used* her — even meaning it kindly, sated, spent-in-a-good-way — put a small chill on the afterglow. I don't think it's an accident, and it's exactly the kind of word this book plants innocuously to detonate on reread. It worked on me as a cold thread through warm cloth.

**The title.** "Famished" — it's her own word, said out loud: "Are you hungry?" / "Famished," and she "heard it a beat after he did." The whole book's hunger motif comes to a head in a single deliberately-chosen word, and then the chapter *feeds* her twice — the sex, then the actual meal — so the title reads as fulfillment. But knowing this book's title engine a little from how the others have landed, "famished" is also a warning about the *size* of the appetite that's just been let off its leash. She's been starved so long that being fed once might not read to her as enough — it might read as the first taste. The title illuminates the chapter warmly on first pass and I suspect turns hungry-in-a-scarier-sense on reflection.

**Book and series titles.** *A Polite Invitation* — this chapter is the invitation accepted, the door at the end of the hall she leaned toward last time now walked through. *With a Long Spoon* still reads to me, as it has, like the proverb about supping with the devil: you sit at the table, you eat, but you keep your distance — and Vee has just closed all her distance. The tagline I was given, **"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,"** is *exactly* this chapter, isn't it. Every yes here was hers — she kissed him first, she asked for the bedroom, she pulled him up, she said "No" to being bothered and *made it true*, she traded the panties. Not one thing was taken. And the tagline tells me that's the trap, not the safety. This chapter is the tagline dramatized in full, at full heat, and that's why the warmth and the dread are the same temperature.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here):** I want more of this Vee — undefended, fed, unlocked — and I dread the bill coming due. I expect the next beat is Vee *telling* someone about tonight (Cassie, or worse, Randi), and I dread the Randi version, because the last lunch showed Randi drawing the secret out of her one soft *yeah* at a time and then closing the door at the exact moment of most exposure. I want to know what that "private" thing in Pace's shoulders was, and I half-dread finding out. And somewhere out past all of it sits the thing I know and she doesn't, getting closer.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** Building, and paying off. This is the payoff the "A Round" fitting set up and "How It's Done" complicated; it's the peak the run has been climbing toward. No sag, no repeated beat — it advances the Vee/Pace bond decisively past everything prior, and by keeping Randi entirely offstage it stores tension rather than spending it.
