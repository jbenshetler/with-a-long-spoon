# Felt read

Well. That's the chapter I've been braced for since the truck, and it landed harder and softer than I expected at once.

The thing that got me most isn't the sex itself — though it's the most sustained heat the book has given me — it's the *floozie* voice threaded all the way through it and then, for the first time, failing to close her. That's the whole architecture of Vee's shame paid off in one scene: "she waited for the shame to crest and close her, the way it always had, and it didn't. The shame was still running and she was burning through it." And later, in bed, "the shame was still running and I could not find the top of it and I did not try. It was the first time that had been true." I felt that as a genuine release, not a trick. The book has spent seventeen chapters loading that gun and it fired clean. I was moved.

The green set and the frosted plum — I loved that she bought them "for the color," "reason enough," and painted the toenails "without thinking too hard about why," and we the reader (and she, half-knowing) understand exactly why. That's her one small rebellion (the blue polish, way back) grown up into deliberate seduction she won't quite admit she's staging. The whole dressing sequence — the kitten heels that "said out loud the thing she was trying not to," the retreat to the blouse-and-skirt that "had never let her down" — is her negotiating with her own wanting in real time. "Long enough to cover the subject and short enough to keep you reading" is a lovely nervous line.

**Pace.** My distrust of the machinery is still theoretically there, but this chapter did almost nothing to feed it and a great deal to erode it. The *floozie* she brings; he never sees it, never names anything. His "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" is the fitting scaled up — her body as proof, and the proof being the good thing. That lands as tenderness, not manipulation, in the moment. And then the scar. That surprised me — "surgical and old," inside his thigh near the hip, and he "took her hand and moved it without speaking." That's the first crack of something in *him* he doesn't hand over. She "filed the scar in the place where she kept facts without frames." So did I. That's an open wound in him I now want explained, and it slightly rebalances the power — he's not all control; there's a history he won't touch. Maybe connected to the "small closing" at *parents*? I'm guessing.

**The panty trade at the end** is the chapter's cold-charge, and it's the one place my dread came back. She folds the soaked green panties "the spot centered," "exactly where she'd put it," and offers them: "Trade." She wore the set *for him to have this*. It's her reclaiming the shame from the fitting — she made the wet thing a gift instead of a betrayal, on purpose, in control. That's her power, real and hers. But: he already has her flannel-proxy at her house; now he has a trophy of the wet. And "Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private." The reader who knows about Randi, the two towels, the game — I can't help wondering what that folded green satin *becomes* once she's gone. She thinks she's giving it to a lover. I'm braced for it to be evidence in a collection. That's the tagline doing its work: every yes freely given.

**Erotic charge.** This is the book's peak so far — a 3. The standing oral, the "crystal strings... obvious and excessive," "you were so wet they went in with nothing to stop them," the orgasm breaking over her standing up with his hand taking her weight — sustained and hot and never clinical because the shame-wire runs under all of it. Where a lesser version would go slack, the *floozie*/heat "same wire, no seam" kept it psychological. The post-sex fra diavolo, the not-talking, "the evening had used her well" — that afterglow is earned and I liked being allowed to just sit in it.

**Friction.** Very little. "Like a short story, she told the mirror" — I clocked it as slightly writerly for Vee's interior, but she immediately calls it out herself ("a nervous thing to say to an empty room, kidding herself"), so it's forgiven. That's about it. The prose trusted me.

**Title.** "Famished" — the feeding grammar has run the whole book (froyo, hand-fed dinner, "are you hungry?"), and here it's literally her line — "Famished," said a beat late so she hears her own double meaning. On the page it's innocent (dinner). Underneath, she's the hungry one now, out loud, and he feeds her twice — mouth first, food second. It doesn't give anything away; it crowns the motif. The book title *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" is the proverb sitting under it, and this is the chapter where she sups without any spoon at all, all the way in, no distance kept. The tagline's "trap" is quietest here and loudest in the panty-trade: her most freely-given, most self-authored gift is the one that could be turned into evidence.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the scar explained. I want to know what he does with those panties, and I dread that the reader is about to watch it enter Randi's orbit — the two worlds touching. *Guess from here:* the collision comes through the panties or the flannel or Randi noticing the green, and Randi's leaking jealousy (that second-press kiss) collides with Vee having now actually slept with Pace. I'm also newly wondering if Pace's scar and his closing at *parents* are the same wound.
