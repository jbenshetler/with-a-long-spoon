## Reader reaction

So this is the one where they finally do it — and God, it *worked* on me. This is the warmest, most straightforwardly beautiful chapter in the book so far, and that's exactly what makes it frightening on the back-of-the-neck, because everything I've been carrying about Pace says warmth is the instrument. But I have to be honest: reading it in sequence, in the moment, I mostly just felt it. The eroticism is high and sustained and never once clinical. It peaks for me twice — the slow drag of his palms down her legs ("a man learning ground he meant to come back to"), and then the standing orgasm where "the shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her." That line landed. The whole chapter is organized around one interior event — the shame that always closed her *doesn't*, for the first time — and I felt the release of that as a reader even while a colder part of me thought: this is the exact thing he wanted to give her, the proof-of-safety, and she's now had it in the body.

**Vee.** My love for her sharpened here into something almost unbearable, because she's *happy*, and unashamed, and it's the first time. The detail of her chasing his fading smell in the flannel, having to work to find it now — that's ache. And the getting-dressed sequence is a small masterpiece of her particular self-deception: the kitten heels that "said out loud the thing she was trying not to," the "like a short story" quip to an empty mirror. She's so busy managing what she *appears* to want that she walks straight into wanting. She bought the green set "for the color," painted the frosted plum "without thinking too hard about why," folded it in the drawer "the one thing under all of it she hadn't had to think about." The whole day is her body deciding and her mind narrating around the decision. Classic Vee, and the text lets it breathe without underlining.

**Pace.** He did the thing again — did all the preparation and *stopped*. "He'd done all the preparation and stopped." The pan dry and clean, the mise en place complete, the meal held at the exact edge so he could resume it *after*. He staged the dinner so that the stopping-point was built in, so that the sex happened in the gap he'd carved. And "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" — that's his signature turned inside out: he makes *his own* arousal into a question she has to grant, so that his wanting becomes something she gives permission for, another yes freely given. It's beautiful and it's chilling. He never took; she pulled him up, she got the jeans open, she asked to see the bedroom. Every initiation is hers. That is the taking-by-not-taking, executed perfectly.

Two new things about his *body* though, and these moved me toward him as a person, not just a mechanism. One: he raised his arms so she could pull off the henley — a small submission, letting himself be undressed. Two: the scar, "large, high on the inside of his thigh," that stopped her hand. And at the end, "Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private." Twice now his body has a closed room in it — the face that shut at "parents," now the scar and the private shoulders. I don't know what's in there, but the text is telling me there's a real wound somewhere under the craft, and for the first time I felt Pace might have a cost, a history, something not-tooled.

The ending — "Trade." She folds the panties in thirds, spot centered, *chosen*, and hands them to him, and takes... what? The chapter doesn't say what she gets back. "Trade" implies exchange but he only takes. That's the whole book in a two-line gesture: she offers the evidence of her own wanting, folded and presented like a gift, and he receives it, and something in his shoulders goes private, and neither of them looks at the dropped sheet. She thinks she's giving a saucy present. I think she's handing him a trophy — the physical proof, kept. It rhymes horribly with the made objects that lock "so it stays." She gave him a thing to keep.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new *fact* to distrust — this is a clean, tender, mutual encounter on its own terms, and I want to be fair to that. My dread is entirely imported from what I already know (that he chose her on sight, staged the tutoring, runs Randi). Nothing on *this* page betrays her. That's the awful part: if this were the whole book, it'd be a lovely first-time scene. The suspicion the text itself earns is small and structural — the *held* meal, the "too hard?" question, the kept panties. Those three are the seam showing.

**Friction.** Very little. This is the most controlled chapter yet and I didn't catch the thumb on the scale except maybe "It was the first time that had been true," which is the one place the interiority gets stated flat rather than enacted — but it's earned enough that I forgave it; it's the thesis-line of the chapter and it wanted saying once. The scar arriving and then getting stepped past so fast ("Then he drew her arms up around his neck, and she went") almost frustrated me — I wanted her to *stay* on it — but I understood she was too far gone to stop, and that's true to the moment.

**Title.** "Famished" pays off directly and slyly — it's her word, said a beat too late, "and heard it a beat after he did." On the surface it's just hunger, dinner, appetite. On reread it's her *wanting* finally spoken aloud without the shame closing over it, the whole chapter's engine in one word she can't take back once she's heard herself say it. It also rhymes with the food he *made and held* — she's the famished one; he is the one who feeds on a schedule of his own setting. Good title: innocent on the plate, loaded once you've eaten.

**The book's signals.** *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the purest demonstration of the tagline so far. Every single yes was hers. She kissed him. She asked for the bedroom. She said "No" to being bothered by his hardness and "made herself say it so it was true." She handed him the folded proof. Freely given, all of it. And "sup with a long spoon" — you dine with the devil at arm's length; here she's climbed into his lap. There's no long spoon anywhere in this chapter, and that absence *is* the point.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread the convergence — Randi's kiss and Pace's bed closing into one operation, the two towels. I want to know what the scar is and what went private in his shoulders; that's the first thread that felt like it might be a real wound rather than a tool, and I'm hungry (ha) for it. Guess from here: the kept panties will *return* — shown, referenced, used — the way every made object comes back. And I'm bracing for the moment Vee learns Pace and Randi are the same hand, because right now she's given herself to the sweetest man she's ever met and I'm the only one in the room who knows the tutoring was staged.
