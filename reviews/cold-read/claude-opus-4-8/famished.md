## Reader reaction

So this is it — the first time. And it landed on me hard, exactly the way the book has been promising and threatening it would. This is the scene the whole staircase has been climbing toward, and it earned every riser.

The opening days are lovely and small — her chasing the fading smell of him in the flannel, "fainter than the night before, so that she had to chase it," and then the *floozie* arriving "a beat behind the shame," the heat coming second. That sequencing — nipple, then the word, then the heat — has become the book's signature and it still works on me, because it keeps finding new occasions. Here the occasion is longing, plain longing, and her body outing her in her own dark bedroom with no one to see. That's a new indignity: shame with no audience.

The green silk set she buys "for the color," the frosted plum polish she "chose without thinking too hard about why," the toenails hidden inside the ballet flats — I love how the chapter lets her lie to herself in exactly the register we've learned. "She drove out to his house and told herself none of this meant anything in particular." She dressed for him head to toe, down to the hidden plum toenails, and narrated the whole thing as nothing. I'm completely inside her here.

The dressing-scene — the bed filling up with rejected outfits, the borrowed kitten heels that "said out loud the thing she was trying not to" — is one of the truest things about wanting someone I've read in this book. And "Like a short story, she told the mirror, long enough to cover the subject and short enough to keep you reading." That's her wit, her econ-major dryness, and it's charming precisely because it's a nervous joke to an empty room.

**Pace.** He opens the door in a canvas apron, everything for dinner prepped and *stopped* — "He'd done all the preparation and stopped." The wine already breathing, opened before she arrived. On first read this is a man ready for a lovely evening. And it is that. But the phrase "done all the preparation and stopped" is the kind of thing this book has taught me to feel a half-second chill under, because *preparation* is what he does. Still — in the moment, the tenderness is real. "Would you be bothered if I was too hard?" is a staggering line: it reframes his arousal as a gift to her shame, turns the proof-of-wanting back on itself. And it worked on *me*, not just on her. That's the trap the book keeps laying — I fall for him in the same beat I clock the calculation, and the two don't cancel.

The scar. "surgical and old… She had not known." And crucially: "he took her hand and moved it without speaking… she filed the scar in the place where she kept facts without frames. / Later. Not now." That's a beautiful, dread-loaded beat. A body has a history he won't narrate, and she agrees to not-ask. It joins the wound-at-"parents," the right-hand rule, the two towels — the growing list of things Pace won't explain and she won't press. I don't know what the scar is. I want to. The book has trained me that his silences are load-bearing.

**The heat.** This is the most explicit the book has been and it's very good. It peaks for me twice: first at the standing orgasm — "It broke over her standing up. Her knees gave and his hand took her weight" — and the extraordinary line "the shame ran the whole length of it and never closed her." That's the payoff of every scene where shame *did* close her. The staircase of shame finally tips into liberation, and the book makes me feel the difference. Second at the plainest sentence: "And then he wasn't careful… the shame was still running and she could not find the top of it and she did not try. / It was the first time that had been true." That reads as a genuine threshold crossed, and it moved me. The taste thread pays off explicitly here too — his mouth on her, "tasted her and was glad of it" — which I've been tracking since he planted "the taste of another woman" with Randi way back. He is now tasting the woman. I feel the machinery humming under the tenderness and it does not cool the scene down; it makes it worse in the way this book wants.

**The panties.** The ending detonated the whole chapter for me. She retrieves them, folds them "in thirds, the spot centered," drops the sheet and doesn't pick it up, and says "Trade." She hands him the evidence — the deep green satin "particular and chosen," the wet spot placed exactly — as a gift. "He took them. Something in his shoulders was private and she let it be private." That's her turning her own deepest shame (the wet betrayal, the body announcing its wanting) into an *offering*, a claiming. It's her version of ownership — the book's whole thesis, liberation as ownership of chosen shame, enacted without a word of explanation. I felt proud of her and afraid for her at once. Because she's handing the machine exactly what the machine was built to collect. "The taste of another woman," a folded green trophy with the spot centered — I don't know what he does with it, and that's the dread.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Randi isn't in this chapter at all, and her absence is loud, because I now can't read a Pace/Vee scene without her ghost in it. Pace himself — the suspicion is entirely pattern-based, not moment-based: nothing he does here is unkind, everything is attentive, and *that consistency* is the thing I distrust. "He'd done all the preparation and stopped" and the withheld privacy in his shoulders at the very end are the only two places the text let a cold draft in, and both are faint. The scene is genuinely warm on first read; the cold is only in what I'm carrying.

**Friction.** Very little. The prose is disciplined and doesn't over-explain — the scar beat especially trusts me. The one place I felt the thumb, lightly, was "It was the first time that had been true," an explicit italicized landing that names the threshold the scene had already made me feel. I didn't need it told. But it's a small thing and it's earned.

**The titles.** "Famished" — surface: dinner, are-you-hungry, fra diavolo. Charge: her one-word answer, "Famished," said "a beat after he did," is about hunger for *him*, and the whole chapter is appetite let off its leash — hers finally not closing her down. It's a clean oblique title: innocuous domestic word, detonates into bodily wanting once read. It doesn't telegraph; it names the frame (dinner) and means the other thing. Good.

*With a Long Spoon* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the tagline made flesh. Every step tonight was hers: she bought the set, she painted the toenails, she kissed him first, she pulled the apron strap, she asked to see his bedroom, she said "No" to being bothered and "made herself say it so it was true," she said "Trade." Freely given, all of it, and it's exactly the freedom that walks her deeper in. The long spoon is arm's length, and tonight she closed the distance herself. That's the chill under the warmth.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to see Randi again now — because Vee just had her first night and the confidante track means she'll *retell* it, and Randi will extract and reshape it, and I dread watching that. I especially dread whether the folded green panties, the "Trade," reaches Randi somehow. *Guess from here:* the next Randi scene mines this night, and the jealousy tell from last chapter sharpens now that Pace has actually had Vee. And the scar — I don't expect an answer soon; the book withholds these on purpose. I want to keep reading badly.
