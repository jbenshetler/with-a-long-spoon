# Cold read (grounded) — Rye

## Reader reaction

**Rye** is short, and it's the come-down chapter — Vee back in the cool dorm room, retelling the mountain to Cassie, wearing the flannel three sizes too big. And what strikes me first is how *good* it feels to be back with Cassie. After the long, charged solitude of the truck cab, this room is where Vee gets to be a person talking, and the two of them fall into their rhythm — "No windowless van." / "No van." "I had your blood type ready." — and I loved it. Cassie is the one relationship in this whole book with no undertow, and after a chapter where I was holding my breath watching Vee undress in front of a man she's known three dates, coming into this cool cracked-window room felt like being handed the water bottle. I trust it completely.

What moved me: watching Vee *narrate* the hike back to herself. She says the eating "the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" — and then when she gets to the actual undressing, she goes quiet, "as she'd slowed at the tree the other night," and Cassie goes still with her. That slowing is the tell. She's proud of the mountain ("Me. I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain") in the loud voice, but the shirt she can only get to sideways, turning the cuff in her fingers, not looking up. "And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." That doubled *Me* — half disbelief, half claim — is the whole of her right now, and it broke my heart a little in a tender way, not an alarmed one. She *did* do it herself. I believe her the way I believed the kiss.

And Cassie's response is the thing I'll carry out of this chapter. Not "Oh, Vee" — the narration tells me directly it isn't that — just her name, "like setting something down where it wouldn't break." Then "I know." / "I know." That exchange is the most loving thing anyone's done in the book, because Cassie doesn't warn her. She *sees* it — she's rapt, she's not dry anymore — and she chooses not to break it. "Borrow real boots next time." / "Keep the shirt." That's Cassie loving her by leaving the shirt on her. It undid me a little. It's also, quietly, the one moment my low background dread flickered: Cassie is the clear-eyed one, and even Cassie decided not to say the thing. If the protector stands down, who's left?

But I want to be honest — the page didn't earn fresh *suspicion* here. It earned tenderness. This chapter is almost pure swoon-processing, and the swoon held: the flannel smelling "of soap and of a man and of the inside of a gym bag," the man standing in the rain with his back turned so nobody would look at her. My warmth toward Pace is intact from the last chapter and this only reinforced it, because I'm now hearing it *through Vee's telling* and it survives the retelling, which is a real test. The wariness I carry about him is mine, from the checkpoint — the bench, the game — none of which touched this room. That's what makes it ache: Vee gets to have the purest possible version of the night with the one person who'd protect her, and the protection didn't come.

Erotic charge here is low and that's right — this is the warm bath after. The heat is all recalled, banked. The chapter's job is the friendship and it does it beautifully; no friction, nothing I didn't buy, no thumb on the scale. If anything the restraint of Cassie's "Vee." is a masterclass in *not* over-explaining.

**The titles.** "Rye" — the bread, the horseradish sandwich at the summit, the thing she ate with real hunger and no watching voice. As a chapter title it points at that: the plain dark loaf, appetite met and fed, "you carry out what you carried in." It sits sweet and small over a chapter that's really about what Vee *keeps* — the shirt, the pit-in-the-hand lesson, the memory. It doesn't give anything away; it just names the nourishment. The volume line, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, and the title **Rye** on the cover board — "long spoon" pulls the old saw about supping with the devil needing a long spoon, which sits cold against all this warmth and keeps my dread alive; "A Polite Invitation" fits Pace exactly (the *may I*, the froyo, the hiking-ask) and the politeness is precisely what I can't fully trust, because I know what the invitation is *into*. No tagline was supplied.

**What I want next:** the induction dinner and that russet silk dress she set back on the shelf — I want her to have it, and I suspect (guess from here) someone will put it on her, which will feel like a gift and read to me like the game tightening. And I dread, mildly, the next time Randi and Cassie are in the same frame now that even Cassie has decided to keep the shirt on her.
