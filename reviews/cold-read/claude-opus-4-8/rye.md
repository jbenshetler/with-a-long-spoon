## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is a quiet one — a comedown chapter, a debrief. After the enormous golden-tender-dreadful hike, we come back to the cool room with the window cracked two inches, and Vee tells Cassie the whole thing. And what I mostly felt was: relief that Cassie is still here, still in the frame, still getting the story. After "Dear," where Vee handed the whole confession to Randi and shut Cassie out, this landed almost like a correction — Vee came *home* and told Cassie. That mattered to me more than I expected.

Cassie is the best she's ever been here. The deadpan is doing real love: "I had your blood type." "No windowless van." "I had questions." / "You had a whole witness statement ready." It's funny and it's also the exact opposite of Randi's technique — Cassie doesn't *draw out* the shames and hand them back as gifts, she just sits across the room "warm and dry in her sweats" and lets Vee talk, and when the story turns tender she goes "still and stayed that way," rapt. She doesn't perform listening. She listens. And her one intervention at the end — "Vee." Soft. "Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break" — is the whole chapter. She's worried. She sees exactly what I see: this girl is gone, this is not a crush anymore, this is a girl inside an enormous shirt that isn't hers turning the cuff over in her fingers. And Cassie's love is not to yank her back. It's "Borrow real boots next time." Practical. Present. Non-controlling. I trust her completely and I'm scared for both of them because she's outmatched by machinery she can smell but can't name.

Vee — the ache is total now and I feel it. "She said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" — about the *sandwich* — is a beautiful, aching line, because the food is the intimacy, that's the whole seduction distilled. And "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain. Me." That's the thing that got me most, actually — not the erotic beat, but that Pace (or the machine) has given her a sense of her own body as *capable*, not just shameful, for maybe the first time. Whoever's doing it, that's a real gift landing in a real wound. It's exactly what makes this frightening.

And she notices him, physically, out loud, for the first time to Cassie: "I think he's built. Under the jacket. I think there's a whole situation under there." That made me laugh and then brace, because it's the first time her wanting has turned toward *his* body as an object of appetite rather than only receiving his attention. The current's running both ways in her telling now.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Nothing new to distrust *in the room* — Cassie is clean, Vee is transparent. But the chapter quietly re-rings every alarm I already had, because Vee narrates "There's a leave-nothing thing with him, you carry out what you carried in" as *romance* — and I hear the phrase from the jacket-shaped dread it's been carrying since chapter one. She's repeating his liturgy back as her own wisdom now. The "we didn't leave a pit" is charming and it's also the manipulation's own slogan installed in her mouth. And the irony the chapter itself points at, gently: she keeps saying *leave nothing* while sitting inside the shirt she *took* and *kept* — "I kept the shirt. I didn't ask. He didn't ask for it back." The one trace in a no-trace method. I still can't tell if he *let* her keep it on purpose.

**Erotic charge.** Low and warm — a 1. There's no sex here; the heat is entirely in retelling. But the striptease retold to a friend has its own charge — "I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me. And he looked at me like I'd handed him something." Saying it out loud to Cassie makes it more real, more owned, and that ownership is its own quiet erotic thing. It didn't work on my body so much as on my chest.

**Friction.** Almost none. The one risk is that this chapter is entirely recap — everything in it I watched happen in "Leave No Trace." A lesser version of this would bore me. It didn't, because the *frame* is new: it's about who she tells and how Cassie receives it. But I'll admit a flicker of "I was just here." The chapter earns itself on Cassie, not on plot. If I hadn't loved Cassie already it might have felt like a victory lap.

**The title.** "Rye" — the bread. On the surface, nothing: it's the sandwich. But the chapter puts real weight on it — "Rye bread and this horseradish... I have never tasted anything like that sandwich in my life." The food *is* the love, in this book's whole grammar (froyo, hand-fed dinner, cherries), so titling the debrief after the bread points straight at the mechanism: hunger made first, then met. It also sits oblique and a little cold — such a plain, dry word for such a flooded chapter, which is maybe the point, Cassie's dry register against Vee's flood. It doesn't give anything away. It just quietly insists the sandwich was the seduction.

**The book's title and tagline.** "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is almost a demonstration of it in miniature. Vee keeps stressing *on purpose, me, I chose*: "I knocked on the glass and I called him back in." "I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." Every beat she narrates as *her* agency, her choosing — and that's exactly the thing the tagline told me to distrust. The more freely she gives, the more the trap closes, and she's *proud* of the freeness. That's the cold thread under the warm scene. "With a long spoon" still hums in the background — you sup with the devil with a long spoon — and here she's forgotten the spoon entirely, she's eating from his hand and calling it the best meal of her life.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Randi back on the page — she's been absent and the dread lives in her return, because two of these worlds have to touch. I want to see the house, the bench. I dread the moment Vee learns there was a game, and I especially dread it happening in front of Cassie, or Cassie learning first. Guess from here: the induction in November is the collision point — the russet dress, the mother attending, Randi and Pace both in Vee's orbit. And I keep waiting for Randi and Pace's secret to crack from the *inside* — for one of them to actually be falling, per the jacket — because this chapter, spent entirely away from them, made me realize how much I want to know what *they* are doing while Vee glows.
