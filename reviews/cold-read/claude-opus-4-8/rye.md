## Reader reaction

Oh, I *needed* this. After the wet, aching intimacy of the hike, this lands like the exhale — and it hands me back Cassie, whom I've been mourning as she got "subtracted." Here she is, warm and dry in her sweats, brown shag untangled, doing her dry deadpan thing, and I felt actual relief in my chest. She's alive on the page. She's the one person the machine can't turn, and this whole chapter is Vee bringing the mountain *to* Cassie — which is the opposite of the pattern I've been dreading, where Cassie gets edged out of the frame.

The gag runs so well — "No windowless van." "I had your blood type." "You had a whole witness statement ready." That's love rendered as forensic dread; Cassie loves by cataloguing the ways Vee could come to harm. It's funny and it's also the only cold-eyed intelligence in the book pointed *at* Pace, however lightly. And she keeps it going — "He took you up a mountain nobody can see from the road, on a Sunday, and brought you back. With your shoes on… like she was revising a file." She's literally auditing the safety of it, and — crucially — the audit comes back clean. Which is exactly the thing that keeps me braced: the more Pace passes Cassie's suspicion test, the less anyone will ever catch him.

What moved for me: watching Vee *retell.* This is the first time I've seen her narrate her own scene back, and the tell is how she frames the food — "And then we ate." She said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed.* That gutted me a little. The sandwich is the erotic peak of her whole day and she knows it. It confirms how completely he's colonized her — a rye-and-horseradish sandwich is now the most sensual thing that's ever happened to her ("I have never tasted anything like that sandwich in my life. I think you have to almost die on a mountain first").

Erotic charge here is low and warm, deliberately — it's a recounting, not a scene. Where it flickers is Vee describing his body to Cassie: "he's got these forearms. Cass. I think he's built. Under the jacket. I think there's a whole situation under there." That's the giddy, girlish register I haven't quite heard from her — the shame-watcher off duty, just a girl gushing to her friend. "I'm reporting what I observed" — she's caught Cassie's forensic language and turned it playful. I liked her a lot in this.

The retell of the truck is where Cassie goes still — "Cassie was rapt" — and so was I, again, even knowing the beat. What's interesting is hearing Vee narrate her own authorship out loud: "I knocked on the glass and I called him back in… I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." She's *claiming* it, the way she claimed the kiss in the dorm debrief. That "Me." is her whole arc — the working-class girl who's never gotten anywhere on her own legs, insisting on the one thing she did herself. And she does the same "Me" about the mountain: "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain." The rhyme between those two — the summit and the undressing, both *her* doing — is doing quiet work.

Where I sat up: Cassie's final move. Not the joke, not the file. "'Vee,' Cassie said finally. Soft. Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." And Vee answers "I know." *I know.* That exchange has the whole ambivalence of the book in it — Cassie sees something worth worrying about, says nothing but the name, and Vee already knows and is choosing anyway. That's the truest, most economical version of the Cassandra shape yet: the warning is one syllable, it lands, and it changes nothing. Then Cassie *lets her keep the shirt* — "Keep the shirt." She doesn't fight it. The anchor loves her enough to let go of the rope a little. That's the sad part, buried under the sweetness.

Trust/suspicion: nothing new was *earned* against Pace here — if anything he passes another inspection. My unease is entirely structural and carried-in: the fact that even Cassie's blood-type-level paranoia finds nothing is the quietly chilling note. The one line that pricked me is Cassie's soft "Vee." — the text is inviting me to feel that Cassie senses the undertow, without saying what. That felt earned, not telegraphed.

Friction: almost none. It's a short warm chapter and it knows what it is. If I reach — the danger of a retell chapter is that it re-narrates a scene I just read, and there's a little of that; I'd already lived the moleskin and the flannel. But it's saved by being *Vee's* version, which tells me new things (that the sandwich is the erotic center for her; that she can say "Me" out loud now; that she'll gush like a girl). And it's short enough not to overstay.

What I want next: Cassie won't be able to keep doing this indefinitely — I keep waiting for the moment Vee tells Cassie something Cassie *can't* let slide, and Cassie says more than a name. And I still want the dress, the induction, and above all the house off 614. *Guess from here:* the two tracks — Randi and Pace — are going to touch in Vee's awareness, and Cassie may be the one who accidentally connects them, because she's the only node not inside the machine. I dread the chapter where Vee stops bringing the mountain back to Cassie's room.
