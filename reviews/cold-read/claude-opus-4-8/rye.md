# Cold read — Rye

*scene: scenes/rye.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: leave-no-trace*

## Reader reaction

This is a decompression chapter — the whole hike relayed back through Vee's mouth to Cassie in the cool dorm room — and after the near-unbearable intimacy of the last chapter, I welcomed the air in it. It's short and it's all talk, and it works because I already carry the scene it's describing; watching Vee *retell* it is a different kind of ache.

What lands hardest is the gap between how Vee narrates the strip and what I know it was. To her it's the triumph of her life: "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain," and then, three beats later, "And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." That repeated *Me* — she keeps claiming the agency, keeps pinning the daring on herself, and she's *right*, the invitation did issue from her, and that's exactly the thing I can't celebrate with her the way Cassie almost does. She's proud of the one move that is the con's whole success. "He looked at me like I'd handed him something." Yeah. She handed him something. My chest did the warm-and-sick thing again.

The rye sandwich detail got repurposed beautifully — "I think you have to almost die on a mountain first" — and then "We didn't leave a crumb. We didn't leave a pit. You carry out what you carried in." She's quoting his creed back as a charming outdoorsy fact, *leave no trace*, with no idea it's the thing I flinched at. That's the chapter's cruelest small move and it's done with a totally light hand — no thumb on the scale, just Vee being delighted.

Cassie is the whole reason this chapter has a heartbeat. The "windowless van" / "blood type" / "witness statement" runner is genuinely funny and I loved her for it — the protective friend playing detective, relieved he brought Vee back "with your shoes on." And then the turn: Cassie goes still, "rapt," and at the end all she says is "Vee." Soft. The narration flags it precisely — "Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." That's Cassie clocking *something* — the size of what Vee's feeling, maybe the exposure, maybe just how far gone her friend is — and choosing not to name it. Then "Borrow real boots next time" / "I'm keeping the shirt" / "Keep the shirt." I melted. That's the seam-free love doing what it does: not fixing, not warning, just staying next to her and letting her keep the shirt. The fold I've been carrying (Cassie folding something away, Vee excluding her) — here Cassie's the one being excluded from the *truth* and still shows up warm, and I felt the wrongness of that under the sweetness.

Do I distrust anyone new? No. Nobody's on the page but these two and it's the cleanest bond in the book. My dread is all structural, all carried-in: the shirt Vee's proud of keeping is a thing he *let* her keep ("He didn't ask for it back" — of course he didn't), another object left on her with no fingerprints, cousin to the filed russet dress. She thinks she took a trophy; I think she was left a marker.

Erotic charge is near zero here and correctly so — this is the after, the telling, cotton-warm and dry where the last chapter was wet and exposed. The heat lives entirely in Vee's face being "open" and Cassie choosing not to look away.

**The title — "Rye."** Innocuous on its face: the bread in the sandwich, the specific homely detail she swoons over. But sitting with it — rye is the plain, dark, honest bread, and the whole sandwich is wrapped in his creed (leave no pit, carry out what you carried in). Naming the chapter after the one thing he *fed* her points me back at food-as-instrument: the hunger made first "so it could be satisfied." A single grain of bread standing in for the whole engineered meal. It doesn't give anything away — it's exactly the kind of title Vee herself would fondly choose, which is the unnerving part. **The book's title *A Polite Invitation*** got its inversion reconfirmed last chapter (the invitation now comes from *her*), and this chapter is Vee narrating that inversion as her own courage. **With a Long Spoon** — you sup with the devil with a long spoon; here she's telling her best friend about the loveliest meal, no spoon in sight.

What I want next: Randi. She's been off-page two chapters and the held breath is getting long — after this direct Pace/Vee deepening I need to see the closer re-enter and I dread the angle she comes in on. And I'm still waiting for the house, and the dress, and the induction dinner. *Guess from here:* the kept shirt becomes a thread — either it's the thing Cassie later can't stop looking at, or it's the first of a set of left-behind objects that accrue into a claim.

**Symbolism:** The oversized borrowed flannel = she's inside *him* now, swallowed by his warmth, wearing the marker she thinks is a trophy. "Carry out what you carried in" spoken innocently = the creed of the man who leaves no trace, in the mouth of the woman he's marking.

**Characterization:** Both deepen. Vee: her habit of narrating desire as achievement is now fully visible — she frames her own exposure as the day's summit, more proud of the strip than the mountain. Cassie: her love shown as *restraint* too (the mirror of Pace's restraint, but real) — she reads the size of it and holds it, chooses "borrow real boots" over any warning. The rhyme between Cassie's held-back care and Pace's engineered restraint is quietly devastating and, I think, earned by the page.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Brisk and right-sized; the detective banter earns its length, the retelling never drags, the ending lands on three short lines and stops. No fat.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate exhale after the erotic peak — momentum holds rather than builds, which is the correct move; two chapters now with Randi entirely off-page, and that absence is starting to register as a real held breath I want released soon.
