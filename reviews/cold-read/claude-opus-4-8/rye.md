## Reader reaction

This is a small, quiet chapter — the debrief. The hike I just watched happen, replayed once more in Vee's own mouth to Cassie, and the pleasure of it is watching what she chooses to keep and what she skips. She tells it the way she'd tell a first kiss — "She said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed*" about the sandwich, of all things — and that landed for me. The whole thing gets relived through the food and the shirt, and she's still glowing.

I love Cassie here. She's the best character in the book for my money, and this is the most she's gotten to be herself in a while. The deadpan is warm, not cold — "I had your blood type," "I had a whole witness statement ready," "No windowless van." She turned her worry into a running bit so Vee could land safely inside it. That's love that wants nothing again — she was genuinely scared, she says so obliquely ("I had questions"), and she folds the fear into a joke so Vee doesn't have to carry Cassie's fear on top of her own giddiness. And then the pivot: the moment Vee slows down at the shirt, "as she'd slowed at the tree the other night, and Cassie... went still and stayed that way." Cassie *reads* the register-change. She knows this is the real thing and stops performing.

And then the ending gutted me a little. "Vee," Cassie said. "Soft. Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." That's Cassie seeing it — seeing that Vee is *gone*, all the way in — and choosing not to say the warning she clearly has loaded. "Borrow real boots next time" is the whole warning compressed into something survivable, something Vee can laugh off. She hands over one practical, tiny caution and lets the enormous one go unspoken. My dread lives right there. The one person immune to all of this, the one true instrument, just decided not to sound the alarm because she could see it would only cost her Vee and change nothing. "Keep the shirt." She lets her have it.

No suspicion moved for me here because there's nothing to be suspicious of *in the room* — it's two friends. But the dread deepened precisely because Cassie, my trusted one, chose silence. That's the earned unease of the chapter: the true word available and withheld, by its own owner, out of love.

Vee: still my whole heart. The line "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain" is the one that isn't about Pace at all, and it's the one I most want to be true and separate from him. That's the tragedy in miniature — the real growth is tangled inseparably into the thing being done to her. "His hands, though. And he's got these forearms... I think there's a whole situation under there" — that's just delicious, a girl allowed to be a girl about a man, and Cassie letting her.

**Erotic charge:** low and secondhand, which is right — this is afterglow, not heat. The charge is in the *retelling* of the shirt moment, "I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me." The word "Me," repeated (also on the mountain), is the erotic and the emotional core both — her astonishment at her own agency. It simmers, it doesn't burn, and it shouldn't.

**Friction:** almost none. It's a short interstitial and it knows it. If anything I could feel it slightly re-narrating what I just read — the sandwich, the pits, the shirt, the standing-in-the-rain — but it's re-narrated *through Vee's selection*, which makes it new rather than redundant. I bought it.

**Title:** "Rye." Surface: the bread in the sandwich, the literal detail she fixates on. But rye pulls two ways for me now — *rye* the wry, dry register (Cassie is nothing *but* wry here, the whole chapter is played in a dry key), and there's an old catch-in-the-rye sense, someone standing at the edge of a field ready to catch children before they go over. Cassie *is* the catcher here — and she deliberately doesn't catch. That reading may be me reaching, but the wry/rye pun on Cassie's whole affect feels intended and it's lovely; it names her voice, not the plot.

**Book title / tagline:** "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is that line's proof-of-concept — Vee reciting her own freely-authored yeses (I called him back, I took my shirt off *on purpose, Me*) as triumphs, to the one witness who can see the frame around them and says nothing. The long spoon stays down.

**What I want next:** I want to get back to Pace or Randi — I've been in Vee's warmth for a couple chapters and I'm bracing for the machine to click again. *Guess from here:* the induction/November and that russet dress are loaded and coming; and I suspect Cassie's chosen silence here is a cost she'll pay later, that this was the moment she could have and didn't.
