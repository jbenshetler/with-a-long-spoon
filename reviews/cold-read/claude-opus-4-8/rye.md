# Cold read — Rye

*scene: scenes/rye.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: leave-no-trace*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Short chapter, mostly two women talking, and I loved it. After the sustained high-wire of the mountain, this is the exhale — and it does something I didn't know I needed, which is let me *hear* Vee's version. Everything I watched happen with my suspicious eyes gets re-narrated by a girl in love, and the gap between those two tellings is the whole book in miniature. She says "he didn't make it weird. Didn't make me feel like I'd wrecked the day" about the boot fix, and she's not wrong, and I'm not wrong either. Both true. That's the trap working in stereo.

Cassie is the best she's ever been here, and my feeling for her moved a lot. The comedy of "I had your blood type" is warm, not snide — she's the one person in the book who considered that Vee might be driven off into the woods by a man nobody has vetted, and she said so, and then she was gracious about being wrong. Then: "Cassie, who had been doing the dry thing, the file-revising thing, went still and stayed that way." That stillness gutted me a little. She hears what Vee is telling her, and I think she hears more than she says. And the ending — "Vee," she said finally. Soft. Not *Oh, Vee.* Just her name, like setting something down where it wouldn't break." — that's the most loaded line in the chapter. *Not "Oh, Vee"* means she had an "Oh, Vee" available and swallowed it. She sees something. She doesn't have language for it, or she won't spend it. And then she does the only thing she can do: "Borrow real boots next time." Practical love. She's the only person in this book giving Vee something with no return on it, and she's the one being edged out. My dread for Cassie is now sharper than my dread for Vee.

Vee herself: delightful and heartbreaking. "I have never gotten anywhere on my own legs in my life and I got up a mountain." That's the achievement she's proudest of, and it's real, and it's also his gift, and she doesn't distinguish. "And then we ate." She said it the way other girls said *and then we kissed.*" — beautiful, and exactly the shame-architecture the book has been building: eating *is* her erotic confession. Also she's funny. "I'm reporting what I observed." "I think there's a whole situation under there." I hadn't seen her this loose and girlish before; she's usually monitoring. The absence of self-monitoring here is itself the tell of how far gone she is.

No new suspicion earned by this chapter — nothing is off in the room. What's off is what's *not* in the room: Randi. Vee is telling this to Cassie, not Randi, and I noticed that with something like relief and then immediately with worry, because I expect Randi to get her own version soon and I dread the difference.

Erotic charge: low but not zero — 1. It's recounted heat, and the striptease gets one sentence in the telling ("And I took my wet shirt off in front of him. On purpose. Me.") where on the mountain it took pages. That compression is right; the charge here is social, the thrill of *saying it out loud to a friend* rather than the thing itself. The forearms bit is the only live flare.

Friction: almost none. It's the shortest chapter yet and I wanted maybe one more beat of Cassie — one question she asks that goes somewhere Vee doesn't want. The nearest thing to a thumb on the scale is "she slowed then, as she'd slowed at the tree the other night" — a small backward nod I registered as authorial bookkeeping more than memory, and honestly I couldn't place "the tree the other night" with confidence, which is a gap I'll carry.

Title: **Rye**. Before reading, meaningless — after, it's the sandwich, and the sandwich is the moment she narrates like a kiss. It points at the food-as-eros spine dead-on, and it's charming that the chapter names itself after the least erotic noun in it. (Rye also whispers *dry, wry* — Cassie's register.) It gives nothing away; it just tells me afterward what the chapter thought it was about, which is Vee's hunger finally spoken aloud without apology. The book title and tagline sit heavier every chapter: "Every yes was freely given" now includes her yes to *telling Cassie*, and the long spoon keeps meaning that everyone here is eating with someone they should be handling more carefully.

What I want next: Randi's version of this conversation, and I dread it. And Cassie's unspoken "Oh, Vee." Guess from here: Cassie is going to be the one who notices Randi and Pace are connected, and nobody will believe her.

**Story so far**
Ch.1–10 as before: Pace & Randi (lovers; she a poised sorority woman, he a young mathematician off six-fourteen) decide, mid-encounter, to make Vee — a shame-raised, over-giving econ major Randi picked across the quad — the third in their bed, telling her nothing. **How I feel**
Ch.11 got past my defenses; ch.12 makes that worse by letting me hear Vee's own joyful version of what I watched with suspicion — both accounts true, which is the whole trap in stereo. My tenderness for Vee is very high and so is my dread. Cassie has become the person I care about most: funny, protective, unpaid, seeing something and swallowing it, and being quietly displaced. Pace's shiver still unresolved — either his body is outrunning his strategy or it's the coldest patience yet. Randi's absence from this room is conspicuous and I dread her version. Pointed forward: the induction dinner, the russet dress, and whatever Cassie almost said.
