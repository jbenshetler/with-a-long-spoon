# Oracle (grounded) — professor · neutral

*model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: professor · tier: neutral · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Was there anything that read as a running joke or a wink — aimed a little over the characters' heads, at you? Anything you caught yourself smiling at on a second or third appearance?

---

Yes — and the big one was the stats class. Every lecture Vee dutifully sat through turned out to be a caption for the thing being done to her, and I caught on early enough that by the third or fourth I was grinning in a slightly guilty way whenever the professor cleared his throat. Latent variables — "the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer" — I flagged as a half-inch too neat the first time (that was in "See You Later"). Then it kept coming: substitution ("turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do"), error-correcting codes ("say it more than once so the noise can't kill it"), Simpson's paradox ("short stretches can lie against the whole"), and finally, literally, "The Outlier." By Simpson's paradox I'd stopped taking it straight and started reading it as the book leaning over Vee's shoulder to whisper to me — she underlines the sentence "because it had the ring of an exam question," and I underlined it because it was the ring of the plot. It's the one place I consistently felt handled rather than swept, but I mostly enjoyed the handling, because it wore comedy as cover (the professor "reading the back of a cereal box").

The thing I actually laughed out loud at, more than once, was Cassie failing to make Pace add up. "Are you sure he's not gay?" — a man who's had this girl half-naked twice and reached for a tape measure — and then "He's a bodybuilder who makes dresses." That running gag of the one clear-eyed person in the room being genuinely unable to solve him delighted me every time, partly because I knew the answer she couldn't reach and she was closer to the real wrongness than her joke let her know.

A smaller, colder wink I caught on its second appearance: "Her name is Vee." Randi says it in the pointing-game aftermath as she hands the target's name over; then in the diner, Sheri corrects Pace's "the redhead" to the name, and the echo rang deliberately — the same three words meaning "acquired" in one mouth and "she's a person" in the other. That one I didn't smile at so much as feel the back of my neck go cold — the book reusing its own line at my expense.

And one running gag purely between me and the pages: Randi "turning the dress over in her hands without a hand on it" / working everything while touching nothing. By the time I'd seen her hands-on-nothing a few times it had become her signature the way "Hi / Hi" was Pace's, and I'd started watching for it — the book and I sharing a private read of her that Vee never got let in on.
