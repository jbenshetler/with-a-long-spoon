## Neutral

**Q:** "Tell me about Randi and Vee. What do you make of how Randi feels about her?"

**A:**
Randi and Vee read to me as the most tender relationship in the book so far — and also the most quietly troubling, once you know what I know.

On the page, Randi is the friend who lifts Vee into a brighter life. I have a strong sense of her as *warmth-as-method*: she listens, she draws out the shames Vee was raised to bury and hands them back as something to be proud of. Her voice — "you've got the goods, gorgeous girl" — literally runs in Vee's head this chapter as courage-fuel. And she's the one who knows the shoes, always the shoes: the cognac heels, the ankle-strap at the induction, now the black heels Vee wore through the whole reunion. There's an intimacy in that attention that goes way past friendship.

What do I make of how she feels? Here's the thing I keep circling: I *specifically remember* carrying forward that Randi is genuinely, romantically in love with Vee — and has *decided never to tell her*. That decision is the part that unsettles me. Her love is real, but it's held behind glass. She engineered the naked-under-the-coat plan and let Vee own it as her own idea. So even the courage Vee feels — that "powerful, self-authored" feeling that was the emotional high of this chapter — is partly Randi's design that Vee can't see.

So I feel two things at once. Warmth: Randi's tenderness doesn't read as fake, and I don't think she's cold toward Vee. But dread: she loves the girl and is simultaneously steering her, with Pace, into a bed whose real shape Vee doesn't know. That's exactly the jacket's "every yes was freely given, that was the trap." Vee's "best friend" is the architect.

I'll also flag the ache underneath — I remember Randi's own hollowness, that Gstaad night, the locked bracelet hidden from cold parents, the warm lie she told. She gives Vee a permission and belonging she may not have herself. My *guess from here*: the cost of never telling Vee lands on Randi eventually, and it's going to hurt her as much as anyone.
