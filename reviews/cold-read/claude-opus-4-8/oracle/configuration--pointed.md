**Question asked (verbatim):** Do you understand Randi and Pace to be secretly together and steering Vee toward something she doesn't know about? 0-5, with what on the page confirmed it (vs. only the jacket).

---

5.

Not just yes — this is the one thing in the whole book I'm certain of, and it's certain because the pages showed it to me directly, over and over, not because the jacket whispered it. The jacket only primed me; the prose confirmed it from the very first chapter and then kept confirming it.

The bedrock is "The Bench." I read the opening woman as Vee until the last line turned it over: "There you are, Randi." Randi and Pace are alone in his house, and she's the one being taken apart on the built bench — so before I ever met Vee I'd watched two people who are plainly lovers, in the house that later becomes Vee's whole world. Then "The Pointing Game" put the steering on the page as an actual event: the two of them strolling the quad appraising strangers, "I point. You pick," Pace saying "Her," and Randi executing the collision so cleanly the redhead apologizes — "Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together." That's not implication. That's the plan being built in front of me.

After that it's confirmed at intervals so I never got to doubt it. "Gone" is the clincher — Pace and Randi in bed, spent, discussing Vee by name as a project they're pacing: "I want her to get there on her own," "she's not ready — she'll get there." I watched them coordinate the seduction as pillow talk. And the whole architecture of the Randi-lunches ("How It's Done," "Fed," "One Bite," the practice room) is Randi mining Vee's confessions and feeding her back her own life as bait — I heard Randi describe Pace's bench and counting disguised as some "soft-spoken ex." In "Fairytale" I even got Randi's private resolution to keep it hidden: "Let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were."

The only thing the book keeps deliberately ajar — and it's a real ambiguity, not a doubt about the fact — is how cold the collusion is versus how much it "got real" for the two of them. Randi's stillness when Pace glows about Vee, her confession that Vee "got past me before I could help it," Pace helplessly overtalking her to Sheri — the feeling turned genuine on both sides. But that they're secretly a couple steering an unknowing Vee? Fully, repeatedly, on-the-page confirmed. A 5.
