**Question asked (verbatim):** Where do things stand between the three of them, as you understand it?

---

Where they stand, as I close the book: the machine has done exactly what it was built to do, and everyone in it is more caught than they meant to be — including the two who built it.

Vee and Pace are back together, and the reconciliation is the crest the whole break was loading toward. She drove out in the coat with nothing under it and he took the wall down all at once — the starving-and-forgiven look, going to his knees, the cracked "I missed you so much." So the December rupture is closed. But it closed without the thing she actually broke it open to get: he still won't say the word. That's the exact shape of where they are — she has every atom of his care, the drawer, the made dress, the body, the aftercare, and not the one syllable she can carry out into daylight. "Believe what I do" is still the wall. And what unsettles me most is that the reunion she thinks she authored — the bravest, freest, most self-made yes she's given — was staged by Randi down to the heels pressed into her hands. So the couple is "healed" on a set Randi dressed.

Randi is the one who's moved, and where she's landed is the real charge of the ending. In Gstaad I watched her decide, over a stranger she felt nothing for, not to tell Vee — to keep the brunches exactly as they are, to "have Vee this way and keep the friendship both, as long as the word kept its cover." She named it two ways to herself and chose the cover. So she's not the cool operator from the jacket anymore; she's a woman genuinely, helplessly gone on Vee (the "she got past me before I could help it," the held-too-long hugs, "so jealous I could scream" passed off as a quip) who is nonetheless still steering — and steering Vee back toward the man they share. The love and the handling are the same gesture, and she can't or won't pull them apart because, in her own words, she arranged her whole life never to have to be brave enough to risk losing anyone.

So the geometry, stereo, at the close: Vee at the warm center reaching openly toward two people who each keep one load-bearing secret from her — Pace withholding the word, Randi withholding the whole plan and her own want. Vee doesn't know Pace and Randi are together, doesn't know she was chosen off a lawn, and has just begun — in "Seconds," "Bare," "Believe Me" — to feel the shape of the trap through the wrong lens (she fears "just sex," when the truth is bigger and worse). The two architects, meanwhile, are no longer aligned the way they were: I clocked the first crack back in "Gone," Randi going still when Pace lit up about Vee, and everything since has widened it. My strong sense leaving this volume is that the danger has quietly relocated — it started as something aimed at Vee, and it's become something live between Randi and Pace, with Vee's un-looked-at pull toward Randi (the half-beat-too-long goodbye kiss, the "flat helpless want" from the very first lunch) being the fuse the next book lights. Nobody has the long spoon. Everyone handed themselves over freely, and that was the problem.
