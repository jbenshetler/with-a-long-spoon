**Question asked (verbatim):** The statistics professor and his lectures — the paradox, the error-correcting codes, the outliers. How did he land the FIRST time, and had that changed by his third or fourth lecture? A straight teaching device, or a joke/wink shared with you — and if a joke, did you enjoy him or did he wear thin? 0-5 on how much you enjoyed him by the end (0 = groaned, too on-the-nose; 5 = delighted, wanted more), with what changed your read.

---

Lead number: **3.**

The first time, in "See You Later," it was the professor's "latent variables" line — "the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can." Vee underlines it and it sits there going nowhere, and I remember clocking it as landing "a half-inch too neatly," a small authorial wink through the lecture. I noticed the thumb, but lightly — it didn't spoil anything. So the very first landing was: this is a device, aimed over Vee's head at me, and I saw the hand and forgave it.

It didn't stay that light. By "All Told" and the Simpson's paradox lecture — "short stretches can lie against the whole" — I felt it more openly as a thumb; I actually called it "the chapter's thumb," clever and slightly too on-the-nose at once. What kept me from groaning was that it was genuinely funny: the professor reading the back of a cereal box, Vee the good student coming up for air from her own life. The comedy was doing real work as cover for the machinery. My honest read there was: handled, but laughing.

By the "outliers" material (the practice room's additive-vs-innovational bit, then "The Outlier" itself) I did register the pattern as a recurring gesture — I wrote that the stats-as-commentary moves were "getting a touch familiar," and the closing paragraph of "The Outlier" sat "right at the edge of the author's thumb," spelling the metaphor one degree past where I'd found it myself. But it didn't wear all the way thin, and the reason is that "The Outlier" cashed the device into actual plot — Randi as the literal worst point on the graph, Miranda Holdings up in the white — so the wink stopped being only a wink and became a knife. That redeemed it.

So: yes, a joke shared with me, not a straight teaching device — and I enjoyed him more than he annoyed me, but it was a near thing, propped up by the comedy and by the one chapter that made the gag load-bearing. A 3: I smiled, I didn't groan, but I was aware by the end that one more Simpson's-paradox-style nudge without a payoff would have tipped me toward eye-rolling.

One honesty note: part of what the question folds in — the error-correcting codes ("say it more than once so the noise can't kill it") — I actually remember as *Pace's* line in "Two Towels," not the professor's. So my professor-specific memory is really the latent-variables / Simpson's-paradox / outliers thread, and that's what the 3 is scoring.
