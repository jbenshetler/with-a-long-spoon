# Oracle (grounded) — identification · pointed

*model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: identification · tier: pointed · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** For each — Vivienne, Randi, Pace — 0-5: how much could you see yourself making the same choices in their circumstances? (0 = alien to me, I'd never; 5 = honestly, I'd probably do the same.) Name the one choice of theirs that most tests your answer.

---

**Vivienne — 4.** Honestly, yes, mostly. What she does across the whole book is want something and let herself have it, and reason her way into the wanting — and the book put me so far inside that reasoning that I kept catching myself doing it with her. The froyo cup getting away from her with no mother's voice counting, saying "I did it on purpose, nobody did anything to me" in the dorm, taking her top off in the truck "Me," walking the long step at the induction — I understood every one of those from the inside and would, I think, have made them. What keeps it from a 5 is the maxed emergency credit card in "Sorority" — the shoes she bought on money that had nothing under it because "they were what the dress was owed." That's the one I watched with my stomach dropping, wanting to grab her wrist. I can feel the pull of it and I still don't think I'd have swiped that card, which is exactly why it's the choice that tests me. The *steering* behind it I couldn't see; the yes itself, I could.

**Randi — 2.** Most of her is alien to me — the professional face, the smile that gets "arranged," the running of a whole extraction on a girl who trusts her. But I can't put her at 0 or 1, because the book made her wound legible from the bench onward: the bottle cap under the thumbnail, the deciding "not ever" to be seen again, the "warm because she was warm" cold in Gstaad. I understand *retreating* behind a face after being cracked open; I understand being too scared to lose someone to risk telling them the truth. The single choice that tests me hardest is the one she makes over the sleeping stranger in "Fairytale": *let Vee arrive at it on her own, keep the brunches exactly as they were, keep the word under cover.* That's the coward's love I recognize the shape of — I can imagine, in real terror of losing someone, choosing to keep them in a fog rather than risk the truth. Recognizing the shape is why it's a 2 and not lower; that I'd hope to be braver is why it isn't higher.

**Pace — 1.** Almost entirely alien to me, and the one that got *more* alien as the book went. Scene by scene his tenderness is the thing I'd want to be capable of — the water bottle left in advance, the dress matched to *her* not the shade she named, the aftercare, the juice held to her mouth. If the question were only "would you try to be that attentive," I'd score him high. But the whole of him is a man who built a bench and ran a pointing game to *select* a person, and then, when she finally asks the one plain thing — *what am I to you* — folds his arms and says "words are cheap, believe what I do." The choice that tests me most is that refusal in "Believe Me" / confirmed in "The Usual": Sheri lays it out flat — "not saying it matters more to you than having her" — and he looks down and doesn't argue. I cannot see myself choosing a rule over a person I'm watching walk out into the cold. That I still found him *moving* the whole time is what keeps him off 0 — I understand the wish to give by making rather than saying. But the withholding at cost to someone else, on purpose, is the one place I genuinely can't follow him.
