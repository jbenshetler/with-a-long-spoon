# Oracle (grounded) — sympathy · neutral

*model: claude-opus-4-8 · probe: sympathy · tier: neutral · stage: end (whole book) · battery: oracle-battery.json*

**Question asked (verbatim):** Whose corner are you in? Who do you feel for, and who leaves you cold or wary — Vee, Randi, Pace?

---

I'm in Vee's corner, all the way, and it isn't close. From the froyo cup that got away from her with no mother's voice counting, to the pool in "Water Wings" where the water was "the one part of it that had never been taken," to the induction walk where she made the second step long on her own — I ached for her the whole book and rooted for her happiness so plainly it embarrassed me. What sealed it is that she's decent and sharp and starving, and every yes she gave was real and glad and hers, and I knew from chapter one it was being harvested. Being ahead of her the whole way didn't cool me on her; it made me protective. By the break chapters — the flannel that was "warm because she was warm," choosing the cold over a comfort she'd caught being a lie — I wanted to reach into the book and get her out.

The other person I feel for, to my own discomfort, is Randi. I met her being taken apart on the bench in chapter one — the face, the deadbolt, the bottle cap under the thumbnail she doesn't know she's pressing — before I ever met the "glamorous operator" the jacket promised, and that ordering did its work on me permanently. She's the co-architect and I distrust nearly everything her warm mouth does, and I still ache for her, because "Gone" and "Fairytale" showed me she got caught by her own game — "she got past me before I could help it," the borrowed heat in the sleigh that's only her own given back, the coward's decision in Gstaad to keep loving Vee in secret. She's dangerous and she's bleeding, and the book never let me collapse those into one. So: wary of her hands, tender toward her wound. Both at full volume.

Pace is the one who leaves me cold — or rather, he's the one I can't get warm about without immediately going cold, and by the end the cold won. Scene by scene he undid me: the dress matched to her and not the shade she named, the drawer cleared to the top one, the cracked "I missed you so much." I found him attractive the whole way and hated that I did — exactly the trap the jacket promised. But "Rock" gave me a possible engine under the engine (Daphne, named as luck), and the "Seconds"/"Under the Rug"/"Believe Me" run turned his patience from a gift into a wall: a man who'd give her his whole body and every true thing except the one word, and choose a rule over a person and refuse to look at why. Sheri saying "not saying it matters more to you than having her — that feels pretty shitty" is where my last defense of him dropped. I never caught his tenderness being fake. That's precisely what makes him the one I trust least: the care is real and it's the instrument, and I couldn't unbraid them, and I stopped wanting to give him the benefit of the doubt.

The clean warmth in the whole book, for me, was Cassie — the only person orbiting Vee who wants nothing, the "keep the shirt," the splash in the pool, the questions she declines to force open. If the question is who I'd protect, it's Vee, and I'd send Cassie to do it.
