# Cold read — Grace

*scene: scenes/grace.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: burn*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me hard, and I want to say that before I say anything cooler about it. Almost the whole chapter is two people alone on a mountain, and it's the most swept-up I've been in a while — the tikka masala carried up a mountain in a thermos to still be hot on a freezing porch, the cider that "should not work" and does, "I wanted to please you" said plainly and going "into a place the cider hadn't reached." That last one got me clean: *days ago, in his kitchen, with her nowhere in the room, he had stood there and thought about what would please her. He carried her into rooms she wasn't even in.* That's the thing I keep falling for about Pace and the chapter knows it.

And then the key. He makes her a box, oiled wood, the corners she knows by his hand, and inside a plain new brass key on oyster-pale silk, and: *"You're more than a guest in my house. I've never given a key to anyone."* I felt the lift she felt — the old December wish rising and *this time meeting something solid under it to stand on.* But I also caught, exactly as she did, the word that came and the word that didn't: *He had said house... marked the word and waited half a beat for the other one, the one with a hearth in it. The other one didn't come.* Still no "love," still everything-but-the-word, and she's gotten so good at letting it go that the letting-go is starting to scare me more than the wanting did. "The word she wanted had only ever been air" — she's talking herself out of needing it. That's not healing, that's training.

The center of the chapter is the blindfold. He strips her, binds her eyes with his own flannel, stands her blind in full afternoon light where he can see all of her and she can see nothing, and goes over her body inch by inch, naming each part and what it does to him. On the page it's worship — genuinely, meltingly so; the thing that undid me was that he keeps naming *the exact things she's spent her life hiding* and wanting them: "the spill is the part I want most," the wet as "the most honest thing I've ever stood in front of," her walking-away-in-the-morning body he'd lie there treasuring while she thought herself unseen. *Named, and wanted, in one breath. It got in under the guard before the guard knew to close.* That's the whole seduction of him in one line and I felt it land.

But this is also where my skin prickled, and I think the page earned it. This isn't just being adored — it's total immobilization. Blind, still, "he had all of it and she had none of it." And it doesn't stop at being looked at. It turns into "Show me" — he steps back, won't touch her, won't fill the silence, and *makes* her masturbate in front of him, blind, then reaches into even that: *"What are you thinking about?"* and pulls "you, you, you" out of her at the exact moment she comes. He took the one thing she did behind a locked door and made her do it *for* him and *name him* while she did it. It's the most explicit the book's been and it's extraordinary — but it's also the deepest reach yet into her privacy, and I clocked the through-line: he keeps finding the last room she has to herself and getting the door open. "Some of what he gave her she could not give herself, that it had needed him" — that's rendered as tenderness and it's also a dependency being installed.

Then the ending, which is the quietest and the one I'm most uneasy about. Afterward, in the dark, he asks: *"Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?"* And she's already got her hand around him, mind on the next thing, so the yes comes "easy and breathy and barely attended at all, the way you say yes to something small." That's her signature — yes before the mind catches up — and this time she's agreeing to hand him her private fantasies going forward, to report the locked-door self to him. It slid past her while she was busy wanting him again. I don't think she'll remember agreeing to it. I do.

The other thing I can't unsee: when she describes *him* in the mirror — brown eyes, cleft chin, the birthmark by his ear — *"something crossed his face — a shadow, there and gone before she could put a name to it."* The man who sees everyone flinched at being seen. He wants to be the one doing the looking; being looked at, being described, cost him something for a half-second. That's the same locked interior as the withheld "love," the father, not home since sixteen. He'll open every room in her and keep his own shut.

What genuinely deepened Vee: when the "show me" is underway and the self-consciousness falls off, her own appetite comes up — one hand "closing harder than he ever had, rough now, dragging at the nipple in a way he was too reverent for, her own appetite, what she was when no one watched." I loved that. She isn't only what he makes of her; there's a rougher, hungrier her that he's *too reverent* to touch. That's a real her, off-leash for a second.

Trust/suspicion: no Randi, no Sheri, none of the other machinery on the page — just the two of them, "so far from everywhere," which is itself the note I flag: the isolation went literal. My suspicion of Pace here isn't a plot suspicion, it's the same one that's been growing — the care and the taking are one gesture, and this chapter he "ran clean out of asking" for the first time, took her without her word, and she felt it as *power*, that she'd undone the most careful man she knew. The book keeps handing her that reciprocity — that she has power over him — and it's true on the page (he really came apart) and it's also exactly what keeps her from counting the cost. I hold both.

**Titles.** "Grace" — grace before the meal (he says grace over her body, essentially); grace as elegance, the dancer's-ask "would you like an adventure"; and grace in the older sense, unmerited gift — she keeps feeling she's "the kind of girl," and he gives her worship she's sure she hasn't earned, plus a key, plus a box. The word "savior" is right there in the panic — "searching for a savior" — blind, reaching for someone to save her. Grace as the thing given freely to one who can't repay it, by the one who's steering her. On the surface warm; under it the series title **With a Long Spoon** (sup with the devil, use a long spoon) recolors it — this is grace poured by the hand you'd want a long spoon between. **A Polite Invitation**: the key is the invitation deepening — into the house, and now into her private self. The tagline isn't in my memory, so I'll leave it.

What I want next: the machine's other half back. Randi and the debrief and the Sheri dance have been offstage for a while, and after a chapter this sealed-off and complete I want the world to come back and touch this — I want to know what, if anything, "would you tell me when you touch yourself" becomes. I dread that the reporting she just agreed to has somewhere to go.
