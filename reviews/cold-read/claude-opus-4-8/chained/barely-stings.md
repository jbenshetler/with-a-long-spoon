# Cold read — Barely Stings

*scene: scenes/barely-stings.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: another-round*

## Reader reaction

This one got into me. It's a solo Vee chapter — a waxing, of all things — and it turns out to be one of the most concentrated renderings of the whole trap-mechanism I've read yet, and also one of the most quietly erotic, in a way I found unsettling to be moved by.

The engine is the yes. Pace asked her three nights ago, in his kitchen, "in the plain voice he used for facts about the world" — "I'd like you waxed. By the weekend. Bare." And then, because he always asks, "Will you?" And she said yes "out ahead of her, her mouth answering before her mind had caught up to it — while she was still somewhere back down the sentence going *bare?*, going *the weekend?*, going *why.*" That's the whole book in a paragraph. The thing I've been circling for chapters — the freely-given yes as the exact instrument of the trap — is here laid absolutely bare (the chapter knows what it's doing with that word), and it worked on me as ache, not just as thesis. "He asked for the thing itself, clean, and left the place where the reason should be for her to fill, and she filled it, every time, with *yes, yes, tell me when.*" I felt the tenderness of being asked and the trap of being asked in the same breath, which is precisely the chord I can't resolve about him.

And the fear that comes after, never before — "the fear arriving too late to be any use — that was nearly the point of it." That's a hair thesis-y, the kind of too-neat self-narration I've flagged in her before, but by now it's so thoroughly earned across the arc that I let it stand. It doesn't feel like the author's thumb; it feels like Vee, who narrates herself a half-inch above her own life to survive it. Which the chapter also names: the reflex to joke — "*you're going to want me drunk for this?*" — "the joke that would set her a half-inch above the moment and let her look down at it." She takes the wine instead and drinks a third of it fast and is *glad*. So the joke-armor drops in exactly the moments that matter.

The wax itself is brutal and I felt every strip — the first tear, the "*Fuck—*" out of a girl "who said sorry to furniture," the "white place with no words anywhere in it." But the peak, the thing that actually undid me, is the wrong hand. When she squirms — not from pain but from the wanting-to-hide of her own arousal — and Sondra's palm comes down flat and says "Don't move," and Vee's body *knows that grammar*: "had learned that grammar in a warm dark room from a man who meant it, and here was the shape of it with everything true scooped out of the middle. The wrong hand. It held her the exact way she wanted to be held and it did not want her at all." God. That's the cleanest, saddest thing the book has done with his control — she has been so trained to the pin-and-hold-still that a bored stranger's clinical hand reopens "a hole in her a mile across where the right hand should have been." I ached. And I noted, uneasily, that what I was aching for was *him* — the chapter made me want the right hand too, which is exactly its trick and exactly its honesty.

Randi. This is where the title lands and where my suspicion re-armed hard. "Barely stings" — that's what Randi told her at Chi Latte, with the hand laid over Vee's, "So worth it, baby. You'll walk out of there feeling like the most expensive thing in the room." And the wine, "she'll get you a glass of something first, she's civilized about it." And *not one word* about rolling over, about the back, "the part Randi hadn't said." It's a small omission — but it rhymes so exactly with the enormous omission Randi lives inside that I can't read it as innocent. "Randi was always one road further along. It was restful, being told." That line is the whole seduction of Randi rendered as its own trap: the pleasure of being managed by someone who has the passport. She breaks a corner off Vee's pastry — "Randi never ordered her own, only ever *for the table*" — and I clocked that as the same small taking, the same helping-herself, that I've been logging. Randi undersells every cost to Vee. That's the title. It recolors backward onto everything Randi's ever reassured her about.

Trust: my read on the reprieve holds. This chapter is the trap tightening in the softest possible register — no villainy, just a wax appointment and a wine and a lie of scale. And Pace's part in it is not soft at all when you sit with it: he asked to have her *made smooth*, an object prepared for one set of hands, and she's "being taken apart and made smooth for him." The devotion and the instrumentation are the same act. I hold that.

Erotic charge: high and strange and central. Not a sex scene, but the whole chapter runs on wanting — the arousal she's "appalled to feel" in the cold room, the fear the body will tell on her, and that final beat alone on the table, her own hand on "the bare astonishing smoothness of herself, and the sound she made was not one of pain," already gone from her own touch to imagining his. It peaks there and in the wrong-hand passage. Nowhere did it go slack.

Friction: almost none. The mother-voice — "*the kind of girl who*," "the small ruthless voice out of the kitchen" — is a shade schematic on its second outing, but it's shorthand I trust in her now. That's it.

The book's titles: *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon (sup with the devil, use a long spoon) has never felt more apt than watching Randi pass Vee a sanitized version of a cost across a café table. And this chapter's own title, "Barely Stings," is Randi's exact reassurance turned into a verdict on all her reassurances. It didn't give anything away going in — I read it flat — but coming out it's a small quiet indictment.

What I want next: the weekend, obviously — the moment he finds her like this, which she's aching toward. I dread it a little, because the chapter has made me want it too. And the debrief call between Randi and Pace is *still* unmade on the page, chapters late now; Randi is still offstage and heavy. *(Guess from here:* whatever Randi does with a success that hurts is still coming, and this chapter's little omission is a warm-up for it.)
