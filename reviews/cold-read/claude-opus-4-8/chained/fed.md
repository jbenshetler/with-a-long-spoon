## Reader reaction

This is the chapter my whole ledger has been bracing for, and it arrived wearing the softest possible face. Randi picked the room — "Randi picked all of them" — and Vee walks in "chosen, which was lit, which was a half-second behind." That half-second is everything. And then the extraction, done so lovingly I almost forgot to be afraid: the mimosa Vee prices against Thursday, the "sad little story" Randi leads with to open the channel, and then "Be cruel. Tell me everything."

And Vee does. The exact middle she ladled out of Cassie's account last chapter — "the part that was only hers" — she hands to Randi, because "Randi would not flinch." I felt the ache of that so precisely: the thing she couldn't tell the one clean person, she gives to the one person running her. Randi harvests it item by item and calls each one a gift: the toes painted Friday "for him," the frosted plum that "isn't about a color," the new underwear that's "not nothing, that's a *campaign*." Every extraction reframed as being *seen*. And the masterstroke — when fear crosses Vee's face at how much she's handed over "without ever once deciding to," Randi covers her hand: "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." She takes the one flicker of self-protection and dissolves it into permission to go further.

Then: "So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" And Vee, disarmed, gives up the deepest thing — "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." That's the whole treasure, and she laid it on Randi's table. "Good," Randi said, and I know what "good" costs.

Knowing what I know, this is the most efficient operation I've watched, and it never once raised its voice. The swoon and the dread are the *same* motion here — I was genuinely moved by "wrapped up in him, watching him cook," it's a real and beautiful thing Vee felt, and Randi's right that it's a painting. But every warm beat doubles as intake. Where it lands hardest: the goodbye kiss, "the second press," and "she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them, the way she had the first time." The tell that Vee's guard is fully down, told as tenderness.

The ajar thing from last chapter — Randi's involuntary tightness — I got no fresh crack this chapter. She was seamless. "Her face open and warm and glad for her friend" — I can't find the join. That smoothness itself is the unease. The one place I let myself just *feel* it: "The friend she'd wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting to get. Who liked her, some days, better than she managed to like herself." That's true and it's the trap in one sentence.

**The title — "Fed."** After "Famished," the parallel is brutal. Vee is fed — the mimosa, the porch, the warmth — and Vee *feeds*: she sets the night down "in front of someone who would not flinch." Fed both ways. And what's really being fed is the operation — Randi walks away full, running no longer on fumes. The kindest word for the coldest exchange. It gave nothing away going in; on the far side it's the whole chapter.

**The series — *With a Long Spoon*.** "He that sups with the devil should have a long spoon." This chapter is the sup. Vee brought no spoon at all; she reached across the cloth with a bare hand and Randi laid hers over it and left it there. *A Polite Invitation* — this was the politest possible interrogation.

What I want next: I want to see Randi *away* from Vee again, because the only place the seam might show is when no one's watching her be warm. And I still want that scar, those two towels. I dread — genuinely — the moment Vee learns any part of what I know, because this chapter showed me she'd defend Randi harder than she'd defend herself.
