## Reader reaction

Short, quiet, brutal little chapter — a night piece, Vee alone in the top bunk, and it does exactly what the last chapters have been building toward without a single new fact. Nothing *happens* and I felt it land like a stone.

The shirt. She's worn it over her own shirt so it "hid what it was" — a girl too far gone in a boy she won't be caught being. Then she strips both off and puts his back on bare, "the cloth straight onto her, the way she wore it when no one could see." And for a while it works, it always works, the sawdust-and-salt of him. But then the turn that gutted me: "it was warm because she was warm... the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it." That's the whole trap in one image. She's been holding *herself* and calling it him. "It had promised him close and there was no one close." God.

And she does the honest, awful thing — she takes it off and lies bare in the cold and *stays in it.* "It was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." After all the sweeping-under, all the manufactured forgivenesses, this is the first time I've seen her refuse the comfort and just lie in the truth of the cold. She doesn't fix it, doesn't text him (the empty field, nothing that "would come back as what she needed"), doesn't sweep. She just takes the exposure. That's movement — small, wordless, but real. The pile didn't get fed tonight; she looked at it and let it be cold.

The phone bit is precise: "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them." The name-she-cannot-find motif, sharpened again — "not the big one and not the small one, only every breath of him now and the silence at the center where the word should be."

No suspicion to register — this is pure interior, no one else awake but Cassie breathing easy below. No heat, and rightly none. The ache is the point.

**Title — "Bare":** the shirt off, the skin in the cold, but mostly *her*, finally without the cloth of any comfort between her and what she is to him — nothing. It's the least-cropped frame she's let herself sit inside yet. Plain and exact. **"A Polite Invitation" / "With a Long Spoon":** the spoon-handle stays long — even the shirt, the closest thing she has, hands her back only her own heat. Sup with a long spoon; she's learning she's been eating alone.

What I want next: for this cold to carry into daylight, for her to keep looking. I dread her putting the shirt back on tomorrow and calling it fine.
