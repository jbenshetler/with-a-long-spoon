## Reader reaction

So the door I dreaded opened, and behind it was — a dress. And I have to be honest: for most of this chapter I was swept, fully, warmly, the way I wanted to be. The house is not a dungeon. It's a small post-war house a man built order into. The shoes squared in the closet, "one right pair for each thing and no more." The kitchen that "looked like it had a purpose." I liked being walked through it as much as Vee did.

And two things genuinely moved me past my guard. The patent: he "lit up like a six-year-old who'd just found out everything there was to know about dinosaurs," and the thing it does is get "a kid who can't lie still" out of the MRI tube faster — and then "It does other things. That's the part I like." That is real. And the dress. The burgundy matched not to the magazine shade she'd fixed on for weeks but to *her* — "He'd matched *her*" — the color she named once on the summit, half-embarrassed, and let go inside the same breath, and forgot she'd said. He didn't forget. That is the single most swoon-heavy beat in the book so far, and my eyes did roughly what hers did.

But this is the chapter, too, where the tell finally has a shape I can hold. Three things pricked, and the page earned all three. **The kiss he stopped** — she reached, he met her, and then "the stopping was his," and "no boy she'd ever been with had once been the one to stop." Something in her "went still and started paying attention," and so did I: he is the one holding the pace now. **The closed bedroom door he wouldn't open** — she'd worn the satin *for* that door, leaned toward it, waited "for his hand to go to the handle," and "It didn't." He declines the obvious seduction. That's either genuine restraint or a longer patience, and the page leaves it ajar. And **the offer itself**: "To do it right I'd measure you first — down to what you'd wear under it, in good light, so I can see how it'll fall." The gift requires her stripped to satin under his eye — and "the only thing standing between her and it" is "her own willingness to be looked at." That lands *exactly* on her shame-as-heat, her wanting to be the watched thing. The door didn't open onto a demand. It opened onto a gift she has to undress for. That's the method entire.

The house "was warm. Too warm — a close, even heat with no draft in it," and she "filed it under the small mysteries of him," "the way you walk past a wrapped box on the counter without asking who it's for." I noticed the box. I noticed the heat left on for an arrival.

The title, **Two Towels** — I clocked it the instant I hit it: the bathroom, "two towels on the rod." A man who lives out of a gym bag, one right pair of shoes for each thing, no excess anywhere — and two towels. It's the one un-spare detail in a spare house, and the chapter is named for it. Two towels means a second body is expected, or kept ready for. That's the quiet chill under all the warmth, and the page trusted me to find it without a word. **A Polite Invitation** paid out precisely: the invitation was bare and courteous, and the thing behind it is a gift with a measuring tape in it.

What I want next: the measuring. I dread it and I want it, same as her. I don't think this is going to turn ugly on Saturday — I think it's going to stay this tender, and that the tenderness *is* the mechanism, both true at once, which is worse and more interesting than a trap. *(Guess from here.)*
