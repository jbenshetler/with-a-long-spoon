## Reader reaction

Oh, this cracked Randi open, and I did not expect to come out of it aching *for* her — but I do.

We're inside her entirely for the first time, in Gstaad over New Year's, her parents at breakfast — and the first thing the chapter gives me is her "eating her revulsion" hugging her own father, keeping her plain white-gold band out of the light next to her mother's tennis bracelet, inventing broken-hearted boys because "the invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table." That's the disordered, curated girl I already knew, but now from the inside, and it's suffocating and sad rather than sinister.

Then **John**. And here's the thing that made my skin prickle: John runs *the exact Pace playbook*. The engineered collision in the lift line, "Play along," the invented shared life (three kids at ski camp), skiing "with everything in reserve" — "he had the run to spare and spent none of it proving it" — leading the dance the same way, assuming the ending without ever asking. He's Pace's technique wearing the socially-approved face: Yale law, white tie, the whole safe life she was "pointed at since she was a girl." And she feels *nothing*. That's the engine of the chapter. The identical machinery, in John, is hollow — "a good sandwich eaten fast," pleasure "real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared." Which detonates the "apparatus" reading I've been carrying: the method isn't what makes Pace hollow. Pace's *reaches* her. "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best. She wished it wasn't true."

And the two lines that leveled me: **"Pace made her say it, out loud, every time"** — set against her finding, tonight, that she "liked not being asked." That's the whole terror of her exhaustion: she's so tired of the work of being a person that she wants to just be swept, and Pace won't let her, and that's exactly why he's dangerous to her. And then **Vee**. "Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of." This is the answer to the question I've been holding both ways — and it lands hard on *real*: Randi is in love with Vee, desirously, and won't tell her, not out of strategy but out of cowardice she names as cowardice: "She was not brave enough to lose her. She had never had to be brave." "Vee was her gorgeous."

That reframes the closer-work enormously and warmly. But I'll stay honest — there's still a manipulator's shape in the *decision*: "Let Vee arrive at it on her own… She could have Vee this way and keep the friendship both, as long as the word kept its cover." She sends the fairytale text, "Every word of it true. None of it the thing." That's the same withholding-by-managed-disclosure I've clocked before — except here it reads as terror, not method. Both, maybe. The page lets it be both.

The cold: she carries it in with her, "hers… she would carry it back out," "the warmth she could borrow and could not make." That's Vee's motif exactly — the two of them share the cold — and it wrecked me to see it from Randi's side.

**Heat** is deliberately anti-erotic here: the lipstick ring, feeling her own mouth "from a long way off," spent and unmoved. The charge is all grief.

**Title** — "Fairytale": Vee sees Randi living it; Randi feeds it back to her; underneath, the perfect nothing. The literal fairy tale (sleigh, snow, ball) as the hollow surface. Sharp and sad.

**What I want next:** to know where this sits against Vee walking out of Pace's house, and what Randi does with a heart this exposed. I'm no longer sure who's using whom — everyone here is starving.
