# Cold read — Fairytale

## Reader reaction

So the machine has an inside, and it hurts. This whole chapter is Randi — first time I've been let into her head, and it detonates half of what I thought I knew.

First: the parents' table. That opening breakfast is beautifully cold. "eating her revulsion," the mother "all edges and points against her," the tennis bracelet playing with the light while Randi keeps her own plain locked band *out* of the light. The invented boyfriends "safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table." I felt for her instantly, which unsettles me, because I've spent the whole book fearing her. This is a girl who learned to counterfeit warmth at the family table before she ever did it to Vee. That's not an excuse but it's an origin, and it lands.

Then John. And here's the thing — John is *Pace's move, run against Randi.* "I could see you were skiing alone. So was I." The invented wife-and-three-kids improv, the hand under the elbow she doesn't need and takes anyway, and above all the skiing: "he had the run to spare and spent none of it proving it." That is exactly Pace's not-taking, the power held visibly in reserve. Except John is the *hollow* version — cotillion-good, the safe world's best product — and the whole point of the chapter is that the safe perfect version reaches nothing in her. "It had meant no more than a good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon." That sandwich line is devastating and I believed every word of it.

The dance comparison is the hinge, and it's brutal in what it reveals: "He was a better dancer than Pace." John leads hard, unmistakable, no work required — "she thought she was done with the work." Pace's lead was "the lightest pressure, a thing she'd had to be listening for." She chooses easy tonight, and easy turns out to be nothing. So the whole chapter is Randi discovering, against her own will, that the thing she runs on Vee — the thing Pace runs on everyone — is the only thing that reaches her, and that the approved life is a beautiful dead end. "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best. She wished it wasn't true." That "she wished it wasn't true" — I trust her for the first time, precisely because she doesn't want it to be so.

And then the real bomb: **Randi is in love with Vee.** Not as instrument. "Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways." "She loved Vee and she had told her so... never once before said that to anyone without meaning it as currency... With Vee there was neither." "Vee was her gorgeous." This reframes the whole farming operation. She's harvesting the person she's in love with, and she won't say the word because saying it would make Vee "decide what to do with it," and she's "not brave enough to lose her." The parallel to Pace is exact and I don't think it's an accident: **Randi withholds the word from Vee for the same reason Pace withholds it from Vee — because the cover is the safety, and spending the word ends the game.** Two operators, same one-way valve, both aimed at the same woman. That's chilling and also, weirdly, the most human either of them has been.

But — and this is where my dread spikes — this does NOT redeem her. She ends the chapter deciding, coldly and completely, to *keep the brunches exactly as they were,* to let the thing "keep its cover," to have Vee "this way and keep the friendship both, as long as the word kept its cover." She loves Vee and she's still going to farm her. The love makes the farming worse, not better. The last image — Vee texting from the warm Ohio kitchen, "i'm here if you want to talk. or not," and Randi handing back the *fairytale*, "Every word of it true. None of it the thing" — that's the whole book's engine in one text exchange. Vee gives real air; Randi gives back a cropped truth. The chapter title *Fairytale* is the lie Randi hands Vee to keep her, and the story Randi wanted to tell a daughter she doesn't have, and the thing this chapter proves is empty.

The chronology jolted me. This is clearly the *past* — New Year's Eve, "the year I met your father," Randi already knowing Pace and already harvesting Vee ("across a table on a nothing Tuesday"). So this is set during her time already inside the operation, but it reads as formative — the night she tested the safe world and confirmed it can't hold her. It clarifies why she runs Pace's playbook: she's a true believer in it because it's the only thing that's ever reached *her.*

Trust/suspicion: my read of Randi has moved hard — not toward trust, but toward *comprehension.* She's no longer just "the instrument." The crack I'd logged twice ("It doesn't"; "Cheating bastards") now has a whole cold room behind it. And the load-bearing danger is worse now: she loves Vee and will still hand her to Pace. Speaking of which — this chapter does *not* mention delivering the photo or the harvest, and it makes me newly uneasy about whether Randi's love complicates the delivery, or whether she's already rationalized it.

Erotic charge: deliberately anti-erotic where it counts. The seduction is all charge on the mountain and the floor — the dance is the sexiest thing here, "his hand told her body where to go," genuinely good. Then the actual sex is rendered as absence: "her body had spent itself on him and she remained unmoved," the lipstick ring left on him like a mark she watched herself make "from a long way off." That's the point and it works — the hollowness is the content. Peak charge: the waltz. Slack (by design): the bed.

Friction: almost none. The parents are a touch broad — the label-on-the-belt, "keep your eye out for an eligible bachelor" — but it's Randi's contemptuous read, so the breadth is hers. The only line I paused on was "She walks in beauty" — the Byron quote is a little on-the-nose for John, but he *can't finish it,* which saves it and quietly rhymes with every man who can't finish the sentence with Randi.

Book signals: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter turns it on the operator. Randi freely said yes to John, freely chose easy, and the trap was on *her*: the safe world she was raised to want can't reach her, and the only thing that can is the thing she's using to hurt Vee. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — the long spoon is her, reaching Vee across the table with warmth she borrows and cannot make ("the warmth she could borrow and could not make"). That last line is the whole series title in one sentence.

What I want next: I want to know if this love ever cracks the operation. I *dread* the moment Vee learns that "gorgeous" meant two things and was still a harvest. And I'm braced back toward the present — Vee has just walked out on Pace; Randi is primed for best-friend consolation; and now I know the consoler is in love with the consoled and committed to keeping the cover. That's a worse countermove than I feared.

Consistent with every prior crack; nothing contradicted, everything explained. John is a deliberately thin foil and reads as intended. Vee and Pace, offstage, are sharpened by contrast — both now defined by "reaching further" than the safe world can.

**Pace — within the chapter:** earns its length; the slow build (breakfast → lift → dance → bed → mirror → text) is a controlled descent from fairytale to void, each beat doing work. The mirror/aftermath monologue is long but it's the payload, so it holds.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** a hard POV pivot away from Vee's walkout into Randi's past — jarring at first, but it recontextualizes the whole operation and doesn't sag. Momentum is *sideways-then-deeper* rather than forward; I trust it because of what it reveals, though I'm impatient to get back to the present standoff.
