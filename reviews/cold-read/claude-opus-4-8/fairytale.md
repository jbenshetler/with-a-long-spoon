## Reader reaction

# Felt read

So this is the chapter that turns the camera around. For the whole book I've been inside Vee, and suddenly I'm inside Randi — on a mountain in Gstaad on New Year's Eve, exactly while Vee is home in Ohio texting her — and it rearranges the furniture in my head about who Randi is.

The opening at breakfast did a lot fast. The mother "all edges and points against her," the father wanting to know "how many hearts she had broken this fall," Randi *inventing* boys because "the invented ones were safer than the real ones; they always had been, at this table." And her "eating her revulsion" behind the sorority smile. So the poise I've watched her run on Vee is a survival skill built at that table, and the warmth-as-method now reads to me less like predation and more like the only fluent language she has. I felt sympathy I didn't expect. And the bracelet — "plain white gold, locked on her wrist," which she "kept out of the light" while her mother's tennis bracelet plays with the window — that detail landed hard. It's not jewelry. It's a mark she hides from her parents. Later on the dance floor it "caught on the heel of her hand, unable to be slid off." Somebody put that on her and it doesn't come off. I read Pace. It reads like a collar in white gold.

Then John. And here's the thing that made my skin prickle: John runs the *exact* play on Randi that Randi and Pace ran on Vee. He picks her out of a line, invents a shared story ("I'm with her," the fake wife, the three children packed off to a ski camp that doesn't exist), assumes the table, assumes the sleigh, assumes the room — "he had never once asked her where the night was going. He had assumed it." And Randi, who I know engineered a girl into her bed without that girl's knowledge, gets to be the one *inside* the engineered fairytale for once, and — chillingly — she *likes it.* "She found, tonight, that she liked not being asked. Pace made her say it, out loud, every time." That line put a cold hand on me. The whole book's ethic — Pace's "may I," the consent-every-time — Randi is here choosing its opposite and finding it a relief. It's the tagline turned inside out: *every yes freely given, that was the trap*, and Randi is walking into the pretty version of the trap on purpose, eyes open, because being asked is *work* and she's tired.

And then it hollows out exactly where I hoped it wouldn't. The sex is good and means nothing: "a good sandwich eaten fast on a busy afternoon: the pleasure real, complete, and gone before the plate was cleared." "She was warm only where she touched him... the cold did not belong to the room. It was hers. She had brought it in with her." That's beautiful and desolate. The lipstick ring she left on him — "a ring of her lipstick, where she had taken him in her mouth and felt her own mouth do it from a long way off" — dissociation rendered as a stain. And at the mirror: "Nothing had happened to her eyes." The eyes she armed for the room held; nothing reached them.

The gut-punch is the last third, where I finally understand what Randi *wants*, and it's Vee. Not as a project — as a person she's in love with and terrified of. "Something came off Vee across a table on a nothing Tuesday that this room could not counterfeit one thread of." She can run every scenario to the end except one: "she could not picture Vee's face while she said it." And so she decides, coldly, deliberately, to *not tell her* — "Let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were... as long as she never once said it out loud." That's Pace's rule again, mirrored: he won't say the word to Vee; Randi won't say the word to Vee. Both of them love this girl and both of them have decided the love survives only unspoken. The withheld name is a *house style* between these two.

The last beat — Vee texting "thinking about you 💛... i'm here if you want to talk. or not." and Randi answering "it's honestly a fairytale out here... i'm never coming home... miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋" — and the narration: "Every word of it true. None of it the thing." That flattened me. Vee is the only warm true thing in Randi's life and Randi is lying to her by telling her only true things. That's a very precise cruelty, and Randi knows it's cruelty, and does it to keep her.

**Where my feelings moved.** Randi moved the most she's moved all book. She's been the conspicuously-offstage operator I didn't trust; now she's the loneliest person in the book, and the "operating Vee" and "genuinely gone on Vee" that I'd held as two facts collapsed into one sad fact: she operates *because* she's gone, because the plain-true version costs more than she can pay. I trust her *motives* more and her *choices* less. She's just decided, on the page, to keep Vee in the dark forever. That's the same decision that's hurting Vee from Pace's side.

Pace, offstage, deepened by comparison. "Pace on his worst night reached further than this man had managed at his best." "Pace went straight to her. That was the whole terror of him, that he found the person she kept hidden and did not look away." So the thing I've been reading as Pace's coldness — the withheld word — Randi reads as *reach*, as the terror of being found. Interesting. It doesn't excuse the game, but it complicates my picture of what these two get from him.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Nothing new to *distrust* — this chapter is confession, not concealment, at least toward me. But it confirms the dread: the collision I've been fearing is now structurally locked, because Randi has *chosen* the lie and named her reasons. And there's a new dread — that Vee's love for Pace and Randi's love for Vee and Pace's ownership of Randi (that bracelet) are a triangle where every side is hidden from at least one other person.

**Erotic charge.** Deliberately low and clinical, and it's *supposed* to be — the point is that a technically perfect night leaves Randi cold. It's the anti-erotic chapter. The one place with heat is oblique: the dance, "his hand told her body where to go... she could have followed it in her sleep," the surrender of steering. That's sensual. The actual sex is summarized to nothing on purpose. Worked on me as *sadness*, not arousal, which I think is the intent.

**Friction.** Very little. The food set-piece (the smoke bubble, the foams, the truffle over venison and sour cherry) is gorgeous but ran a touch long for how much it's a metaphor-delivery-vehicle — illusions, foams that taste of "things she couldn't place until a second after they were gone," i.e. the whole night. I clocked the thesis under it. The only line that felt a hair like the thumb: "The flaw, if that was the word for it, was hers, carried into the room the same as the cold" — slightly over-articulated, though I forgave it because Randi *is* that self-analytical. Also, faintly, the "she walks in beauty" / "She walks in beauty" he can't finish — a little pat as romantic shorthand, but it's clearly meant to be his stock charm, so fine.

**Titles.** "Fairytale" — the chapter hands it to me twice: the whole night IS a fairytale (sleigh, bells, ball, prince in white tie, snow into her hair) and it's *empty*, and then Randi literally "gave her the fairytale," the lie she sends Vee. So the title names both the beautiful hollow night and the beautiful hollow lie. On reflection it's the cruelest possible title, because the real fairytale — the thing that's alive — is a nothing Tuesday brunch with Vee, and the "fairytale" is the counterfeit. The book title, *With a Long Spoon* — I still don't have a fixed read, but "sup with the devil, use a long spoon" keeps hovering: keep your distance from the thing you're feeding with. Randi tonight kept every man at spoon's length and felt nothing; the two people she *can't* keep at a distance (Pace, Vee) are the dangerous ones. The tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" got a new face here: Randi's yes to John was free and meaningless, and her yes to *keeping Vee in the dark* is the freely-chosen trap she's building for someone she loves.

**What I want / dread next.** I want back to Vee, and I dread it now double: she's home warm and loved, texting the truth into a phone that answers her with beautiful lies. Guess from here: the collision I keep dreading is going to come from Randi's side, not Pace's — some slip, or the bracelet, or a moment where Vee sees the two of them in the same frame. And I suspect this chapter is here so that when it breaks, I'll grieve for Randi too, not just Vee. I also want to know who locked that bracelet on her wrist and when.
