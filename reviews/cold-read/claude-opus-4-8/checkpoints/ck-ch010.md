### Story so far

Ch1 (The Bench): In Pace's warm house, a black-haired woman arrives, locks the door, straddles him, eats sliced apple. He undresses her slowly, places her on a bench he built and she'd mistaken for a table — a device that opens and exposes her. Through slow spanking and repeated edging he brings her past her practiced sexual performance until she truly begs ("please, please") in an unguarded voice, comes shattered, and weeps. He calls her beautiful; she flinches, locks herself in the bathroom, and privately resolves that being *seen* is unbearable — he can have "the face," but the real her is going back behind it forever. She returns smiling, lies that it was a lovely night, agrees to stay. He is tenderly happy, misreading it as her opening up. She is revealed as **Randi**.

Ch2 (Standards): Morning after, Randi slips into her sorority house in last night's clothes; chapter president Brooke, with Sloane and Kinsey, pleasantly interrogates her — the "paper," where she worked, Pace's name (math PhD, not her instructor). Randi manages the smiling cover perfectly, goes up to her single room, and lies fully dressed under an inadequate duvet, unable to cry, the crack from the bench sealed over.

Ch3 (The Pointing Game): Four weeks into Pace and Randi's affair, after sex, Pace asks if she'd like to taste another woman; her body says yes before she can dress it as "only in a threesome, with you." On a campus walk he plays a game — he points at women, she picks — steering past mirrors of herself, matched pairs, closed "projects," and room-runners, until a lush, red-haired girl laughing with her head thrown back comes out of the dining hall. Randi's "Yes" is bare and helpless. Pace sends her to engineer a "chance" collision; Randi executes it flawlessly, comes back glowing: the girl is **Vee**, and they share a stats class.

Ch4 (See You Later): Vee's POV, next day. She anxiously waits; Randi finds her in the stats lecture, sits between her and Cassie, lavishes warmth, and sweeps Vee (not Cassie, who declines with an oddly final "Goodbye, Vee") off to Chi Latte. Vee, dazzled, remembers only the feeling, not the words, and floats home having been "chosen."

Ch5 (Substitution): Studying at a coffee shop with Randi and Cassie, Vee is stuck on an integral. Pace crosses the room "for her," introduces himself as if a stranger (Randi coolly gives only her name — they pretend not to know each other), and tutors Vee by handing the pencil back so the solving stays hers (Euler's substitution). He listens to her econ dreams, asks "May I call you?", takes her number, and leaves without his coffee. Randi and Cassie tell Vee he plainly wants her.

Ch6 (The Long Way): Pace texts within the hour; that evening they get frozen yogurt and walk the long way. He asks and waits; Vee confesses it's the kindest anyone's been to her in a long time. He lets the true thing stand.

Ch7 (Water Wings): Vee and Cassie at the rec pool. Vee runs a bleacher gauntlet of catcalls with her "face" on; underwater she finds her old weightless refuge and grieves; Cassie breaks it with a splash-fight, restoring her.

Ch8 (May I Choose): Saturday. Pace, freshly showered and in his own good worn things, walks Vee to a tiny Middle Eastern place run by Christine, who knows him. Unable to read the menu, Vee lets him choose ("May I choose?"); he feeds her a slow feast and she eats unwatched, drops her guard entirely, loses track of herself, and is fully present for once. He takes her hand walking home; under a moonlit tree she recites her rule — she doesn't kiss a man she's known days — he accepts it and lets it go; then she stops him, closes the distance herself, and makes "an exception."

Ch9 (Off Six-Fourteen): Back in the dorm, Vee spills it all to Cassie — the tea, the kiss she initiated, insisting nobody talked her into it, she wanted to. Cassie, teasing everywhere but here, confirms "that's a man." Vee has said yes to a hike; she knows only that Pace lives west of town off Route 614.

Ch10 (Dear): Stats again; Cassie leaves as Randi claims Vee. At the Smoothie Bird, Vee (cardigan over the better shirt, feeling underdressed) tells Randi everything — the "May I choose," the going-soft, the kiss "because of him." Brooke and a silent taller woman arrive; Randi publicly calls Vee "my dear friend" and holds her hand on the table in front of them. Vee, half-cowed by the finished women, feels claimed and picked, and walks home warm, sure her place is now beside Randi.

### Impression

**Vee**: I'm wholly on her side and a little afraid for her — warm, hungry, decent, finally letting herself want, and completely unaware she's the target of a coordinated game. Her joy is real and earned-feeling; that's exactly what makes it ache. **Pace**: genuinely magnetic — courtly, attentive, the consent-first care disarms me the way it disarms them — and that is precisely what unsettles me: the same exact attentiveness is the instrument of a con, and I can't yet tell where the tenderness stops and the operation begins. High wariness sitting right on top of real pull. **Randi**: the most tragic figure so far — a woman who was cracked open and fled her own feelings, now running a seduction that seems to be turning real on her against her will. I distrust what she's doing and pity what it's doing to her, both at full strength. **Cassie**: the one clean, trustworthy note — no reason at all to doubt her; she's the tuning fork for how wrong this could go. Overall: seduced and uneasy in the same breath, which appears to be the point.
