### Story so far
**Ch1 (The Bench).** In his warm house, Pace receives Randi — his established partner. He feeds her apple slices; she straddles him; he leads her to a bench he built and hid under a tabletop. Facedown, he spanks and edges her at length, refusing to let her come until she begs in a raw, unperformed voice — until "*her*" real self appears. She comes, weeps, then locks herself in his bathroom and looks at her face in the mirror: she has been more alive, and more seen, than ever, and decides she cannot bear it — she will give him "the face" and never come out again. She returns smiling, lies well, and stays the night. Pace, tender and pleased, respects the door she closed (recalling Daphne, whose door he once forced).

**Ch2 (Standards).** Saturday morning, Randi slips back into her sorority house in last night's clothes. Brooke, warmly and from a seated position of authority, interrogates her — the "paper," where she worked, Pace's name, that he's a math PhD (not her section). Sloane and Kinsey chime in. Randi manages every beat, then goes to her single room, lies on her made bed in last night's clothes, and does not cry.

**Ch3 (The Pointing Game).** Randi asks Pace for "something different"; in bed he asks if she'd like to taste another woman — her body answers *yes* helplessly before she reframes it as a gift ("only in a threesome, with you"). On a campus walk he plays a game: he points at women, she picks. She rejects the mirrors, the matched pairs, the room-runners — then a red-haired girl (Vee) comes laughing out of the dining hall and Randi's *yes* is bare and instant. Pace sends her to meet her; Randi engineers a "collision," gets the girl's name and that they share a stats class. "Do you think she'll like the bench?"

**Ch4 (See You Later).** Vee's POV of the day after: giddy over the collision, she waits to see if Randi shows to stats. Randi does — sits beside her, dazzling and warm, meets the blunt Cassie. After class Randi sweeps Vee to Chi Latte, the high table, an hour Vee can't reconstruct but glows over: "love you, girl," "see you later." Cassie's odd flat "Goodbye, Vee."

**Ch5 (Substitution).** Studying at the place on Wilson (Randi, Vee, Cassie), Vee stuck on a calculus problem. Pace crosses the room — eyes only on Vee — and offers help. He tutors her by *giving back the pencil* (Euler's/substitution), listens to her econ dreams, answers Randi's stats question briefly and Vee's at length, asks "May I call you?", takes her number and leaves without his coffee. Randi and Cassie both note he looked only at Vee.

**Ch6 (The Long Way).** He texts within twenty minutes; the friends push Vee to go. That afternoon: froyo, his overflowing cup she doesn't check, the long unhurried walk, his patient listening; she confesses it's the nicest anyone's been to her in a long time.

**Ch7 (Tannin).** That evening Randi comes to Pace's, locks the deadbolt, straddles him, and demands every detail of the froyo date — feeding him wine from her own mouth. Her want to hear about Vee is huge and held tight behind her face; jealousy flickers when he says Vee is "easy to listen to." He has no wine himself; he can taste nothing else.

**Ch8 (Water Wings).** Vee and Cassie at the rec pool. Vee runs a gauntlet of bleacher catcalls with her game face; underwater she finds her old weightless refuge, grieves a little, then surfaces into a splash-fight Cassie starts to pull her back. The body that's "a problem" waits on the deck.

**Ch9 (May I Choose).** Friday: Pace texts a Saturday dinner invitation and leaves the choice with her — which moves her. He picks her up, freshly showered/shaved, in his real good clothes; walks her to Christine's small Mediterranean place. Overwhelmed by the menu, she lets him choose ("May I choose?" — "Yes. Please."). She eats freely, unwatched, for once fully present; mint tea in brass cages. He takes her hand walking back — it feels like something *resolving*. Under a tree she recites her rule ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"); he lets it go without pressure — and she pulls him back and makes the exception, kissing him, then leaning in for more.

**Ch10 (Off Six-Fourteen).** Vee tells Cassie everything, glowing — especially that *she* initiated the kiss, sober and on purpose, "nobody did anything to me." She's giddy about a hiking date; realizes she's never seen his house (off 614 past the gravel) and doesn't yet know if he's rich — filed happily under things to find out. Cassie, glad but dry, tells her to borrow good shoes.

### Impression
I'm charmed and uneasy in exactly the split the book wants. **Pace** reads as genuinely tender and attentive, and I find myself trusting the care even while I hold the fact that his gentleness is aimed at a target chosen in advance — the courtesy and the calculation are the same gestures, and I can't yet tell how much is real; I lean toward "more real than he planned," but I'm watching. **Randi** is the one I feel most for and most wary of — a woman running a cruel-tender game on a friend while quietly starving behind her own perfect face; her jealousy and her buried self make her human, not just a manipulator. **Vee** I like without reservation and fear for: open, hungry, coming alive, handing over trust freely — and every "yes" of hers is one the reader knows more about than she does. **Cassie** is the instrument of my unease, the one clear-eyed voice; I trust her instincts more than anyone's. No one has been cruel to Vee's face — that's what makes it land. Right now: warm toward all three principals, and quietly bracing for the cost.
