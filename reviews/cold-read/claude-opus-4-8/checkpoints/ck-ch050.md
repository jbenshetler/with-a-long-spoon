### Story so far
**Randi & Pace, established (Ch 1–2).** Pace and Randi are secret lovers in a D/S relationship. In his warm house he takes Randi over a custom-built bench, edging and spanking her until her performed "face" cracks and she begs in a real, raw voice — "There you are, Randi." Shaken at being *seen*, she resolves in his bathroom mirror never to come out from behind the face again — he can have the face; not her. Back at the sorority, Brooke and the sisters gently interrogate Randi about "Pace," whom the house believes is her boyfriend. Randi lies smoothly about a paper and a study spot, then lies awake in her cold room, not crying.

**The game / choosing Vee (Ch 3).** In bed, Pace asks Randi whether she'd like to taste another woman; her body answers yes before she can dress it as a gift "for him." On a campus walk he plays a "pointing game," letting Randi choose a woman — she passes over the polished mirrors of herself and stops helplessly at a laughing redhead outside the dining hall. Pace points: "Her." Randi engineers a "collision," learns the girl is **Vee**, and that they share a stats class.

**Courtship (Ch 4–17).** Randi seats herself beside Vee in stats and lavishes warmth on her; they become inseparable (Chi Latte, the smoothie place, brunches). Meanwhile Pace "happens" into Vee's coffee shop, helps with a calculus problem (Euler's formula), and asks to call her. He courts her with froyo, a Middle Eastern dinner ("May I choose?"), a mountain hike (the flannel shirt in the rain; a campus-cop interruption), and dancing lessons. Vee falls hard; she initiates their first kiss under a tree (Ch 8). Randi becomes the confidante Vee tells everything to, drawing her steadily open; Randi's mouth-kiss goodbyes begin.

**The dress & first sex (Ch 14–18).** Pace, remembering Vee's offhand wish for an unaffordable magazine dress, buys burgundy/plum silk and makes it *to* her — measuring her nude on a riser, an erotically charged fitting. Vee and Pace consummate the relationship (Ch 18, "Famished"), then a pattern of frequent, unhurried, self-last sex sets in.

**Deepening & kink (Ch 19–35).** Randi teases Vee's confessions further open at brunches; Vee's body responds to Randi even as she explains the kisses away. A spanking dynamic emerges (Ch 29, 32): Pace "checks," wins a bet, and Vee discovers how much she wants it. On Halloween she models the dress and then her bare self for him (Ch 33). Over a fireside night (Ch 25) Pace tells her his real name is Peter, that he skipped ahead to college at 15, was punished for loving math (desk in the hall), and lost Daphne — a story he frames as luck, not harm; Vee glimpses the wound and the scar she doesn't ask about. He gives her a bathroom drawer of her own (Ch 35). Vee privately admits she loves him (Ch 35–36) but won't say it first.

**The induction & Rusty Nail (Ch 22–31).** At country dancing, a blonde stranger insinuates Pace beds the women he escorts home; Vee lashes out, humiliates him publicly, and learns from the bartender that the jealousy was aimed at *her*. Randi coaches her recovery (loans her the cognac heels, the scarf), and Vee apologizes with a "humble pie" — Pace lifts the whole weight off her. Vee wears the finished dress to her honor-society induction (Pace away at a Berkeley conference), walking the stage bare beneath the silk, and sends him the photo.

**The near-declaration & the break (Ch 43–46).** Vee tells Pace her "crackers/a meal" line; he answers "I love how good we are together" — warm, but not the word. It lodges as an unhealed gap. Their public/private tension festers (Chili's, Ch 44). Finally (Ch 46) Vee comes to his door and asks flat-out what she is to him and how she'd name this to anyone; he offers "believe what I do," "words are cheap," and holds his line. She cries and leaves.

**Meanwhile (Ch 40, 47–50, the break).** A stats group project (Vee, Randi, Cassie) surfaces the PPP-loan fraud tied to Randi's father — the outlier "MIRANDA"; Vee sees it, says nothing, and shields Randi. Over New Year's: Randi, in Gstaad on her father's money, takes a faultless man (John) to bed and feels nothing — realizing Pace and Vee both reach the self she hides, and that she loves Vee but is too afraid to lose her to confess. Vee lies alone in her outgrown childhood bedroom, uncertain whether she and Pace are over, tormented by imagined rivals, missing them both. Pace has diner cheeseburgers with Sheri, who tells him his withholding "feels pretty shitty"; the next morning, hungover and grieving, he mistakes a passing truck for Vee's car, breaks down, and force-feeds himself.

### Impression
- **Vee:** I'm wholly with her — tender toward her waking appetite and how hard-won her willingness to be seen is. My unease isn't about her; it's *for* her. She's inside a beautiful thing that two people built and pointed at her, and the sincerest parts of it (Pace's love, Randi's love) are real, which makes the deception worse, not better. Her instinct that her feelings have "gotten out ahead of the facts" is right for a reason she can't see.
- **Pace:** I believe he loves her; I ache a little at his Daphne-shaped silence. But I distrust him structurally — he's a co-author of a secret plan and a secret second relationship, and his "believe what I do" is both true and a way of never being accountable. Warmth and real menace share the same hands.
- **Randi:** the most dangerous and the most moving. Her care for Vee reads as genuine now, and her cowardice (won't confess, won't stop the game) is exactly what will hurt Vee most. I trust her least and pity her most.
- **Cassie:** the one clean instrument in the book; no reason to doubt her. She's my proxy for the plmain world Vee is drifting from.
- Overall: suspicion sits squarely on the architecture, not on any single tender moment — and the tenderness is real, which is the trap the jacket promised.
