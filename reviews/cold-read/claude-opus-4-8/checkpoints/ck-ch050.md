### What I know that they don't

- **Vee does not know Pace and Randi are a couple.** The reader has known since ch 1. (Using side AND caring side: they conspired to bring her in; they have also both fallen for her for real.)
- **Vee does not know she was chosen as a game.** The quad "pointing game" (ch 3) selected her; the collision with Randi and the coffee-shop "rescue" by Pace were engineered. The jacket's promise — "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one."
- **Vee does not know Randi is in love with her.** Reader saw Randi's full interior admission (ch 46): she loves Vee both ways, can't run the scenario where she tells her, chooses to keep the word covered.
- **Randi does not know Vee saw "Miranda Holdings."** Vee read the PPP-fraud record (ch 39), recognized it, and deliberately hid that she'd seen it — a protective secret Vee keeps *for* Randi.
- **Pace does not know how completely Vee has fallen** in the specific shape of needing the word — though ch 42/50 he's close. Sheri and the reader both see he is "half-starved" for Vee and not with anyone else in the way Vee fears.
- **Vee's fear of "someone else" (Sheri) is false** — the reader knows Sheri is only a friend; the "blonde model" rival is Vee's 3-a.m. invention.
- **Caring side, broadly:** the reader knows both Pace and Randi genuinely, achingly love Vee — not only that they engineered her. The heat is real "for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew there was one."

### Story so far

The book opens (ch 1, "The Bench") on an unnamed man and woman in his too-warm house: he undresses her with ceremony, spanks and edges her on a purpose-built bench until her practiced "please" breaks into a real one, and she comes apart and weeps — and we learn, in the last line, she is **Randi**. In the bathroom she resolves to go back behind "the face" and never let him have the real her again; she is his established, secret partner. Ch 2: Randi slips back into her sorority house in last night's clothes, deflecting Brooke's polite interrogation about "Pace," and lies in her cold room, not crying.

Ch 3 ("The Pointing Game"): in bed, Pace asks Randi if she'd like to taste another woman; her body says yes before she can dress it as a gift "for them." On a campus walk he points at women and she picks — rejecting mirrors, matched pairs, closed projects — until he points at a laughing redhead: Vee. Randi engineers a "collision," gets her name and a shared stats class. The two of them have chosen her.

From ch 4 the story follows **Vee**, who believes she met Randi by accident and is dazzled — the right pen, Chi Latte, the high table, "love you, girl," "see you later." Ch 5 ("Substitution"): at the coffee shop Pace "happens" to help Vee with a calculus problem (the Euler's-formula lesson), asks "May I call you," and leaves without his coffee; Cassie notes he looked at Randi "like somebody's great-aunt." The courtship proceeds at Pace's unhurried, choosing-centered pace: froyo (ch 6), the pool with Cassie (ch 7), the "May I choose" dinner and Vee's first kiss, which she initiates against her own rule (ch 8–9). The hike (ch 11, "Leave No Trace"): the summit, the horseradish sandwich, cherries, cherry-pit ethics; caught in cold rain, Pace gives Vee his only dry shirt and stands out in the rain so she can change; she knocks him back in, strips to her wet bra and lets him look — and the campus cop's siren ends the night.

Vee narrates each beat to Cassie (the clean version) and increasingly to Randi (further, but never the "middle"). Ch 14–16: at Pace's house she discovers he built the furniture, licenses an MRI patent, and made the flannel shirt; he offers to make her the burgundy silk gown she'd once wistfully described. Ch 15 ("A Round"): the fitting — he measures her nearly nude on a riser he built, patient and admiring; her body betrays her (soaked satin); when she finally opens her eyes he's smiling up at her, having never once looked down. She starts "spending" a new power; he pins on, choosing to do it right.

Ch 17 ("How It's Done"): the first lunch — Randi, in the green-velvet restaurant, draws out that Pace measured Vee braless, that it was "hot," edging the confession, then softly closing the door before Vee can give up her deepest secret; goodbye kiss on the mouth. This becomes the rhythm: brunches (ch 20, 34, 37) where Randi extracts Vee's sexual life and works her to the edge, sometimes till Vee flees to a bathroom (ch 32, 34). Ch 18 ("Famished"): Vee and Pace first sleep together; the shame "runs the whole length" and never closes her. She trades him her worn panties.

The relationship deepens: the finished dress and its hip-slit reveal (ch 21); Randi buys Vee the expensive strappy heels the dress "is owed," discovering they share a shoe size (ch 23). Ch 24 ("Gone"): Randi, post-sex with Pace, tells him how much she unexpectedly likes Vee; Pace, of Vee, "she comes in and the whole thing goes brighter" — and something tightens in Randi. Ch 25 ("Rock"): the long fireside talk — Pace's history (college at 15, exiled to the hallway for being gifted, the tutor **Daphne**, the untold thigh scar, real name **Peter**); Vee says "Peter" to keep it.

Ch 26 ("Lesson"): country-dancing — Pace taught the class; a jealous blonde needles Vee ("all us girls love him"), and Vee lashes out, accusing him of "getting all the girls home safe." He leaves; the older bartender tells her the girls were jealous of *her*. Ch 27–29: Randi coaches Vee to apologize "sure, not sorry," dresses her, lends shoes/scarf; Vee brings "HUMBLE PIE," apologizes, and they reconcile — the dish-washing tickle escalates into the first swat (ch 29, "how his hand landed"). Ch 28 ("hills-and-valleys"): Pace and Sheri cycle; Sheri clocks that he's "gone" on Vee and juggling "two of them at once."

Ch 30–32: the spanking thread blooms — Randi plants the "mouse that roars"/over-the-knee story (ch 30) that leaves Vee wet in a practice room; Vee wears the dress (and nothing under it) across the induction stage on purpose, sending Pace the uncropped photo (ch 31); ch 32 ("We Find Out") is the full first spanking — Pace bets her that she enjoys it, "checks," she loses, and is undone and then treasured. Photos (ch 37) and the Halloween "two costumes" reveal (ch 33) follow. Ch 35 ("Above Him"): she realizes she loves him. Ch 38 ("All the Time"): to Randi she confesses the sheer *amount* — "sex brain" — and shows both the cropped and uncropped photos; a story lands hard on Randi ("It doesn't," low and uneven).

Ch 39 ("The Outlier"): the stats group project (Vee, Randi, Cassie) on PPP loans. Cassie's father was laid off in 2020; her thesis (the money went up, jobs went down) is borne out. Randi plots her own county to complicate it — and hits a lone outlier: **Miranda Holdings, LLC**, her father's fraud. Randi goes white ("Cheating bastards"); Cassie, unable to see the screen, takes it as agreement. **Vee sees, recognizes it, and hides that she saw** — protecting Randi.

Ch 40–42: the "new ordinary" — the drawer, the re-scented shirt, and ch 42 ("Seconds") the near-miss declaration: Vee offers her built sentence ("I had been eating crackers my whole life, and you fed me a meal"); Pace answers "I love how good we are together" — *not the same shape*. She files it and won't raise it again.

The fracture (ch 43–45): Vee pushes — "Is this what we are now?", then "What am I to you?" Pace: "You're the most important person in my life," "Words are cheap. Believe what I do" — but never the word. She walks out into the cold; he lets her go; silence holds through the December break.

Ch 46 ("Fairytale"): Randi at Gstaad meets **John**, a "perfect" man — better skier, better dancer, the whole approved life — sleeps with him and feels nothing; her long interior reckoning: Pace "goes straight to her"; Vee gives off something the perfect room can't counterfeit; she loves Vee both ways but won't risk telling her, chooses to keep the word covered. Ch 47: Vee, home in Ohio, mirrors her — sleepless, doomscrolling Randi's fairytale, building the false "Sheri" rival. Ch 48 ("The Usual"): Pace and Sheri at the diner; Sheri presses him — "she wants you to say it" — "I can't"; "then not saying it matters more to you than having her."

Ch 49–50: Vee, wrecked off the red-eye, goes to Randi, who reframes everything (the silence is a proud boy holding still, not a man done with her; the rival has "no face") and prescribes the cure: **show up in the good coat with nothing underneath**, no words. Ch 50 ("Nothing Underneath"): Vee prepares herself at length, edges herself and stops (twice — once at the mirror, once driving, nearly caught by a passing cruiser), and arrives at Pace's dark door in only heels and a coat. She opens it; his wall drops; they reconcile without words — sex on the doorstep and couch, then midnight pancakes, him on one knee, voice cracking, "I missed you so much." Still no "I love you," but she is full enough not to need it tonight. She returns to the dorm Sunday in his sweats and Randi's heels; Cassie takes it in with one "Hmm."

### Impression

I'm charmed by Vee and ache for her — she's warm, funny, starving in exactly the way she can't name, and I want her happy; my sympathy is total and my worry is sharp, because she is being loved and handled at the same time and can't see the handling. Pace reads as genuinely tender, careful, almost unbearably attentive — and I trust the care while distrusting the withholding: a man who can build a woman a place in a drawer but not say four words, who chose her by a game she'll never suspect, sits right on the line between devotion and control, and the untold thigh scar and the estranged parents keep me uneasy. Randi is the one I watch hardest: I believe she loves Vee now, and that belief is what makes her most dangerous — every brunch is affection and reconnaissance at once, and she is steering Vee toward something Vee thinks she chose. The bench opening still colors everything: these two took each other apart in private and then went looking for a third. I have no reason to doubt that the feeling is real on all sides — the book keeps insisting the trap became true for the trappers too — which is precisely what unsettles me. Cassie is my one safe harbor; when she's wary, I'm wary. Right now I'm tender toward Vee, half-seduced (as promised) by Pace's care, and quietly braced for the moment Vee learns what the reader has known since page one.
