# Cold read — Leave No Trace

*scene: scenes/leave-no-trace.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: dear*

## Reader reaction

Oh, I'd been *waiting* for this hike, and it did not go where I braced for. I expected the house off 614 — the bench, the apparatus. Instead the trail comes straight out of the gravel and up, and the whole chapter is on the mountain and in the truck cab. The house stays unseen, which I notice, and I half-love that the book keeps withholding it.

Let me start with my body, because the last third of this chapter did something to me the brunches never have. The undressing in the cab is the most genuinely erotic thing I've read so far, and what makes it hot is that *she* runs it. She raps the glass, she crooks the finger, she keeps his eyes on hers and takes the buttons by feel — "She didn't have to look down for the fourth. She could have taken it by feel like the others and kept his eyes the whole way. / She looked down anyway, and his eyes went with hers." That line got me. The heat is all in the *authorship* — after ch.9/10 where Randi kept stealing the kiss-claim and reframing Vee's agency into helpless surrender, here Vee does a thing that is unmistakably, unstealably *hers*. He doesn't lean in. He doesn't lift a hand. "the gladness in him was the whole of the answer." That restraint is what made it work on me — a man being handed something and just *receiving* it, shaking with cold, not converting it. Peak charge: her hands on the buttons with her eyes on his. It went slack nowhere in that scene.

And the shame engine is doing its thing but on a *new* indignity — not the body announcing itself at dinner, but her body wanting to *be the floozy the voice names*, wanting him to watch her be it. "the heat that knew the voice was right, that wanted to be the very thing the voice said." That's the ownership-of-chosen-shame idea landing in the flesh instead of in a thesis. It's fresh because the occasion is fresh: wet, cold, three dates, a truck. The mother's voice hasn't gone quiet — it's become the fuel directly. That's escalation by kind, and I felt it.

Now — Pace. This is the most I've *liked* him, and it worries me, because his tenderness is real and instrumental at once and I'm not supposed to be able to see the seam. And I can't. The boot fix is gorgeous caretaking — the moleskin warmed with his thumb, "a pat that meant only *done*," "Should've done that at the lot. I'm sorry. I didn't think to ask about the boots." He apologizes for a real lapse. The horseradish sandwich, the shared water bottle "where her mouth had been without making a thing of it." And then the flannel — his only dry shirt, given to a girl in a truck while he walks back out into the rain and takes it full-on with his back to her. *And* he parks in the fire lane so she won't cross the rain.

Here's where my reader-suspicion actually stirs, and I want to be precise that the *text* earned it, not my paranoia: the flannel gesture is *too* perfectly legible as sacrifice. "His only dry shirt... and he'd put it in her hands instead." He stands in the rain with his back to her — a tableau of visible self-denial, staged where she'll see it and read it. The chapter even walks her through decoding it: "For a moment it made no sense to her. Then she looked down at the flannel in her lap. His shirt, and then the truck. Both." That's a man who knows exactly what a woman will feel watching him suffer for her, standing where she can watch. I can't prove it's calculated — he *is* genuinely shivering, his hand really does shake in hers, and that shiver is the thing that stops her from escalating. The cold is real. But the *placement* of the cold — back turned, in view, for "nothing she could see" until she decodes it — is the exact shape of the thing I've learned to distrust. And I love that I can't tell. That's the trap working as designed on me.

The dress moment is the other thing I'm turning over. She points at the russet silk in the magazine — "like the red of a brick. That kind of red. It just looked like the dress. Like the one" — and then puts it back on the shelf herself, the pointing game again (agency and captivity the same act). And he doesn't buy it, doesn't offer, doesn't even pick it up: "That sounds like a beautiful dress." That was the most restrained he's been and I trusted it *least* precisely because of that. He filed it. I would bet — *guess from here* — that russet dress shows up at the induction, provided by someone, and the not-reaching-for-it now is him letting her set the price so he can pay it later without it looking like taking. The bill motif is all over this: she keeps *setting things back on the shelf*, and the book keeps letting me feel a cost being deferred.

"Leave no trace" — the cherry pit. He corrects her without shaming her, lays his own pit beside hers, doesn't look. "She had it now. She had it and he hadn't made her small to give it to her." That's the exact opposite of what Randi did at the Smoothie Bird, and the contrast is deliberate enough that I felt it: Pace teaches without shrinking her; Randi and Brooke shrink her while calling it care. But the title sits on my chest — *leave no trace* is wilderness ethic, and it's also what a person does who doesn't want to be caught having been somewhere. He folds every scrap of wax paper into the pack. He takes nothing and leaves nothing. On the mountain that's virtue. Applied to a man running a plan on a girl, it's chilling. The title detonated for me the second the cop lights came on — the one time there *is* a trace (an illegal park), authority arrives, and he's *gone*, taillights down the curve, taking her half-sentence with him. He leaves no trace of himself at her dorm. She's left holding his flannel that smells "of soap and of a man and of the inside of a gym bag" — the only trace, and it's one he'll want back.

The cop timing bugged me the tiniest bit as craft — it's a convenient way to cut off her declaration ("The thing she wanted to say was bigger than any words she had") right before she says it, so the big thing stays unsaid. But it didn't feel like the author's thumb so much as of a piece with the withholding this whole book runs on. And "the fire lane, the red-painted curb" — *red* — the safeword color, sitting under the tires. Probably me seeing ghosts, but the book has trained me to.

One small friction: "The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for." That's the closest the chapter comes to narrating its own mechanism — telling me the hunger was engineered. It's in her POV as a nice realization, so it mostly reads as her noticing, but it brushed against the "don't explain the machine" line and I clocked it.

What I want next: the induction dinner, and whether that russet dress arrives. Cassie — she's completely absent here and I miss her; the last I saw she was being subtracted, and a whole radiant day with Pace and no Cassie to come home to worries me. And I still want the *house*. Three dates and we've been to a coffee shop, a dinner, a mountain — never where the bench is. Every date is the long way around to it. I'm starting to dread the first time Vee crosses *that* threshold, because this chapter just proved how completely she'll walk in on her own two feet and call it hers.
