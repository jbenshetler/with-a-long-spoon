# Cold read — Leave No Trace

*scene: scenes/leave-no-trace.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: dear*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me in the body before I could think, which I suspect is exactly the point, and it left me a little wrecked in a way I want to be honest about.

Let me start with the heat, because it's the center of the chapter and it's the best-executed erotic scene in the book so far. The undressing in the truck is extraordinary. What makes it land isn't skin — it's that **she** does it. He's outside in the rain when she decides. She raps on the glass, crooks a finger, and he doesn't open the door until she calls him. Then: *"her hands went to the top button of her shirt"* and she holds his eyes the whole way down. *"She didn't have to look down for the fourth. She could have taken it by feel like the others and kept his eyes the whole way. She looked down anyway, and his eyes went with hers."* That's the line that got me — the deliberate looking-down, taking him with her. It's authorship rendered as an erotic act, and it's hot precisely because nobody is doing it *to* her. And Pace's restraint is almost unbearable: *"He didn't lean in. He didn't lift a hand. He sat and looked at all of her and didn't move, and the gladness in him was the whole of the answer."* The heat peaks there and holds. Then the turn — she reaches for his hand and it's **shaking**, he's freezing, he gave her his only dry shirt and stood in the rain — and the scene pivots from her wanting to be taken to her wanting to warm him. That reversal is genuinely tender and it undid me a little.

The shame engine is running hot and it stayed fresh. The mother-voice is louder and cruder here than I've heard it — *"floozy" in her mother's exact word,* *"what kind of girl lets a man,"* *"Three dates and you'd — your mother"* — and the new move is that the voice and the heat have **fused**: *"the voice was the heat and the heat was the voice, the two run together inside her now."* She wants to be the exact thing the voice condemns, *wanted him watching her be it.* That's the wanting-she'd-be-ashamed-to-name from the jacket, made flesh. It's the strongest version of Vee's shame the book has given me and it didn't re-light an old occasion — it found a new one (wet, cold, half-undressed, watched).

Pace. God. He is the most dangerous thing in this book and this chapter is *why*. Every single move is care: the moleskin on the hot spot, warming it with his thumb; *"I'm sorry. I didn't think to ask about the boots"*; lettuce against the bread so it doesn't go through; the cherry pit — *"We take nothing and leave nothing"* — and crucially he lays his own pit next to hers **without looking at her doing it**, so the correction never shames her. The dress: she sets the russet silk down, puts it back on the shelf, and he doesn't pick it up, just *"That sounds like a beautiful dress"* and lets it lie. Parking in the fire lane so she won't cross the rain. He is, on the page, a perfect man. And I don't trust a single molecule of it, and the chapter gives me almost nothing to point at — which is the horror. The one place my ear pricked: *"The hike had made her hungry first. That was the trick of it. That was what it was for."* That "the trick of it / what it was for" is the closest the prose comes to letting me see machinery — a man who engineers hunger so the feeding lands. It's ambiguous (it could just be Vee noticing the pleasant logic of exercise-then-food), but after ten chapters of watching this exact pattern — policed hunger, then permitted feeding — it read to me as the tell. He *made* her hungry and then handed her the food. That's the whole book in a sandwich.

Where's the cold in this warm chapter? It's in the **dry shirt**. Read once, it's the most selfless thing anyone's done for her — his only dry shirt, and he takes the rain himself. But he *had a dry shirt behind the seat the whole time.* He packed for after. The gesture is real and it's also a resource he brought, deployed at the perfect moment, and it produces in her the exact thing — *"I want him to see me"* — that the whole apparatus wants. I can't tell if he planned to give it to her or genuinely sacrificed. The chapter is built so I can't tell. That not-being-able-to-tell is the tenderness-real-AND-instrumental thing, and it's doing its job on me: I fell for him in this chapter even while my ledger screams.

Vee: my whole heart, wider open than ever. She's not passive here — she's the *author* of the erotic turn, which is new and which I loved seeing. And that's the trap tightening, isn't it. The more she authors, the more freely-given every yes is, the deeper in she goes, and she has no idea there's a game. Cassie's boots (the half-size-too-big borrowed boots that rubbed her raw) are almost a little rebuke from the absent true friend — the borrowed thing that didn't fit, that Pace then fixed.

The friction: honestly very little. This is the most assured chapter in a while. The prose occasionally leans on "push and lift / push and drop" repetition to the point I noticed the device, but it earned the rhythm of the climb. The only thumb I felt was that "trick of it" line, and I'm genuinely unsure it's a thumb versus an intended tell. The cop at the end is a great, deflating beat — it snatches the sentence out of her mouth (*"the small thing was the one that fit"* — again her habit of putting a small word where a big feeling goes) and it's the one uncontrolled element in a day Pace ran perfectly. The universe interrupting the man who leaves no trace.

**The title.** *Leave No Trace* — surface is the hiking ethic, said aloud on the page (*"We take nothing and leave nothing"*), pack out your cherry pits. The charge detonated hard for me on the walk home: **Pace leaves no trace.** No evidence, no trail, no fingerprints on the seduction — he never leans in, never lifts a hand, never picks up the dress, never makes her small; everything she does she does herself, so there's nothing to point back at him. He can pack out every pit. The one thing that *did* leave a trace is the cop's lights — the one moment he lost control. And there's a colder read waiting: whatever's really being done to her, it's designed to leave no mark she could ever find. That's a title that deepened the second I finished, and it slightly recolored the whole tender day.

**The book title and tagline.** *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil, keep your distance — and this chapter is the one where Vee throws the long spoon away. She's the one who leans across the bench. *"Every yes was freely given. That was the trap."* — I have never felt that tagline more than here. The undressing is the freest, most self-authored yes yet, and that's exactly what makes it the trap. The more it's hers, the more caught she is. The jacket promised "asking before he takes and taking only what she gives" — this chapter is that line dramatized to perfection, and knowing (from the jacket) that it's a *trap* made the tenderness read as dread underneath the arousal. That double vision is the whole experience of reading this book right now.

**What I want and dread next.** I want to know what he does with what happened — do we get his POV, and does the gladness in the cab have any calculation visible in it, or does the book keep withholding his interior. I dread the induction dinner, because the russet dress got set down so carefully that I'm certain (guess from here) it's going to come back — someone will buy it or produce it, and I'd bet on Randi, because a gift like that is exactly her currency, and it would mean Randi and Pace's channels touched Vee's life through the same object without her seeing the seam. I also keep waiting for the taste thread and for Randi and Pace to become *connected* in Vee's mind, and I still think that won't happen until it's catastrophic. And I miss Cassie, whose boots just rubbed a hole in Vee's heel — I want the true friend back in the room.
