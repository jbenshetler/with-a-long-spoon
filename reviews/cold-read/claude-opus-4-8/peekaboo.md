# Cold read — Peekaboo

*scene: scenes/peekaboo.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: fed*

## Reader reaction

This is the first chapter in a while that let me sit inside a pure high with no one else in the room — no Randi, no brunch, no harvest. Just Vee and Pace and the dress. And I have to be honest about the split in me: on the page, as an experience, it *worked* on me. The dress reveal is genuinely beautiful. The line that got me is the same one that got her — "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like it had cost him nothing." That's exquisite, and it's exactly the thing I've been carrying since he first said "so it stays." The made object with no visible seam. The gift engineered to read as effortless. She cries at the effort *hidden*, which is precisely the mechanism I've been dreading — and dread and pleasure sat right on top of each other for me the whole time.

The erotic charge here is the strongest and cleanest the book has given me. The undressing-into-it sequence — the gown coming down "like water finding its level," catching at the hips, the lined bodice, "a soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" — is sensual and specific without going clinical. And then the two-step with the slit and the underwear is the real peak: her first thought to keep her steps small so it won't show, the second thought coming "up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed," stripping herself bare *on purpose to be looked at*. That's a genuine turn, and it landed on me as arousal before I could get analytical about it. Then the stride to him — "the length of her given and taken back and given" — beautiful rhythm.

But here's where my dread runs cold under all that gold. Everything she experiences as *her own agency* in this chapter is the exact shape of the hook I've been tracking. She takes the edge away herself. She's "the one who'd taken the edge away." She strides instead of the careful walk. And the chapter closes on "getting away with something." I've watched Randi do this to her — convert passivity into "I took it. For once." Now the dress does it without anyone in the room needing to speak. The garment itself performs the con: it's cut so the shame flares ("*Floozy*") and then converts to wanting, and she reads the conversion as her own liberation. The dress *is* the operator now. He didn't have to be present to run it; he built the run into the object and left her alone with a mirror he'd hung, screw heads still bright.

That mirror. He set the pedestal in front of a full-length mirror "that hadn't been there before." He staged the whole reveal — the light, the height, the glass — so that she would do to herself, alone, exactly what she did. "Being looked-at as reward" has graduated: he's arranged for her to look at herself and want. And the closing daydream is the tell that chilled me most — she imagines wearing it into the *induction*, arriving late on purpose, letting the room turn, "and the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." That last line is the whole trap in seven words. What she was *for*. She's naming herself as an object to be looked at and calling it belonging.

On trust: I have zero on-page reason for Vee to doubt Pace, and the chapter is careful to give him a disarming innocence — "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth." That grin going "off his face into something quieter, harder to look at" when she comes to him in it is the one flicker where the mask thinned for me; something in him gets very still and hungry there. But mostly this chapter's menace is structural, not behavioral — he's tender the whole way through, which is the point I keep making to myself: the goodness is the weapon. The detail that he was up all night finishing it, that she "knew without being told" — that's real devotion *and* it's the sunk cost that binds her. Both true.

Friction: almost none. If I reach, "never far" ("he stepped out into the den, not far, never far") is a touch on the nose — the narration leaning in to reassure me of his attentiveness in a way that felt slightly like a thumb. And the daydreamed induction is the closest the chapter comes to over-explaining its own theme; "it just felt like what she was for" is gorgeous but it's also the author handing me the thesis. I didn't mind it much because the image earned it.

The title — "Peekaboo." At first it's just the slit: the leg appearing and vanishing, "given and taken back and given," the pale edge of underwear peeking through. Cute, sexy, apt. But sitting with it, it recolors darker. Peekaboo is the game you play with an infant — the delight of a thing hidden and revealed, and the whole point is that *nothing is really gone*, the hiding is theater for the reveal. The dress hides the seam so it can reveal the effortless. She hides herself (small steps) then reveals herself (strips) and reads the reveal as choice. And "peekaboo" is a game an adult runs *on* a child who doesn't yet know it's a game. That's the flavor that stayed with me — the delighted subject, the one running it, and the fact that the disappearing was never real.

The book's frame — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the cleanest illustration of that tagline yet. Every yes here is hers. He leaves *whether, and when, and what for* entirely to her. She undresses herself, she strips herself, she strides to him, she doesn't ask him to leave. Nobody made her do a single thing. That's the trap, stated plain: the freely-given yes is the mechanism, not the exception to it.

What I want and dread next: I want to see this dress worn into that induction, and I dread it, because I think the room turning will not feel to Vee the way it feels in the daydream. *Guess from here:* the induction is where the two hands — Randi's and Pace's — become one in front of her, and the dress she strode into as her own triumph will turn out to be the costume they cut her for. I also keep waiting for the panties he "traded" and now this second pair left on the chair — kept objects. And I still don't know where Randi is in Pace's life, and this chapter's silence about her felt almost loud.
