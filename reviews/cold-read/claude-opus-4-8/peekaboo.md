# Cold read — Peekaboo

## Reader reaction

This one is almost pure warmth, and it worked on me — which, given where my head is by now, is exactly the trouble. I read the whole chapter with my guard mostly down, the way she has hers down, and only at the very end did the cold thing I've been carrying tap me on the shoulder.

The dress reveal got me the same place it got her, "in behind the ribs." That detail about the labor — "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like it had cost him nothing." That's a beautiful sentence and it landed clean as tenderness. But I've been trained by this book, and my second thought came up hot behind the first, like hers with the underwear: *hiding the effort so it looks effortless* is the exact thing I've been logging about him for twenty chapters. Pre-warmed heat, prepped-and-stopped fra diavolo, conditions engineered in advance and laundered as care. The dress is the pre-warmed condition made into an object. The seam he hid on the silk is the seam the whole book hides. So even inside the swoon, the motif rang the register I distrust. That's the book doing its designed thing to me and I felt both halves at once — genuinely moved, and quietly totaled.

The eroticism is the strongest sustained heat since the first night, and I think stronger, because it's all *her* engine. The underwear beat is the peak for me — the pale edge cutting the clean line, and her taking the edge away herself: "she was the one who'd taken the edge away." That's the shame-into-wanting conversion done at its most economical, and it's her doing it to herself, unprompted, no Pace working the confession out of her, no Randi renaming it afterward. She strips bare "on purpose to be looked at" and walks the un-careful walk to him. It's the most self-authored she's been and it's genuinely hot, not clinical anywhere. The kiss in the doorway before any of it — "and under the circumstances it was never going to stay gentle" — is a lovely, unforced onramp.

Pace. The grin undid me a little, honestly. "Helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth one more second." That's the most boyish, least-sealed I've seen him — the man who moved her hand off his scar, grinning like he can't stand to wait. And "You made this." / "Yes," he said, and didn't make it smaller. He doesn't deflect the credit. Against all my suspicion, that read as real, and I *want* it to be real, and the book has told me it's both at once and won't let me have the clean version. He goes to sleep "hard and fast," up all the previous night finishing it — which is true devotion and also, and also. I can't stop hearing the *and also*.

Where the cold finally landed: the closing daydream. She imagines the induction — walking in *late, on purpose,* in the dress that looks "flown in from Milan," letting the room turn, "let them look, and the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." That last line is where the afterglow curdled for me. Being displayed, being looked at, is being framed as her *purpose* now — the pedestal, the mirror with the bright new screw heads, the whole apparatus he built to watch her see herself being seen. She's been put on a literal pedestal in front of a mirror that wasn't there before. The induction is the room where her name gets read at the front, where she's displayed publicly — and she's now going to arrive as an object "fitted to her by a man who'd thought about nothing else." I keep thinking of Brooke measuring her, the chapter-president appraisal. And the very last words — "getting away with something." I don't think she is. I think the thing is getting away with *her*, and she's calling it getting away with something. That phrase chilled me on purpose, I think — the text earned it, it didn't just drop it on me.

Cassie's absent, Randi's absent, and I felt the absence as relief in the moment and unease in retrospect — because the last two chapters were Vee editing this exact happiness down for Cassie and up for Randi. This is the raw thing before it gets sorted into two versions, and I know now it'll get sorted.

Friction: almost none. If I'm reaching — the "peekaboo" of the slit is worked maybe one beat past where it needed working (three "given and taken back and given" rhythms in a couple paragraphs), but it's rhythmic on purpose and I didn't resent it. And "getting away with something" as the button is doing a lot; I bought it, but I noticed the author reaching for the closing chill.

What I want next: the two tracks to finally *touch in her awareness*. She's about to wear this dress, publicly, at the induction — the most exposed, most watched moment planned for her — and Randi is the one she's been feeding the erotic middle to. My dread from here (a guess): the induction is where something converges — Randi there, or Brooke, or the dress becoming the thing that's *seen* by the wrong eyes. I dread the induction now in a way I didn't a chapter ago. And I still want to know where the bench is. It wasn't in the bedroom, it's not the sun porch, it's still nowhere — and this chapter cheerfully didn't mention it, which I noticed.
