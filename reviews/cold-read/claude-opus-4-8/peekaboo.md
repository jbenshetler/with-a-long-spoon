# Cold read — Peekaboo

*scene: scenes/peekaboo.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: fed*

## Reader reaction

This is the warmest chapter I've read, and it worked on me almost against my will. I know too much — I know Pace and Randi built Vee for a threesome she can't see — and still, this landed. That's the frightening part.

**Vee.** God, I love her here and I'm scared for her. The whole chapter is her body making decisions ahead of her head — "most of the way to a decision her body had already made" before she's even at the door — and for once the decisions are joyful rather than shamed. The underwear moment is the emotional peak of the whole thing for me: the slit opens, she sees the "pale edge" cutting the line, and her first instinct is to hide it, and then "the second came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed." She strips herself bare *on purpose* to be looked at, and "she was the one who'd taken the edge away." That's the shame-into-ownership move the book keeps making, but this is the first time she's the author of it, not Pace and not Randi. It felt like a genuine step for her, and it made me happy and uneasy at once, because I keep thinking about who taught her to want the room to turn.

**Pace.** The dress got to me the way it got to her, and I resent it a little. The detail that undid me is the same one that undid her — "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like it had cost him nothing." That's a beautiful thing to notice, and it's also a perfect description of *him* — a man who hides the labor so the effect looks effortless, so you never see the work being done on you. The dress is cut "to the actual shape of her, and to no one else's," lined only where it "had decided not to give away." He made her a thing that gives her away everywhere except where he chose. I don't think Vee reads that sentence the way I do, and the gap is exactly where my dread lives. And that boyish grin — "helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth" — is disarming precisely because I can't tell if it's real or if there is no line anymore between real and made. The book told me his tenderness is real *and* instrumental and never resolved, and this chapter is that rule as a scene. I fell for him. I hated falling for him.

**The "wait, the dress" beat.** This is the bit I most enjoyed and it's the bit I trust least about my own reaction. Both of them stopping mid-tumble to hang the dress "with the two-handed care you'd give something alive" — it's genuinely charming, it made me smile. It also made the dress the third body in the bed, which is doing something I can feel but can't name yet. She keeps "coming back to" it. She falls asleep looking at it. It's not quite a person and it's not quite an object and the chapter keeps treating it as sacred. I don't have a suspicion I can pin to text there — I just noticed the dress gets more reverence than either of their bodies, and I filed it.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new betrayal surfaced — Randi isn't even here, this is a clean two-hander. My coldness is entirely carried in from before; the chapter itself gives me nothing fresh to indict Pace with except the *shape* of his gift, which is genuinely double: a real act of love and a garment engineered to make her display herself. The daydream at the end is where the underneath showed through for me: she imagines walking into the induction late, on purpose, letting the room turn, "and the looking didn't cost her a thing. It just felt like what she was for." *What she was for.* That's the sentence I'd underline in red. It's her happiest thought and it's a thought about being *for* being looked at — which is exactly the use two other people have planned for her. She thinks she's getting away with something. I think she is the something being gotten away with. The last line — "getting away with something" — is the whole book's irony compressed, and it's the one place I felt the author's hand, just slightly, letting the theme peek out. But it's earned, and it's in her voice, so I didn't mind.

**Erotic charge.** It works, and it works because it's slow and clothed for most of its length. The undressing-and-dressing is more charged than the sex, which is deliberately blurred — "after that there was nothing left for either of them to decide" and then a cut to after. The peak is the dress coming down her body: "It came down her like water finding its level — cool against her bare skin for half a second and then warm with her own heat." That, and the bared leg striding to him. The actual sex is elided, and I didn't mind; the chapter's erotics are about *display and being seen*, not friction, and it knew that about itself. Nothing went clinical.

**Friction.** Very little. The prose leans hard on the dash-and-trail-off ("both of them already most of the way to somewhere —") and it's used three or four times for the same held-breath effect; by the third I felt the technique. And I'll admit "getting away with something" and "what she was for" are the author reaching toward me over Vee's shoulder — thematically gorgeous, a hair louder than the rest. But these are quibbles. The chapter mostly trusts me.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the induction now — she's built the daydream, the dress is loaded, and I dread the real version because a scene where "the room turns" is a scene where Randi and Pace could both be watching. *Guess from here:* the induction is the next detonation, and either Randi turns up there, or someone from that room sees the two of them and the seam between Vee's two admirers starts to show. I also keep waiting for the moment she says "Peter." Not yet. But the more she loves him, the more I want it to cost.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Pace invited Vee over for a midweek afternoon (their first "hello after" the first night). In the doorway a careful peck became a deep kiss; then he pulled her — not to the bedroom, but to the sun porch, where he'd hung the finished burgundy induction dress in the window light, with a full-length mirror newly hung and the fitting pedestal in front of it. She wept, happy. She tried it on bare (he'd built it to be worn braless, lined only at bodice/hips). It fit to her exact shape; a hip-high slit exposed her underwear, so she took the underwear off *herself*, on purpose, to keep the line clean — shame crossing into wanting, her own choice. She strode to him in it; they had sex. Mid-tumble they stopped to reverently hang the dress. After, he slept (she knows he'd been up all night finishing it); she lay awake daydreaming of arriving *late, on purpose* at the induction and letting the whole room turn — "it just felt like what she was for" — happy, "getting away with something."

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — happiest she's been; here she authored her own display for the first time (stripped bare on purpose to be looked at) rather than being steered into it. Shame-into-ownership now partly self-driven.
- **Pace** — flawless maker; made her a dress cut to her shape alone, hiding all the labor so it looks effortless — the same thing he does to *her*. Boyish, helpless-grinning tenderness that reads as real; I still don't trust the effortlessness. Still: right-hand-only, "parents" flinch, inner-thigh scar (none surfaced this chapter). Secretly Randi's co-orchestrator.
- **Randi** — absent this chapter. Still the hidden confidante/harvester; escalating mouth-kisses with Vee.
- **Cassie** — roommate, moral floor, gets the clean version. **Kayla/Meg, Brooke** — orbit.

**How I feel.** Deep affection and mounting dread for Vee. Reluctant, resented attraction to Pace — the chapter made me fall for him exactly as warned. Coldness toward the setup unchanged but *unfed* here; this was a genuinely warm two-hander with the ice only in the pattern (the dress gives her away everywhere except where he chose; "what she was for"). Mood: golden, tender, sweet, with the underneath humming.

**Open questions.** The induction is loaded and coming — will Randi and/or Pace be watching when the room turns? When does Vee learn Pace and Randi already know each other? The dress is treated as almost a third living thing — why so sacred? Still waiting on "Peter," the scar, the "parents" wound, and where the goodbye-kiss staircase with Randi leads.
