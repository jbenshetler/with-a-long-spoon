# Cold read — Peekaboo

*scene: scenes/peekaboo.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: fed*

## Reader reaction

This one is almost pure sweetness and heat, and it worked on me — right up until that last word, "getting away with something," which put a small cold finger on the back of my neck because *I* know what she's getting away with, or rather what's being gotten away with on her.

Vee is my whole heart still, and this chapter deepened her. The dress reveal is genuinely moving — I teared up a little where she did. "She stopped and put her hand over her mouth." The thing the chapter nails is *why* it lands on her: "that he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like it had cost him nothing." That's the no-tag motif from all the way back, paid off in the biggest object yet, and rendered as tenderness so pure I felt it in my own ribs before my suspicious reader-brain could get a word in. Which is exactly the trap, isn't it — the effort hidden so the gift reads as effortless is *also* the seducer leaving no fingerprints. I can hold both. The book has trained me to.

The undressing-and-stripping sequence is the hottest thing to me since Famished, and it's hot in a *new* register — she's alone, no one touching her, and the escalation is entirely her own. The two-step is beautiful writing: slit opens, shows the pale edge of underwear, "Her first thought was to keep her steps small, so it wouldn't show. The second came up hot behind the first and didn't care what showed." And she takes the edge away herself. "she was the one who'd taken the edge away." That's the shame-into-wanting engine running clean, and the crucial thing is *she* drives it this time. The gown that's cut "to the actual shape of her, and to no one else's" — the lining set "into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away" — my god, he built a garment that decides for her what to hide and what to give. That detail is erotic and slightly frightening at once and the chapter doesn't blink at it. Good.

Pace. Offstage-looming for chapters, and here he's fully present and I fell for him exactly as hard as I'm supposed to and hate a little that I did. The grin is the thing — "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth one more second." He breaks off a deep kiss not to control her but because he can't wait to show her the dress. That's disarming. He worked through the night. He hung the mirror, screws still bright. And "Yes," he said, and didn't make it smaller" — when she says *you made this*, he doesn't deflect. There's no tell of calculation on the page here, and per the book's own terms that's the point: the tenderness is real. It IS real. It's also the most elaborate act of engineering in the book so far — a full-length mirror newly installed, the pedestal repositioned, the light staged through silk. He built her a *stage*. And she ends the chapter rehearsing walking into her induction in it, letting the room turn. The dress that was "the night that was hers" — my open question about whether it stops being hers — she's now imagining wearing *his* object as her declaration of self. It became her armor and I can't yet tell whose.

Randi is entirely absent, even from mention, and I felt her absence like a held breath. This is the cleanest, least-mediated Pace/Vee scene in a while — no confidante retelling to reshape it afterward — and it made me trust the *feeling* more and fear the *pattern* more.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new tell landed on the page. Pace does nothing here a genuinely smitten man wouldn't do. My unease is entirely structural — it comes from what I already carry (he's Randi's lover, this whole courtship was staged, the induction dress has a purpose I don't fully know), not from anything this chapter exposed. That's honest to report: the text did *not* signal he's sinister, and refused to. The last line "getting away with something" is the only thing that pings, and it pings because of dramatic irony, not because Vee suspects anyone.

**Erotic charge:** High — a 2 pushing 3. It peaks in the solo mirror sequence, not the sex, which is smart: the sex is almost elided ("there was nothing left for either of them to decide"), and the real heat is her stripping bare *to be looked at* with no one in the room. Nothing went slack or clinical.

**Friction:** Almost none. The prose is lush and I let it carry me. If I reach: the "water finding its level" simile for the dress coming down gets a touch overworked ("cool for half a second and then warm... sliding the length of her"), but it earned it. Nothing felt like a thumb on the scale — the restraint at the end (no editorializing about *why* she's getting away with something) is exactly right; a lesser version would have explained it.

**The titles:** "Peekaboo" — going in it read cute, childish, a game. After reading: it's the slit. The leg given and taken back and given — "the length of her given and taken back and given." Peekaboo is the game of *showing and hiding your own face*, and the whole chapter is Vee learning to control her own concealment and reveal — she removes the underwear so nothing "peeks," turning peekaboo from accident into offering. It also has that infant-game innocence laundering something frankly sexual, which is the house style. And there's a colder read available: peekaboo is a game an adult plays *at* a child, controlling when the face appears — which is uncomfortably close to what's being done to her. It didn't give anything away; it deepened on arrival. Good title.

*With a Long Spoon* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the tagline's clearest illustration yet. Every single yes here is hers: she drives over, she strips herself bare, she strides to him, she imagines the induction. Nothing is taken. And that's exactly the horror the jacket promised — the freedom of the yes IS the mechanism. The long spoon: she keeps setting her own table, and here she literally puts on the meal and admires herself in it.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the induction — she's rehearsed it now, and I dread it, because a scene the character daydreams this vividly rarely arrives clean. *Guess from here:* Randi will be at that induction, or will hear this dress described and reshape it, and the two threads I keep waiting to collide will edge closer. I also still want to know why he built the lining exactly where he did, and I half-dread that the dress's "purpose" is for someone besides Vee to see her in it. Mostly I just want to keep reading, which is the trap working on me too.
