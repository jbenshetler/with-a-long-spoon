## Reader reaction

Oh, this one is a small mercy and a small dread at once. After chapters of Cassie being walled off, prefer-Randi'd, nearly cut loose — she opens the door. "Go on, then." And Vee goes on. My chest actually loosened when Cassie turned the book face-down. That's the gesture the whole chapter turns on: the dry held a beat *and then given over, glad*. Cassie loves her. She's tired of being edited out, and she still says *go on*.

What moved for me: I've missed Cassie so much and didn't fully clock it until she was back in the room being exactly herself. "You're doing the streetlight thing again. You're a public utility." That's the funniest, warmest, most *her* line she's had — and it's her old motif (lit-up-Vee, the streetlight) turned into affection instead of warning. She's not doing the flat "Vee." register here. She's letting Vee have her happiness. That's what makes what she does underneath so quietly devastating.

Because Cassie is auditing again, and she's doing it the way she does — no face on at all, letting Vee run herself out, and then dropping the one flat true thing. "With not enough on," she said, pleasant. That landed like a needle. Vee's telling the soup story as tenderness — he had a cold cloth on the burn before she'd got the first swear out — and Cassie catches the thing Vee glides right past: *the burn happened because she was half-dressed at his stove.* Vee's living there. Vee's cooking there half-clothed. Cassie says it "pleasant" and Vee's "already past it," and that gap between what Cassie sees and what Vee will hold is the whole Cassandra shape in one exchange. It's not a warning this time. It's an observation Vee can't receive.

Then the real one: "You don't come home school nights." Not a question. And Vee *goes back over the week she hadn't counted and found her own bed had sat empty more nights than it hadn't.* That's the chapter's cold click for me — the losing-herself rendered as a bed-count. She didn't even know. Same as the shrinking bras she doesn't mind, the weeks running into each other so she can't give Cassie "any one night." The dissolving is total enough that she's stopped tracking it, and it takes Cassie's flat arithmetic to make her look.

And I love that Cassie doesn't push. "Mm," said Cassie, which was Cassie for whatever you say. She's learned. She watched the warning get her walled off last time; this time she just *names the count and lets it sit.* She's stopped trying to save Vee and started trying to keep the door open. That's sadder and wiser than the ch.12 "Vee." warning.

The heart of it: "Do you love him," she said, the same flat way she'd said the rest of it. And Vee — who hadn't stopped talking since the door — *heard herself go small.* "Maybe. I think so." And then "It's all so fast." This kills me a little because *I know* from last chapter Vee already privately said it — she loves him, she whispered "Peter" to him asleep. So the "maybe" isn't discovery, it's the thing she can only fully own alone in the dark with him and can't quite say out loud to the one person who'd hear it straight. To Pace, silently, she can love him completely. To Cassie, out loud, it shrinks to "maybe." That inversion is heartbreaking and exactly right.

Erotic charge: near zero, and correctly so — this is the outside room, the cold-window room, the sober-count room. The only heat is secondhand and immediately audited (the soup, "with not enough on"). This chapter is the *anti*-heat by design: it's where the sealed bath gets counted from outside.

The soup story is doing a lot: he had her at the sink, cold cloth on the burn, "gentle as anything, bent over it," and then "kept the burn watched two days after, asking." And the house "warm as a held breath" because "he'd worked out she went cold the minute they'd stopped." That last bit is verbatim my accumulated cold — the pre-warmed conditions laundered as care — and here it's Vee *reciting it as romance to Cassie.* She's narrating the cage as the proof. I didn't feel the author's thumb; Vee sells it as love and Cassie just goes "no face on at all." The reader does the arithmetic. That's the book working.

Friction: almost none. This is the cleanest, quietest chapter in a while and I trust it completely. If I reach for anything — "the whole of himself turned on her until the rest of the room went quiet and dark around her" is doing the room-goes-dark thing that's near the tunnel-vision I've felt before, and it's lovely, but it's the one line that flirts with over-stating the size of her feeling. It stays on the right side.

What I want / dread: I dread that this is the last true word from Cassie — "Go to sleep, streetlight. Some of us have class." She kept the door open and let the maybe stand and *didn't say I know* — that restraint is her love and also possibly her giving up the fight. I want one more Cassie scene where she gets heard. From here (guess): the induction in November is the next stress point, and Cassie's the one still keeping the two-inch window cracked for cold — I keep waiting for that window to close, i.e. for Cassie to finally be gone from the room. This chapter felt like her holding it open on purpose. I'm bracing for the chapter where she doesn't.
