## Reader reaction

This is the first time in a while I've felt genuinely *un*-dreaded reading a chapter, and I noticed that with some relief — and then noticed the relief itself, because the source of the calm is Cassie being back in the room. She forced her way back in, or rather she just *opened the door* and Vee poured through it. That's a small joy. I trust Cassie more than ever.

I love how she's rendered. "You're doing the streetlight thing again. You're a public utility." That's affection wearing dryness like a coat, exactly the Cassie I've been carrying. And the whole chapter is her doing what she does — caring by deflection, reading Vee true, keeping the window cracked (literally, again — the two inches against the building's heat, the cold Vee "didn't feel"). The window motif landing on "Go to sleep, streetlight" at the end is lovely and gentle and I didn't feel a thumb on it.

But Cassie is also doing something sharper here, and it's the only thing in the chapter that carries a chill. Two flat, un-pushed observations: "You don't come home school nights." And "You've had boyfriends. You never went like this over one." She's naming the pattern from the outside — the disappearance, the total dissolution — the way only the moral-floor character can. She's not alarmed exactly; she's *marking* it. When she asks "Do you love him," flat, and then lets the "maybe" stand and says nothing against it and doesn't say "I know" — that restraint is the most trustworthy thing in the book right now. She won't manage Vee. That's the opposite of what Randi does, and the contrast is quiet but total.

The Pace material comes filtered through Vee's retelling, so it's warm and secondhand and I let it be warm. The soup burn — him at the sink with a cold cloth "before she'd got the first swear out," keeping the burn watched two days after — is the tenderness working on me the way it always does. And the house "warm as a held breath" because he clocked that she goes cold the second they stop and gets something over her before she finds the cold herself. That's the drawer-love again: real, total, anticipatory care. It still moves me. It also still reads, underneath, as a man who has mapped her body's responses so completely that she never has to have her own experience unmediated — the same watchfulness that provisions her also *owns* her, and she can't tell the difference, and neither, quite, can I.

Cassie can, a little. "With not enough on," she says — pleasant — right into the middle of the soup story. She's the only one who registers the erotic undertow of "he had me half-dressed at the sink" and gently pins it. But Vee is "already past it," already back inside the warmth. That's the whole dynamic in one exchange: Cassie snags the detail, Vee's already downstream.

Erotic charge: low and that's right. This is a cool-down, a domestic exhale. No arousal here and none wanted. The heat is all in the retelling of care, not touch.

Friction: almost none. One line I stumbled on mechanically — "the dry held a beat and then given over, glad" — the grammar wobbles ("held… then given") and I had to reread to get that it's Cassie's dryness giving way to gladness. Small. Otherwise the prose is clean and doesn't over-explain, which I appreciated after chapters that risked telling me how to feel.

What I want / dread next: I'm braced for the return of Randi, and I dread her *more* now precisely because this chapter reminded me what an un-managed friendship sounds like. Cassie asks "do you love him" and lets the maybe stand; Randi would have named it, framed it, made Vee say it and then own having said it. *Guess from here*: the collision I keep waiting for might come through Cassie — she's the one person outside the two tracks who's clocking the pattern, and she's now back inside Vee's orbit. If anyone trips the wire, it's her. I also note, quietly, that Randi's name didn't come up once tonight. Vee told Cassie the whole shape of her weeks and left Randi out of it. That omission is the coldest thing in a warm chapter.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Vee comes home past eleven "lit up," and Cassie — for the first time in a while — opens the door and lets her talk. Vee gives her not one night but the shape of her weeks with Pace: the cooking-from-scratch, the watching-her-eat, the soup burn he tended before she'd finished swearing, the house kept warm because he clocks when she goes cold. Cassie, dry and un-pushing, marks the pattern from outside: Vee never comes home school nights; she never dissolved like this over any earlier boyfriend. She asks flatly, "Do you love him." Vee goes small: "Maybe. I think so. It's all so fast." Cassie lets the maybe stand, says nothing against it, and sends her to bed — "Go to sleep, streetlight." Notably, Vee never mentions Randi.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee / Vivienne** — dissolving into Pace by degrees; can now say only "maybe" to loving him though she's privately sure; retells his care as wonder; already past every detail Cassie snags.
- **Pace / Peter** — seen only through Vee's retelling here: cooks from scratch, anticipates her cold, tended her soup burn, watches her eat. Tenderness still real, still total, still (to me) indistinguishable from ownership.
- **Randi** — absent, and *unmentioned by Vee* even in a full accounting of her weeks — the coldest note in a warm chapter. Still (inferred) Pace's partner; the extractor/reframer.
- **Cassie** — back in Vee's orbit, and reaffirmed as the moral floor and the person I trust most: keeps the window cracked, cares by deflection, reads Vee true, and crucially *won't manage her* — lets the "maybe" stand where Randi would have framed it. The one person outside both tracks who is openly clocking the pattern.
- **Sheri** — Pace's blunt riding partner (offstage).

**How I feel.** A rare warm exhale, and I let it be warm — but Cassie's two flat observations (never comes home, never went like this over one) and Vee's silent omission of Randi are the cold running under it. My trust in Cassie is at its highest; my dread for Vee is patient rather than acute here. I love Pace no less; the anticipatory care still seduces and still reads double.

**Open questions.** Why did Vee leave Randi out entirely? Will Cassie — now the one clear-eyed person back inside Vee's life — be the wire that trips, the collision point? When do the two tracks meet? Still open: are Pace and Randi coordinating (unconfirmed from inside); the inner-thigh scar; the right-hand-only eating; the withheld family; the induction/threesome timing; Vee's quiet weight loss.
