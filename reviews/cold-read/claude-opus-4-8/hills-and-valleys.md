**Felt read**

This is a short, breathing-space chapter, and I liked it for exactly that — a cold Saturday road, two bikes, and a man who can't stop himself talking about a girl. After the gorgeous, suffocating closeness of the Randi scenes, getting outside Vee's head and onto a quad of asphalt with Sheri felt like opening a window. And it works on me, quietly, because it's the first time I've gotten to watch Pace be *caught out* by his own feeling rather than executing. "He heard himself going and didn't stop." That undid me a little. The thumb-down-the-grain-of-the-walnut detail, the math book she pulled off the shelf "like it had burned her" — these are the things *he* chose to keep and retell, and they're not strategic beats, they're the tells of a man smitten. "Listen to you," Sheri says, and "He didn't know what there was to listen to." That's the chapter's whole swoon, and it's a real one.

My feeling about **Pace** moved, warmer, against my better judgment — which is the engine of this whole book. I *know* he and Randi ran the game. But here, with no Vee to perform for and no Randi to plan with, he's just helplessly glowing to an old friend, and the glow reads true. The checkpoint told me Sheri had already clocked him this way; getting it in full prose, I buy it more than I expected to. It complicates my wariness in a way I find genuinely uncomfortable, in the good way.

**Sheri** I flat-out enjoy. She's the frank, unsentimental outsider the book needed — remarking on Vee's backside "the way other people remarked on the weather," turning threat on and off "inside a breath." The line that landed hardest: "You want me to not like her?" and his answer, "No. She couldn't take your heat." That's affection and a tiny cruelty in one breath, and it tells me these two have a real, weathered friendship. I trust her read of things precisely because she's not inside the plan.

The erotic charge here is low and I wouldn't want it higher — this isn't a heat chapter, it's a *confession* chapter dressed as sports talk. Where it does spark is Sheri's blunt appraisal and the arithmetic she runs out loud: "Two of them at once. How do you sleep? That must be better than your dreams." That's the chapter saying the quiet part — and then the dread arrives in her follow-up: "If that's not already complicated, it will be. But I bet it's worth it." That's the closest thing to a storm-warning I've gotten in a while, delivered casually by the one person with no stake in pretending.

And here's where my suspicion sharpened, hard: "She know about the redhead?" / "She knows. She likes her." / "And the redhead?" / "Not yet." *Not yet.* That little word is a clock. It confirms from Pace's own mouth that Vee's ignorance is a scheduled, temporary condition — something they are *timing*. After a run of chapters where I could almost let the tenderness lull me, this put the floor back under my unease. Vee doesn't know. They know she doesn't. And they're waiting for the moment. The warmth of the whole chapter is sitting right on top of that cold two-word admission, which is the book's favorite trick and it got me again.

Friction: almost none — this is a clean, confident little chapter. If I'm honest, the "two of them at once / how do you sleep" is *slightly* on-the-nose as thesis-statement, Sheri voicing the reader's exact question, but it's so in-character for her bluntness that I forgave it. No thumb on the scale beyond that.

The title, **"hills-and-valleys,"** reads first as literal — the rolling road, the climbs that "took the breath for themselves," talk on the flats and silence on the grades. But it recolors as the geography of the thing itself: the easy descents where he can talk, the climbs that silence him, Sheri's engine-out-of-nothing on the hill while "there was never as much of her as the work she got out of it." And underneath, the shape of what's coming — this affair that's all gentle flats right now and an uphill waiting. "If that's not already complicated, it will be" is the valley floor before the climb. It doesn't give anything away; it sits as a nice physical metaphor that I only leaned on after reading.

The volume board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** — still rings its warning in my ear. "He that sups with the devil needs a long spoon," and a "warm reception" is the welcome Vee's being readied for. Against "Not yet," that reception is starting to feel like something scheduled *for* her rather than offered *to* her. This chapter deepened that dread without a single harsh act on the page.

What I want next: back to Vee, honestly — I want to see her carrying her won-back peace while I'm holding Pace's "not yet," and I want that gap to ache. *Guess from here:* the invitation's coming, and Sheri's "it will be [complicated]" is the chapter planting the flag for it.
