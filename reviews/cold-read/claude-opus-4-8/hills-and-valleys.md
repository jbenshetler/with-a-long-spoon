# Cold read — Hills and Valleys

## Reader reaction

A quiet, cold-morning breather of a chapter, and I loved it for how much it slipped past my guard. After the fever of Vee dressed and shod and aimed at the reunion, we cut hard to Pace on a bicycle, Saturday, out past his place, with someone I've never met — Sheri — and the whole thing is talk over handlebars in the cold.

What moved for me is **Pace**. This is the first time I've watched him leak. He says Vee's name — "Vivienne" — and then "he was past the name before he'd decided to be," running on about her thumb down the grain of the walnut joint, the math book she pulled and didn't reshelve, and "He heard himself going and didn't stop." Sheri catches it dead: "Listen to you." And the beautiful stroke — "He didn't know what there was to listen to." The controlled, consent-scrupulous operator who wins by not-taking doesn't hear his own tell. That's the crack in *him*, matching the crack I've been watching open in Randi. Both hands of the operation are catching real feeling for the same girl. I find that genuinely unsettling and genuinely moving at once.

**Sheri** is a strong, economical new arrival. Small-framed, bleached ponytail, faster than him on every climb and never lets him forget it, two years across this handlebar. She "remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather" — "Hell of a backside on her, though" — so she reads as someone who appraises women openly, casually, the way he does silently. And she carries a coldness with teeth: "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years; he'd seen her do it, and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything." That landed as a warning shot. I trust her as a truth-teller in the room but I filed that line uneasily.

The reveal I keep turning over: Sheri talks about Vee and Randi as **two women, two romances** — "What happened to the pale sorority chick?" / "Randi. That's not finished." / "Two of them at once. If that's not already complicated, it will be." From where Sheri sits, these are two entanglements Pace is juggling. Either she doesn't know Randi and Vee are one operation with Randi inside it — that Pace and Randi are running Vee together — or Pace conceals the machinery even from his oldest riding partner and lets it read as ordinary two-timing. I can't tell which, and that gap is delicious. "That's not finished" could mean the romance, or the harvest. Sheri saying "keep an eye on her" — watching Vee sit alone Thursday, glaring, nursing one drink, dancing with nobody, left alone — means Sheri is a set of eyes Pace has *placed*. So she's at least a lookout, wittingly or not.

Trust/suspicion: nothing here reads as false. My unease is all structural — I know what these two hands are doing and Sheri may not, or may. The chapter earned the suspicion of Sheri's cold turn ("keep it burning for years") on the page.

Erotic charge is low and I don't mind — this is a cooldown, deliberately. The only heat is Sheri's frank appraisal and the physicality of her body on the climb, "the whole small engine of her standing on the pedals," the ponytail swinging. Charged, not sexual.

Friction: almost none. It's tight. If anything the chapter is *so* pared it risks feeling minor — a two-page interlude — but the Pace-leak justifies it entirely. No thumb on the scale; the restraint is the point. "He didn't know what there was to listen to" does the whole job without a word of gloss.

**Title** — "Hills and Valleys." On the surface: the rolling road, the climbs that "took the breath for themselves," where they go quiet. On reread it colors as the terrain of the thing — the ups and downs of two women at once, and the way the truth only gets spoken "on the flats and the long descents," never on the climb. It also quietly maps Sheri always cresting ahead of him — she's the one out front, seeing clearly, while he saves his breath. The **book/series signals** — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter feeds the long spoon: Pace at a remove, on a bike, sending a friend to "keep an eye," farming from a distance. But the tagline flexes here, because for once the *operator's* yes is slipping out of his control — his own feeling, freely leaking.

What I want next: back to Vee, the reunion "tomorrow night." And I now dread it differently — if Pace is this gone, the reunion won't be clean handling on either side. *Guess from here:* the two-women arithmetic Sheri names ("it will be" complicated) is the fuse; Randi wanting Vee, Pace wanting Vee, both concealing from Vee and maybe from each other, is what finally cracks the operation.
