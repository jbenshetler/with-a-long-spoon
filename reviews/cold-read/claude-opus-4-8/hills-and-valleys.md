## Reader reaction

# Felt read

A quiet one, and a welcome change of air. After the golden, tightening intensity of the last few chapters — the wound at the bar, Vee wrecked, Randi dressing her for reconciliation — this drops me onto a cold road on a bicycle with Pace and a woman I've never met, and lets me just *watch him* for a few pages. It breathes.

**Sheri** lands immediately and cleanly, which I liked. Two years of riding this same handlebar, faster than him on the climbs and never letting him forget it, "small on the bike and fast on it, all of her folded down into the work." She "remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather" — so when she says "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that," it's not a come-on, it's just her weather report, and the fact that "it had never once cost him anything" tells me exactly what kind of friendship this is: easy, sexless, honest, load-bearing. I trust her more than I trust anyone else in this book so far, precisely because she has no agenda I can see and she isn't sweet. The line "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years" gives her teeth — she's kind to Pace but not soft, and when she offers "You want me to not like her?" she means it as a real service she'd perform.

What this chapter is really *for*, as a reader, is watching Pace get caught. That's the whole quiet event. He starts to tell her about Vee — the thumb down the grain of the walnut joint he spent a weekend on, the math book she didn't reflex back onto the shelf — and "he was past the name before he'd decided to be… He heard himself going and didn't stop." And then Sheri's sideways look, "the look she got when she'd caught him out," and just: **"Listen to you."** He genuinely doesn't know what there is to listen to. That's the tell — he can engineer a woman's whole night but he can't hear himself falling. It's the most nakedly *gone* I've seen him, and it moved my needle toward sympathy again, the way his un-sulking exit did last chapter. He's a man in it deeper than he'll admit.

But the chapter also does something colder, and I felt it land like a small stone: **"What happened to the pale sorority chick?" / "That's not finished."** So the two women are, at this moment, *both live* in Pace's actual life, running in parallel — Randi is "not finished," Vee is beginning — and Sheri, an outsider with no stake, does the plain arithmetic anybody could run: **"Two of them at once… If that's not already complicated, it will be."** Coming from someone with clean eyes, that reads as a verdict, not a worry. It's the first time a sane bystander has looked at the shape of this and said, flatly, *this ends badly.* And Pace "didn't answer." That non-answer is him, exactly.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** Sheri I trust fully — she's the clearest clean witness yet, cleaner even than Cassie, because she's not sweet and has known him longest. Pace: no *new* suspicion, but the old unease got its clearest confirmation from an unexpected angle — his own mouth outran him, and someone who loves him plainly said "this will be complicated" and he wouldn't meet it.

**Erotic charge:** none, and rightly. This is a cold-fingered, breath-over-the-shoulder chapter. The only carnal note is Sheri's backside remark, which is deliberately *un*-charged — locker-room, not desire. Zero heat, and I didn't want any.

**Friction:** almost none. If anything I wanted a hair more of *Sheri* — she's vivid enough that I'd have taken another exchange. One tiny thing: "He didn't know what there was to listen to" gets echoed by "he said nothing" and later "He didn't answer" — three beats of Pace-won't-say in a short space. It's characterizing, not a flaw, but I noticed the repetition.

**The title:** "Sheri — First Ride" puzzles me a little. This is explicitly *not* their first ride — they've ridden Saturdays for two years. So "First Ride" must point somewhere else, or it's marking *my* first ride with Sheri, the reader's introduction. The "First" makes me think there'll be more of her — that she's a recurring seat at this handlebar and I'm meant to file her. I'll hold it loosely.

**Book title / tagline:** nothing new pressed on them here, except that Sheri is the first character to name the trap's *shape* out loud from outside — "two of them at once… complicated" — without knowing there's a game at all. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." She's the un-managed voice: nobody's steering Sheri, so she just says the true thing.

**What I want next:** back to the reconciliation, surely — it was set for "tomorrow night." I want to see Vee and Pace collide again after the wound, now that I've watched him privately unable to shut up about her. *Guess from here:* Sheri's flat arithmetic is a plant — the "two of them at once" is going to detonate, and the "pale sorority chick" being "not finished" is the fuse. I still keep waiting for Vee to learn Pace and Randi are connected.
