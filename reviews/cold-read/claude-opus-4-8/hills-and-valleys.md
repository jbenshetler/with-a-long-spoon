# Cold read — Hills and Valleys

*scene: scenes/hills-and-valleys.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: broken-in*

## Reader reaction

First time I've been inside **Pace's** head, and the chapter's whole quiet power is that there's nothing to catch him at. I braced for the schemer's interior and got a smitten man who doesn't know he's smitten. He starts telling Sheri about "the redhead" running her thumb down the walnut joint, pulling the math book off the shelf "and not put it back like it had burned her" — and "he heard himself going and didn't stop." Sheri's verdict lands on me too: *"Listen to you."* He "didn't know what there was to listen to." That gap — him not recognizing his own warmth — is the most disarming thing the book has done to my read of him. From the inside, this doesn't feel like a con. It feels like a man catching feelings inside his own game and not clocking it.

Which genuinely complicates what I thought I knew. I've been carrying Pace and Randi as lovers running a coordinated cold con. This chapter shows me Pace's interior with zero visible scheming — and, crucially, Sheri (two years across this handlebar, an outside honest eye, not in on anything) reads it as a plain man-with-two-women mess: *"What happened to the pale sorority chick?" / "Randi. That's not finished." / "Two of them at once."* No coordination visible to her. So either Pace compartmentalizes so completely that even his own head reads clean, or the "con" is more tangled with real feeling than I'd let myself think. I'm holding that ajar, not resolving it — but the page moved me toward "he's actually falling for Vee," and it did it without a single line of special pleading.

**Sheri** I like immediately and cleanly. She's the third outside-eye who reads true (with Cassie and Meg), and the coolest-drawn: small frame "not much bigger than a child's," "the whole small engine of her standing on the pedals," bleached ponytail swinging, faster on the climbs and never letting him forget it. She remarks on women "the way other people remarked on the weather" — reads queer, appraising, unsentimental (*"Hell of a backside on her, though"*). And she has teeth: "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years." Pace steering her off Vee — "No. She couldn't take your heat" — reads as protectiveness, shielding Vee from Sheri's capacity for cold. That's a warm beat, and it's not aimed at anyone as craft; it's just him.

**Vee** and **Randi** are offstage, only named. What I got is corroboration of the rupture from a neutral witness: Vee alone at the bar Thursday, glaring at the floor, one drink, danced with nobody, left alone. Sheri watched because Pace asked, or because Sheri watches — either way it confirms the bar scene from outside and adds nothing warm-or-cold to it. Just: she was wretched, and Pace has Sheri keeping an eye.

**Swoon / pull:** low-key but real, and it's Pace's uncalculated tenderness that did it — the chair joint "he'd spent a weekend on," the involuntary spill of Vee-details, "he heard himself going and didn't stop." After a run of chapters where every warm gesture was a stitch in a trap, getting a warmth here that even the schemer doesn't recognize as his own is a genuine turn.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** nothing off about Sheri — she reads honest, an outside eye. The unease I carry is structural, not from this page: I know something she doesn't (Randi and Pace aren't two separate romances, they're witnessed coordination), and watching her run "two of them at once, if that's not already complicated, it will be" as ordinary arithmetic — while I know it's engineered — is the chapter's one cold current under an otherwise easy ride. Sheri's naming the complication is a small dread-seed for me: even the honest outsider only sees a love triangle, not the machine.

**Erotic charge:** none, and rightly. This is a cool-down.

**Friction:** basically none. Short, clean, well-cut. The Sheri portrait is gorgeous. If anything I wanted one more beat, but the restraint is the point — "that was the whole of it."

**Title — "Hills and Valleys":** literal on the surface, the ride's terrain — they talk on the flats and long descents, "went quiet on the climbs, which took the breath for themselves." On reread it colors toward the emotional terrain, and toward the going-quiet: where he can't or won't speak. And "two of them at once… it will be" — the country he's riding into. Innocuous surface, ache underneath. **The book's *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / the tagline** — the long spoon stays absent from this bright cold morning too; nobody's supping with a devil here, they're just riding. Which, given what I know, is the irony: the honest friend doesn't see the devil at the table at all.

**What I want next:** tomorrow night at Pace's door, still. And now a new hunger — to know whether Pace's warmth about Vee is real enough to bend the plan, or whether "That's not finished" about Randi means the two women collide. *Guess from here:* Sheri's "it will be" complicated is a plant; Vee and Randi both being real to Pace (not just marks) is where the fracture comes.
