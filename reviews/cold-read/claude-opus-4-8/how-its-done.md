## Reader reaction

# Felt read

Okay. That was a masterclass and it made me deeply uneasy in exactly the way I think it wanted to, and I want to be careful to separate what the *page* earned from what I've been bracing for.

The whole chapter is Randi extracting Vee's fitting story out of her, one soft "yeah" at a time, and I could *see the hands doing it* the entire way through — which is the horror of it. This is the first time in a long while I've had Randi on the page for real, sustained, working, and after chapters of half-believing her warmth, this chapter tipped me. Not all the way — but a lot. Because I watched the technique. "Nobody measures over a bra... it's not a *thing,* it's just how it's done." That line is the title, and it's the exact move: she manufactures *ordinariness* so Vee will hand over the thing she was ashamed of. She dissolves the shame precisely so the shameful detail comes loose. And then — the part that turned my stomach — the moment Vee is finally, genuinely reaching to give up the deepest thing ("*There was —*"), Randi says *"You don't have to tell me,"* and closes the door. Gentle. Kind. And the text names exactly what it did: "a door closing very softly," "her secret was still half out of her with nowhere to go, risen and reaching and met with nothing." She pulled her all the way to the edge and then *withheld the reception* — left her hanging over the drop. That is a colder, more deliberate cruelty than anything I've watched Pace do, because Pace always *completes* the thing he starts in her. Randi opened her up and walked off with the lid.

Why do that? My reader's gut says: she didn't need the words. She got everything she needed — she *knows* now, from the flat panicked "It was cold in the house" and Vee's face, exactly what happened on that pedestal. Making Vee say it would've cost Vee something Randi apparently didn't want to spend, or — the darker read — leaving it un-said keeps Vee owing, keeps the wound open, keeps her leaning in. Either way it's control.

And there's a genuinely new note I have to log honestly: **Randi is not clean in this.** Twice the text shows me something involuntary cracking through her mask — "something had gone tight behind it, some muscle she was holding, and her thumb had found the base of her water glass," when Vee says "he had the house warm for me... he'd thought of that." That tightness lands right on the domestic-care detail — the warmed house, *the thing she'd read as Pace's tenderness toward Vee.* I don't think Vee has any idea what she's watching, but I do: that's jealousy, or something adjacent. "a brightness wound a notch tighter than the coffee places had ever drawn out of her." The jacket told me both founders fall as hard as the girl, and this is the first time I've *felt* Randi being caught in her own machine rather than just running it. The tit-goddess longing, "with open longing" at Vee's chest — I can't fully tell if that's flattery-tool or real want. Probably the awful answer is both.

And then the kiss. God, the kiss. "not the dry tight press Vee gave her grandmother," "a small drawing-back, the barest pause, and then back again with a little more, a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't." That is not a French-goodbye and I don't believe it for a second, and neither, underneath, does Vee — she has it "explained away almost before her mouth was her own again," which is Vee's whole survival mechanism, the reflexive reaching-for-the-kinder-reading that's been quietly destroying her since chapter one. The second press. Randi *checked*, drew back, and chose to go in again with more. That's the game reaching out and touching her directly.

**Vee** — I ache for her and I'm angrier on her behalf than I've been. The money stuff gutted me quietly: reading the menu "the way you read a road in fog, looking for what was passable," the scuffed pump she rubbed at that morning, tipping well "because the other thing she would not do was be the broke girl who tipped badly," the debit card and "the number that would be smaller tonight in a way she would feel on Thursday." She paid for her own seat at a table where a single glass of wine cost more than her whole meal, so she wouldn't be bought — and I love her for it and it hurts because she's being bought anyway, just not with money. And the recurring self-erasure: her instant assumption that Randi's tightness means "she thinks I'm awful," then the reflexive upgrade to "maybe she's *moved,*" reaching for the kind reading "because you want it to be true." That reflex is the exact door the whole book keeps walking through.

The **erotic charge** — it's real and it's queasy, which I think is the point. The Randi-tied-to-the-headboard story worked on me *and* worked on Vee, and I clocked that it was a deliberate seed — Randi opening the topic of *helplessness, being tied, letting go, "a little helpless can be a very good thing, done right"* — a philosophy delivered as anecdote, planted in Vee before she's anywhere near a bench she hasn't seen. The heat "she had no shelf for." And the pedestal re-telling, "all the best ones start at the left hip... which meant nothing at all and landed like a benediction" — the seduction-by-narration, Randi voicing Vee's own memory back to her hotter than she lived it. Peak charge for me is the kiss, precisely because it's the least explicit thing in the chapter and the most loaded.

**Friction:** honestly very little. The one place I felt the thumb, mildly, was the density of the naming near the door-close — "a door closing very softly," then immediately the paragraph spelling out "risen and reaching and met with nothing." The image alone (the door) was doing it; the gloss slightly over-insured a moment that had already landed. But it's a small thing and the writing mostly trusts me.

**The title** — "How It's Done" is the knife. On the surface it's Randi's disarming line, *it's just how it's done,* garment-trade ordinariness. Read back, it's the chapter telling me flatly what I just watched: *this is how it's done* — how you take a girl apart with warmth, how the game is run. It's the most nakedly the-title-names-the-machine of any so far, and it didn't spoil anything because the spoil *is* the reading experience. The book title, *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." I've been circling it; this chapter is the first where I really feel who might be the devil at the table. And the tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — every "yeah" in that pedestal sequence was freely given. I watched them be given. That's the trap, demonstrated.

**What I want / dread:** I want to see the two worlds touch, and I now dread it more, because after this kiss I don't think Vee is being steered toward *only* Pace. The bed the jacket promised has three people in it, and Randi just made her move on the girl directly. **Guess from here:** Randi's jealousy at the warmed-house detail is going to matter — the trapper is getting trapped, exactly as I've been wondering, and I think her wanting Vee is becoming real enough to threaten whatever she has with Pace. And I dread the moment Vee learns the two of them were connected all along — that lunch, this warmth, will curdle retroactively into something unbearable.
