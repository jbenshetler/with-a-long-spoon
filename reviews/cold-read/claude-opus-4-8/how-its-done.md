# Reader reaction — "How It's Done"

Two rooms, two confessions, and the second one gutted me in a way the first never meant to. The Cassie scene first — I came in grinning. That's the Vee I love, loud and generous, acting out the bra coming off with her hands flying "*out*. Just out," throwing a pillow, patting her own backside "brisk and clinical, like she was indicating it on a diagram." The "is he gay" bit and then "*I* was grinding on *him*" — I laughed out loud. And underneath the comedy, Cassie does the thing she always does: catches the real weight and sets it down gently. "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?" then, when Vee says *like I was supposed to be there*, the hand on the knee, once, and taken back. Cassie is still the clean thing. I trust her completely and I'm grateful the book keeps giving us a room where Vee is safe and known.

And then the car, and the lunch, and I spent the whole second half with my stomach tightening — because I know what Vee doesn't. This is a debrief. Randi is extracting the fitting from her one *yeah* at a time, and the chapter lets me watch the instrument do its work in real time: "he started at your hip, didn't he. Which hip?" "The left one." "Oh, all the best ones start at the left hip" — which means *nothing*, lands like a benediction, and leads her down. "Nobody measures over a bra… it's not a thing, it's just how it's done." Watching Randi dissolve Vee's shame precisely so Vee will keep talking — kind and predatory in the same breath — is the most unsettling thing the book has done to me yet, because it works so beautifully you almost forget to be afraid.

But here's what moved my read of Randi, hard: she's *losing*. The thumbnail against the glass rim, bearing down — that's her hidden self-hurt from the very first chapters, the bottle-cap under the thumbnail. It surfaces the instant Vee says, glowing, "*he looked at me and I was fine.*" Something tightens behind the smile. And then the door she slams shut: Vee finally, finally opening her mouth to give up the deepest thing — what Pace saw on his knees — and Randi says "*You don't have to tell me.*" Gentle. Kind. A door closing very softly. And the laugh that always joined them doesn't come. I read that as jealousy she couldn't sit inside for one more sentence. She went hunting for the report and got to the exact place where she couldn't bear to hear it. The instrument turned live in her hand, like the jacket promised. Suspicion and ache, both at a new high.

Then the kiss. Not the grandmother press — "soft, and in no hurry," the drawing-back, the *second* press "that had something in it the first one hadn't." And Vee's two fingers to her mouth, and the instant reflex to explain it away — *French, it's a French thing, she's been everywhere* — and then the cold scan of the dark windows: who saw. That last beat chilled me. The same window-of-watchers motif, but now the dread is hers, and it's the right dread for the wrong reason: she's afraid of being caught in something she can't yet name wanting.

Swoon and heat: this ran hotter than I expected for a lunch. The scarf-and-tickling story is doing double work — it's cover, and it's Randi handing Vee a picture (Randi helpless, laughing, wrists bound, control gone) that lands low in Vee "with no shelf for it." That's the unexamined pull the checkpoint flagged, finally pressing against the surface. "The letting go… somebody takes them and you can't hold them even if you wanted to" — that's Randi describing her own bench, her own undoing, dressed as girl-talk. The charge peaks there and again at the car kiss. It's not consummated heat; it's the heat of a want neither woman is looking at straight, which is exactly my favorite register.

Friction: almost none. The one place I felt the thumb lightly was Vee's internal narration spelling out her own class-consciousness at length (the Corolla, the wine price, "the number that would be smaller tonight… she would feel on Thursday") — it's true to her and I didn't mind it, but it's the most *told* stretch in a chapter that otherwise trusts gesture. Tiny. The green-velvet restaurant, the self-sealing Mercedes window "tucking itself in around her" — those I bought entirely.

The title. "How It's Done" — I first took it as Randi's line, "it's just how it's done," the worldly authority she uses to disarm Vee about the bra. But by the end it recolors: it's *how it's done* to a person — how you take someone apart gently, over soup, so they hand you the thing you want and thank you for the lunch. It points right at the method, and it gave me a small cold feeling once I'd finished. The volume line, "A POLITE INVITATION," and the series "WITH A LONG SPOON" — the spoon you need to sup with the devil — hang over this whole courtly, warm, dangerous lunch. The politeness *is* the long spoon. I feel it taking me somewhere the warmth is the mechanism of the harm.

What I want next: I want to know what cracked in Randi at that table, and whether Pace will get a version of this fitting the way he's gotten every other report — and whether, this time, Randi can even relay it. *Guess from here:* Randi's want for Vee is outrunning the plan, and the kiss was her stepping off-script. I dread the moment Vee learns the two of them are connected, and I'm starting to dread it *for Randi* too, which is new.
