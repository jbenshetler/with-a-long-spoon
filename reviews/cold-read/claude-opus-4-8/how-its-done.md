## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got under my skin. This is the first time in ages I've had Randi live on the page instead of in memory, and the chapter spends its whole length showing me exactly the thing I've been carrying as suspicion — and it's *worse* to watch than to fear. It's the confidante engine running at full power, and I could feel the machine even while I was charmed.

Let me start with how it *feels*, because it's disorienting in a good way. The opening is genuinely seductive. The Mercedes with the window that drops half an inch to meet the door and seals itself back up "like the car was tucking itself in around her" — I loved that, and Vee's helpless "*of course it does that, of course a car like this does that*" is her whole class-position in one breath. Set against the Corolla and her father's *keep the mileage down*, it aches. Randi's softened smile at the curb, "a thing spent rather than handed around" — Vee reaching for it, wanting it so badly she distrusts her own read of it. I felt the want in her, and I felt the cost of the want.

And then the lunch, which is where I got scared while smiling. Randi tells the headboard-and-scarf story, and it's *hot* — Randi tied and helpless and laughing so hard she nearly wets herself, "that finished surface finally cracked all the way open," and Vee's heat with no shelf for it. That worked on me. But watch what the story *does*: it's a demonstration. Randi hands Vee a live model of surrender — "the letting go... somebody takes them, and you *can't* hold them even if you wanted to" — and dresses it as gossip. "A little helpless can be a very good thing. Done right." She's teaching. She opened the chapter's real business by making herself the example, so that what she's about to draw out of Vee arrives pre-approved.

Then the extraction. This is the part I can't shake. Vee came with a *rehearsed* version — the dress, the burgundy, the safe romance — and Randi takes it apart stitch by stitch, each pull disguised as ordinary knowledge. "Nobody measures over a bra... it's not a *thing,* it's just how it's done." That line is the title, and it's a key turning in a lock. The instant Randi frames the shameful thing as *universal procedure*, Vee's shame has nowhere to stand, and she gives up the bra, then the "challenges," then "it was cold," then — cornered, laughing the laugh "of the absolutely cornered" — "actually it was really *hot.*" I watched a secret get walked out of her one small *yeah* at a time. "*All the best ones start at the left hip,* which meant nothing at all and landed like a benediction." That's the whole trick named on the page: nonsense delivered as authority, and Vee "warmed and confirmed and led, one small *yeah* at a time, down and down."

Here's what moved my feeling about Randi hard, and it's the most interesting beat in the chapter. There's a moment where Vee, telling *he looked at me and I was fine*, catches Randi go still — "something had gone tight behind it, some muscle she was holding," her thumb working the base of the water glass. And Vee mis-reads it twice: first cold (*she thinks I'm awful*), then kind (*she's moved, she's happy for me*) — and settles on the kind one because she wants it. But I saw the tell, and I don't think it's happiness. Randi went tight hearing about Pace looking at Vee's bared body and Vee being *fine*. That's the un-sipped-cup thing again, the plan escaping her — except now I think it isn't only the plan. Something in Randi flinched at another woman on that platform in front of *her* man. I can't prove it, but the water glass gave it to me.

And then the cruelest, quietest beat: Vee finally, *fully* opens — the thing she never told Cassie, "folded up the smallest and pushed the deepest," rising because the booth is so safe and Randi so close and the wanting to *give* it opens her mouth — and Randi says "*You don't have to tell me.*" Gentle. Kind. "A door closing very softly." And the laugh doesn't come. Vee's secret is left "risen and reaching and met with nothing." I felt that as genuine cruelty wearing the mask of tact. She spent the whole lunch prying Vee open and then, at the exact moment of full surrender, *withdrew the reward.* Vee doesn't understand what happened; I think I do, a little — the withholding is the hook. Leave her reaching. It's the same shape as the shut bedroom door, the two towels, Pace's closed doors: intimacy dangled and pulled. They do the same thing. That parallel landed in my stomach.

The kiss at the end. Not the cheek thing — a real one, "a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't." And Vee explaining it away instantly — "*French,* it's a French thing... that's just how women like her say goodbye" — the reflex I now know so well, the editing catching up to the yes after the yes is already given. And then the cold turn: *had anyone seen?* Scanning the dark windows, counting them, heart going fast. That's the shame arriving late, not for the wanting itself but for being *witnessed* wanting — which is Vee's whole wound. The chapter ends on her afraid of being seen, which is exactly the appetite Pace has been curing her of and Randi has just fed.

**Trust and suspicion:** My suspicion of Randi is no longer abstract. The text *earned* it this chapter — the water glass, the withheld laugh, the door-closing-softly, the "how it's done" that turns Vee's shame into compliance. But — and the book keeps this brilliantly — none of it would read as sinister if I hadn't been watching for the pattern. On its own, this is a fabulous, warm, sexy lunch with your glamorous new friend. That's what unsettles me most: I can see the machine *and* I can feel how good it feels from inside. Vee has no reason to doubt her, and every reason not to want to.

**Erotic charge:** Real, and interestingly displaced. The peak isn't a sex act — it's the extraction itself, which is eroticized: the booth, the closeness, the *yeah... yeah... which hip... the left one,* Vee going "dreamy," "the booth very far away," being led "down and down." Randi produces with words what Pace produces with the body — I've had that line in my head for chapters, and here it's just *true*, and it's the most charged thing in the scene. The scarf story is a clean 2 on its own. The kiss is a low simmer with a cold aftertaste.

**Friction:** Very little. Once or twice I felt the interpretive hand a touch heavy — "a door closing very softly," then the follow-up spelling out that the laugh didn't come and the secret was "met with nothing." I'd caught it; the gloss slightly over-served me. But it's a small thing and the beat was strong enough to carry it. Mostly the chapter trusts its images (the glass, the window sealing, the dark windows) to do the work, and they do.

**The title:** "How It's Done" — on the way in it's nothing, a shrug, an idiom. Read, it's the pivot of the whole chapter: Randi's line "it's not a *thing,* it's just how it's done," the sentence that dissolves Vee's shame by calling the intimate thing routine. And on a second turn it's colder still — it's *how it's done* meaning how the extraction is performed, the method itself. The chapter is a demonstration of a technique, and the title is the technique winking at me. It doesn't telegraph — it detonates on arrival. One of the good ones.

**The book's title and tagline:** *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil with a long spoon, keep your distance. Vee has no long spoon; she leaned all the way across the gearshift. And "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the tagline dramatized in miniature. Every *yeah* was freely given, walked out of her by kindness, and each one a rung down. Nobody forced anything. That's the horror the tagline promises and this lunch delivered.

**What I want / dread next:** I want to see Randi with Pace again — because that tight muscle behind her smile makes me think the game is starting to cost her something she didn't budget for, and I want to know if she knows it. *Guess from here:* the two tracks are going to keep not-touching in Vee's awareness right up until they can't, and the withheld-laugh moment tells me Randi's own want is leaking. I dread the scene where Vee's deepest secret — the one Randi just declined to receive — finally comes out to the wrong person or at the wrong cost. And I dread that the "French kiss, that's all it is" reflex is going to be the last thing Vee tells herself before something she can't explain away.
