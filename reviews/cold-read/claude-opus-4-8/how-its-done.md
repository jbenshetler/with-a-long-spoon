**Felt read**

That was the most dread-soaked chapter in the book so far, and it did it in a *booth*, over tomato soup, with nobody's clothes off. I came in warm — the convertible, the wind in Vee's copper hair, the way she lifts inside a stranger's glance on the Hahn steps — and I left cold, two fingers at my own mouth with her. The title is the tell, and it's a cruel one. *How It's Done.* Randi uses that exact phrase — "it's not a *thing*, it's just how it's done" — to coax Vee past the one locked door she meant to keep shut. By the time I finished I read the title as the whole chapter's method: this is how it's done, the extraction, step by patient step. It gave the game away a half-beat before the page did, and I hated that I saw it coming.

Because this is a *harvest.* I've known since ch7 that Randi comes to Pace and says "tell me," that she runs on the details of Vee. Here I watched her get them at the source, and the craft of it is sickening in the best-written way. Every move is a friend's move — "I let this beautiful idiot take me home," the scarf, the tickling, offered up first so Vee will trade back. The "a little helpless can be a very good thing, done right" is pure grooming dressed as older-sister wisdom; it's also, horribly, *true*, which is why it works. Then the reframes: "nobody measures over a bra, sweetheart," said like which fork to use, specifically to dissolve Vee's shame so the fact falls out. "Were there any *challenges*?" with its little hook. "Which hip?" "All the best ones start at the left hip" — meaningless, landing "like a benediction." I could see the tongs closing and Vee couldn't, and that gap is the whole horror.

And then the two places Randi *stops*. "You don't have to tell me" — a door closing softly, right when Vee's secret is half out with nowhere to go. That's the most chilling beat in the chapter. She's already got the wetness (the "cold"/"hot" trap, beautifully sprung — "*Cold.* Hmm."); she doesn't need the thing on the platform, or she's decided not to spend the closeness getting it, or — this is what unsettled me most — she's leaving Vee aching to confess, primed, so the real telling happens later, on Randi's schedule. The withheld laugh where the laugh always came: "her secret was still half out of her." God.

My feeling about Randi *moved* — hardened. I've held real sympathy for the wounded woman on the bench, the thumbnail, the face. There's a flicker of it here: the tightness behind the smile when Vee says "he looked at me and I was fine," the thumb working the glass edge, some muscle held. Vee reads it as *moved, happy for me.* I read it as — jealousy? The scene she engineered working too well, Pace's tenderness landing on Vee exactly as it lands on her? Something cracked for a second and got folded away. I don't know which, and the page keeps it ajar, so I will too. But the tenderness Randi performs here is a tool, and watching a tool get pointed at Vee's deepest shame burned off a lot of my patience for her.

And the *kiss.* That's new and it rearranges things. Randi's hand at the hinge of Vee's jaw, soft, unhurried, and then the little drawing-back and coming *back* with more — "a second press that had something in it the first one hadn't." That's not French-goodbye and I don't think Vee fully believes her own "it's a French thing" either, because the very next beat is her heart going fast, scanning the dark windows: *had anyone seen?* The want between women the checkpoint kept flagging as real in Randi is now on Vee's mouth. I felt the charge of it — and the trap of it simultaneously, which is the book's whole trick.

Vee I ache for, completely. The right-side-of-the-menu reading "the way you read a road in fog," the scuffed pump she tucks under the booth, the debit card and the Thursday she'll feel it on, the refusal to be "the broke girl who tipped badly" — all of that is so tender and so *hers* that watching Randi work her over the top of it made me want to reach into the booth. And her reflex to reach past suspicion for the kinder reading — "maybe Randi was moved" — is exactly the reflex that's going to cost her. She explains every alarm away almost before she feels it. I trust no one in that booth but the absent Cassie.

Erotic charge: present but braided tight with dread, which is its own heat. The peak for me wasn't the kiss — it was the extraction itself, the slow *yeah… yeah… the left one* leading-down, Vee going "dreamy," the room "very far away." That's seduction, structurally, even though it's information being taken, not a body. And Vee's own buried heat at the image of Randi "naked and helpless and laughing… that finished surface finally cracked all the way open" — "a heat she had no shelf for." That landed hot and sad at once.

Friction: almost none. If I reach, the scarf anecdote is *slightly* too perfectly engineered as bait — but it's in character for how Randi builds a story, so I let it go. The book's thumb is on the scale, but it's pressing on Vee, not on me; I never felt told what to feel.

Series title *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." That reframed hard this chapter. Vee is supping with no spoon at all, handing over the cutlery. *A Polite Invitation* — the lunch was exactly that, and "polite" now reads as the weapon.

What I want next: Cassie, badly, as ballast. And I dread the version of this where Vee *does* tell — where the thing on the platform ends up in Randi's mouth, and then in Pace's bed. *(Guess from here.)*
