## Reader reaction

Oh, this one turned the lights off. After chapters of Vee glowing in that bright porch, we drop back into Pace's house — and it's the first time in ages I'm sitting *outside* Vee's warmth, watching the two people who made her, and it's cold in here in a way the stew's afternoon-long simmer can't fix.

The chapter is built on one image I couldn't stop staring at: the bowl. He ladles her the small one — "the one he sliced her apples into" — and she draws the spoon *along the top* of the sauce, tastes what it brings up, sets it down, says "It's wonderful," and never touches it again. All evening it sits there with the one line through it, the sauce skinning over the peas. That single drawn line is the whole chapter. Randi, who is *fed* nothing she'll actually take, who eats only the salad — the leaves, the periphery — and pointedly pours her own wine from the bottle he's always poured. "He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house." That detail went into me the way it went into him: something small and structural coming apart. She's refusing his portion. She's refusing to be *portioned*.

And I finally understand the frame from the makers' side, and it's bleak. Randi is jealous — flatly, humanly jealous — and I did not fully expect to feel it land as *hurt* rather than scheme. "How long are you going to take?" "It's selfish, Pace." She's watched him get Vee Friday-to-Sunday, unhurried, tender, and she's the one who engineered the whole thing and is now starving at the edge of it. The line that chilled me most was hers, flat, "with nothing on it": *"She's not ready. Or you're not."* And his answer — "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything" — is the truest, most decent thing he's said, and also monstrous when you remember the "it" is a trap they laid. He believes in the consent so much he's built a cathedral of patience on top of a con. That contradiction is the book.

The sex here is the anti-fitting. No "May I." No inch-and-stop, no *tell me* at his ear, no eyes. She drives, eyes shut, hair down over her face, nails in his chest, comes loud and gets off him *at once* — no staying down, no aftercare, rolls into the cashmere with her back to him. I've been told about their D/s ritual (the bench, safeword, aftercare); this is that ritual detonated. She took instead of submitting, and hid her face doing it — the exact thing the checkpoint told me she vowed, to never be *seen* again. So it wasn't hot to me, and I don't think it was meant to be; it was grief with a body on it. Him lying there setting "each piece beside the next" and finding they "would not go together into anything he could name" — that's the powerlifter-mathematician who reads everyone, locked out. He respects her closed door so completely he can't see she's dying behind it. And he covers her untouched bowl with a plate and puts it in the fridge *for her*. God.

My feelings moved hard. Randi cracked open for me — she's the wound now more than the schemer, and I'm unexpectedly aching for her even as I know what she's doing to Vee. Pace deepened and darkened at once: the tenderness is real, the blindness is real, and "It isn't for me" / "Isn't it?" caught something self-serving under the courtliness that I'd suspected and now feel confirmed. Vee's not on the page and I missed her; I also dreaded her, because everything sweet she told Randi Sunday is being weighed on this table as a *schedule problem*.

Trust/suspicion: no new deception to catch — instead I got the machine's *interior*, and it's more troubling than a plot twist. The thing that feels "off" is earned all the way: the game has a fault line down its middle, and the two makers want different things. That's the dread I'm carrying.

**The title — "Portion":** it landed cleanly and beautifully. A portion is what you're served, what you're allotted, and to portion is to ration — the apple slices from chapter one made whole. Randi gets a portion of Pace (weeknights, the small bowl) and won't eat it; Vee gets the weekend. Someone here is being starved on purpose and it isn't Vee. The cover line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — I read now as *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon*, and I can't tell who's the devil and who brought the spoon. "A polite invitation" sits very cold over a chicken stew nobody was allowed to enjoy. It points me toward: this arrangement is going to eat someone.

What I want next: Vee back, and the pit-of-stomach knowledge that Randi's hunger is now a live wire under the whole gentle courtship. I want to know what Randi *does* with being second. **(Guess from here, marked as guess):** Randi accelerates — pushes the seduction of Vee past what Pace's "as long as she needs" allows, to reclaim control, and that's where it breaks.
