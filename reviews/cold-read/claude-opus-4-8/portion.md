# Felt read

So we're back in the frame — Pace's house, Pace's head, for the first time since the very beginning, and after twenty chapters inside Vee's glow it lands like a door opening onto cold air. I spent those chapters half-forgetting the machinery because Vee's happiness was so persuasive; this chapter walks me straight back to the engine room and makes me watch it seize.

What got me, immediately and physically, was the food. Pace has done his whole liturgy — pot on since three, bread cooling, the small bowl, *the one he sliced her apples into* — and Randi draws the spoon *along the top* of the sauce, tastes what it brings up, and sets it down. One line through the skin of it. She won't be fed. After a whole book built on feeding, on Vee's open hungry mouth, Randi sitting in that kitchen refusing the bowl is the loudest thing on the page. And then she pours her own wine — "He had poured every glass she had drunk in this house" — and I felt the ritual rupture like a bone going out of socket. He feels it too and can't name where it went in. That not-knowing is the whole chapter.

My feeling about Randi moved, hard. Last chapter she was the warm confidante mining Vee at brunch, in total control, kissing her at the curb. Here she's cracked — jealous, or wounded, or impatient, and I honestly can't tell which, which is the good kind of not-telling. "How long are you going to take?" "It's selfish, Pace." She heard about Friday — that Vee got the whole weekend, drove home with no bag, is *happy* — and something in that is eating her from inside even as she won't eat the stew. I've carried her "terror of being seen" and her resolve to hide from Pace since the opening, and this chapter is that resolve made flesh: the sex where she tops with her eyes shut and *doesn't say tell me* — the phrase she says every night — and then rolls off him into the blanket with her back turned, no staying down, no aftercare. She took herself out of his reach on purpose. "Whatever this was, it was not for him." That's not a schemer. That's someone hurting in a way she'd rather die than show.

And Pace — God, Pace. The last image wrecked me a little: he covers her untouched bowl with a plate and puts it in the fridge, *for her*, and turns off the stove light. He's been called selfish for the one genuinely tender thing he does — refusing to rush Vee, letting her choose every step — and his answer, "She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn't anything," is the most honorable sentence in the book and lands nowhere near where she aimed. My unease about him actually eased a notch here; whatever he started as a game, his restraint with Vee reads as real principle now, and he's bewildered and alone at the end of it. "He did not know what he had done. He looked for it... and it was not there." Me too, Pace, sort of — I think I know more than he does and still can't fully name it.

Swoon? Not the usual kind — this chapter gave me ache instead, the devotion-met-with-a-turned-back kind. The erotic charge ran deliberately cold: the sex is fast, eyes shut, armored, and the heat in it is all wrongness and withholding — the exact photographic negative of the bench where he took her open. It worked on me *as* a rupture, not as a swoon. No suspicion was manufactured here; the text earned every bit of my unease plainly.

The title. **"Portion"** — the small bowl, the rationed apple slices, what *portion* of Vee each of them gets, what portion of herself Randi will ever hand over. And the series line finally clicked for me: **WITH A LONG SPOON** — he who sups with the devil must use a long spoon. Randi drew her spoon *along the top* and kept her distance; she sups and will not be taken in. **Book One — A POLITE INVITATION** sits oblique over all of it, courtship-shaped, and I now read "invitation" with a chill. No cover tagline was given me beyond those lines.

What I want next: back to Vee, and dread — because I've now seen the crack in the person feeding her open, and I can feel the weekend she drove home from already costing something she can't see.
