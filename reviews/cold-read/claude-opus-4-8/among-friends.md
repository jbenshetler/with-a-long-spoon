# Cold read (grounded) — Among Friends

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one worked on me — and it worked by being almost entirely *nice*, which is exactly what made my skin prickle.

The whole chapter is Vee's dream night landing: her two worlds in one warm pine roadhouse, her name on butcher paper, and Pace — courtly, withholding Pace, who won't say four words — walking around saying **"Vee's boyfriend"** to six different faces like it costs nothing. And it undid me a little, honestly. That's the swoon of the chapter and it's real: he gives her *in public* the small plain claim she's been starving for, over and over, until the tables are grinning ahead of it and she's mouthing it to herself at the buffet. After all the ache of "I love how good we are together" not being the right shape, watching him spend the one word he *will* spend, freely, in front of everyone — that landed low in me the same way it landed low in her. I rooted for her hard here.

And the party set-piece is genuinely lovely craft-as-affection: Pace handing each person their first line and standing back to laugh at their second. "How's your bench progressing?" and Danny never learns what Pace lifts. "Where does it start, for you?" and Theo's shoulders come down. The Cassie beat is the one that actually moved me — Cassie, my safe harbor, the goalkeeper who's watched every man near Vee for two years, *talking in paragraphs*, glowing "at low wattage." Vee standing back with her sweet tea, feeling "something enormous and quiet go by underneath her, like a whale under a boat." That image is beautiful and it's the truest thing in the chapter, and I don't think Vee knows how true.

Because here's what tightened my stomach: **Randi got there first.** Twenty minutes out on a road with no reason on it, before the hosts, at the chair facing the door, a beer already sweating. The chapter frames it as devotion — "she beat everyone to Vee's things, always had" — and it *is* devotion, but I've read the bench, I've read Gstaad, and I know what it also is. And then Pace and Randi across the same table for the first time on the page in front of Vee, "we get along," laughing so Vee "never once got there in time." The reader's whole secret is sitting at that table pretending to be two friends being friendly, and the dramatic irony ran cold and constant under all the warmth.

The moment that gutted me quietest: Randi going glazed and still after "the highlight of my year — to my beautiful best friend," her fingers turning the glass "a slow quarter at a time." Vee reads that stillness as *I moved her, I'm proud I can move her.* I read it as Randi holding a word down with both hands. That gap — Vee foolishly proud of producing an emotion she's completely misnaming — is the book operating at full strength. And the glass-turning rhymes straight back to the previous chapter's restaurant, where Randi worked the rim under her nail telling the underwear story. Same tell, twice, at the edge of saying a true thing.

The two little stingers Vee feels and talks herself out of — Randi laughing at Pace's elbow ("a phone that didn't ring and a kitchen she'd never seen"), and the closing line's "small sentence still standing at a window, watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time" — those are the text handing *me* the unease directly. That last image especially: her body drowns out the part of her that noticed something. It's the "forbidden kiss" residue from two chapters back, the minor-key thing that "did not go all the way; it settled somewhere and stayed, waiting." The book keeps planting these little watchmen in Vee and then flooding them out with heat. I trust that they're accumulating.

**Erotic charge:** the truck. Oh, the truck. This is the hottest the book has run on me in a while, and it earned it — the blowjob built out of the whole evening ("greedy with the whole night... *Vee's boyfriend* said six times to six measuring faces, all of it going into this"), his hand going still in her hair, "the man who did six things at once, down to zero." Then her undressing herself on a moving county road, pulling the lace aside herself — "an offering she had to keep making, choosing it again every second it went on." That line is doing the real erotic work: consent rendered as continuous active choosing, her shame ("there was so much of her") folded into the heat rather than pardoned. And the September-mountain-road rhyme, his trembling hand then vs. dry-and-easy now, the county-road-in-December rhyme, the coat with the dark bloom she kept — the wanting is layered with memory and it burns. Peak of the book's heat for me? Close.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** I have no suspicion of *Vee* — she's radiant and being loved and can't see the floor under the boat. Pace I still can't fully settle; tonight he was almost purely wonderful, and "Vee's boyfriend" cost him something to say and he said it, which softened me. But the withholding man who spends exactly one credential "with no plans to produce another" is still, precisely, controlling how much he gives. Randi is where all my dread lives, and the dread is affection-shaped, which is worse. When she toasts "my beautiful best friend" she means it *and* she's drowning, and Vee is toasting back inside a lie she'll one day have to reread.

**Friction:** almost none — the party ran a touch long in its parade of "Pace hands person their line" (Danny, Theo, Meg, Kayla, Cassie in sequence, same move each time), but it built the "they share an interest — you" payoff, so I forgave it. The only thumb I felt was that closing sentence spelling the watchman out; but it's so pretty I let it.

**The titles.** "Among Friends" — sits perfectly and a little cruelly. On the surface: Vee finally has all her friends in one room, among friends, safe and warm. Underneath: the two people running a plan on her are *among* those friends, indistinguishable, seated at her table being toasted. "Among friends" is also what you say to mean *you can relax, nobody here will hurt you* — which is exactly the thing I can't quite believe. The volume line **"WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION"** — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" is the ghost behind it, and this chapter is one long warm reception in every sense: the pine room, the welcome, the hospitality that is also a trap being sprung slowly. "A Warm Reception" reads sweet on the cover and colder the more of the book I hold. No tagline supplied that I can see beyond those cover/series lines.

**What I want / dread next:** I want Vee to keep her happiness one more chapter. I dread the reread — the day she learns the two friendliest people at her party knew each other in the dark first. Guess from here: the "brunches" and Randi's kiss are drifting closer to the surface with Pace watching the parking-lot kiss "from twelve feet across"; something in the Vee-Randi pull is going to stop being explainable as French. And Randi's held-down word feels closer to breaking than Pace's.
