## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This one worked me over sideways, and quietly. It's the "meet the people" chapter, the one where Pace walks into Vee's actual world, and on the surface it's the warmest thing in the book — and I spent the whole warm surface braced, because I know something Vee doesn't, and this is the first chapter where that knowledge sat in the room *with all three of them physically present.*

Let me start with Pace, because he genuinely disarmed me. That kitchen opening — "I want to meet your people" said into the sink with a plate in his hand — and then the reveal that he's been shelving her throwaway sentences all fall. Meg's boots, the heel coming up out of the right one, Kayla who can "talk the paint off a wall," the Bricklayers, Cassie's roommate saga, Kayla's sister's *dress*. He handed every one of them back, one-handed, without turning around. That's the most attractive he's been to me — not the sex, the *listening*. And then the party itself, where his whole method is laid bare: "he walked around handing people the first line and then he stood back and laughed at their second one." Danny's bench, Theo's jazz "where does it start, *for you*" — the key going into the lock. It's lovely and it's real and I believe every person left that table taller.

And here's my problem, the same one the book keeps handing me: it's *also* a technique. I've watched him do this. Randi does exactly this — "she let him feel like it was his idea," the lavender sweater, the counter guy folding — and Vee opens this very chapter narrating Randi's version of the trick admiringly. So the chapter puts Randi's method and Pace's method side by side in the *first two pages* and I don't think that's an accident I'm inventing. These two run the same play. And they are, per the jacket and everything I carry, secret lovers who chose this girl together.

Which brings me to the thing that actually gutted me: **"Vee's boyfriend."** Said flat, at volume for three, to Randi first, and then six more times to six measuring faces. Vee gets what she's been aching for since December — the word, out loud, in public, spent on her. And I felt her joy climb her throat, "the first swallow of something hot," and I could not enjoy it, because of the hand. *"Randi's hand closed hard around hers, hard enough to feel the bones."* Vee reads it as her best friend being happy for her. I read it as the secret lover of the man standing right there hearing him publicly claim another woman as his — and squeezing so she doesn't do anything with her face. That squeeze is the whole book in one gesture. It's the first time the two lovers have had to perform *not being lovers* in front of the girl, and it's agony to watch precisely because Vee is beaming at the middle of it.

And then Randi's glazed quiet at the end — turning the glass a slow quarter at a time, "more of it arriving than she seemed to have planned," the toast that slid from "highlight of my week" to "highlight of my *year*." Vee is "foolishly proud" she can move Randi, who moves rooms and is so rarely moved. I don't think Vee's watching pride. I think I'm watching something breaking in Randi in real time and getting relabeled as sentiment. "To my beautiful best friend" — *beautiful*, her coded word — lifted at the exact table where the man she shares a bed with just called this girl his girlfriend seven times. Whatever the game was, it is not a game to Randi anymore, and this chapter is where I watched that cost her something on the page.

The one moment Vee's own instrument twitched: the twinge. *"him, easy, somewhere she wasn't, being wonderful at a woman"* — Randi laughing, ponytail swinging, Pace at her elbow with the pitcher. Vee gets the stinger of it "from Christmas, from a phone that didn't ring and a kitchen she'd never seen," and then talks herself out of it: this is Randi, my own doing, "some corner of her didn't know how to watch a thing go right without checking it for the trapdoor." Reader, the trapdoor is *right there.* She looked directly at it and named her own suspicion a character flaw. That's the tagline made flesh — every yes freely given, and here's her freely deciding not to see.

Then the truck. The heat is high and it landed — the blowjob in the dark cab built on "Vee's boyfriend said six times to six measuring faces, all of it going into this," and that stillness of his, "the man who did six things at once, down to zero," she could live on it. And the drive home, jeans worked down "a graceless honest inch at a time," his hand where her own hand had been denied on the county road — the book folding its own earlier scenes back in (the September mountain road, the cold vents, his hand trembling then and steady now; the coat's bloom). It's genuinely hot and it's genuinely tender and I was moved by it. But the chapter's own last sentence does the work I can't shake: *"if some small sentence in her was still standing at a window somewhere, watching a glass turn a slow quarter at a time, it couldn't have made itself heard tonight over her own blood, and it didn't try."* That's Vee's instrument telling her something about Randi's glass-turning and getting drowned out on purpose. The book *tells me* she chose not to hear it.

Pace's line in the truck is the other knife: "We get along. They share an interest." "Who does?" "Randi. Your friends. All of them. You." Vee melts — they all have *her* in common. And it's true, and it's also the flattest possible cover for the specific thing Randi and Pace share, which is not Vee-as-friend. He answers her direct question — "what did you and Randi talk about" — with a true sentence that hides the whole world. That's the seam I keep watching, and he sewed it shut smiling.

**Trust/suspicion:** No longer abstract. This chapter *earned* the dread on the page for the first time via Randi's body — the bone-crushing hand, the glazed glass-turning, the toast that overshot. I'm not inventing foreshadow; her physical reactions don't fit "best friend delighted for best friend" and fit "lover watching her lover claim someone else" exactly. Pace I trust *less* the more perfectly he performs, because the performance is indistinguishable from Randi's con. Vee I trust completely and fear for completely.

**Friction:** Very little. The party maybe ran one beat long — Danny, Theo, Kayla, Cassie, each a set-piece of the same move — and by the fourth "handing people the first line" I'd fully gotten the technique and the fifth was confirmation not development. But it's the good kind of length; the accumulation *is* the point (six measuring faces, six "boyfriends"). The one line that's the author's thumb, gently: "she wasn't going to look at when" and "some corner of her didn't know how to watch a thing go right without checking it for the trapdoor" — both a touch on-the-nose about Vee's refusal to see, telling me she's not-seeing rather than just letting her not-see. But they're small and in-voice.

**Titles:** "Among Friends" — on the surface, the cozy truth: her friends, gathered, among friends. On the reread it curdles, because the two people running the same seduction-play *are* among these friends, unrecognized as anything but friends, and the deepest intimacy in the room (Pace/Randi) is the one nobody at the table can see. "Among friends" is also where you let your guard down — where every yes gets freely given. And **With a Long Spoon** — I still only half-hold it, but tonight it rhymed: you sup with the devil with a long spoon, and here's a whole warm supper, a buffet even, everyone dealt out and gathered back in "new arrangements" by the food itself. The long spoon is the distance that keeps you safe, and nobody at this table has one — least of all Vee, who's reaching across every seam with both bare hands.

**What I want/dread next:** Vee promised Randi "the whole debrief" tomorrow — and I dread that call now the way I dreaded it last chapter, doubled, because I've now *seen* what the reunion working, and Pace saying "boyfriend," did to Randi's face. *(Guess from here:)* the pressure isn't going to come from Vee finding out — it's going to come from Randi cracking under her own success, the architect who can't stand in the wings watching her creation claimed out loud. That glazed quiet and the toast that overshot feel like the first fissure. I want the next chapter and I'm afraid of it, which is exactly where this book keeps leaving me.
