# Cold read — In His Hands

*scene: scenes/in-his-hands.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: new-ordinary*

## Reader reaction

This one landed on me as pure honey with one cold thread pulled taut through it, and the cold thread is the photograph.

The opening sex is good — the "red curtain drawn against the lamp," the "deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of." I'm inside her body and it works; the "*He's all mine*" thought is the kind of sweet certainty this book keeps handing Vee right before it makes me wince for her. His crawling around to lay his cheek on the lower slope of her breast, the barely-kisses, her theory that "they're not about sex for him" being gently disproved — I liked that. It's playful in a way I haven't gotten much of. Pace teasing, being a boy who "got away with something," grinning over the top of the phone. He's charming here and I felt it.

But the photo. God. She offers it — echoing, for me, the uncropped induction photo she once sent him, except this time it's a nude and she's the one initiating. And the exchange that made me go still:

"Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone."
"Never. I'd never share your photograph."

She asks him never to share *her*. He promises never to share the *photograph*. That's a narrowing, and the text put them one line apart on purpose so I'd feel the seam. Did the page earn my suspicion? Yes — barely, but yes. It's the exact kind of precise, technically-true answer this man gives ("there was never a note," never saying love). And her own narration hands me the dread on a plate: "pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them. She knew all of that. It was why she'd made him promise… and believing him was the brave part." She names the danger, disarms it by trusting him, calls the trust brave. With the two-women setup I'm carrying, and the inferred Randi coordination, a nude of Vee living on a device that a partner-in-a-plan could see — that is the coldest object anyone's put on the table yet. "gave him herself to keep" is beautiful and it made my stomach drop.

The composing itself is hot and unsettling at once. She wants to arrange herself; he stops her — "No, not like that. You'll have to wait" — and does it himself, setting her elbow, her hair, her hand on her hip, "a kiss to the point of her shoulder, a kiss to the rise of her hip," until she's "laid out like a woman in an old calendar." Total control rendered as tenderness, exactly the register I've come to trust the book for. And it's arousing. It worked on me. He also fusses over the light, tries three shots, "That's the one" — he's making an image, curating it, the way he makes everything.

The kitchen half is warm and slack in the good way. The chicken piccata built with the same wine she's drinking, the taste-separation again ("she could take the thing apart on her tongue"), "there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was." That last clause is about as close as this book comes to letting her half-see, and I like that it stays a pleasure to her.

Two things twisted. "This is my home" and then the closed door on his parents — "I don't go back. That was all of it." The withheld-family thread gets its hardest beat yet, and I felt real sympathy, plus the ache of watching Vee learn where the walls are and choose to "let the quiet be kind." She's training herself not to touch him where he's sealed. And then Sheri coming for Thanksgiving — the blunt friend, "the one he tells the truth to" — "She's alone." I got Vee's little jealous flicker ("another woman at his table on a day meant for family") and then it going out. From *my* seat, Sheri at that table isn't a rival, it's the one relationship in his life that runs on truth, and I found it oddly moving that the two people he feeds on that day are the two he doesn't perform for — Sheri, and… well, the question is whether Vee is performed-for or not.

Friction: almost none. The nail-tapping-the-tooth gesture ("brought a nail up to her front tooth and tapped it three times") is a repeated tic I've now noticed twice, mild. Nothing felt like the author's thumb — even the promise-narrowing is done with a light enough touch that I could believe it's just how he talks.

What I want next: I dread that photograph surfacing. *Guess from here:* it gets seen by Randi, or it's an asset for whatever the two tracks are building toward, and the "I'd never share your photograph" will turn out true-to-the-letter and false-to-her. I also want the Thanksgiving scene with Sheri — I trust her to say a true thing out loud.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Reunion sex, then Vee offers Pace a nude photo so he has her when she's away. He refuses to let her pose herself — composes her by hand into a calendar-pinup, kissing each placement, shoots it on her phone (better camera), curates the light, picks "the one." She makes him "promise you'll never share me with anyone"; he answers "Never. I'd never share your *photograph*" — a narrowing she doesn't catch. She names the real danger (nudes get loose, ruin women), trusts him anyway, calls the trust "the brave part," sends it: "gave him herself to keep." Then kitchen: he cooks chicken piccata from scratch (same wine in the pan she's drinking); she tastes every component separately again. Thanksgiving talk: she's flying home to Ohio; his parents are a sealed door — "I don't go back," and she learns not to push. Sheri is coming to his Thanksgiving; "She's alone." Vee's jealous flicker rises and goes out.

- **Randi** — Vee's confidante and (inferred) Pace's partner; not present here but the reason the photograph frightens me.

**How I feel.** Golden-on-top, cold-underneath, as ever — the photo scene both aroused me and dropped my stomach. Pace charmed me more than usual (playful, boyish) while doing the most controlling and potentially dangerous thing yet. The narrowed promise earned a small, specific suspicion, on the page, not just from pattern. I love him no less; I dread more for Vee.
