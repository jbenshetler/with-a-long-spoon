# Reader reaction — "In His Hands"

This one ran hot and then, in the last beat, put a cold thumb on the back of my neck. For most of it I was just *with* Vee — the post-orgasm collapse, the red curtain of her hair kissing him blind, that sore-tomorrow gladness. The opening is genuinely tender: Pace lying square across her, his cheek on the lower slope of her breast, the small wandering kisses she decides aren't "about sex for him," and then her nipple coming up bright "with no say from her" and spoiling her own theory, pleasing her to no end. I love how the book keeps letting her be *right about him and wrong about herself* at once. The whole first movement is her thinking *He's all mine* and *He takes such comfort there* — and I believed the comfort, and I believed her joy.

The photograph. That's where I sat up. The whole scene is rendered as trust rewarded — "believing him was the brave part," she sends it before her nerve leaves her, "gave him herself to keep." And Pace posing her himself, kissing each place his hands set her — shoulder, hip, knee — laying her out "like a woman in an old calendar," is tender and controlling in the exact proportion the book always mixes them. But I could not read past *"Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." / "Never. I'd never share your photograph."* without a flinch, because I know what she doesn't: there is a *her* he already shares everything about, in bed, with Randi. The promise is precise — "your photograph" — and precision from Pace has always been a place where a trapdoor hides. I don't have proof the photo goes anywhere. But the text earned the unease here; it's not me being on guard. The tapping of the nail against her tooth before she offers it — a little tell of a decision being made — landed as her stepping willingly onto something.

And the version of herself she goes to build for the camera unsettled me in a good, sad way: she leaves the sated wrecked woman in the bathroom and comes back "composed, arranged, a girl who had decided to be looked at." He *wanted the wrecked one* — "I like you exactly like this" — and she couldn't let him have her, had to put the face back on. That's the whole shame regime speaking, and it made me ache. She can give him her body helplessly but not her undone face; that she reserves, or can't help reserving.

The kitchen is where the swoon fully worked on me — no complications, just heat gone domestic. The chicken piccata cooked in real time, the pan's "talk" changing, lemon zest in a bright rain, him pouring in the wine she's drinking and telling her the pan needs it worse than she does. And her tasting — being able to take the dish apart on her tongue now, when she "used to just eat" — "there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was." That's the feeding motif doing its quiet devotional work, and it's lovely, and it's also *him remaking her palate*, her body, her weight, all of it his doing and gladly ceded. I melted and noted the ledger at once.

Then the two flickers at the end, which are the real payload. *"Are you going home?" / "This is my home."* — easy, warm. But when she presses to his parents, "the ease went out of it and left something level and exact behind," each word "set down clean and apart" — *"I don't go back."* No door. That's the same "Not tonight" wall as the thigh scar; the checkpoint's Ohio-plain parents and the hallway exile suddenly have a cost attached I hadn't weighed. And Sheri coming for Thanksgiving with a pie she'll "pretend she made" — the fondness there is plain and *unguarded*, which is itself telling: he closes the door on his parents and opens it wide on Sheri. Vee's little jealous flicker ("another woman at his table on a day meant for family") going out when he says "She's alone" was sweet, but I noticed the book quietly stacking women at Pace's table again.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** Randi's absent here, so my usual wariness had nowhere to sit — and the chapter smartly relocates the unease onto *Pace*, via the photo and the shut door about his family. The prose never tips its hand; there's no thumb on the scale, no telegraph. My suspicion is entirely earned by his own precision and by what I carry that Vee doesn't.

**Erotic charge:** peaks early — the nipple rising under his mouth "with no say from her," the posing, "you do this to me," "sometimes I take care of it myself, thinking of you." Genuinely hot, and warmer than clinical because it's threaded with her wonder at being wanted like this. Goes gently slack (in a lovely way) through the cooking, which trades heat for tenderness.

**Friction:** almost none. If anything, "believing him was the brave part" is the one line where I felt the book lean a half-inch toward telling me how to feel about the photo — but it's inside Vee's POV and true to her, so I let it pass.

**The titles:** *In His Hands* — after this chapter it's almost too apt. He literally arranges her body with his hands for the photo, kisses each place; his hands make the food that remakes her. She is in his hands, fully, gladly, and the phrase has a grip in it I now hear. Against the volume line **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** and *In His Hands*: "sup with a long spoon" is what you do when you dine with the devil, and the book keeps setting the most gorgeous tables. The tagline promise — polite invitation, sup carefully — sits right under all this feeding. It points me toward a bill coming due for all this being-fed and being-held. That photograph feels like the first thing she's handed over that she can't fold in thirds and take back.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the Thanksgiving-home trip to give Vee a beat outside his gravity — Cassie's compass, her own bed, her mother's voice tested against being cherished. I dread (guess from here) that the photo surfaces where Vee can't control it — that "I'd never share *your* photograph" is a sentence with a loophole named Randi.
