# Cold read — In His Hands

*scene: scenes/in-his-hands.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: school-nights*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me erotically more than the last few, and it also frightened me more, and those two things happened in the same gesture, which I think is exactly the trap of this book landing on me directly.

The opening sex is genuinely good — "shaking and stupid and full of him," the red curtain of hair, the way he rolls his whole body square across hers and lays his cheek on the lower slope of her breast. That's tender, post-coital, unhurried, and I felt it. The bit where he mouths at her and her nipple comes up "with no say from her, spent as she was" — the body-ahead-of-the-head motif again, and here it's warm and real and I didn't resent it. Her little internal theory — "They're not about sex for him" / "Which rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end" — is charming and human. "A girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it" is a lovely line and it made me like *her*, and, for a beat, like *him* for giving her that.

And then the photograph. This is where the cold came in for me, hard, because I know things she doesn't. She offers it — "Would a picture help?" She thinks she's the one giving. Her one condition: "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." And his answer is *precise* in a way that made the back of my neck prickle: "Never. I'd never share your photograph." Not "I'd never share you." He answered the narrower thing. From where I sit, this man harvests — the blonde's insinuations were true, Randi drains everything to him, Sheri is his placed eyes — and now there is an image of Vee, staged and arranged *by his hands* (he moves her, elbow, hair, hip, knee, kisses each placement — "In His Hands," the title, is literal), that she *sent*, that crossed to Delivered, "and gave him herself to keep." The whole chapter is the not-taking that is the taking: she believes she's the author of the picture, she even thinks "some part of why she could think so was that the picture was his, was her as his eyes had her." She has been arranged into an asset and experiences it as being adored.

The line that did the most damage to me was the honest one: *"under the wanting to keep it, the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this. She looked at him, at the plain open want in his face, and she believed him, and believing him was the brave part. She sent it before the nerve could leave her."* She *felt* the alarm — the cold sane thought — and overrode it with trust. That's the book's whole engine in one beat: the yes freely given, the alarm noticed and dismissed. It's not that she's stupid; it's that she chose bravery, and the bravery is the wound.

The dinner is the anesthetic in full flower again — the lemon chicken, the pan's "talk," feeding her tastes off the spoon. I'll admit the cooking goes long. Gorgeous, sensory, "the yellow off them in a fine bright rain," but it's the third or fourth time we've watched him cook lavishly and watch her eat "as though she'd just handed him something," and I felt the repetition. The register is so consistent it's starting to sedate *me* a little, which may be the point, but as a reader I felt the drag.

Two live wires in the dinner, though. First: "This is my home." / "I don't go back." His face going "level and exact," each word "set down clean and apart from the last" — that's the flat over-ordered voice I've clocked before as the tell for a covered wound. The parents are a locked door, connected to leaving home at 15. And Vee does the thing that's becoming her signature and her doom: "she took her hand off it. She didn't ask." Same as the scar. She keeps choosing the not-asking. I feel that choice accreting into a cage.

Second, and this is what closed the chapter with a chill for me: **Sheri's coming to Thanksgiving.** "We cycle together. She'll bring a pie and pretend she made it." Vee reads it as fondness for a lonely friend, and she talks herself out of the flicker of jealousy — "neither with anywhere else to be." But I know Sheri is his placed eyes on Vee. So the picture I'm left with is Pace at his own table on Thanksgiving with his watcher, while Vee flies to Ohio grateful he won't be alone. The dramatic irony there is quietly awful. And "She's alone" — said "the way a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed" — is exactly how he talks about *Vee*. The feeding-as-annexation motif just quietly extended to Sheri, and it made me wonder what Sheri *is* to him, and whether "alone" is a status he cultivates in people he keeps.

Trust: zero movement toward Pace, deepening dread. Vee I ache for more, not less — because she noticed the alarm and chose past it. No Randi at all (three chapters offstage now, though the green-citrus wine — the Chilean sauvignon blanc, grapefruit/lime/cut-grass — is *her scent* poured into the pan and the glass, which I clocked hard. Her scent is now literally in Vee's mouth and cooked into the food. That can't be accidental).

**The title** — "In His Hands" — is doing double work and it's the good kind. On the surface: he arranges her body with his hands for the photo, tenderly. On reread it's the whole condition of her: she is in his hands, the image is in his hands, she is being handled and calls it being held. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on arrival. The book's title and tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is almost a thesis statement for that line. She offered the photo. She sent it. Freely. And the cold sane thought was there and she called overriding it *brave*.

What I want next: I dread that photograph. It's out of her hands and into his and I've been told he shares his harvest with Randi. I want to know what "I don't go back" is. And I badly want Randi back on the page, or Cassie, or Vee's mother — some outside air, because this chapter was almost entirely inside the warm box.
