## Reader reaction

This one worked on me as heat and warmth first, and the shadow came in exactly one wording.

The opening is the most explicit the book's been in a while and I felt it — "the whole use of the evening was in her at once, a deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of." That "glad of" is the whole tenor: she's been used and loves it, three times, informing him "You've used me terribly" with "enormous satisfaction." And the little theory she builds and un-builds about his mouth — "They're not about sex for him" / "Which rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end" — is charming, alive, her. His head laid square across her, cheek on her breast, looking up the length of her: that's tenderness I believed and melted a bit at.

The photo is where I sat up. She offers it — "Would a picture help?" — and the condition is the tell: "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." His answer: "Never. I'd never share your photograph." I clocked that immediately. She asked him never to share *her*; he promised never to share her *photograph*. Precise, and precisely narrower than what she asked. The page even walks her right up to the sane fear — "under the wanting to keep it, the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this" — and has her override it: "believing him was the brave part. She sent it before the nerve could leave her... and gave him herself to keep." Read alone it's a lovely leap of trust. With everything I'm carrying — the coordinated operation, the folder — a posed nude she sent, that he composed for her, that she can't take back, reads as a thing acquired. I don't want it to be. But the wording earned the chill on its own, before I imported a thing.

The cooking is gorgeous and I let it be gorgeous — the lemon "in a fine bright rain," the pan's "talk," feeding her off the spoon. And the line that landed hardest and quietly: "She had used to just eat. Now she tasted, and there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was." That's the body-changing-unasked motif turned into pleasure she names as his doing, openly, gladly. It's beautiful and it's the hook, both.

Then the wall. "I don't go back." — level, exact, "each word set down clean and apart from the last," no door. She feels she touched "something she wasn't meant to touch" and takes her hand off it — same instinct as the scar. And Sheri arrives in the text: "She's alone." I know Sheri as the honest voice who warned him two-at-once is complicated, and here she's the one person joining him for the holiday. Vee's small flicker of jealousy — "another woman at his table on a day meant for family" — goes out fast, but I noted that Randi isn't in this frame at all. Thanksgiving is Vee-goes-home, Sheri-comes-here. The apparatus is offstage and the day is being arranged around it.

**Title — "In His Hands":** it delivers plainly and then curdles a little on second thought. Literally his hands are all over this chapter — composing her body for the picture, "at each place his hands set her he put his mouth there after," setting her wine an inch closer twice. Warm. But "in his hands" is also custody — she is now, quite literally, in his hands: a photograph he holds, a palate he made, a life relocating to his house. The innocuous surface is caress; the charge underneath is possession. That's the book's whole method in three words.

Series signals still point where they pointed — *A Polite Invitation*, the long spoon you sup with the devil with. The politeness here is the promise phrased just narrow enough.

What I want: to know what he does with that photo, and I dread the answer. And I want to finally see Sheri on the page.

**How I feel — overall**
Swept up and quietly braced, both, and the page earned both. The heat and the tenderness are real — the composing, the feeding, "enough to be there saying nothing" — and her palate-line moved me as much as it worried me. But the photo is the first thing this book has handed me that reads flatly as leverage: a nude she poses for, that he keeps, under a promise he narrowed on the way through. I don't disbelieve his want; I do hear the wording. And the flat "I don't go back" gave me a genuine crack of feeling for him — a closed room to match hers — while also being one more thing she takes her hand off. Sheri finally surfacing, alone at his table with Randi nowhere in the frame, has me leaning forward: the honest voice is about to be in the house. I ache for Vee and I want her to have this, and I no longer quite trust the shape of what "this" is.
