## Reader reaction

This one seduced me and scared me at the same time, and it did both quietly, which is the worst.

The opening is pure sated warmth and it's *good* — "shaking and stupid and full of him," the red curtain of hair, the soreness "she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of." I believe her happiness completely. The bit where he lies square across her with his cheek on her breast and does the small kisses, and she constructs a little theory (*they're not about sex for him*) and then her own nipple betrays her — "it drew tight under his mouth and came up bright, with no say from her" — and *he feels it happen too*, the corners of his mouth pulling against her. That's the shame-engine again but tender, playful, mutual. It landed as genuinely lovely. Her voice is delightful here: "You've used me terribly," "feed me first, or I'll starve to death at this table." I like her. I like *them*.

And then the photo. The whole middle of the chapter is a naked picture of Vee, and I went cold in the exact place the prose stayed warm. She's the one who offers it — "Would a picture help?" — three taps of the nail on her tooth first (Randi's glass-fidget lineage, that little tell that a decision's being made). She makes him promise: "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." And his answer is *precise* in a way that snagged me hard: **"Never. I'd never share your photograph."** She asked him never to share *her*. He promised never to share *her photograph*. I don't know if I'm meant to catch that gap, but I caught it, and after thirty-five chapters of a man who pre-starts every meal and words every consent to the letter, I do not think that seam is accidental. The text even walks right up to my dread and names it for me: "that pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them. She knew all of that." And then she sends it anyway, and "believing him was the brave part," and "gave him herself to keep." *To keep* — like the scar he means "to keep," like the drawer, like the folded green panties. A naked photo of Vee now lives on his phone. With Brooke's folder out there and a threesome she doesn't know she's being aimed at, that photograph is a loaded gun on the mantel and I hated watching her hand it over while I was also, honestly, aroused by the scene. That doubling is the book working on me exactly as it means to.

The posing is the tell inside the tell. She arranges herself for pinup and he stops her — "No, not like that. You'll have to wait." — and does it *himself*, sets her elbow, draws her hair back, places her hand on her hip, moves her legs "where he wanted them," a kiss to each place he sets her. "Laid out like a woman in an old calendar, warm under his hands, with no wish to argue with any of it." She thinks she's giving him a picture; he's composing it. And then: "her as his eyes had her, and she didn't want to give the phone back." The picture she loves is the one where he authored how she's seen — same as the remade palate last chapter, the dress "made to her," her senses, her image, all refined by him and experienced by her as her own loveliness. He even shot it on *her* phone, "because hers took the better picture and they both knew it," which is such a small perfect detail of him getting exactly what he wants while it reads as practicality.

Then the kitchen, and I relaxed into it against my better judgment because it's so warm. The chicken piccata cooked in real time, feeding her tastes off the spoon, "she could take the thing apart on her tongue" — the palate-payoff from last chapter cashed again, "and she knew exactly whose doing that was." That line used to read as pure devotion; now it reads to me as a woman noting her own authorship-by-him and calling it love. I don't think she's wrong to. I think it's both, which is the whole trick.

Two new cracks opened at the table and I'm turning them over. **The parents.** "Are you going home?" / "This is my home." She pushes gently and he shuts — "I don't go back." "There was no door left open for her to step through." That's the first time I've watched *Pace* be the one behind a shut door instead of the one holding it open on the far side. Something happened with Ohio. The scar, no father at her induction, "I don't go back" — I don't have the piece yet but there's a wound there and it's his, not the design's. And **Sheri**: "She's alone." "He said it the way a person says it about someone they're glad to be able to feed." Vee's jealousy flickers and dies honestly. But my reader-ear pricked, because feeding the alone one is his whole grammar of possession — he'd have made it for an empty house. Sheri's at the Thanksgiving table with no family; so, increasingly, is Vee, her bag riding light, her things in his drawer. I don't distrust Sheri. I notice she's another woman he keeps fed and alone-adjacent, and the "two-women arithmetic" from way back ticked in the back of my head.

Randi is not here and hasn't been for ages, and this chapter's very warmth is making her absence louder. A naked photo now exists. I cannot stop thinking about who else might end up seeing it.

Erotic charge: high in the first third — the nipple-betrayal and "Again?" worked cleanly on me — then it deliberately cools into unease through the photo, which is right; the scene doesn't want me only turned on. The cooking is sensual-domestic rather than sexual and I was fine with that.

Friction: almost none. The one place the thumb pressed a little was "that pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them. She knew all of that." — that's the text naming my dread out loud, close to over-explaining. But it's inside her own knowing-and-doing-anyway, so I forgave it; it made her brave rather than stupid.

What I want / dread next: I dread that photograph, plainly. Guess from here: it surfaces where it can do maximum damage — the folder, Randi, the reveal — and the "her" vs "her photograph" wording is the loophole that lets him keep his promise while it happens. I want to know what "I don't go back" costs him. And I'm still braced for Randi's return.
