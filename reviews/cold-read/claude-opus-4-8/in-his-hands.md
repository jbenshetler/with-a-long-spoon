## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This one made my stomach drop, and it did it quietly, which is worse.

The chapter opens in a warm blur — she comes down over his chest inside a "red curtain" of her hair, that lovely "shaking and stupid and full of him," the "deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of." I'm right there with her; the intimacy is real and I don't doubt it. Then he does that thing that undoes me every time — lies across her, sets his head on her breast, the small barely-kisses, and she narrates *He's all mine* and *he takes such comfort there.* That "all mine" is the line I flinched at. Because I know something she doesn't. She's holding the wrong end of the possession.

And then the photo. This is the whole chapter for me, everything else is table-setting. She offers it — "Would a picture help?" — and I watched it happen with genuine dread, because of the promise: *"Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." "Never. I'd never share your photograph."* A man who is, as far as I know, secretly conspiring with another woman to install this girl in their bed, promising he'd never share her. The word *share* is doing terrible double work and only I can hear it. When she watches the bar cross to Delivered and thinks she "gave him herself to keep" — I did not feel warmth, I felt the trap close another notch. The narration even hands me the cold sane thought — *"pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them"* — and then has her override it because *"believing him was the brave part."* That's the tagline made flesh: the yes is free, considered, brave even, and that's exactly what makes it a trap.

What's masterful and awful is that **he arranges her with his hands** — "he pressed her shoulder... set her elbow under her... moved her legs where he wanted them," a kiss set at each place he positioned her, "laid out like a woman in an old calendar." She wanted pinup and he *made* her the pinup, composed her, chose the light, worked the blinds, took the one that was "the one." She thinks the picture is beautiful partly because "it was her as his eyes had her." He is literally authoring how she is seen and she experiences it as being cherished. The induction seed pays off here — she "kept the interior" that time, sent an uncropped photo; here she brushes her hair, puts her poppy-red mouth back on, becomes "a girl who had decided to be looked at" and hands over a fully naked, fully composed image. The self she withheld before, she's spending now. That felt like a threshold crossed.

The kitchen half is a breather and I mostly loved it as craft — the chicken piccata built step by step, the lemon "in a fine bright rain," feeding her tastes off the spoon, her palate coming apart into named flavors again ("and she knew exactly whose doing that was"). It's tender and it's also the same quiet horror: everything she now *is* — how she tastes, how she sees herself — is "his doing," and she says so gladly. The food-as-love motif is running at full warmth.

Two things pricked me in that half. First, **Thanksgiving.** She's flying home; she asks if he goes home to his parents, and the door slams again — *"I don't go back,"* voice "level and exact," "each word set down clean and apart." Same closed room as the scar, the "parents" thing that keeps sealing shut. I still don't know what's behind it and I'm now quite hungry to. Second — and this is the one that made the golden mood go cold — **Sheri's coming over for Thanksgiving.** "We ride together on the weekends. She'll bring a pie and pretend she made it." Vee feels a flicker — "another woman at his table on a day meant for family" — and then reassures herself Sheri's alone and it's fine. But I remember Sheri. I remember "two at once." I don't think Sheri is only a friend, and even if she is, the picture of Pace and one woman at the holiday table while another flies home is exactly the geometry this book keeps almost-touching and never quite letting collide. The flicker Vee suppresses is the one I couldn't.

Nobody moved much for me — I trust Vee's heart completely and fear for her more each chapter; Pace stays the thing I can't fault and can't trust, and the photo pushed the distrust harder than any chapter since the bench. Randi's still offstage, a held breath. Sheri came back into frame and I don't like what she might be for.

The **erotic charge** worked, but its true peak wasn't the sex (rendered mostly in warm aftermath — the coming-down, the second and third rounds off-page or glancing). The peak was the *photograph* — the arranging of her body, the shutter, the sending. That's where the heat and the dread fused, and it's the most charged the book has been in a while precisely because it's not just arousal, it's exposure.

Friction: almost none. The one place I felt the thumb was the double-underlining of the photo's danger — "that pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them... She knew all of that." It's a hair over-explained, spelling out the risk the scene had already made me feel. But honestly I'll forgive it, because a naive reader without my suspicion needs it, and it makes her override deliberate rather than naive — which is the point.

**The title — "In His Hands."** It gave me a chill before I even finished. Literally: he arranges her body with his hands, sets and kisses each placement, holds the phone, hands her the image, holds her hand at the counter. Figuratively: the photograph is now *in his hands*, and so, more and more, is she — her body, her palate, her self-image, all "his doing." It doesn't give away plot but it colors the whole chapter as a handing-over. Innocent surface (a lover's caretaking hands), a cold second read (everything she is now sits in the hands of a man running a game). The **book title and tagline** are just loud here — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." She freely, bravely, considered-ly hits send. That IS the trap, on the page, in real time.

What I want next: I dread that photo. I want to know it stays where he promised and I don't believe it will. I want the "I don't go back" door to finally open. And I'm bracing — *guess from here* — that Thanksgiving, with Vee gone home and Pace at his table with Sheri (and Randi somewhere), is where the two worlds finally scrape together, or at least where the seam shows.
