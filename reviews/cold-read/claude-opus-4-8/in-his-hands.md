# Cold read — In His Hands

## Reader reaction

So this is the chapter I was braced for without knowing it, and it worked on me and chilled me in the same breath, which is exactly the book at its best.

First, the heat — it's real and it's high. The opening comes in already mid-collapse, "a red curtain drawn against the lamp," her folding down over his chest "blind," and the whole render of afterglow — "a deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of" — is generous and specific and lands on the body. Then the slow crawl of his mouth up her breast, the nipple coming up "with no say from her, spent as she was," and his lips just *holding* it, not taking it — that's beautifully observed, and her little interior theory-and-refutation ("Which rather spoiled her theory, and pleased her to no end") is the warmest kind of comedy. "With Pace she was a girl who had a body, not a body that happened to have a girl attached to it" — that's the whole ache of her put in one line, and it swept me. I believe her pleasure completely.

And then the photograph. This is where the chapter split me clean down the middle. On the page it's tender past arguing: he takes his *time*, tilts the lampshade, "That's the one," and then looks at her over the phone "at her and not the picture of her," grinning "like a boy who'd gotten away with something." She gets handed back a version of herself lovelier than she could catch alone, "her as his eyes had her," and doesn't want to give the phone back. The bravery framing — "believing him was the brave part," she "sent it before the nerve could leave her" and "gave him herself to keep" — is genuinely moving as a beat about trust.

But I'm the reader who knows what he and Randi run, and a nude glamour image of the mark, now on *both* phones, sent willingly and framed as her own courageous act of trust — that is the single most convertible piece of material in the whole book so far. And there's a seam the page laid down that I can't un-see: she says "promise you'll never share me with anyone," and he answers "Never. I'd never share your photograph." She asked about *sharing her*; he promised about the *photograph*. Maybe it's nothing, a man matching her words. But it's a narrower promise than the one she asked for, and the careful precision of this man is established. "Gave him herself to keep" is the most beautiful and the most frightening line in the chapter at once. The dread here is no longer only carried-in; the page put the object in my hand.

The posing is the other thing that did double work. "No, not like that. You'll have to wait" — and then he arranges her himself, elbow, hair, hand along the hip, legs "where he wanted them," a kiss set at each place his hands set her. It's rendered as devotion and it *is* devotion, warm under his hands, "no wish to argue with any of it." But it's also her being handled into shape, made into an image he designed. The maker's hands as tenderness-and-enclosure, the exact chord — and it's the title.

The cooking is a whole aria and I let it wash over me — the lemon "in a fine bright rain," the pan's "talk," "poured in enough of the wine to make her genuinely sad." Her being able to take the meal apart on her tongue now, "she had used to just eat. Now she tasted, and there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was" — I love it as intimacy and it names the thing precisely: he's remade her palate, and there's no going back. The line is warm and it's about permanent alteration, both.

Then the Thanksgiving turn, which is the quiet gut-punch. "This is my home." "I don't go back." The ease going out of him, "something level and exact behind," words "set down clean and apart" — and "there was no door left open for her to step through." This is the shut door finally made *verbal*, the same closed thing as the scar she keeps unasked, and she does the same thing she always does: takes her hand off it, lets the quiet be kind. That's her tenderness and it's also the trained not-asking. I felt for both of them here — whatever was done to him lives behind that door, and she'll never be let through it while she pours everything of herself out into his.

And Sheri. "She's alone." Vee's jealousy flicker — twice, small, "all her own," extinguished fast — is very human and I liked her for it landing and passing. But I'm sitting with the fact that Sheri is now the alone friend he feeds on the family day, spoken of with plain unguarded fondness, and I still don't know if she was the blonde who told Vee the truth. The chapter didn't answer it; it just made Sheri warmer and more embedded, which somehow tightened the question rather than loosening it.

Trust vs. suspicion: the photograph earned real unease this time — that's page-earned, not carried-in, because of the narrowed promise and everything I know about what the con converts. Everything else is the usual chord: the warmth is not fake, and the leverage is real, both.

**Title:** *In His Hands* is the most literal-and-loaded title yet. She is posed by his hands, held in his hands, and an image of her is now in his hands. On the read it's pure tenderness; on the reread it's custody. It didn't give anything away — it just quietly named the transfer that the chapter enacts.

**What I want / dread next:** I dread that photograph traveling. I want to see whether "I'd never share your photograph" holds. Guess from here: that image is a Chekhov's gun and it will go off, and Sheri's "alone" is not as simple as it sounds.

**How I feel — overall**
Split clean, and I trust the split — that's the book. On the page this was the tenderest chapter yet: her pleasure fully her own, "a girl who had a body," the meal as an aria, the photo handed back lovelier than she could catch alone. I was swept.
