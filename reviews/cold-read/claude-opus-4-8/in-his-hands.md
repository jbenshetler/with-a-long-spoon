## Reader reaction

This one is warm and terrible in the same breath, and the terrible part is a single object: the photograph.

The opening is her at her most undone — "shaking and stupid and full of him," legs "gone unreliable," a "deep worked soreness low down that she'd be aware of tomorrow and glad of." That's good, unforced, and it worked on me. The heat is real but it's the aftermath heat, the loose-limbed satiety, not the athletics. And then the thing I keep loving and keep dreading: he lies his whole body across hers, sets his head on her breast, and does the small kisses, and she does that thing she always does now — narrates his interior for him. *"He takes such comfort there. They're not about sex for him."* And then he closes his lips over her risen nipple and she's delighted to be wrong: *"Which rather spoiled her theory."* She's forever building him a self and then getting to be sweetly corrected by him. I notice she never gets to be right *against* him. He is always the one revealed to be a little more than she guessed.

The pillow-talk exchange is where my stomach started to tighten before the camera even came out. "Who does this to you when I'm not around?" — "I take care of it myself. Thinking of you." And what does he think about? *"That look on your face when you take the first bite of something I've made you."* There it is again — her face, her palate, the data he's been collecting for chapters now. He told her flat out, in bed, that the thing he replays is her tasting his food. And two pages later she shuts her eyes over the chicken piccata and "he was watching her with the exact look he'd described to her in bed, as though she'd just handed him something." She's *feeding* him the exact expression he confessed he collects. The loop closed on the page and she doesn't see it as a loop; she sees it as being cherished.

Now — the picture. She offers it. *"Would a picture help?"* That's her idea, freely, and that detail is doing a lot of work on me, because the tagline on the cover is right there: *every yes was freely given.* Her one condition: "Only if you promise you'll never share me with anyone." His answer is precise and I clocked the precision: *"Never. I'd never share your photograph."* She asked him never to share *her*. He promised never to share her *photograph*. My ear caught the narrowing even if hers didn't. And I'm sitting here holding a jacket that told me three people are in a bed and this girl doesn't know it — so "never share you" from the man running the game landed like ice water.

Then the staging. She arranges herself; he says no, *you'll have to wait*, and does it himself — turns her, sets her elbow, draws her hair back, lays her hand on her hip, moves her legs, "one knee carried a little ahead of the other," and kisses each place after he sets it. "laid out like a woman in an old calendar." He is composing the image. He goes to the window and works the blinds until "the glare slid off her skin." *"That's the one."* He is very, very good at making the picture he wants, and she experiences the whole thing as tenderness — being handled, being chosen. And the gut-punch is that she's not naive about the object itself: "the cold sane thought any girl had... that pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them. She knew all of that." She knows. She overrides it: "believing him was the brave part. She sent it before the nerve could leave her... and gave him herself to keep." She *sent* it. It crossed to Delivered. It's on his phone now, and she framed sending it as courage. That's the trap the cover keeps promising, and this is the closest I've felt it actually snap shut on something concrete and portable. A nude photo, on the phone of a man who is running a game with a second woman, given as an act of trust and pride. I am afraid of that photograph.

The kitchen half is a breather and it's lovely and I let it be lovely — the chicken piccata built step by step, the wine he cooks into the pan (the same grapefruit-lime sauvignon blanc she's drinking, which is a nice loop), him giving her the water glass first as he always does, feeding her tastes off the spoon. "She had used to just eat. Now she tasted, and there was no going back to the other, and she knew exactly whose doing that was." She knows *he* did it to her palate. She names it and loves it. I read that line twice — she's aware she's been changed and she consents to the change retroactively, which is its own kind of caught.

Two darker seams in the domestic warmth. First, Thanksgiving: "Are you going home?" — "This is my home." She pushes gently — "But your parents. Do you go back to them?" — and his face goes "level and exact," voice careful, "each word set down clean and apart from the last": *"I don't go back."* The sealed wound again, and this time she actually touched the door and felt it not open. "she had put her hand on something she wasn't meant to touch, and she took her hand off it." Good — she's learning where the walls are and agreeing not to test them. That's another thing being trained into her.

Second — and this pricked me — Sheri is coming for Thanksgiving. "She'll bring a pie and pretend she made it." Vee gets a "flicker... another woman at his table on a day meant for family," and it goes out when he says "She's alone." And Vee lands on plain, uncomplicated gladness. But I'm not Vee. I remember Sheri "knows about both women" and "read his falling," and that her heat can "turn it." So Vee is going to be at, or adjacent to, a Thanksgiving table with the one person in this book who knows the whole shape of the thing Vee doesn't. That flicker Vee dismissed — I did not dismiss it.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** My suspicion isn't new but it got a physical anchor this chapter: a naked photograph, sent, Delivered, promised-over with a promise I heard narrow in real time. I trust his tenderness is *felt* — the boy's grin over the phone "landed warm under her breastbone," and I believed it landed warm in him too. That's exactly why it frightens me. Vee: total sympathy, and a new specific ache watching her *name every mechanism* (the palate, the photo's danger, whose doing it all is) and consent anyway.

**Erotic charge** — a 2, and it peaks not in the sex (which is aftermath and offstage-ish, one round narrated coming down and two more elided) but in the staging: him arranging her body piece by piece and kissing each placement. That posing is the most charged passage — control rendered as devotion, which is this book's whole engine.

**Friction.** Very little. The one place I felt a faint thumb was "the cold sane thought any girl had about a picture like this... that pictures like it got loose in the world and ruined the women in them." It's the narration underlining the danger a hair more than it needed to — though I half-forgive it because Vee overriding a danger she can fully articulate *is* the point, and having her think it plainly is more honest than letting her be dumb about it. Still, it's the closest to the author leaning in.

**The chapter title — "In His Hands."** On the surface: the posing, literally in his hands, being set and kissed. On second thought it's colder — she is *in his hands* now in every sense, including the photograph that is now, literally, in his hands / on his device. And "in his hands" is where you put yourself when you trust. It names the trust and the possession as the same act. It doesn't give away plot but it recolors the sweetness the second I sit with it.

**The book's title / tagline.** *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil and you need a long spoon; the horror is there's no spoon here because it's tender, she keeps reaching in with her bare hand. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is nearly a demonstration piece for that line: the photo was *her* idea, *her* condition, *her* courage. Nobody made her. That's what makes it a trap and not a crime.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread that photograph resurfacing — as leverage, or shared with Randi as the "third's" first delivery, or simply as a thing that exists past her control. Guess from here: the Thanksgiving-with-Sheri setup is being loaded; either Sheri and Vee actually meet, or Sheri says something to Pace that starts turning. And I keep waiting, four-plus chapters now, for Randi to come back loud. I want the game to touch Vee's awareness. I dread it doing so through that phone.
