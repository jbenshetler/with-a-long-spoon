## Reader reaction

After the two chapters before this — the long molten *Forbidden Kiss* and the quietly seismic *Back* — I came into "Unpacking" braced for another turn of the screw, and instead got a warm bath. And I loved it for that. This is the exhale chapter, the one where Vee comes home to the one room in the book without a hidden agenda, and it did the thing a rest chapter is supposed to do: it made me feel how tired she's been, and how safe this particular corner is.

**Cassie.** God, I love her. She's my safe harbor and she's rarely been more precisely herself than here. The whole chapter is built on her *listening* — she turns "the whole chair," folds her feet up "where they never reached the floor anyway," and gives Vee "the entire front of her attention." That gesture is the opposite of Pace-listening (the back at the sink, kept-and-catalogued) and Randi-listening (affection-plus-reconnaissance across a little table). Cassie listens with nothing underneath it. And then the deadpan runners — "He salts the persimmons," "He burned the toast," "Pace. Shrank your sweater. / To a doll size." — that flat echoing is her whole comic register and it landed every time. The payoff, her putting her head back and laughing "the rare full one she never spent on her own jokes," genuinely warmed me.

**The cover story.** Here's where I sat up. Vee lies to Cassie, smoothly, and I felt the small cold of it even inside the coziness. The shrunken cranberry sweater, the dye in the good pants — a whole engineered alibi for why she had no clothes, delivered in a rush "her mouth set off without her." And the narration names it plainly: "the record stood exactly where Vee had left it, corrected by nobody." That's Vee editing again, the way she's always given Cassie "the cleaned-up version" — but this time it's not just softening the sex, it's manufacturing a fiction to hide the coat-over-nothing. I don't distrust *Vee* for it; I ache a little, because the one uncomplicated relationship she has is the one she's now curating too. She's protecting the thing with Pace by shading the truth to the person who'd tell her the truth back.

**The card.** The turn I didn't see coming and the one that got me: "My mother says thank you for the card." Vee's hand stops "partway to the towel and went, without her, to the collar of her robe" — the covering gesture, the flinch of being *seen* and cared for at once. "Something had gone through her middle... warm and specific, like a hand set flat between her shoulder blades." That's belonging arriving from the plainest possible source — a friend's mother who saves boxes and wraps cookies badly — and it undoes Vee more quietly than any of Pace's ceremonies. Cassie's family loves her without a plan. After a whole book of being *chosen*, *fitted*, *provided for* by two people running a design she can't see, this offhand "when does she get to see you again" is the only love in the chapter that asks nothing and hides nothing. It's the tenderest beat in the chapter and it's not romantic at all, which is exactly why it landed so hard.

**Swoon / pull.** Not erotic here, and it shouldn't be. The pull is the friendship and the homecoming — "the weekend still on her like sun she'd been out in," the room "going on around it, ordinary, in no hurry, asking nothing." I melted at Cassie's full laugh and at the card. Vee falling asleep "before she decided to be," still in her robe, "too content to solve it" — that's a person genuinely at rest, and after the December wreckage I was glad to let her have it.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new suspicion from *this* page — but the two prior chapters left the alarm humming under my sternum, and this chapter's coziness didn't quiet it, it framed it. *Forbidden Kiss* planted that awful small seed: the faint salt-and-sharp on Pace's mouth "that had not been there this morning," and Vee's tongue going hunting, and the buried question she wouldn't chase — "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been —." I still don't know what that was (a guess from here: another woman on him — and the reader knows who), and it "settled somewhere under the affection... minor-key, waiting." Then *Back*: Pace wanting to meet "your people," and the nachos ghost — *I listen as hard as I know how and I still can't hear you* — and "Let me handle it." So when *this* chapter shows Vee cheerfully lying to Cassie about the sweater, and shows Cassie as the one person who "could hear the size of things," I feel the architecture even in the calm: the truth-teller is being managed out of the loop, gently, by Vee herself. That's the unease the earlier pages earned, sitting quietly in a scene that on its surface is just two friends laughing.

**Erotic charge.** Deliberately near zero, and rightly. The heat is entirely offstage, recollected as food ("the best plate of food I have had in my life and it was mushrooms on toast"), sanitized for Cassie. This is the cool cloth after the fever.

**Friction as a reader.** Almost none. It's a short, clean, well-judged chapter that knows exactly what it is. If I reach: the sweater alibi is *convenient* enough that I half-wondered whether the book was handing Vee too tidy a cover — but the narration flagging it ("corrected by nobody") tells me the tidiness is the point, not an oversight, so I'll trust that. Nothing felt like a thumb on the scale.

**The titles.** "Unpacking" is lovely and does exactly the double-work I've come to expect from these chapter titles: on the surface, Cassie's half-emptied duffel, the socks and tees; underneath, the *un*packing that doesn't happen — Vee has nothing to unpack (no bag, "there wasn't anything important in it anyway"), and the real unpacking, of the truth, she declines. She repacks the story instead. It also whispers at the emotional unpacking with Cassie that stays deliberately half-done — the card, "which was not the size of it at all," left folded. Quietly apt; it didn't give anything away, it just glowed a little brighter on second look.

*A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / the tagline — I wasn't given a tagline or jacket copy in this packet, so I'll only say the series title keeps doing its slow work on me: *he who sups with the devil must use a long spoon.* Every warm chapter now reads through it. This one is the "sups" — the shared table, the shared laugh — and the long spoon is the distance Vee is quietly keeping from Cassie without knowing she's keeping it.

**What I want / dread next.** The end of *Back* set the collision in motion — Pace wanting to meet "all of them," Randi in the middle of them all, "Let me handle it." And Vee, at the end of *Forbidden Kiss*, planning to tell Randi "nearly everything" on Tuesday. So I dread and crave the moment Pace and Randi are in a room with Vee watching, or the moment Vee brings the "strange small thing" (the taste) to Randi of all people. (Guess from here: Randi will know *exactly* what to make of it, which is the horror.) I want Cassie kept safe and I fear she won't be — she's the one who'll see the seam. Mostly I'm braced, tenderly, for the calm to end.

Food as the safe language for what can't be said aloud.

**Symbolism:** The unpacked-but-not bag — Vee has nothing to unpack and won't unpack the truth either; the alibi is a repacking. Cassie's mother going to get "a better box" for every wrapped gift reads, lightly, as the book's whole engine — the impulse to *improve the container* around a thing, presentation over contents — landing next to Vee presenting a false container (the sweater story) around her weekend. The welcome cold draft vs. Pace's engineered warmth: honesty has a chill in it Vee has learned to miss.

**Characterization:** Cassie deepened without a wasted word — her listening rendered as physical fact, her comedy, and her tact ("done with the subject the exact moment before it would have needed anything"). Vee consistent and quietly advancing: the same editor-of-her-own-life, now editing toward the person who least deserves the edit, and visibly moved by plain belonging in a way the designed belonging doesn't reach. Nobody thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Well judged; short and unhurried, earns its length as the deliberate comedown after two heavy chapters. The card lands late and lifts the whole thing from cozy to quietly aching right before the lights go out.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate de-escalation after the *Forbidden Kiss* peak and the *Back* pivot, and it works as a breath — but it's a breath, not a stall: it quietly advances the "Vee curates the truth-teller" thread and keeps the collision (from *Back*) loaded offstage. Momentum holding, not sagging; I trust the book is winding up to Tuesday and to the room with everyone in it.
