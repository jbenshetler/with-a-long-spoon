## Reader reaction

So this is the reconciliation, and I braced for it to feel like a con — Randi dressed her, staged her, scheduled her — and the strange thing is that on the page, in the moment, it mostly doesn't. It plays as one of the most genuinely tender things in the book, and that's exactly what makes my stomach uneasy, because I can *feel* Randi's fingerprints on the scaffolding even while the scene itself refuses to let me hate it.

The heels doing that thing at the door — Vee coming level with Pace and then *past* him, "the small wrongness of it went through her like a missed stair" — that's a beautiful physical beat. And it lands as pure mortification, one more disrespect laid at his feet before a word. Then Pace: *"Leave them on. Beauty like yours made for the gods and loaned out — I'm not going to insult the loan by complaining I have to look up."* That's him doing the exact thing the book keeps saying he does — "the true extravagant thing in the plain voice you'd use for the weather" — and I felt it work on me the way it worked on her. I melted a little. And then I distrusted the melt, because I've been trained by twenty-six chapters to. But the text earned the warmth honestly here; I can't point to a tell.

The confession is the strongest part. When the clean machine breaks and she has to say the *why* — the blonde at the club, "all us girls love him," "he's so good about getting a girl home safe at the end of the night," and Vee catching herself doing the blonde's *lilt* in her own mouth and hating it — that's genuinely good. And her self-indictment cuts deep: *"She handed me a lie and I picked it up because some part of me was just standing there waiting for one."* That's the truest thing Vee has said about herself in the whole book. She's not blaming the blonde; she's naming the waiting-to-be-lied-to in herself. Which, given that she's currently being lied to by Randi and doesn't know it, made the hair stand up on my neck.

And then Pace's response — *"Thank you for telling me what she said"* — is the one moment where my suspicion actually spiked *inside* the tenderness. It's disarming, it makes her laugh wetly, it's "such a strange thing to be thanked for." But wait. Why is *that* the thing he seizes? He's not thanking her for the apology. He's thanking her for the *intel* — for reporting exactly what the blonde said and how. From inside the scene it reads as him refusing to let her flog herself. But some cold part of me registered that a man managing a situation would very much want to know precisely what got said to his girl at that club, in what words, by whom. I don't know that that's what it is. But the line has a second face and I saw it flicker.

The morning — the folded undershirt, "he had set it there for her to find" — is lovely and I don't have a knife for it. The kitchen scene is where the title detonates, and it's the best comic-to-erotic turn in the book so far. "Soaking is a recognized method... you're scrubbing things that want to be left alone" — genuinely funny, genuinely her, and the pivot from tickling-shriek to her swatting him across the seat of his jeans to *him* turning off the tap and the kitchen going quiet — that's expertly built. The single swat, "warm and wet from the dishwater," and her body's involuntary answer: nipples drawing up to aching points "so fast and so hard it was nearly pain," the soundless *oh*, "her body saying two or three things at once that she had not agreed to have it say."

That's the shame engine, and it's the freshest firing of it yet, because *she* initiated the swat. She smacked *him*, playing, in charge, and the retaliation-swat turned her whole authorship inside out in one landing. "No joke for this, no doctrine, no clean machine." And her recovery — kissing him hard "to cover the thing he'd seen with a thing she was choosing," fumbling his button — is heartbreakingly consistent with everything I know about her: cover the involuntary with the chosen. The erotic charge here is high and it's *clean*, it peaks exactly right and cuts away at the button, leaving the pan soaking. That final image — the thing she told him to leave alone, left alone, doing its work in the water — is quietly perfect.

The Monday coda with the hair and "You're always beautiful. Not *you're beautiful*, which she'd have argued. *Always*" — got me. Fully got me. And that's the danger the whole book keeps flagging about itself: I'm falling for him as hard as she is.

Where's my friction? Almost nowhere on craft — this is a strong, warm, sexy chapter. My only real friction is the *situational dread* I carry in: I know (from ch.26) that Randi built the sureness, chose the dress, fitted the heels blind. So the heels that undo Vee at the threshold are *Randi's* heels, and Randi's the one who put the "go in sure, not sorry" doctrine in her mouth — and the doctrine is literally what breaks so beautifully at the door. The scene is warm on its own terms and cold only in the pattern, exactly as the book keeps doing to me. I noticed it. It didn't ruin the warmth. It just sat underneath it like the cool side of the bed.

What I want next: I want to see whether Pace clocked *anything* about the costume — did he recognize Randi's hand in how Vee arrived, the heels, the staging? He's the man who "catches every signal." If he knows those are Randi's heels... And I dread, mildly, that the swat in the kitchen — her body's brand-new discovery that it *likes* being struck — is the next riser, and that the bench is waiting somewhere up that staircase. *Guess from here:* the involuntary response to the swat is a door that just opened, and someone (Pace, or Randi through Vee's retelling) is going to walk through it.
