This chapter is a masterclass in the quietest kind of dread. After the bench — after that shattering, gorgeous scene where Randi came out from behind the face and *begged* and then decided, in the mirror, never to come out again — I braced for aftermath. What I got wasn't confession or collapse. It was four minutes of sorority small talk, and it terrified me more than any of it.

The whole common-room scene works on me because I can read the meter Randi can't stop reading. "The pause was a fraction of a second long." "The fraction-of-a-second was longer." "The fraction-of-a-second was wider here." That recurring beat is exquisite — the tiny latency between question and arranged smile, and the fact that *she's clocking her own lag* while Brooke clocks it too. I felt the pressure in my own chest. This is the machinery of the face I watched her retreat behind last night, running live, in daylight, under interrogation dressed as warmth.

Brooke is the chapter's real event. I don't trust her, and the text earned that precisely — not through villainy but through technique. "The not-standing was the move." "Brooke had decided it was over." She's dismantling Pace by increments: *Chase? Pace. engineering? — math. he doesn't teach, does he.* Each guess "landing him a notch down without seeming to reach for it," the smile "absorbing it without conceding the notch." And Randi *knows* the move from the inside — she's on Standards, she runs the same authority — which makes her helplessness under it worse. The relief exhale, the *I was about to be scandalized*, the "Sorry to grill you. Go" — that's a woman establishing she can grill, and granting release as a favor. My suspicion here is fully earned by the page, not manufactured. Something is being decided about Pace in that room, and Randi is losing a negotiation she can't admit is happening.

The two small self-harm-adjacent notes chilled me — well, one carried over: last chapter the water-bottle cap under the thumbnail. Here it's subtler, displaced into the whole body's stillness. And the chapter's cruelest image is domestic: the closet. "She had come to the closet to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what. Her hand was partway lifted toward the row. She brought it down." The buffed, disciplined life — "bone to camel to oatmeal with the one disciplined oxblood at the end," the same palette-thinking as the thin apple slices — and she can't even remember why she opened it. The ending devastated me quietly. Lying in last night's clothes, shoes on, under "a duvet. It was not — it was a duvet." "It felt like there being no door." After a whole book of locked doors — the deadbolt she turns first thing, the bathroom lock, "the size of a key turning in a lock" — this is the scariest door image yet: not shut, *absent*. The crying is locatable and she simply doesn't go, and the not-going has stopped registering as an act. "The breath was held." I ache for her and I'm frightened for her in equal measure.

There's essentially no heat here, and that's right — this is the morning-after in the social world, the machinery Pace's bench briefly defeated, reasserting total control. The erotic charge is entirely retrospective: *he's hard to say no to*, said as a landed line downstairs and re-heard "in a different key" upstairs, "a key she could not sing in." That single re-keying carries all the residue of last night's want and turns it to grief.

Friction: almost none. If anything I wondered fleetingly whether the fraction-of-a-second device gets used one beat too often, but each instance escalated, so I forgave it. Pace isn't on the page at all and I missed his warmth — which I think is the point; the chapter is what she's protecting the face *from*, and he's the thing that got in.

The title, **"Standards,"** lands hard and doubles cleanly: Randi's actual seat on the Standards board (the body that judges sisters' conduct), and the standards being applied to *her* — to Pace, to whether he's up to code, engineering-vs-math, the white pickup, "short-cute."
