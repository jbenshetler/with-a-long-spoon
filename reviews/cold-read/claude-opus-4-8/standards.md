# Cold read — Standards

*scene: scenes/standards.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: the-bench*

## Reader reaction

This is a quiet, tense little chapter and it worked on me — but as suspense, not heat. There's no sex here, barely a touch. What it is instead is a four-minute interrogation staged as a friendly Saturday morning, and it's genuinely nervy to read. Randi comes home in last night's clothes and has to walk a gauntlet of three women — Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey — who are all smiling and none of whom are quite as harmless as the smiling suggests.

The thing that got me was **Brooke**. She's new to me in person (mentioned only, before — the president Randi lied to about a paper), and she is a *predator in cashmere.* "The not-standing was the move; Brooke was the chapter president and did not need to stand to stop someone in her own common room." That's a beautifully controlled bit of writing and it made my stomach tighten. Brooke runs the whole exchange like a soft cross-examination: opens with the paper (catching Randi in the lie she planted last chapter), moves to *where did you work,* then pivots seamlessly — "the next thing arrived without a seam" — to Pace's name, his degree, whether he *teaches her section.* That last one is the real question under all of it: is a sister sleeping with a grad student who could be grading her? Brooke lands it lightly, gets her "no," exhales *thank god,* and releases Randi before Randi can deploy the defensive lines stacking up in her mouth. "The conversation was over because Brooke had decided it was over." I believe every ounce of that power, and it's delicious to watch precisely because it's all done with warmth. Brooke never once drops the smile.

And here's the pleasure of it for me as a reader: **last chapter Randi was the one running the machine of composure; this chapter she's the one being handled.** She *knows* she's being handled — "she knew exactly how much authority it took to stay seated while another person stopped walking. Brooke had more of it than she did" — and she still can't beat it. That's a real deepening. The poised, room-owning woman of the jacket is, here, a fraction of a second behind on every smile. The chapter counts those fractions like a metronome: "The arrangement was a fraction late." "The fraction-of-a-second was longer." "The fraction-of-a-second was wider here." I loved that device. It's her tell, rendered from inside, and it never once names what she's feeling — just the *lag.*

**Sloane and Kinsey** are sketched fast and true — Sloane with the laugh "that meant nothing on its own and was therefore available for anything," Kinsey pitching her cereal-bowl question a hair too high, wanting the room to warm to her. The little status ecology of a sorority common room is exactly observed. Sloane's Raptor bit ("sir. You are five-foot-nine") is funny and also does the class/status work sideways — Pace drives an anonymous gray pickup, and the girls read it as *refreshing* rather than poor, which is its own tell about what they'd read as poor.

My sympathy for Randi, which was already where my heart was, deepened hard in the second half. The room alone. She can't sit — vanity, bed, closet, sink, all approached and abandoned. She reaches into the closet "to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what." She lies down in last night's clothes with her *shoes still on,* pulls a duvet over a buttoned cardigan. And then the line that reframes the whole book's title for me: *"He's hard to say no to,"* said to the girls as a joke — *what could I do* — and then heard again "in a different key. It was not a key she could sing in." That's the tagline made flesh. Every yes freely given; and here's the yes turning strange on her when she's alone and the audience is gone.

The not-crying passage is the best writing in the chapter and maybe the book so far. "It did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." The held breath that stops feeling held after twenty-one years. It connects straight back to the bathroom mirror last chapter — the face that had been lying for years — and gives me the *why* under it without explaining it: this is a woman so practiced at not-feeling that the not-feeling has gone silent, automatic, load-bearing. I find her genuinely moving. She is not, on this page, an orchestrator of anyone. She's someone drowning very quietly and neatly with the shoes on.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** nothing here felt like a thumb on the scale — this chapter is almost entirely earned. If anything my suspicion has *shifted objects.* I came in braced by the jacket to distrust Randi as the game-runner; this chapter made me distrust **Brooke** instead, and pity Randi. Which may itself be a setup — the jacket promises Randi will pick and manage Vivienne, and watching Randi get expertly managed *here* teaches me exactly the technique the book says she'll turn on someone else. So the chapter arms the irony without announcing it. I noticed that only on reflection; in the moment I just felt for her.

**Erotic charge:** near zero, and correctly so — this is the cold morning after, the world of surveillance and lies the heat has to survive inside. Heat's not the job here.

**Friction:** very little. The one place I felt the prose lean slightly hard was the repeated "fraction-of-a-second" tally — by the third or fourth I clocked the device as a *device.* But it's a small price and it mostly works as rhythm. Brooke's tag "a woman who would one day be some CEO's third wife" is a great cruel line, though it's Randi's judgment leaking into narration; I liked it enough not to mind.

**What I want next:** Vivienne. The jacket keeps promising her and two chapters in she hasn't arrived — I'm ready. And I want to know what "Standards" (the board Randi sits on, that gave her the single room) is going to *mean.* A woman who sits in judgment of other sisters' conduct, coming home in last night's clothes having lied about it — the irony is loud and I want to see it pressed. *Guess from here:* Vivienne gets spotted "across the quad" soon, and the technique Brooke used on Randi — the warm, seamless, extractive interrogation — is the technique Randi will use on Vivienne, gentler and disguised as friendship.

**On the titles.** "Standards" — the board Randi sits on (it earns her the single room), and, detonated by the chapter, the judgment-seat turned on its own officer: a woman who polices sisters' conduct comes home in last night's clothes and gets audited for it, and loses. Plain word, ironic charge, meaningful only after reading. Good. **With a Long Spoon** — supping with the devil with a spoon you hope is long enough. This chapter complicates who's who: I walked in from the jacket thinking Randi is the devil, and here she's the one supped upon, by Brooke, in her own house. The tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" gets its cleanest gloss yet in "*He's hard to say no to*" curdling in private — the freely-given yes going strange the moment no one's watching her give it.
