## Reader reaction

FELT READ

After "The Bench" I came into this chapter already braced, and it did exactly the cruel, quiet thing I hoped and feared it would do. No sex here, and it didn't need any — this is the morning after, and the whole chapter is Randi trying to walk the woman who begged on that bench back into the body of the woman who runs Standards, and the seam not quite closing. I felt the tension in my chest the entire foyer scene. It runs cold and controlled and it is one of the most genuinely suspenseful things I've read in a while, and there is not a single raised voice in it.

The foyer interrogation is masterful and horrible. Brooke never stands up — "the not-standing was the move" — and I believed every inch of that power read because Randi reads it *from the inside*: "she sat on the board herself, Standards." That's the knife. Randi knows this game because she plays it. And here she's on the wrong end of it, in last night's clothes, arranging a smile "a fraction late," matching Kinsey's pitch "very fast... She had not had time to choose the pitch." I loved how the surveillance is disguised as warmth — Sloane's laugh "that meant nothing on its own and was therefore available for anything," Brooke's *thank god* exhale about the section, the little demotion of Pace from engineering down to math done "without seeming to reach for it." These women are doing to Randi precisely the thing Pace did on the bench — watching past the face — except they're doing it to *police* her, and she can feel it. The chapter earned every ounce of my dread; I was reading the girls the way Randi was, tracking who knew what.

And then the private half, which quietly gutted me. The walking — vanity, bed, closet, sink — starting an action and losing it, "She had come to the closet to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what." The phone face-down, notifications unread. Lying down in last night's clothes with her shoes on under a duvet that is "not — it was a duvet," measured against a cashmere blanket in another house that she'll never let herself name. That withheld comparison did more than a paragraph of interiority could. This is a woman who cannot get warm and won't say why.

My feeling about Randi has deepened hard and turned tender in a way I didn't expect after the last chapter, where I mostly ached *for* her while she decided to go back behind the face. Here I'm inside the cost. The line that undid me: "*He's hard to say no to,*" the throwaway she fed the girls, heard back "in a different key... It was not a key she could sing in." And the terrifying revision of the closed-door image from last night — "the not-going did not feel like holding a door shut. It felt like there being no door." Last chapter she turned a lock; here the lock has become architecture. That's bleaker and I felt it as bleaker. The held breath "stops feeling held" — she's so practiced at the not-crying that it's no longer even an act of will. That's the darkest note the book has hit, and I take it as the book's meaning, not a betrayal of it.

Trust vs. suspicion: no one here feels false to *me* as a reader — everyone is exactly what they are, which is the point. But the chapter earned a real chill about the sorority as an ecosystem: the warmth is a monitoring system, and Brooke deciding when the conversation is over ("The conversation was over because Brooke had decided it was over") is the same authority Randi wields. I trust the book completely to know what it's doing to her. And I notice — holding it open as the page does — that Pace is entirely absent and entirely present; I still don't have his measure fully, and after the jacket's hints of a "plan" I keep a small ear cocked, but this chapter gave me no fresh reason to distrust him. It's about *her*.

Erotic charge: near zero on the surface and that's correct — but there's a low residual heat in the withholding, the body that was "more alive on that bench than she could remember being anywhere" now lying rigid and cold in its shoes. The charge here is grief-adjacent, not arousal. I scored heat low honestly; the chapter isn't trying for it.

Friction: almost none. If anything the walking-in-circles sequence risks over-literalizing the dissociation, but "It was not — it was a duvet" is so good that the repetition-and-fragment technique earned its keep for me. I didn't feel a thumb on the scale; the girls' dialogue is doing character work and menace at once, not telegraphing.

The title. "Standards" — the board Randi sits on, the committee that judges other girls' conduct, dropped in twice and then made literal ("Standards rated a single"). On the page it's the sorority office; on second thought it's everything. She is a woman held to a standard, enforcing a standard, and the standard is *the face* — perfect, safe, portioned. The single room she got from the seat, "a door she did not have to share, which she had wanted for reasons she had told herself were about the work and were not entirely about the work" — that door left ajar, and the chapter leaves it ajar too; I won't close it. The title illuminates rather than gives away: it names the machine she's caught in without telling me where she's headed.

Book/series signals: *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." land harder now. Randi's whole crisis is that she *chose* the bench, gave it freely, and the freeness is what she can't survive — because it reached *her*, not the face. "Sup with the devil, use a long spoon" — keep your distance from what could consume you; and Randi has just discovered she got too close to something in *herself*. The jacket centers Vee, and I notice Vee is nowhere in these two chapters — "Randi" is Vee's glamorous friend per the jacket, and here I'm deep in Randi's private ruin. I'm holding that gap open; I don't know yet how these connect.

What I want next: I ache to know what she does with "figure out what she's going to do about this," and I dread that the answer is *nothing — go back behind the face forever*. Guess from here, marked as a guess: I suspect we're about to meet Vee through Randi's eyes, and I'm frightened of what a woman this expert at portioning and lying will do to a hungry girl. I want to keep reading badly.
