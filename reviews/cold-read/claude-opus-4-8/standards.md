## Reader reaction

This chapter cools the room right down, and I mean that as praise. After the heat and dread of the night, we get four minutes of a sorority common room at 9-something on a Saturday, and it's one of the tensest things I've read in this book so far. Nothing happens. Everything happens.

**Randi** — my sympathy deepened hard here, and it shifted in kind. Last chapter I saw the strategist under the shock; here I see the *cost* of the strategy in real time. The opening move — closing the door quietly, "which was already wrong. Randi did not close doors quietly" — is a beautiful, economical way to show her diminished. The interrogation with Brooke is agony precisely because Randi is so good at it and it's still not enough. I loved the recurring "fraction-of-a-second" beat — "The fraction-of-a-second was longer," "wider here" — the delays stacking as the questions get closer to the bone (his section, the course). She's running defense at full processing speed and losing anyway.

And then the room upstairs. This is where the chapter earned its ache. She can't sit. She goes to the vanity, the bed, the closet, the sink — "She had come to the closet to do something and could not, for a moment, locate what." That disorientation, in a woman whose whole competence is composure, gutted me a little. The duvet passage — "It was not — it was a duvet" — with the unspoken comparison to the cashmere blanket at Pace's house sitting right underneath it, unnamed. She lies down in last night's clothes with her shoes on. That's a person who cannot perform being-home for herself.

**Brooke** — she moved from an offstage name to a genuinely unsettling presence. She's warm the way Pace is warm — "She had a voice the way other people had a perfume" — and I clocked the parallel immediately: another person who runs a gentle surface over a hard mechanism. The not-standing detail is razor-sharp: "the not-standing was the move." She's frisking Randi under a smile, and Randi *knows* the technique because she does it too (she's on Standards, the irony of the title landing). Brooke pushing on the section question — "you're not in one of his sections" — and then the "I was about to be scandalized" release is pure soft power. I don't trust Brooke, but I also don't think she's a villain; she's just doing the ambient female-surveillance thing at expert level, and it's terrifying to be on the wrong side of it.

**Sloane and Kinsey** — nicely observed. Sloane's laugh "that meant nothing on its own and was therefore available for anything." Kinsey landing her laugh "slightly above pitch," waiting for the room to warm to her. And Randi's own laugh coming "at the same pitch as Kinsey's. The matching was very fast. She had not had time to choose the pitch." That involuntary mirroring — she who *chooses* everything — is a lovely small tell.

**Pace** — absent but named, and it's the first time I've heard him discussed by outsiders. "Short-cute," the gray pickup behind Hahn, math PhD not engineering, "he doesn't teach, does he." He's being appraised and diminished by these girls and Randi has to defend the version of him she's presented while carrying the version she actually knows. The gap between "he's cute, yeah" and what happened on that bench is the whole engine of the scene.

**Erotic charge** — none, and that's correct. This is the hangover chapter, the shame-processing chapter. The heat here is social dread, not sex. It works completely on that register.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — my suspicion of Pace didn't move (he's not present), but I now have a *new* wariness of the sorority as an apparatus — Brooke specifically. The line "a woman who would one day be some CEO's third wife" is the narrator's one sharp claw, and I noticed it as slightly editorializing, the only place I felt a thumb. It's a good line, but it tells me how to feel about Brooke rather than letting her do it, and she was doing it fine on her own.

**Friction** — very little. The prose repeats "fraction-of-a-second" as a deliberate motif and I mostly liked it, though by the third instance I felt the device announce itself a hair. The "It was not — it was a duvet" hesitation is the kind of thing that could read as precious but it landed for me because the withheld word (cashmere) is doing the work.

**What I want / dread next** — I want to know what Randi *does*. The carry-forward question ("figure out what to do about this") is still live and this chapter deliberately doesn't answer it — she lies down and closes her eyes instead of deciding anything, which is its own answer: she's not okay and can't yet act. *Guess from here:* I suspect Brooke's careful filing of "Pace" (not Chase), the section question, the pickup behind Hahn — that Randi's two worlds are going to collide, and the sorority's surveillance is a threat she'll have to manage. I also dread the food thread and the not-crying escalating. And I'm now curious whether Pace has any idea what the cost is on this end — the whole night he was planning "scene by scene," and this is the invoice, and he wasn't there to see it.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Randi comes home to the sorority the morning after the bench scene, in last night's clothes, and gets softly interrogated in the common room by Brooke (chapter president) with Sloane and Kinsey present. She holds her composure through questions about her cover-story "paper," about Pace (his name, his gray pickup, math PhD, whether she's in his section), lying smoothly but a beat slow each time. Brooke ends the exchange on her own authority. Upstairs in her single room, Randi can't settle — drifts between vanity, bed, closet, sink, unable to locate what she came to do — then lies down on top of the made bed in her clothes and shoes under a thin synthetic duvet (silently measured against the cashmere blanket at Pace's) and does not cry. She's so practiced at not-crying it no longer registers as an act. She closes her eyes.

**Who's who.**
- **Pace / Peter** — the man from last chapter; absent here but discussed. Math PhD, not-teaching (confirmed not her instructor), gray pickup parked behind Hahn, "short-cute" to the girls. Tender-and-instrumental dom who cracked Randi open.

**How I feel.** Aching for Randi; the dread has turned domestic and social. My unease about Pace holds (offstage); new wariness of Brooke and the sorority as a watching machine. Mood: cold, quiet, hungover, a held breath.
