## Reader reaction

This chapter unnerved me more than the first one did, and it did it with almost nothing happening — a four-minute conversation and then a woman lying on a bed with her shoes on. That's a hell of a trick.

The whole first movement is a soft interrogation dressed as girl-chat, and I felt it in my stomach as an interrogation the whole time, before Randi confirmed it for me. Brooke sitting there not standing — "the not-standing was the move" — and me being told, through Randi's own board-seat expertise, exactly how much authority it takes to stop someone while staying seated. I loved that the read comes from *inside* Randi's fluency in this game. She's not a victim of Brooke; she's a peer who's losing on points. That's new. In the first chapter I felt protective of her; here I watched her be genuinely, professionally good at survival — matching Kinsey's laugh pitch before she could choose it, arranging the smile "a fraction late" — and the protectiveness curdled into something colder and sadder. She's so practiced that the practice runs without her.

Brooke is the standout new presence and she scares me in a completely ordinary register. "She had a voice the way other people had a perfume." Every warm beat is a needle. The pass about Pace's name — "I kept thinking you'd said *Chase*," "Pace, like she was filing it" — is the moment I sat up. She's *collecting* him. And the section where she probes whether he teaches Randi's section — "I was about to be scandalized," then "I don't even know why I went there. Sorry" — that's someone who reached for exactly the thing she wanted to check and then covered the reach. I don't trust her an inch, and the text earned that; it's all behavior. The little detail that she does this admin "every Saturday in this seat" tells me the ambush wasn't an ambush, it's a standing position, and Randi walked into a room that was already arranged.

The cruelest small stroke: Randi had "three lines that had assembled in her mouth in the last forty seconds" and never got to use them, because "the conversation was over because Brooke had decided it was over." The release was "the cleanest hit." That's precise and awful — being dismissed as the deepest wound. It rhymes with Pace deciding things for her body in the first chapter. Two people this year have run her by controlling when she's allowed to stop.

Then the second movement, alone in the room, is where the chapter really got me. The prose deliberately stalls — she walks to the vanity, doesn't sit, walks to the bed, doesn't sit, opens the closet and "could not, for a moment, locate what" she came for. Runs the sink and doesn't put her hands under the water. It's a body with all its routines intact and nothing behind them. Lying down in last night's clothes with her shoes on, under a duvet that "was not — it was a duvet," felt genuinely bleak in a way I didn't expect after the heat of chapter one.

And the closing pages pay off a thread from the bench directly. "He's hard to say no to," the line that "landed perfectly" for the girls, heard again "in a different key. It was not a key she could sing in." That's the crack. But then the not-crying — "it felt like there being no door" — is the darkest thing in the book so far and it recontextualizes the locked bathroom from chapter one. Last night she went to the crying and hid it behind a locked door. Here there's no door because there's no going to it at all. "She had gotten so good at the not-going that it no longer registered as an act, the way a held breath stops feeling held." That landed on me as the actual wound the cap-under-the-thumbnail was pointing at. Twenty-one years of it. This is the interior I said I wanted most, and getting it made me ache rather than satisfied me — which is right.

**Erotic charge:** basically zero, and correctly so. This is the morning-after chapter, the cost chapter. There's no heat here and I didn't want any; the total absence of it is doing the work. If anything the chill of the room is the anti-erotic answer to Pace's too-warm house.

**Trust/suspicion:** Brooke is now the thing that feels most "off," and earned it — collecting Pace's name, probing the section question, controlling the exit. Whether she's just a status-predator sniffing an off-note or something more pointed, I can't tell yet. And I notice the book has now shown me *two* people expertly managing Randi; it makes me wonder if her chapter-one resolve to "figure out what to do about this" is less a planner's cunning than the only muscle she has — management, because feeling isn't available to her.

**Friction:** almost none. The one place I felt the thumb *lightly* was the duvet riff — "It was not — it was a duvet. It was light and synthetic and adequate. It was not — it was a duvet." I see what it's doing (the thing she can't name, the missing comfort) and it mostly worked, but the stammer-repeat came the closest to telling me how to feel. I'd have trusted it flat.

**What I want next:** I want to know what Brooke is actually doing — whether "filing" Pace's name pays off. And I want to know what's under the not-crying, the twenty-one years. A guess from here, clearly marked: I suspect the "different key" is the first hairline of Randi genuinely feeling something she can't administrate, and that her chapter-one "plan" is going to be her trying to convert that feeling into a manageable operation — and I dread that the operation involves Pace and someone else, because a book keeps introducing sorority women for a reason.
