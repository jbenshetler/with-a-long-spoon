## Reader reaction

No Pace, no Vee — this is Randi alone, coming home in last night's clothes, and it's a quietly excellent chapter that works entirely on dread and social choreography. The whole first half is an interrogation that never once stops smiling. Brooke *not standing* — "the not-standing was the move" — and Randi reading it from the inside because she sits on the same board, that landed hard. This is a house that runs on exactly the apparatus Pace runs on: engineered pressure wearing a warm face. Brooke has "a voice the way other people had a perfume," and she uses it to file away *Pace, math, PhD, white pickup, not his section* one seamless click at a time. Something is *off* here and the text earned it — Brooke is collecting, not chatting. I don't know what for. The jacket whispered a game aimed at Vee; I'll hold whether Brooke connects to that as an open question, because the page gives me only surveillance, not purpose.

What swept me — or rather gutted me — is the second half. The empty choreography of a person who cannot land anywhere: walks to the vanity, doesn't sit; walks to the bed, doesn't sit; runs the sink, doesn't put her hands under it. The closet "worked to its limit, everything earning its hanger, the palette running bone to camel to oatmeal with the one disciplined oxblood at the end" — that's the face rendered as a wardrobe. And then the line that broke me: "It felt like there being no door." Last chapter she locked the self away like a deadbolt; here the horror deepens — she's so practiced at the not-going that there's no longer even a door to hold shut. "The breath was held" and she's forgotten it's held. That's the sealing from *Famished*'s resolution, now shown as lifelong, automatic, load-bearing.

Trust/suspicion: I'm wary of Brooke in a fresh, specific way — this is the first person the book has shown *hunting* information, and Randi handing over Pace's name feels like a small door left open that she'll regret. No heat, no romance, and correctly so.

Titles: **"Standards"** is doing lovely double work — the disciplinary board Randi sits on (and the seat that got her the single room, the private door), and the *standards* she holds herself to that are strangling her. The board that judges others is staffed by the most performed girl in the house. **A Polite Invitation / With a Long Spoon** — "sup with the devil, use a long spoon." This chapter is all long-spoon caution: everyone keeping polite distance while reaching for something. I feel the spoon here in Brooke.

Want next: Vee, finally — and to learn what Brooke does with a name.
