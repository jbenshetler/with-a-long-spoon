# Cold read — All the Time

*scene: scenes/all-the-time.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: in-his-hands*

## Reader reaction

This one hurt in a slow, particular way, and it's the near-misses that did it.

For three chapters Randi's been offstage — present only as scent and wine in Vee's mouth — and here she finally sits across the booth again, and the whole chapter is Vee handing herself over. There's no "scene" to report this time, no dress or swat, and that's exactly what makes it worse: Vee says "it's not a thing that happened… it's just how it *is* now," and then pours out the whole shape of her life — the sex, the sleeping, the being-fed, the being-*looked-at* — into the one person I know is the drain everything flows toward. I've read enough of this book now that the brunch-as-harvest pattern is baked in, so every confession landed on me as a deposit into Pace's ledger. When Vee thinks "the one friend she trusted with all of it… There was no one else, anywhere, she could have shown," I felt sick for her. She's ranked Cassie below this. Cassie keeps the window cracked; Randi is the sealed room.

And then the photo. She shows the cropped one, gets needled — "Miss November," "you only gave him *half*?" — and I watched the exact mechanism I've seen before: Randi files her as a girl who *flinched*, and Vee, who will not be that girl at this table, hands over the uncropped nude to prove she isn't. "I gave him *all* of it." That's the induction move in miniature, and it's devastating that Vee authors it herself, feeling proud. The nude photo has now reached Randi's actual hand and eyes — the harvest I've been waiting to see land, landed. "Showing him your toes, eh?" is such a light, awful little needle.

But here's what genuinely moved me, and moved my *feeling about Randi* for the first time in the book: she cracks. Twice, maybe three times. When Vee says "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached. Not — the other way around," Randi's eyes go "bright and a little unfixed," there's a catch in her breath she doesn't smooth over, and she says "*It doesn't*," low and not even. And Vee misreads it as her own story landing hard — pride. But I don't think that's what I watched. Something reached Randi there. I can't name it — is it grief? recognition? Does *she* not get looked at that way? Is "it doesn't" the truest thing she's said, escaping her? And again at "Just us," softer than the plan called for, "a small roughness under it." And the goodbye: Vee leans up after the kiss the last half-inch, taking a second for herself, and Randi "looked right into her eyes from there — close, steady, a beat past what a goodbye needed." For the first time I don't fully know that Randi is untouched. The text earned a real suspicion that something in her is not entirely an instrument — and given how opaque she's been, that's a genuine crack, not me manufacturing it. It complicates my "trust zero." Trust still zero. But the *human* count went from zero.

The erotic charge is high but it's a strange, sad heat — it's Vee *narrating* the sex rather than the sex itself, and the yolk-breaking, toast-dragging, "the middle, when it goes everywhere" is doing double duty as food-porn and sex-porn while Randi watches Vee's mouth. The heat is really in the watching-of-Vee, which is the whole book's move, so it works on me and unsettles me at once. The peak, oddly, was the photo pass and the eye-contact at the end — charged and dread-laced.

Friction: almost none. If anything the "sex brain… I used to be a person with thoughts" line is the closest to Vee performing charm for the reader, but it's in character and I bought it. The chapter is nearly all dialogue and it earns its length by accumulation — each confession a fresh deposit.

The title, **"All the Time"** — on the page it's the amount, the *doesn't stop*, "the more we do it the more I want it," the color that comes up Friday-to-Sunday. Innocuous, warm. But it recolors: *all the time* is also total surveillance, the attention "on me the whole time," and — chillingly — it's the constancy of a thing that has no beginning she can point to, "no place it began," which is exactly what a trap that's already closed feels like. And the phone is "for the music, and… to take pictures." All the time.

The book's frame — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is almost a clean demonstration of the tagline. Vee freely orders the expensive eggs (over her real debt, which I still carry and she's still not saying — "signed and tipped well and said nothing about the number"), freely shows the nude, freely proposes the shared class that puts Randi beside her three more mornings a week. Every yes hers. The sum, a woman handing her whole self to two hands she thinks are two friends.

What I want next: I want to know what cracked in Randi and whether it recurs, or whether she seals it. I dread the debrief — Vee just gave Randi the uncropped photo and the full inventory, and it will reach Pace. And I want Cassie, badly, as the cracked window; Vee just chose Randi over her *again* by signing them up for a class "just us," Cassie explicitly out. *Guess from here:* the shared Anthro class is Randi tightening the tether — three mornings a week now, not one. And that goodbye kiss suggests the Randi-Vee line may not stay only a farming tool for Randi.
