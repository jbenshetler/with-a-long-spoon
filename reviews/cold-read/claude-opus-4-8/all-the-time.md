**Felt read**

This one worked on me quietly and then caught in my throat. There's no *event* in it, and Vee knows there isn't — "there was nothing to *give*, not the way there usually was. There was no scene." That's the loveliest thing about the chapter: for once she isn't handing Randi a story with edges, she's handing her the whole diffuse texture of being happy, and I felt how new that is for her. "I used to be a person with thoughts." I laughed, and then I ached, because the thing she's describing — waking up hungry, sleeping whole nights in the woods, being *looked at* with the body attached — is a person coming alive in real time.

Randi's absolution is the beat that got me: "You're just finally hungry out loud… You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*." That's exactly the book's whole thesis about Vee spoken tenderly to her face, and it's *true*, and it's also — I can't unsee it — the most efficient possible thing to say to keep her open and reaching. That's the doubleness I've lived in this whole book, and it's never been more comfortable *and* more uneasy than here. Because it soothes Vee, and it's kind, and it's real, and it's also management. I don't think Randi is faking it. That's what makes it worse.

And then the crack. Vee says the thing she's never said — "Like he wants *me*. With the body attached" — and it lands on Randi. "Randi's eyes had gone bright and a little unfixed… a catch in her breath she didn't quite smooth over." "It doesn't," Randi says, low, not quite even. I sat up. Vee reads it as pride, as a good story landing — but I know what I know from the bench in chapter one, from the bottle-cap under the thumbnail, and I heard Randi being hit square by the one thing she gave up for good: to be wanted with the body attached, not for the face. Vee said the exact sentence Randi can't have, to the woman running the plan, and the woman flinched. That's the whole chapter for me. Ahead of Vee, again, and this time it *hurt* to be ahead.

The photo hand-over is where the erotic charge lives, and it's charged less by skin than by nerve. Vee escalating from the cropped one to "I gave him *all* of it," turning the phone around after working up to it — that's her performing daring *for Randi*, wanting not to be "a girl who flinched" at Randi's table. "Showing him your toes, eh?" and the two of them pink and delighted together. I felt the pull of it, but I also felt the drift: Vee is now handing Randi the same nudes she handed Pace, and calling it the joy of a friend she can tell everything. The word "greedy" she's afraid of about herself — I keep thinking she's the generous one being spent.

The kiss reversed on me. Every goodbye before this, Randi lingered and Vee's arms were slow to come down. Here *Vee* is the one who "wouldn't let it," leaning up the last half-inch, "taking a second of it for herself," and — crucially — "decided it was what she wanted, and didn't bother about it again." The disavowal is thinning. That's a real movement since the practice-room panic; the wanting for Randi is surfacing and she's stopped fighting it. And then Randi holds the eye contact "a beat past what a goodbye needed," and I couldn't tell if that was the operator or the woman genuinely caught. Neither could the page, quite, and I loved that it left it ajar.

The class sign-up — "Just us," said "softer than the plan called for, a small roughness under it" — pointed me somewhere. More scheduled Vee-time, engineered as easily as everything Randi engineers, but with that catch in it again. I read it as Randi wanting the hours for real now, not only for the plan.

Trust/suspicion: nothing *new* went wrong. No cruelty on the page. What unsettles me is structural, and I've carried it a while — I just watched the plan run on pure warmth, no scene required, and watched it start to cost the operator something. My fear for Vee is the same and quieter: every yes is hers, the credit card, the skipped Tuesdays in the library with "sex brain," the nudes now living in two phones. She's spending herself gladly and someone chose that she would.

Title: **"All the Time"** — it lands as the amount of it, the sex, the wanting that "doesn't *stop*," the every-week saturation. But read twice it's colder: *all the time* she gives, all the hours parceled between two people who know each other and she doesn't. The volume line **A POLITE INVITATION** under **WITH A LONG SPOON** — the spoon is for supping with the devil, and this chapter is the politest, warmest supper yet, which is exactly the chill of it. **Book One** tells me we're nowhere near the drop.

What I want next: I want Vee to keep sleeping whole nights and eating out loud — I'm rooting for the aliveness even as I brace. Guess from here: the anthro class is Randi buying weekday hours, and Vee's own desire for Randi is the fuse this book is now lighting on purpose.
