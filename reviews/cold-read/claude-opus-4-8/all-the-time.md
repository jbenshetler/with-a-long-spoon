## Reader reaction

# Felt Read

This one is quiet and it terrified me in a low, steady way, like watching someone talk happily on a floor that I can see is thin and they can't.

It's a brunch. Randi's back — finally, after all those chapters of her being the held breath — and she's exactly as I remembered her: total attention, the wine forgotten, eyes going to Vee's mouth and hands, reframing everything Vee is ashamed of into a gift. "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud." That line is genuinely beautiful and it did work on me, warm-bath warm, and *that's* the problem — I can't tell anymore where the real love ends and the method begins, and I've stopped being able to tell because Vee stopped being able to. She's inside it now. "The one friend she trusted with all of it. There was no one else, anywhere, she could have shown."

And she shows her the photo.

That's the chapter's gut-punch, and it's done so softly I almost missed how bad it was. The nude Pace composed and promised he'd "never share" — Vee hands it across the table herself. Both versions. The cropped one first, and then, because Randi needles her just right ("you only gave him *half?*"), the whole uncropped length of her. Randi didn't take the photo. Randi got Vee to *offer* it, twice, working up her nerve, "the thrill and the shame running together." I sat there thinking: he didn't have to break his promise. He never will have to. She carried it out of the house and put it in the other lover's hand herself, and felt *proud.* That's the tagline made flesh — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." I've never felt the mechanism land as cleanly as it did watching that phone turn around.

The moment that actually made the hair stand up: Vee says the thing about feeling wanted "with the body attached," and Randi cracks. "Randi's eyes had gone bright and a little unfixed… a catch in her breath she didn't quite smooth over." And the two flat words: "It doesn't." Vee reads it as *her story landing on Randi,* pride that a Saturday of hers could reach a woman like that. But I know what Vee doesn't — that Randi is Pace's lover, that Vee is describing, in intimate forensic detail, *their* man, the slow build, the twice-three-times-a-night, the strength then the gentleness. Randi is sitting there being described her own lover by the girl they picked, and the girl doesn't know she's doing it. "It doesn't" — doesn't what? Doesn't sound unhinged? Doesn't stop? Or *he doesn't* — doesn't do that with me anymore, doesn't build it slow for me? I couldn't close it and I don't think I'm meant to. It's the first time I've seen the game *cost* Randi something on the page, and it's the most human she's ever been to me. I didn't expect to feel for her. I did.

**Vee:** I love her and I'm frightened flat. Her happiness is real and it's the most exposed she's ever been. The detail that got me most wasn't sexual — it was "I *sleep* there… whole nights… I go down like somebody reached over and switched me off. I didn't know I *could.*" That's a woman handing over the deepest tell there is, that she's finally safe enough to be unconscious, in the house at the end of the woods with nobody around, and the person she's giving it to is one half of the thing built to catch her. And then she signs up for a class to get *more* Randi. Every move she makes this chapter tightens the net, and every one is hers, freely, gladly.

**Randi:** moved, hard. Still can't trust her — she does the photo-extraction with a craftsman's touch, the not-leaning-in, letting the unfinished sentence "be finished," "Or to." / "Take pictures." That's a professional working a source. But the catch in her breath is the first crack I've seen, and "something crossed her face and was gone" when Cassie's ruled out and it's "just us" — twice that happened. I think this is starting to cost her. Whether that makes her more dangerous or less, I can't tell.

**Pace:** offstage entirely, and yet the whole chapter is *about* him, rendered in worship. Nothing new about him except by refraction — but hearing Vee catalogue him made the distrust and the ache sit right on top of each other again.

**Erotic charge:** it's a clothed chapter and it runs warmer than some of the sex scenes have. The peak is the food — the yolk breaking and running gold over the ham, Vee dragging the toast through it, "a low sound got out of her before she could help it," her hand going back before she'd decided. That's the whole book's engine — hunger made then met, the body publishing what she'd hide — done in daylight over eggs. Charged, not clinical. The recounted sex ("the sheets in both hands before he's even really started") is hot precisely because it's her telling it, pink and past caring.

**Friction:** almost none. If I reach — "warm as a bath" and "warm as a room light" are both lovely but they're the same move twice in a page, warmth-as-illumination. And I clocked the "Miss November" / "Showing him your toes" banter as the author enjoying the double-edge a hair too much. But these are tiny. The chapter mostly trusts me to feel the floor under Vee, and I did.

**The title — "All the Time":** on the surface it's her line, near enough — *how much of it there is,* the amount, the sex all the time, the wanting that doesn't stop, the color that comes up every weekend "all switched on." Innocent, drunk-on-abundance. But sitting here after, it curdles a little: *all the time* is also what Randi now has — a standing weekly channel into Vee's every private thing, Tuesday-in-stats plus Saturday-the-real-thing, the pipeline running on schedule, forever. And it's what Pace has: a woman thinking about Friday in the middle of the library. The title's the trap's own boast. It didn't give anything away going in; it recolored coming out.

**The book's title and tagline:** never landed harder. This is the cleanest illustration yet of the freely-given yes. Nobody took the photo. Nobody demanded the class. She initiated the longer kiss herself — "she was the one who wouldn't let it, leaning up the last half-inch." *With a Long Spoon* — you sup with the devil with one — but the whole horror here is there's no spoon at all, no distance, she's climbing across the table to close it.

**What I want / dread next:** I dread that photo existing in Randi's phone, or Randi's memory, now outside the one house it was promised to stay in. I want — badly — to be in the room when the two worlds finally touch, and I keep bracing for the class ("just us," that thing crossing Randi's face) to be where it happens, or Thanksgiving. *Guess from here:* Randi's crack — "It doesn't" — is the first sign the game is turning real enough to hurt the players, and I think Book One is walking toward the moment Vee finds out there was a game at all. I don't want to watch it and I can't stop.
