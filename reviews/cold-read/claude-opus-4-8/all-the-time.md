## Reader reaction

Randi's back. After five chapters of her being a gathering absence, the dread I've been carrying got a face again — and the chapter is doing exactly the cruel thing my carry-forward warned me about: it's warm, it's funny, it's genuinely lovely, and I *enjoyed* it, and that enjoyment is what scares me.

Vee first, because she's who I love here. She's radiant. The whole opening — "the weeks in between ran gray; it was only Friday to Sunday that the color came up" — is beautiful, and I felt happy *for* her even while my stomach tightened. Her confession that there's "no scene" to give this time, "just — all of it," is the most content she's ever been on the page, and it's the most exposed. I noticed she's ordering the eggs Benedict now instead of the safe cheap soup, "the daring a little easier each time" — and that small thing moved me, because it reads as real growth and also as her guard coming all the way down at the exact table where she should keep it up. The line "I used to be a person with thoughts" made me laugh and then wince.

Her worry — "Am I turning into some kind of — sex pervert?" — and Randi's answer is the hinge of the chapter for me. "You're not greedy. You're just finally hungry out loud... You've been starving your whole life and calling it *fine*." On first pass that is genuinely the kindest, most liberating thing anyone's said to Vee, and I felt the relief break out of her. And underneath — because I've been reading this book — I heard *the plan*: Randi naming Vee's appetite to her, giving her permission to want more, converting shame into appetite. She produces with words what Pace produces with the body. That's my ledger talking, and the text earned the double-read without ever tipping its hand. That's the thing that unsettles me most: I couldn't point to a sinister line. There isn't one. It's all warmth.

Then the two beats that actually made me sit up.

First: **"Like he wants *me.* With the body attached. Not — the other way around."** And Randi doesn't answer. Her eyes go "bright and a little unfixed," a catch in her breath, *"It doesn't," Randi said. Low, and not quite even.* — she didn't finish the sentence. That's the plan escaping her. Vee reads it as pride, that her Saturday could reach a woman like Randi that far. I read it as Randi being genuinely hit by something — maybe want, maybe the machinery of what she's building landing back on her. "It doesn't" — it doesn't *what*? Doesn't stop? Doesn't sound unhinged? The withheld object is the whole point. I felt a small cold thrill there.

Second — and this is where the chapter tightened into something I dreaded — **the photograph.** My carry-forward had a gun on the mantel: the composed nude on Pace's phone, "I'd never share your photograph." And here Vee, unprompted-but-steered, hands it across the table. The steering is so soft it's almost invisible: "You don't have to show me" is what makes her show it. Then Randi's "Legs like yours, though — and you only gave him *half?*" — needling the *cropped* version until Vee, refusing to be "a girl who flinched," pulls up the full uncropped one and puts it in Randi's hand. "I gave him *all* of it." I felt genuinely queasy watching that, because Vee experiences it as trust and daring, and I'm watching the one image that could "ruin the woman in it" travel from Pace's phone into Randi's eyes — the two tracks brushing against each other for the first time in a way *Vee doesn't register* but I do. Randi "kept looking — past a glance, past what a verdict needed." "Okay, *Miss November.*" That's a joke; it also read to me like acquisition.

The goodbye kiss moved another riser, and this time Vee is the one who leans in the last half-inch, "taking a second of it for herself," and does it "before she'd thought." The staircase is climbing and now *she's* reaching for it. Randi "looked at her a moment, close, something in the look Vee didn't try to read." I've stopped being able to tell how much of Randi is want and how much is plan, and I think that blur is deliberate and it's working on me.

The anthro elective — "Sign up with me?... It'd be like stats, except we'd have picked it" — is a lovely innocent-sounding hook, and "something crossed her face and was gone" when Cassie's confirmed *out* ("just us") is the beat I'd flag. Cassie removed from the picture, Randi and Vee alone in a class together: Vee reads it as "the ordinary pleasure of a plan made." I read the little face-flicker as the plan liking that Cassie's not there. That's the one place the text let me feel the machinery move.

**Erotic charge:** medium and mostly displaced — this is *reported* heat, Vee narrating the volume and variety of her weekends ("I've got the sheets in both hands before he's even really started," "two, three... and then there's mornings"). It's warm and I felt her flush, but it's told-across-soup, so it simmers rather than burns — a 1, maybe touching 2 at the photo, where the actual erotic act *in the room* is Randi looking at Vee's naked body and Vee's foot going under the table while she's looked at. That was the live charge.

**Friction:** honestly very little. The prose is confident and the register held. The only place I felt the author's hand at all was "*You've been starving your whole life and calling it fine*" — it's a gorgeous line and it's *also* nearly a thesis statement, and for a half-second I felt it wanting to be underlined. But it stays in Randi's mouth as absolution, so it earned its place. I'll allow it.

**What I want / dread next:** I dread the photo now that it's out of Pace's exclusive keeping — I want to know if Randi asks for it, keeps it, or if it surfaces somewhere it shouldn't. *(Guess from here:)* the anthro class is the next room where the two tracks fuse, with Cassie deliberately absent. I dread the moment Vee sees the whole picture, and I keep circling my oldest open question — the two tracks have now *physically* touched at this table (Pace's image, in Randi's hand) and Vee still can't see it. That's the blindfold, early. I want to keep reading and I also want to look away, which I think is the intended effect.
