# Cold read — Sorority

*scene: scenes/sorority.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: all-told*

## Reader reaction

This is the shoe day, and I've been braced for it since Randi claimed it in stats — "Don't you dare tell me no" — so I came in already wary of exactly what happened. And it happened anyway, gently, warmly, and I still felt the trap close.

Let me start with what actually worked on me, because it did. The store-comedy stretch is the best pure *fun* the book has given me in a while, and it disarmed me completely. The yeti bit, the canoe-sized shoe, the six-inch bedazzled platforms — "Do these go with burgundy?" — the limp clearance flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." I laughed. And crucially it's Vee winning here: this is her turf, "the ground was hers," she's the funniest woman in Virginia in her own store, and Randi lets her have it. The laugh "that costs her something to give" — Randi folded over the bench — is the closest I've felt to the two of them being *equals* just enjoying each other. Which is, I now understand on cold reflection, exactly why it's placed here. You put the warmest, silliest, most genuinely peer-to-peer scene right before you walk her past what she can't afford. Earn the dark by being light. But in the moment: I liked them. I liked *her.*

Then the pivot. Vee finds the tan heel — sensible, holds her, a price that "didn't make the debit flinch," goes with everything so she'll actually wear it again. This is the whole Vee I've been loving for twenty-two chapters: the money-watcher, the TJMaxx-math girl, the one who buys the thing she'll use twice. And Randi kills it with one line I have to quote because it's the hinge of the chapter: *"A dress a man made to you doesn't want to go with everything. It wants to walk in late and stop the room cold."* She says out loud the fantasy Vee's been running privately all week — the door, the room turning — and "hearing it in someone else's mouth made it real." That's the confidante move I've watched her make over and over: she produces with words what Vee wants and won't claim. Only this time it costs Vee money she does not have.

And here's where my stomach turned. The second store. The number "written small on the leather of the sole," and "the number on that sole started where her most ended and kept on going." She can't say it. She *cannot* stand in the cream-and-brass room and tell Randi she can't afford to be inside the door — "so she did nothing, which was its own kind of going under." That line landed hard. Then the credit card. Not the debit — the debit "only ever spent what was already behind it." The emergency card, "nothing under it," "it would be sitting there next month with nothing behind it but next month," and she signs and smiles and "would not flinch." I felt genuinely bad. This is the first time the falling has cost her something concrete and self-damaging, something Randi's warmth walked her straight into.

And then the detail that made it worse and made me trust Randi *less* than at any point so far: **the same-size reveal, and Randi buying her own loafers.** "That's me exactly. Same feet, baby. Same size, same everything... we can share... it's practically a marriage." I don't know yet what that's *for*, but it doesn't feel like nothing — Randi being able to wear Vee's shoes, the identity-swap language, sits cold under the warmth. And then Randi buys loafers "a multiple of Vee's, several times it," "never once looked at it," walks out wearing them "like it was nothing on earth." The chapter puts Vee white-knuckling a box she can't afford right next to Randi trading shoes mid-afternoon like weather. The class gap, which the book usually keeps as texture, is *blunt* here on purpose — and Randi is oblivious to it in a way I can't tell is real oblivion or performed. Either way, she just spent Vee into a debt Vee will carry alone, and felt only delight.

The kneeling shoe-fitting is the erotic peak, and it's Randi doing the Pace move now — down on one knee, "I've got her," holding Vee's bare foot in both hands, the thumb across the frosted-plum toenail, and Vee's hand shooting out to grab Randi's shoulder because the floor of her *tipped.* "Felt it go all the way up the length of her." That's the physical staircase again, one clear riser up from the goodbye kisses, and it's transacted in a shop with a saleswoman standing right there, disguised as service. It charged me — the polish "chosen to catch the light as the silk did," the callback to his mirror. But it also frightened me, because Randi looked up and "smiled and said nothing at all" and went back to the box. The withholding. She felt what she did to Vee and pocketed it.

"Lover" is the other word that did work here. Randi hands Vee the word — "A dress your *lover* made you?" — "more than Vee had ever let herself call him," and "Pace rearranged in her own chest into something adult and lit." Randi naming Vee's own man to her, upgrading him in Vee's mouth. She's doing to Pace-in-Vee's-head what she does to everything: naming it, making it real, steering. And of course *Randi is his actual lover*, and she's the one teaching Vee the word. That's vicious under the sweetness and I don't think Vee feels a thing.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** lower on Randi than it's ever been, and the text earned it this time — not through a sinister signal but through *consequence.* She walked a girl who counts every dollar into an unpayable card and felt nothing but pleasure, and the same-size/"share"/"marriage" beat and the "lover" handoff all sit oblique and cold on the reread even as they play warm live. Vee I trust completely and fear for. The one flash where Vee "did nothing, which was its own kind of going under" is the closest she's come to her own alarm, and she overrode it to not flinch.

**Friction:** almost none. The comedy earns its length. If anything the ending sidewalk beat — "You can put it in the trunk, you know. It's not going to *spoil*" and the unperformable laugh, "she had never once in her life had a friend like this" — is the author leaning warm right at the moment I'm meant to feel the trap, and I can feel the thumb *a little* ("could not stop" thinking she'd never had a friend like this is laid on close). But it's also just true to Vee, and the placement — that thought landing *while she carries a debt she can't pay* — does more damage warm than any cold line would. I bought it.

**Title — "Sorority":** the chapter's not set at the sorority; the induction and the girls are only invoked ("the whole room is going to stop," "every one of those girls in the one dress she owns"). So the title points forward, to the room the shoes are *for.* But the charge I feel is the other sense: the two of them made a sisterhood this afternoon — "practically a marriage," "same everything," "a friend like this" — a private sorority of two, sealed over shoes, on Vee's dime. Sorority as the belonging she's buying her way into and can't afford. It's oblique and it deepens; it doesn't give anything away going in.

**The book's title and tagline:** *With a Long Spoon* — supping with the devil, and she keeps reaching for the shared bowl. Literalized here: same shoe size, "we can share," she takes the word "lover" from Randi's hand. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this is the cleanest illustration yet. No one made Vee buy those shoes. She chose the credit card. She chose not to flinch. Every step was hers, and every step was steered so gently she'll swear it was her idea, which it also genuinely was. That's the trap the tagline names, and I felt it work in real time.

**What I want / dread next:** the induction, obviously — the room those shoes are for, the entrance she's rehearsed. I dread the money coming due; I dread that Randi will be there watching her own creation walk in. *Guess from here:* the same-size thing pays off later — Randi wearing something of Vee's, or Vee's shoes ending up somewhere they shouldn't (his house). And I keep waiting for the two tracks to touch in Vee's awareness. Not yet. She's never had a friend like this.
