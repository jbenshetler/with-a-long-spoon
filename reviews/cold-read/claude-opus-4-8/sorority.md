# Cold read (grounded) — Sorority

*scene: scenes/sorority.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch024 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter is the happiest Vee has been in the whole book, and I spent most of it braced against exactly that — because I know what she doesn't, and every time she calls Randi "the friend I've never had" it lands on me like a bruise. The whole thing runs golden: late-morning glow, both her girls in one room, the dress finished, laughter over shoes. And the cruelty of my position as reader is that I can't enjoy it cleanly. I *want* to. Vee has done nothing but be lovely and hungry and brave. But Randi kneeling on the boutique floor, holding Vee's bare foot up to the light, thumbing the frosted-plum toenail until Vee's hand shoots out to steady herself — I know that hand belongs to the woman who sat across a stew she wouldn't eat and asked Pace "how long are you going to take," and it makes the tenderness feel like a net closing.

The stats lecture nearly undid me. Simpson's paradox — "same numbers, nobody touched one of them," each year one man's ahead, put the years together and it reverses. Coming right after the checkpoint's latent variables and error-correcting codes, I read it as the book naming its own con out loud: every single thing Randi does to Vee is warm and true *in isolation*, and only when you aggregate them does the sign flip into something predatory. That's the most unsettled I got, and the text earned it — the professor's red dot on the one number that changes everything, while Vee sits happy in the middle, is almost too on the nose, but I felt it anyway.

The heartbreak of the chapter is Cassie, and it's the cleanest thing in the book. "Cassie did not care about the dress... the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." Cassie never looks at the numbers, never cares about shoes, watches only Vee — and that's precisely the opposite of Randi, who cares about the charmeuse and the heel height and reads the *cut* the way her eyes read the girl. The book set two ways of looking side by side and let me feel which one loves her. "You lucky slut" from Randi lands lower than she meant, and Vee's body keeping "the night's account since eight" — the soreness up both thighs — is the closest thing to heat here, and it's private, not for Randi at all.

The credit card gutted me quietly. Vee putting the debit back — "the card that only ever spent what was already behind it" — and signing for shoes with "nothing under it," knowing it'll be sitting there next month, not letting it reach her face "because the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch." That's her whole thaw curdling into something that'll cost her, literally. And Randi beside her buying loafers at several times the price without a glance, wearing them out. The asymmetry is the point: this friendship is a current Vee steps into that's "already moving," and it's moving her past what she can afford, in every sense. "It's not a want I have to own as a want. They were what the dress was owed" — that reasoning is the sound of someone talking herself over an edge, and I recognized it because it's the same move she made with the panties, the same as the mimosa she didn't price the second time.

Where did it *land* warmly? The shoe-store slapstick — the canoe-sized yeti shoe, the pink rhinestone platforms, "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal," Randi folding over the bench in the laugh "that costs her something to give." That worked on me straight, no shadow, and I let it. And the box held to her chest with both arms "like it might get up and leave her," Randi's "you can put it in the trunk, it's not going to spoil" — that's genuinely dear, a girl who's never trusted the world with a beautiful thing. I ached for her there rather than feared for her, which is a different, softer register the chapter earns.

**The titles.** "Sorority" — the chapter barely touches the actual sorority (only Vee's future induction, the other girls in "the one dress she owns that has to do for everything"), so I read it as pointing past the institution to *this* — the real sisterhood Vee thinks she's found, a chosen closeness that's warmer than the blood-navy department-store kind. Which is exactly the thing being weaponized. The book title **Sorority** now reads doubled and cold to me. The series line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." That's the whole machine in a proverb, and "A Polite Invitation" is Pace's courtliness and Randi's warmth both, the invitation Vee keeps saying yes to. It didn't give away plot so much as confirm the dread I already carry: someone at this table is the devil, and the spoon is manners.

**What I want/dread:** I dread the induction now — the daydream of walking in late and stopping the room is being built toward too lovingly not to become the site of the drop. *Guess from here:* the shoes she can't pay for and the dress and the public entrance all converge there, and Randi's the one arranging it. I want Cassie to catch it first, because she's the only one looking at the girl.
