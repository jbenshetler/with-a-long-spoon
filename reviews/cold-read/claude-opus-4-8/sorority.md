# Cold read (grounded) — Sorority

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter is the lightest the book has let me feel in a while, and that lightness is exactly what's making me braced. Vee underslept, overfull, sore in the best way, happy "and on time, now, for nothing" — I love her completely here. The three-girls-in-one-room setup is genuinely lovely: Cassie cracking the made-bed case before Randi even starts, letting Randi have the reveal anyway; Vee turned "happy in the middle" between them. I melted at Cassie not caring about the dress — "the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." That's the cleanest, truest love in the book, and it's the one I trust.

And that's the knife. Because the whole chapter is Vee being adored from both sides, and I know one of those sides is the trap closing. Randi reading "charmeuse… and a good weight," going down on one knee in the boutique, running her thumb across Vee's painted big toe so the floor "tipped" — that landed on me as heat and as predation at once, and I couldn't make it be only one. The toenail touch is the same move as the lunch interrogations: finding the private thing Vee chose for Pace and laying a hand on it. It's tender. It's also Randi collecting.

The "to" versus "for" line — "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you" — got me the way it got Vee, throat-tight, because it names the thing the whole dress has been about: a thing built onto her while she held still. But I can't unhear it from the other chapters. Randi naming Vee's feelings back to her clean is precisely how she's been reeled in. When Vee reads Randi's long quiet as *jealousy* — "nobody who had ever sat up a night over anything of hers" — and aches for her, that's where I nearly came apart, because from the recent "Portion" chapter I carry what Vee can't: Randi rode Pace with her eyes shut and no *tell me*, slept with her back to him, and left a bowl untouched. Vee's generosity toward Randi's "loneliness" is aimed at a wound real but pointed the wrong way. That gap — Vee pitying the woman engineering her — is the chapter's cruelest sweetness.

The shoe shopping is where the book's money-and-appetite spine does quiet, devastating work. Vee's TJMaxx math, the tan heel that "goes with everything," talking herself into the sensible one out loud — and then Randi, with "a dress a man sat up nights making doesn't want to go with everything," dismantling it. I felt the credit card go down. "There was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it." That's the first thing in chapters that read to me as a real cost landing, not a joy — Vee spending money she doesn't have because the dress is "owed." Randi buying loafers at several times the price "like it was nothing on earth" right beside her sharpened it: the whole friendship runs on Vee being carried into a world where she can't afford the entry, and being told the flinch is the only unforgivable thing. "The one thing she would not do in this room was flinch." That's the mother's voice flipped inside out — shame repurposed as glamour's discipline.

Trust: I have no new reason to doubt Cassie, every reason to keep loving her. Randi I distrust exactly as much as before, but the distrust got more intimate this chapter — the toe, the kneeling, the "same size, practically a marriage," the "lover" she hands Vee like a gift that rearranges Pace "into something adult and lit." She's not just mining intel now; she's grafting herself into the place Pace holds. Pace isn't on the page at all, and after "Portion" his absence feels loaded — he's "in no hurry," and Randi's whole afternoon is hurry.

Erotic charge: lower than the two chapters before (which were peak), but it's there and it's all displaced — the soreness Vee can't stop feeling in the lecture, "slut" landing lower than Randi meant, and that toenail thumb-stroke that swung the room. The heat here is Randi's hands on Vee, not Pace, which is the point and the dread.

Friction: almost none. The Simpson's paradox lecture is the book's usual trick — a stats concept mirroring the con — and honestly it's the most elegant one yet: "Same numbers. Nobody touched one of them." Each year the first man wins; put the years together and the second man does. That's Vee's whole life reframing without a single fact changing — the same autumn read as luck or as trap depending on what you aggregate. I noticed the author's thumb, but the thumb pressed on something true, so I didn't mind it.

The titles: this chapter is "Sorority," and the dress is still "shown to not one living soul" — the induction it was built for hasn't happened, so the title points forward, to the room Vee's rehearsing walking into late. It reads less like this chapter's content than a promissory note: the sorority is where this all gets performed, and where Randi's and Brooke's world owns the terms. The volume frame — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, titled *Sorority* — the "long spoon" is the sup-with-the-devil proverb, and a whole book one shaped like courtship ("A Polite Invitation") tells me the invitation is the devil's and the spoon won't be long enough. That recolors every warm thing. Vee's being invited — to brunch, to the Mercedes, to the boutique, to the house, eventually to the sorority — and the frame says the invitation is the mechanism.

What I want/dread: I want the induction, the room turning, Vee getting her one swept-up triumph before anything breaks. I dread the credit-card bill as the first visible seam, and I dread Vee discovering Randi and Pace through exactly the intimacy she's trusting most. *Guess from here:* the shared shoe size — "we can share… practically a marriage" — feels planted, like Randi is literally stepping into Vee's dimensions. And Randi's long quiet over the all-night dress wasn't jealousy of a man; I think it was jealousy of *Vee*, of being made something *to*.
