# Cold read (grounded) — Sorority

## Reader reaction

This one went down sweet and easy and I kept bracing for the knife that never came — which is its own kind of dread, because I know it's coming eventually. Mostly, though, I was just *happy* alongside Vee, and a little sick about how happy she is.

The stats-room opening is the loveliest thing the book's done with its three women in one frame. Vee "turned between the two of them, happy in the middle" — I felt that exact warmth, both her girls in one room, her overfull and underslept and glowing. And I love how the chapter quietly tells me Cassie is the only one looking at the *right* thing. The forty seconds Vee plays good student at the Simpson's paradox lecture, and then she surfaces and realizes Cassie "had never gone down into them at all" — Cassie watching her friend, not the dress, "because the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." That got me. Cassie is my clean heart in this book, and her devotion is rendered without a drop of schmaltz. The made bed as "a tattletale in the cold morning light" — plain, and Cassie cracks the whole weekend off it hours before Randi does, and lets Randi have the reveal anyway. That generosity is so Cassie.

And then there's Randi, and here's where my pleasure and my unease ran on the same track the whole time. The shoe-shopping is genuinely, disarmingly *funny* — the yeti shoe, the pink-rhinestone six-inch platforms held to the light ("Do these go with burgundy?"), the flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." Vee "the funniest woman in Virginia." I laughed, I rooted for the friendship, I wanted it to be real. But I've read the checkpoint and I've read "Portion," so I can't *not* see the architecture. Randi talks Vee out of the sensible tan heel — the one that "goes with everything," that she'd "actually wear again" — and into the backless credit-card heels she can't pay for, using the exact line Pace's whole method runs on: a dress "made *to* you" can't sit on "an apology." She's escalating Vee's commitment, her exposure, her *want*, same as always. The "same feet, same size, practically a marriage" bit is charming and also a leash.

And the toe moment. God. Randi on one knee on the pale floor, holding Vee's bare foot to the light, the frosted-plum polish — "a thumb moved once across the nail of the big toe, light — and Vee felt it go all the way up the length of her." Vee's hand shooting to Randi's shoulder to stop the room swinging. That's the hottest beat in the chapter by a distance, and it's a *shoe fitting*. Randi has now put herself exactly where Pace knelt at the fitting, doing to Vee's bare foot what Pace's mouth did to Vee's fingers in "Peekaboo." The echo is deliberate and it burned. The seduction of Vee by Randi is no longer subtext.

The credit-card beat is where the dread sharpened to a point. Vee putting the debit back — "the card she paid her own way with" — and laying down the emergencies card, knowing "there was nothing under it," knowing "it would be sitting there next month," and signing and smiling and refusing to flinch. That's the cost arriving, finally, in dollars, and nobody in that boutique will feel it but her. Set beside Randi signing "a multiple of Vee's, and then several times that again" without a glance — I felt the gulf open. Vee carrying her box out "against her chest with both arms" like it might leave her. That image undid me a little. She has never owned a beautiful thing and doesn't trust the world with it.

What moved for me: my tenderness toward Vee only deepened, and my fear for her with it — she's spending money she doesn't have on a life two people engineered. Randi I find myself *liking* more on the page even as I trust her less, which is the exact vise the book keeps tightening. And I keep hearing "Portion" under all this gold — Randi, the night before, demanding of Pace "how long are you going to take," riding him with her eyes shut, no *tell me*, turning her back. Whatever's cracking in her, she walked straight from it into a day of radiant generosity with Vee. The one flicker: the hand that "stayed" at the shoulder seam when Vee said "He was up all night finishing it," Randi "somewhere else, behind her own face." Vee reads it as *poor Randi, nobody ever sat up a night for her*. I read it as something colder and sadder and I'm not sure which.

Erotic charge: lower-key than "Peekaboo," but the toe moment is a real spike, and the body-soreness beat — the "good soreness no gym had ever given her" when "slut" "landed lower than Randi meant it to" — kept the weekend humming under the comedy. It simmered rather than burned, which felt right for a shopping-trip chapter.

Friction: almost none. The Simpson's paradox lecture is the book's now-familiar move — a stats concept ("same numbers, nobody touched one of them") laid beside the plot as a quiet key — and after latent variables, substitution, error-correcting codes, I can feel the pattern as a *device* now, the author's hand just visible. But it's handled with a light enough touch (the professor "reading the back of a cereal box") that I didn't mind it. It even lands: two true things that reverse when you combine them. That's Vee's whole situation — every tender fact is real, and together they add up to the opposite of what she thinks.

**The titles.** "Sorority" — oblique and a little ominous to me. The dress is *for* the induction, the sorority is the stage Vee's rehearsing walking into late, stopping the room. But it's also Randi's world, Randi's board, the house that thinks Pace is her boyfriend. Naming the chapter "Sorority" when the induction hasn't happened points me forward, toward that room full of girls "in the one dress she owns," and makes me wonder whose sorority this really is and what it costs to be let in. The volume line — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*, title *Sorority*. "He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon" is what that rings for me: someone's dining with something dangerous and the spoon is the safe distance. A "polite invitation" is exactly how Vee's whole courtship has been staged — everything offered, nothing seized, "May I." The series name promises the devil's already at the table. I believe it.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here):** I want Vee to walk into that induction and stop the room — the daydream earned it. I dread the bill, literal and otherwise. My guess from here: the shoes and the credit card are a small rehearsal for a larger reckoning, and the induction is where something tips — either the moment of Vee's triumph or the moment the two worlds she doesn't know are one world finally touch in a room full of witnesses.
