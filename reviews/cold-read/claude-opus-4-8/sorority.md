# Cold read — Sorority

## Reader reaction

This one hurt to watch, and it was supposed to, and knowing that didn't make it hurt less. It's the smoothest, most frictionless chapter yet, and the frictionlessness is the horror.

Start with the dress finally being *seen*. That's the payoff I've been waiting on for chapters — the whole ache of the induction was that Vee ran the seduction on herself in his mirror with nobody there, and here at last someone looks. And of course the someone is Randi, not Cassie, not her mother, not a crowd — the one person in the world it flows *back* to. "Give me a second," and then she puts it on instead of holding it up on the hanger, because "the wanting got there before the plan, the wanting to be *seen* in it as she'd been seen in his mirror." That line did exactly what it meant to. The being-looked-at motif got its release, and it landed straight into the trap.

And then Randi's verdict: "He didn't make this *for* you. He made it *to* you." That's the knife. Because it's *true* — I felt it land the way Vee felt it land, and I've been feeling it since the sun-porch chapter without the word for it either. Randi hands Vee the word for her own tears. That's the terrible intimacy of this operator: she names your deepest feeling more precisely than you can, and the naming binds you to her. "Charmeuse… and a good weight" — the connoisseur's read of the thing another hand made *to* Vee. Randi reads the cut the way her own eyes traveled it, following the bias across Vee's body. Two hands on the same girl, and she doesn't know it's one design.

The shoe store, part one: this is the funniest the book has been, and it's the most dangerous part. The yeti shoe, the pink rhinestone platforms — "Do these go with burgundy?" — the limp clearance flat "one brisk walk from becoming a sandal." I laughed. Genuinely. And *that's* the tooling. This chapter spends its whole first half making me love Randi, making Vee "the funniest woman in Virginia," building the thing Vee names at the end: "she had never once in her life had a friend like this." Every carry-forward instinct in me is screaming that this warmth is a medium, an operation — and the chapter still got the real laugh out of me. The "real laugh, the one that costs her something to give," folded over the bench. Is it real? I can't tell, and neither can Vee, and that's the point.

Then the pivot, executed flawlessly. Vee finds the *right* shoe — the tan heel, the sensible one, "goes with everything," the price "that didn't make the debit flinch." Ground she owns, math she can run. And Randi doesn't argue the shoe. She goes for the size. "Seven." "That's me. That's me exactly." Same feet. And out of that delight — genuine or staged — she pulls the reframe: "A dress a man made *to* you doesn't want to go with everything. It wants to walk in late and stop the room cold." She says out loud the picture Vee's been running behind her eyes all week, and hearing it in someone else's mouth makes it real. That's the harvest-and-return move again: take the private thing, hand it back as permission.

The second store is the trap closing, and it's rendered so I felt the floor tip with Vee. The shoes with no price "anywhere near them," the number "written small on the leather of the sole," the TJMaxx math running out and "the number on that sole started beyond where her most ended and kept on going." The class-as-fog motif at its most acute — she "could not stand in this expensive hush and tell Randi… that she could not afford to be inside the door. So she did nothing, which was its own kind of going under."

And then — this is where I actually flinched — "A dress your *lover* made you?" Randi hands Vee the word *lover*, the word Vee "had never let herself call him," and "Pace rearranged in her own chest into something adult and lit." Randi is upgrading Vee's relationship to Pace *for* her, and Vee doesn't know Randi has any Pace to upgrade. The two hands, again, working the same seam.

The kneeling. Oh, this is the erotic peak of the chapter and it's terrifying. Randi down on one knee on the pale floor "in her own beautiful clothes," takes Vee's foot in both hands, and — before the box — holds it, turns it to the light, looks at the frosted-plum polish "chosen to catch the light as the silk did." "Mm." A thumb across the nail of the big toe, "and Vee felt it go all the way up the length of her… her hand shot out and found Randi's shoulder to stop it." That's the want-for-women lit and firing, exactly as flagged, and the operator is the one lighting it, on her knees, silent, smiling. The Pace signature echoes hard here — the maker kneeling, prepping, the one who touches and then bends back to the task. And "being stood up by" the shoe, "the arch of it firm as a hand under her foot" — the shoe does to her foot what Pace does to her whole self: holds, carries, so it's not balance but capture. The tan one would only have "balanced" her; this one *holds*.

Then the card. This is the chapter's real gut-punch, quieter than the polish. Vee puts the debit back — "the card she paid her own way with, the one that only ever spent what was already behind it" — and takes out the credit card, "and there was nothing under it. She knew that even while she did it." She goes into debt she can't service for shoes owed to a dress a man made to her, and "the one thing she would not do in this room was flinch." The class fog isn't fog anymore; she can see the number perfectly and pays it anyway, because the new arithmetic — "the shoes were what the dress was owed" — has "taken the wheel." That's the trap in its cleanest form: every yes freely given, fully seen, and the sum is a debt with nothing under it.

And the contrast the chapter sets right beside it: Randi buying loafers "a multiple of Vee's — the price of the shoes Vee was white-knuckling, and then several times that again — and Randi never once looked at it." Wearing them out. "Like shoes were a thing you simply traded mid-afternoon." Vee carrying her box "against her chest with both arms… like it might get up and leave her." The class gap made physical, and Randi's "you can put it in the trunk… it's not going to *spoil*" landing "right under the rib." The last laugh, "whole and unrehearsed" — and then the closing line, the one that's meant to be sweet and reads to me like an epitaph: "she had never once in her life had a friend like this."

**Trust vs. suspicion:** I have no new suspicion of Randi because I already have total suspicion of Randi, and this chapter only fed it — but here's the thing I want to be honest about: the chapter is *good enough* that Randi's warmth reads as possibly real even to me. The "real laugh, the one that costs her something." The delight at "seven." I can't cleanly separate the tool from the person, and I don't think Randi always can either. That ambiguity is the chapter's best trick. My protective dread for Vee is at its highest.

**Where the author's thumb showed:** honestly, barely. This is the most controlled chapter in a while. The one place I felt the machinery a touch too visibly was "which was its own kind of going under" and "the last careful thing in her give way" — the prose narrating the surrender a hair more than it needs to; I'd already felt her go under from the number-on-the-sole beat. But it's minor, and the card sequence earns its interiority. The "to/for" beat gets restated ("He didn't make this *for* you… made it *to* you," then Randi again "a dress a man made *to* you," then Vee's "made *to* her by a man") — three times is one past where I needed it, though the escalation of who's saying it (Randi → Randi → Vee's own head) partly justifies the repeat.

**The title:** I wasn't given a chapter title in the packet — only the chapter text. So I'm reading it titleless. If I had to feel for what it's *about*, it's the shoe, the fit, the "seven — that's me exactly," the sharing that's "practically a marriage." The book title *A Polite Invitation* and series *With a Long Spoon* — the long spoon (the thing you sup with the devil with, kept absent from every scene) hums under this whole chapter: Vee has no long spoon, she puts her whole bare hand in. And the tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this is the chapter that *is* the tagline. Every single yes here was hers: put the dress on, laugh, want the shoe, pay with the credit card. Nobody made her. That's the trap, fully sprung and fully consensual.

**What I want/dread next:** I want the dress to finally meet its *event* — the walk-in-late, the room stopping — and I dread it, because I now suspect the room it stops is the one Randi and Pace built. Guess from here: the "we should all get dinner," the sorority event, is where the dress lands, and Randi and Pace are both in that room. I dread Cassie finding out about the credit card. And I'm braced for the moment Vee learns the two hands are one — this chapter made me feel how far that fall is now.
