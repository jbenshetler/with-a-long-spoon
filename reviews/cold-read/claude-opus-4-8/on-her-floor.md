## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This is the chapter I've been braced for since page one, and it detonated exactly where I couldn't fully defend against it. The kiss finally happens. And the book was cruel enough to make me want it as badly as Vee did and then take it away from her in the same breath — hand it back "neat and folded," a joke, "I almost believed it" — while I sat there knowing it was the truest thing Randi has done to her yet. I finished it a little wrecked, which I think is the point, and I'm not mad about it. This is the "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" the jacket promised, delivered.

Let me do the swoon honestly first, because it's real and I won't shortchange it. The held inch before the kiss undid me — "the held inch became its own long event," Vee's eyes going over Randi's face "as they had wanted to all autumn and never let themselves," and then the smell arriving "the perfume beneath it that Vee had been quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping the file." That line is gorgeous and exactly the interior I read for — the want she wouldn't look at squarely, named at the instant she stops being able to not look. And the kiss itself is the erotic peak of the entire book for me so far, more than any of the Pace scenes, precisely because it's been dammed up for fifty chapters of French goodbye-kisses explained away. "It started like the goodbye... and then it didn't stop where goodbye stopped. It went on." I felt that go through me. The whole passage — the fisted hair, the hand "owning the curve of her through the skirt," her body making "the same humiliating announcement it always made... and making it for Randi" — it's heat with the interior life fully loaded into it. I was swept up.

And then the trapdoor, which is where the chapter earns its unease honestly rather than manufacturing it. Randi builds the whole thing on a pretext — "There's Harrison. Let's put on a show" — obtains a real, mutual, months-deep kiss under cover of a bit, and then reframes it back into performance: "You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it." I know from everything before this that Randi is helplessly in love with Vee and has *resolved to keep the word hidden*. So this isn't Randi being cold; it's Randi slamming a door on her own feeling to protect her cover, and using Vee's willingness as the material. The genius and the cruelty of it is that Vee gets to have the kiss AND gets told it meant nothing, and has to agree, and walks home ringing with nowhere to put any of it. "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter *is* that sentence.

What I can't resolve — and I'm going to leave it ajar, because the page does — is how much of that reframe was strategy and how much was panic. Randi "came back from it a moment behind," "drew a breath, and shook her head, blinking," *then* the grin came up. She was more affected than her script allowed. So the "I almost believed it" might be cover, or might be a woman who felt too much and had to bury it fast, even from herself. The checkpoint told me she wanted to let Vee "arrive on her own" — but here she didn't wait; she engineered the arrival and then denied it happened. That's a real complication of her own resolve, and I don't think the page wants me to decide which it was. I'll hold it open.

My unease is sharpest, actually, around the managed drink. The second margarita "she hadn't seen Randi order it." The Chambord punch pressed into her hand. Vee's own plan — "she'd decided on it days ago, the way you decide what kind of girl you'll be at a party you're nervous about," champagne — quietly overridden. The apparatus is greasing her, and the text lets me see it without underlining it. That said, I want to be fair: Vee's *want* long predates the wine. This isn't desire manufactured by liquor; it's inhibition lowered on a want that's been building since October. So the yes is genuine. The frame around it is the rot.

The bracelet is the other thing that went off in my chest that Vee can't feel go off. "The bracelet is called Love... you don't take it off... Taking it off requires tools." A commitment band, permanent, that never comes off, that she keeps "out of the light." I know Randi is Pace's secret established partner. I don't *know* the bracelet is from him — the page doesn't say — but it sits there as a mark of a commitment Vee is standing right next to, admiring, uncomprehending, "tucking it away with the thousand glamorous things Randi knew that she didn't." As a reader ahead of her, that's a quiet knife.

On the people: Randi is landing as the most compelling and most dangerous person in the book, and this chapter deepened her rather than flattening her — the affection is real, the operation is real, and the two are the same gesture. Vee I ache for completely; she's warm and funny ("It's not a skirt. It's a rumor.") and the exposure-shame-into-heat turn on the dance floor — "found out twice over: once to the room, and once, worse, to herself" — is the book's engine running at full heat. Cassie, my safe harbor, gives the half-inch eyebrow and "whatever it was for, she didn't say it," and my unease tracks hers exactly. Brooke unsettled me in a small institutional way — "the smile widened by some exact amount, the whole face switching on behind it," "any friend of Randi's" — the sorority-as-apparatus welcoming Vee into the world she watched from outside, which the Chambord-in-a-tub ethos ("spend a fortune to have you think she didn't spend a dime") quietly rhymes with the whole operation being run on Vee.

Very little friction. The "put on a show for Harrison" device is a hair convenient, but psychologically it's exactly the pretext Randi would need, and Harrison being pure furniture ("that was the whole use of him") is honest about it. I bought it. The prose runs lush and occasionally piles on, but it earns the intensity here more than anywhere I've read.

The titles. "On Her Floor" — the dance floor first, but it recolored the second I finished: it's *Randi's* floor, Vee on Randi's territory and terms, and it rhymes with the chapter's own repeated image of a floor giving way — the opening "a yes with no floor under it," the closing body with "no floor," "there was no floor" mid-kiss. It also picks up the earlier "a floor below which Randi's closet did not go." A quiet, good title that doesn't give anything away going in and tightens on the way out. *A Polite Invitation* is literal here — "Wanna go to a party with me?" — the whole seduction arriving as an invitation Vee can't see the shape of. *With a Long Spoon* keeps its promise of supping with something you'd better keep your distance from, and the tagline **"Every yes was freely given. That was the problem."** has never been more exactly the chapter I just read.

What I want next: I need to know what Randi does with the fact that she felt it too much, and I dread — pleasurably — the collision of this with Pace, since Vee belongs to Pace and Randi belongs (bracelet and all) to a secret Vee doesn't hold. *Guess from here:* Vee will try to explain this kiss away as "the show," the way she's explained away every goodbye kiss, and won't manage it this time — the thong she has to peel off, "no one at all to hand it to," is the body refusing the story. I'm pulled hard to keep reading.
