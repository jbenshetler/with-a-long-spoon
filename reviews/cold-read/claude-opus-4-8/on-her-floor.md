# Cold read (grounded) — On Her Floor

## Reader reaction

Well. There it is. I've been braced for this since the goodbye kisses started — the little second press, the "See you soon, gorgeous" Vee kept filing under *French* — and the book finally pulled the pin. And I have to be honest before I get careful: it landed on me, hard. That kiss ran hot and it swept me up and I didn't want it to stop any more than Vee did. This chapter got its heat all the way onto the page.

**Vee.** My tenderness for her is total and it just got more painful. What kills me about this chapter is the whole first stretch — the borrowed skirt, "I don't have anything that's right," the room with no mirror far enough back to see herself whole, so she "got herself in pieces." That image is the chapter for me: a girl who can only ever see parts of herself, being assembled by someone else's eye. She's happy. She's *included*. And she's dressing for a want she's decided isn't hers to answer for because the skirt is Randi's — "in Randi's skirt the wanting wasn't hers to answer for." God, that's the whole self-deception laid out plain, and she says it to herself as comfort. When the kiss comes and she *stops performing* — "she had not been performing — somewhere in there she had stopped entirely" — the cover story collapses under her and she has to stand in the noise holding a want with "nowhere to put any of it." The last paragraph wrecked me: she knows almost nothing except that she wants to do it again so badly her hands aren't steady, "and that there was no version of the night in which she could ask." That's the ache I read for.

**Randi.** This is where the swoon and the dread run on the same wire, exactly as the book keeps doing to me. Because I *believe* she wanted it — the held inch before the kiss, the breath, the way she comes back "a moment behind," shakes her head, blinking, before the grin snaps on. That's a woman who lost the thread of her own performance too, or close to it. And then she reaches for the cover instantly: "You really did a great job putting on a show. I almost believed it." Harrison as the alibi. That maneuver is *chilling* precisely because I can't fully tell how much of it is protecting Vee, how much is protecting herself, how much is the game still running. She manufactured the whole apparatus — the plus-one, the too-short skirt she *knew* would climb, "Cold's good. Cold gets us inside faster," the hands moving Vee's hips wider so "the skirt got braver in exact measure," lifting her arms so Vee's thighs opened to the room with "nothing Vee could do about it, because Randi had her hands." She engineered the exposure and then the kiss and then handed it all back "neat and folded." I don't trust the folding. But I also can't call it cold, because her own body was in it. That's the merciless thing the jacket promised and it's working on me.

**The bracelet.** This is the line that put a stone in my stomach mid-swoon. "The bracelet is called Love… you don't take it off. It's a real commitment. Taking it off requires tools." Vee tucks it away "with the thousand glamorous things Randi knew that she didn't." I know from before that Randi never removes it and keeps it out of the light. Now it has a name, and the name is *Love*, and it's a locked commitment that takes tools to remove — and Vee is standing there being kissed breathless by the woman wearing it, with no idea what, or who, it means. I don't have the referent. Neither does Vee. But the page absolutely earned my unease there: that's a wedding-grade object with a story attached, worn by a woman running a seduction on someone who can't read it. It's the reader-ahead-of-her dread the whole book trades in.

**Cassie.** My safe harbor did her job and it chilled me the right amount. "That hem's not going to stay put… You're going to be tugging that all night." She's *right*, precisely, mechanically right — the skirt climbs exactly as she said and hands Vee to the room. And "No Pace tonight?" with the eyebrow that goes up a half-inch and stays. She sees the shape of the night before Vee does. When Cassie's wary, I'm wary, and she was wary.

**Brooke.** She unsettled me in a small clean way. The total recall — the cardigan, the smoothie shop, the Friday Vee came in wrecked — delivered as warmth, and then the withholding of the wrecked-Friday detail read to Vee as kindness. But "the smile widened by some exact amount, the whole face switching on behind it" — *switching on* — I clocked that. The page earned a flicker of "something's managed here," not alarm, just a note.

**Swoon / heat.** The kiss is the hottest thing in the run since the doorway reconciliation, maybe hotter because it's forbidden and Vee's whole apparatus of shame is live under it. The build is expert — the held inch, the smell "she'd been quietly cataloguing for months without admitting she was keeping the file," the taste "salt and lime and under it Randi, particular, the specific *her* of her." That detail — cataloguing the file without admitting it — is the interior life under the wanting I came for. It peaks clean and doesn't go clinical for a second.

**Friction.** Very little. If I'm honest, the Harrison-as-cover beat is almost *too* tidy a device — a boy conveniently across the room to aim the "show" at — but it works because it's exactly the kind of alibi Randi would reach for, so I forgive it. The panties-showing exposure thread got worked maybe one beat past where I needed it ("How much of her center had they seen?") but it earns its place because the shame-flipping-to-heat is the actual engine.

**Titles.** "On Her Floor" — I read it first as Vee on the dance floor, Randi's floor, the sorority's turf. After the chapter it recolors: *whose* floor is she on, exactly — she's on Randi's ground the whole night, in Randi's clothes, running on Randi's grip. And there's the older sense the book has trained me for — someone brought down onto a floor. It sits nicely oblique; it didn't give anything away going in. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon proverb ("sup with the devil, use a long spoon") keeps getting more pointed: everyone here is supping with someone dangerous and nobody's spoon is long enough. The tagline on the jacket — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — landed like a bell this chapter, because Vee's yes to the party, to the skirt, to the floor, to the kiss she "gave back before the question reached her" were all freely, gladly given, and that is exactly what makes them a trap.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to know what Vee does with this — she can't unknow it, and Tuesday there's brunch with Randi, and I dread and crave that table. *Guess from here:* Randi will keep the "it was a show" frame nailed down and Vee will let her, and the wanting will go underground and get worse. And somewhere the bracelet named Love is going to matter enormously, and Vee is going to be the last to understand it.
