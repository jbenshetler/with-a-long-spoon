## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is a quiet chapter — one girl alone in a cold bedroom on New Year's Eve, a phone in the dark — and it worked on me almost entirely as ache. No sex, barely a person in the room, and I stayed with it the whole way because the interiority is so exact and so sad.

The room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her" is the spine of the chapter and it landed hard. The synchronized-swimming trophies, the "little gold girls frozen mid-lift with their toes pointed," the clarinet in the closet, the friends who "hadn't texted in a year" — a whole former self, dusted and preserved and dead. And the line I keep hearing: "She'd been breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." That's the truest thing about coming home after being cracked open. I felt it in my chest.

What moved for me this chapter: Vee is *unwell* here, in the ordinary way a person can be unwell alone at midnight. The spite-invention of the blonde is the cruelest, best beat — she "found a who," built her rival "from spite: blonde, tan, a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's," handed Pace the exact opposite of herself. And then the horror move: she reaches for her own best memory — the fra diavolo, the falling sheet, the wine light — to warm herself, and *it turns while she's in it.* "The one at the table in the falling sheet stopped being her. The hair went gold and the leg went tan." She can't even keep her own memory. That's a genuinely frightening image of how insecurity colonizes the good things. I didn't see it coming and it hurt.

My feeling about Pace here is filtered entirely through Vee's not-knowing, and the not-knowing is agony. "He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That reframe of his whole gentleness — the leaving-space that the jacket sells as tenderness — into abandonment is sharp. And the Sheri detail resurfacing as poison: "*She's alone,* he'd said... in November, when she was the one he cooked for. But about Christmas he'd said nothing at all." She's doing the thing where every kind fact becomes evidence against her. I don't distrust Pace more from this — I distrust *her state*, which is right.

Randi. Oh, this is where I got scared in the good way, because I know what Vee doesn't. Vee lies there and thinks "Randi, the only girl she could talk to about real things. The real things they talked about were mostly Pace." And she reaches for Randi as the one sure thing the new year holds: "a face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it." I know "gorgeous" means what it means now. I know Randi is off in a chalet bought with fraud money Vee saw and buried. So Vee's warmth toward Randi — "an open hand offered," the carefully-nothing-demanding text — reads to me as a girl walking toward the person who is managing her, calling it her one refuge. The dramatic irony is thick and it aches. And Randi's text back — "you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here" — I now read every word of that as the true-words-that-hide-the-truth engine, and it's chilling how *good* it makes Vee feel.

The detail that Vee "was in the glass room when the name came up and Randi blanched; she knew whose money it was" and "envied the mountains anyway, because... even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had" — that's the whole tragedy of Vee's class position and her hunger, and it's honest that knowing doesn't cool the envy.

Trust/suspicion: nothing new felt "off" that the book hasn't already earned. My suspicion is all structural — I'm holding what Vee can't see. The one thing I'll flag as a reader: I'm now *certain* the collision is coming and Randi will be the one to catch Vee when she breaks, and that's exactly the worst possible comfort.

Erotic charge: low by design — a 1. The fra diavolo memory carries the old heat ("the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark"), but it's a memory being poisoned in real time, so the charge is grief, not arousal.

Friction: very little. This is a beautifully controlled chapter. If I push, the machinery of "true words / the thing she folded away and never said" gets named a few times close together — "Not the thing — the thing she'd folded away in the glass room and never once let on she'd seen" — and I felt the author making sure I connected it to the fraud. I already had. Small thumb. Also the phone choreography ("set it down and picked it up and set it down") is right but I felt it land twice.

Title — "Old Acquaintances": the New Year's "Auld Lang Syne" pull, obviously — should old acquaintance be forgot. And it's exactly what the chapter is *about*: the old acquaintances are the dead friends on the corkboard, the outgrown room, the former Vivienne — the ones being forgotten, or who forgot her. Oblique and good; it doesn't give anything away, it recolors on the read. The book title *With a Long Spoon* still sits as a warning I can't fully cash — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." Reaching toward something dangerous with false safety. Tonight Vee reached toward Randi with an open hand, no spoon at all. The tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — pulses under every warm text she sends into the dark.

What I want/dread next: I want the term to start and I dread it, because "she'd have Randi back the day the term started." Guess from here: the reunion with Randi will be tender and devastating, and Vee will pour the Pace-wound straight into the hands of the person co-authoring it.
