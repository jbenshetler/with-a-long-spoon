## Reader reaction

The felt read: this is the other half of the last chapter, and reading them back to back is a quiet devastation. Same New Year's Eve, same text exchange — I already watched Randi compose that "fairytale" from inside her starving dissociation, and now I watch Vee receive it and believe every glittering word. Vee lies under her childhood shelf of little gold swimmers, envying a "poisoned Gstaad," imagining Randi "off somewhere in all that snow with a man she'd just met, a devastatingly handsome billionaire," having a weekend so wild Vee "wouldn't recognize half of what they did." And I know it left Randi cold, that she dissociated through it, that "John" reached her not at all. The gap between what Vee envies and what actually happened is the whole ache of the pairing. Two women in two cold beds, each reaching for the other, each getting the other exactly wrong.

What got me most: Vee cannot keep her own best night. She reaches for the fra diavolo evening — the sheet slipping, "the evening having used her well" — to get warm, "and it turned while she was in it. The hair went gold and the leg went tan and the blonde sat where Vee had sat." She builds a rival from spite, hands Pace "the exact opposite of herself," and can't evict her from her own memory. That's a specific, brutal kind of insecurity, and it landed on me bodily. The reader knows there's no blonde on the page — this is a fear conjured at midnight, "what you believe at midnight. Cold. Alone."

And the Pace question is left exactly where she left it: "not a word across the whole break." She replays the fight — "her mother's question in her own mouth by then, *what am I to you*" — that's a gorgeous, awful line, that the interrogation she's fled at home is the one she brought to his door. "Maybe he thought nothing had happened at all." So this NYE sits *after* she walked out, and nothing has healed it. Good — one of my open questions answered on the page.

The supports failing broke my heart in a controlled way — the bartender, "*he doesn't take advantage*," Randi's "*he leaves you the room.*" I've been carrying those as the caring-side truths. Vee reaches for them and "they went straight through her hands… The floor was gone," because they only held her while she knew she was the one. That's the cruelty: the true reassurances stop working the instant she loses her footing.

Trust/suspicion: no new suspicion — this is pure interior. What it does is deepen my sympathy for Vee to nearly unbearable. She is the one who "love[s] out loud" at home yet can't answer "how's your young man," who scissored her own induction photo off at the waist so her parents "did not need to know what the dress Pace had built for her actually did." She divided herself with her own hands. And she still protects Randi's shame — "the thing she'd folded away in the glass room and never once let on she'd seen." Both women guarding each other's secret in the dark, neither knowing.

Heat is near zero here and rightly — the fra diavolo memory is the only charge, and it's warmth reached-for, not present; it curdles.

Titles: "Old Acquaintances" is Auld Lang Syne — should old acquaintance be forgot — and the room is full of them: the ribbons, the frozen gold swimmers, the clarinet, "the girls she'd once have said were her whole life and who hadn't texted in a year." The room "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her." The NYE song about remembering old friends, sung by a girl whose old life no longer fits and whose new loves are two people she can't reach. *A Polite Invitation / With a Long Spoon* — the long spoon (you sup with the devil with a long spoon) keeps meaning distance from something dangerous you're eating with anyway; Vee has no spoon at all, she's all the way in.

What I want next: to know whether this crisis with Pace breaks or pulls back, and I dread the collision more with every chapter both women spend starving in parallel.
