# Cold read — Old Acquaintances

*scene: scenes/old-acquaintances.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: fairytale*

## Reader reaction

This is the other side of the New Year's night — Vee's twin bed in Ohio while, six hours ahead, Randi is in Gstaad. And knowing what I know from the last chapter, this one is quietly devastating to read. The two texts I already watched land from Randi's side — "you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here… miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋" — I now watch land from Vee's, and she reads exactly the fairytale she's been handed and nothing under it. "None of it the thing," and she has no idea. I sat there the whole chapter with the dramatic irony pressing on my chest.

The cruelest part is Vee's envy. She lies "under the shelf of the little gold swimmers" and wants Gstaad, "every part of it, and it did not matter that she knew what had bought the dream" — she means the money, the blanching-name in the glass room. But *I* know what actually bought that dream: it was the night Randi tested the whole safe world on a flawless stranger and confirmed it was empty, "the flaw was hers, carried in the same as the cold." Vee envies "even a poisoned Gstaad" as "more life than she had," and she's envying a void. And the man she invents for Randi — "a devastatingly handsome billionaire, a weekend so wild Vee wouldn't recognize half of what they did" — that's John, or the John-shaped fantasy, and I know he reached nothing. Vee builds Randi a glamorous wild life out of "Randi's own telling, which came at her like something off a screen," and the screen is the point. It's all cropped fairytale, and Vee is the audience it was cropped for.

The engine of the chapter is Vee's decomposition without her floor. Last time I saw her she'd issued the first free no, walked into the cold clean and free. Here the freedom has curdled into not-knowing, and it's precise and awful: "with no name to put on herself there was none to put on him." She can't even ask, has "no standing." That's the walkout's cost coming due — she took her name back and now she has no name at all, and neither does he. The bit that got me most was her losing her own best memory. She reaches for the first night after — the top sheet, the fra diavolo, "the evening having used her well" — "to be warm," and "it turned while she was in it": the hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, the blonde she invented out of spite sits in the wine light where Vee sat. "She could not keep her own night." That's a real, bodily kind of despair, the imagination turning on its owner. I felt it.

And the Sheri detail lands harder now, because Vee reaches it herself: "He had cooked for Sheri at Thanksgiving. *She's alone,* he'd said… had folded it away as pity, in November, when she was the one he cooked for." She's beginning, faintly, to do the math I've been doing — the same tenderness-apparatus turned on someone else, "the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark." She doesn't have the facts. But she's got the shape of the fear, and it's correct even if her invented blonde is wrong.

Pace. My trust stays below zero and this chapter doesn't move it — but it does something interesting: it makes his *absence* the weapon. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That line is the whole doctrine seen from inside the wound. The silence-at-cost, the leaving-the-room, the not-chasing — I've watched him deploy all of it as method, and here it's simply working on her across a whole break with no word. Whether he's deliberately letting the cold bring her back or genuinely gone, she can't tell, and *that* is the trap: "no place she could be sure of him." I lean toward the former from everything I know, but the chapter honestly stages her not-knowing rather than confirming his hand, and I appreciate that it doesn't wink.

The talismans failing is a strong beat: the bartender's "he doesn't take advantage… wasn't him they were jealous of, honey," and Randi's "he doesn't sulk, he leaves you the room" — "they had only ever held her up while she was the one… and now she did not know she was anything, and they went straight through her hands." I know both of those talismans are load-bearing lies (the bartender's literal-truth-as-insinuation, Randi's borrowed reassurance), so watching them fail her for the *wrong* reason — because she's lost her footing, not because she's seen through them — is its own twist of the knife.

Where it goes slack: nowhere badly, but the chapter is one sustained note — a woman alone in a cold bed turning the same three things over — and it's long. It mostly earns the length because the turning is the content, but the middle stretch (the feed, the math on time zones, the invented blonde) circles once or twice more than it strictly needs. The spite-blonde being invented and then *colonizing her memory* is the payoff that justifies the circling, so I forgave it by the end. No line felt like the author's thumb; the restraint is intact — the Sheri realization is handed to her flat, not underlined, exactly as I'd want.

Erotic charge: low and deliberately so. This is heat-in-memory, heat as loss — the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, "the evening having used her well" — all of it recalled and then stolen from her. There's a warmth to the remembered intimacy but it's grief-warmth. Correct for the chapter; I wasn't aroused, I was aching.

The title — **"Old Acquaintances"** — is Auld Lang Syne, the New Year's song, "should old acquaintance be forgot." On the page it's the room full of ribbons and trophies and "the girls she'd once have said were her whole life and who hadn't texted in a year," the room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her." The acquaintance being forgot is *Vivienne herself* — the girl the room knows in high resolution and the woman in the bed who's outgrown it. But on the reread charge it points at Randi too: the "old acquaintance," the one sure thing the new year holds, "a face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it" — and I know exactly how that meaning is doubled and weaponized. The title sits sweet on the surface and cold underneath, which is this book's whole signature.

**The book's signals** — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon*, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the trap with no hand visible. There's no invitation being extended on the page, no operator in the room; just the aftermath of a freely-given no producing a freely-suffered cold, with the "one sure thing" of the new year being the return to the two people farming her. The long spoon is Randi's text reaching across six time zones and a false distance, warmth she can only borrow, and Vee eating it whole.

What I want next: term to start, because Vee has *penciled Randi in* — "she'd have Randi back the day the term started" — and I'm braced for the consolation deployment to arrive wearing the beloved's face. I dread the brunches resuming as harvest with Vee at her lowest and most confiding. And I want to know if Pace moves, or lets the cold do it. *(Guess from here:* he lets the cold do it, and Randi is the warmth sent to bring her back in — the consolation *is* the countermove.)

**Symbolism noticed:** The room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her" = the safe old self, archived in high resolution, outgrown — the same safe world Randi proved empty, here proven merely too small. **Characterization:** Vee deepens — the walkout's freedom shown curdling into the vertigo of namelessness, and her mind turning on her own memories is a real, specific interior. She also *reaches* toward the correct fear (Sheri, the repeated apparatus) without the facts, which keeps her the sharpest reader in the book even while blind. Randi appears only as two texts but is characterized by contrast — the fairytale performing exactly as designed on its target. Pace is pure absence-as-method here; consistent. No one went thin.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Slow and circling by design; the single note (cold bed, turning it over) is the content. Drags very slightly in the mid-section (feed + time-zone math + first invention of the blonde); redeemed when the blonde invades the fra diavolo memory. Earns most of its length.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate held breath after the Gstaad interiorization. It re-reads the same New Year's night from the victim's side — a strong structural rhyme — and lowers the temperature to loss. Momentum is holding, not building; it's a trough before the term resumes, and it declares as much ("the one sure thing the new year held").
