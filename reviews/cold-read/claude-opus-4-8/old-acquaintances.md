# Cold read — Old Acquaintances

*scene: scenes/old-acquaintances.md · read after: fairytale*

## Reader reaction

This is the answer to the dread I've been carrying, and it lands like a slow weight on the chest. Chapter forty-four gave me Randi's fairytale text from the sending end — every word true, none of it the thing, a lie made of love and cowardice. This chapter puts me in the twin bed where it *arrives*, and makes me watch Vee open it and believe it. When the ceiling lit near midnight — *"you ANGEL 😇 it's honestly a fairytale out here… miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋"* — I felt sick, because I know exactly what that message is and what it's costing to send, and Vee reads it as the one warm thing in a cold night. *"And Randi had taken the time out of whatever amazing thing she was doing to write back to Vee."* That's the trap working in real time. She's grateful for the crumb from the person who's decided never to feed her the real thing.

The thing that opened my eyes wider, though, is that Vee is now running the *same doctrine* on her side without knowing it. She sends Randi warmth "and made sure it demanded nothing — an open hand offered. Not the thing — she never said the thing." She learned to fold the true thing away. She scissored her own induction photo off at the waist for her mother's phone — "she was the one who had divided it." She's become fluent in the technically-true, load-bearing-false. Two women lying in two cold rooms, each carrying the sealed self, each sending the other everything-but-the-thing. The book has now shown me both ends of the same wire and they're identical.

My heart is entirely with Vee. The room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her" is a beautiful, sad conceit — the gold swimmers frozen mid-lift, the clarinet, the girls who haven't texted in a year, the whole shrine to a Vivienne who's been breathing bigger air and comes home too big for the ceiling. And her parents' question — *how's your young man, when do we get to meet him, is it serious* — landing on a door she can't open because she doesn't know the answer herself. *"She had no answer that would fit in this house. She was no longer sure she had one that fit anywhere."* That's the whole crisis in two sentences.

The most brutal move in the chapter is when she reaches for her own best memory to get warm — the first night, the top sheet at the table, the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping and him seeing and her not fixing it — and it *turns on her* while she's inside it. "The hair went gold and the leg went tan and the blonde sat where Vee had sat." She can't even keep her own memory; the invented rival takes the sheet off her. That's a real, specific way jealousy works, and I felt it in the gut. And the reader knows something she doesn't: there *is* another woman, and it isn't the dance-hall blonde she's built from spite — it's Randi. The gap between the phantom rival Vee invents and the real one who's texting her "gorgeous" is the cruelest thing on the page.

Pace comes through this chapter only in her memory and her fear, and the balance the last few chapters struck holds: I can see why she can't let go — the stove, the garlic in oil, the building-then-giving, "the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark" — and I can see the exact hole he's left her in, because he would not spend the word. The Thanksgiving-Sheri fact resurfaces as a splinter: *"She's alone, he'd said,"* and Vee folded it away as pity in November when she was the one he cooked for — but now she isn't sure she's the one he cooks for at all, and the whole week of Christmas is a blank she can't see into. The supports that used to hold her — the bartender's *"wasn't him they were jealous of, honey,"* Randi's *"he leaves you the room"* — "went straight through her hands," because they only held while she knew she was the one. That's exactly right and it's awful.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No new suspicion of anyone — this chapter isn't about a hidden move, it's about the ache. But my dread about the collision sharpened. Vee is now leaning her whole weight on Randi as "the one sure thing the new year held," "a face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it" — and I know that face is lying to her by omission on two fronts (Pace, and the game). She's building her new year on the one person most dangerous to her. Watching her circle the day term starts as her anchor made me want to look away.

**Erotic charge:** Low by design — this is a cold-room chapter, no heat in the present. But the *memory* of the first night carries a banked warmth, and the way it gets hijacked mid-recall is the erotic material turned into a weapon against her. Heat 0, but the food-as-hunger thread is doing real emotional work.

**Friction:** Very little. The chapter is patient and it earns its patience. If anything the mirroring is almost *too* legible to me because I just read Randi's side — the "fairytale" word arriving verbatim, the cold-each-carries doubling — but I'm the reader who's been tracking it, so it reads as devastating rather than heavy-handed. The one place I felt the author's hand was "what you believe at midnight, cold and alone on the one night you're not supposed to be" — a small tell-me-how-to-feel, but mild, and true enough that I let it pass.

**Titles:** "Old Acquaintances" — the auld-lang-syne pun sits right on New Year's, "should old acquaintance be forgot." But it recolors: the room full of old acquaintances that no longer know her (the swimmers, the ribbons, the girls who stopped texting), and the two *new* acquaintances — Pace and Randi — who've displaced them and whom she can't be sure of either. She's stranded between old ones who loved a girl who's gone and new ones who won't tell her what she is. It doesn't give anything away; it deepens on the read. And *With a Long Spoon* / "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — this chapter is the trap felt from the inside without her knowing there's a trap. Every warm text she sends, every crumb she's grateful for, every day she circles for Randi: freely given, freely received, and the cage closing. The long spoon: she's being fed just enough to keep her at the table, never enough to be full.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the term to start and I dread it in equal measure, because Vee walking back into Randi's light is walking deeper in. *Guess from here:* the new term reopens the whole apparatus — brunches, Randi, and Pace un-resolved — and I expect the Pace question to get forced one way or the other early, because Vee can't hold this suspension much longer. I dread the moment Vee learns Randi is Pace's lover; I keep bracing for the photo in Randi's hand to surface. And I want, badly, for Cassie to be there when it breaks.
