## Reader reaction

# Reader reaction — "Old Acquaintances"

This one gutted me quietly. No scene, no second person in the room — just Vee on her back in a twin bed her legs outgrew in middle school, the year running out forty cold minutes at a time, and somehow it's one of the most aching chapters I've read. The whole thing is an interior, and it holds. "The room did not know her. It had known Vivienne" — that line did it immediately. The split between the two names the book has been working all along lands here as pure loss: the trophies of the little gold swimmers, the ribbons, the clarinet in the closet, a whole corkboard of girls who haven't texted in a year, all of it "dusted" and loving a girl who no longer lives inside her. I felt the ceiling come down close over her. The homecoming-that-no-longer-fits is a familiar note, but the execution — "breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her" — earned every inch of it.

My feeling about Vee hasn't *moved* so much as deepened and hurt more. I'm still wholly with her. What's new and devastating is watching her lose her own best memory in real time: she reaches for the fra diavolo night, the top sheet slipping, the evening "having used her well," to put herself back inside warmth — "She went into it to be warm" — and then the hair goes gold and the leg goes tan and a built-for-nothing blonde takes her seat in the wine light and she *cannot keep her own night*. That's the cruelest image in the chapter. The reassurances she tries to clutch — the bartender's *wasn't him they were jealous of, honey*, Randi's *he leaves you the room* — "went straight through her hands" because they only ever held her up while she knew she was the one. "The floor was gone." I believed the floor was gone.

And here's where my dramatic-irony ache sharpens into something close to pain. She writes to Randi "warm, and made sure it demanded nothing — an open hand offered," guarding the PPP secret even in a text, *thinking about you 💛*, and she envies the poisoned Gstaad fairytale knowing exactly whose money bought it. She reads Randi's *miss your face most though, gorgeous 💋* as proof that glamorous wild Randi carved time out of a wild billionaire weekend for her — "the year's gift she had literally stumbled into." I know it wasn't a stumble. I know the face that will light up on the first day of term is the co-architect of this whole thing. Vee circling the start of term as "the one sure thing the new year held" — a class, a face that will call her gorgeous and mean it — while having "nothing at all" to circle for Pace, is horribly poignant precisely because the sure thing is the trap and the uncertain thing (Pace) is the one who's actually, as I know from the other side, breaking down over a passing truck. Both of them love her; both of them are lying; and she's lying awake rationing her hope between them.

Pace: my structural distrust is intact but this chapter made me ache for *her* read of him more than suspect him. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." That reversal is the whole relationship in one line. And her logic about Sheri — *she's alone*, folded away as pity in November "when she was the one he cooked for," and now Christmas is "a whole week she couldn't see into" — is sound paranoia built on an unsound premise, and I can't comfort her because the *shape* of her fear (there's someone else, there's a brunette) is true even if the blonde she invents from spite isn't. The spite-blonde is beautifully done: "blonde, tan, a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's… what you believe at midnight."

Trust/suspicion: no new suspicion of the text — my unease still sits on the architecture, not on any beat here. This chapter is clean, honest interiority; the only "thumb on the scale" I even half-felt was the faint over-tidiness of the Vivienne/room conceit getting restated a couple times ("the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone" as the closer is lovely but close to one restatement too many). Minor. It mostly sang.

Erotic charge: low by design and correctly so — this is a chapter about the *absence* of touch. The one genuine flare is the fra diavolo memory, the slipping sheet, "his hands worked on her in the dark," the garlic blooming as the tell for how he builds and gives — and then it's poisoned by the blonde takeover. The final image, "his arms, the strong warm weight of them drawing her back into him," arriving and refusing to hold because she can't stop seeing whose body they're around — that's heat curdled into dread in a single breath. "The wanting and the fear in the one image, and no way on earth to know which was the true one." That's the chapter's thesis and it's exactly where I live as a reader who knows.

**The titles.** "Old Acquaintances" — Auld Lang Syne, obviously, *should auld acquaintance be forgot*, New Year's Eve made literal. But it cut deeper than the holiday: the old acquaintances are the ribbon-girls who stopped texting, the Vivienne the room still loves, the self she's outgrowing — and, in the ironic register I carry, Randi and Pace are the newest "acquaintances" who feel oldest and truest to her and are the least what she thinks. The title points at forgetting and being forgotten, and at who gets kept. It didn't give anything away; it recolored warmly on arrival. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**, title *Old Acquaintances* — "long spoon" still rings the proverb (sup with the devil, use a long spoon) over all this real warmth, and "A Warm Reception" sits bitterly against a girl in a furnace-savings-mode house who can't get warm. The promise remains: warmth you shouldn't trust, offered by people you shouldn't sup with — and I'm all the way in.

What I want next: honestly, I dread the term starting as much as I crave it, because Randi walking back in as "the one sure thing" is Vee reaching for the exact hand that's set to hurt her worst. *Guess from here:* the reconciliation with Pace and the Randi reunion are going to collide, and this chapter just loaded the gun by making Vee's whole surviving hope rest on Randi.
