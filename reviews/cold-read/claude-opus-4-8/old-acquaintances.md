## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is a whole chapter of one girl not sleeping, and it worked on me more than I expected a chapter of nothing-happening to. It's Vee alone in her childhood bedroom in Ohio, New Year's Eve, the furnace clicking into "savings mode," the lavender plugins, the swim trophies — "the little gold girls frozen mid-lift with their toes pointed" — and it's saturated with a specific ache I recognized: coming home too big for the room. "She'd been breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." That landed clean. The room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her" is the whole chapter in one line, and it earns its repetition at the end.

**Vee.** My love and fear for her only deepened. What got me most is the thing she does with her *own* memory near the end — she reaches for her best night (the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, "nothing that needed saying") to warm herself, and the memory *turns on her*: "The hair went gold and the leg went tan and the blonde sat where Vee had sat." She can't even keep her own good night — her mind hands it to an invented rival. That's a devastatingly true picture of jealousy eating a person from inside. She builds this rival "from spite: blonde, tan, a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's" — and that word *stripper*, out of her own mouth about her own body, is the old mother-voice shame surfacing exactly when she's undefended. She has no name for what Pace is to her, so "with no name to put on herself there was none to put on him. He could be anyone's." The nameless relationship isn't abstract here — it's the thing that leaves her with no standing to even ask.

**Pace.** He's not on the page and I trusted him *less* for it, which is interesting. The reassurances she reaches for — the bartender's "he doesn't take advantage, I'd have heard; Pace don't bring dates," Randi's "he doesn't sulk, he leaves you the room" — "went straight through her hands" because they only held her up while she knew she was the one. And I, the reader, don't trust them either, because I know what she doesn't: he's connected to Randi. The Sheri detail resurfaced and stung — "*She's alone,* he'd said... folded it away as pity, in November, when she was the one he cooked for. But about Christmas he'd said nothing at all." She's circling the truth without the key. And "he left you the room" — a phrase I've carried as his tenderness — she now *hates*, and I felt that flip.

**Randi.** Oh, this is where the dread lives for me now, because I have the carry-forward that Vee doesn't. Vee reads the Gstaad feed and the "fairytale" text at face value — "She was fine. Better than fine" — and even envies "a poisoned Gstaad," knowing the money's dirty. But I *know* that fairytale text is a beautiful lie built by the loneliest person in the book, sent after a hollow perfect night. So the whole exchange is unbearable in a way Vee can't feel: two women alone in the dark, each performing okayness at the other, each hiding the real thing. Vee "made sure it demanded nothing — an open hand," never says the thing; Randi answers with the *you ANGEL 😇* performance. Two withheld truths passing in the night. And the cruelest irony the chapter doesn't spell out but I couldn't stop seeing: "The real things they talked about were mostly Pace." Vee wishes she could talk to Randi about Pace. She's confiding her Pace-heartbreak to Pace's other lover.

**Erotic charge** — low by design and right for it. There's no live heat; the only sensual material is memory (the sheet, the garlic in the oil, "the strong warm weight of them drawing her back") and it's deliberately spoiled — the arms she conjures at midnight immediately become "whose he had them around instead. The wanting and the fear in the one image." I didn't want it to be hot; the point is that even her arousal-memory has been colonized by fear. Heat as absence. It worked as ache, not as charge.

**Friction.** Very little. This is the most confident quiet chapter so far. If anything I felt the faintest thumb on the scale in how *thoroughly* the "she can't keep her own night" turn gets narrated — it borders on explaining the metaphor it just made — but it stayed on the right side. The doubling of "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone" at the very end vs. the room that "loved a girl who no longer lived inside her" earlier felt earned, not repetitive. And the food-as-love machinery ("the plate set down only once she was aching for it — the same building, then giving, that his hands worked on her in the dark") is a connection the book has made before; here she makes it herself, which is fair — she's learned to read him.

**The title.** "Old Acquaintances" — obviously "Auld Lang Syne," New Year's, "should old acquaintance be forgot." Sweetly innocuous on the surface. But the chapter turns it: the *actual* old acquaintances — the ribbon-board girls "who hadn't texted in a year," the trophies, the clarinet — are already forgotten, already gone. And the new acquaintances (Pace, Randi) are the ones she can't forget and can't hold. The old life dropped her; the new life won't name her. On reread the title also whispers at the two people she's holding who are, unknown to her, *each other's* old acquaintance — the connection she can't see. That's the reread-charge if it lands.

**Book title / tagline.** "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is the trap *felt from inside* without her knowing it's a trap. She freely offered the open hand to Randi, freely reaches for Pace and pulls back — all her yeses, all her wanting, entirely her own, and all of it leaving her alone in a cold bed with no standing to ask anyone for anything. "With a Long Spoon" — sup with the devil, use a long spoon — still reads to me as: get close to something dangerous, keep your distance to survive. Vee has no long spoon. She's all the way in and doesn't even know what she's supping with.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread the term starting, because that's when "the two worlds still haven't physically touched" — and the chapter *ends* pointing straight at it: "She'd have Randi back the day the term started... And Pace came with none of that." Randi is the sure thing, Pace the blank. *Guess from here:* term starts, Randi's warmth pulls Vee back up into the bright life, and somewhere the seam between Randi-world and Pace-world tears. I keep waiting for the moment Vee sees the bracelet, or the house, or the bench, and the floor really goes. This chapter was the held breath before that.
