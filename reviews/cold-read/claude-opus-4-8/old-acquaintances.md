# Cold read (grounded) — Old Acquaintances

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This chapter is a held breath — the whole thing is Vee alone in her childhood bed on New Year's Eve, and it's one of the quietest, most interior stretches I've read in this book, and it wrecked me a little. After the run of chapters where the fight with Pace kept escalating (Cropped, Seconds, Under the Rug, Bare, Believe Me), and then the Randi/John/Gstaad chapter which pulled me out into a whole other cold room, this lands us back inside Vee's skull at the exact moment she has nothing — no Pace, no Randi, no answer to the question she blew her relationship open to ask. And the genius, the cruel genius, of putting this right after "Fairytale" is that I know what she doesn't. I've just watched Randi lie down against a "perfect nothing" and manufacture the whole fairytale text word by word. So when Randi's message lights Vee's ceiling — "it's honestly a fairytale out here… i'm never coming home" — and Vee lies there envying "even a poisoned Gstaad," I feel the trap close on her from both sides at once. Vee thinks she's the poor one, the small one, the one behind the shut door; and I know the woman in the gold windows was colder and more alone in that bed than Vee is in hers. That dramatic irony is the ache of the whole chapter.

How I feel about Vee: closer than ever, and sadder for her. The room that "did not know her" — that loved Vivienne, the synchronized-swimmer with the ribbons and the clarinet, and "had no idea who was in the bed" — is the cleanest image the book has given me yet for what's happened to her. She's outgrown the bed her legs literally outgrew years ago, and there's no going back into that girl. I love that the book won't let her be simply happy about the change: she's freer, awake, "breathing bigger air," and it has cost her a home she can return to. "She was no longer sure she had one that fit anywhere." That's the loneliness of waking up — nobody warned her the price of a bigger appetite is that the old plate stops feeding you and the new plate has a shut door in front of it.

The image that turned my stomach in the good way: she can't keep her own memory. She reaches for her best night — the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, "the evening having used her well" — to put herself back inside it and get warm, "and it turned while she was in it." The hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, the blonde takes her seat in the wine light, and Pace is still at the stove smiling. "She could not keep her own night." That's jealousy rendered as a kind of possession, the mind unable to hold the door against the thing it fears. It's the same "building, then giving" she loves in his cooking and his hands, now weaponized against her by her own imagination. Devastating.

Pace: my feeling toward him is complicated by his absence here, which is exactly right. He's not on the page, so what I get is the shape of him in Vee's dread. "Nothing from him." The break has held across the whole holiday — no word, no coming-after — and she genuinely doesn't know if they're anything anymore, whether he even thinks something happened. And the Sheri thread comes back with teeth: "She's alone," he'd said at Thanksgiving, and Vee "folded it away as pity, in November, when she was the one he cooked for." Now she can't see into his Christmas week and the pity reframes as threat. I notice I don't actually believe the blonde-model fantasy — the text is careful to name it as "what you believe at midnight," spite-built — and yet the Sheri detail is real, and "complicated" (two of them at once) has been sitting in my memory since Ch 28. So the page leaves the door ajar honestly: I don't think Pace is off cheating, but Vee has no standing to know, and neither, quite, do I. That's the point. "He left you the room. He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now." The whole grammar of his consent, his patience, his not-crowding, turned inside out into abandonment. That line is the chapter's thesis about him.

Randi: this is where I'm most unsettled, and it's earned, not manufactured. I just read her decide, in that hotel room, not to tell Vee — to "let Vee arrive at it on her own," to keep the brunches exactly as they are, to "have Vee this way and keep the friendship both, as long as the word kept its cover." And now I watch Vee receive that decision from the inside, calling Randi "the year's gift she had literally stumbled into," "the only girl she could talk to about real things," and — the knife — planning her whole new year around "the one sure thing": Randi's face lighting up and calling her gorgeous "and mean it." She does mean it. That's what's awful. Randi's warmth is the realest thing in Vee's life and it is also the cover for a want Vee isn't allowed to know about. The two lines of that text — "Vee heard it one way; Randi meant it two ways" — I now hear stereo, and Vee is deaf in one ear.

And Vee's own denied want for Randi is doing quiet, gorgeous work here. She "wanted it, every part of it" — the mountains, the dress, the life — but the wanting keeps snagging on Randi's face, Randi's message, Randi as the reliable warm. The book is letting Vee's envy of Randi's life and her pull toward Randi's person blur together in the dark where she doesn't have to name either. "Reached for the best night… and it turned" — the blonde intrudes on Pace; but the whole chapter, structurally, is Vee reaching for Randi to get warm and not letting herself feel what kind of reaching it is.

The PPP secret: I love that the book keeps it lit and unspoken. "She'd been in the glass room when the name came up and Randi blanched; she knew whose money it was. And she envied the mountains anyway." Vee holding Randi's shameful secret in silence, protecting her, envying the poisoned dream — that's the exact currency of this friendship, one-directional generosity flowing toward the person who's running a game on her. "Had let Randi cover and never said, not then and not after."

Swoon / pull: this isn't a heat chapter and doesn't pretend to be — its pull is ache, not arousal, and it's strong. Where I melted was the tenderness of the cooking-memory before it curdled: the sheet, "nothing that needed saying," her body quiet and used well. And where it caught me hardest was the last paragraph — the poppers, the dog, the year over, and Vee "letting herself have the thing everyone else was having somewhere tonight — arms around her, his arms" — and it won't hold "because in the same breath she could not keep from it: whose he had them around instead. The wanting and the fear in the one image." I felt that. To not even be able to imagine being held without the fear photobombing the fantasy — that's real, and it's the loneliest thing in the chapter.

Trust vs. suspicion: no new suspicion of anyone — my suspicion is all confirmation now. What the text does here is let me feel the full asymmetry: Vee genuinely, decently reaching toward two people who are each keeping a load-bearing secret from her, and me holding both secrets while she holds neither. The unease is entirely earned by what I've been shown. Cassie isn't here but her absence over the break is part of Vee's isolation — the one clear-eyed person is offstage.

Friction: almost none. This is very controlled. If anything I'll note that the interiority is relentless — a full chapter of one girl's circling thoughts with no dialogue and no scene-partner — and there's a risk of it feeling like a lot of the same beat (the phone picked up and set down; the empty text field for the second time in three chapters: "She'd learned that already, in another bed, in another cold room"). But the book names that repetition on purpose, so it reads as pattern rather than padding. The one spot where I felt the author's hand a hair too visible was "the cold room kept watch over the girl who was gone" as the final line — it's beautiful, but it reaches back and re-rings the "room did not know her" bell so deliberately that I felt the frame close rather than simply feeling it close. A small thing; the bell is a good bell.

The titles: "Old Acquaintances" — this landed sideways and then bloomed. Auld Lang Syne, of course, New Year's Eve, the song about old acquaintance not being forgot — and the whole chapter is about acquaintances Vee HAS forgotten, or been forgotten by: "the girls she'd once have said were her whole life and who hadn't texted in a year," the room full of trophies belonging to a girl who's gone. And it points forward too, oblique: who are the "acquaintances" she carries into the new year — Pace, who may not be anything anymore, and Randi, whom she'll "have back the day the term started"? It didn't give anything away; it colored the chapter warmer-sadder on the reread of the title. Nice. The book title, "A Polite Invitation," and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — this chapter is the underside of that promise. Here's the girl who said all those free yeses, alone, having asked the one question (what am I to you) that a polite invitation never has to answer, and getting silence. The series line "With a Long Spoon" — sup with the devil, use a long spoon — sits heavy over Randi's manufactured fairytale text and Vee's grateful reply to it: Vee is supping with someone she should be eating with a very long spoon, and reaching across the table with a warm open hand instead. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over" — she's handing over her New Year's warmth to the exact person managing her.

What I want / dread next: I want the term to start so I can watch that first Randi-and-Vee scene with everything I now know loaded into it — the "face that would light up and call her gorgeous and mean it," meaning it two ways. And I dread the Pace reunion, or non-reunion: the break has held long enough that I genuinely don't know (guess from here) whether he'll come find her with an answer, or whether his "words are cheap, believe what I do" turns out to be a wall she breaks on. I half-expect (guess) that the plan's architects will have to decide whether to let this fight actually cost them Vee, or whether one of them — probably Randi, whose feeling has "gotten real" — steps in to repair it, which would be its own quiet horror: being handled back into happiness. What I most want is for Vee to get one true thing she can name. The whole chapter is her not being able to name anyone, and I ache for her to be given a word that's hers.

STRUCTURED BLOCK

Cast present (in person): Vee (alone, the entire chapter — no one else physically present). Mentioned only / offstage: Pace (no contact), Randi (via text and Instagram feed), Vee's mother and father (asleep/around the house over the break, in memory), Cassie (absent, felt), Sheri (recalled — Thanksgiving), the dance-hall blonde (imagined/spite-built), the older bartender (recalled).

Heat: 0 — no sexual activity on the page. The one erotic memory (fra diavolo, the slipping sheet, "used her well") is reached for as comfort and curdles into jealousy before it can warm her. Longing, not heat.

Romance: 2 — no one's in the room, but the chapter is saturated with the ache of two bonds: real tenderness toward Pace (the cooking, "he always left you the room") and real, denied warmth toward Randi (the one who'll "mean it"). Intimacy present as loss and yearning rather than contact.

Motifs & images: The empty text field / thumb over the little field (repeat — from Bare, "another bed, in another cold room"; the book names the echo itself). The cold room / heat gone into savings mode (repeat/inversion — Pace brings the heat up for her; here no one does). The induction photo, whole vs. cropped-at-the-waist (repeat — Ch 31/37; here she names that SHE is the one who divided it, "same picture, one house"). Randi's white-gold bracelet + solitaire in the feed (repeat — the never-off band, now glimpsed from Vee's side of the glass). "Gorgeous" / "call her gorgeous and mean it" (repeat — Randi's signature). Garlic in the oil / building-then-giving as both cooking and sex (repeat — the through-line of Pace's method). "He leaves you the room" (repeat — Randi's Ch-27 coaching, now soured). Auld Lang Syne / New Year (first, and the chapter's frame). The room-that-loved-a-girl-who's-gone / outgrown bed (first — strong new image).

Symbolism: The childhood room as the outgrown self — dusted, high-resolution, loving Vivienne and blind to Vee; the bed her legs outgrew years ago. The cropped photo she scissored herself — dividing her own body to fit the house, the exact leg that made her feel like herself cut away for her parents; her whole predicament in one gesture. The memory she "could not keep" as the mind losing possession of its own good — jealousy as dispossession. Randi's "poisoned Gstaad" — a dream Vee knows is bought with fraud and envies anyway; wanting a thing while knowing what bought it (the book's whole moral weather).

Characterization: Vee deepens markedly — this is the most sustained interior we've had, and it holds: her ambivalence about waking up (freer AND homeless), her honesty about spite ("she found a who… built her from spite"), her inability to name Pace or herself, all consistent with and extending the woman I know. Randi and Pace are characterized entirely by absence and Vee's reading of them, which is faithful to their established grammars (Randi's manufactured shine, Pace's leave-the-room patience). No one flattens; no one serves plot at the cost of self. Vee carries the chapter alone and earns it.

Pace — within the chapter: Well-judged for a single-POV interior vigil; the circling (phone up/down, memory reached-for-and-turned, the who-she-invents) is the point and mostly earns its length. One or two loops of the same beat (the empty field, "maybe it's over") verge on redundant but are named as pattern. The last paragraph lands the hour cleanly.

Pace — chapter to chapter: Holds, and deliberately decompresses. After the escalating fight-chapters and the Randi/Gstaad set-piece, this is the exhale that lets the damage settle — a paired-vigil to "Fairytale" (both women alone in cold beds on the same New Year's Eve, warm only where they aren't). Momentum is emotional, not plot; the "term starts" promise plus the unresolved Pace break points the engine forward. Not sagging — but this is now several chapters in a row of aftermath/interiority, and I'm ready for two people back in a room together.
