## Reader reaction

So this is the other side of the same night — Vee's twin bed in Ohio against Randi's Gstaad chalet, and the chapter is built so I *know* what Vee doesn't: that the fairytale text she's aching under was sent by a woman who felt nothing, phone face-down, having just decided to keep the real thing from her. That knowledge made the whole chapter hurt in a low, steady way. It's not a loud chapter. It's a person lying in the dark failing to sleep, and it earns every one of its slow minutes.

The room did the most to me. "The room did not know her. It had known Vivienne — knew her still, in high resolution, and loved her." That landed hard, and it kept paying off — the ribbons, the little gold swimmers frozen mid-lift, the clarinet in the closet, the corkboard faces who "hadn't texted in a year." The room loving a girl who no longer lives in the body in the bed. And the physical rhyme underneath it — she keeps her knees bent because her legs outgrew this bed in middle school, and by the end she's "small in it again." The bed she outgrew as a girl, now too small for the woman he made hungry. "She'd been breathing bigger air for months, and come home to a place that no longer fit her." I felt the claustrophobia of that ceiling coming down close.

The food beat is the thesis doing its quiet work and it didn't feel like a lecture this time. Her mother's Christmas casserole and good meat, "because it was Christmas and it was love and had never once been the thing Vee was hungry for, not then and not now; she just hadn't had the word for it then." That's the remade-palate cage told from inside the family that loves her out loud and can't feed the new hunger — and it's genuinely sad, because the mother's love is real and now insufficient. The parents' recurring question, *how's your young man, is it serious,* "landing every time on a door she kept shut and smiled in front of" — she's become a keeper of shut doors like him.

The photo passage got me: same induction picture, one cropped at the waist for her mother's phone, sent whole to Pace. "Same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it." She's the one doing the blindfolding now, on herself, splitting the leg away from the family — that reflex I've watched all book, named plainly.

Then the two turns where her own memories betray her. First she reaches for the best night she has — the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, the wine light — "to be warm," and it *turns while she's in it*: the hair goes gold, the leg goes tan, the blonde sits where Vee sat and the sheet comes off a body "that had never in its life needed building for." That's a beautifully cruel move — her one refuge reaches into her hands and puts the invented rival under her own sheet. "She could not keep her own night." And then the people who reassured her — the bartender's "wasn't him they were jealous of, honey," Randi's "he doesn't sulk, he leaves you the room" — "they had only ever held her up while she was the one," and now they "went straight through her hands. The floor was gone." The whole scaffolding of comfort I've been carrying for her, dissolved in one paragraph because the load-bearing assumption (she's the one) is gone.

Where the dramatic irony bites hardest: Vee lies there *envying* Randi's out-loud life, the white-tie crush where everyone knows her name, a man out of the stories — and I've just read that same night from inside Randi, who felt nothing with the empty prince and is alive only to Pace and Vee. "even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had." Vee knows the money's poisoned — she was in the glass room when the name came up and Randi blanched — and envies it anyway. And she sends the warm, demand-nothing text, careful to give nothing away, the mirror of Randi's careful text. Two women each performing a warmth that hides the real thing, each thinking the other is the one living out loud. "Randi, the only girl she could talk to about real things. The real things they talked about were mostly Pace." That line is quietly devastating now that I know what Randi is steering toward.

The Sheri thread got a real turn of the knife: "about Christmas he'd said nothing at all — a whole week she couldn't see into." She folded Sheri away as pity in November "when she was the one he cooked for," and now she isn't sure she's the one, so the pity won't stay folded. And then she *invents* the rival — blonde, tan, "a fashion model's body where Vee's was a stripper's," "handed Pace the exact opposite of herself and named it the thing he would want the moment he was free of her." That's the shame engine with no occasion to run on, so it manufactures one. Self-torture as reflex.

**Trust/suspicion:** No new suspicion of anyone — this chapter is pure interiority. But my dread about Randi is now fully operational as *irony* rather than menace: the sweetness of that "you ANGEL 😇" text is unbearable because I know the hand that sent it. The text doesn't feel sinister to Vee, which is exactly right and exactly the trap. Nothing here felt like the author signaling. If anything the restraint is the achievement — Randi's cruelty is entirely invisible from Vee's side, so the cold only exists in *my* head, laid over Vee's warmth.

**Erotic charge:** Low and deliberately so — this is a cold-bed chapter. The one real heat is the fra diavolo memory, and it's engineered to curdle: it starts warm (the sheet slipping, "the evening having used her well," "nothing that needed saying") precisely so the gold-haired hijack can take it away. So the heat here isn't for arousal, it's for loss. That worked on me.

**Friction:** Very little. It flirts with over-repeating "she was the one / now she doesn't know she's anything" — that beat comes around maybe once more than it strictly needs. And "what you believe at midnight, cold and alone on the one night you're not supposed to be" nudges toward telling me how to feel about the moment I'm already feeling. But these are small; the chapter mostly trusts the objects (the trophies, the clarinet, the cropped photo) to carry it, and they do.

**What I want / dread next:** I want Cassie, badly — she's been absent since ch.39 and this is the exact ache she exists to answer, and Vee explicitly has "no one to turn to." *Guess from here:* term starts, the brunches resume, and Randi walks back in as "the one sure thing the new year held" while Pace stays "a blank" — so the next real collision is Vee bringing this raw, unmoored, is-it-even-over grief to the one person steering her, who has just decided to withhold. I dread the brunch where Vee, at her most defenseless, hands Randi exactly the opening the plan wants. And I still want to know what actually happened at the dance, and whether Pace reaches out first — the whole chapter turns on him leaving her the room, which she now "hated."
