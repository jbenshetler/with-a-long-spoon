# Cold read — Broken In

*scene: scenes/broken-in.md · read after: lesson*

## Reader reaction

This one goes down easy and warm, which by now is exactly what makes my neck prickle. I've read this chapter enough times over — I mean, I've read *this shape* enough times over: Vee arrives cracked open, Randi takes the worst minute of her life, turns it over in her hands, and hands it back as proof she's winning. "That's not the disaster, honey. That's the only thing that matters, and you handed it to him for free." It's such a *good* move. It genuinely comforts. And "the thing Vee had been holding clenched since the dance floor let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so." That last clause — *because Randi had said so* — is the whole book in five words. Vee's relief isn't reasoned, it's obedient. She doesn't test it. She just unclenches on command.

Vee herself I loved this morning. The opening is the best sustained stretch of *her* alone I've had in a while — pretending to sleep so Cassie won't see her, the cold water "through her like a verdict," the sad-clown in the mirror, deciding to be "a woman with bare legs in October." She walks out into that rinsed blue day eating an apple and I was genuinely rooting for her, buoyed, *she's going to fix this.* The bare legs and the cold she decides to like — there's something clean and self-owning in it, a girl choosing to feel awake. I want her to keep that. My dread is that she carried it straight to the one house that will spend it.

Randi. My trust in her is on the floor and this chapter doesn't move it — it just makes the floor more comfortable. Two things landed hard. First: **"Not today."** Vee is ready to go make it right *this afternoon,* which is the honest, human impulse, and Randi shuts it down — "Tomorrow night." The stated reason is plausible, even wise-sounding ("he'll smell the panic on you"). But I've watched Randi manage Vee's timing over and over, and here she's literally scheduling the reunion. She's setting the *stage,* the wardrobe, the hour, and telling Vee what Pace will and won't do ("He won't text you… he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing"). She knows exactly what he'll do. Of course she does. Second: the whole back half is a fitting, and it rhymes with the shoe day and with Pace's kneeling tending both at once. Which brings me to the part that actually got under my skin.

The shoe-fitting is the erotic peak, and it's doing something sly. Vee's *skin came up singing* — and she can't see any of it, the long skirt falls over the whole operation, "somebody kneeling to tend to her, and Vee left to stand there and be tended to and never watch it happen." A hand up under the skirt to the outside of her thigh, a thumb pressing "into the soft hollow behind her knee," her breath doing "something she hoped wasn't out loud." This is Randi producing with her hands what Pace produces with his — and Vee frames it as *nothing,* dress-up, "the oldest game there is." "Standing in her bra in front of Randi was no event; she'd done as much for Pace." That line is the tell she can't hear herself ringing. And the shoes themselves: "the holding didn't bind. It steadied. Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." That is the consent-architecture sentence again — *safety through surrender* — this time worn on the foot. I felt the charge and I felt the cold under it simultaneously, which I think is the point, and I resent how well it works on me.

The goodbye kiss on the mouth, and Vee "caught up to something a half-beat late: she hadn't braced." The staircase there is invisible from inside — she's stopped noticing it's a staircase. That's the scariest small beat in the chapter. Body ahead of mind, and the mind not even bothering to catch up anymore.

The friction, for me: almost none, and that's its own kind of friction — the chapter is *so* smooth I kept waiting for a snag and there wasn't one, which after a while reads as a room with no exits. The one place the thumb felt heavy was the mirror close: "behind her in the glass Randi watched her like her own good work." I know it's meant to be tender-with-a-cold-underside, and it *is* the honest image, but "her own good work" is about as close as this book gets to saying the quiet part, and I noticed the author's hand on it. Same with "*Dinner in Paris.* Vee would have walked into traffic for the woman who said *dinner in Paris* about her" — I loved it as a line, but it's the narrator telling me how total the capture is rather than letting me infer it. Small. Mostly the chapter earns its warmth.

The Edna Mode laugh was a real gift — the two of them helpless, "the small dark one and the tall red one, laughing in the cold bright room." That's the thing this book keeps insisting on and keeps making me believe: the friendship is *real.* Whatever else Randi is doing, that laugh isn't fake, and that's what makes it unbearable. And "shoe whore… my very own shoe whore," offered "like an endearment, the way the worst words did between them" — that's the shame-reframe engine running in miniature, the worst word made warm. Randi hands Vee back her own worst names as gifts. I keep circling that because it's the jacket copy come to life: "drawing out the shames she was raised to bury and handing them back as gifts."

Brooke at the door: "you're the stats friend. Not a question. Vee had told this house nothing in her life and the house had a folder on her anyway." That chilled me more than anything Randi did. The house *knows* Vee. She's been discussed. She's a file. The composites in the foyer, "rows of bright assembled faces," a museum "that hadn't yet decided whether to let her up to the paintings" — she's being auditioned for a wall.

**The title.** "Broken In." Surface: the boots "worn soft," a shoe broken in until it holds your foot — and the chapter's shot through with footwear, the cognac heels that "took her foot and meant it." But by the end "broken in" recolors hard: it's *Vee* being broken in, gentled into the harness like a good shoe or a good horse, until the structure that gives her "no say in where her foot went" feels like being steadied. And the third sense, uglier: to be *broken into* — the house with a folder on her, the room she's being fitted for. The title didn't give anything away going in; it detonates on the last page, exactly the way I've come to expect. I didn't feel it telegraph.

**The book's title and tagline** feel more literal this chapter than they have in a while. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — Vee freely chooses the bare legs, freely walks to the house, freely laughs, freely lifts her foot for the shoe, freely lets Randi set tomorrow night. Every single yes is hers. And she's being scheduled, dressed, and delivered to a reunion the other woman is orchestrating. *With a long spoon* — she keeps sitting down to sup, and she keeps letting Randi set the table and pick the hour.

What I want next: I want to see the reunion Randi has staged — Vee walking into Pace's door in the black dress, the neckline down, the cognac heels, the green silk she'll tie herself. *Guess from here:* it goes exactly as Randi predicted, which will feel to Vee like magic and to me like a script. And I dread — mildly, but it's there — that Cassie, the one person who saw Vee wound herself into a man's shirt and made only a low *Hrm,* is going to keep making only that sound while the folder on Vee gets thicker.
