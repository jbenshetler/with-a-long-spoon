# Cold read — Broken In

*scene: scenes/broken-in.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: lesson*

## Reader reaction

This one hurt in the exact quiet way I've come to expect from this book, and I mean that as praise. Nothing "happens" — a girl gets dressed, walks across campus, gets fitted for shoes, gets a pep talk — and yet the whole chapter is the machine tightening one more notch, invisibly.

**Vee.** The opening domestic stuff won me completely. The bit where she lies on the top bunk feigning sleep so she doesn't have to face Cassie — "imagined the picture she must make up there... a girl wound into a man's shirt, hair gone off across the pillow like something that had happened to her" — that's her routing around her moral floor again, and she's doing it in her body before her mind admits it. She waits for the door to close. That's the coldest thing in the chapter and Vee doesn't even clock it as avoidance; she just wants to get to Randi. The cold-water-as-verdict ritual, fixing "the sad-clown thing," the bare legs in October — I liked her here. She's resilient and funny and self-aware in the small ways and completely blind in the large one. The Edna Mode laugh is a genuinely lovely beat; it's real friendship-texture, which is what makes me sad, because I don't believe it's mutual in the way she thinks.

**Pace.** He's not on the page and I ache for him. Vee's retelling of the bar — "his face just *went.* I watched it land. I'd hurt him, and the worst of it is some piece of me was glad" — that's a real confession, and it makes me love them both more. And Randi's line "he doesn't sulk. He leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing" reads to Vee as insight into a man she barely knows. To me it reads as Randi describing a man she knows *intimately*. That's the knife.

**Randi.** Okay, this is where the text earned my dread rather than me importing it. Watch what she does mechanically: Vee arrives ready to go apologize *today*, on her own initiative, in a rush of genuine feeling — and Randi shuts it down. "Not today." "Not a suggestion — an instruction, and Vee stopped." She delays the apology, scripts the timing, scripts the wardrobe, scripts the *scarf-tying* down to "you'll be tying it yourself tomorrow night, so — watch." She's not helping Vee reconcile with Pace. She's staging Vee's arrival at Pace's door. The apology has become a production she's directing, and Vee experiences it as being loved. "the thing Vee had been holding clenched since the dance floor let go of her, all at once — because Randi had said so." *Because Randi had said so.* That line is the whole cold engine in six words.

And the reframe itself is textbook by now: Vee's shame ("I punished a good man") gets flipped into proof of investment ("You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about"). It's genuinely comforting. It's also load-bearing — it keeps Vee *in*, keeps her from the one healthy instinct (sit with the shame, maybe call Cassie, maybe question the whole glittering apparatus).

**The shoe-fitting — erotic charge.** This worked on me, strongly, and it's the peak of the chapter. The choice to hide all of it under the long skirt is what does it — "She couldn't see any of it... there was only the feel." The hand coming up under the hem to the outside of her thigh to steady her, the thumb pressing "into the soft hollow behind her knee on the way down," "Randi's hand bracing high on the back of her thigh each time she had to lift a foot." Vee narrates it as being *tended to*, and her breath "did something she hoped wasn't out loud." It's the same grammar as Pace's dance lead — being given no say makes her *surer*: "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." That sentence detonated for me. It's the thesis of the whole book smuggled into a shoe. The shoe holds her foot the way the lead held her body the way Randi is holding her whole life — and the binding reads as steadiness. That's the seduction and the horror in one image.

The "same shoe size" seed from before pays off here — the shoes fit, they're the one thing of Randi's Vee's body *can* wear. "Me, in here, in nothing but your shoes." "You'd be my very own *shoe whore.*" And then the "Evil Queen" line: "Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet." That's Randi telling the truth as a joke, which is the only way this book ever lets the truth out. It landed hard.

**The goodbye kiss.** The staircase beat I've been tracking — and the chilling detail is that Vee catches up "a half-beat late: she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed... It was only what they did." The escalation has become baseline. That's exactly the thing the book keeps doing: the new threshold is invisible from inside because the last one already normalized it.

**Trust/suspicion.** I have no *new* reason to trust Randi and every structural reason not to, but I want to be precise: the text is playing fair. Randi is warm, funny, generous, genuinely fun to be around. If I didn't have the cold pattern in my memory I'd read her as the best friend a girl could have. The dread is entirely in the *pattern* — the delay, the scripting, "because Randi had said so," the touch that goes a hair past friendly. Nothing on this page announces sinister. That restraint is why it works.

**Friction.** Very little. One or two lines flirt with over-signaling. "behind her in the glass Randi watched her like her own good work" — "her own good work" is the narrator leaning in a quarter inch, telling me the thing the chapter has already shown me. It's earned enough that I didn't recoil, but it's the closest the chapter came to the author's thumb. Same with "something moved in her face, there and gone, that Vee didn't have a name for and was too happy to go looking for" — that's the wordless-beat-of-the-plan-escaping device, and it's effective, but it's the one moment I felt the machinery wink at me. The counterweight — "too happy to go looking for" — mostly saves it by keeping us in Vee's blindness.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the apology scene. I dread it too, because I now expect it to be a *performance* Vee walks into believing it's her own initiative, in shoes and silk Randi chose, at a time Randi chose — and I expect it to go *well*, which is the awful part. *Guess from here:* Pace will receive the apology gracefully and the reconciliation will be real and warm and a step deeper into the trap, and somewhere near it — or soon — the induction Randi keeps prepping her for arrives. I keep waiting for the floor to drop on the Peter/Randi thing, for Vee to see one seam. Not yet. And I still want Cassie to come back into frame; every chapter Vee avoids her, the book gets lonelier.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Morning after the bar disaster. Vee fakes sleep to avoid Cassie, deliberately lets her leave without talking, folds Pace's shirt away, and does her cold-water ritual to shake the funk. She dresses with care (long skirt, bare legs, the good coat) and walks to Randi's sorority house — Brooke answers ("you're the stats friend"; the house has its folder on Vee). Randi, just-woken and bare-faced, reads the whole disaster off Vee and pulls her upstairs. Vee confesses the bar in full, including that a piece of her was *glad* to hurt Pace. Randi reframes the shame into proof of investment ("you don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about"), then takes command: **not today** — the apology is scheduled for *tomorrow night*, staged, wardrobe and all. She scripts it: the little black dress, neckline down, the real earrings, and — crucially — a pair of her own very expensive open-toe cognac heels (fitted to Vee under her skirt, unseen, a charged tending), plus a green-and-gold silk scarf Vee is drilled to tie herself. Randi tells Vee to walk in "sure," not sorry, and predicts Pace "won't text... he doesn't sulk." Vee leaves with the shoes in a drawstring bag, class skipped, aimed at tomorrow night. Goodbye mouth-kiss again — and Vee notices she no longer braces for it.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee / Vivienne** — in love, ashamed, resilient and funny; routing around Cassie; now fully steered by Randi and experiencing the steering as love. Wearing borrowed shoes that fit where nothing else of Randi's would. From Ohio, ex-synchro swimmer, broke among wealth.
- **Pace / Peter** — off-page, wounded by the bar, presumed waiting; secretly Randi's co-orchestrator. Every good thing about him true and load-bearing. The apology to him is being staged for tomorrow night.
- **Randi** — dazzling confidante and secret co-orchestrator; delayed/scripted the apology, dressed Vee down to the scarf-knot, fitted the heels with a touch that ran a hair past friendly ("Evil Queen," "shoe whore"). Warm, funny, generous on the surface; directing a production underneath. The kiss is now baseline.
- **Cassie** — Vee's roommate and moral floor; Vee avoided her again this morning by faking sleep. Still the person I trust; still being routed around.
- **Brooke** — sorority door-answerer, warm-surfaced, confirms the house "knows" Vee.

**How I feel.** Golden-on-top, cold-underneath, and lonelier than ever. The chapter never announces the trap — Randi is genuinely delightful — but every mechanical beat (delay the apology, script the outfit, "because Randi had said so," the under-the-skirt touch, the automatic kiss) tightens it. I love Pace more, dread for Vee more, and the shoe-fitting is the erotic peak: being given no say makes her *surer*, which is this book's whole seduction and whole horror in one image.

**Open questions.** How the staged apology at Pace's door goes (I expect: well, and deeper in). When the "induction" Randi keeps prepping arrives. When/whether Vee sees a single seam of the Peter/Randi connection. Whether Cassie forces her way back in. Still open from before: the scar and "not tonight," right-hand-only eating, what his mother calls him, his withheld family.
