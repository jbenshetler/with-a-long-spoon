# Cold read — Broken In

*scene: scenes/broken-in.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: lesson*

## Reader reaction

So the two tracks finally touched, and I feel a little sick about how smooth it was. Not because anything went wrong — because nothing did.

The opening third is Vee alone, and it's the best I've felt reading her in a while. The cold water "went through her like a verdict," putting her face in it on purpose, deciding the funk had been a choice all night and being *done making it* — that's the Vee I like, the one with a spine and a wit. "Wouldn't be half bad without the sad-clown thing," fixing the clown, bare legs in October "with the conviction of the converted." The apple, the bin for the core "like a person raised right." She's self-authoring here, walking herself out of a bad night on her own steam, and I was rooting hard. When she thinks *I'm going to fix this* and it "put length in her stride" — genuinely lovely. The cold on her bare legs being "the same clean daylight verdict" — I liked her.

And then she walks straight into the machine and I wanted to grab her coat.

The door opening on **Brooke** landed with a small drop in my stomach. This is the house — the composites she's "only ever seen the back of," the museum lobby "that hadn't yet decided whether to let her up." "You're the stats friend. Not a question. Vee had told this house nothing in her life and the house had a folder on her anyway." That line did real work on me — the sense of being *catalogued* by an institution before you've spoken. Brooke's warmth that leaves Vee "feeling small" is consistent with everything, and her being the one who greets her, coffee in hand, running the door — it made the sorority feel like an apparatus, not a place. I didn't trust that foyer for a second.

Then Randi at the top of the stairs, robe, no makeup, "read the whole thing off her like a label." *Oh, honey. Come up.* And here's what unsettles me most: the whole scene is *warm and good and it works.* Vee tells the fight in pieces, "the way it does when someone's letting you tell it instead of waiting for their turn" — that's the confidante engine named right on the page, and it's seductive to watch. Randi calls the blonde a bitch with feeling, holds her hand, and then does "the thing she did, which was take the worst minute of Vee's life and turn it over in her hands and show her the back of it." *You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about.* *He took you. On a Thursday.* Reframing the disaster into proof of how in-love she is. And "the thing Vee had been holding clenched... all at once — because Randi had said so." That last clause is the tell for me. Not because it's true, but because *Randi said so* is doing the releasing, not the truth of it. My carry-forward has been screaming that Randi produces with words what Pace produces with the body, and here it is, undisguised: she takes Vee's guilt and dissolves it exactly the way she dissolved the affordable shoe.

The **"Not today"** was the coldest beat. Vee's already half off the bed to go fix it *today* — genuine, urgent, her own instinct — and Randi shuts it down. *Not a suggestion — an instruction, and Vee stopped.* Then engineers the timing: tomorrow night, so it "reads like something you chose," go in "sure" not "sorry." And *"He won't text you... he leaves you the room and waits to see what you walk in wearing."* On first read that's shrewd best-friend advice. Underneath it, Randi is scheduling the reconciliation, staging it, dressing it. She's directing a scene Vee thinks she's living. I noticed I couldn't actually fault a single word — which is exactly what the book keeps doing to me, and I resent it in a way I think I'm meant to.

Then the dress-up, and this is where I have to be honest: **the erotic charge worked on me even while I distrusted it.** The Edna Mode laugh is genuinely funny and disarming — "you just straight-up Edna Mode'd me," both of them gone laughing, "the small dark one and the tall red one." I liked them there. It bought real warmth. The freckle mirroring — Randi's "sparse cocoa scatter she plainly never gave a thought to" against Vee's "cinnamon excess" she'd just spent ten minutes burying — quietly aches; Randi's ease with her own face is another kind of power over the girl who paints hers over.

And the shoe-tending. This is the third foot-kneeling scene now (Randi thumbing the toenail, then this), and it's the most charged of them. The whole thing done *under the long skirt where Vee can't see* — "there was only the feel — the cool quick hands somewhere down past the hem," a hand coming "up under the skirt to the outside of her thigh," the thumb "pressing once into the soft hollow behind her knee on the way down," her breath doing "something she hoped wasn't out loud." That's blindfold eroticism — being *tended to and never watching it happen* — and it got under my skin the way it got under hers. It peaked for me on the third pair: the architecture of the shoe that "took her foot and *held* it," and Vee turning over that "the holding didn't bind. It steadied. Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less." I actually stopped on that. Because that's the whole book, isn't it — surrender as the thing that makes you *more* solid, said about a shoe. "Broken In," the title, lands there: the shoe, and her. It's the thesis laundered through footwear and it's *good*, and it scared me a little that it read as comfort.

The unaffordable-shoe beat repeats from the heels chapter — "more than a whole semester of her books... decided not to finish the math." Randi again giving her something ruinous and waving off the protest: *You're wearing them, that's settled.* The "shoe whore" / "Evil Queen" / "move in" banter is fun and filthy-friendly, but I clocked "Standards" and "room inspection" — the institution again, joking about *covering her up*, dressing her, inspecting her. Even the play routes through the apparatus.

Where did it go slack? Honestly nowhere badly. If I'm hunting for the author's thumb, "*Dinner in Paris.* Vee would have walked into traffic for the woman who said *dinner in Paris* about her." is the one place the narration nudges me — it's telling me how completely Randi owns her rather than letting me feel it, and it's a hair broad. But it's Vee's own giddy voice, so I mostly forgave it.

The goodbye kiss climbed one more quiet inch, and the book flagged it precisely: Vee "caught up to something a half-beat late: she hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." The staircase I've been tracking since the double-press just went from *accepted-unscanned* to *reflexive, unnoticed*. She's fully broken in to it. That's the second meaning of the title and it's the colder one.

What I want next: the reconciliation with Pace, tomorrow night, in Randi's costume. I *dread* it a little, because Vee's about to walk into her own genuine love wearing another woman's staging, carrying another woman's shoes, and she thinks she's fixing something of her own. And I keep waiting — 26 chapters in — for the moment the two men, the two tracks, snap into one picture for *Vee.* This chapter is the closest the wires have come to touching, and she felt nothing but held. That's the horror of it working.

*Guess from here:* the reconciliation goes beautifully, exactly as staged, and Randi's fingerprints on it are invisible to Vee and total. And those shoes, that black dress, that scarf — I'd bet they resurface pointed at something bigger than one apology.
