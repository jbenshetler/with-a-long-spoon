## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This one is a warm chapter, and it worked on me even as I sat there watching the trap tighten — which is exactly the queasy pleasure this book keeps handing me.

The opening is lovely, small, precise. Vee tracking Cassie's morning by sound with her eyes shut, imagining "the picture she must make up there on the top bunk: a girl wound into a man's shirt, hair gone off across the pillow like something that had happened to her." That last clause is the whole book in miniature — *something that had happened to her*. And then she gets up, shuts the window Cassie cracks for the cold, folds the flannel "the way it deserved," and puts the totem back on the pillow. The shirt as relic, still.

The face-washing beat is the strongest bit of pure character in the chapter. The cold water "went through her like a verdict," the mascara up near a cheekbone "it had no business being near," "Wouldn't be half bad without the sad-clown thing." She fixes herself from *crime scene* to *long night* and calls it far enough. I like this Vee — self-mocking, moving, decided. The bare legs in October, "a woman with bare legs in October," the apple, the length coming into her stride when she thinks *I'm going to fix this.* For once she's the agent of her own morning, and the prose lets her have it. I felt genuinely buoyant walking across that rinsed blue campus with her.

And then she walks into Randi's house, and my stomach did the thing.

Because I know what she doesn't. She's going to the co-founder of the game to get "fixed" for the man they've been steering her toward all along. "Vee had told this house nothing in her life and the house had a folder on her anyway" — that line is doing the reader a favor Vee can't see. Brooke's warmth "had somehow left her feeling small." The composites, "rows of bright assembled faces in oval frames," the museum "that hadn't yet decided whether to let her up to the paintings." The whole foyer read to me as an institution appraising a candidate. It's beautifully unsettling precisely because Vee experiences it as almost-belonging.

The counsel session upstairs is where I trust Randi least while liking her most. Watch what she actually does: Vee comes in wrecked with real remorse about having hurt a good man by weaponizing his decency — a *true*, clean instinct — and Randi takes that true thing and "turned it over in her hands and show her the back of it." She reframes the wound as proof: "You don't get that jealous over a man you don't care about... That's the only thing that matters, and you handed it to him for free." It's genuinely comforting, and it's genuinely a redirection away from the apology Vee owes. The chapter even flags it: "And the thing Vee had been holding clenched... all at once — because Randi had said so." *Because Randi had said so.* That's the tell. The relief isn't earned by Vee thinking it through; it's granted by the authority figure. That's the machine.

Then "Not today" — the instruction, not a suggestion, and Vee *stopped*. Randi choreographs the timing: not today, tomorrow night, don't go in sorry, go in sure, "he doesn't sulk... he waits to see what you walk in wearing." Randi is directing the reconciliation between her secret lover and the girl she's dressing. And the line "He won't text you, by the way" — she *knows* he won't, because she knows *him*. Vee hears a wise friend reading a man off a description; I hear a co-conspirator with inside information. That gap is the whole engine and it's running hot here.

The dress-up sequence. "Stand up, top off. I have to see what I'm working with" — and Vee cracking up, doing the *Edna Mode* voice, the two of them "gone, the small dark one and the tall red one, laughing in the cold bright room." That laughter is real and I loved it. This is the part that makes the trap a trap: it isn't grim, it's *fun*, it's the oldest game there is, two girls and a morning. Vee stands in her bra and it's "nothing... she'd done as much for Pace." And then "Randi looked, and that was the part Vee felt." The pass of attention "went over Vee's skin." "He doesn't stand a chance" going "straight through Vee... because it was Randi who'd said it."

I want to be honest about the erotic charge, because the shoe-fitting is where it peaks and it's the most double-edged thing in the chapter. It rhymes hard with the boutique scene from before — Randi kneeling, the foot, the shoe as erotic site — but this time it's *hidden under the skirt*. Vee "couldn't see any of it," "left to stand there and be tended to and never watch it happen." The hand "came up under the skirt to the outside of her thigh," the thumb "pressing once into the soft hollow behind her knee," the breath that "did something she hoped wasn't out loud." This is seduction disguised as service, and Vee reads it as pampering. It absolutely worked on me as erotic — a 2, quietly — and it worked *because* of the concealment, which is also what makes it predatory. The shoe that "took her foot and *held* it," where "being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it, not less" — that's the thesis wearing a cognac heel. Engineered constraint experienced as steadiness. I actually stopped on that paragraph. It's the cleanest statement of the book's whole argument I've hit, and it's about *footwear*.

The "Evil Queen" line made me sit up. Vee jokes she has no evil stepsisters, and Randi says "Maybe you just haven't met your Evil Queen yet." It's flirty, it's a fairy-tale gag — but the book handed me *Evil Queen* out of Randi's own mouth. Poison apple, the fairest of them all, the queen who dresses up to destroy the girl. Vee ate an apple on the steps on the way in, too. I don't think Vee hears anything in it. I heard a lot. Whether the text *earned* it or is nudging me, I can't fully tell — but it's Randi naming herself the villain as a term of endearment, and that landed cold under the warmth.

The scarf lesson: "you'll be tying it yourself tomorrow night, so — watch," hands over hands, "Tension. It has to hold its shape or it'll go limp." *Dinner in Paris* vs. *dorm mixer.* "Vee would have walked into traffic for the woman who said *dinner in Paris* about her." That line is doing double work — it's Vee's genuine devotion *and* a quiet horror-flash of how completely she's captured. And the shoes she can't afford, again: "More than a whole semester of her books... decided not to finish the math." The overspending motif, except now Randi just *gives* them, which puts Vee in her debt in a way money can't square.

The goodbye kiss closed it perfectly and chillingly: Vee "hadn't braced. Hadn't tensed. Her mouth had just met Randi's. It was only what they did." The escalating second-press kiss has become baseline, unremarkable to her. That's how far she's traveled without noticing a single step.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No movement in the good direction. This chapter *hardened* everything I suspected about Randi, and it did it through kindness, which is the most damning possible way. The text earned it: the "because Randi had said so," the inside knowledge about Pace, the "Evil Queen," the hidden-hands fitting. I am fully on the side of dreading Randi now, even as I enjoy her. Vee I trust completely and fear for. Pace is offstage and I ache for him — the wounded man being managed back into position by the woman who wounded him, via the woman he shares.

**Where it went slack:** Almost nowhere. If I'm reaching — the morning-routine getting-dressed inventory (skirt, boots, coat) ran a beat long before the plot moved, but it earned its place by grounding her agency. No thumb-on-scale this time; the book trusted me to hear "because Randi had said so" without underlining it, and I'm grateful.

# Titles

**"Broken In"** — I read it first as the shoes: new expensive heels, broken in. But by the end it's plainly *Vee* who's being broken in — a body trained to a shape, a foot taught where to be by a structure that "held" it, a mouth that no longer braces at the kiss. "Being given no say in where her foot went made her surer of it." That's breaking-in as gentling, as taming — the way you break in a horse or a boot or a girl. The title's innocent surface (comfortable shoes) and its real charge (she's being domesticated to their design and calling it steadiness) are exactly the split the whole book runs on. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it recolored the shoe scene into something colder on the way out.

**With a Long Spoon** — "he who sups with the devil must use a long spoon." And this chapter, of all things, has Vee eating an apple on the way to the Evil Queen's house, then being fed relief, reframing, shoes, silk, all of it — with no long spoon at all. She sups with both hands. The tagline, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap," is practically the caption for the shoe fitting: every lift of her foot was hers, the constraint felt like safety, and she walked out in debt and in love and one day from walking into their design on purpose, *sure*.

**What I want / dread next:** The reconciliation with Pace, tomorrow night, in the black dress and the scarf and the shoes Randi built her — that's clearly the next beat and I both want it and dread it, because Vee will walk in thinking she authored it. *Guess from here:* I keep circling the idea that the two worlds have to touch — that at some point Vee learns Randi and Pace are connected, and I still half-expect the November induction to be where it detonates. Nothing this chapter confirmed it; if anything it showed me how smoothly the two hands are working in concert without Vee feeling the seam. My dread is that the reveal is a long way off and I'm going to watch her fall further first.
