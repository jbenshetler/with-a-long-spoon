**Felt read.**

Oh, this one *worked* on me. I went in braced — the last chapter left me uneasy because Randi engineered this whole gesture, coached Vee into it, handed her the shoes — and I kept waiting for that shadow to fall across the scene and spoil it. It never quite did, and I'm not going to pretend it did. This chapter is Vee alone getting ready, then Vee driving out, then the door opening, and for long stretches it's just her and her own nerve and her own body, and it's some of the most purely *her* writing in the book. The getting-ready sequence is wonderful: the underwear drawer, her hand going for the everyday bra out of habit, then correcting to the pretty lace, then the little jolt of realizing Pace is getting neither — "the one thing no bra in that drawer could beat." The shoes-first, shoes-only joke. "Last thing, only thing." The frosted-plum toes she kept up "over Christmas in her old bedroom for a man she'd no longer been sure would ever see them again." That line got me — it's the whole break's grief folded into a pedicure.

The heat is real and it's *hers*, which is what I loved. She winds herself up at the mirror and then denies herself on purpose — "It was not hers to finish" — because she wants to carry it out into the cold "wound exactly this tight" and put it in his hands. That's such a specific, generous erotic idea, and the book earns it by making her the author of it. Then the drive, the hand off the wheel — reckless, a little unhinged with wanting — and the cop lights that turn out to be for someone else. That fake-out is the one beat where I felt the author's thumb a little; it's a near-repeat of the campus-cop interruption from the hike, and I clocked it as *device*. But it also did its job: denied a second time, wound tighter, "no nearer anything that was hers to take."

The doorstep. I melted, fully. The wall coming down — "so plainly glad and so plainly wrecked" — "He looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look." That's the feeding motif and the being-seen motif landing in one image, and after fifty chapters of him going behind his face, watching it *fail* to hold against her body in the doorway is enormously satisfying. The sex is graphic and warm and a little graceless on purpose — the three hobbling steps toward a bed they don't reach — and the gracelessness is what makes it feel true rather than staged. It runs hot without going clinical.

And then the part that actually undid me isn't the sex — it's after. "You sure know how to make an entrance. Vee." Her own name, easy in his mouth, the same name he said as she walked out in December that *wasn't enough* then, and now it's enough, and she laughs and cries at once. Then the pancakes at midnight. Him going down on one knee by her chair — "I missed you so much," and his voice cracks and doesn't come back. That crack is the closest this man has come to the word, and the book is smart enough to know a cracked voice over a plate of pancakes says more than the three words would. I didn't need him to say it there. Neither did she — "there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." For once the hole at the center didn't ache. That's a real turn.

Where's my suspicion? Honestly, mostly dormant this chapter, and I'm going to be honest about that rather than manufacture dread. But it's not *gone* — it's parked one layer down, and the very completeness of this reconciliation is what keeps it warm. Vee thinks she authored this. I know Randi built the coat idea, chose the shoes, talked her past the shame word by word — "that's the bravest thing a woman can do." So the most triumphant, self-possessed night of Vee's life was, at the root, staged by the woman who's secretly Pace's lover and co-architect. The reconciliation is real; the strings are also real. And the Sunday-evening button — Vee back in the dorm in Pace's sweats and *Randi's black heels*, Cassie's dry "Hmm" — quietly reminds me that Randi's shoes carried her through the whole thing. Cassie, my clean instrument, raises the one eyebrow I'd raise. I love that she's still there.

**The titles.** "Nothing Underneath" — before reading I'd have guessed it meant the bare-under-the-coat gambit, and it does, literally. But by the end it recolors: nothing underneath the coat, yes, but also nothing held back, nothing kept in reserve — "nothing had hung back to be argued out of this later." The stain on the lining as proof she *meant* it. It's a lovely double. It didn't give anything away; it delivered exactly and then deepened. The volume line — WITH A LONG SPOON, BOOK ONE, "A POLITE INVITATION" — "long spoon" pings the old proverb, *sup with the devil, use a long spoon.* That sits under all of this like a cold hand: whoever's supping with a devil here, and whether Vee's holding a long enough spoon, I can't yet say — but naming the book that keeps my trust in this warmth provisional. "A polite invitation" reads almost sweet now and might not stay that way.

**What I want / dread next.** I want them to *stay* in this — and I distrust my own wanting, because the architecture Vee can't see is still standing. Guess from here: this reconciliation closes the "are they broken up" question and reopens the bigger ones — the word, the brunette, and whether Vee ever learns who really sent her out in that coat. I dread the moment those two facts meet.
