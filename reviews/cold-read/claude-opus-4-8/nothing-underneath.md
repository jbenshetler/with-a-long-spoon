# Cold read — Nothing Underneath

*scene: scenes/nothing-underneath.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: my-friend-randi*

## Reader reaction

So this is the door opening. The whole volume has been driving at this coat, and here it comes off, and I sat there feeling two things at once so hard they cancelled into a kind of nausea: the pull of the reunion — it's tender, it's hot, he goes to his knees, he cracks — and the certainty that I'm watching the trap close on a freely-given yes. That doubled feeling is the tagline made flesh. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." I have never felt a book cash a tagline this precisely.

What guts me is that I'm watching her seduce *herself* with a borrowed voice. Randi is literally in her head at the mirror — "take this into your own hands, you've got the goods, gorgeous girl" — and Vee "said it first in Randi's warm certainty and then in her own less certain voice, to see if it would bear weight." That's the whole operation in one sentence: the operator's line tried on until it fits like her own. The heels are Randi's. The plan is Randi's. The doubled "gorgeous" is right there, the harvest-tag she can't hear. And Vee experiences every bit of it as the bravest free thing she's ever done — "she had decided this, standing up, in her right mind." She's right that she decided it. She's wrong about whose hand pointed her. I could see both at once and it was awful.

The mirror sequence is the erotic peak of the whole book so far, and it's doing double work. She winds herself up and denies herself on purpose — "It was not hers to finish… She wanted him to be the one" — which is Pace's own patience-and-denial method turned inward, self-administered. She's internalized his machine and calls it desire. And her mother's word — the "slut" shame that's run under everything — "came up out of old habit and found nothing to hold to." Read as liberation, it's beautiful. Read with what I know, it's the last internal brake dissolving. The one voice that might have stopped her is gone, and the going is what dooms her.

The drive, the masturbation at the wheel, the police-car scare — I'll be honest, the cop was the one beat that felt like a set piece, a thriller jolt to wind her tighter, and it teetered toward too-much. But it earned its keep: it denies her a second time and delivers her to the door "wound exactly this tight," which is how the machine likes its offerings. The wet bloom soaked into the good coat lining — "it only told her she meant it" — the body telling the truth again, and her reading it as proof of consent rather than proof of the wind-up.

Then the door. And here's the thing I keep circling: the text hands me the *surface* — porch light off, door shut, "no one had left it open for her," Pace formal and careful, "It's late. Is everything alright?", the same December wall. His shock reads real: "the wall was simply gone… so plainly glad and so plainly wrecked." As a reader who's been told he and Randi are one hand, I don't know whether the surprise is genuine or whether the two hands moved without a specific heads-up, and the chapter deliberately won't resolve it. I hold both — the man who genuinely missed her ("all those weeks of it going out of him in one breath") and the co-conspirator whose door she was steered to. The book has trained me to hold both all volume, so I do.

And the blonde comes up in her head one last time on the doorstep — "The blonde arrived, tan and laughing in his kitchen, and could get no grip, because no man put on music like that for a woman." Sheri, warped, dismissed *again* — and dismissed for the wrong reason. It's the sad horn music that disarms the fear, not the truth. Vee gets the right conclusion (no other woman here tonight) off a wrong instrument, exactly as Randi taught her.

The real close of the trap, though, is quiet, and it's the withheld word. He says "Vee." Twice the text flags it — "the same name he'd said behind her in December… the one that hadn't been the word she'd come for then and hadn't been enough" — and now it *is* enough. He cracks: "I missed you so much," and his voice breaks. Beautiful. And STILL — even here, at the absolute peak, wrecked on one knee — **he does not say the word.** He gives her the name, the pancakes, the blanket, "Those stay on," and she has "come to the end of asking the man behind the wall to come out and answer her." That's it. That's the snap. She's stopped *needing* the word. "There was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." The trap isn't that he took her. It's that he's fed her into not wanting the one thing he withholds. The volume ends with the demand extinguished, and she calls it peace.

The pancakes are the apparatus one more time — made not poured, "made and not poured," the whole seduction-as-nourishment recited as love, and I clocked it and she never will. "Those stay on" — Randi's shoes, kept on his command: she leaves the dorm in *his* sweats and *Randi's* heels, dressed head to toe by the two hands, and can't see either. And Cassie — the one clean reader — takes it in in one pass, cocks an eyebrow, "Hmm," goes back to her book. Sees the shape, not the facts. The clear eye in the room, and even it only gets the outline.

Where the book leaves me: not on a horror beat but on contentment, which is far worse. Vee is *happy*. Randi still holds the undelivered nude and inventory. The word is still unsaid and no longer wanted. The trap closed softly, with syrup. I dread the next volume and I want it immediately, which is, I suppose, the same feeling Vee had on that county road.
