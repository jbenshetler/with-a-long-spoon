## Reader reaction

This is the doorstep, and it delivered — I've been leaning toward it for two chapters and it paid off without a false step. What surprised me is how much of it happens *before* Pace even opens the door. Almost the whole chapter is Vee alone: waking, showering, doing her face, dressing in nothing, the mirror, the drive. And it's the best kind of solo — the erotic charge is very much working on me, but it's braided so tightly to her *deciding* that I never felt like the book was killing time. The mirror scene where she brings herself right to the edge and then stops, on purpose, "denied by no one but herself," because "she wanted him to be the one" — that's the whole thesis of her body in one gesture, and it made me ache for her and admire her at once.

The stuff I loved most is small and domestic and true. The underwear drawer — habit reaching for the good everyday bra, then *correcting* to the pretty lace for Pace, before she catches up and realizes "Pace was getting neither." The little joke of a girl putting her shoes on last, always, and here putting them on first and only: "last thing, only thing." The frosted plum toes she'd touched up "even over Christmas in her old bedroom for a man she'd no longer been sure would ever see them again." That detail wrecked me a little — the month of silence sitting inside a bottle of nail polish.

Kayla finally appears (I'd been told she was coming) and she's exactly the right note of horror-comedy: the girl who can talk the paint off a wall, running a *laundry report about washing a week of underwear* at a woman standing there wearing none of it, bare under the wool. "If there were any justice she'd be struck where she stood." Funny and merciless. And the cop — the blue-and-red coming up behind her while her hand is between her legs at speed on a county road, the flat certainty of *caught*, and then the cruiser blows past after something real. That scare tightening the screw instead of releasing it, leaving "a dark bloom of herself soaked into the good coat" she'd once have died of shame over and tonight reads as proof she meant it. That's the chapter's cleverest move: every interruption *winds her tighter* instead of cooling her, so by the time she knocks she's a live wire.

Pace. He opens the door and does the exact thing I feared — the wall, the courtesy, "It's late. Is everything alright?" — the same December formality. And Vee's answer, that she's "come to the end of asking the man behind the wall to come out and answer her" and "had brought something the wall could not refuse," is the payoff of Randi's whole coaching: don't ask for the word, hand him the thing words can't argue with. And it works. The wall goes down "all at once," and what's under it is "so plainly glad and so plainly wrecked" — the starving-man/forgiven-man double look. The sex on the doorstep with the door banging open and the cold on her wet nipples, him on his knees on cold boards, is graphic and sustained and it landed — heat all the way up. It didn't feel clinical because it kept doubling her two ends (his mouth below, the January air above) and kept the emotional stakes present.

And then — the thing I've been watching for a month of story-time — he still doesn't say *the word*. He says her *name*. "Vee." Muffled against her breast, teasing. And the book is completely honest that this is the same name he said behind her in December when it "hadn't been enough," and now it undoes her into laughing-crying. So he gave her everything — the wall down, the kiss with "all the missing in it," his knees, the pancakes at midnight, "I missed you so much" with his voice cracking and not coming back — *everything except the word.* I noticed that. She got him back; she did not get the thing she went to the door in December to demand. The chapter lets her be so full there's "no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given," and I believe her happiness completely — and I still clocked the hole in it. That's the trap doing its quiet work.

The pancakes closing it out is pure Pace: build the hunger, then give the plate, "made and not poured." And "Those stay on" about the heels — Randi's heels, staying on in his kitchen — is a small, hot, possessive beat. Which is the one faint chill in an otherwise warm chapter: Randi is *all over this night*. Her voice in the shower ("you've got the goods, gorgeous girl"), her heels on Vee's feet through the whole thing including the reconciliation fuck, her plan executed to the letter. Vee drives out "doing the exact thing Randi had told her to do" and it works flawlessly. I keep feeling the third person in the room who isn't in the room.

The coda is perfect and dry: Vee back Sunday in the coat, his rolled-up sweats, and Randi's heels — "the outfit told the entire story before she'd got the door shut" — and Cassie's unhurried eyebrow and "Hmm." Trust Cassie to say everything with a monosyllable.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** nothing on the page turned me against Pace tonight — his gladness reads real. But the withheld word is now conspicuous by its survival: he gave a wrecked, unguarded night and *still* couldn't/didn't say it, and I no longer think that's an accident of the moment. And Randi's fingerprints on a night this intimate keep the dramatic irony live and faintly sinister under all the warmth.

**Titles.** "Nothing Underneath" — on the surface it's literal, the naked-under-the-coat fact, and it's charged all chapter. On second thought it recolors: *nothing underneath* the plan but Randi's engineering; nothing underneath the reconciliation but the same unspoken *can't*; the coat "decent over" a wound. It's the coat-as-thesis motif named outright. As for the book title, *With a Long Spoon* still sits oblique to me — "sup with the devil, use a long spoon"? — and tonight fits the tagline exactly: every yes here was hers, freely and even joyfully given, the mirror, the drive, the open coat, all her own decision, "in her right mind" — and that's precisely why it's the trap. She's never been more the author of her own capture than tonight.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the two worlds to touch. Everything is now so complete for Vee that the only place left to go is the collision — Vee finding out Randi and Pace are connected, or seeing the bench, or the word finally landing (or being asked for again and refused a second time). *Guess from here:* the very completeness of this night is the setup for the fall; a happiness this total in a book called a trap is a floor about to give.
