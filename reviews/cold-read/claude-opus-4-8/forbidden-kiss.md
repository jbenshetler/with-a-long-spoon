### Forbidden Kiss — Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one got me in the body first and worried me second, and I want to be honest that the worry took a while to arrive — the chapter earned a long, uncomplicated swoon before it turned the knife, and I let myself have the swoon.

**Vee.** God, I loved being inside her here. The morning-after opening — her hand going out "before she was anyone" and finding cold cotton, the December lurch, then his voice arriving from the night before, *farmers market in the morning, sleep as long as you like* — that's the whole reconciliation still glowing, and I felt her heart "come down off its ledge" with her. What delighted me most is watching her *play*. The "pleasure slave" thought that turns itself inside out into "pleasure princess" — "Not the kind of woman who is kept for a man's pleasure. The kind a man keeps *in* pleasure" — is the most awake, most un-ashamed I've ever seen her, and it's her doing it to herself, counting his devotions off on her fingers (he brings her off first, he burned the toast because she exists) until she's laughing naked in a hallway. After fifty chapters of the mother's voice riding the same wire as her heat, watching her arrive at *joy* on her own body's terms felt like a genuine graduation. The cashmere laid smooth over the foot of the bed before he's even home — that little stage-managing — made me grin. She's not just being swept up anymore; she's directing.

**Pace.** The single best image in the chapter is him stopping in his own doorway, a bag in each arm, and losing his words: "a man who always had the right words — few, and exact — stand in his own doorway with none at all," reassembling his composure "piece by piece like a man picking up dropped change," and landing on "I got mushrooms." I melted. And then the cooking — the blood oranges done "the way he did everything," the knife riding the curve, "nothing between anybody and the fruit," which she reads as "the most him thing she had ever seen done to food." I have always half-distrusted Pace's care because it's so total it looks like control, but here the care is so specifically rendered — the salt on the persimmon that "wakes the fruit up," the toast burned because he can't stop looking at her — that I let the distrust go slack and just enjoyed a man undone.

**The swoon / the heat.** The oral sequence is the hottest sustained stretch the book has given me. It runs on her *pointing* — the finger at her wrist, her nipple, "lower," the center of herself — a whole erotic grammar of command that keeps failing her because the wanting keeps outrunning the performance ("her hand was less languid about it than she meant it to be"). The long, cruel travel down to her feet and *back*, kissing her painted toes, "the destination beyond all doubt and the arrival nowhere in sight" — I was as strung out as she was. And the thing the book does that I trust most: it did not skip her shame or pardon it. "He did not skip past it or pardon it or wait for it to thin. He was drinking it." That the "give of her that came whether she permitted it or not" is met with *appetite* rather than tolerance — that's the exact tenderness-under-heat I read this book for. It burned all the way through.

**Where it turned, and where the page earned unease.** Then the forbidden kiss. He comes up and kisses her carrying the wet of her, "his mouth wetter than it had ever once come to her," and her *tongue goes to it first* — "a small live reach toward the taste before any part of her had ruled on it... curious, unforbidden, hers" — before "the flat clean voice that had been installed in her before she could have argued with it" slams it shut: "Ugh — gross. That's — no. Wrong," the back of the hand, the child's gesture. That's the mother, and it broke my heart a little that the one place she still can't follow her own yes is *taste*. And I loved that he "did not push it and he did not sulk and he did not explain" — "Okay," and moved on, leaving her mouth for her until she came and took it back herself.

But underneath the sweet reading she gives it, the page planted something cold, and I have to name it because the text truly earned it, not because I was on guard: her tongue, hunting in his mouth *after*, finds "a faint edge of sharp and salt that had not been there this morning. Or found nothing." And again on her own lip afterward, "only salt or the memory of salt, already gone." The chapter keeps this scrupulously ajar — "if it had been there at all" — and I'm going to leave it ajar, but I can't un-feel it: he was *out* this morning, and something tasted different when he came back, and I have known since page one that there is another woman who is not a rival Vee invented. The chapter never says the word. It just lets her tongue keep going back to a place she's decided not to look — "how many times, and how carefully, and had there always been —" and lets her file it under *he forgot to wipe, this once*. That rationalization, arriving "tidy and kind and she took it, grateful," is the most unsettling thing in the chapter precisely because I watched her *choose* it over what her mouth already suspected. The book's whole engine — being loved and handled at once, the yes that's real and the knowing she won't let herself have — is right there in a single involuntary lick.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** My suspicion isn't of Pace's feeling — I believe the tenderness completely, it's not performed here. It's of the *fact* the taste points at, and of the machinery still running under all this warmth. And the last beat sharpened it: she'll tell Randi everything on Tuesday, "the strange small thing" included, because "Randi would know what to make of it. Randi always did." I felt the floor drop. She is going to carry the one true clue she has straight to the person least able to be trusted with it — and hand it over as a puzzle for the friend who helped build the whole thing. That's dread, clean and earned.

**Friction.** Very little. If anything, the "pleasure princess / pleasure slave / gladiator" riffing runs a hair long and self-pleased in the middle stretch — "A gladiator, once. Her beauty had turned him" — but it's *her* interior giddiness, so I forgave it as character rather than thumb-on-scale. Nothing felt telegraphed except the salt, and that was placed with real restraint, held open rather than underlined.

**The titles.** "Forbidden Kiss" — on the surface it's the taste of herself, the mother's *that's not a thing you do*, the one door in her own body she still won't walk through. On reread it tilts: the *forbidden* thing may not be her own taste at all but what's mixed with it — the kiss that carries a trace of someone she doesn't know to look for. The title told me before I started that a kiss would be the hinge, which kept me a little alert to her mouth throughout; I don't think it gave the game away, but it pointed cleanly at the crux. The volume title **A Warm Reception** and series **With a Long Spoon** — the "warm reception" is literal all chapter (the house that keeps the cold on the far side of the glass, the man kept warm for whoever he loves), but "long spoon" (you sup with the devil with a long spoon) keeps its cold hand on my shoulder: the warmth is real *and* you'd best keep your distance. The tagline — "Vee is a willing player in two games. She doesn't know there are three." — landed hard right here, because this whole chapter is her playing her *one* game (the coat, the princess, the pointing) beautifully, gloriously, all while a third thing sits "minor-key, waiting" under her affection and she pets it back to sleep.

**What I want / dread next.** I want her to keep the joy — the princess who found out she was never the slave. I dread Tuesday's dinner. *Guess from here:* the salt is another woman, and Vee is about to narrate her one real clue across a little table to Randi, who will "know what to make of it" — i.e. will make it disappear, the way she made the silence into a proud boy holding still. I'm braced for Randi to smooth this over so gently Vee thanks her for it.
