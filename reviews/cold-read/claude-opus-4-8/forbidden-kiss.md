# Cold read (grounded) — forbidden-kiss

## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one melted me for most of its length and then, in the last three pages, quietly slid the knife in — and I mean that as praise. It's the morning after the reconciliation, and for once the book just lets Vee be *happy*, luxuriously, top to bottom, and I was happy right alongside her. Waking to the cold sheet and the half-second lurch back to December, then his voice from the night before arriving to bring her heart "down off its ledge" — that's the whole fracture healing in one paragraph, and it earned my relief. I've spent so many chapters braced for her that being handed a chapter where she gets to bask felt like a gift I hadn't known I was owed.

The "pleasure slave / pleasure princess" bit is the most purely delighted I've ever seen her, and it delighted me. The reasoning is genuinely good — she counts it off on her fingers, *he brings me off first, he rises in the dark to bake me bread, he's out in the January cold buying my food* — and lands on "Not the kind of woman who is kept for a man's pleasure. The kind a man keeps *in* pleasure." I love that she got there by herself, that her own body-joy did the arithmetic and "got happiness for an answer every time." After fifty chapters of the mother's voice riding the wire, watching her stand naked in a warm hallway "sugaring with laughter" was a real payoff.

**Pace.** This is Pace at his most disarming, and I felt the pull hard. Him stopping dead in the doorway, groceries in both arms, the color climbing his throat, "Whoa—" and then the sentence never arriving — a man who always has the exact word standing there with none — and then reassembling himself "piece by piece like a man picking up dropped change" only to land on "I got mushrooms." I laughed out loud with her. The whole cooking sequence is a seduction played through knifework, and the blood orange — "he was opening her as he had opened the orange, nothing hurried, nothing skipped, everything in the order the fruit demanded" — is the chapter telling you exactly how it intends to make love, and then making love exactly that way. The breakfast reads as tender and generous, and the going-down that follows is patient to the point of cruelty in the best sense: the feet, the toes, the traveling-away-from-where-she-needs-him. My trust in his *care* is total in this chapter. He is, in the room, unimpeachably good to her.

**The heat.** It's a 3 and it ran the whole back half. The pointing game — her commanding him with one finger, wrist then breast then lower — is the eroticism the jacket keeps promising: choosing as the hottest act. The reversal where *she* runs the clockwork, banks his half-beat-late turns "like earnings," burns the toast on purpose — that's her power fully weaponized and joyful about it, and it worked on me completely. The oral is genuinely, unhurriedly hot, and crucially it kept the shame-as-heat wire live: "the old fused thing rose in her, the cringe and the heat one arrival, indivisible... He was drinking it. His palms held her open and nothing in them asked her to be any less." That's the book's whole thesis about her body in one image and it's the hottest thing on the page.

**And then the turn.** The kiss. He comes up, kisses her, and the slick of her is on his mouth "wetter than it had ever once come to her" — and her tongue goes to it *before her mind arrives*, "a small live reach toward the taste... her mouth answering a question it had not been asked, curious, unforbidden, hers." And then the installed voice — "no, that's wrong, that's not a thing you do" — shuts it down, the back-of-the-hand child's gesture, "Ugh — gross. That's — no. Wrong." I felt that whole sequence in my chest. The tongue said yes and the mother said no and the no won by half a second. That's not a betrayal of the swoon; it *is* the meaning, exactly the interior life under the wanting I read for. And his response — no push, no sulk, no explanation, "something in his face gentled toward her as if she had done something young and dear" — is him at his most careful, and I trusted it.

But here's where the chapter earns real unease, and this is the first time in a while the *text itself* has handed me a suspicion rather than my carrying one in. Two things Vee lays down at the end and can't quite make lie flat. First: *he liked it.* "Not tolerated it... *liked* it, gone down glad and stayed glad and come up wanting to bring it to her mouth like a good thing he did not see why she would not want. Why does he like it so much." Second, sharper, the one she reasons away: he *always* kisses her after, "dozens... hundreds," and it has "never once been like *that*" — so he must have "forgotten to wipe. This once." And her tongue, unbidden, hunting on his lip earlier, and on her own lip now, for "salt or the memory of salt," finding "maybe" a sharp edge "that had not been there this morning. Or found nothing." 

That's the chapter quietly telling me something Vee is working very hard not to add up: he has been wiping his mouth, carefully, every single time, for months — so she'd never taste herself — and today the once he didn't, her body *leapt* at it before shame caught it. I don't know what that means and the page holds it ajar — is it just his courtesy, protecting her from her own installed disgust? Or is it a managed thing, one more calibrated door he's holding open at exactly the right width until she walks through on her own? I can't resolve it and I won't. But it settled in me "minor-key, waiting," same as it did in her, and that's the most genuinely unsettled I've been in the actual reading rather than in my dread.

**The last beat** tightened it further. Tuesday, Randi across the little table, wanting every detail — "whose mad gorgeous idea it had been, who had handed her the play like a queen lending a sword." And the strange small thing, the taste — "she'd just say it anyway. Randi would know what to make of it. Randi always did." I felt that as a cold little drop. The one confusion Vee can't metabolize she's going to carry straight to the person the reader knows is the least neutral possible listener — the woman who is Pace's partner, who edges these confessions open for a living, who is in love with her. The chapter that gave Vee her happiest morning ends by walking her, smiling, back toward the apparatus. That's the merciless-tenderness the jacket promised, and it landed.

**Friction.** Very little. The "pleasure slave / pleasure princess" riff runs maybe one beat long — "some slave," "pleasure princess," "kept *in* pleasure" — I got the distinction the first time and it circled it three times. But it's so in-character for her giddiness that I forgave it. Nothing felt like the author's thumb; the ending questions are planted lightly enough that they read as *her* not-looking rather than the book nudging me.

**The title.** "Forbidden Kiss" — before reading I assumed some illicit lip-meeting, maybe finally Randi. Read now it's exactly literal and much better than literal: the forbidden kiss is *herself on his mouth*, the taste her mother forbade her before she could argue, the yes her tongue gave before her mind could veto it. It illuminates rather than gives away — I didn't see it coming and it recolored the whole oral sequence in hindsight. The one small thing it does spoil is that it told me a kiss would go wrong somehow, so I was faintly waiting for the shoe; but it didn't rob the moment. Against **A Polite Invitation** and **With a Long Spoon** — that spoon phrase ("who sups with the devil needs a long spoon") keeps getting louder every chapter that the feeding is this good and this managed; here the whole seduction is literally at a table, spoon-fed, and I'm the one being asked whether all this warmth is safe to eat. The tagline, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," pointed like a laser at this chapter's central beat: her tongue's freely-given yes to the taste, shut down by the installed no. Every door here was left open for her to choose, and choosing was the whole erotics of it — and that's exactly what's starting to frighten me.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the Tuesday lunch, badly, and I dread it. Guess from here, marked as a guess: Vee brings the taste-thing to Randi, and Randi — who wants precisely this door opened — does something with it, either steers her toward wanting to taste *Randi*, or reframes the disgust away so smoothly that Vee sheds the last of the mother's voice right into Randi's hands. I also can't stop turning over "why does he like it so much" — the page put that question in play deliberately, and I don't think it's nothing.
