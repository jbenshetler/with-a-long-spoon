## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This one melted me and then, in its last few pages, put a cold pin through the melting — and it did both so quietly that I trust it completely. It's the morning after the reconciliation, and for most of its length it is just *happiness*, which is a hard thing to make readable, and this chapter makes happiness readable. Vee waking with her hand across the wide cold bed, the December lurch, and then his voice arriving from the night before — *farmers market in the morning, sleep as long as you like* — I was already soft. The bread timed to her waking. The sweats set out and left unworn. "Even his absence had been cooked in advance." I love how the chapter lets her be *giddy*, alone, naked, laughing at herself in the hall — I've watched this woman flinch at her own body for fifty chapters and here she shakes her shoulders "just to feel everything move" and calls herself magnificent, and I felt the whole book's ache turn over into joy for a minute. The "pleasure slave / no, *pleasure princess*" turn is genuinely charming and, more than charming, it's the shame apparatus finally running *backward* — she counts his devotions on her fingers and comes out sovereign. I rooted for her the whole way.

And the seduction game is the best pure-heat sequence I've had from these two, because for once *she's* running it. She's the one leaving the room to choose now — the "May I" grammar has migrated into her hands. The burnt-toast game, the breast dragged along his cheek at the sink (a deliberate callback to the fitting, and she *knows* it), the wagged finger, the pointing — pointing at her wrist, her nipple, "lower," at the center of herself with a finger that isn't steady anymore, "and it was not a command now and both of them knew that too." That line undid me. The whole erotic architecture is her pretending to reign while the wanting gets ahead of the performance, "somewhere in those few yards she stopped being able to tell the performance from the wanting." The oral is graphic and sustained and it *worked* on me — the "opening her as he had opened the orange, nothing hurried, nothing skipped," the breath-only over open flesh, the groan that "arrived not in her ears but in her flesh." Heat at a 3, and warm, not clinical.

Then the title lands, and it's not the kiss I thought it was. "Forbidden Kiss" — I'd assumed, going in, something to do with Randi (the goodbye kiss on the mouth is the book's standing forbidden thing). Instead it's Pace kissing her afterward and her tasting *herself* on his mouth — and her tongue reaching for the taste, "curious, unforbidden, hers," a beat of pure appetite — before "the flat clean voice that had been installed in her before she could have argued with it" slams the door: *ugh, gross, that's wrong.* The mother's wire, live as ever, riding right on top of the heat. That's the chapter's real hinge, and it's beautifully done: her body says yes to the taste of her own body before her mind and then the shame arrives *third,* after the yes.

But what the chapter is actually *for* — and here's where the warmth turned, honestly, cold — is the thing she's left holding at the end. Not her own shame. *His pleasure.* "Because he had liked it. That was the thing that would not lie flat." He didn't tolerate it, he wanted to bring it up to her mouth "like a good thing." And then the smaller, worse noticing: he didn't kiss her mouth again, all the rest of the way — "only the one door, closed the moment she had closed it." And her tidy, grateful explanation — he must have forgotten to wipe, *this once* — is so plainly a lie she's telling herself that the prose lets me watch her choose it. And then her tongue goes out over her own lip, "looking for something," and she catches it, and "did not ask herself what it had been looking for." God. That last movement is the interior life I read for — she's editing herself in real time, and the question she won't ask "settled somewhere under the affection and stayed, minor-key, waiting." The chapter is warm all the way through and quietly *merciless* right at the end, exactly as advertised.

I have no new suspicion of *Pace* here — his tenderness read as real, and his "okay," low against her jaw, no push and no sulk, was one of the most trustworthy things he's done. What unsettles me isn't him; it's the machinery of *her*, the practiced ease with which she reaches for the kind explanation and puts the true question in a corner. That's the handling she does to herself, and it rhymes with the handling done to her.

And then the last beat — Tuesday, anthro, and *Randi across a little table wanting every last detail.* "She would tell her everything. Well. Nearly everything." That landed on me like a cold hand. Because I know what Vee doesn't: that Randi is Pace's, that these brunches are reconnaissance as much as love. Vee is going to carry this strange small thing — this thing about a woman's *mouth* and where it's been and whether she'd taste it — straight to the one woman she won't let herself know she wants, and hand it over trusting Randi will "know what to make of it." "Randi always did." That's the chapter's quietest and most dangerous line. The forbidden kiss she recoiled from is her own; the one she's walking toward, without a word admitting it, has Randi's name on it.

**Friction:** almost none. It's a long chapter for a domestic morning and one sex scene, but it earned the length for me — the breakfast is not filler, it's the whole ethic of him rendered in food, and I'd have missed any of it. If I press: the "pleasure princess" riff runs a hair long, and "sugaring with laughter" is a phrase I noticed twice-cousin to itself. Tiny. The cooking is so lovingly done it flirts with food-writing indulgence, but the knife-work rhythm doubles as the seduction clock, so I forgave it.

**Titles:** *Forbidden Kiss* is the rare one that names the literal event and *still* keeps a second charge in reserve — on the page it's the taste of herself; on reflection it points down the hall to the goodbye kiss she keeps calling "French." *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* keep gathering weight: "sup with a long spoon" is what you do with the devil, and the closing turn toward Randi's little table is exactly a long-spoon supper Vee thinks is just lunch. The tagline — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — sat right on this chapter: every yes here *was* hers, the game was hers, the coat was her play; and the one "no" she uttered was the installed one, not hers at all.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the Tuesday lunch, badly, and I dread it. *Guess from here:* Vee tells Randi the strange small thing, and Randi — who has decided to let Vee arrive on her own — does something with it. I don't think Vee stays innocent of her own appetite much longer; the tongue that reached for the taste is the same one that leans into the goodbye kiss.
