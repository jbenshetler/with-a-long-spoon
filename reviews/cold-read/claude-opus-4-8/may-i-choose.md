# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This one worked on me and I hated a little that it did. On the page it's pure swoon: the shower and the shave "that meant *for this*," the boots he already looks after instead of new ones bought to impress, the walk on the street side without making anything of it, the little brass cages for glasses too hot to hold. He hands her a whole evening with the demand built out of it — *Saturday. Dinner.* — and leaves her the room to carry it around. I felt the ease of that surrender right alongside Vee. The froyo chapter had already softened me on him; this dinner deepened it. The kiss at the end — her rule spoken and then let go, "I'm making an exception," him leaving the last inch for her twice — is genuinely lovely, and the grin coming up in her mouth mid-kiss made me grin.

And I can't sit in it, because I know what Vee doesn't. I watched Pace do this exact thing to Randi — feed the girl who portions herself, wait her out, look *past the face* until she comes out from behind it — and I watched Randi flee back behind her own face and decide *not ever*, and then turn around and hand Vee to him. "She built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop" was reported *to Randi* two chapters ago; now I'm inside the cup being built. So the whole tender apparatus reads double: every kindness is also a technique I've already seen run to completion on someone else. "Somewhere in there she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong… that low background readiness she carried everywhere… and it just wasn't there." I wanted that for her and dreaded it in the same breath, because dropping the guard is precisely the door he walks through.

So: trust vs. suspicion. My suspicion isn't of *this scene* — the scene is honestly warm, and I think he honestly likes her. It's structural dread the earlier chapters earned outright. The thing that chilled me most was tiny: "It was the right hand again, a pattern now… some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it, one more thing he knew that she didn't." Rendered as charming mystery to Vee; to me it's the recurring note that *this man runs on rules and knows things his partner doesn't*, and the not-knowing is the point. Same with her not being able to reconstruct a word Randi said at Chi Latte — Vee keeps losing the transcript and keeping only the feeling. She's being managed by two very good people and can't hear it.

Erotic charge: it runs at a warm simmer that climbs. It peaks at the hand — "less like something beginning than something resolving," her hand wrong all its life for want of his — and at the final kiss. The food is where it's most alive: the bright green thing and the smoke-dark thing, "one to wake you and one to fold you under," her mouth watering "at promises she couldn't have read aloud." That's the eating-as-hunger motif from Randi's apple, turned up. It's hot without a stitch coming off.

Friction: almost none, which is itself a small friction — the prose is so competent at making the seduction land that I felt the machinery humming under it. The one place the thumb pressed a touch hard was the doubling-up of "not-noticing": she stops watching herself eat, stops hearing the room, stops keeping the thread, stops waiting for it to go wrong — four variations on *her guard is down* in a row. Effective, but I saw it being done.

The title. "May I Choose" — three words he says at the table, asking to order for her, and the relief of handing the choice across "went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." On the page it's the sweetest beat: courtesy, care, the *may* she also heard in "may I call you." But it recolored the second I set it against what I know. The real choosing in this book isn't Vee's — it's his and Randi's, choosing *her*, from the quad, off a pointing game. "May I choose" is the polite form the taking wears. **A Polite Invitation**, the volume line, and the series **With a Long Spoon** — *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* That proverb sitting over all this niceness tells me the tenderness is the spoon, not the meal. I'm not reassured; I'm braced.

What I want / dread next (guesses from here): I expect the threesome Randi engineered to start closing in — Vee falling for Pace *and* adoring Randi, not knowing they're a unit, not knowing about the bench. I dread the moment Vee learns Randi and Pace are together, and dread more that she might not care by then. And I keep thinking about Cassie's flat *"Goodbye, Vee"* — the one person who reads the room straight. I want her to be the alarm that goes off. I'll keep reading fast.
