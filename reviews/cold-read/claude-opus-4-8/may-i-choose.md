## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is the froyo date's grown-up cousin, and it worked on me almost against my better judgment. My protectiveness for Vee is louder than ever, because everything that makes this chapter *lovely* is also everything I've been trained by the pool chapter to fear.

The opening beat with Cassie is the thing I most trust. "That's a face. That's a whole face." — Cassie is still the clean spot, still the one who reads Vee true without prying, and her verdict "That's not a boy. That's a man" is doing double work for me. Vee hears it as the highest praise. I hear it as the exact hook. What Vee's actually falling for is the *withholding of pressure* — "The other ones always made you answer. Right then, on the phone... So they'd know where they stood. He just left it with me. Like it could keep." That's the whole seduction in one line, and it's chilling precisely because it's *true*: he did give her the room. The kindness is real and it is also the technique. I can't separate them and neither can she, and that's the trap the jacket keeps promising.

The elevator/lobby bit — "he'd showered... that color that meant *recently*, that meant *for this*" — is doing the appraise-by-cost motif inverted. Vee, who's been graded her whole life on what things *cost*, is now reading care off a man who wears the *real* things, "nothing bought to impress her." She reads authenticity. And it lands on me as: this man is calibrated to be legible to exactly her wounds. He wore the boots that had actually lived because the boys who buy new shoes to fake it is *her* whole social world. It's too perfect. It's perfect the way a lure is perfect.

The dinner is where the erotic charge lives, and it's all displaced onto food and hands. "May I choose?" — there's the chapter title, the "May I" formality that keeps rhyming with the book. And her "Yes. Please." The surrender of the menu is played as pure relief, and honestly it *is* erotic on the page — the handing-across of choice, the letting-him-carry-it. But I clocked hard what she names as bliss: "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was... She let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could." Every good thing in this scene is a *relinquishing*. The pool chapter told me water was the one place she was never taken from. Here she is, on land, *volunteering* to stop watching herself, to let someone else hold the choices, the words, the direction. It's the sweetest thing that's happened to her and it's her giving away the guard.

The hands. "It was the right hand again, a pattern now... always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." I don't know what that means and I don't think Vee does either — she files it under "one more thing he knew that she didn't," charming. From the reader's chair, after the bench, "some consistent rule he kept" on a man this deliberate reads faintly like discipline, ritual, a thing withheld. Maybe it's nothing. But it's the one small note in the room that felt *off* in a way the text put there on purpose.

The ending is genuinely good and I felt it in my chest despite everything. The reversal — she recites the rule ("I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days"), he doesn't argue, doesn't lean in, doesn't do "the patient little wait for her to change her mind" — and *that* non-move is what makes her not go. "She didn't go. Her feet stayed where they were." Then the pull on the taut hand, and "But... I'm making an exception." The point the chapter is making — and making beautifully — is that his refusal to push is the most effective push. She has to walk it back *herself*, so it's hers, freely. Every yes was freely given. That was the trap. I have literally never felt that tagline more than right here. She chooses it, entirely, and I dread it entirely.

Where it went slack: nowhere much, but the middle food-reverie ran a hair long — the "bright thing and the dark thing, one to wake you and one to fold you under" is lovely and then the softness-going-soft-at-the-edges paragraphs pile a little, three or four different phrasings of "she stopped watching herself." I got it the first time. Small thumb-on-scale.

The one line I half-resisted: "less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his exactly." Gorgeous, and *almost* too composed a thought for a girl mid-swoon — it's the narrator naming the destiny-feeling a touch neatly. But it's close enough to her interiority that I let it stand.

Trust/suspicion: no new *evidence* against Pace here — he does nothing but be kind. That's exactly the problem, and I carry the bench and the phone-taking and Randi with me, so the kindness reads as instrument. The absence of Randi from two chapters running (she killed the wait-a-day rule in ch.7, then vanished) is starting to feel like a held breath — she engineered the froyo, and this dinner runs *without* her thumbprint visible, which almost makes it feel more like Pace working solo, or Randi working invisibly.

**Title** — "May I Choose" is his line, the menu surrender, and on the reread it's the whole chapter: the erotics of Vee handing over choice, dressed as his courtesy. It points right at the tagline. It gave nothing away; it deepened on landing.

What I want/dread next: the kiss lands, and then — the house. We've had froyo, now proper dinner, and Randi's been offstage too long. I *guess from here* the next escalation folds Randi back in, or moves toward Pace's place. And I keep waiting for the water to get touched.
