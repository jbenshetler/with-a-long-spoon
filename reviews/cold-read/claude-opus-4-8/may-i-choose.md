# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This chapter just worked on me, cleanly and almost entirely as swoon — and the fact that it worked is the part I want to sit with honestly, because I know things Vee doesn't, and the pleasure of the scene runs right alongside a low dread the page never once mentions.

Taken purely as a first date, it's lovely. The whole architecture is the thing I love in the good version of this: a man who builds room into everything and hands it across the table. The text Saturday that "he'd built the quiet into it without being asked" — I felt that ease it something behind her ribs the same way it eased something behind mine. The froyo warmth carried straight in. And then the two title beats, "May I choose?" and later "May I" (last chapter's) and here "Yes. Please." — I melted at the surrender of the menu, at "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would." That's the erotic engine the jacket promised — choosing, and being permitted, as the charge — rendered small and domestic over baba ghanoush, and it landed.

The eating is where it peaked for me emotionally. Vee "wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." After a whole run of chapters of her mother's voice policing every bite, of the cardigan-over-the-better-shirt, of the apple sliced thin — watching the hollow just open and get fed, watching the braced-for-the-bill part of her go quiet and her not even notice it went, I ached for her. The hunger being answered rather than managed is the tenderest thing this book has shown me from her side, and I wanted her to have it.

And the hand outside. "It felt less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe, for want of his." That's a gorgeous line and it swept me. The ending — the rule offered and then broken, "I'm making an exception," her feet not going when he gave her the out — is exactly the consent-as-heat this book keeps promising: he doesn't argue, doesn't lean, doesn't turn up the charm, just lets it close and starts them walking, and she is the one who stops him. She chooses. It's genuinely hot precisely because he made it fully hers to refuse.

So why the dread. Because I am not naive about Pace, and the book has spent chapters making sure I'm not. I have watched this exact courtliness — the "May I," the room-to-choose, the leaving-the-quiet-in-it — deployed as instrument. I sat inside Randi on the bench, took her all the way to the place where the polished surface broke, and then watched her decide, quiet as a deadbolt, never to come out again. I watched "the pointing game," where Pace pointed and Randi picked, and the girl at the dining-hall doors — Vee, the redhead — was the last point, the "her," the yes with a name and a stats class. So I know, walking into this dinner, that everything Vee is reading as "he just knew this street," "he built the quiet in without being asked," is a competence I've seen aimed. The froyo, the "may I," the deliberate not-asking-that-night — I've watched the grammar of it work on someone else and cost her.

That doesn't sour the chapter for me — and I want to be precise, because I don't want to manufacture a shadow the prose is fighting. Inside Vee's POV there is nothing off. She reads him right, actually; her econ eye clocked in the last chapter that he "kept himself out of it so the solving stayed hers," and here she's just receiving the same care as care. The unease is entirely mine, carried in from outside her frame, and the chapter is honest enough not to wink at me. There's no thumb on the scale, no line where the author nudges me to be afraid. If anything the restraint is the tell — this is the most purely warm, least ironized chapter in a while, and after the bench I read that warmth as the calm the book gives you right before it collects. "Nothing was taken from her that she did not hand over," the jacket said. This is a chapter of her handing things over, gladly, and me watching her do it knowing there was a plan she can't see.

On Pace specifically: my feeling hasn't moved so much as doubled. On the page he is exactly the man I'd want across that table — the right hand every time, the tearing of hot bread, the listening that waits a sentence out. And I cannot un-know that I've seen this precise repertoire produce a woman crying and locking a bathroom door. He remains, to me, the most unsettling kind of dangerous: not cruel, genuinely attentive, and using the attention. I trust his hands and not his project.

Cassie is the one clean thing. "That's not a boy. That's a man" — she teases Vee about everything and doesn't tease her about this, and I trust that instinct completely. She's the reader's ally, the flat true voice, and after her odd finished "Goodbye, Vee" two chapters back I'm holding a small worry for her — that she sees more of the shape than she can say, or that she's being edged out of Vee's brighter new life.

Erotic charge: it's the simmering register, a 1 — no sex, but the froyo/dinner/hand-holding tension is doing real work, and the menu surrender and the final kiss-exception are charged. It peaked at "May I choose?" and at the withheld hand outside.

Titles: "May I Choose" is the chapter's spine and it's doing double duty on me. On the surface it's his three words about the menu, and it's tender. Underneath, with the pointing game fresh — where Randi chose and Pace pointed — the word choose rings loud and cold. Who chooses whom here? Vee thinks she's choosing (the exception, the yes). I know she was chosen, off a dining-hall walk, as the endpoint of a game. So the title sits oblique and a little cruel: her felt experience of choosing freely is real, and it's happening inside a choice already made about her. That's the whole jacket thesis compressed into three words at a dinner table. "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" — the spoon is the one you sup with the devil with, and this chapter is Christine's little dishes and the mint tea and the froyo, all of it food, all of it warm, all of it him feeding her. Long spoon, long table. The tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — this chapter is a catalogue of freely given yeses, and it's the first time the tagline felt less like a warning and more like a description of what I was reading in real time.

What I want next: I want to stay with Vee's warmth and I dread the moment the two women's lines cross knowingly — the moment Randi's "yes, I like her" and Vee's "I'm making an exception" turn out to be the same event seen from two sides. Guess from here: the next collision is Randi entering Vee's romance not as her friend but as the third the pointing game was always building toward, and I'm bracing for how gently that door gets opened.
