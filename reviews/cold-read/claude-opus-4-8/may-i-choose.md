# Cold read — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: water-wings*

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one worked on me. Fully. This is the chapter where I stopped watching for the wire and just fell, exactly the way Vee does — which is either the loveliest thing the book has done or the most frightening, and I genuinely can't tell, and that not-being-able-to-tell is why I couldn't put it down.

Start with the plainest thing: this is a *dinner scene*, no sex, and it's the most sustained pleasure I've had reading this book. The whole architecture of Pace's tenderness is the plot here. "He didn't say where, and he didn't ask her to answer that night." The text lands the seduction on *quiet* — he "built the quiet into it without being asked," and that eases something behind her ribs. That's a devastatingly specific read of what care looks like to a girl who's been trained to perform readiness. And Cassie's line — "That's not a boy. That's a man." — coming from *Cassie*, the one voice I trust cleanest, gave the whole thing a warrant I didn't want it to have. When Cassie doesn't tease her about this, when Cassie *co-signs* him, I felt the floor tilt. Cassie is my Geiger counter and she isn't ticking. That scared me more than any red flag would have.

The restaurant is beautifully done. The menu she can't read, the "beautiful nonsense," and then those three words: **"May I choose?"** God, that's the title and it's the whole chapter and it's the whole *book*, isn't it. He asks. He always asks. And the relief that goes through her at handing the choice across the table — "more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would be" — is the erotic center of a scene with no sex in it. The word *surrender* is doing so much work. This is the consent architecture the jacket promised: "asking before he takes and taking only what she gives." And it is genuinely, meltingly attractive. I wanted to hand *him* my menu.

Then the eating. The hollow opening since noon, the bright green sour thing and the smoke-dark cool thing — "one to wake you and one to fold you under." She eats *without watching herself eat*, and for a reader who's carried Vee's mother-voice policing every bite since chapter four, that landed like a held breath let out. "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was." The whole meal is the food-motif paying off as liberation — she's fed, she chose to be chosen-for, and the censoring voice is simply gone. And the low-key detail I clocked hard: **always the right hand, never the other.** "A pattern now… always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it." From this chapter alone that's just intimate observation, a man with a private discipline. But I've read seven chapters. A man who keeps rules about hands, who built a bench seven months before he had anyone to put on it — I *felt* the cold draft come through that detail even as Vee only felt charmed. That's the book doing its double thing perfectly. Nothing on the page tells me to worry. I worry anyway, and I can't point to the sentence that did it, which is the trap.

"Somewhere in there she stopped waiting for the evening to go wrong." "That low background readiness she carried everywhere… always braced for the bill of a thing to come due." *The bill coming due.* That's the tagline breathing under the prose — every yes freely given, and the girl who has stopped bracing for the bill is precisely the one who won't see it. The text let her put down her guard and I felt how good it felt and I wanted to shout at her to pick it back up.

The ending is the peak. The hand taken "like it was already settled between them and always had been" — "less like something beginning than something resolving." And then the kiss. Her rule — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — and the beautiful move of it: he *doesn't push*. "No lean, no charm turned up, no patient little wait for her to change her mind. He just smiled… and let it close." He gives it up completely. And *because* he gives it up, she doesn't go. "I'm making an exception." She closes the distance he'd already surrendered. That's the engine bared: he takes only what she gives, so she gives it, so it is entirely, unbearably *hers* — and that ownership is exactly what makes it a trap, because a thing you chose this hard you will defend against anyone who tells you it was arranged. The tagline is that final paragraph, dramatized.

**Erotic charge:** high for a chapter with no sex — a 1 that punches above its weight. It peaks twice: "May I choose?" / "Yes. Please," and the final withheld-then-taken kiss. The eating is sensual without being sexual and I didn't mind. It never went clinical because Pace never got clinical; the one place it could have (explaining the menu, walking her through the page) is exactly where he *declined* to, which is the seduction.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** Here's my honest state. The *text* of this chapter earned me no suspicion — it's warm, clean, a genuinely great first date. Everything cold I feel, I imported: the right-hand rule ringing against the built-bench, "the bill coming due" ringing against a jacket that told me it's a trap, Cassie co-signing a man I know is not what Vee thinks he is. Vee thinks he's a kind stranger who helped with math. I know he chose her across a quad with Randi and staged the coffee shop. So the whole chapter is dramatic irony I hold alone, and the horror is that it's *not visible in the scene* — this chapter is pure light. The dread is entirely structural. That's the most unsettling kind.

**Friction:** almost none. This is the smoothest chapter yet. If I reach: "he seemed to know more of the world than she did" and "one more thing he knew that she didn't" and "some consistent rule he kept" — three beats of *he-knows-what-I-don't* in close succession. On the page it reads as a girl charmed by a worldly man. Faintly it's the author pressing the asymmetry a hair — but it's in her voice, it stays a felt thing, and I didn't resent it. No thumb I'd quote as a miss.

**Titles:** "May I Choose" is his three words, and it's exquisite — surface: a man offering to order dinner. Charge: the entire consent-machine of the book, permission dressed as courtesy, and the awful irony that the one choice he hands her (the menu) is a tiny lit square inside a room he built. He asks *may I choose* — for her — and she says *yes, please*, and that yes is the freely-given one. It also rhymes cruelly with the *other* choosing: he already chose *her*, across a quad, in a game. The title doesn't telegraph; it detonates on the reread of "that was the trap." And *With a Long Spoon* — supping with the devil, needing distance from what you eat — sat right on the table tonight: she ate from his hand, no spoon at all, no distance, the shortest possible reach. The girl who eats without watching herself is the girl with no long spoon.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the kiss to be everything and I dread that it is. Guess from here: with Pace/Vee physicalized now, the erotic staircase starts climbing for real, and I expect the next Randi brunch to *extract* this dinner — Vee telling it, Randi naming it back as a gift, the two tracks converging on Vee's body. I dread the first time her permitted wanting gets handed back as shame. And I keep waiting for the moment the right hand, or the bench, or "Peter," surfaces — for the reader's cold to finally touch the scene instead of only the margins.
