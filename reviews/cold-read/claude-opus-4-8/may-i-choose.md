## Reader reaction

# Felt read

So this is the froyo date's dangerous older sibling, and I read the whole thing with my stomach — which I think is exactly what it wanted from me, and I'm a little annoyed at how well it worked.

Let me start with the thing I can't get around: **the meal made me hungry.** The bright green sour thing "went down into the hollow and the hollow only opened wider, asked for more before she'd swallowed" — that landed in my own mouth. This is the feeding motif doing its most seductive work yet. The froyo cup overflowing (ch.6) was the first crack; here the mother-voice ("that's plenty, that's enough now") doesn't even show up to be silenced — it's just *gone,* and Vee "wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." That's the whole seduction in one sentence, and I felt the relief of it *with* her, which is the trap the jacket keeps promising me and which I keep falling into on cue.

**Pace.** Here's where my body and my mind split clean down the middle, and the chapter knows it. On the page, moment to moment, he is *impossibly good* — the text he built the quiet into "without being asked," the showered-and-shaved care "that meant *for this,*" the boots worn instead of bought, "may I choose," the one question (is there anything you won't eat), the not-explaining-the-menu, the not-filling-the-silence when her "Hi" came out small. Every single beat is the thing a person starves for. And I've read seven chapters. I know what "may I" *is* for him now — it's the same three-word key he used on Randi's body on the bench, the same "may I call you," the courtesy that is also the mechanism. So the more perfect he is, the colder I get. The right hand, "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept" — she reads it as "one more thing he knew that she didn't," charmed; I read premeditation, a man who has *rules,* who does nothing without a system. "The bright thing and the dark thing, one to wake you and one to fold you under" — she imagines dimly that he chose them to pair like that. I don't think it's dim at all. I think he did. I fully distrust him and I could not stop watching his hands either, which is horrible.

**The ending got me despite myself.** The rule — "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" — is the *wait-a-day* rule's sister, the last little fence Vee owns. And he does the most devastating possible thing: he doesn't push. "No lean, no charm turned up, no patient little wait for her to change her mind." He *lets it close* and starts her gently back. And *she doesn't go.* "I'm making an exception." That's her choosing, her feet, her hand gone taut on the line and stopping him. Every yes freely given. This is the cleanest illustration of the tagline in the whole book so far — he engineered a situation in which *she* breaks her own rule and experiences it as her own agency, exactly like the pencil handed back with Euler's substitution. I found it genuinely moving and genuinely sinister at the same time, and I don't think I'm supposed to be able to separate those. I couldn't.

**Cassie**, briefly, and she's the clean bell again: "That's not a boy. That's a man." And — "She teased Vee about everything; she didn't tease her about this." I trust Cassie's radar completely, and it's notable that here Cassie reads the *no-making-her-answer* as evidence of a real man, not a red flag. Even the clean witness is charmed by the technique when she only sees the surface of it. That chilled me more than anything Pace did.

**Trust/suspicion.** No movement — I distrust Pace as fully as I did, and the chapter only deepens it by being so relentlessly good at the exact thing that reads as care to her and as craft to me. Randi is entirely offstage; the "game" is invisible here, which almost makes it worse — a whole tender evening with no visible hand on the scale, which is the most effective hand on the scale.

**Erotic charge.** No sex, but this is a 1 that punches above its number — the peak is honestly the food and then the hand. "It was too much for what it was" — a hand around hers, and the reading that it "felt less like something beginning than something resolving." That resolving-not-beginning note is doing the erotic work; it's the sensation of a body being told it was made incomplete on purpose so it could be completed by him. The kiss we don't even see; the charge is all in her crossing the line.

**Friction.** Very little. The prose leans hard on its own tics — "unhurried" appears a lot, "the hollow," "she hadn't expected" three or four times — and once or twice the interiority over-narrates the thing I'd already felt: "the ease of letting it go again pulled at her, distinct and close" tells me what the tea-scene already showed. But it mostly earns its length; the meal *is* the seduction and it needed to be slow. The one line where I felt the thumb: "It came sideways, ahead of her deciding to say it" — the book keeps telling me Vee's truths arrive before her will does, and after seven chapters I've got it, I don't need the narrator flagging every instance.

**The titles.** "May I Choose" — his three words at the table, and it's a lovely, ugly title. On the surface it's the perfect courtesy: he *asks permission to choose for her,* the gentlest possible surrender-request. Reread through what I know, it's the whole apparatus: choice is the thing being transacted, and he's the one who gets to hold it while making her feel she handed it over freely. It also rhymes forward into the ending, where *she* chooses ("I'm making an exception") — so the title quietly points at the swap: he asks may-I-choose over dinner, and by the tree it's her choosing, and the chapter's argument is that those are the same move. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it bloomed after. That's the good kind. **With a Long Spoon** — still my supper-with-the-devil read (you sup with a long spoon), and this whole chapter is literally a supper, and I noticed he's the one *feeding* her, hand to mouth, the right hand every time. The spoon that lets you eat with the devil without getting caught — she's eating from his hand and doesn't feel the length of the handle at all. And the tagline is just the last two lines of this chapter in miniature.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to know when Randi comes back into it, because the two worlds haven't touched and the dread of that collision is my strongest forward-pull. *Guess from here:* the exception she just made is the last fence; I expect the next escalation to be his house — the bench, which I've been carrying since chapter one and which has not appeared to Vee yet. I dread that room with her in it. I also want one more Cassie chapter as my clean reference, because she's the only person in this book who could name what's happening and I keep wondering when or whether she will.
