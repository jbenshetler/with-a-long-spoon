# Cold read — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: water-wings*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me almost entirely against my will, and I want to note that up front because it's the whole experience of the chapter: I know what I know about Pace, and the chapter still got its hand around my ribs the way it gets its hand around Vee's.

The engine here is food, and it's beautifully chosen. **"May I choose?"** — three words, and I felt the same drop Vee felt. It's a surrender dressed as courtesy, and the chapter is honest about that: "the relief of handing the choice across the table went through her, more than she'd have guessed such a surrender would be." That word *surrender* is the whole book in miniature. He's teaching her that giving up control feels like rest. And because I distrust him utterly, I read the meal as a rehearsal — he chooses, she eats what she can't name, she stops watching herself, she "let him carry it. It hadn't occurred to her that she could." That last line is where my dread spiked. The thing being dismantled is her *self-monitoring*, the exact reflex that would protect her.

The mother's shaming-voice motif pays off hard and quiet. "She wasn't watching herself eat, as some part of her almost always was, and she didn't notice she'd stopped." No fanfare, no naming it. The appetite that her mother taught her to police, she indulges completely and doesn't get punished. That's genuinely moving *and* it's the trap, and the chapter lets both be true without a wink. I appreciated that it didn't announce the mother — the earlier chapters did the work, so here it just lands.

On Pace: my feeling didn't *move* so much as deepen along the same axis. The detail I keep turning over is the **right hand, always the right, never the other.** "some consistent rule he kept without seeming to think about it, one more thing he knew that she didn't." As a naive reader I read it as worldliness (the eating-with-hands custom), and Vee reads it as one more competence to admire. But the text flagged it three times, deliberately — "a pattern now, enough times over to be one" — and that repetition earned a small itch in me. Why is the chapter making sure I clock the hand? I don't have an answer and I'm not going to invent one; I'll just carry the itch. It's the one place the surface felt like it was pointing at something under it.

The **not telling her where** they're going, "built the quiet into it without being asked" — this is the froyo-listening trick escalated. He engineers spaces where she doesn't have to decide, and her body reads it as safety. The froyo chapter did this with time (letting the text "keep"); this does it with the whole evening.

Erotic charge: it's almost all displaced into eating, and it works *because* it's displaced. The bright-green thing to wake you and the smoke-dark thing "to fold you under," the bread torn "with care for the heat," the drip he brings to his mouth mid-sentence — it's sensual without being sexual, which made the actual erotic beat, the **hand-taking**, hit like a threshold. "It was too much for what it was." And then the reframe: "less like something beginning than something resolving — like her hand had been wrong all evening, all its life maybe." That destiny-language is exactly what should terrify me and it's rendered as the most natural feeling in the world. The peak for me was the tea "bringing her up" — surfacing, the pool-language of coming up out of water sneaking into a restaurant. She "surfaced." Her one untaken refuge, the underwater, and now his voice is the water she goes under in. That quietly gutted me.

The ending: "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days" / "I'm making an exception." The chapter earns it because it's *her* choosing to breach *her own* rule, and it stages his non-pursuit — "He didn't argue... no lean, no charm turned up... He just smiled." He gets it by declining to reach for it. That's the most Pace thing possible and I found it both swoony and sinister in the same breath, which is presumably the point.

Cassie: "That's not a boy. That's a man." She teases about everything and doesn't tease about this — I trust that instinct in her, and I noticed she's *approving*. The one person immune to scripts likes this. That's the coldest thing in the chapter, actually: even Cassie can't see it, because from inside a single instance there's nothing to see.

Friction: very little. The prose leans hard on the em-dash-and-qualify rhythm — "the not-coming was fine," "the not-needing-to was the most restful thing" — and there were a couple of beats where the interiority over-narrated the restfulness ("It hadn't occurred to her that she could" twice-adjacent in feel). The line I most side-eyed was **"the part of her always braced for the bill of a thing to come due, and it just wasn't there, and she let it not be there."** It's lovely, but "the bill... to come due" is the author leaning a hair on the ledger-metaphor — I felt a faint thumb, because *I* know there's a bill coming and she doesn't, and the metaphor almost winks. Almost. It stayed on the right side.

What I want / dread next: I dread the shaming-voice returning and I dread the moment she learns Pace and Randi already know each other, because this chapter just showed me how completely she's stopped bracing. A guess from here: the right-hand detail is load-bearing — either a physical tell (something about the *other* hand, an identity thing) or a discipline that connects to the Randi/BDSM side of him. I have no evidence, just the triple flag. Mostly I want to keep reading and I resent that I do.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Pace texted a low-pressure dinner invite ("Saturday... he didn't ask her to answer that night") — Cassie clocked Vee's helpless grin and said "That's not a boy. That's a man." He picked her up (first time), freshly showered/shaved, in his own well-kept clothes, and walked her to a small Middle Eastern restaurant she'd never known existed, where the owner Christine knows him warmly. Vee, ravenous and unable to read the menu, was rescued by his **"May I choose?"** — he ordered for her, she ate without self-surveillance for the first time, stopped monitoring herself, lost the thread of conversation and let him carry the whole evening. Mint tea "brought her up." Walking back he took her hand (first time, felt like "resolving" not beginning), let her voice her no-kiss-in-two-days rule without pushing at all — and she chose to breach it herself: "I'm making an exception."

**Who's who.**
- **Pace** — the orchestrator. This chapter: engineers spaces where Vee doesn't have to decide (where they're going, what she eats), and her body reads it as rest/safety. Declines to pursue and gets everything by declining. Noted quirk: eats only with his **right hand, never the left** — flagged three times, unexplained; I'm carrying it as an itch. Distrusted utterly; the tenderness works on me anyway.
- **Randi** — Pace's girlfriend/sub and Vee's co-opted "friend"; offstage this chapter. Admiration-as-dread.
- **Vee** — warm, econ-sharp, wide open, lonely; mother's appetite-shaming voice. This chapter she stops policing her own hunger and her own self-watching entirely, and *likes* it. Her pool-refuge language ("surfaced," "come up") is bleeding into how she experiences him — his voice is becoming the water she goes under in. Chose to break her own kiss-rule. I ache for her harder now.
- **Cassie** — roommate, immune to scripts, reads Vee true, teases about everything but not about this — and she *approves* of Pace. The coldest note in the chapter: even the moral floor can't see it from inside one instance. Still hasn't warned Vee.
- **Christine** — restaurant owner, older, knows Pace warmly by name. A real place he already belongs to; another world he knows that Vee doesn't.
- **Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey** — sorority machine, offstage.

**How I feel.** Deepening sick-with-knowing dread under real, involuntary pull. The seduction is now visible as a method: he dismantles her self-surveillance by removing every decision from her and calling it care. Nothing wrong-noted on the page except that the *one* person who could catch it (Cassie) admires him. Mood: warm, tender, restful, quietly total.

**Open questions.** Why the right-hand-only pattern — worldliness, discipline, or a tell? Who is Pace really (Peter)? When does Vee learn he and Randi already know each other, and will her newly-disarmed radar catch anything? When does the shaming voice come back — and what occasion brings it? Will Cassie ever sense the wrong note she can't currently see?
