# Cold read (grounded) — May I Choose

*scene: scenes/may-i-choose.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · memory: raw ch001..ch008 (pre-first-checkpoint) · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

I'll be honest: this chapter worked on me, and that's exactly the problem, and I think the book knows it. Read straight, as Vee lives it, it's one of the most swoony evenings I've had in a while — the shirt that's good but not new, the boots kept the way boots have to be kept, "May I choose," the hand taken with no question in it as though it were "already settled." That last image — her hand feeling *less like something beginning than something resolving*, wrong all its life for want of his — landed in my chest before I could defend against it. I wanted it for her. And then I remembered what I know and she doesn't, and the wanting curdled into something close to nausea. That double exposure is the whole chapter for me.

Because I've read the last several chapters. I know Randi asked, "Do you think she'll like the bench?" I know Pace tasted Randi's wine off her tongue while she demanded he *tell* her about the froyo girl, the want too big for her face. Vee is being hunted. Pace didn't wander into that coffee shop; he was pointed at her on a lawn. So every tender thing here that Vee reads as a man being decent — the plainness, the quiet built into the text so nothing "needed her right this second," the not-telling-her-where because she's *learning she can trust where he's taking her* — I read as technique. The care I'd normally melt at is the same care he uses to put Randi facedown on a bench: the checking-in, the *may I*, the consent theater. He is very, very good, and I have watched him be good at this before, in a room with restraints.

My feeling about Pace has moved hard. In "Substitution" he was almost admirable — the returned pencil, the refusal to show off. Here that same restraint reads as predation with excellent manners. "May I choose" is him choosing her *food*, and I know it's also him choosing *her*, and the book let those two meanings sit right on top of each other. The bit where she stops watching herself eat — where the hollow "had been empty a lot longer than since noon" and she lets him feed her — is genuinely moving as a portrait of a starved girl, and it's the exact thing that moved him about Randi and the apple slices. He has a type of hunger he likes to find and answer. That recurrence chilled me.

And yet — the swoon is not fake to *me*, and I refuse to pretend it is. The froyo tart-holding-up callback, the mint tea in brass cages made to be held while too hot, the "I don't kiss a man I've known a couple of days / I'm making an exception" — that's a beautifully built seduction beat, and my body rooted for it even as my mind flinched. I think that's the intended cruelty: to make me complicit in the wanting so I feel the trap from inside it. Cassie is the one clean instrument here — "That's not a boy. That's a man" — and it reads to me now as an epitaph, not a compliment. I trust Cassie. I trust no one else in this book, and I'm no longer sure I trust the tenderness itself.

Erotic charge: low and warm, not the point. This chapter runs on romance and dread, not heat — the sensuality is all displaced into food and one held hand. The one erotic flicker is her eye catching on his forearms as he rolls his cuffs, and it's chaste. The heat lives offstage, in what I know is coming.

Friction: almost none — the prose is controlled and I bought it. If anything, "always the right, never the other, some consistent rule he kept" got a touch heavy, the narration underlining a mystery I don't need underlined. But it's minor. The bigger unease is structural and intended, not a stumble.

The title, now that I've read it: "May I Choose" is his line, but it's a lie of grammar. He performs asking permission while he has already chosen — the food, and her. Vee thinks *she's* the one choosing ("I'm making an exception"), and the horror is that her choosing is the thing being engineered. The cover packet: **WITH A LONG SPOON**, Book One, **A POLITE INVITATION**. "He who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" — someone here is supping with something dangerous, and the *politeness* is the invitation, the bait. This whole chapter is the polite invitation. It recolors "may I" completely: courtesy is the devil's instrument. I dread where it goes — and I want, uneasily, to keep reading, which is itself the hook working on me.

What I expect/dread (guesses from here): Vee gets drawn toward Randi and Pace both, and the bench Randi mentioned is the destination. I dread that Vee's genuine, unguarded wanting — she has none of Randi's armor — makes her far more vulnerable than Randi ever was.
