## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This chapter is one long held breath, and the title tells you the whole shape of it before you understand it: *the reach*. Vee alone in the overheated dorm room, in Pace's flannel and nothing else, buttons open, working herself over — and the entire chapter narrows, twice, onto one gesture she cannot complete: lifting her own fingers to her own mouth. That's the reach. She gets a breath's width away and stops. "Nothing in the room told her why. The wanting was still there... She could not close the distance." I found that genuinely arresting — it's the most naked thing in a chapter full of nakedness, and it's not about her body at all.

The heat worked on me, and worked *cleanly*, which I appreciated after two restaurant chapters of heat-by-retelling. This is Vee unmediated, no Randi across the table shaping the story, no white cloth to fold the truth down small — just her, and the flannel's nap moving over her ribs, and the hothouse smell of herself rising in the shut room. And the smell is the hinge. She braces for "the stink the boys' jokes had promised" and it isn't there: "Warm, damp, alive — a hothouse with its doors shut, the air around a tropical flower without its sweetness. Hers." That undoing of the installed disgust — testing it against her actual senses and finding the mother's-voice verdict simply *false* — was quietly devastating to read. She's dismantling the shame one sense at a time, and still, at the last inch, something older than the smell wins. The reach fails anyway.

What moved my feeling about Vee: I ache for her more than I have in a while, because here there's no audience and no performance, and the war is stripped down to its bones. Alone, she can't reach the absolution Randi handed her ("Not greedy. Hungry out loud"). "Alone in his open shirt, already planning this after everything he gave her, it was harder to believe. *Greedy.*" That's the honest floor of her — the loud princess of the last chapters folded back down to a girl who still can't believe she's allowed. I love that the book keeps her this contradictory.

But the thing that actually made the hair stand up: **where the reach points.** She wants Pace's *look* again — the wet mouth, the calm glad eyes, no apology. Fine, expected. But the unfinished gesture keeps resolving toward Randi. "*It was powerful. It turned me on.* She had said it like a simple fact." And then, twice, at the very edge of her own mouth: "A mouth carrying it back. Or Randi's own fingers." And the fantasy that had been Pace between her thighs slides, mid-build, into cashmere — Randi's world, Randi's texture — and the last image of the chapter, after she comes, is not Pace at all. It's the question she can't put down: did Randi mean *a mouth carrying it back, or her own fingers, lifted to her mouth?* Her mouth is still empty. That's the last line. **The reach isn't toward her own taste — it's toward Randi's.** The book didn't say it. It let the empty mouth say it, and I sat there with it.

That's where my suspicion-radar and my swoon both lit at once, and I want to be precise: the *unease* here isn't about Randi doing anything on the page — Randi isn't even present. It's the reader's unease of watching Vee's wanting turn a corner she hasn't noticed she's turned. She thinks she's lying in bed thinking about her boyfriend. The chapter quietly shows her thinking about her best friend's mouth, using her best friend's verdict as the permission she couldn't grant herself. And she buries it — literally: she wipes her hand, and at the end she settles her hip *over* the damp patch she made, "Her mess. Hers to lie in." The old motif of the body's evidence, but now she's not fleeing it or explaining it — she's lying down on top of it and pulling the blanket up. That felt like a real move. She's keeping the secret from herself the way she keeps the coat.

The window is the other quiet marvel. She shuts Cassie's defended two inches of cold to make her private hothouse — closes the room to have her want in it — and then afterward has to *shove the swollen sash open* and stand in the January flood to air the evidence out before Cassie gets home. "January entered like water through a broken door." Opening the window to erase the smell of what she did with the window shut. The room won't hold the secret; she has to physically expel it. And then she climbs back in and the hothouse *lingers under the winter air anyway.* You can't air it all the way out. Neither can she.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No one's "off" on the page because no one else is on the page. What the text earned is subtler and it's about Vee herself — the gap between what she thinks this is (missing Pace) and what it keeps becoming (reaching for Randi). I trust the prose completely here; it's not telegraphing, it's just refusing to name the thing and trusting me to feel the empty mouth. Given everything I carry — that Randi loves her, that the brunches are reconnaissance dressed as affection, that Randi has been steering all along — this chapter reads to me like the steering *working from the inside now*, without Randi even in the room. Randi planted "I don't [mind it]" across a dinner table two nights ago, mild as a fact, and here it is doing its work in the dark. That's the quietest, most unsettling thing the book has done in a while: the influence has gone airborne.

**Erotic charge:** high and sustained, and it earns it by being about interiority the whole time — the heat *is* the shame-thawing, the reach, the buried turn. It peaks not at either orgasm but at the raised hand, the breath moving over her fingers, the tongue coming forward — *and stopping.* Denial rendered as the hottest beat. Nothing went slack for me.

**Friction:** almost none. Once or twice the fragmented sentence-shards ("His tongue deep. His groan inside her. Her fist closing in his hair.") risk feeling like a technique I can see, but they mostly land as the disassembly of a mind under its own hand, so I forgave it. No thumb on the scale — this is the book at its least explanatory, and better for it.

**The titles:** *The Reach* — perfect, and it doesn't give anything away so much as it *withholds the object*. A reach toward what? The chapter makes you supply it, and the answer it keeps nudging you toward isn't the one Vee thinks she's answering. On second thought it recolors everything: the failed gesture, and the direction the gesture keeps pointing. The volume line — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* — "he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon," and Book Two called *A Warm Reception*: this chapter is a very warm reception indeed, a room heated on purpose, a welcome that's also a snare. The "long spoon" keeps promising that someone here is supping with something dangerous while telling themselves it's just dinner. Right now that someone feels like Vee, reaching bare-handed toward exactly what she should be keeping at a spoon's length.

**What I want / dread next (guess from here):** I want Vee to get to Tuesday's table and *not* be able to make this small — the way she couldn't make the wet kiss small. I dread, a little, how gently and how completely Randi will receive it. My guess from here: the reach she couldn't finish alone gets finished, eventually, toward Randi — and Vee will keep telling herself, right up to the moment it happens, that it was about Pace.
