**The felt read**

This is a small chapter and it knows it — one girl, one bed, one shut window, one thing she cannot make herself do — and it landed on me harder than a lot of the louder ones, because it's the first time in a while the book has left Vee completely alone with herself and let me watch her *not* get away with something.

What moved in me here is my sense of Vee. Across the last few chapters she's been running the table — the pleasure-princess weekend, the roadhouse, the restaurant court sessions where she and Randi turn the sex into performance. She's been *managing* it, dressing it up as farce, keeping the shape she can sit behind. This chapter strips all of that off. There's no audience, no Randi to hand it to, no punchline to build on the drive home. Just her, in his thinned-out flannel, doing the exact thing she confessed at dinner as a joke — and finding out it isn't a joke. The whole chapter is her body running one experiment on repeat: she gets herself slick, lifts her fingers, breathes her own smell, opens her mouth — and stops "a breath's width" short, twice, and cannot say why. *"Nothing in the room told her why. The wanting was still there... She could not close the distance."* That got me. It's the most honest she's been with herself in the book, precisely because she reaches no conclusion. She just meets the wall and lies down next to it.

The erotic charge is real and I felt it, but it's a strange, muted heat — turned inward, almost lonely. The solo masturbation is rendered fully and I wasn't cold to it; the flannel-nap-over-nipples, the taking-the-long-way, the two fingers, the low broken note the shut glass keeps in — it works. But the actual peak of the chapter isn't the orgasm, it's the *aborted taste*, and that's where the writing goes electric and uneasy at once. The book has taught me to read Vee's body as the truth-teller and her mind as the censor — the "floozie" wire — but here the usual arrangement glitches: her body *wants* the look Pace gave her, wants to be admitted "without argument," and yet at the last inch it's not the mother's flat voice that stops her. It's something with no words attached. That's new, and it unsettled me in a good, earned way. The old shame I could name. This I can't, and neither can she.

The thing I keep circling — and the chapter clearly wants me to — is *why she can taste it off him but not off her own hand.* On the couch it broke her open (the deep clench, twice). Randi said "it was powerful, it turned me on," flat, no mercy. And here Vee's got the exact same material in her own fingers, alone, permitted, nobody watching — and she can't. Which tells me the block was never really about the taste. It's about *who's offering it.* Off Pace's mouth it arrives wrapped in that look — glad, unpardoning, *the plenty of her requiring no pardon.* Alone, there's no look to launder it through; it's just her, being greedy, and "greedy" is the word that keeps surfacing with a sting on it. So the reach she can't complete is, I think, a reach toward accepting herself the way he accepts her — and she can only get there secondhand, through his gladness, never direct. That's a genuinely sad little discovery and the chapter is right not to spell it out.

The window is doing quiet work and I loved it. She comes in and *shuts* Cassie's defended two-inch gap — seals the room, keeps every small heat "where it was put," makes a private hothouse she can be loud in. Then afterward the room "held everything... it had nowhere to go," and she has to shove the swollen sash open and stand in the January flood in the open flannel until the smell of her clears. Closing the window to sin in, opening it to air the evidence — that's the whole shame-engine of the book in one pane of glass. And the last line, *her mouth was still empty*, after all that release — that's the ache the chapter is actually about. Not unsatisfied. *Un-reached.*

Trust and suspicion: I have no new suspicion of anyone here, because no one else is in the room — but the chapter quietly deepens the unease I already carry about Randi's coaching. *"Not greedy. Hungry out loud"* comes back to Vee as something that "had felt like an absolution then" and now, alone, "was harder to believe." That's the first crack I've seen in Randi's gospel from *inside Vee.* Randi's reframes have been landing as liberation all fall; here, at 2 a.m. with no one performing for anyone, one of them doesn't hold. I don't think Vee clocks it as Randi's fault — she doesn't — but I clocked it, and it made me a little colder toward the whole apparatus of being talked into your own appetite by someone with a plan. And the taste thread itself — Pace's wet kiss, unwiped "exactly once," then Randi so easy and knowing about it — sits in me as something not yet finished. Vee filed "how many times, and how carefully" away last chapter; this chapter she's living inside the aftermath of it without ever naming that's what she's doing.

Pace, though present only in memory, comes off tender and unnerving in the same breath as always — the folding-her-open, the helpless groan, the *okay* against her jaw, the care to stop when she turned away. I believe the tenderness completely. What I notice is that even her masturbation is organized entirely around *his* face and *his* approval; she can't get off to her own permission, only to the memory of his. That's beautiful and it's a leash, and the book keeps refusing to tell me which.

Friction: almost none — this is tightly controlled. If anything the repetition of the reach (fingers up, stop, wipe, again) risks feeling like a loop, but I think it earns it: the loop *is* the point, the not-getting-past-it. The one beat I felt the thumb slightly is "*Slippery... the texture felt newly made,*" which nudges toward the wide-eyed-discovery register the book leans on a touch often. But it's minor. Mostly I trusted it.

**The titles.** "The Reach" — before reading I'd have guessed something about reaching for Pace, or reaching across to Randi. It turns out to mean the literal, physical reach of her tongue toward her own fingers, stopping one breath short — and then, on second thought, it colors outward: the reach toward self-acceptance she can only make by proxy, the distance she can't close alone. It's a good, quiet title; it doesn't give anything away, it just names the ache once I've felt it. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** — the "long spoon" (sup with the devil, use a long spoon) keeps its faint warning against everything the book keeps calling warm and generous; "A Warm Reception" reads double now, the roadhouse welcome and the too-warm rooms she's always being kept in. Nothing here made those titles snap into place, but "the reach" as a *thing you can't quite complete with a long enough spoon* rhymes with the whole engine oddly well.

What I want next: I want to know whether this reach ever closes, and what happens when it does or doesn't — because the book just planted, very quietly, that the thing standing one breath from Vee's mouth is not going away. And I'm braced, still, for the day the two dinners she's been keeping separate — her people, and the two who chose her — stop being separate. But tonight I mostly just ached for her, alone in a sealed room with a want she couldn't name and couldn't finish. *(Guess from here: the "taste" is going to turn out to be about Randi before it's about herself — that's the reach the book is really walking her toward, and she doesn't know it yet.)*
