# Cold read — Turned Up

*scene: scenes/turned-up.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: a-round*

## Reader reaction

This whole chapter is Vee coming home and telling Cassie about the fitting, and honestly the thing that surprised me is how much I *liked* being back with Cassie. After A Round, which was all heat and held breath, this is a big exhale — two girls on the beds, a pillow thrown twice, someone's music two doors down, the radiator clicking. I laughed out loud at "*I* was grinding on *him*." That landed. The whole rhythm of the retelling — Vee up, Vee down, Vee at the window and back, unable to sit still — is exactly right for someone who's carrying something too big for her body.

But here's what got me, and it got me the way the *pattern* is supposed to and not the way the joke is: Cassie is the one doing the work I keep waiting for someone to do. She lays it out. "He's a bodybuilder who makes dresses." "He has had you standing in his living room in your underwear, and he picked up a *tape measure.*" She's the puzzle-worker, the one whose face does "the small frown," and she is *this close* — she has all the pieces on the table. The no-tag shirt. The sewing. The two shirtless encounters. And then she resolves it into "Are you sure he's not gay?" — which is the one wrong answer, the answer that closes the file. That chilled me more than anything in the flirty stuff. Cassie, my most-trusted voice, has the anomaly in her hands and she *jokes it shut.* She's turning the exact same crank Vee turned with the no-tag shirt — "I gave up on it. Filed it under weird things about him." The person best equipped to see is looking straight at it and mis-naming it. That's the tell for me, and it's invisible to both of them.

The heat-turned-up beat is where the chapter earns its keep emotionally. Vee didn't mean to say it — "I had not meant to say this part yet, or maybe not at all" — and then it comes out flat and factual: he turned the heat up hours before, as soon as he knew she was coming, "He thought of it then." And Cassie goes still and says "Oh, Vee," and her face goes soft. That's the moment the room stops being a comedy. It's the smallest gesture in the whole book and it's the one that undoes them both. And I sat there with it knowing what Cassie doesn't — that this is a man who thinks of *everything* hours before, who stages the temperature of a room the way he staged the tutoring pickup and the froyo and the color that went "deeper" than she asked. The tenderness is real. It's also a system. Vee reads it as being thought-of. I read it as being *handled*, and the fact that both are true at once is the thing I can't put down.

I trust Vee's happiness and it scares me, which is the same feeling I've had for a few chapters now, only sharper because now I've watched her *tell the story* and turn every ambiguous thing into evidence of being chosen. "Like I was supposed to be there." The knees-pulled-up-smaller-than-she-usually-sits when she says it. Cassie's hand on her knee, once, and taken back. It's genuinely moving. I don't distrust anything *between* these two women — the affection is clean, Cassie wants nothing, the pillow fight is the realest thing in the book.

Erotically this is a 1, and correctly — it's the debrief, not the act. The charge is all secondhand, filtered through laughter, and it works *as* laughter. The grinding confession is the peak and it's funny before it's hot, which is the right call for the register. The "May I look" beat quiets the room and that's where the residual heat pools. Nothing here felt clinical because nothing here is trying to be hot; it's trying to be two friends, and it is.

Friction: almost none. The one place I felt the author's hand, very lightly, was Cassie's "You keep taking your shirt off in front of this guy and not getting any?" — it's a great line but it's also the line that flags "the rain" and "you knocked on the window," which I don't have a scene for. That's a gap in my memory (was there a rain scene I'm compressing?), and the text leans on it like I should feel it. Minor. It reads as a shared history I'm on the edge of.

**The title.** "Turned Up" — going in it's nothing, an idiom, showed-up / turned-up. Having read it: it's the *heat*. He turned the heat up. And it's Vee, turned up — dialed higher, lit, on. And there's the dark third reading that only exists because I've been watching the pattern: what "turns up" is the evidence, the anomalies surfacing one after another — the shirt, the sewing, the tape measure — turning up in Cassie's hands and getting waved back down. The title launders the tenderest fact (he warmed the room) through a flat idiom, and that's the same move he makes. I like it. It doesn't give anything away and it deepened after.

**The book's title and tagline** sit heavier every chapter. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is *nothing but* freely-given yeses being retold as joy — she took the bra off, she let him look, she leaned back on purpose, she's beaming about all of it. There is no coercion anywhere and that's exactly the point that's starting to frighten me. *With a Long Spoon* — you sup with the devil using one — and Cassie is holding the spoon and calling it a tape measure.

What I want next: I want the dress finished, and I dread it, because "the night that was hers" is still the phrase I can't shake and I don't believe it stays hers. *Guess from here:* Randi resurfaces soon — she's been absent and weaponized-in-memory, and the confidante track has been running through Cassie lately, which feels like a substitution waiting to be corrected. And I keep waiting for the moment Cassie's joke curdles — for her to look at the pile of anomalies once more and *not* laugh.
