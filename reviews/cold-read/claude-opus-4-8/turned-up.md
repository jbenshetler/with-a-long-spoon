## Reader reaction

The whole chapter is the debrief, and I loved that it's a debrief — after the near-unbearable intimacy of the fitting, we get Vee at home with Cassie, safe, cracking herself open by trying to tell it. It works on me because the telling *shape* is the meaning: she keeps pivoting away from the thing that undid her. She hands Cassie the burgundy first, then the shirt, then the riser, then the too-warm house — and it's the house that stops her cold. "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold." That's the beat where I felt the chapter land: not the nudity, not the pins, but the pre-planned tenderness she's only now decoding, hours late. "Oh, Vee," from Cassie, is exactly right, and I said it too.

I'm still wholly on Vee's side, and I love watching her *narrate* the power she discovered on the box — "I was grinding on *him*" — flat on her back hugging a pillow, giddy and wicked and safe enough with Cassie to say the worst-best of it out loud. The froyo-cup girl and the girl who chose the kiss are here fully unfurled now. There's a real swoon in it, and it's clean, because it comes through the one relationship in this book with no angle: Cassie. That's the emotional engine of the chapter. Cassie *screamed in the hall* when the letter came; Cassie has the blood type ready; Cassie catches the pillow and hugs it. Every time Vee's voice drops to the true thing — "Like I was supposed to be there" — Cassie goes still and says the small right thing, or just puts a hand on her knee and takes it back. I trust her completely and the chapter let me rest in that trust.

But Cassie is also, quietly, doing the thing the reader does. Her puzzle-frown: "Walk me through this... he picked up a *tape measure.*" She's the clear-eyed one working the pieces that won't fit, and the "are you sure he's not gay?" gag is funny *and* it's Cassie circling something she can't name — a man this deliberate, this restrained, this good at building a woman's shame into a shape she can stand inside. I don't think Cassie suspects the truth (she can't; she doesn't know about Randi or the bench). But her instinct that *something doesn't add up* is the reader's own, played as comedy. That's where my low wariness about Pace lives this chapter — not in anything he does, but in how completely he's engineered Vee's undoing into gifts she can't refuse. The shirt riddle she "filed under weird things about him and stopped looking" is exactly how the whole seduction works: she keeps meeting a mystery, deciding it's benign, and walking on.

Heat is real but banked — it's recollected, not live, and that's the right call after the fitting's near-unbearable charge. The peak here is emotional, not erotic: the reveal of the warmed house. The erotic beats are delivered as *comedy between friends* ("his *package*... definitely not gay"), which defuses them on purpose and lets the tenderness carry the weight instead. It never went clinical or slack; it just chose warmth over wanting, and I bought the choice.

No friction to speak of — the dialogue is fast and true, the "I was on a box" / "I was on a box" runs land, the withholding structure never feels like the author's thumb. If anything I noticed how *little* new happens — this is a reaction chapter, a breather — but it's earning its keep by letting the previous chapter finish detonating in Vee, and by keeping Cassie close, which I wanted after the checkpoint left me protective.

The title, "Turned Up": I take it two ways at once and both landed. The heat turned up in the house — the literal fact Vee decodes, the tenderness pre-set hours early. And Vee turned up, showed up, delivered, was *turned up* to full wattage — she came home lit, spending the power she found on the box. There's a third, darker read hovering: she's being turned up like a dial, adjusted, made ready. The chapter itself doesn't push that; I only hear it because of what I know that she doesn't. The volume line — "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION" — keeps ringing. "He who sups with the devil should have a long spoon." That proverb sits under all this politeness and gift-giving like a floor I can feel through my feet. "A polite invitation" is what Saturday two o'clock was, and it's the whole method: everything offered, nothing forced, the door always left open — and I dread, precisely, how safe it all feels. That's the promise the title makes: that the courtliness *is* the trap.

What I want next: I want Vee to have the dress, the induction, the good thing — genuinely, I'm rooting for the joy. And I dread the moment the pieces Cassie is turning over finally fit, because I know they will, and I know Randi is on the other end of the line. *Guess from here:* the finished dress is going to be the occasion for something — a delivery, a fitting, a night — that closes the distance Pace keeps so carefully leaving open.
