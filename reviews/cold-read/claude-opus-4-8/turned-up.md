## Reader reaction

### The felt read

This is the third of these debrief chapters now — Cassie, then Kayla and Meg, now Cassie again — and I braced for it to feel like a rerun. It mostly doesn't, because the retelling has started to do something to Vee. Watching her narrate the fitting, I can see her deciding *what it was* while she says it. She keeps rearranging the room with her body — sets the bag down, picks it up, puts it on the chair, sits on the desk, gets up, crosses to the window and back — and that fidget is the whole chapter's temperature. She's overfull.

The thing that got me is the pivot around the heat. She's mid-comedy, and then: "And the house was warm," she said, before Cassie could answer. She interrupts *herself* to hand over the detail she hasn't processed. "He turned it up hours before, probably. As soon as he knew I was coming. He thought of it then." That's the beat where I felt my own chest go tight, and Cassie's "Oh, Vee" — the one she *swallowed* back in the rye chapter — finally lands out loud. That mattered to me. Cassie has been the person holding back a sentence for chapters, and here she lets a small one through, and it's tenderness, not warning. I don't know whether that means she's stopped worrying or that she's given up trying.

I'm still uneasy in exactly the place Vee is warm. The thermostat, the pre-built riser, the two towels on the bathroom rod — a house that was *ready*. Vee reads the heat as forethought, which it is, and I read it as staging, which it also might be. The chapter doesn't nudge me either way, and I respect that it doesn't. Cassie's "Are you sure he's not gay?" is the funniest line in the book so far and also, weirdly, the chapter's only real interrogation of Pace — and it's the *wrong* question, comically wrong, aimed at the one thing that isn't strange about him. A man who lifts, builds furniture, sews clothes, keeps a shirt he made on a girl's pillow, and picked up a tape measure instead of taking the obvious yes: Cassie's puzzle-frown is right, her solution is nonsense. I liked that a lot. The room can *feel* the anomaly and can't name it.

What I noticed most, and what I keep turning over: Vee doesn't tell Cassie about the wet panties. She tells her the grinding — "*I* was grinding on *him*" — and frames that as "the thing she'd been keeping all the way at the bottom." It isn't. The bottom thing is the dread, the shame, being seen at her worst and not found wanting. She swaps the vulnerable secret for a bawdy one, and gets a laugh for it, and I don't think she knows she did it. Same move as the Kayla-and-Meg chapter, where she cut the striptease. The retellings are becoming a place where she launders the parts that cost her.

Erotic charge: low, and correctly low. This is heat recalled through a screen of comedy, and the two live sparks are both quiet — "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*," and the kneeling geometry she can't finish saying: "and the riser put me up, and he was—" She stops. Let the geometry say itself. That ellipsis did more than a paragraph would have. And "I kept waiting to feel wrong about it… I didn't." The slapstick around the bra ("*out*. Just out. In broad daylight. On a box.") is charming and deliberately unsexy; it's her defusing.

Friction: two small things. The Cassie-and-Vee volley "I was on a box." / "—I was on a box." is a good gag but the second repetition is one beat past its best. And I noticed Cassie says "standing in his living room in your underwear" when the riser was on the sun porch — probably just Cassie being loose, but it snagged me. Bigger friction, honestly, is structural: this is the fourth chapter in a row with no Randi. She's mentioned nowhere at all here, and Vee, who is bursting to tell someone, doesn't say "I have to tell Randi." Five chapters ago Randi was the person she'd have called first. That silence has stopped feeling like a gap and started feeling like a held breath.

**Turned Up** — the thermostat, obviously, and the chapter puts it in the center. But it also reads as Vee turned up: dialed louder, all hands and pacing and laughing too hard. And a third one, the one I sat with — *he turned up*, in the sense of showing up prepared. What a title like that gives away is nothing, which is the right amount.

**A Polite Invitation** / **With a Long Spoon** / "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — the tagline has never fit better than here, where a girl is joyfully cataloguing her own yeses to her best friend and the friend has no counter-argument, only "Oh, Vee." The long spoon keeps meaning: something is being supped with. Nothing in this chapter tells me what.

Next: I want the dress finished and worn, I want the induction dinner with the mother in the room, and I dread that in exactly equal measure. *Guess from here:* the dress arrives, the mother sees it, and the word "stripper" gets tested in daylight. And I still want to know whose the second towel is — that stone hasn't moved in two chapters and it's the loudest thing in the book.

**How I feel**
Warm, entertained, and quietly more uneasy than the chapter's mood wants me to be. Cassie is the person I love most in this book and the person the book keeps giving the wrong question to. Vee is radiant and I like her enormously and I've now watched her twice sand the frightening part off a story before handing it to a friend — that pattern is starting to feel like a cost accruing. Pace, secondhand here, still reads as either the most attentive man alive or the most patient one; the thermostat is the perfect emblem of not being able to tell. The riser and the two towels remain stones in my shoe. And Randi's absence — six chapters, and now not even a thought — has gone from a gap to a pressure. I want a scene next, not another retelling.
