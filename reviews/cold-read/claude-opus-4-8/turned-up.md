# Cold read — Turned Up

## Reader reaction

This one is a warm bath, and I mean that as a compliment. After the intensity of the fitting itself in "A Round," this is the exhale — Vee bursting through the door still lit up, and Cassie catching her. I've been waiting for a Cassie chapter. She's been gone since ch.12, and I've missed her forensic warmth. It's a relief to have the one unbiddable person back in the room.

The pleasure of this chapter is watching the two registers Vee holds at once. She keeps *pivoting* — every time the retelling gets too near the tender center, she swerves into comedy. "The bra too!" with her hands cupping and flying outward, "here you go, I guess." She's performing the fitting as a bawdy anecdote because the real thing is too big to say straight. And Cassie, who "was good at that," lets the pivots happen and then quietly walks her back. That dynamic is doing real emotional work — Vee narrating with her hands, up and down to the window, physically unable to sit still, and Cassie pulling her knees up "smaller than she usually sat" when the truth lands. Body before mind, both of them.

The beat that got me — genuinely, in the chest — is the heat. Vee blurts it out of sequence, "before Cassie could answer," which is exactly how a person spills the thing that actually matters: "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold... He thought of it then." And Cassie goes very still, and says "Oh, Vee," soft. Here's the thing though. That pre-warmed house is the one cold flicker my memory won't let go of — I've been carrying it as *maybe staging, maybe care*. And now the chapter puts it right in the center, and both women read it as pure tenderness, unqualified. Vee is *moved* by it. And I felt moved with her — and then a half-second later felt the small chill, because I remember it as ambiguous and they don't. The text didn't tell me to feel the chill. It let both of them be undefended about it, which is worse, somehow. That's the closest thing to unease in the chapter, and it's mine, not theirs.

The shirt. Cassie snaps to it fast — "Vee. The shirt." — and I love that she's the one who won't let it slide. Vee's account of the first night, turning the shirt "the whole thing inside out — no brand, no size, nothing... So I gave up on it. Filed it under weird things about him." That line — "I'd decided it was a riddle and gone to sleep" — is quietly devastating. She had the answer in her hands and chose not to solve it. It reads as innocent here, sweet even, but it also reads as a small portrait of how she handles evidence about him: notices, can't make it add up, files it under charming, sleeps.

Then the comedy climax, which is genuinely funny and genuinely revealing: "Are you sure he's not gay?" and Vee's brisk clinical ass-pat "his *package. Right here.*" and the reversal — "*I* was grinding on *him.*" The delight on her ("wicked," "a little") pays off the ch.15 discovery of her own erotic power. She's not ashamed of it anymore; it's the punchline she's been saving all the way at the bottom. That's a real arc for the shame engine, and it lands because Cassie's the one safe audience for it.

Erotically the chapter is a 1 — it's all recollected, filtered through comedy and through Cassie's couch. But the "May I look" beat — "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look*" — actually re-charged for me even secondhand, because Cassie stops laughing and the key changes. The retelling of the kneeling hem work ("He was on his knees... and looking up. And I was up on the box looking down") is more charged in the telling than I'd expect, because Vee lets "the geometry say itself" and Cassie "said nothing, which was the right thing." The restraint is the heat.

No friction to speak of. If anything the chapter is *almost* too comfortable — a whole chapter of debrief with no new plot, just the same event re-lit. But it earns it, because the re-lighting is the point: we see what Vee chose to keep at the bottom, what she swerves around, what she can only say to Cassie.

What I want next: Pace said "He'll call me when it's ready." So the dress is coming, and the induction with it — mother yes, father no, name read at the front. I want to see her wear it. And I'm still braced. Randi has now been gone six chapters. Six. This whole chapter is two girls warm on a bed with gold light and a clicking radiator, and the loudest thing in it is who isn't in the room. *Guess from here:* the drop, when it comes, comes through Randi, and this gold-lit safety is the setup for it.
