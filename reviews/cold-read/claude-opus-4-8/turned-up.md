## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is a debrief chapter — Vee comes back from the fitting and tells Cassie everything — and after all the golden intensity of the last two chapters at Pace's house, it's a relief and a pleasure to be back on the twin beds with the highlighter and the cracked window. It reads like exhaling.

What I love here is that it's a **Cassie chapter**, and Cassie has been getting edged out for a run of chapters, so having Vee come straight to *her* with this — not Kayla, not Meg — felt like a small mercy. And Vee gives her the real thing, the whole thing, right down to the bottom card. My trust in Cassie is total and it deepened again: she does the thing she's good at, "she let the pivot happen, she was good at that," she catches Vee's deflections and lets some of them go and lands on others. The "Oh, Vee" when the too-warm house lands is the exact same soft register from before, and it's earned every time.

The **too-warm house** beat is the emotional peak for me, and it's the one place the chapter goes quiet and serious. Vee narrates it the way you narrate a thing you've only just understood about yourself: "He turned the heat up before I got there. So I wouldn't be cold... He thought of it then." And Cassie goes very still. Here's where my own doubleness as a reader bites — I *know* something Vee is only feeling. I know that too-warm house has meant, for many chapters, a *second inhabitant*, two towels, kept heat for someone else. And now Vee's reading it as pure tenderness aimed at her. She's probably right that he warmed it for her — but the house runs warm anyway, for reasons she doesn't know, and watching her assign the whole meaning of that warmth to being cared for is exactly the ache the jacket promised. "Every yes was freely given." She's freely, gladly making meaning out of it. The tenderness is real and the frame is a lie and both are true at once. That's the trap doing its quiet work and it landed on me hard.

The **erotic charge** is real but it's the retold, giggling, girls-on-a-bed kind, and it works *because* it's filtered through Cassie. The "the bra too!" bit with her hands cupping and flying outward — funny and hot at once. But the peak is when the comedy drops out: "It was like his eyes asked, *May I look.*" That's the "may I" signature landing in Vee's own mouth now, unprompted, and it's the moment Cassie stops laughing and changes key. And then the withheld card — the whole chapter is structured around Vee sitting on one thing "all the way at the bottom" — "**I was grinding on him.**" That's the discovery from the fitting (the "new power, and started spending it") given to me plainly for the first time, and giving it to *me* through her telling *Cassie* is better than watching it, because I get her delight in having done it. "Her whole face going warm and delighted and a little wicked." That's a woman who has found something and is thrilled by it.

Pace, secondhand: every detail Cassie surfaces makes him stranger and more magnetic — the shirt with no tag she slept in all week without solving, the silk he went *deeper* on than she described, the riser built in the corner. Cassie's "**he's a bodybuilder who makes dresses**... Are you sure he's not gay?" is genuinely the sane outsider's read, and it made me laugh, and it also quietly underlines how *anomalous* this man is. A normal explanation would be reassuring. There isn't one. Cassie can't make the pieces fit — "the way she worked a puzzle when the pieces wouldn't fit" — and she's the clean witness who keeps almost-sensing the machine. She's circling it with a joke.

The one thing I'll flag as very light friction: this chapter is entirely a **recap** of A Round, and I already read A Round. Some of it is literally re-narrated. It earns its place because the *frame* is new (Vee's delight, Cassie's reading, the withheld grinding reveal), and because the too-warm-house beat gets a second, deeper pass that it needed. But there's a stretch in the middle — the measuring, the bra, the hem — where I'm being walked back through geometry I already stood inside. It's shorter and lighter than the original so it doesn't drag badly, but I felt the doubling.

**The title — "Turned Up."** On the surface it's the heat: "He turned the heat up before I got there." That's the literal hook. But on a second beat it's doing more — Vee's *turned up*, the way a song is, the whole chapter is her cranked bright and giddy; and there's the shirt she "turned the whole thing inside out" looking for a tag. And, colder, someone *turned up* the temperature of this whole thing on Vee without her clocking it — the phrase has a manipulated-thermostat quality that fits the too-warm-house dread. It doesn't give anything away, but it points cleanly at the chapter's warmest and most double image. I like it.

**The book's title and tagline** sit where they've sat: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is almost a demonstration of it — nothing is done *to* Vee here, she's radiant and free and choosing, telling her best friend, laughing, and I'm the one feeling the floor under it. *With a Long Spoon* still reads to me as "sup with the devil with a long spoon" — keep your distance from the thing you're feeding with. Vee has no spoon at all; she's climbing into the bowl gladly.

**What I want next:** the dress isn't finished — "He'll call me when it's ready." So there's a fitting-to-come, or a delivery, or the induction itself in burgundy. And Randi has been gone a *long* time; her absence is loud. *Guess from here:* the dress finishing and the induction are where Randi comes back into the frame, and I keep dreading the moment Vee's two worlds touch — that Randi and Pace are one machine is the fact sitting under every warm scene, and this chapter, by being so purely happy, tightened that dread rather than loosening it.
