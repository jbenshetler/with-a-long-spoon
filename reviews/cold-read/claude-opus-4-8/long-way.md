# Cold read (grounded) — The Long Way

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This chapter is the cruelest kind of lovely: it works on me exactly as a first-date scene is built to, and I spent the whole of it sick to my stomach because I know what Vee doesn't. She thinks she's been chosen out of a crowd by a kind, attentive man. I watched Randi *shop* for her on that quad. I heard "Do you think she'll like the bench?" So every warm beat here lands double — once as swoon, once as trap, and the two don't cancel, they amplify. That's the chapter's whole engine and it's brutally effective.

Taken purely as romance, it's genuinely swoony. "He asked her things the way her grandfather used to ask things, as though the answer mattered and there was no wrong one, and then he waited through all of it." That got me — the ache of being *waited on*, of being the thing worth hearing out, when you've spent your life being the one who smooths and fills the gaps for everyone. Vee naming it — "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo" — is so disarmingly honest it hurt. And "Your tart's holding up," landing instead of a reassurance, a man letting "the true thing have its space" without inflating it — that's a real, specific tenderness. If I didn't know the frame, I'd be rooting without reservation.

But I do know the frame, and so the thing I actually feel toward Pace is *dread dressed as charm.* The froyo system — "tart on the bottom holds up under the heavy ones" — read to me instantly as a thesis statement for his whole method: build the structure that survives the weight you're about to add. "I've thought about most things." Yes. He has. He's thought about *her*. The attentiveness I'd call a virtue in a vacuum is, here, the exact instrument. He is doing to Vee what he did to Randi on the bench — the slow hand, the taking-his-time, the refusal to hurry — except Randi at least knew she'd walked into a scene. Vee thinks she's met a nice grad student at a coffee shop.

And Randi — Randi is the part that genuinely chills me now. "He wants to see you naked." Said grinning, to the girl she is delivering. The warmth she pours over Vee (established over three chapters as a weapon, a face, a lie the face is good at) is now aimed at steering her straight into bed with the man who built the bench. Vee's whole floating joy — the right pen, the high table, *love you girl* — is the mark being kept happy. I can't un-feel that. The dramatic irony is so loaded it tips into something close to horror.

Cassie remains my one point of safety and the person I ache for. "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" — that history, Vee up till two keeping someone alive in a game, tells me exactly who Vee is (the sustainer) and who Cassie is (the one who notices). Cassie's refusals, her flat "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter — she's reading something none of them are saying aloud, and the book keeps letting her be right. I trust her completely and I'm frightened for both of them.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** this is no longer suspicion — the text handed me the trap outright in the pointing-game chapter, so I'm reading *past* Pace's warmth as a matter of knowledge, not instinct. The chapter itself offers no on-page tell; Pace is flawless, which is the point. What unsettles me is precisely the flawlessness.

**Erotic charge:** low and almost entirely deferred. This is a froyo date with no touch to speak of — the heat is all Randi's narration ("He wants to see you naked") and the knowledge of where the long way leads. The charge is anticipatory and queasy, not hot. It simmers only because I know the bench is waiting.

**Friction:** very little — it's a clean, short, well-made scene. If anything, the froyo-system metaphor is *almost* too on-the-nose as a key to Pace ("an argument the whole way down"), but it's charming enough in his mouth that I forgave it. The chapter's brevity is right; it doesn't overstay.

**The titles:** "The Long Way" — they "walked the long way, not toward anything," and last chapter Vee "went home the long way... warm the whole way." The long way is Pace's method entire: the slow seduction, the patience that reads as tenderness, taking the scenic route to a destination already chosen. It's the froyo walk and it's the whole campaign against Vee. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — "he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon." That proverb is the author telling me flatly: someone here is dining with something dangerous, and the question is who's holding the long spoon and who forgot one. "A Polite Invitation" — that's *"may I call you,"* that's froyo, that's the warmth. The politeness *is* the danger. And the title *The Long Way* set against that proverb makes me read this sweet chapter as the patient approach of the trap.

**What I want / dread next:** I dread the bench, obviously. I want — hopelessly — for Vee to keep something back, the way Randi did behind her face. (*Guess from here:* I suspect the book is setting Vee up to go where Randi wouldn't, or to be the one who sees through it; and I suspect Cassie is the tripwire.) Mostly I want to keep reading because the irony is unbearable and I have to know how far in she goes before she knows.
