# Felt read

So this is the froyo date, and it's Vee's POV again, and God, it's the gentlest chapter yet and that's exactly what makes it hurt.

The opening lands physically: "Her stomach went before she did, the drop of a stair missed in the dark." That's the phone lighting up — the phone she left face-down last chapter, letting herself want it to light up. It lit up. And there's this small detail that told me something: "She'd given him hers; it hadn't occurred to her to ask for his." He kept the asymmetry. He holds the initiative. She didn't even notice she'd handed it to him.

Vee's defense — "Is he desperate? Texting this fast?" — and her own narration naming it: "It was easier to go looking for the flaw than to sit in the wanting." I love her for this and I ache for her. She *knows* she's flinching from the wanting. She just can't route around it.

And then Randi. Randi takes the phone out of her hand — takes it, physically — and reads it, and delivers the verdict: "He looked like a man who wants you... He wants to see you naked." That's Randi doing the thing she does — naming the shame-adjacent want out loud and handing it back as permission. And it's Randi shutting down the one protective instinct Vee has: the wait-a-day rule. "The rule's for boys you're not sure about. You're not unsure." That's smooth and it's *effective* and it's Randi closing the last door between Vee and the thing Randi and Pace built. I felt the dread there — Randi isn't just cheering, she's steering, and she knows the exact lever ("you're not unsure") because she's already decided the outcome. "Vee wasn't, and all three of them knew it, and that was the whole debate." All three. The line quietly counts Cassie in, and Cassie's already said her goodbye.

Cassie's healer line — "As long as he doesn't make you roll a healer again" — is the one bit of the real, funny, ordinary Vee-and-Cassie friendship, a whole history in two lines (a semester up until two keeping a business major alive). It's warm and it's also the only voice not pushing. Cassie doesn't push *toward* Pace here, notably. She just makes a joke about a past guy. She's holding her tongue about this one.

Then the date. And here's where I have to be honest: the erotic charge is almost zero and the *tenderness* charge is enormous, and it's working on me hard, in the worst way. Pace does the froyo-as-system bit — "Tart on the bottom holds up under the heavy ones. You put chocolate on tart and it's an argument the whole way down" — and it's charming and it's *exactly* the calculus move again: turn the thing in front of you into a small solvable system, be the person who's thought about most things. "I've thought about most things." He said it without apology. Of course he has. He thought about *her* for a whole pointing game before he ever crossed a room.

The froyo cup getting away from her — "too much, the cone of it leaning. Usually she heard her mother before she'd finished, *that's plenty, that's enough now.* Today there was nothing." This is the one that got me. The mother's policing voice, gone silent for the first time. She lets the cup lean. She bites her lip knowing it'll drip and "found she didn't care." That's the seduction working at the deepest level — not toward sex yet, but toward *unbuttoning the shame*. Randi does it with the naming; Pace does it just by being present and un-judging so the internal warden goes quiet. Both of them are dismantling the exact thing (the mother, the concealment, the "floozies") that the earlier chapters set up as Vee's cage. And it reads as liberation. That's the trap the jacket keeps promising: it *feels* like being freed.

"He asked her things the way her grandfather used to ask things, as though the answer mattered and there was no wrong one, and then he waited through all of it." The waiting. "She wasn't used to being the thing a person waited on." This is the jacket's "listens like no one ever has / attends the way no one ever has" made concrete, and I believe it as an experience for Vee completely. And the whole time I'm reading it I'm sick, because I know — from the bench, from the pointing game, from the cup signals — that this attention is a built thing.

The ending: "This is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time. And it's froyo." Her embarrassed offer to let them both pretend she didn't say it — and "He didn't pretend. He didn't make it large either." He lets the true thing have its space, and answers with "Your tart's holding up," and it *is*. That callback is lovely and I hate how lovely it is. He gave her permission to overfill the cup and then confirmed she was right to. The tart holding up under the heavy stuff — I can't not read it as him telling her she can bear what's coming. Or telling himself.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** unchanged and total on Pace and Randi — the machinery is confirmed, so now every kindness reads double. What's new is that this chapter gave me almost nothing to *catch him* on. He was genuinely, unimprovably good to her. That's the unsettling part: there's no false note to point at. Either he's that good a performer or — the jacket's threat — he's actually falling and it's real, which doesn't make it less of a trap. Cassie remains my one clear eye, and she was quieter here, which itself feels like a held breath.

**Friction:** almost none. The froyo-system speech is *just* slightly on-the-nose as a Pace showpiece — "an argument the whole way down" is a hair writerly for a guy at a counter — but it's in character and Vee's "You've thought about this" earns it back. No thumb on the scale. If anything I trust the restraint: nobody explained to me that the silence-of-the-mother was significant; it just sat there and I felt it.

**Titles:** "The Long Way" — they "walked the long way, not toward anything," and that's the literal surface: the unhurried date, Pace's whole no-hurry register, the pleasure of not going straight at the thing. But it recolors immediately — the *long way* is the method. Pace never goes straight at what he wants; he took the long way through the coffee shop, through Euler, through Randi, through months of a game before this froyo. The long slow approach *is* the trap. Also faintly: "the long way" as in the long game, the patient hunt. It doesn't give anything away — it just names, tenderly, the very thing that scares me.

*With a Long Spoon* / "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the purest illustration of the tagline so far. Every single yes here was freely given: yes to the text, yes to skipping the wait-rule, yes to the froyo, yes to the walk, yes to the confession. All hers, all glad, all free. And Randi's "you're not unsure" is the trap dressed as a friend removing your last hesitation. "Sup with the devil with a long spoon" — keep your distance from the dangerous thing. Vee is being taught, kindly, to put the spoon down.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the call, the next escalation, and I dread it in equal measure. Guess from here: the froyo date's whole function was to un-silence the mother's voice, so the next step reaches for the body — Pace's "may I," something small and asked-for that Vee gives freely and is amazed to want. And I keep waiting for the two worlds to touch: for Vee to be somewhere and catch the cold-citrus scent, or a signal, or a look between Pace and Randi she isn't meant to see. Cassie's quiet worries me — she knows, and she's not saying.
