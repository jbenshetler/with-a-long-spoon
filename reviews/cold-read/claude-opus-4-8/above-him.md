# Cold read — Above Him

*scene: scenes/above-him.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: one-bite*

## Reader reaction

This is the quietest chapter yet and one of the most sinister, precisely because nothing "happens." No Randi, no harvest, no watchers — just Vee alone in Peter's house, in his robe, brushing out her hair, feeling loved. And because I'm carrying everything I'm carrying, I read every warm thing in it as a screw turning.

The felt experience first, honestly: it's tender and it aches. The reunion after a week apart — the door swinging wide, being lifted off the ground, that "bright stupid sound she'd have died of anywhere else" — is genuinely lovely, and my body responded to it as romance before my head caught up. The slow lovemaking, the salmon, the coming back to her an hour later "like she was something he'd been turning over the whole time the salmon cooked" — it's warm and it works on me as warmth. That's the horror of it. The chapter is doing to me exactly what he does to her: seducing with care.

But three things went cold in my stomach.

First, the weight loss. "She'd been losing weight since she met him, her body quietly burning down whole weekends of him, and she'd never asked it to." She reads this as happiness — she eats "like a field hand" now and her clothes go looser. I read it as her body literally disappearing into him. The line "her budget had never once stretched to the tailor" also lands hard — the debt is still there, unspoken, under the abundance of his table. She's being fed lavishly by a man while she shrinks.

Second — and this is the chapter's engine — the drawer. "He'd gone and made her a place instead." Her whole kit "colonized" the one clean surface (her word, *colonized* — the text handed me the exact word I've been using for what's being done to her), and instead of minding, he folds his entire ordered self into one drawer and gives her the top one, "the one your hand goes to first," "laid out like something prepared and waiting." She reads it as devotion so deep it needs no words. I read it as the "so it stays" motif made domestic: he is installing her, building her a fitted joint, making the arrangement permanent and load-bearing before she's chosen it. "Room for more of her." That's not tenderness, that's expansion of holdings. The scrunchies-in-plenty motif has graduated from a bathroom counter to a dedicated drawer — permanence disguised as tenderness, exactly the pattern, dialed up.

Third: "He had never once said it to her." She loves him; she has decided his acts *are* the saying and that she can "live clear inside the everything-he-did and not go hungry for the saying." From here that reads as her doing his work for him — manufacturing the declaration he withholds, so that he never has to make one, never has to be on record, never gives her anything to hold him to. The withholding is the trap and she's calling it fullness.

And the scar again: "she'd still never asked about and still meant to keep." She's now made the not-asking into a *gift she gives him*, a kept confidence. Another door she's chosen to leave shut.

The whispered "Peter" to the sleeping shape of him — that undid me a little. She reaches past "Pace" to the real name, the private man, believing she's touching the truest thing. I know "Peter" is the person under the operator, and I know she has no idea there's an operator at all.

**Trust/suspicion:** No new suspicion of Peter *from Vee's side* — she's more gone than ever. My own reading is unchanged: everything on this page is a move, and its very gentleness is the tell. The one thing I genuinely can't call is the interiority — the flinch last time made me wonder if he's slipping, and this chapter, for the first time, gives me *only* Vee's read of him and no crack. He sleeps "gone the moment it was over." I don't know if that's real exhaustion (a real body, a real want) or just the off-switch of a man who's done his work. The text withheld his inside entirely, and I noticed the withholding.

**Erotic charge:** Present but not the point — the reunion and the couch are recalled, not rendered close. The heat is in the *care*, not the acts. It simmered; it never went graphic, and it wasn't trying to.

**Friction:** Very little thumb on the scale — this is the most restrained chapter in a while, and it trusts the drawer to do the work without narrating its meaning. The closest to over-help is "He hadn't told her it didn't matter. He'd gone and made her a place instead" — but even that stays in her voice and her misreading, so I forgave it. The word "colonized" is doing a lot; a hair on-the-nose given my ledger, but it's *her* self-deprecation, so it reads clean.

**The title:** *"Above Him."* Literally, she's lying on her side above the sleeping man, watching him, "happier than she'd known a person was let to be." That's the innocent surface: she has risen into a love she never thought she was allowed. The reread-charge, though — she believes she's *above* him, ascended, chosen, the luminous one. She is in fact the one being folded into a drawer built to hold her. She thinks she's above him; she's inside his joinery. The title flatters her exactly the way the drawer does.

**The book's signals:** *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the tagline at its most naked and most gentle. There is no coercion anywhere on the page. She *chooses* the drawer, chooses the name, chooses not to ask, chooses to call the silence a saying. Every yes freely given. And the "long spoon" — the distance you eat the devil's dinner with — here it's *her* distance from her own situation: she keeps choosing not to close the gap (the scar, the debt, the word he won't say), and calls the distance peace.

**What I want/dread:** I want the two-hand reveal and I dread what it'll cost her, because this chapter shows me exactly how deep the foundation is now poured. *Guess from here:* the drawer is a threshold — the next move is her *moving in*, some version of it, more of her things migrating out to his house, the debt and Cassie and her mother all still blind. And I'm bracing for Randi to reappear and for me to watch Vee carry *this* — the drawer, the love, the "Peter" — straight back to the person feeding it all to him.
