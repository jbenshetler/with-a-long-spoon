## Reader reaction

# Reader reaction — "Coming Due"

Oh, this one lands like a slow tightening. After the fever of the mixer chapter, I came in braced for aftermath, and what I got was something quieter and more frightening: the discovery that the thing she did on that dancefloor didn't *close*. It's not an incident anymore. It's a condition. The title told me before I read a word — *coming due* — and by the end I understood it as a debt. Marsh even hands me the frame in her lecture, reciprocity, the gift as a thing that "wants to go home," the obligation "to give and to take and to pay back," a man who "could beggar his whole house handing things away." That's Vee. She's been taking from Randi for months — the world, the coaching, the shoes, the sword handed over like a queen lending it — and now something's come due and she has no idea the size of what she owes or to whom.

What moved most in me is Vee herself. In the last two chapters she was performing bravado — the pleasure princess, the court reconvening, the whole delicious retelling. Here the performance is *gone* and she knows it's gone. The image that undid me: standing at the closet rail "with a hanger in each hand, and her hands stopped being any use," unable to say "whether she was dressing to be looked at or dressing to be covered up, and the not-knowing was worse than either answer." That's the whole chapter in one gesture. She's lost the ability to steer her own wanting, and — this is the part that aches — she's smart enough to feel it happening and too frightened to name it. "Not one name she'd give to any of it." The girl who narrates everything to somebody has hit the one thing she can tell no one, because the one person she'd tell is the thing itself.

The Lifesaver moment is where the chapter got its hooks in me physically. It's *nothing* — a candy passed in a lecture, a fingertip on the inside of a wrist, "the same as any of the few classes they'd been doing this" — and it goes through her "low and complete," she nearly drops the roll, and she sits there insisting *static, static* while it goes on not being static. That's the erotic engine of the whole book working in miniature: the smallest touch charged past bearing, and the body telling the truth the mind refuses. It's Pace's grammar (the wrist! he parked at her wrist) coming back on her from Randi's hand, and I don't think Vee heard that echo. I did, and it chilled me a little.

My feeling about **Randi** has sharpened into real dread. She is doing *nothing* a priest could catch — and that's exactly the horror the chapter names outright: "over nothing anyone could have taken to a priest, and the heat came up for her all the same." But I've known since page one what Vee doesn't, and so "I've wanted to do that for the longest time" and "having you at the dance was so great" don't read to me as a friend's warmth. They read as a woman who has been patient for a very long time watching the fruit finally ripen. And then the neck. Vee turns her mouth away from the goodbye kiss *on purpose* — a real act of self-protection, the first one she's managed — and Randi simply finds the side of her neck instead, the exact spot, "the exact place she'd put her own mouth twice and been told not to weaponize," and laughs, *"How do you like it?"* That flipped something in me. Weeks ago Randi flinched from that kiss and forbade it. Now she deploys it, delighted, unsorry. She has stopped defending the border and started crossing it. And the Standards line — "I've got to go discipline a bad girl" — dropped into Vee's hair while still holding her, is Randi feeding her exactly the spanking-adjacent charge she knows lights her up. That's not accidental. Nothing Randi does is accidental; the earlier chapters taught me that in the parking lots and the loft.

The swoon in this chapter is real but it's a *dark* swoon — I wasn't rooting for anyone to have anyone. I was watching a person come apart under a pleasure she can't refuse and can't afford. The heat is genuinely working on me, but it's laced with the same nausea Vee's feeling: the wrist, the neck, the sleepless bunk with her hand held flat on her stomach as "a lid held down on a pot." That last is the truest erotic image in the chapter precisely because nothing happens — she *won't* let her hand go lower "because as long as she didn't give it the thing that would make it true, it could stay only wanting." She's rationing her own body to keep a truth from becoming real. That's devastating and it's hot in the specific bruised way this book does best.

And then the ending. Cassie — my safe harbor, still and always — comes in cold, takes "the measure of Vee's face," and asks the plainest question there is: *"What was that with you and Randi?"* Not flat this time. Worry under it, "a thread of disbelief she didn't trouble to hide." And Vee has *nothing*. "She didn't know. That was the whole of the trouble... her oldest friend had asked her the plainest question there was, and she could not answer it." I love that the chapter ends on Cassie, because Cassie has been right since "Goodbye, Vee" in chapter one, and here she is again, the one clean instrument in the room, registering that something is wrong that Vee can't even name. It's the book handing me my own dread through the one character I trust.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** My suspicion of Randi isn't new but it's never been this earned by the immediate text — the reversal on the neck kiss, the *"having,"* the *"discipline a bad girl,"* all in one short scene, is the page doing it, not my prior knowledge. And there's a fresh unease about **Pace** curdling under this that the checkpoint set up: he *told* her, in the dark, that she was free to touch herself thinking of whatever — "the message ran the other way, and Pace had made a point of telling him so." Knowing what I know about him and Randi and the game, his permission reads less like generosity and more like a door being held open for her to walk through toward exactly where they've always been steering her. I can't prove that from this chapter alone, so I'll mark it a guess from here — but the way her wanting keeps flowing toward Randi while Pace *encourages* the current makes the trap feel like it's closing on schedule.

**Friction:** Almost none. If I'm honest, the Marsh lecture-as-thesis is getting close to the author's thumb — "reciprocity, tonight, the three obligations, to give and to take and to pay back" lands a hair too neatly on top of a chapter literally titled *Coming Due*, and I noticed the machinery for a second. But it's brief and Vee "slid off" it herself, which half-excuses it. Otherwise the chapter is all interior and it earned every line of it.

**What I want / dread next:** She asked for brunch. Saturday. She turned her mouth from the kiss and then *invited the whole table back* — that's the pot boiling over even as she holds the lid. I dread and want that brunch in equal measure. My guess from here: Randi's been waiting, and Vee just walked toward her. And somewhere out west of town is a man who told her she was free to want this, and I don't think that was innocence.
