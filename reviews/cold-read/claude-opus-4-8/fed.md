## Reader reaction

This one made my skin crawl in the best/worst way, and it did it entirely with warmth. I've watched Randi work Vee before, but here I got to sit at the table for the whole extraction, front to back, and it's masterful and awful. She opens with her own "sad little story" — the beautiful ex-boyfriend, the boy in a hurry — and I clocked instantly what she was doing: she goes first, she offers a small self-deprecating disclosure, she makes cost into a costless story, and she does it *to lower the price of admission*. "I have to tell you my sad little story first, to clear the air, and then you are going to make me feel so much worse." That's priming. Give a little, get everything. I've seen this exact shape from her before and it's chilling to watch it run clean.

And it works completely. The thing that got me most is that Vee's *own hunger to tell* is the engine. "how much she had wanted, all week, to set it down on a table in front of someone who would not flinch." That's the trap from inside her body again — she isn't being pried open, she's *dying* to be opened, and Randi is simply the one who won't flinch. Cassie got the clean version and let the door close where Cassie closed it; Randi gets more "because Randi was not Cassie, because Randi would not flinch." So the very tenderness that made Cassie safe is what makes Randi dangerous — Cassie's kindness protected Vee, Randi's "kindness" is a scalpel.

The extraction beats are so precise I almost want to quote all of them. The nails — "You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands. You did those Friday. For him." Randi doesn't accuse; she *names*, and lets the heat come up in Vee's throat and doesn't say the rest "and did not have to." The polish color she can't explain (the silk, the dress he's still making her, the Ulta aisle — that killed me, the deliberateness of it). The underwear: "That's not nothing, that's a *campaign*." Every beat converts Vee's passivity ("he was patient, he was gentle") into agency — "you keep saying *he*... like you just stood there and let it happen to you." And she lands it: "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." Randi says "Good," lays her hand over Vee's, "and Vee felt the word settle into her and stay."

Here's what unsettles me most: on the surface, Randi is *right*, and what she's doing looks like liberation. She's dissolving Vee's shame, reframing "confession" into "campaign," telling her she's lucky not caught. And it's true — Vee *did* want it, Vee *did* choose. But I know what Vee doesn't: this is the same craft Pace runs, and Randi is farming her, and this "friend she'd wanted at fifteen" is an instrument. The line "who could put a name to what Vee was only feeling and hand it back to her clean" — that's exactly the reframe/ladle mechanism, except now it's being done *to* Vee by someone she trusts absolutely, and Vee is grateful for it. The single scariest sentence: "how much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to, and a thread of fear went through her." She *feels* it — the fear is right there — and Randi covers her hand and converts it: "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." The one moment Vee's body warns her, Randi disarms the warning. That's the whole book in a gesture.

The kiss at the curb: this time Vee "half-expected it... and met it," and "didn't look down the street for who might have seen them, the way she had the first time." So the acclimation is measurable — Randi is stepping her forward, and Vee's own instinct to hide is being sanded off. That progression frightened me more than the kiss itself.

Erotic charge: this is mostly a *charged* chapter, not an explicit one — but the retelling of the after (the sheet, the wine, the black window, him cooking, "nothing missing") is genuinely warm and did work on me, and the extraction has its own erotic tension, the slow undressing of a secret. The heat is in the *telling*, which is the point. It peaks at the toes/campaign beats and the hand-over-hand.

Friction: almost none. If anything, I noticed how *good* Randi's dialogue is — occasionally I wondered if she's too flawless an operator to be quite human, but the text keeps her plausible as a person Vee would adore. The one place I felt the author's hand lightly is the closing summary paragraph ("Not the way Pace was easy — Pace was the thing the week bent around") — it's a little explaining, telling me the taxonomy of the two relationships. But it's Vee's own thought and it's doing work (drawing the Pace/Randi contrast for her while I hear the rhyme she can't), so I forgive it.

The title, "Fed": oh, it lands hard. On the surface it's brunch — she's literally fed, mimosas and egg things. But by the end "Fed" means Randi has been *fed* — "give me something to live on... I'm running on fumes here. How was he?" Vee is the meal. Randi came hungry and left full, and Vee thinks *she's* the lucky one who got to eat. It recolors the whole chapter: the person being fed isn't Vee. And it rhymes with "Famished" in my memory — the hunger motif keeps turning: Pace hungry "for me... singular," Vee hungry to be told, Randi feeding on the telling. Everyone's eating someone.

Series/book titles: *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon." Vee is supping with no spoon at all; she's handing herself across the table by the fistful. *A Polite Invitation* / "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the purest demonstration yet. Every disclosure was freely given, *wanted*, and that wanting is the mechanism. "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky" is the tagline weaponized in dialogue.

What I want/dread: I want to know what Randi *does* with what she just harvested — does it go to Pace? (Guess from here: yes; this is intel, and the two-sided operation means Pace now "knows" the nail color, the campaign, the sheet, through Randi.) I dread the convergence more than ever. And I keep waiting for the two towels / the second person, and this chapter makes me even more sure the second towel is Randi.
