# Cold read (grounded) — Fed

## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This is a quieter chapter than the ones around the first time with Pace, and I think that's deliberate — it's the morning-after and the debrief, two rooms, two women who want the story from her, and the whole ache of the thing is in what she gives each of them. I came in happy for Vee and I leave happy for her and faintly sick for her, which is the exact doubled feeling this book keeps producing in me.

The dorm scene with Cassie landed softest and truest. That beat where Vee half-unzips her skirt for the shower and *stops* — "the next thing she took off would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say" — is the best thing in the chapter. She gave the panties away as a trade, so there's literally nothing between her and her roommate but the thing she can't tell, and her body won't let her undress until Cassie's gone. That's shame and intimacy fused into one small physical refusal, and it moved me more than any of the sex talk. Cassie reading her "fast and whole," aiming the spoon, "Don't wait up, lover" — she's warm and clean and *wrong* ("kindly, certainly, wrong"), and I trust her completely and dread what she doesn't know. The ladle image — lifting the middle out so "the surface closes without a hole" — is Vee telling herself editing is kindness, and the narration quietly calling it the lie that lets her keep what she wants. I felt the self-deception named without being scolded.

Then the brunch, which is the engine, and here my unease sharpened to something specific. Randi's "sad little story" about the beautiful old boyfriend who was "so nervous, and in such a hurry, like the building's on fire" — *I* clocked that as Pace. The reader knows Randi and Pace are the couple; the checkpoint has Pace as patient, the opposite of hurried, so either she's spinning a fiction to make Vee feel safe talking, or she's dropping a tiny cruelty only she and I can hear — her lover, recast as a disappointment, to prime Vee to pour out how *un*-hurried he was with her. "At least I got dinner out of it ahead of time… before he lowered my expectations." It's a feint. And it works beautifully: Vee gives her everything. That's the chill under the warmth.

And yet — this is what keeps the book honest — the extraction didn't read as purely cold. When Vee's face colors over "frosted plum" and Randi goes "That face isn't about a color, is it," and then catches the thread of fear and says "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky" — I believed the comfort even knowing the hands giving it are mining her. The craft of it is that Randi's best lines are *true*: "You knew how that night was going to end before you ever got in the car… So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" That's good for Vee. It hands her permission. The horror is that permission is the bait.

The one moment the page itself tipped its hand to me: Randi's voice slurring warm, the esses running long, and Vee thinking *she's a little drunk* — then checking the glass, one mimosa barely touched, "So it wasn't that. Nothing came along to take its place." The text deliberately opened a gap and left it ajar. Something else is lifting Randi's voice. Arousal, hunger, appetite of her own — I don't know, and I like that I don't; it's the first crack where Randi's "face" slips in front of me and even *she* maybe doesn't control it. That's the most interesting half-second in the chapter.

Erotic charge: lower than the consummation chapters, but it's genuinely there, and it's displaced — the heat is in the *telling*, the sheet-and-wine midnight painting, "hungry. For me… Like singular," and Randi drawing it out of her. "You bought them hoping he'd give you no reason to keep them on." The charge runs between the two women more than toward the absent Pace, which is its own quiet escalation. The curb kiss — second press, and this time Vee "didn't look down the street for who might have seen" — is the real needle-move. She's stopped flinching. That small surrender frightened me more than it warmed me.

Swoon: the sheet-wrapped midnight kitchen, honestly. "neither of us saying a word and nothing missing… wrapped up in him, watching him cook." Randi's right, it's a painting, and I let it work on me. Vee's "I wanted it. And I took it. For once," hair drawn through her fingers — that's the book's whole thaw in one line, and I rooted for it even while the person receiving it is the trap.

Friction: almost none. The ladle and the closing-soup-surface metaphors come close to over-explaining the editing, but they earned it. My only resistance is structural fatigue — this is now the *third* brunch-debrief-with-Randi-extracting-details beat, and the shape is getting familiar. It's done well enough that I forgive it, but if the next chapter is a fourth lunch, I'll feel the groove.

**The titles.** "Fed" — this chapter is the aftermath of being fed, and it recolors the word: Vee is the one being *fed* lines, permission, comfort, by Randi, while she thinks she's the one feasting. Being fed and being fattened for something are the same verb here. The volume title *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil must use a long spoon" — sits right over this chapter: Vee at a table being served by someone she should keep her distance from, and she's leaning in instead. *A Polite Invitation* as Book One suggests we're still in the courtship, the invitation — the trap hasn't sprung. That frightens me pleasantly.

What I want next: I want Cassie to catch the seam she didn't pull. I want to know what lifted Randi's voice. And I dread, as a guess from here, that the "threesome, with you" line is the invitation this volume is named for, and it's coming soon.
