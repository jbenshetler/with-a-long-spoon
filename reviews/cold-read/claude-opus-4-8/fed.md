## Reader reaction

This is the scene I've been bracing for since Cassie, and it landed exactly where I dreaded — but the thing that actually made my stomach drop wasn't the extraction. It was Randi's "sad little story" up front.

**Randi's old boyfriend.** She opens with a beautiful man she ran into, dinner first, then bed, and "the poor boy tries, he does try, but he's so nervous, and in such a hurry, like the building's on fire." "At least I got dinner out of it ahead of time." I read that and went cold. *That's Pace.* Or it's built to rhyme with Pace so precisely — the beautiful man, the dinner, the bed — that I can't unsee it. And she's telling it to the woman who just spent the night with him. Whether it's literally a recent encounter or a decoy she's spinning, the function is the same and it's vicious in a way Vee can't feel: it pre-installs a frame ("beautiful men are a disappointment, all hurry") right before Vee hands over her own beautiful-man-at-a-stove night. And it sets Randi up as the one who got *dinner and nothing else*, so Vee's fumbling generosity — telling her more, feeding her — reads to Vee as consoling a friend on fumes. I don't fully trust that she "ran into" anyone. The text earned my suspicion here: the parallel is too exact, and I already know these two share a bed I'm not supposed to forget. The chapter is called "Fed." Who's feeding whom.

**The extraction itself.** This is the "middle" Vee protected from Cassie — "the way you fold a thing back into its tissue before anyone can pick it up" — and Randi picks it up anyway, gently, one finger at a time. And here's the horrible craft of it: she never demands the sex. She lets Vee keep "the middle" soft and quick. What she takes instead is worse — the *evidence of wanting*. The toes ("You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands. You did those Friday. For him."). The polish name, frosted plum, and the face Vee makes that "isn't about a color." The new underwear — "That's not a story, that's a *campaign*." Each one walks Vee to the same confession: *you knew how the night would end before you got in the car.* And Vee, who came in curating, walks out having said the sentence out loud — "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." Randi produces with words what Pace produces with the body: she gets Vee to *announce her wanting*, the exact thing Vee's shame is built to prevent. That's the whole engine and it ran clean.

**How I feel about Randi now.** Worse, and more impressed, which is uncomfortable. Everything she says is genuinely kind on its face — "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." "That's the best thing there is, being that gone on somebody." If I didn't know what I know, I'd want a friend exactly like this. That's what makes her frightening: I can feel her *liking* the planning "like the planning was the part she liked best" — and that line is the plan escaping her. She likes the campaign because the campaign is her product. When Vee's fear flickers — "a thread of fear went through her" — Randi's move is to soothe it away, to convert *caught* into *lucky*, so Vee stops examining the very thing she should examine.

**Vee.** Aching for her. "How much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to" — she feels the edge of it, the fear shows, and then it gets petted down. The line "the friend she'd wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting to get. Who liked her, some days, better than she managed to like herself" — that's the hook in her mouth, and it's the loneliest thing in the chapter. Vee is starving for exactly this and Randi is the one supplying it. Fed.

**Erotic charge.** Lower-key than the physical scenes, and rightly — this is heat by retelling. It works best in the sheet passage: "wrapped up in him, watching him cook," "the oil going in the pan and starting to tick, the window black behind him." That's genuinely lovely and I felt the warmth of it. The charge in the extraction is more about exposure than arousal — the pleasure of being *seen* and the shame of it, fused, which is Vee's whole register. It didn't go clinical anywhere. The goodbye kiss at the curb is soft, almost tender, and I clocked the escalation-marker cleanly: last time she looked down the street for who saw; this time she "met it" and didn't look. Randi's smile "stayed a beat past where it needed to." The physical track is climbing.

**Friction.** Very little. The one place I felt a faint thumb was the roll-call of "the friend she'd wanted at fifteen" paragraph — it's doing a lot of explaining Vee's need to me directly, where the scene had otherwise been showing it. But it's beautifully written and I mostly forgave it. The mimosa pricing bit ("the small arithmetic, the kind that ended in Thursday") is a nice callback to her reading the price column, and the second mimosa she "did not price at all" is a quiet marker of her being carried out of her own carefulness.

**What I want / dread next.** *Guess from here:* that "old boyfriend" story is going to matter — either it IS Pace and Vee will one day recognize the description, or it's a seed Randi planted that'll surface when the two men/worlds collide. I dread the moment Vee learns Randi and Pace already know each other, and I now suspect that revelation will retroactively poison every one of these brunches for her (and me). I want to see whether Pace's side ever registers that Randi is *harvesting* Vee's interior and feeding it back into the plan — do they compare notes? And I'm watching the goodbye kiss: it's one riser higher each time, and I want to know where that staircase is walking her.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Brunch on the bistro sun porch, Randi's pick. Randi opens with a "sad little story" about running into a beautiful old boyfriend — dinner, then bed, but he was nervous and "in such a hurry, like the building's on fire" (the details rhyme uncomfortably with Pace). Then she turns it on Vee. Vee gives her the same clean version she gave Cassie (dinner, apron, Chianti) but goes further — the sheet-wrapped midnight in his kitchen, "wrapped up in him, watching him cook." She still folds away "the middle" (the sex itself). Randi doesn't push for the sex; instead she extracts the *evidence of wanting*: the toes painted Friday "for him," the frosted-plum polish and the face that "isn't about a color," the new underwear ("a *campaign*"). She walks Vee to say it out loud: "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." Reframes Vee's flicker of fear as luck, not being caught. Second mimosa, unpriced. Curbside goodbye kiss on the mouth again — Vee met it this time, didn't look to see who saw.

**Who's who.**

**How I feel.** Tender toward Vee, colder toward Randi than before, and unsettled by how good Randi is at seeming good. The chapter title "Fed" hangs over it: Vee thinks she's feeding a friend on fumes; she's the one being fed a frame. Warm on top, precisely cold underneath — and I could feel the machine run.
