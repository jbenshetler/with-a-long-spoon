## Reader reaction

This one worked on me even as it scared me, and that's exactly the problem — the two feelings ran on the same rail.

Randi opens with her "sad little story," the old boyfriend who was "beautiful" but "so nervous, and in such a hurry, like the building's on fire." I laughed the way Vee did. It's a genuinely funny bit. But knowing what I know — that Randi produces with words what Pace produces with the body, that she extracts — I clocked the shape instantly: she leads with her own small confessed defeat to open the door for Vee's. "I have to tell you my sad little story first, to clear the air, and then you are going to make me feel so much worse." That's not clearing the air. That's baiting the hook with her own self, spending a cheap coin to buy an expensive one. And the line **"the way she made everything that had cost her into a story that had cost her nothing"** — that's Vee half-seeing it and calling it charm.

What got me most was the middle of the extraction, the toenail turn. Randi already knew about the toes — I remember them, the frosted-plum polish survived from the first night. And here she names it: **"You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands. You did those Friday. For him."** Then — this is the move — **"'Mm,' Randi said, warm, and did not say the rest of it, and did not have to."** That's the withheld laugh again, the door closing softly, the exact device from the extraction lunch. She doesn't complete the humiliation; she makes Vee complete it in her own body. The heat comes up in Vee's throat and Randi immediately pivots to "That color, though. It's beautiful. What's it called?" — pulls the pressure off and gives a gift the same second she takes one. That rhythm is hypnotic and it's the coldest thing in the chapter and it's *invisible as cruelty* because it reads as warmth.

And then the reframe that is the whole engine: Vee keeps saying *he* — "he was patient, he was gentle" — like it happened *to* her, and Randi will not let her keep that. **"You keep saying *he.* ... like you just stood there and let it happen to you."** She converts Vee's passivity into agency: the nails, the toes, the bought underwear, "a *campaign*," "You knew how that night was going to end before you ever got in the car. You drove over there sure." This is Randi doing with words what Pace did with his body at the fitting — taking Vee's shame and relocating it as ownership. "So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" And Vee lands on **"I wanted it. And I took it. For once."** Which is — genuinely — liberation. The thesis, ownership of chosen wanting. And Randi says "Good" and covers her hand. On first feel it's the best kind of friendship, the one that likes you better than you like yourself. On the cold read, Randi just walked Vee one more step down her own staircase and taught her to call the descent freedom.

The thing that turned my stomach quietly: Vee gave Cassie "the good version, the whole clean one," and gave Randi *further*. **"because Randi was not Cassie, because Randi would not flinch."** The anchor gets the sheet-and-wine painting; Randi gets the campaign, the confession, the face that "isn't about a color." The gap I've been watching widen — Vee walling off Cassie — widens again, and Randi is now explicitly the one who gets the parts Cassie is denied. "The friend she'd wanted at fifteen and stopped expecting to get." That line is lovely and it's the whole trap: the loneliness Randi is feeding on is real, and old, and fifteen.

**Trust/suspicion:** No new evidence of a *plot* against Vee lands on the page — but I don't trust Randi, and the text keeps arming that. The tell-fidget didn't fire this time; instead the "did not say the rest of it" and the held-past-its-length final smile did the work. The goodbye kiss came due again — the double-press — and the marker I've been waiting on paid off: **"she didn't look down the street for who might have seen them, the way she had the first time."** Vee's shame-governor around Randi is being filed down, exactly as it was filed down around Pace. That's the staircase, and Vee has stopped scanning the windows. That frightened me more than anything Randi said.

**Erotic charge:** Present but low and it's *retold* heat, not live — the sheet-wrapped kitchen at midnight is genuinely gorgeous ("the oil going in the pan and starting to tick, the window black behind him... nothing missing"), and it's warmer than most live sex scenes because it's love remembered. The charge here is really the charge of *telling*, of being extracted — the throat-heat, the face arriving. Call it a 1. The eroticism is in the confession, which is the point and also the tell.

**Friction:** Very little thumb this time — it's a clean, warm, functional brunch and it earns its length. The one place I felt the machine almost show: **"how much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to, and a thread of fear went through her at it and showed before she could catch it"** — and then Randi's "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." That pairing is *almost* too on-the-nose, the fear and the reassurance-that-is-a-lock delivered back to back. But it stayed under the surface tension enough that I bought it as Vee's live panic being soothed. Barely. I noticed it.

**What I want / dread:** I want the two tracks to touch — Vee has now told Randi the near-whole of the first night, including that it happened at *his* house, his kitchen, his sun porch where she saw the silk. Randi is Pace's weekend girlfriend. *Guess from here:* the collision is coming through the material details — the silk dress Pace is making, the sun porch Vee described, the house — some object or sentence that lets Vee's two lives see each other. I dread the induction (Nov, coming) and I dread whatever the bench is for. And I keep waiting for Cassie, who was "wrong for the first time," to be right again — too late.
