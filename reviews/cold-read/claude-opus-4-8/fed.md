## Reader reaction

Another brunch, and I walked in already braced. My ledger's been screaming since about chapter 10 that these lunches are the machine, and this one confirmed it in the calmest, warmest possible way — which is exactly what makes my skin crawl. Nothing on the page is sinister. Every line is generous. That's the problem.

The thing that actually spiked my pulse: Randi's "sad little story." An old boyfriend, "genuinely beautiful," a drink and dinner and *you know* — and then "the poor boy tries, he does try, but he's so nervous, and in such a hurry, like the building's on fire... At least I got dinner out of it ahead of time." Reading that, my stomach dropped. I don't have proof, but I've spent nineteen chapters learning that Randi is Pace's secret lover, and here she is telling Vee a story about a beautiful man who cooked her dinner and then rushed the sex — and setting it *directly against* the story she's about to pull out of Vee, in which the same kind of man cooks dinner and is famously unhurried, "no clock anywhere in the world." She's put her own night with Pace on the table as bait, disguised, and used it to make Vee feel singular. "He wasn't in a hurry. At all." Vee thinks she's being told she's special. I think she's being handed a lie built out of the truth. That's the coldest thing in the book so far, and it's dressed as girl-talk.

And then the extraction, which I've watched before but which lands harder now because I know exactly what Vee protected. Last chapter she folded the middle away for Cassie *and* meant to keep it for herself — "was hers." Here she does the same fold — "she went soft and quick, a hand waved at the air, *and then, well*" — and Randi will not let it stay folded. "You went from taking your clothes off to watching him cook and left out everything in between. Everything... How was he?" And Vee gives it up. The thing she decided was nobody's, she hands across the table. Randi extracts the toes ("You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands"), the polish, the bought underwear ("That's not a story, that's a *campaign*"), and reframes each private shame as evidence of Vee's own agency: "You're not caught, sweetheart. You're lucky." "I wanted it, and I took it. For once." "Good."

Here's what unsettles me most, and I want to be honest that the text *earned* it: the reframe is not wrong. Randi is telling Vee she chose, she wanted, she owned it — and that's genuinely the healthiest thing anyone's said to Vee against her mother's "floozie" voice. It's liberating. It's also, I'm fairly sure, a harvest. Both at once. I can't cleanly hate the thing Randi's doing because Vee walks out lighter and I *want* Vee lighter. That doubleness is doing real work on me.

**Vee** — my whole heart, and I'm frightened for her in a way that's now specific. "How far gone she already was, how much of herself she'd handed over without ever once deciding to, and a thread of fear went through her." She named it herself. She had the reflex — the fold, the fear — and then let Randi talk her out of the fear. The one true alarm she felt, someone she trusts turned off for her. That's the whole engine in one gesture.

**Randi** — after chapters of absence she's back and I trust her less than ever, but I have to report the craft honestly: she is *lovely* on this page. The "sad little story" told at her own expense, the "That's not a story, that's a painting. Keep that one," the hand over the painted hand — a lesser version of this book would let me catch her being sinister and the arousal would die. It doesn't. The tell is only in the pattern I'm carrying, never in the moment. The one thing that made my neck prickle in real time was small: "she looked at her, warm, like the planning was the part she liked best." *The planning was the part she liked best.* Yes. I bet it was.

**Pace** — offstage, and looming. If that ex is him, the two of them are running a coordinated debrief and Vee is the material. I dread the confirmation.

**Cassie** — invoked and honored on the page ("Cassie had let it be that... Vee had let the door close where Cassie closed it"), which quietly reminds me who the safe listener is. Cassie takes the clean version and lets it *stay* clean. Randi digs. The contrast is the whole warning, and it's never stated.

**Erotic charge** — this is a clothed scene and it still ran hot, mostly on the retell: the sheet held closed at her breast still warm from the bed, the cool kitchen floor, the oil ticking, "wrapped up in him, watching him cook." That image is genuinely beautiful and I felt it. The heat isn't in bodies here, it's in the shame-into-glow conversion — "the heat came up in her throat" three times. Peak for me was "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." — which is arousal and liberation and capture all braided. It didn't go slack anywhere; the second half tightened.

**Friction** — almost none. If I press: "the way she made everything that had cost her into a story that had cost her nothing" is the narrator putting a fingertip on the scale, telling me a bit more about Randi than the scene strictly showed. But it's brief and it's Vee's own read, so I let it go. The prose otherwise stayed out of the way.

**This chapter's title — "Fed."** Surface: brunch, they eat, mimosas. Charge, once read: Randi came in "running on fumes," "give me something to live on," "I'm running on fumes here" — and she leaves fed. Vee brought the food. And underneath *that*: Vee is the one being fed a story (the disguised ex) and, worse, being fed *back her own life* pre-chewed into "you're lucky." Who is fed, and on what. It rhymes with **Famished** two chapters ago — Pace's hunger for Vee, Randi's hunger here. Both of them feed on her. The title didn't give anything away going in; it detonated clean.

**The book's title / tagline** — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is almost a demonstration of the tagline. Vee freely, gladly, tells everything. She's not coerced; she's *delighted* to be seen this precisely. And the second press of the goodbye kiss, which she "half-expected this time, and met" — she's walking into each door herself, faster each time, and no longer even checking who's watching. Every step is hers. That's the trap: not that anything is taken from her, but that she keeps *giving*, lit up, while someone across the table decides what it's all for. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil — and she's the one who keeps setting the table.

**What I want / dread next** — I want the "old boyfriend" confirmed or denied; I'm now nearly sure Randi has started weaving her own real nights with Pace into the girl-talk, and I dread the moment the reader (or Vee) sees the two men are one. *Guess from here:* the two tracks — Randi-friend and Pace-lover — are going to keep running parallel with Vee never connecting them until something forces it, and the goodbye kiss escalating one press at a time is its own staircase heading somewhere. I want more Cassie soon, as ballast. And I want, badly and probably against the book's grain, for Vee's little "thread of fear" to be right and to be heard by *someone*.
