## Reader reaction

This chapter jumped me forward — "four weeks in" — and it opens mid-sex, which after two chapters of aftermath and surveillance felt like a plunge back into heat. And it worked on me, hard, right up front. The taste thread is the spine of the opening: he comes up "with his mouth still wet from her," she kisses him "harder for it," and then he lays the question in while she's still undefended — *"Would you like to taste another woman?"* Her body answering before she can — "her sex clenching hard around him" — is genuinely erotic and genuinely awful at once, and the doubleness is the whole charge. I was aroused and I was uneasy about being aroused, which I think is exactly the trap.

**Pace.** My feeling moved, and darkened. Chapter one I thought "tender and calculating at once." This chapter drops the *at once* and shows me the calculation almost naked. He asks the question when he *knows* it's not fair — "It was not fair to ask it now, and he knew it was not fair. He asked it anyway." He reads three orgasms as "evidence." He decides beforehand to slide into her before the first is done so she'll be undefended. And then the daylight game: pointing at strangers, reading her like an instrument, letting her keep her cover story because the cover is the "wrapper the wanting needed to come out in." He's magnetic and he's frightening. The line that chilled me most is the theory of the door: *"A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was… a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there."* That's not a lover, that's a compulsion wearing a lover's manners. I no longer trust his tenderness at all — but I'm still, damningly, drawn to him, which I think is the point.

**Randi.** Sympathy complicated by watching her become the hunter. The chapter is devastating about her self-management — the scrunchie she "allowed herself," deciding "how much of herself the day would get." Her one flinch, then the beautiful recovery where she "moves" the wanting instead of taking it back, handing the appetite to him so she can be "a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy" instead of "a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable." That's heartbreaking and I recognized her from the mirror in chapter one — the same reassembly. But then she *shops*. She stops being a good sport "a quarter mile back," and the collision she executes is a cold marvel. So my sympathy sits next to a new discomfort: she's doing to Vee what Pace is doing to her, one rung down. The "I can't eat that" about the cheeseburger landed like a small knife — the food thing again, casual and total.

**Vee.** New person, and I liked her instantly and protectively. The whole afternoon is a rejection catalogue — the expected, the matched pairs, the closed "project," the room-runner — and then Vee, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." After two chapters of women who are *only* seen, that line made me ache for her. She's the one unmanaged, unposed presence in the book so far, and she's just been marked. I dread what's coming for her. The redhead's hand flying to her own mouth to apologize for a collision Randi engineered — that's the shape of the whole trap in three seconds.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No suspicion needed here — the text isn't hiding the mechanism, it's *showing* me the hunt. If anything the danger is now overt to me and invisible to Vee, which is a different, dreadful kind of tension.

**Friction.** My one real reservation is the density of Pace's interior narration. Because we're deep in his head and he's a compulsive reader-of-people, a lot of the chapter is him telling me what each beat *means* — the flinch, the frame, "the offer was the camouflage and the wanting was hers and entirely hers." It fits his character and it's often gorgeous, but there were stretches where I felt the mechanism explained to me twice — e.g. the whole "Pleasure was not a trick… It was also the bridge. Let the wanting feel good…" paragraph spells out a thing the scene had already dramatized. And the pattern of every rejected girl getting a *reason* ("Too expected," "those two go everywhere together," "She'd be a project," "too much run at people") started to feel like a checklist marching me toward the one who breaks the pattern. I saw the winner coming a few girls out. Not fatal — the prose is beautiful and the reveal still landed — but I felt the author's hand arranging the row.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Vee's POV, badly — I've now met her only as an object of two people's designs and I want inside her. Guess from here: the "stats class together" is the hook; Randi will run the confidante/friendship track while Pace runs the body, and Vee won't know either is aimed at her. I dread the moment Vee trusts one of them. And I'm still holding my chapter-one questions about who "Pace" is under the name — nothing here touched it, which now feels deliberate.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Four weeks into Pace and Randi's affair. Mid-sex, Pace uses the taste of herself on his mouth to plant a question — would she like to taste another woman — and reads three orgasms plus one flinch as proof she wants it but can't own it. Afterward she reframes the want as a gift to him: "only in a threesome, with you." He accepts the frame because it's exactly the frame he needs. Then he takes her on a campus walk and plays "the pointing game" — pointing at women, letting Randi reject each ("too expected," matched pairs, "she'd be a project," room-runners) until a lush red-haired girl comes out of the dining hall laughing, and Randi's managed surface breaks: a bare, unedited "Yes." Pace sends her to "meet her." Randi executes a flawless staged collision, makes the redhead apologize, gets her name and a shared stats class. The redhead is **Vee**. Randi returns glowing; Pace, content, says "Let's go feed you."

**Who's who.**
- **Pace** — the man. Now shown as a compulsive orchestrator: reads Randi as an instrument, plants desires when she's undefended, "cannot leave a forbidden door closed." Tender surface, engineering underneath — and I've watched the engineering directly. Still magnetic, no longer trusted. Real name behind "Pace" still unrevealed.
- **Randi** — his girlfriend/sub. Deeply self-managing (allots how much of herself the world gets; won't eat the burger). Reframed her own want as a gift to survive it. Became the eager hunter on the walk; executed the collision with cold, trained skill. Sympathy plus new unease — she's doing to Vee a rung of what Pace does to her.
- **Vee** — new. Tall, lush, red-haired, freckled, athletic, and *alive* — laughs like she's forgotten she can be seen. The only unposed person so far. Just been marked as the threesome target without knowing it. Shares a stats class with Randi. I'm protective of her and dreading for her.
- **Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey** — sorority (chapter one/two); absent here but still the watching machine in the background.

**How I feel.** The warm-on-top / cold-underneath is now openly cold to me: I'm watching a hunt in daylight. Aroused by the opening, then steadily more dreadful. Pace: fascinating, distrusted. Randi: pitied and slightly feared. Vee: instant tenderness and alarm. Overall mood: beautiful, predatory, sad.

**Open questions.** Who is Pace under the name? Does Randi ever balk at what she's being made into — or turn her steering on him? What happens when Vee (warm, unmanaged) meets Randi in that stats class and the confidante track begins? How much of Vee's "gift" frame will hold, and what breaks it? Will Vee's world and the sorority's surveillance collide? And I want Vee's own POV — soon.
