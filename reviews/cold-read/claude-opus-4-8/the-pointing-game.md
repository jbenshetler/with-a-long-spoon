## Reader reaction

# The Felt Read

So there she is. Two chapters of waiting and the jacket's third girl finally walks out of a dining hall with her head thrown back laughing, and the book has been very deliberate about making me wait for exactly that image. "Her name is Vee." That last page landed hard — I got a little thrill of arrival that I think was earned rather than manufactured.

But the thing I keep turning over is *how* this happened, because the chapter is much stranger and colder than the jacket let on. The jacket told me Randi "picked Vivienne Thorne out across the quad and decided, with him, that she would be the third in their bed." That's true in the letter and a lie in the spirit. What actually happens is that Pace pulls a wanting out of Randi's *body* while he's inside her — asks a question timed to a moment when she can't defend against it — reads her clenching, her flinch, her three orgasms as "evidence," and then spends an entire afternoon running her like an instrument down the quad until she thinks *she's* the one choosing. "He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could." I found that genuinely unsettling. It's seduction as diagnostics.

The opening sex is doing something I've now come to recognize as Pace's whole method, and it's where the tagline suddenly has teeth. "Would you like to taste another woman?" asked *while she's still coming*, deliberately, timed — "It was not fair to ask it now, and he knew it was not fair. He asked it anyway." That's the trap the cover promised, spelled out. He waits for the moment her body will answer before her mind can dress the answer up. Every yes here *is* freely given and also completely rigged. The chapter is almost brazen about telling me this: "A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was… a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there." I believe that about him now. It's not lust exactly — it's compulsion dressed as generosity. He *needs* the forbidden door open.

My feeling about Pace has moved, and not toward him. In chapter one he was magnetic-with-an-undertow; here the undertow is the whole ocean. The narration keeps insisting on his kindness — "what he was bringing her was good," "the gift was moving toward her hands," "letting her keep it was the kindness" — and I don't buy the framing even as I find him fascinating. He's decided in advance that he knows her better than she knows herself, and the entire chapter is him being *right*, which is its own kind of horror. The line "he understood how to close" the gap between what her body wants and what her mind allows — that's a man who has appointed himself the authority on another person's interior. Beautifully rendered, deeply distrusted.

Randi breaks my heart a little more. The move she makes — "she had not taken back the wanting… she had moved it. She had handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument" — is such a precise, sad piece of self-protection, the same reflex I watched in the mirror scene and with Brooke. "Not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable… but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy." And Pace *lets her keep the costume* because the costume happens to be exactly the frame he needs. Two people using the same lie, one of them knowing it's a lie and the other needing not to. Her happy footwork on the brick at the end — "a half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do" — undid me a bit. She's genuinely lit up. She got to want something and be praised for it. That's the trap working on her, and it *feels good*, which is the point.

Vee herself: I only see her from a hundred feet, through two sets of watching eyes, and I already like her more than anyone in the book. She's the one woman on the quad "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Everything Randi is not — unmanaged, unpinned, taking up room, "nothing apologized for." The whole shopping sequence is built as a taste-test that keeps rejecting mirrors and closed doors and performers until it lands on the one person who isn't performing at all. And that's what curdles it: the thing that makes Vee lovely — that she's forgotten she can be seen — is the exact quality being selected *for* by two people whose entire art is seeing. I dreaded for her on sight. She apologized when *Randi* walked into her. She has no idea she's already been chosen, weighed, named as a note in someone else's chord.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** No more ambiguity about whether something's off with Pace — the text has now shown me his mechanism in full daylight, no guilt-tinted lens this time, straight from inside his head. I distrust him completely and I think the book wants me to. Randi I trust as a person and fear for. Vee I trust and dread for.

**Erotic charge:** The opening worked on me — the taste of herself, his mouth still wet, the question landing in her body — genuinely hot, and then the heat turned to unease exactly as it should, which is a hard trick and the chapter pulls it. The quad sequence is charged in a different, cooler key: the kiss where "he had not wiped his face since the bed and he knew the moment her own taste reached her" is a great filthy little beat. It goes slightly clinical in the middle stretch — the parade of rejected girls is more taxonomy than heat — but I think that's deliberate; it's Pace's cataloguing mind, not the author's slack.

**Friction:** The one place I felt the thumb press was the sheer volume of narration telling me what everything meant. "He knew the offer was the camouflage and the wanting was hers and entirely hers." "It was, in fact, the whole of what he was doing out here." I got it the first time; the chapter explains Pace's reading of Randi maybe three times more than I needed. Because I'm inside his head, I can *almost* forgive it as his own compulsive over-reading — but a couple of times it tipped from characterization into the book making sure I didn't miss the thesis.

**The taste thread** — I clocked it hard this time. "You like the taste of yourself." Her own taste on his mouth in bed, then again in the kiss on the quad. And the whole enterprise launched by "Would you like to taste another woman?" Taste is doing a lot of quiet work — appetite, the forbidden mouth, and the food refusals riding right alongside it. Which brings me to the other thing:

**The hunger motif is screaming now.** "I'm hungry," Randi says, and then "No. I can't eat that" about the cheeseburger — automatic, no glance back. Bookended by Pace's two lines about food: nothing at the start, and at the very end "Let's go feed you." A man who feeds her. Set against a mother on egg whites and tofu and a girl who polices every bite. And the whole seduction is framed as appetite she won't let herself have. Feeding and being fed is clearly the spine here — the thing she starves in every register.

**The titles:** "The Pointing Game" is exactly right and slightly sinister once you've read it — the literal pointing down the quad, but "game" is the tell, the same word Pace uses to keep it from becoming "a task, a thing being driven at." It has to *stay* a game so she doesn't notice it's a hunt. And the book's title — *With a Long Spoon* — I finally feel it reaching for me. "He who sups with the devil must use a long spoon." Someone here is supping with something dangerous, and I'm no longer sure the devil is Pace alone — it might be the game itself, the thing all three of them are about to eat at a distance and get pulled toward anyway. The tagline reads now less like a warning to Vee than to *Randi*: her yes was freely given, in bed, before she could think, and that's the trap she walked into first.

**What I want / dread next:** I want Vee on the page as a person, not a note — I want her stats class, her voice, her own head. I dread how good Randi and Pace are going to be at this, because I've now watched the machinery work flawlessly for a whole afternoon. *Guess from here:* the next stretch is Randi befriending Vee for real — and the jacket already told me both founders end up "falling as hard as she is," so my real dread is that the instrument develops feelings the operator didn't plan for. Randi's grin at the end wasn't only appetite. It looked like a girl who just made a friend.

**Story so far**
Ch.1 (The Bench): In Pace's house, Randi came over after lying to her sorority; Pace revealed a homemade bondage/spanking bench, worked her through a long patient cycle until he broke her social performance (naming Brooke), she begged and came crying; aftercare; she flinched at "You're beautiful," locked the bathroom, reassembled her face in the mirror, returned performing contentment; he privately made plans for her; she stayed the night.
Ch.2 (Standards): The morning after. Randi is stopped in the sorority common room by Brooke (with Sloane, Kinsey) for a four-minute, all-smiles interrogation about her "paper" and Pace. She holds the mask, escapes upstairs, and can't function — lies in last night's clothes under an inadequate duvet, and does not cry.
Ch.3 (The Pointing Game): In bed (~4 weeks in), Pace brings Randi off, then — while she's still coming, deliberately, knowing it's unfair — asks if she'd like to taste another woman. Her body says yes helplessly before her mind can; she relocates the wanting into "only in a threesome, with you," a gift for him. Pace, who cannot leave a "forbidden door" shut, lets the frame stand because it's the exact frame he needs. He takes her on a walk on campus and runs a "pointing game" — pointing at women one by one, reading her verdicts (too expected / matched pair / a project / she'd run the room), learning her taste, letting her believe she's choosing for him. Finally a tall, lush, red-haired woman comes out of the dining hall laughing head-back; Randi stops dead and can't look away — a bare "Yes" before the editing. Pace sends her to "go meet her." Randi engineers a clean "accidental" collision so the redhead apologizes, gets a name (Vee) and discovers they share a stats class, and comes back glowing. "Let's go feed you."

**How I feel**
Sympathy for Randi deepening into ache; she's genuinely happy in a trap that felt good. Pace: fully distrusted now, no longer just an undertow — I've watched his machinery run flawlessly and self-justify the whole time; magnetic but chilling. Vee: instant liking and instant dread — the loveliest, most unguarded person in the book, chosen precisely for that. Brooke still an open ambiguity. Overall mood: a golden late-September afternoon with something predatory moving under it; the jacket's "game" has now openly begun, and I'm bracing for it to get "real" for everyone the way the copy warned.
