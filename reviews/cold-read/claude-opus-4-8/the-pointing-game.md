## Reader reaction

So this is it — the chapter the jacket has been promising, the one where the third person walks into frame, and where "the game" stops being a word and becomes a thing I watch happening. And God, it's effective and it's cold and I feel a little sick in a way I think I'm supposed to.

Let me start with the sex, because the chapter does. It opens beautifully — genuinely. Her hand flat on the top of his head "not pushing, just holding, as if to confirm he was there." The distinction between "a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it" — that landed on me as tender, as real intimacy, four weeks in, past the performing. And then he comes up into her "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended. **He had decided this beforehand.**" That sentence is the whole book, isn't it. The tenderness is real and it is timed. He waited for her most undefended second to plant the question. "It was not fair to ask it now, and he knew it was not fair. He asked it anyway."

And here's where I have to check myself against my own ledger — because I've been reading Pace and this unnamed "she" as *Randi*, and the chapter confirms it: it's Randi in his bed. This whole seduction-of-Vivienne apparatus is being *run through Randi's own body first*. He extracts the wanting from Randi — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — reads her helpless clench, and then, crucially, watches her *hide it from herself*: "Only in a threesome. With you." The move where she "handed the appetite to him and made herself its generous instrument." I knew from ch. 1 and 2 that Randi builds survivable frames around unbearable wants; here I watch her do it in real time and I watch Pace *let her*, because "the frame she'd built to hide in was the exact frame he needed to proceed." That's genuinely chilling. He doesn't correct her lie. He needs it.

So my feeling about Pace has moved, and darkened. In ch. 1 he was attractive-and-wary; here the calculation is fully on the page and it is relentless. "Pleasure was not a trick. It was a true thing he was giving her. It was also the bridge." He's a man who has completely reconciled real love with total instrumentation and feels *no friction between them* — "entirely present to all of it and entirely inside the thing he had decided to do, and the two were not in tension." That non-tension is the scariest thing about him. He's not conflicted. He's happy. "He was happy, plainly and simply." A happy predator with immaculate manners.

And yet — the craft is such that I still find him magnetic, and I'm uneasy about *that*. The forehead kiss "for no reason but that he wanted to." The way he reads her like an instrument he loves playing. I don't trust him at all now and I'm still half-seduced, which I think is the trap the tagline names.

Randi — my sympathy stays, but it's complicated by watching her become a *predator too*, in the same motion as being a victim. The pointing game down the quad is horrible and I couldn't stop reading it. Watching her go from social propriety offended ("the bald social wrongness of it") to full appetite — "She had stopped being a good sport about anything a quarter mile back. She was shopping" — is the chapter turning her from the person I ache for into the person doing the hunting. And she doesn't know she's the instrument. That's the part that keeps my sympathy: Pace is reading *her* the whole time, "Randi was the instrument he was reading and the girl by the doors was only the note." She thinks she's choosing. He's watching her *learn that she can want*. Both true. She's the huntress and the hunted in one body.

And then Vee. Finally, Vee. And the chapter *earns* her entrance — after a whole quad of rejected types (the mirror-of-Randi ponytail "too expected," the matched pair, the closed camel-coat "project," the room-running blonde "God, no"), the taxonomy teaches me Randi's taste before Vee arrives, so that when she comes out of the dining hall the "Yes" that "came out alone, with nothing around it" feels inevitable and involuntary. Vee is drawn as the opposite of everything Randi polices: lush, un-curated, "nothing apologized for," clothes "failing at an impossible task," laughing "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That last line is lovely and also a knife — because being seen without knowing it is *exactly* what's about to happen to her at scale. She laughs unwatched while two people watch and select her.

My feeling about Vee: instant warmth and instant dread. She's the only warm, un-sealed, un-performing person we've met, and she's the mark. The dread the jacket planted ("the girl who never knew there was a game") is now fully loaded. She walks off with a name and a stats class and no idea. "The wind... got almost nothing for the trouble" from Randi's hair — Randi gives the day exactly what she decides — versus Vee's hair "nothing pinned and nothing trying to be." The whole contrast is doing the work.

The collision on the lawn — Randi's engineered "accidental" bump, executed so cleanly that *Vee* is the one who apologizes — is a small masterpiece of horror-as-charm. "It was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth." The victim apologizing to the hunter. That's the mechanism of the whole book in three seconds, and I felt the chill land exactly as intended without the text announcing it.

**Erotic charge:** The opening sex worked on me — the taste-kiss, the undefended entry, the three orgasms worked out of her "slowly, deliberately, giving her more than she'd asked for." Peak heat is that mouth-still-wet kiss and the question landing in her body. It stays warm but goes *cold underneath* the second the pointing starts — and deliberately so; the arousal curdles into unease and I think that's the design. The mid-walk kiss where he hasn't wiped his face and "he knew the moment her own taste reached her because her breath caught" — that's charged and cruel at once. The taste thread is clearly a *thing* here, recurring hard.

**Friction:** Very little. This is the most controlled chapter yet. If anything, I felt the author's hand once or twice in the *density* of Pace's interior taxonomy — the paragraph parsing "a wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door" is gorgeous but it's also the book explaining its own engine to me a touch more explicitly than ch. 1 did. "By some old architecture he did not examine, a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there" — that's close to naming his compulsion, though it stops short and stays as behavior. I didn't disbelieve it; I just felt the machinery hum for a second. And the closing "Let's go feed you" ties back to Randi's food-policing so neatly it's almost a bow. But these are quibbles. The chapter is scary-good.

**What I want / dread next:** I want Vee's own point of view, badly — the jacket promises close attention on her, and everything so far has been *about* her while she's absent or unaware. I dread how tender it's going to be, because I now know what tenderness is *for* in this book. Guess from here: the next Vee-track will be Randi working the stats-class connection, warm and confiding, exactly the way Brooke worked Randi in ch. 2 — the extractive interview turned outward, downhill. And Pace/Vee will start their own staircase separately. Two hunters, one girl, neither track visible to her. The tagline — "every yes was freely given, that was the trap" — just got its thesis statement: Randi's own yes was extracted from her undefended body and then *re-owned as a gift*, and that's the template for what they'll do to Vee.

**On the titles.** **The Pointing Game** — surface is a couple's playful people-watching game, "which one." The charge on the page: the pointing is *selection of a human being for consumption*, dressed as flirtation, and the deeper charge is that the "game" is being played on *Randi* as much as by her — Pace is pointing at *her* the whole time. "The game was working." Innocuous frame, predatory content. Meaningful only after reading, and it detonates. It fits the book's title too — *With a Long Spoon*, sup with the devil — and I'm no longer sure any of these three is only the devil or only the diner. If anything ch. 3 makes the proverb queasier: the person doing the pointing is also being dined on, and the girl being pointed at thinks she bumped into a stranger.
