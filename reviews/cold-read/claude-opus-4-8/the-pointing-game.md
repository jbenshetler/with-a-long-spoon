## Reader reaction

## Felt read

So *this* is the game. Two chapters of cold undertow and dread and here it is, out in the open — and the thing that unsettles me most is how much *fun* it is. I didn't want it to be fun. It's fun.

The chapter opens in bed, in Pace's POV, and I have to say the sex worked on me — the first page especially, the coming-up-over-her-before-she's-done, the wet kiss, "you like the taste of yourself." That's the taste thread the jacket comps promised and it's charged, it's specific, it lands. But it curdled the second he asked the question mid-orgasm — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — because I could see exactly what he was doing. He himself calls it: *"It was not fair to ask it now, and he knew it was not fair. He asked it anyway."* He's using the undefended moment. He asks when she can't answer with anything but her body, reads the body's answer, and then — the part that chilled me — reframes the whole thing so she never has to *know* she wanted it. "A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was... a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there."

That's the tagline made flesh. Every yes freely given. He *engineers* the yeses. He watches her build a costume — "only in a threesome, with you" — to hide her own appetite inside, and then he *takes the costume as a gift* because it's exactly the frame he needs: "the frame she'd built to hide in was the exact frame he needed to proceed." He lets her keep her lie because her lie is his door in. I found that genuinely sinister underneath how tender it's dressed. And it *is* dressed tender — the forehead kiss, the "I want her gathered back into herself... free to weigh it and choose; that mattered to him more than the answer did." Does it, though? He already had the answer from her body. The "choice" is theater and he knows it's theater and he stages it anyway because he needs her clear daylight yes on the record.

My feeling about Pace has moved — hardened. Chapter one I called him magnetic and privately distrusted; this chapter earns the distrust in full. He's not cruel, he's worse than cruel, he's *certain he's giving her a gift.* "Because what he was bringing her was good." He has fully talked himself into benevolence. That self-justification is the most frightening thing on the page.

And Randi — God, Randi. The walk is the whole chapter and it's the best thing the book has done. She starts offended by the pointing, "the propriety offended," and by a hundred yards in she's *shopping* — "She had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." The follow-leading-the-lead dance metaphor is beautiful and it's doing real character work: she's the one hunting now, feeling for the next one before his hand comes up. And her verdicts are *characterization* — "too expected," "you'd get both or neither," "she'd be a project," and the fast real "God, no" at the girl who runs the room (that one told me something about Randi — she can't stand the woman who performs ease outward, probably because that's her own trick and she despises it). Watching her taste reveal itself through who she rejects was genuinely thrilling to read. This is the "co-architect of someone else's seduction" the jacket promised, and now I see how a hollowed-out girl becomes it: she isn't co-architecting from strength, she's being *drawn out* the same way Pace drew her out on the bench, her wanting extracted before her mind can dress it.

Then Vee. The prose slows way down and *lavishes* — "built on a lavish scale, lush in a way that had nothing curated about it and nothing apologized for." Everything about her is the opposite of Randi's grammar: uncurated, unpinned, copper hair the wind plays with, freckles, laughing with her whole body "like a person who had genuinely forgotten... that she could be seen." That last clause landed hard given what we know about Randi, who has never once in three chapters forgotten she can be seen. Vee is being introduced to us as *the thing Randi cannot be*. And "her" — Randi's bare *yes*, "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — that's the third helpless yes of the day, matching the three orgasms. She wanted Vee on sight and couldn't manage it.

The collision at the end is a small masterpiece of dread-as-delight. Randi engineers a "chance" bump so clean that *Vee* apologizes for it — "it was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth." That detail did something to me. The victim thanking the hunter. And then "her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together" — the happy footwork on the brick, the grin breaking through — Randi is *lit up*, and I'm sitting here knowing this poor laughing girl just got selected off a quad like produce, and the two people who selected her are in love with the game and with each other and think they're kind.

**Trust/suspicion:** No suspicion needed anymore — the text is fully explicit about the machinery now, from inside Pace's head. What I have instead is *dread*. Vee "never knew there was one," the jacket said. I've now watched there be one.

**Erotic charge:** Peaks in the opening bed scene (the taste, the kiss) and then, strangely, in the *walk*, which is clothed and full of tension — the library-steps kiss where he feeds her her own taste in public and her composure cracks. That's a 2 of charge without a stitch coming off. Never went clinical.

**Friction:** Almost none. If I press — there's a *lot* of interior Pace-narration explaining his own moves, and once or twice it edges toward the author making sure I don't miss the mechanism ("The same act. A different owner. She could live inside the second version. She could not live inside the first."). But it's so aligned with his established omniscient-calculating voice that I mostly bought it as *character*, not thumb on scale. The list of rejected girls ran slightly long — the camel-coat "project" and the room-running blonde blur a little — but each earned a Randi verdict, so I forgave it.

**Titles:** "The Pointing Game" is exactly what it says and it *is* innocuous on the surface — a game, pointing, fun. The reread charge is already active *within the chapter*: "I point. You pick" sounds playful and is in fact a man teaching a woman to hunt a stranger while convincing her the choosing makes it hers. And *With a Long Spoon* — I still don't have the proverb in front of me, but I can feel it circling: something about how you sup with the devil with a long spoon, keep your distance from the thing you're feeding. Nobody here is keeping any distance. They're all reaching in with bare hands. The tagline is no longer a promise, it's a description of what I just watched: freely given, and a trap, and the trap-spring is the *freeness.*

**What I want/dread next:** I want Vee's POV, badly — I've now met her only as prey and I want her to be a person. I dread the stats class. Guess from here: next chapter turns to Vee, innocent, and we watch Randi arrive in her life as the dazzling new friend from the jacket, and the horror will be the warmth of it.

**How I feel**

Pace: distrust hardened into real alarm — the tenderness is real and that's what makes the calculation monstrous; a man wholly convinced his manipulation is generosity. Vee: instant protectiveness and ache — introduced purely as prey, laughing, thanking the person who selected her; I want her to be a person and I'm braced for what's coming. Overall mood: the cold undertow has flipped into a bright, sunlit, wind-and-burning-maple *high* that is somehow more frightening than the cold was — because the game is fun, and I can feel it working on me the way it worked on Randi.
