## Reader reaction

So there she is. Vee. Four chapters of waiting and she arrives as the thing being pointed *at* — laughing with her head thrown all the way back, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." And that is exactly, precisely why she gets chosen. God, that landed on me hard. The one woman on the quad who isn't performing, selected *for* not performing, by two people who do nothing but. I felt protective of her the instant she came out the dining hall doors, and the protectiveness curdled immediately, because I already knew what the walk had been for.

This chapter reframes everything and I need to sit with how it did it. We're in Pace's head now, and the tenderness I logged as genuine last time is genuine *and* it's an instrument, both at once, and the book refuses to let me pick. He "kissed her, slow and tender, for no reason but that he wanted to" — I believe that. And in the same breath he is running an operation on her body: planting "Would you like to taste another woman?" *mid-sex*, when she's "still swollen and still pulsing around nothing, still given over to her body, undefended. He had decided this beforehand." He uses her involuntary third orgasm — "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it" — as *data*. He reads her yes off her sex before her mouth can dress it. "Her body wanted this; her mind had not yet caught up to it." That sentence is the whole ethical knot of the chapter and it made me deeply uneasy in a way the page absolutely earned.

And yet — the thing I keep turning over — his stated care is *for her freedom to choose*. He waits for the heat to leave the room, wants her "gathered back into herself when he asked, free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did." He builds her the wrapper so she can say yes "without making the wanting stand naked between them." That's real gentleness. It's also the most sophisticated coercion I've read in a while, because it's coercion that hands the victim a dignified exit and calls it kindness. This is the "polite invitation." I think I just met the title.

The pointing game itself is a marvel of writing and I was swept up in it against my better judgment. Watching Randi go from propriety-offended ("the bald social wrongness of it") to *shopping* — "She would have told him she was being a good sport about his game; she had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back" — is thrilling and horrible. The dance metaphor is doing enormous work: "a follow gathering herself a half-beat before the lead moves." He's the lead. She thinks she's choosing; he's watching her "learn that she could." The taste-parade — expected/no, matched-pair/no, closed camel-coat "project"/no, the room-runner "God, no" with real feeling — genuinely told me who Randi is by what she rejects. She wants open, warm, unguarded, someone who takes up room. She wants, it turns out, her own opposite. That's a devastating piece of characterization delivered entirely through a shopping list.

The erotic charge is high and it does *not* go slack. The opening sex is graphic and worked on me. But the hottest thing in the chapter isn't the sex — it's Randi's helpless bare "**Yes**," the word "with nothing around it — the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it," and then the scramble to costume it: "Yeah. I like her." The gap between those two lines is the erotics of the whole book, I think. And the shoulder-collision con Randi runs on Vee — "it was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth" — I admired it exactly as Pace did, and hated that I did.

Where do I stand on Pace now? My "can't yet convict him of anything but skill and tenderness" is gone. I can convict him of design. He is cultivating a want in a woman that she "can't fully account for" and steering it at a target who has no idea she's a target. Whether he *pre-knew* Vee would walk out those doors, or that she and Randi share a stats class — that the page leaves genuinely ajar. "He had not chosen the spot in advance. He chose it now, fast and without seeming to." That reads as improvisation. But he walked them to the dining hall at four o'clock, and Randi discovers the shared class as if it's chance, and the jacket told me there's a *plan about Vee between Randi and Pace* — and this chapter shows Randi as the mark, not the co-conspirator. So either the jacket's "plan" is still coming, or Randi is being drawn into a plan she thinks is her own idea. I'm holding both. The unease is no longer waiting on evidence; the page delivered it.

The swoon and the dread are the *same feeling* in this chapter, which is the most unsettling thing about it. Randi lit up — "the grin, breaking the managed surface," the little happy footwork on the brick "she didn't decide to do," the two "yes"es at the end, the second one "the register she only used in his bed and never in daylight" — is the most alive and unguarded I've seen her, the face finally *off*, and it's happening because a trap closed perfectly. I wanted her to have it. I also watched it be harvested. And yet the final beat — him just wrapping her up and holding her there while a couple of students went past, "the fact of her pleasure stayed bright in him" — is the one place in the chapter I liked him plainly, no calculation in it, only gladness at her gladness.

**The titles.** "The Pointing Game" is literal and it gave the mechanism away up front, but the charge wasn't in the mechanism — it was in the target, and the target was worth the wait. *A Polite Invitation* I now read as the whole architecture of this chapter: an invitation constructed so the invited party can't feel the walls. *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." I've been asking who the devil is. After this chapter I think the honest answer is that everyone at this table is holding a spoon and nobody's sure who's supping with whom. Vee, walking up that path listening to her friend with her head tipped, is the only one who brought no spoon at all.

**Friction.** Almost none — this is the most assured chapter yet. If anything, the interiority occasionally over-narrates its own cleverness ("She had not taken back the wanting... She had moved it... The same act. A different owner." — the page explains Randi's move to me a beat after I'd felt it). But it's so good I'll forgive the thumb.

**What I want / dread next:** I want Vee's own head, badly — I've now watched two people appraise her like produce and I need to know what she's actually made of before the machine reaches her. *Guess from here:* the stats class is the next move; Randi walks in as the girl Vee "bumped into," and the courtship begins under Pace's direction. Dread: that Vee's openness — the thing that makes her lovable — is precisely the handle they'll use.

**How I feel — overall**
Swept up and sick about it in equal measure, which is exactly the chord this chapter wanted. My reading of Pace has hardened: no longer "skill and tenderness only," now a designer who cultivates and steers desire while preserving his subject's sense of freedom — the polite invitation with no visible walls. But the page will not let me flatten him into a villain; the tenderness reads real *inside* the design, and the closing hold is one beat where I liked him plainly. Deep, doubled sympathy for Randi — her most alive, unguarded moment is the closing of a trap. And immediate, aching protectiveness of Vee, met at last, appraised like produce, laughing with her throat bare to the sky, bringing no spoon to the table. I want her interior next, and I dread that her openness is the handle.
