## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one worked on me and unsettled me in the same breath, which is exactly the register this book keeps finding. The opening sex is genuinely hot — not the choreography (though the "he came up over her before she was done… before he kissed her" is a real jolt) but the *tactic* of it, the question slipped into her while she's undefended: "Would you like to taste another woman?" and her body answering with a third orgasm before her mind can dress the wanting in anything safer. That's a beautiful, cruel move and I felt the heat of it land.

But I've read "The Bench" and "Standards," and I cannot un-know what Pace doesn't know. He keeps taking "the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." After a whole chapter of Randi at the mirror deciding *not ever* — the face goes back on and she is not coming out again — watching him congratulate himself on excavating her, on giving the wanting "air in daylight," reads to me as tender and invasive at once. He thinks he's handing her a gift. I think he's prying at the exact seam she has decided to weld shut, and he's doing it so gently and so perceptively that it's worse, not better. The line that curdled it for me: "The same act. A different owner." He *sees* that she's relocated the appetite to survive it — and he keeps going anyway, because he's decided her surface is the lie and he's the one who gets to overrule it. That's the thing that's off, and the text absolutely earned it; it isn't me on guard.

And yet — the pointing game itself is delicious. The escalation of *taste* is superb: she names each woman's disqualification (too expected, they come as a pair, runs the room *at* people) and in doing so tells us her whole grammar, and the reveal that "You point. You pick" — the choosing is hers — genuinely charmed me. I was shopping right along with her by the library. Then Vee. That entry is the best writing in the run so far: head thrown back, throat bare, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." Everything Randi cannot do. Of *course* Randi's stride drops out from under her. The bare "Yes," alone with nothing around it, wrecked me a little — it's the same undefended *yes* the bench pulled out of her, and here it's for a person.

The bump — the staged shoulder-collision, so clean the redhead apologizes — is a swoon and a chill together. I loved watching Randi be *competent*, the dance training aimed at a person for the first time. But it's also predation dressed as chance, and Randi running it while telling herself she's "being a good sport." The book knows this; it keeps letting her costume slip ("you dirty boy," the kitten register) and showing me the real heat underneath.

Vee lands on me as a genuine arrival — I want her, the way the page wants me to, and I'm frightened for her, because she's a warm open unguarded person and two people just selected her off a lawn like a dish not ordered. I have no read yet on whether she's a mark or a match. That not-knowing is the pull.

On Randi my feeling moved toward tenderness *and* dread: her helpless delight — "the word squeaking out of her, high and delighted," the half-second of happy footwork she didn't decide to do — is the most unguarded she's been in daylight, and it's real. But I know where she put the wanting, and I know she thinks she's giving Pace a threesome, not admitting a hunger. On Pace my trust dropped a notch this chapter. I liked him at the bench for respecting the locked door. Here he respects nothing — he reads through every evasion and acts on the reading. Confident, attentive, and quietly sovereign over what she's "really" feeling. That's starting to feel like the shape of the whole thing.

Erotic charge: peaks in the bed and, differently, in the *maybe* on the library steps where he kisses her with her own taste still on his mouth. It never goes clinical.

**The titles.** "The Pointing Game" is exactly the chapter — literal, and it *is* the point: a game where he points and she pretends the appetite is his. It didn't give anything away; it recolored on arrival, because "pointing" turns out to mean pointing at *people*, the social wrongness of it part of the charge. The book title, *With a Long Spoon* (Book One), and the cover-board tagline **"A Polite Invitation"** — the spoon proverb is *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon*, and after this chapter it points squarely at Vee: a polite invitation extended to someone who has no idea what she's being invited into. That's where I feel it taking me — someone at that table is the devil, and the long spoon is what you'd need and won't have. It makes me read Pace's "gift" much more coldly.

What I want next: Vee, in her own light, and Randi's stats class. I dread — guess from here — that Randi's welded-shut *not ever* and her actual helpless wanting are on a collision course, and Vee is standing where they'll meet.
