## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

This chapter worked on me and horrified me in the same breath, which I think is exactly the knife it meant to hold. The opening sex is genuinely, bodily hot — the taste of herself on his mouth, the way the *question* ("Would you like to taste another woman?") lands in her and tears a third orgasm out of her "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That's charged, real writing; I felt it. But sitting right on top of my arousal is everything I carry from the bench chapter and from "Standards" — Randi's quiet resolve never to come out from behind the face, the water-bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail, the not-crying under the duvet. So when Pace congratulates himself — *All he had done was find it, and answer it* — I don't believe him, and the gap between his contented narration and what I know of her is the whole dread engine of the book.

Pace unsettles me more than ever, precisely because he's rendered as the *good* dominant: the safeword, the aftercare, the respected closed door, the Daphne lesson about not forcing his way into someone's feelings. But he's steering. "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer" — and then he engineers an entire afternoon to walk her toward taking it anyway, dressed as a game so she never has to look at it. That's not restraint; that's a more patient kind of pressure. His certainty that he's a benevolent force answering a hunger is the thing I distrust most. The book keeps letting me see the man believe his own tenderness while a third party gets drawn, unknowing, toward the bench.

Randi, meanwhile, did the move I now recognize as her signature: she converts her own helpless want into a *gift*. "Only in a threesome. With you." Not a woman who hungers — unsurvivable — but a girlfriend being generous. It's the same self-translation as the apple slices, the "I can't eat that" cheeseburger, the face in the mirror. And then on the quad she stops being a good sport a quarter mile before she'd admit it: "She was shopping." Watching her resolve from prey into hunter — the staged collision so clean *Vee* apologizes — is the most chilling characterization beat in the book so far. The broken bird of the bench is learning to point.

The swoon is there and I won't pretend it isn't: "Yes," the bare word arriving alone, then the daylight kitten voice closing over it; the happy half-second of footwork on the brick she doesn't decide to do; "*Yes*," squeaked out, high and delighted, kissing him. For one beat she's just a girl lit up about a crush. That's the cruelest swoon, because I know what it's being recruited for. I did not root for them. I ached, and I flinched.

Vee (Vivienne) arrives like weather — head thrown all the way back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That one line did me in, because *unselfconscious of being seen* is the precise thing Randi lost, the thing she was built to be the opposite of. They have selected her for the very aliveness the bench extinguished in Randi. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is a perfect, awful last line — Vee is being lined up for the exact machine that broke the woman now recruiting her.

Erotic charge runs high and almost never goes slack — it peaks at the bed (the taste, the question-triggered climax) and again at "Her," pointed at Vee, Randi's walk arrested mid-stride. The only friction: the parade of rejected women runs a touch catalog-like, and Pace's interiority is so relentlessly perceptive and self-justifying it occasionally tells me how to read the scene — "the whole art of it was that it stay a game." But it mostly earns the altitude. Titles: **"The Pointing Game"** is literal and it did pre-load the chapter's structure, but the chill lands retroactively — it's a polite parlor game played with real people. **"A Polite Invitation"** (volume) is the collision itself, the mannered surface over the hunt — the horror *is* the politeness. **"With a Long Spoon"** (series) I read as *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon* — someone here is dining with something dangerous, and I'm not yet sure the spoon is long enough. No tagline was in what I was shown.

What I want next: Vee, in full, from closer up — and I dread it, because I expect her warmth to be exactly what gets spent. I want to know whether Randi's "yes" curdles or deepens, and whether Pace ever registers what I registered in that bathroom. *(Guess from here only.)*
