## Reader reaction

So this is the chapter where the machine turns over and I can finally see it running — and where the jacket's "Vee" walks out of the dining hall and levels the whole afternoon. And it's the first time I've been *inside Pace*. That matters enormously. For two chapters I was in Randi, pressing the wound. Now I'm behind the eyes of the man doing the arranging, and it is both more seductive and more unsettling than I expected.

The opening sex is genuinely hot. I felt it. The "he knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it," the coming-up-over-her-still-pulsing, kissing her with his mouth wet from her — that's expert, warm, present writing and it worked on me. But the mechanism underneath is the exact thing this book keeps putting its thumb on, and here it's naked: he plants *"Would you like to taste another woman?"* into "the warm disorder of her" and reads the answer off her body — three orgasms, the last one "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." He's harvesting an involuntary yes. The whole chapter runs on his conviction that her clenching sex is *truth* and her spoken words are *costume*. "The want had shown itself before she could dress it as anything." As erotics that's electric. As ethics it's the beginning of something I don't fully trust, and the text knows it — that's the charge.

What I love, and what unsettles me, is the same thing: **Randi's laundering**. "Only in a threesome. With you." I *felt* the elegance of it the way Pace does — "she had not taken back the wanting… she had moved it," handed the appetite to him and made herself "its generous instrument: not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift." That lands straight on the wound I've been carrying for her. The face, the arranged smile, the portioned self — here it is at the level of *desire itself*: she can't own that she wants a woman, so she costumes it as service. The bare "**Yes**" that keeps escaping her ("the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it") and then getting dressed — that's the whole tragedy of her in a single repeated gesture, and it got me every time it happened.

The pointing game itself is a beautifully built set piece and I was swept along even as I clocked what it *is*. He points, she picks — and I watched her stop being a good sport "a quarter mile back" and start *shopping*. The parade of rejected girls is doing real work: each "no" carves her taste in relief — not the expected mirror-of-herself, not the sealed matched pairs, not the closed camel-coat "project," not the girl who "ran the room" with performed ease. He's learning her by subtraction, and I'm learning her too. "I point. You pick" — "He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could." That's tender and it's grooming, at once, and the book refuses to let me pick one.

And then **Vee**. The jacket protagonist, finally on the page — and the writing rises to meet her. Lush, uncurated, "nothing apologized for," moving like an athlete *under* all that abundance, copper hair the wind won't leave alone, cinnamon freckles, and — the line that undid me — "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That is *exactly* the freedom Randi does not have, cannot have, whose absence is her whole architecture. Of course this is the one. Of course Randi "did not look away," the coil releasing "into pure helpless looking," the follow breaking frame before the lead called the step. The bare "Yes" here is the truest thing she's said in three chapters. I swooned. I did.

But here's the undertow the page *earned*, and I'm not manufacturing it: Vee has no idea. A stranger who forgot she could be seen has just been *seen*, selected, hunted, and physically engineered-into — that "collision" is a piece of predation dressed as clumsiness, "three seconds of execution so clean it looked like nothing." Pace stood at a light pole "far enough back to be nobody" and watched it like a connoisseur. The most alive, most unguarded person on the quad has been chosen *precisely for* her unguardedness, and the two people closing on her are running a game she isn't a player in. That's the cold thing at the bottom of a warm, warm chapter, and I feel it clearly.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** my read on Pace has moved. I could only convict him of skill and tenderness before. Now I'm inside his self-narration and it's *insistent* about its own gentleness — "She deserved that gentleness. He wanted it for her." "free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did." Read once, tender. Read against what he's actually doing — deploying his girlfriend to physically ambush an unknowing woman — the gentleness starts to look like the *instrument*, or at least his necessary story about himself. I don't think he's a cartoon villain; I think he genuinely believes he's freeing Randi's wanting into daylight. But there are now *three* people in this and only two are playing, and he's the one who set the board. That's earned suspicion, not reflexive.

**Erotic charge:** high. Peaks in the opening (the question-as-trigger) and in the collision. Only place it goes slightly slack for me is the sheer volume of Pace's interior justification — the chapter tells me many times how much her freedom-to-choose matters to him, and past a point I felt the author's hand pressing his innocence a little hard: "that mattered more to him than the answer did" doing overtime. But I half-read that pressure as *his* self-deception, which keeps it interesting.

**Swoon / pull:** the "Yes… Yes" breathy escalation at the end — that landed on me hard, genuine delight breaking through her management. I wanted her to have that unguarded joy. I liked Pace best in the small human beat of just holding her while students walked past, no calculation in it.

**Title:** "The Pointing Game" is exactly what it says and gets darker on the second thought — the game where you *point at* a person, reduce a laughing stranger to a thing selectable off a path. Innocuous as a phrase, quietly awful once you've watched it. **"A Polite Invitation"** now has a vector: a stats class, and Randi will do the inviting. **"With a Long Spoon"** — sup with the devil, keep a long spoon. Somebody at this table is going to need one, and my fear is it's the redhead who doesn't know she's been seated. The **burning maple** running through the walk — "a single tree burning orange in a field of things that had not yet decided to change," and at the end "The maple was burning" as Vee appears — reads straight to Vee herself: the one who's gone over, alive, changed, ripe, among people who haven't.

**What I want / dread next (guess from here):** Randi and Vee in that stats class, and I dread how good Randi will be at it. I want Vee to have interiority now that she's arrived — I want to be inside her, to see whether the book will let the hunted woman be a person and not a prize. I half-expect the next chapter to hand me Vee's POV, unaware, and I dread the dramatic irony of it.

**Heat:** 3 — sustained, graphic opening sex scene; the clothed campus game stays charged through the collision and the closing kiss.
**Romance:** 2 — real tenderness (the unhurried kiss "for no reason but that he wanted to," holding her after, the return kiss) braided with the arranging, which keeps it from a clean romantic peak.
