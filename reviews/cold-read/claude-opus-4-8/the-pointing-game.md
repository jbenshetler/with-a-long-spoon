## Reader reaction

Okay. This one did something to me, and I want to be honest that it worked even as it scared me.

We're inside Pace's head now — first time. And the opening is genuinely the most erotic thing in the book so far for me: him coming up over her "while she was still swollen and still pulsing around nothing," kissing her with his mouth wet from her, and her taking her own taste and kissing him harder for it. "You like the taste of yourself." That landed as heat, real heat, not clinical — the physical logic of it, her mouth still busy correcting what her body already confessed. The three orgasms are structured as a *reading*, and that reading is where the chill lives, but the surface stayed hot enough that I was aroused before I was uneasy. Which I suspect is the point, and it's exactly the thing that makes it disturbing.

Because here's what moved for me: Pace was a pull-plus-distrust silhouette for two chapters. Now I've been *inside* the calculation and it's worse and more seductive than I'd braced for. The tell I keep circling: "*A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was, by some old architecture he did not examine, a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there.*" That's him rendered as compulsion, not villainy — he genuinely believes "Pleasure was not a trick. It was a true thing he was giving her." And the horror is I half-believe him too. He's not lying to her about the pleasure. He's lying to her about *whose want* it is, and letting her keep the "gift for you" wrapper because the wrapper is the exact thing he needs. "He would find a woman for *theirs.*" That reframing — her camouflage being precisely his instrument — is the coldest, cleverest beat in the book so far, and it's delivered with what reads as tenderness. I don't trust him and I'm still pulled. That's the braid the story keeps promising and it finally has real weight.

Randi — my ache for her deepened again. Watching her build the frame in real time ("Only in a threesome. With you.") after her body betrayed her three times — "not a woman who hungered for another woman, which was unsurvivable, but a girlfriend offering her man a gift, which was merely sexy. The same act. A different owner. She could live inside the second version. She could not live inside the first." That's the not-crying woman from chapter two, the water-bottle-cap self-manager, doing the only thing she knows how to do: survive a want by relocating its ownership. And she has *no idea* he's reading her. The pointing game is unbearable because it's presented as *her* choosing, her taste, her edge — "He was not choosing a woman. He was watching her learn that she could" — and it's actually the most thorough act of steering yet, dressed as her agency. She's shopping and thinks she's being a good sport. God.

And then — the reveal I didn't see coming and absolutely should have. **Vee.** The redhead. Named last. "And we've got a stats class together." So *this* is who the machine is for. The whole book so far — Randi's containment, Pace's architecture — narrows to a copper-haired girl laughing with her throat bare to the sky, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That description is the warmest, most alive thing in three chapters, and it's terrifying precisely because of who's looking at her and why. She's the open, unmanaged one. The one who isn't reading surfaces or being read — she just *is*. And two predators (I'll use the word) just selected her, one knowingly, one thinking it was her own free appetite.

The pointing sequence itself is beautifully built — the near-Randi mirror-girl ("too expected"), the matched pair ("you'd get both or neither"), the camel-coat "project," the beanie girl who earns a "maybe" and a kiss, the room-running blonde ("God, no" — real feeling), each rejection teaching Pace her actual taste. It could have been a tedious catalogue and it wasn't; each "no" characterized Randi. The escalation of her *breaking frame* — from propriety-offended, to considering, to "shopping," to the dropped stride at Vee — is a genuine staircase and I felt each riser.

Trust/suspicion: no new suspicion so much as a *confirmation and darkening*. Brooke's still my other open predator; interesting that Vee and Randi share a stats class — I now wonder if that's engineered or luck, and whether Pace already knew (he says "He had not known, going in" about the want — but the *walk* he plainly aimed). The line "He had not planned the maple but he was glad of the maple" is him telling me how much of the rest he *did* plan.

Friction: very little. The one place I felt the thumb was the density of Pace naming his own method — "the whole art of it was that it stay a game," "Letting her keep it was the kindness; it was, in fact, the whole of what he was doing out here." A few of those explanatory beats edge toward him narrating his own thesis. But he's an over-attentive mind, so it mostly reads as character, not author. And "Randi was the instrument he was reading and the girl by the doors was only the note" — I loved and flinched at "instrument."

Small callback that hit: the cheeseburger. "Do you want a cheeseburger?" / "No. I can't eat that." The mother's food-policing from chapter two walks right onto the quad. And "Let's go feed you" closing the chapter — he's the one who'll feed her now. Ownership dressed as care again.

What I want next: I want to meet Vee from *inside* Vee. I've only seen her as prey through Pace's frame, and I badly want her to be a person before the machine reaches her. I dread the stats class. I dread how good this is going to feel while it happens to her.
