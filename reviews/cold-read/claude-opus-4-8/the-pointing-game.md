## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

So this is what the title was for, and I feel it close over me a little coldly even as the prose keeps being gorgeous. We're four weeks into Pace and Randi now, and last chapter left me with that key-turning-in-a-lock image — her deciding *not ever* to come out from behind the face. This chapter he goes looking for the one she's hiding, again, and this time he does it in daylight on a quad, and I can't tell anymore whether I'm watching a man answer a woman's hunger or a man running an experiment on her he already knows the result of. "He had decided this beforehand." "He still meant to ask her." The language of a man who plans the undoing in advance keeps pulling the warmth out from under the tenderness.

The bed opening is genuinely hot — the taste of herself on his mouth, the question *reaching her body before it reached her*, the orgasm arriving too fast for anything his body is doing to explain. That's a beautiful, true piece of writing about wanting outrunning the self. And I felt it. But then the chapter does the thing it did last time: it hands me Pace's reading of her in such total, confident detail that I stop being inside *her* desire and start standing next to *his* mastery of it. "She had not taken back the wanting. She had moved it." He sees through every costume she reaches for. And he's always right. That omniscience about her is becoming the most unsettling thing in the book for me — more than any act. The spanking bench chapter at least let her interiority close the door on him in the bathroom, a place he couldn't follow. Here there's no bathroom. He narrates her completely.

Randi I ache for. The cheeseburger — "I can't eat that," automatic, not even a glance — landed harder on me than any of the sex, right after last chapter's apple. The portioned appetite is everywhere. And the *yes* that comes out of her bare, "already true before she got to it," when he points at Vee — that's her, the real one, surfacing against her will, and I felt the helpless lift of it and the dread at the same time. She's being delighted into something, and the delight is real, and that's exactly what frightens me. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" as the closing line genuinely chilled me — she's already recruiting, already handing a stranger toward the thing that undid her.

The pointing game itself is where my swoon and my suspicion split hardest. As a seduction set piece it's dazzling — the burning maple, the wind in the ponytails, each woman a quick verdict, the choosing-is-hers rule ("I point. You pick"). I was swept up in the *craft* of it, and in Randi shopping, breaking frame like a dancer feeling the music early. But I also can't unsee that he steers her to Vee. He pre-selects warmth, openness, the un-mirrored one, and points. The "choosing is hers" is partly true and partly a lovely fiction he's built so she'll own what he aimed her at. I don't fully trust him, and the text earned that for me — not through menace but through how *managed* every beat of her is, by him and by the narration both.

Vee herself is the first jolt of pure uncomplicated pull in the chapter — the red hair sparking copper, throat bared in a laugh, "forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." After a parade of curated, sealed girls, she reads as actually alive, and I want to meet her. The cover said *A Polite Invitation* and here's the invitation being manufactured out of a shoulder-collision. I'm uneasy that she's being walked toward these two the way Randi was walked toward the bench.

Erotic charge: the bed scene runs hot, a 2 climbing toward 3 at the taste-of-herself kiss, then the quad is a different, cooler heat — anticipatory, predatory almost, charge that lives in the hunt rather than in bodies. It worked on me but with a thumbprint of unease all through it.

Friction: the one place I felt the author's thumb was the relentless perfection of Pace's reads — he is never once wrong about her, never surprised in a way that costs him, and "I liked when I guessed right about her. I liked, even better, the places she surprised him" almost protests too much, since the chapter shows him surprised by *nothing*. A little of his fallibility would make me trust the romance more, not less.

**Titles.** "The Pointing Game" — fully delivered, maybe over-delivered; it named the mechanism before I reached it, so the chapter was partly me waiting for the pointing to start. "WITH A LONG SPOON" I now hear its proverb — *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon* — and it's starting to point a finger I can't yet tell is at Pace or at Randi. "A Polite Invitation" as Book One: this chapter is the invitation being built. I expect Vee to be supped with, and someone to have brought too short a spoon.

**What I want/dread next:** I want Vee to be a person, not a dish, and I dread that she's the next one walked onto the bench. Guess from here: Randi's recruitment of Vee becomes the way she keeps hiding from Pace — offering him a woman instead of herself.
