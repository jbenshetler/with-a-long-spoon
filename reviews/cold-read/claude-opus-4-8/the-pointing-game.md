**Felt read.**

This chapter frightened me, quietly, and I mean that as praise. After "Standards" left Randi flat on her back under a synthetic duvet in last night's clothes, not-crying, having decided in the mirror the night before to go *back behind the face and not come out again* — I came into this scene braced. And Pace, God, Pace opens by doing the exact thing she cannot survive: he uses her body's honesty against her mind's guard. He asks "Would you like to taste another woman?" while she's mid-orgasm, undefended, and takes the involuntary clench as "the true one." He *usually* did. That line chilled me. He is so tender, so attentive, so patient — and he has built an entire epistemology in which the woman's cornered, involuntary body is more real than anything she says, and he gets to be its interpreter. That's the same move as the bench, and it worked on her there, and it's working here.

What's new and awful is how the last chapter taught me to read this one. I *know* what Pace reads as her buried appetite finally answered is, from inside her, the thing she swore in the bathroom mirror would never happen again — being seen past the face. He thinks he's found her hunger and is generously feeding it. I'm watching a man mistake a woman's panic-management for her liberation, and he's too good at reading bodies to doubt himself. The gap between his warm interior monologue ("the best idea he'd had in a month") and what I remember from her side is the whole horror engine of the book, and it's running beautifully.

The pointing game itself is genuinely, uncomfortably erotic — I won't pretend otherwise. Watching Randi's connoisseur's eye go down the quad, "too expected," "you'd get both or neither," is a real seduction, and I was swept along in it even while flinching. And then Vee. The redhead who laughs with her whole throat, "forgotten for the length of the laugh that she could be seen" — the exact opposite of Randi, who manages every strand of hair the day gets. The *yes* that arrives "alone, with nothing around it, the bare arrival of a thing already true" — that's the bench-voice, the real one, breaking out in daylight. It landed hard on me. I ached for it and dreaded it at once, because I remember what Randi does the instant the real thing surfaces: she reaches for the costume. And she does — "you dirty boy," the kitten register — handing the wanting to Pace so it becomes *theirs* and not *hers*, unsurvivably her own.

My feeling about Pace has moved, and darkened. Two chapters ago I could read his patience as care. Now his care looks like the most efficient possible instrument for overriding a woman's stated boundaries by appeal to her flesh — and he's doing it to someone I *know* is using compliance as armor. He respects Daphne's closed door in memory but walks straight through Randi's, because Randi's is invisible to him; she's too skilled to show it. That's the tragedy: her competence at hiding is exactly what makes her unprotectable.

The collision on the walk is a small masterpiece of craft — the redhead's own hand flying to her mouth, apologizing for a collision Randi engineered. "A decade of dance and three years of whatever sorority actually taught... folded down into three seconds so clean it looked like nothing." I admired it and it scared me, because it shows Randi will hunt Vee with the same instrument she uses to hide from Pace.

Vee I've only just met, and I'm already braided into worry for her — she's the open, unguarded one being selected by two people, one of whom points and one of whom executes. The name landing at the end ("Her name is Vee") plus "a stats class together" gives the dread an address. I don't trust this is a lark. Nobody in this book gets fed without a hook in the apple.

**The titles.** "The Pointing Game" — before reading I'd have guessed something abstract; now it's brutally literal and I *wish* it weren't. He points, she picks. It names the exact power arrangement: the man selects the frame, the woman gets to feel like she's choosing inside it. Reread, "game" is the wrapper — the camouflage the wanting comes out in, as the text itself says. The book title, **WITH A LONG SPOON** — "he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon." That's the proverb, and it recolors everything: someone here is dining with something dangerous. Is Pace the devil, or is he the one supping? "BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION" and this being an *invitation* to Vee — the politeness is the horror; the whole predation is conducted in warmth and good manners. It points me toward a slow, courteous corruption. I'm bracing for Vee to be the one who needed the long spoon.

What I want next: I want to stay with Vee, see her from her own side before the machine closes. I dread — guess from here — that Randi will pursue her sincerely and destructively, and that Pace will "give" Randi something that hollows her further under the guise of a gift.
