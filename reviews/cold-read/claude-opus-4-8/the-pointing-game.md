## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter frightened me, and I think it meant to. Not through anything graphic — through its sweetness. The bed scene is genuinely hot: his mouth still wet from her, her taking her own taste off his lips, and then the question — *would you like to taste another woman* — landing in her body before it lands in her, pulling a third orgasm out of her "far too fast for anything his body was doing to account for it." That's the real swoon of the chapter, and I felt it. Her helplessness is beautiful and awful. And then the costume goes back on: *only in a threesome, with you.* I admired her the way he admired her — she moved the wanting, handed it to him, made herself the generous instrument instead of the woman who hungers. After the bench chapter, where she decided he could have the face and never the person again, this is that decision in action. She found a way to want something and survive wanting it by making it his.

But the walk is where my unease hardened into something colder, and the page earned every inch of it. Because this is a *hunt.* He points; she picks. He frames it as her agency — "I point, you pick," "the choosing is hers" — but he's the one who sees, two hours earlier, exactly what her body confessed, and spends the afternoon walking her toward admitting it in daylight dressed as play. The thing I can't shake: he picks Vee *for* what makes her vulnerable. She's laughing with her head thrown all the way back, "like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That forgetting is precisely what the bench chapter told me Pace targets — being *seen* past the surface. And now he's aimed Randi at the one girl on the quad who doesn't have the armor. "Go get her." And Randi does — that con, the engineered collision so clean the *redhead* apologizes, the hand on the arm a half-beat too long — my God, she's good, and watching her be good at it while lit up like a kid made my stomach turn even as I was dazzled.

So where do I sit with Pace now? My trust is gone, and it went quietly. In the earlier chapters I could read his control as care — the apple sliced thin, the water left out, the respected door. Here the same attentiveness is a tool for acquisition. He "wanted her himself," twice noted his own arousal at Vee, and is using his girlfriend as the instrument to get her. "A Polite Invitation," the subtitle says. This is the invitation, and it is very polite, and that's the horror of it. I don't think the book is naïve about this — the final line, *let's go feed you*, after she's refused the cheeseburger all afternoon, is the author telling me what kind of feeding this is. He feeds her the thing her discipline won't let her have. The appetite he's answering tonight is Vee.

And Randi — I ache for her and I'm a little afraid of her. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" is a chilling line out of a woman who wept facedown on that bench two chapters ago. She's already thinking of Vee the way she was used. Victim and recruiter in one body. That's the interior life I read for, and it's here, dark and unresolved. Her bare *yes* at the doors — "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — is the one moment the person came out from behind the face all afternoon, and it was for a woman, not for him. I don't know yet what that means and I love that I don't.

Erotic charge: strong and sustained, not just in bed but strung through the whole walk — "she had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." The charge is doing real narrative work, not decorating. Where it got complicated for me is that the heat and the dread are the *same* sensation here, inseparable, which is exactly the trick of the thing.

Friction: almost none. Occasionally the omniscience over-explains his mastery — "he knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it" skates toward self-congratulation, and the catalogue of rejected girls ran a touch long (the striking blazer-girl, the pairs) before Vee arrived. But the length earned itself: the parade is the seduction, the slow build to the one who stops her cold.

The title: **"The Pointing Game"** is this chapter exactly — it names the mechanism, and knowing it going in made me watch for the pointing, which sharpened rather than spoiled it. On second thought it recolors darker: a *game*, something light, a thing lovers do — and it's procurement. The volume line **"WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION"** — I keep hearing the proverb under it, *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* That casts Pace, and tells me the person without the long spoon is the one being invited. Vee, I think. And **"A Polite Invitation"** is tonight. I dread, cleanly and with full consent to the book, what Book One is inviting her into.

What I want next: Vee. I want her as a person, not a target — and I'm braced for the book to let me fall for her right alongside Randi, which would be the cruelest and best thing it could do. *Guess from here:* Vee is the center of this (the title's "pointing," the naming — Vivienne), and Randi's *yes* for her is going to turn out to be more than an instrument's yes.
