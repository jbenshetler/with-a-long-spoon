## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

Okay. This chapter did something to me that I want to name carefully, because the swoon and the dread came braided so tight I couldn't pull them apart, and I don't think I'm supposed to. The sex opening it is genuinely gorgeous — him coming up into her while she's still pulsing and undefended, kissing her with her own taste on his mouth, and *"You like the taste of yourself"* landing not as a line but as a thing he'd already felt in her kiss. The three orgasms are hot in the real way, the way where the heat is doing psychological work rather than choreography. And the question — *"Would you like to taste another woman?"* — reaching her body before it reached her, taking her all at once. That's the erotic peak of the chapter for me and it's a peak because her *body answered ahead of her*. That's the whole engine of these people.

But here's where I got uneasy, and I think the page earned it. Pace is reading her the entire time, and he keeps taking "the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." He watches her flinch, watches her dress the wanting up as a gift to him, and he *sees through it* — he narrates the whole architecture of her self-protection to himself — and then he uses it anyway. He tells himself he wants her "free to weigh it and choose; that mattered more to him than the answer did," and I don't fully believe him, and I don't think I'm meant to. He's already decided. He points at a girl on the quad in daylight and it's called, right there in the title, a *game*. Something in me tightened at "I point. You pick," even as she laughed and I half-laughed with her.

And yet — the pointing sequence swept me up despite myself. It's structured like seduction and it works like seduction: the type-nearest-her, the matched pairs, the *maybe* on the library steps that gets rewarded with a kiss still tasting of her. I was leaning forward. I *wanted* him to find the right one. That's the trap of the chapter and I walked into it happily. When Vee comes out of the dining hall — thrown-back laugh, copper hair the wind won't leave alone, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten she could be seen" — I felt the *yes* before Randi said it. The bare "Yes," alone, with nothing around it. That undid me a little. After everything I know about Randi from the last two chapters — the face, the not-crying under the duvet, the deadbolt — watching her stop mid-stride and *not look away* felt like the same thing that happened on the bench: the real one coming out from behind the face, involuntarily.

Which is exactly why the dread is real. I *know* Randi. I read her decide, in the bathroom mirror, that the one Pace found was going back behind the face and "not coming out again — not ever." And here she is a scene later, out from behind it again, glowing, doing happy footwork on the brick she "didn't decide to do." She can't control this and it terrifies her and it's the most alive she gets. And now there's a third person with a name and a stats class and no idea. *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"* — that closing line went through me cold. Vee is being pointed at. She's a person who forgot she could be seen, and two people who watch everything have just chosen her.

My feelings moved most on Pace. Two chapters ago I found him genuinely tender — the water bottle, the closed door he wouldn't open, Daphne. That patience read as care. Here the same patience reads as *technique*, and the fact that it's the identical skill set is the unsettling part. He's not cruel. He's generous, attentive, he wants her to have joy. But he's running her, and he knows he's running her, and the tenderness and the running are the same hand. I don't trust the frame he's putting on his own wanting. That's not the text over-explaining — it's the text letting me watch him narrate himself and quietly decline to believe him.

Erotic charge: high through the bed scene, then it transmutes into something stranger on the quad — not slack, but the heat migrates into *watching*, into the hunt. The kiss where she tastes herself again in public is the bridge. It never went clinical for me. If anything the quad walk is charged in a creepier register, and I mean that as praise — the wanting is unmistakable and I'm no longer sure I should be enjoying it.

Friction: almost none. The prose is controlled to the point that I occasionally felt the author's confidence as a slight over-patterning — "The follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step" recurs as a metaphor and got faintly schematic the third time. But it's a small thing.

The title — **"The Pointing Game."** It gave nothing away before I read it, and now it's chilling in retrospect: the whole chapter *is* the game, named as play precisely so nobody has to look at what it is. He literally points. "Go meet her." A predation dressed as a couple being fun, and the title colludes in the dressing. The cover-board line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, series title **The Pointing Game** shares its name with this chapter... actually the packet gives the book title as *The Pointing Game* and the volume line as *With a Long Spoon, Book One — A Polite Invitation*. "A long spoon" — the proverb: *sup with the devil, use a long spoon*. That reframes everything. Someone here is the devil, and someone should be keeping their distance and isn't. "A Polite Invitation" — Vee is about to receive one. No tagline supplied. Together they point me exactly where the last line does: toward a third person walking into something two practiced people have arranged.

What I want next: I want Vee's inside. I've only seen her from Pace's watching eye and Randi's hunger. I dread what's coming for her and I can't stop reading.
