## Reader reaction

# The Felt Read

Well. There's the game. Two chapters of cold undertow and now I see the machine the jacket promised, and God, it's chillier and more seductive than I expected — because it's played entirely as tenderness.

The opening sex is the most explicit the book's been, and it worked on me — the wet-mouthed kiss, her taking herself off his lips, the third orgasm he "worked out of her deliberately." Heat 3, easily. But what's doing the real work isn't the mechanics, it's the surveillance folded into them. "He knew the difference between a woman performing release and a woman delivered to it." He's *reading* her the whole time, and then he weaponizes the reading: he asks "Would you like to taste another woman?" precisely when she can't defend against it, watches her come helplessly *at the question itself*, and concludes — coldly, beautifully — "A wanting she allowed herself was only a wanting. A wanting she'd forbidden was a door. And he was, by some old architecture, a man who could not leave a door like that closed once he'd seen it was there."

That line moved my read of Pace hard. In chapter one I found him magnetic and privately distrusted; here the distrust has a name now. He is a man who *hunts forbidden doors*. He phrases everything as gift — "the thing she wanted and could not let herself reach for, put in her hands in daylight with her own clear yes on it" — and I believe he believes it. That's what unsettles me most. He's not a cynic. He's a true believer in his own generosity, and that makes him far more dangerous than a predator who knows he's one. The tagline finally bit me: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." Randi *does* say yes, twice, freely, and I watched him engineer the exact conditions under which the yes could occur. He even lets her keep the face-saving frame ("Only in a threesome. With you.") because — his words — "the frame she'd built to hide in was the exact frame he needed to proceed." That's the trap operating in real time. She thinks she's protecting herself and she's handing him the wrapper he needs.

And Randi — my ache for her from last chapter deepened and complicated. Watching her "shop" is the most alive I've seen her. "She would have told him she was being a good sport about his game; she had stopped being a good sport a quarter mile back. She was shopping." The verdicts she hands down are *character* — sharp, funny, real: "Those two go everywhere together. You'd get both or neither." "She'd be a project." "Too expected." I liked her more here, and simultaneously I watched her get played, and she has no idea. That's the horror running under the charm. She thinks the game is *hers* — "I point. You pick" delights her, "he was watching her learn that she could" — and it's his all the way down. Both readings are true and that doubleness is what makes the chapter land.

The pointing sequence itself is superb and I didn't get bored once, which surprised me for what's essentially a catalog of passing women. Each girl is a real quick portrait, and each *no* teaches Pace (and me) Randi's taste by elimination — not the mirrors, not the matched pairs, not the closed projects, not the ones who run the room. It reads like a courtship of her appetite. Then Vee.

Vee. The entrance is staged to overwhelm and it did — the copper hair the wind won't leave alone, the freckles, the lush uncurated body "clothes chosen to cover, and failing at an impossible task," and above all the laughing "with her whole body... like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That last clause is the whole point: Vee is the one woman on the quad *not performing being looked at*, which is exactly the wound Randi's built out of. Randi's "yes" arriving bare, the arrested mid-step, the not-looking-away — I felt the pull as real. This isn't just Pace's target; something in Randi genuinely reached.

And the collision. The staged bump where Randi's decade of dance makes *Vee* the one who apologizes — "it was the redhead's hand that flew to her own mouth" — is a gorgeous, sickening little set-piece. Vee walks away thinking she bumped into a nice girl. I know I just watched a hunt conclude. The jacket's "They told her nothing" now has a face and a stats class.

**Trust/suspicion:** No ambiguity left about Pace's method — the text earned the dread this time, I'm not manufacturing it. What I *don't* know is whether he has any capacity to be caught by his own game (the jacket says both founders fall as hard as Vee — I'm watching for the crack). Randi I trust as a person and fear for. Vee I've met for ninety seconds and already feel protective of, which is exactly what the book wants and I'll cop to it.

**Friction:** Very little. Occasionally the interiority over-narrates its own cleverness — "because it was beautiful and because he was built to see it," "by some old architecture" — a hair too pleased with Pace's perceptiveness, telling me he's a genius reader rather than only showing it. And "the follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step" is a lovely image used twice (dance-follow metaphor) and by the second deployment I felt the thumb slightly. Small complaints against a chapter that mostly earned its length.

**Titles:** "The Pointing Game" is exactly right and doesn't give away more than the chapter does — it names the literal thing (he points, she picks) and the awful lightness of calling it a *game*, which is the tagline's whole con. "With a Long Spoon" — I still don't have the proverb consciously, but it's circling something like *sup with the devil, use a long spoon*: keep distance from the dangerous thing you're consorting with. Nobody here is using a long spoon. That's the promise — that the distance they think they're keeping (Pace's frames, Randi's costume) collapses.

**What I want/dread next:** I want Vee on the page as herself, in her own head, before the two of them get to her — I want to know the person, not the target. I dread how good it's going to feel while it happens. Guess from here: the stats class is the next lever, Randi runs the befriending while Pace stays offstage, and the "how real it got" of the jacket starts with Randi actually falling for Vee — or Vee for Randi — outside the plan.

**Story so far**
Ch.1 (The Bench): In Pace's house, Randi (having lied to her sorority about a paper) is worked through a long patient bondage-bench cycle until he breaks her social performance, she begs and comes crying; aftercare; she flinches at "You're beautiful," locks the bathroom, reassembles her face, returns performing contentment; he privately makes plans for her; she stays over.
Ch.2 (Standards): The morning after. Randi comes home in last night's clothes; Brooke (with Sloane, Kinsey) runs a four-minute all-smiles interrogation about the paper and about Pace (name, math PhD, whether he teaches her — a near-slip). Randi holds the mask and escapes upstairs, can't function, lies down clothed and shod under an inadequate duvet in a cold room, and does not cry — revealing she's so practiced at not-crying it no longer feels like an act.
Ch.3 (The Pointing Game): Four weeks in. In bed, mid-orgasm Pace asks if she'd like to taste another woman; her body says yes helplessly (twice). He recognizes it as a *forbidden* wanting — a door — and resolves to give it to her as a "gift." He lets her reframe it as a girlfriend's offering ("Only in a threesome. With you.") because that frame is what he needs to proceed. They walk campus and play the "pointing game": he points at women, she picks/rejects, teaching him her taste (not the expected, matched pairs, closed projects, or room-runners). Randi comes alive doing it. Outside the dining hall, Pace points at Vee — tall, lush, copper-haired, freckled, laughing unselfconsciously — and Randi's bare "yes" escapes her. On his cue, Randi executes a flawless staged "collision" that makes Vee apologize; they exchange names; Randi returns glowing: "Her name is Vee. And we've got a stats class together." He says, "Let's go feed you."

**How I feel**
Sympathy for Randi deepened and split — she's most alive and likable here, and being played without knowing it, and I ache watching both at once. Pace crossed a line in my trust: still magnetic, now clearly and coldly a hunter of forbidden desire who calls it generosity and seems to believe it — the text earned my dread this time. Vee: instant protectiveness after ninety seconds, exactly as intended, and I'm braced. Overall mood: the cold undertow has become a warm, seductive machine — a "game" played as tenderness, doors opening one after another, and a girl who knows nothing walking straight up the brick toward two people who've already decided about her.
