## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This one turned my stomach a little, and I mean that as praise — the chapter is doing exactly what the last two set up, and doing it with a horrible grace. The bench chapter left me with Randi's private vow: *he could have the face, and the one he found was going back behind it, not ever coming out again.* So watching Pace ask *"Would you like to taste another woman?"* into her mid-orgasm undefendedness — and watching her body answer before she can dress the answer — I wasn't swept up. I was braced. He keeps calling her body's answer "the true one." *"He took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did."* That line landed as a confession he doesn't know he's making. He is so proud of his patience, his not-rushing, his refusal to make her name the wanting — and every bit of that gentleness is in service of getting past her *no*, the one she just swore in the mirror. The reframe — *"Only in a threesome. With you."* — is her doing exactly what she vowed: moving the appetite off herself and onto him, handing it away so it costs nothing. He *sees* her do it and calls it beautiful and lets it stand because it gets him what he wants. That's the scariest thing in the chapter: two people colluding to keep her hidden from herself, each thinking they're being kind.

My feeling about Pace has moved hard. Two chapters ago I could read his attentiveness as real care — the sliced apple, the water bottle, the safeword checks, the respected door, the Daphne wound. Here the same toolkit is aimed like a weapon and I can't unsee it. He *points at a stranger in daylight* and coaches Randi through her own recoil until she's "shopping." *"I point. You pick."* The Daphne door he wouldn't open — he's now walking through the front of Randi's whole self with her thanking him for it. He's not a brute; he's worse, a craftsman. And the craftsman just built a bench.

Randi I ache for more than ever. The cheeseburger — *"I can't eat that,"* automatic, no glance back — right after the apple chapter, is a tiny gut-punch. And the tell that she's not being "a good sport" but is genuinely, helplessly *pulled* toward Vee — the arrested mid-step, the not-looking-away — that's the realest thing she does. It's the same helpless *yes* her body gave in bed. I believe her wanting completely; I just watch her instantly costume it into a gift for him.

And Vee. God, Vee. The chapter earns her entrance — the burning maple, the wind, the whole two-century-old quad composed to make the eye travel — and then she comes out *laughing with her head thrown all the way back, like a person who had forgotten she could be seen.* After a whole chapter of women graded by the sorority grammar, here's a body the grammar can't touch and a person who isn't performing. She's the only unguarded, unmanaged human on the page, and she's the one they've now selected. The staged collision — Randi's dance-trained execution so clean *the redhead apologizes* — turned my sympathy fully protective. Vee thinks she bumped into someone. She's already inside the machine.

**Swoon / pull.** The straightforward erotic pull worked on me in the bed scene — the taste-of-herself kiss, the third orgasm pulled out slow, is genuinely hot. But the swoon curdles the instant he starts asking questions, and by the walk I wasn't rooting for anyone to *have* anyone. I was reading a hunt. The pull to keep reading is enormous — but it's dread-pull, not root-for-them pull.

**Trust / suspicion.** Fully tipped over. The text earned it: not a vibe, but the pattern — his framing of consent, her mirror-vow last chapter, the *"with you"* he pockets. I no longer trust Pace's tenderness at all, and I don't think I'm supposed to.

**Erotic charge.** Peaks early, in bed — real heat. Then it deliberately cools into something clinical and predatory on the walk, which I think is intentional; the charge migrates from sex into *selection*, and that migration is chilling rather than hot.

**Friction.** Very little thumb-on-scale; it mostly trusts me. If anything, the parade of graded women runs a touch long and catalog-like before Vee — but I think that's the point, the grammar exhausting itself so Vee can break it. *"The follow had felt the music and broken frame before the lead called the step"* recurs a hair often.

**Titles.** *"The Pointing Game"* is literal and awful — he literally points; and the last line, *"Do you think she'll like the bench?"*, tells me the game's stakes: Randi's now recruiting. The volume line — *WITH A LONG SPOON* — I read as the proverb: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* So someone here is the devil, and the spoon is the distance you'd need and won't keep. *A POLITE INVITATION* is exactly what this chapter is — an invitation extended with perfect manners toward a girl who doesn't know she's been invited. It gave away the shape of the dread before I got there, and I'm glad it did.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here).** I dread Vee. I want the book to give her interiority equal to Randi's, and I suspect it will — the warmth she has is the exact thing this house destroys. I'd guess Vee becomes the next one on the bench, and that Randi discovers she can't hand *this* away as cheaply as she promised.
