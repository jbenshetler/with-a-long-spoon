## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

So the night on the bench told me who Randi is underneath — the girl who begged in a voice she hadn't used since childhood, then went into the bathroom and resolved, quiet as a deadbolt, to put the face back on *forever*. And now this chapter does something that chilled me: it takes that same mechanism — the dodge, the handing-over, the costume — and lets Pace *use* it as the engine of a game. He asks "would you like to taste another woman?" and her body answers before she can dress it. Three times she comes on the question itself. And I watched, with a kind of sick fascination, her do the exact move I'd just seen her name to herself: she *moves* the want. "Only in a threesome. With you." Not a woman who hungers, which is unsurvivable — a girlfriend giving a gift, which is merely sexy. Same act, different owner. The book keeps showing me the inside of her lie and it is the most alive thing on the page.

Here's where my footing goes — and I think that's the chapter working on me, not failing. The pointing game *is* genuinely charged. The walk, the burning maple, the cataloguing of girls she grades against her own standard and keeps rejecting — "too expected," "those two go everywhere together" — and then Vee comes out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bared, forgetting she can be seen, and Randi's stride just *drops out from under her.* "Yes," alone, with nothing around it. That landed. I wanted her to have that — the first undefended thing she's wanted in daylight. The grin breaking the managed surface, the little happy footwork on the brick, "her name is Vee and we've got a stats class together." I was swept up. I rooted for the aliveness.

And *that's* what unsettles me, because I can't tell anymore whose want this is. Pace frames it, start to finish, as a gift moving "toward her hands" — he points, she picks, he's teaching her she's *allowed*. It reads as generous. But I've been inside Randi's head one chapter ago, and I know the yes that comes up helplessly is the one she then spends the whole night re-housing so she never has to own it. He knows that too — "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer" — and he asks the sober version anyway, and when she dodges into *with you*, he lets the dodge stand and calls it the best idea he's had in a month. He is being so careful, so attentive, reading her like an instrument — and the care is exactly what makes me uneasy. He's not overriding her. He's engineering a situation where her undefended want does the overriding *for* him, and getting to feel like a man who only ever answered a hunger she brought him.

Pace unsettled me more than in any chapter yet, and the text earned it — not through cruelty, through the precision. The frisbee, the stat class, Vee's freckles, his own blunt beat of answering her first: he wants Vee too, and the "gift" has him in it. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — Randi's own closing line — is the thing that put ice down my back. She's already recruiting a stranger into the room where she herself got taken apart, cheerfully, because it's safer to be the one holding the door than the one behind it. Vee laughing with her whole body, "a person who had genuinely forgotten she could be seen" — I ached, because I think I just watched two people decide to go find out what happens when they make her stop forgetting.

**The title.** "The Pointing Game" — before I read it meant nothing; now it's almost too literal, and then not literal at all. He points, she picks: that's the surface rule, playful, a couple being filthy on a walk. But the real game is which of them is choosing, and the chapter keeps insisting it's *her* choice while showing me his finger on every beat of it. The volume line **"With A Long Spoon · Book One — A Polite Invitation"** reads now like a warning I can't unhear: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* A polite invitation — the kitchen apple, the unlocked door, "let's go for a walk" — is how you get close enough to be eaten. I can't tell yet who's supping with whom, and I think that ambiguity is the point.

**Erotic charge.** The bedroom open worked on me hard — coming up over her mid-orgasm, the taste of herself in the kiss, the question detonating a climax his body didn't account for. That's the peak. The walk runs cooler and stranger — charged, but the heat is appraising, almost predatory window-shopping, and it kept getting cut by my unease, which I think is deliberate.

**Friction.** A little. Pace's interiority occasionally over-narrates its own tenderness — "that mattered more to him than the answer did," "she deserved that gentleness, he wanted it for her" — and I start to feel the book insisting on his decency a half-beat too hard, which has the odd effect of making me trust it less. Whether that's his self-justification or the author's thumb, I genuinely can't tell yet, and I'll hold it open.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Vee to be a real person and not a dish. I dread that she won't get the one chapter inside her own head that Randi got. Guess from here: Vee is the jacket's center and we're about to be handed to her.
