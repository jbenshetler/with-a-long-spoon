## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter landed on me two ways at once, and I couldn't get them to hold still. The campus walk is genuinely, helplessly charged — I was swept up in it. The pointing game, "I point. You pick," Randi shopping the quad while pretending to be a good sport, the kiss with her own taste still on his mouth so her breath catches — that's expert erotic teasing, and I felt it. And her lit-up grin coming back across the lawn, the happy footwork on the brick she didn't decide to do, the squeaked *"Yes"* — I melted a little. She's *alive* out there. After the last two chapters, where she retreated behind the face and swore never to come out again, seeing something break the managed surface in daylight moved me.

But I can't unsee what's underneath, and I think the page wants me not to. Pace "took the body's answer for the true one. He usually did." He asks the question *during* orgasm, watches her come helplessly before her mind can dress it, and reads that as consent — then asks again once she's gathered, and when she reframes it ("Only in a threesome. With you"), he understands she's *moved the wanting onto him to survive it*, and he lets the camouflage stand because "it was the wrapper the wanting needed to come out in." That's tender narration wrapped around something I found genuinely unsettling: he is extracting and steering, congratulating himself on gentleness while running a hunt. The line "*is this one mine to take?*" gave me a chill precisely because he answers it with a warm nod.

And then Vee. She's the first person all afternoon rendered as a *person* and not a type — head thrown back, "laughing like a person who had genuinely forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." That detail is the whole ache: the one who forgot she could be seen is exactly who they're now watching, weighing, and — the collision engineered so cleanly *she* apologizes — closing in on. I liked Vee instantly and immediately feared for her. She has a name and a stats class now and no idea she was pointed at.

My feeling about Pace moved, and darkened. In the bench chapter I could hold his patience as real care with a shadow. Here the shadow is the point. He is charming, attentive, and predatory, and the prose's very warmth toward him is what unnerves me — I don't fully trust the voice that admires him. Randi I feel tender toward and worried about: she's found a live wire (real desire for a woman, arriving before she can make it "mean something safer"), but she's routing it through Pace's gift-frame instead of owning it, and "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — she's already recruiting, already imagining Vee on the apparatus. That last line chilled me clean.

Trust vs. suspicion: the text earned my unease this time, not just atmosphere — the consent mechanics ("body's answer for the true one") and the fact that a real, unknowing third woman is being maneuvered toward that bedroom. Erotic charge: high and sustained through the bed scene and the game; peak at the helpless mid-sex "yes" and at Vee coming out the doors.

Friction: almost none — maybe a touch of authorial fondness for Pace's cleverness (the dance-metaphor "the follow felt the music and broke frame" recurs a hair often). But it reads as seduction technique, so I forgive it.

Titles: this chapter *is* the book's title — "The Pointing Game" — and now I know what the game is: he points, she picks, and the picking teaches her she's *allowed* to want. It's playful on the surface and predatory underneath, exactly like the chapter. The volume line **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** landed hard now: "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." Somebody here is the devil, and "a polite invitation" is precisely what this was — Vee, invited politely, by a girl who bumped her shoulder and touched her arm a half-beat too long. It points me toward a slow, courteous, consuming harm.

What I want/dread: I want Vee to be more than prey — to have her own weight, maybe to be the one who can't be managed. I dread the bench. *Guess from here:* Vee becomes real enough to break the game open, and someone gets hurt in a way the polite frame can't cover.
