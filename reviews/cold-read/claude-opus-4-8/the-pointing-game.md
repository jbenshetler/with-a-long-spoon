## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This chapter did two things to me at once and wouldn't let me separate them, and that's exactly its trick. The sex at the top is *good* — his mouth on her, sliding into her while she's still pulsing and undefended, kissing her with her own taste still on him and feeling her rise to it. "You like the taste of yourself." That worked on me cleanly; the heat is real and the attention is real. And then the question lands like a trapdoor — "Would you like to taste another woman?" — and she comes helplessly, ahead of herself, her body answering before she can make it mean something safe. I felt the swoon and the unease arrive in the same breath. That's the achievement here and also what frightened me.

What I can't get past is Randi's move, and how tenderly the book renders it: she takes the wanting her body already confessed and *relaunders* it — "Only in a threesome. With you." She can't survive being a woman who hungers for a woman, so she becomes a generous girlfriend offering her man a gift. The book names it: "The same act. A different owner." After the bench chapter, where she decided in the mirror to go back behind the face forever, this is heartbreaking and consistent. She is converting her own aliveness into a service she performs. And Pace — who I keep almost trusting because he's careful, because he waits for the door, because he says *that's not what this night was for* — I now don't trust at all. He sees exactly what she's doing and lets it stand because it serves the game. His care is instrumentation.

Then the walk, and the chill set all the way in. "I point. You pick." He is teaching her she can *shop* for a woman, dressing a hunt as play, and she goes from offended propriety to "she was shopping" over a quarter mile. I was swept along by the craft of it — the burning maple, the wind in her ponytail, the choosing as flirtation — and simultaneously sick, because there's a real person at the end of the finger. When Vee came out of the dining hall laughing with her throat bared, "forgotten for the length of the laugh that she could be seen," I felt genuine tenderness toward her and genuine dread. She is the one unguarded creature on the whole quad, and that's precisely why he points.

The collision is the coldest, most impressive beat in the chapter. Randi's dance training folded into three seconds of execution "so clean it looked like nothing" — so clean that *Vee* apologizes, Vee's hand flies to her own mouth. The predator makes the prey say sorry. I admired the writing and recoiled from what it depicts in the same instant. And the ending — Randi lit up, grinning, "her name is Vee," the squeaked *yes*, and then "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — turned my stomach precisely because Randi is *happy*. The one time all book she's dropped the managed surface in daylight, and it's in service of delivering another woman to that bench.

I have no confusion and plenty of earned suspicion. Nobody feels "off" in the sense of a hidden reveal — it's worse, it's all on the surface. My sympathy sits almost entirely with Randi (the apple, the cheeseburger she "can't eat," the girl portioning herself) and now, freshly and achingly, with Vee, who has done nothing but laugh and be warm. Pace I now read as genuinely dangerous under the gentleness, and the book hasn't flinched from showing me that.

The erotic charge peaks early (the bed) and then becomes something stranger on the walk — not quite arousal, more a predatory thrum the book made me feel from the *inside*, which is its own disturbing kind of charge. It never goes clinical. The friction, if any, is how seductive it all is; I kept catching myself enjoying the hunt before remembering the quarry is real.

The titles: "The Pointing Game" is now literal and awful — he points, she picks, and I didn't know until I'd read it that "pointing" meant selecting a person. The chapter title gave nothing away in advance and then recolored everything. "WITH A LONG SPOON" — the devil's dinner — and "A POLITE INVITATION" as Book One: this is where the invitation gets extended, and it's polite, and that's the horror. The long spoon is for supping with the devil; I think I've just watched the devil set a place for Vee, and I don't know yet which of these two is holding the spoon.

I want to keep reading badly, and I dread it. Guess from here: Vee is the one this book is really about, and the stats class is the next thread.
