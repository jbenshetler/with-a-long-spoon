## Reader reaction

This one scared me, and I think it meant to. The whole chapter is a seduction I was inside of before I clocked what it was doing to me — the burning maple, the wind in the ponytails, the daylight warmth of it all — and then I realized I'd been walking the quad enjoying a man *shopping for a woman using his girlfriend as the cart.* And the thing that chills me is how good it feels while it happens. Pace is so attentive, so unhurried, so genuinely pleased by Randi's aliveness, that the tenderness and the predation are literally the same gesture. I can't separate them. That's the horror and that's the craft.

The sex up front is hot — really hot — and I want to be honest that it worked on me before I got nervous. The orgasm that arrives on the *question* rather than on his body ("the question reached her body before it reached her, and it took her all at once") is the single most charged beat: desire outrunning the self, the body confessing what the mouth will spend the rest of the chapter re-dressing. The taste-of-herself kiss, him sliding in while she's still undefended — I felt that. But I also felt the cold wire under it: "He knew her body's answer. He did not take it for her answer." He's scrupulous about consent in a way that is itself a tool. He waits for her to choose freely *because the free choice is the thing he's harvesting.* That's more unsettling than any coercion would be.

And after "Standards" — after watching Randi lie to the whole chapter house, lie in the mirror, decide the one he *found* was "going back behind" the face and "not coming out again" — I read this entire walk knowing what she's protecting. So when she moves the wanting off herself ("Only in a threesome. With you"), I understood it exactly as he narrates it: she handed the appetite to him to survive it. He sees that too, and admires it, and *uses it anyway.* He doesn't have to find her a woman for her transgression; she'll find a woman for theirs. The cage she built in the bathroom is now the mechanism he steers by. God.

Vee. The redhead is the first person all afternoon drawn with real tenderness instead of appraisal — the laugh with her throat bare, "forgotten, for the length of the laugh, that she could be seen." She's the opposite of the face. Of course that's the one. And Randi's bare *yes* — "the bare arrival of a thing already true before she got to it" — is the same undressed voice from the bench. It's beautiful and I dreaded it simultaneously. The "pointing game": he points, she picks, he's teaching her she *can* pick. I felt the swoon of her lit-up footwork on the brick, the squeaked *yes*, and then the floor fell out when she asked, head down, "Do you think she'll like the bench?" Randi's already recruiting. The prey has become the hunter's apprentice, and I don't think she knows it yet.

My feelings moved hard on Pace. I came into this liking his patience; I leave it afraid of exactly that patience. The Daphne door he respected last chapter now reads differently — not restraint but *technique*, knowing when not-touching is the stronger move. The one crack of real human cost (Daphne) makes him scarier, not softer. And Vee — I ache for her already, laughing by the dining hall with no idea she's been pointed at, named, assigned a role in two people's appetite. The shoulder-collision scene is a marvel of writing and the most sinister thing here: Randi's whole trained body folded into three seconds so clean "it looked like nothing," engineering an accident so the *victim apologizes to her.* That's grooming rendered as grace.

Almost no friction. If anything the chapter is *too* seductive, too in love with Pace's competence — but I think that seduction is the point, implicating me. The one spot I felt the thumb was the repeated insistence on how well Randi "moves," the dance metaphors stacking up (follow, lead, turn called); it's a touch over-sold. But it earned it at the collision.

The chapter title — **"The Pointing Game"** — is doing exactly what the book keeps doing: naming the predation in nursery-rhyme register, light and playful, "his little game," while what it describes is a man selecting human beings off a path. It recolored the whole walk on me the second I understood *pointing* literally. The volume line, **"WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION,"** lands now like a warning I should've heeded: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* Who's the devil here — Pace, or the appetite he's teaching Randi to own? "A polite invitation" is Vee, I think: the collision, the warm hand on the arm, stats class. The politeness *is* the trap. **"The Pointing Game"** didn't give anything away so much as confirm, after the fact, what I'd been enjoying.

What I want next: I dread and want to meet Vee properly. I want to know if this is predation on Vee, on Randi, or on both at once — and I suspect the book won't let me keep them separate. *Guess from here:* Vee is not the simple mark she looks like; the warmth drawn into her so carefully feels load-bearing. And I fear Randi's "not ever coming out again" is going to break precisely on this.
