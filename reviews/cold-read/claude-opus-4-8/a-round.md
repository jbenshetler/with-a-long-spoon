## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

God, this chapter worked on me. It's the hottest thing in the book so far and almost nothing happens — a dressed man with a measuring tape, a woman on a box. That's the whole engine, and it ran at full heat. The structure Pace uses — close, two touches, withdraw, pencil, look from across the room — I felt the *withdrawal* as the erotic thing, exactly as Vee did: "the withdrawal left the place his hands had been ringing." That's superb. The charge lives in restraint, in the geometry she keeps naming ("him there with his shirt buttoned and her here"), and it built and built.

Vee — I adore her and I ache for her. The whole chapter is her shame rendered as heat, which is the thing I most wanted this book to be brave enough to do. The wet satin she can't will away, "the warm betrayal of it," the spot that goes from thumbprint to "big and dark and stupid" — that's humiliation and arousal braided so tight they're one sensation, and the page never once lets her off the hook or flinches from it. And then the turn, when she opens her eyes expecting the grin-of-the-joke-of-her and gets instead a man craning his neck up, waiting, "delighted by her. The whole fact of her." Her spine uncurling without her permission, rising onto her toes toward "something she had no name for" — I melted. "She had spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." That's the whole book's ache in one line, and it earned it.

The swoon is real and I'm not going to shave it down with my own foreknowledge. The dress itself — that he matched *her* and not the magazine shade, that he kept the thrown-away wanting she'd let go of in one breath on the summit — undid me in the previous chapter and pays off here as the reason she's standing half-bare in his light at all. And the discipline of his want: "Keep still," coming "a beat too late, after a silence she watched him spend deciding." He's choosing to do the thing right over the easy yes she's "all but handing him," and the choosing costs him. That reads as genuine honor on the page.

And yet — my low wariness didn't vanish; it just got quieter under the heat, and I notice that I *let* it. I know what she doesn't: Randi, the bench, the pointing game, the fact that a plan reached her first. The over-warm house she filed "under the small mysteries of him" is a thing I can't file so innocently — he heated the rooms hours early so a half-bare woman wouldn't be cold, which is tenderness and is also *preparation*, and the chapter even brushes it: "He'd thought of it hours before she came, probably." The riser that puts her sex at his eye level, built, finished, in the corner of his house — Vee rationalizes it ("of course a woodworker has a riser"), and I let her, but a riser is a thing you build for someone to stand on and be looked at. That's the book's whole method working on me the way it works on her: the engineered thing arriving dressed as kindness, and the kindness being *real too*. I'm holding both. The caring is not fake. That's what makes it frightening.

No new hard suspicion landed that the text didn't carry before — but "she'd nearly toppled this contained, careful man" cuts two ways for me. She reads her power as hers. I read a man who has done exactly this before (the bench, Randi begging), running a practiced sequence, and letting her feel like the one in charge. I can't prove it's a sequence. The page leaves it ajar, so I leave it ajar.

The one near-miss for me: the pin-prick and the kiss on her ribs edged close to too-cute, a convenient device to let him put his mouth on her. But it's saved by "before he'd decided to, the way you put your hand out before you think" — it reads as his control slipping, not the author's.

Erotic charge: peak. It never went slack. The measuring rhythm, the inner-seam "QUIZ TODAY" lurch, the breast-against-his-jaw three times (accident, accident, on purpose — "Oops. Sorry."), him going red and fighting the grin — all of it burned clean.

**The titles.** "A Round" — after reading, it lands as a drink bought for the table (the froyo, the dinners, the series "WITH A LONG SPOON"), but also a *round* in a game, and a going-*around*: he literally knee-walks around the base of her. On second thought it recolors cold — a round in a longer contest, one turn of something that repeats. The series title, "WITH A LONG SPOON" — the proverb hangs over everything: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* It tells me plainly that someone at this tender table is the devil, and the spoon is for whoever doesn't know it. "BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION" and the measured courtliness fit exactly: the politeness *is* the bait. That unsettles me more than any single line, and I think it's meant to.

**What I want / dread next (guesses from here).** I want her to have this — I'm rooting for the dress, the induction, the night that's hers. I dread that the door at the end of the hall, the only locked one, is where this is all walking her, and that the afternoon she just read as being chosen was a round in the game Randi and Pace started. Guess from here: the dress gets finished, she wears it, and somewhere near that the shared world — Randi, the bench — opens under her.
