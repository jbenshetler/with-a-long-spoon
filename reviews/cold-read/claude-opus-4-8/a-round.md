## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is the measuring scene the last chapter set up — he offered to make the burgundy dress and measure her "in good light," she said yes, and now here we are. And God, it works. It's the most sustained heat in the book so far and almost none of it is sex. It's a tape measure and a fully-dressed man on his knees and a girl trying not to come apart standing on a box.

The engine of the whole thing is her wetness and her terror of it being seen — the satin she "put on with hope" turning traitor. "The hope was visible. The hope was sized to be seen across the room." That line landed hard. Her whole life's shame, the mother's voice (*stripper*, *floozie*, *girls like that*), made literal and physical and unhideable on pale shining fabric. I felt the dread with her genuinely — the couch moment, lifting the blanket, "It was big and dark and stupid." I was braced for the humiliation right alongside her.

And then the reveal, which is the emotional center: she opens her eyes expecting the grin of *the joke of her* and gets instead "the smile of a man who had just received an unexpected gift." He hadn't looked down. The whole time. That undid me a little, honestly, because it's the exact wound being met — she "spent the entire afternoon being seen at her most ashamed and had not, once, been found wanting." That's the healing she's been starving for since the pool, since the swimsuit that couldn't hold her.

Here's where my distrust sits, though, and it's sharp precisely *because* the scene is so good. I know — from the two towels, from the bench, from the jacket — that Pace and Randi picked this girl and are steering her. And this afternoon is *perfectly* calibrated to her deepest wound. The kept heat "so a woman could stand half-bare in it and not be cold." The tea with the water already hot. The riser already built. Every detail pre-thought "hours before she came." The narration frames all that pre-planning as tenderness, and in the moment it *reads* as tenderness — but I've been taught by this book that Pace's tenderness and Pace's engineering are the same gesture. A man who "thought of it hours before" is also a man who *engineered* it. The dress "matched to her" is love and calculation in one object; this whole hour is too. So the more it moves me, the more I brace. That's the trap the tagline promised: every yes here is freely, joyfully given. She's rising up onto her toes toward "something she had no name for." I have a name for my unease: I can't tell if he's falling or performing, and the text won't let me, and I think that's on purpose.

But — and this matters — the pin-prick moment cracks my distrust the way the shaking hand did earlier. He kisses the bead of blood "before he'd decided to, the way you put your hand out before you think," and then "Keep still" comes out low and too late, "after a silence she watched him spend deciding." If that's a man holding a line at real cost, it's involuntary in a way engineering isn't. The breast-against-his-cheek business, him going red, the laugh escaping through his nose — those read as a young man genuinely losing his footing. So I'm suspended: the setup is suspiciously perfect, but his body keeps betraying something that doesn't look staged. Which is exactly where the jacket told me I'd be — "Both are falling as hard as she is."

Vivienne herself deepened a lot here. The turn where she discovers "she had a kind of power she'd never been issued" and starts *spending* it — the deliberate "Oops. Sorry." — that's the first time I've seen her go on the offensive, and it's thrilling because it comes straight out of the shame breaking. She stumps him with the pre-aroused measurement joke and I loved her for it: "I made him laugh. And then I stumped him. Standing here like this, I did both." That's the funny, magnetic Vee I've been rooting for finally getting to *use* the room instead of hiding in it.

Erotic charge: peak. The whole measuring rhythm — "closeness, two touches, withdrawal, the pencil" — is genuinely one of the most effective slow-burn sequences I've read, because the withdrawal is what does the work, not the touch. "The withdrawal left the place his hands had been ringing." It never goes clinical because her interiority carries it; the tape is just the pretext. First time he touches her ass, first pin of blood, first breast to his face — the scene knows exactly which firsts to hit.

Friction: very little. If anything, the narration occasionally over-narrates her own psychology — "She felt, distantly, that this was going to matter to her later, in ways she couldn't yet see" is the author's thumb pressing the significance button; I'd have felt it without being told I'd feel it. Same with "the freely-given yes as trap" energy being spelled out through the kept-heat epiphany. But these are small. Mostly it trusts the body.

**The title.** "A Round." At first it's innocuous — a round of measurements, a fitting. Reading it now, it's the *circuit*: he knee-walks all the way around her, top to floor, front to behind to the last hip, "one slow turn on his knees from where she was wettest." The round is the orbit of his attention around her body on the pedestal. And there's a colder second reading I can't unsee — a "round" as in a round bought, something *spent*: she "started spending" her new power; he "spent" the silence deciding. And underneath, the crude pun hums (a-round / aroused / coming around). It's the kind of quiet title this book does — innocent on the surface, loaded on reread. It didn't give anything away going in; it pays off after.

**The book's title and tagline** feel more present than ever. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this is the chapter that *is* that sentence. She says yes to the bra, yes to the box, yes to standing there an hour, and every yes is joyful and hers, and I'm sitting here knowing there was a game she wasn't told about. *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil with a long spoon — the distance you keep to eat safely. Nobody in this scene is keeping any distance at all. The spoon's getting shorter.

**What I want / dread next:** I want to see Randi again badly — she's been offstage and the two-toweled house keeps her present as a threat. I dread the moment Vee's two worlds touch. Guess from here: the dress gets *worn* to the induction, and that's where the collision lives, or begins to. And I keep waiting for the shut bedroom door / the bench. She's now been half-naked in that house without seeing what's behind that door. Whenever she does, this golden afternoon is going to recolor.
