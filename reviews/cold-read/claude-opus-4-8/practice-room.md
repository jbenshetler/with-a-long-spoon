## Felt read

This one got under my skin in a way that made me put the book down for a second. Because I've watched Randi do the extraction lunches before — *leave nothing out* — but this is the first time I've seen her do it and understood, cold and clear, exactly what she was building. She isn't just collecting Vee's stories anymore. She's using them as tools on Vee's own body, in real time, and Vee walks out not knowing what happened to her.

The whole thing is a magic trick with the mechanism showing to me and hidden from Vee. Vee comes in to *tell* — proud, glowing, full of him, wanting to share the good part. And Randi turns the telling into a doing. Watch the moves: "So. Did you deserve it?" — and Vee laughs it too high and says *probably I deserved it*, and the narration says it flat out: "She didn't hear what she'd agreed to. Randi did." That line is the whole chapter. Then "His hand was wet," reconstructing the kitchen from a detail Vee let slip, dropping Vee back into the exact warm-wet weight of it until Vee's voice goes "down into something lower and slower that she didn't entirely recognize." And then Randi's own story — the soft-spoken little man, over his knee, count them out loud, the hand that slips "right between" and by then it's *welcome, very welcome* — landing on that word so that "something turned over low in Vee's belly." Vee has to flee to a bathroom with a loud bolt and sit down and find herself soaked "by nothing but a woman talking in a chair."

Here's what chills me: I can't tell if Randi's ex is real. "Mine. You'd never have picked him out of a room." It might be true. But I've watched this woman lay "the same soft trap a hundred times" — the text says exactly that — and the story arrives at the precise moment, with the precise content, to plant in Vee a want she doesn't have a word for yet. Real or invented, it's a tool. And I notice it hands Vee the thing she keeps almost-asking — "Randi. Have you ever—" — the word Vee doesn't own. Randi gives her the word by giving her the *scene*.

**On the characters.** Vee I ache for harder than ever. Her honesty is the trap's whole leverage — she can't not tell, can't not feel, can't govern the body she's spent her life policing, and now the person drawing it out of her is the person steering her. That reversal the narration flags — "For weeks it had gone the other way… Now it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist… towing the most interesting person she knew toward the door" — is heartbreaking, because Vee reads it as her own new confidence and I read it as the hook set deep enough that the fish swims toward the boat. The bathroom moment nearly broke me: "Her hand knew the way. It moved before she did. She caught it and put it flat against her own knee and held it there. Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." She's still fighting. She still has one border left. And she doesn't know the woman who put her in that bathroom did it on purpose.

Randi — my "charm and menace fused" reading is now just *confirmed with the safety off.* "The small private pleasure of a woman who had laid the same soft trap a hundred times." "She didn't say what she herself could see." She watches Vee's nipples draw up under the thin jersey and says nothing, only "I bet he did," soft. This is the coldest I've felt toward her, and yet I still believe she's genuinely gone on Vee — which makes it worse, not better. She wants Vee *and* she's farming her.

Pace isn't in the room, but he's all over it — the loaned heels, "leave them on," the wet hand. And here's a thing that unsettled me: Randi's spanking story rhymes exactly with the bench I saw in chapter one, the thing Vee has never seen. Randi is describing the bench's logic — over the knee, count out loud, humiliation that "made me buck," the hand that slips — as an anecdote, priming Vee to want the very furniture waiting for her. The two worlds still haven't visibly touched for Vee, but Randi just smuggled the bench into Vee's body as bedtime story.

**Erotic charge.** Working on me, hard, and it's almost entirely clothed and verbal — that's the impressive part. The peak is the stacked landing: "welcome, very welcome" → "something turned over low in Vee's belly with a soft wet heaviness she had no vote in" → the too-fast stand → the loud bolt. It never once goes clinical because it stays inside Vee's shame-and-want war. The bathroom is unbearably intimate precisely because *nothing happens* — she stops her own hand.

**Friction.** Very little. The stats lecture is thesis-loud — "It's in everything that comes after… never quite gets to zero" — and I felt the author's hand on it a touch, the metaphor sitting up and waving (additive outlier = a one-time shock you lift out clean; innovational = the thing that carries forward and never zeroes). But it's *earned* by being a real lecture Vee half-copies while Randi's knee touches hers, and it pays the book's Simpson's-paradox motif, so I forgave it. Barely. It's the one place the machine hums audibly.

**Titles.** "The Practice Room" — a soundproof cell "where everybody used to. You know," where the building "went away. Gone." It reads innocent and turns on me: it's a *practice* room in the other sense — Randi rehearsing Vee's body through a scene, and Vee, in the bathroom, nearly practicing on herself. And it's a small soundproof box you can't be heard in — a stand-in for the bench's private room. Oblique and good; it didn't give anything away going in.

*With a Long Spoon* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — this chapter is the tagline in miniature. Vee freely walks Randi in there. Freely tells. Freely laughs and says *probably I deserved it*. Every single yes is hers, and each one is the point of entry. The long spoon: you sup with the devil holding one, keeping distance — and Vee thinks the distance is friendship. She's the one holding no spoon at all.

**What I want / dread.** I want Vee to keep her one border and I dread that this was the chapter that softened it for good. I keep waiting for the two worlds to touch — I've been guessing the November induction, and I still am (*guess from here*). New dread: that the next time Vee is at Pace's, the bench will be waiting, and Randi's "anecdote" will have already taught her what to want when she sees it. And the smallest, saddest thing — Vee's arms "slow coming down" from the goodbye hug, holding on without meaning to, and putting it down to being tired and full of him. She's starting to want Randi's body too and calling it exhaustion.
