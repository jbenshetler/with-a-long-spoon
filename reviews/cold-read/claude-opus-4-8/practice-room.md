## Reader reaction

This one worked on me physically, which is exactly what it's built to do, and I hated how well it worked. It's a small room and a conversation and nothing more, and by the end Vee is bolted into a bathroom stall soaked through "by nothing but a woman talking in a chair." That's the whole chapter and it's plenty.

The lecture opening is almost too on-the-nose, but the "almost" is doing real work. "Outliers. Two kinds." — the additive one you lift out and the line goes on, the innovational one that "comes in through the part that carries forward… It's in everything that comes after." Vee writes *never all the way gone* because he says it twice, and then Randi's knee arrives against hers and I felt the professor's second plot land on Vee herself. She's the second kind. Whatever's happening to her is the kind that doesn't get lifted out. I usually resent this much authorial telegraphing, but here the mark is copying it into her own notebook without reading it, and that's the horror — the diagnosis passes right through her hand and she never looks at it.

The center of the chapter is Randi's interrogation, and it's the coldest, most controlled thing in the book so far. Watch the sequence: Randi gets the shoes story, then narrows — "A game of slap-and-tickle" — then "did you deserve it?" and Vee, laughing too high, says "probably I deserved it," and only *a moment late* realizes what she agreed to. Then Randi refuses the deflection: "Not the love tap. His." And she *supplies the detail Vee is hiding* — "His hand was wet." She's reconstructing the kitchen scene more precisely than Vee will narrate it, which means she already knew the shape of it. Of course she did. This is the debrief. This is Randi harvesting the exact tell — the swat, the body-response — and confirming for Pace's operation that the spanking-want is real and reachable.

But — and this is what turned my stomach — it doesn't *read* like harvesting to Vee, or even fully to me in the moment. It reads like the older, wiser friend gently helping a girl name a want she's ashamed of. "Oh, baby. Sometimes it's the mouse that roars." Then Randi hands over her *own* confession — the soft-spoken little man, over his knee, counting out loud, skirt up, panties down, "it made me buck," and the hand that slips "right between" and by then "it's welcome. Very welcome." That's the payload. She's not extracting anymore; she's *installing*. She gives Vee permission, a script, a picture, and a promise that the shameful part is the good part. And on the word "welcome" Vee's body arrives.

Here's what I can't stop turning over: I *know* Randi's a co-conspirator, I know this is authored, and the chapter still made me feel the seduction as tenderness. That's the trap the tagline named — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." Vee walks Randi out. Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist. The chapter even flags the reversal explicitly — "For weeks it had gone the other way," Randi towing Vee out of the herd, and now Vee's doing the towing. Vee thinks she's finally the initiator, finally sure. Every escalation comes from her side. And that's precisely the design: the door opens from her side. She freely chose the practice room's memory ("those are where everybody used to…"), she freely told the kitchen story, she freely asked "Have you ever—" and couldn't finish it. Randi barely does anything but sit, go still like a cat, and supply the wet hand.

The bathroom is the chapter's real gut-punch. "Her hand knew the way. It moved before she did." And she stops it — "To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." She thinks *refusing to touch herself* is refusing to say yes. But she's already soaked, already told the whole thing, already asked the question. The yes was given ten minutes ago. Holding her hand flat against her knee is her one act of resistance and it's aimed at the wrong target entirely. That misplacement is the most Vee thing in the book — she reads everyone's gap but her own.

The Cassie beat is quiet and awful. Cassie saves the seat, lifts her backpack away, asks about the problem set, and Vee "answered her without hearing herself, already turned a few degrees the other way, already finding Randi's wrist." Cassie says "see you in the room" *to the side of Vee's head.* The clean love, spoken to a head already turned. Two-night absence still unexplained, and now this. The widening gap made physical in seating.

Erotic charge: this is the highest the book's run for me, and it's almost entirely verbal and involuntary — the nipples drawing up "where Randi could see," the "soft wet heaviness she had no vote in." "No vote in" and "her hand knew the way. It moved before she did" — the eroticism is entirely about the body outvoting the self, which is thematically the whole book, so it doesn't feel like a detour into porn; it feels like the thesis happening in her underwear. It only goes slightly slack in Randi's set-piece confession, which is a hair performed — but that's arguably the point, she's *performing* a confession as a delivery vehicle.

Trust/suspicion: I have no new doubt, only confirmation. I already knew Randi farms Vee; this is her doing it in real time and it's chilling precisely because it's so warm. The goodbye kiss "so worn-in now that Vee barely clocked it," then Randi stepping "back to the distance two people stand at," already half-turned — the professionalism of the exit. And Vee's arms "slow coming down… a held note after the song has already moved on." Vee's the one still holding. Randi's already gone. That asymmetry is the whole relationship in a gesture.

The one line I'd flag as the thumb pressing: "It was Randi who thought of the practice rooms." A small tell that the operator is steering even the improvised, and I noticed the hand. But it's minor.

Titles. "The Practice Room" — innocuous surface: two friends duck into an empty music cell to talk. On the page it recolors fast: it's where "everybody used to… you know," a place made for the illicit, and it's where Randi *rehearses* Vee — runs her through the want, gets her to say the lines, gives her the script for next time. A practice room. She's practicing being what she's being made into. And soundproof — "the building went away. Not muffled. Gone." No one hears. The room that erases the outside is where the yes gets given. The book's title and tagline sit right on top of this chapter: the invitation was polite, the yes was freely given, and I watched it get given.

What I want and dread next: I dread the debrief *behind* this one — Randi telling Pace what she confirmed, the tell being logged. I want to know if Randi felt anything real in that chair (the "widening crack") or if this was clean craft; the chapter kept her opaque on purpose — "she couldn't tell whether she had" meant anything, and neither could I, and I think that's honest. *Guess from here:* the spanking is coming, it'll be staged as Vee's own request, and the counting-out-loud detail Randi planted will surface in it. And Cassie's going to force the absence into the open before long — that seat-side goodbye can't hold.
