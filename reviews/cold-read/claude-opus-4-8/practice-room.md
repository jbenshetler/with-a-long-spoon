## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This is the chapter where the thing I've been watching from the outside finally happens *inside Vee*, and it left me short of breath and a little sick, in the good-bad way the book keeps earning. For a whole run of chapters — the dress, the shoes, the dance, the apology — I've watched Randi mine Vee for the intimate middle of every Pace story, and I've watched Vee hand it over gladly. This time she doesn't just hand it over. She lives it again in the telling, with Randi's eyes on her, in a soundproof closet, and her own body answers a woman talking in a chair. That's the line the chapter crosses, and it crosses it clean.

The swoon and the dread are the same event here, and I can't pull them apart. The kitchen memory Vee's carrying is genuinely hot — "his hand landing where the shirt had ridden up," warm and wet from the dishwater, the swat she twisted the wrong way into — and it was hot in the previous chapter too. So when she relives it and her voice "had gone down into something lower and slower that she didn't entirely recognize," I was right there with her, warm, before I remembered where she was and who was watching. That's the trap working perfectly: the heat is real, the pull is real, and it's being harvested. Randi "very still and very interested, the way a cat goes still" — the book told me the predator out loud and I still felt the charge. That's the merciless part the jacket promised, and it delivered.

What moved for me: my read on Randi hardened and deepened at once. Hardened because this is the most nakedly instrumental I've seen her — the *interrogation* of it, the way she refuses Vee's every deflection ("It wasn't a—" / "His hand was wet," "It was nothing" / "So. Did you deserve it?"), steering Vee to *agree she deserved it* and then brightening when she does. That "So. Did you deserve it?" landed on me the way it landed on Vee: somewhere I wasn't braced for. She's not just collecting intel now; she's installing something. But deepened, too, because of the "mouse that roars" story — Randi handing Vee her own over-the-knee history (the counting, "it made me buck," the hand that slips, "very welcome"). I know from the checkpoint that Randi's true submission with Pace terrified her, that she resolved to hide it behind "the face" forever. So watching her serve it up to Vee as a fond, offhand anecdote — is that a lie, a lure, a cover story that happens to be true, or the closest she'll ever come to being seen, dressed up as seduction? The page left that ajar and I'm leaving it ajar. It's the most interesting she's been.

Vee I ache for, more than ever. The awful, tender thing is that she *knows* something is off and won't look at it. "She wasn't only telling it. She was doing it again. Here. Now." She feels her nipples draw up under the jersey "where Randi could see" and crosses her arms to hide it. She flees to a bathroom with a loud bolt, sits down without meaning to, and her hand "knew the way, it moved before she did," and she catches it and holds it against her knee: "Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." That's the whole book in one gesture — the yes already happening in her body, the no she's still able to say out loud, the shame arriving a beat behind the heat exactly like the mother's voice always does. And then the last beat, the one that got me worst: her arms "slow coming down" from the goodbye hug, holding on a half-second past the song, and she "put it down to" being tired and full of Pace. She's the only one in the room who doesn't know what she wants. I wanted to reach in.

And Cassie — one line, but it cut. "Cassie was gathering her granola bar and her wrong textbook and saying *see you in the room* to the side of Vee's head." The reversal the chapter names explicitly — "For weeks it had gone the other way," Randi towing Vee out of the herd, and now "it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist" — is also Vee turning away from Cassie, her clean sightline, her real friend, mid-sentence. Cassie saying it to the *side of her head* is the small heartbreak under the big one. Vee is being pulled, and the pull costs her the one person who sees her plainly.

Trust vs. suspicion: no ambiguity left about Randi's intent for me as a reader — the text has fully earned it, this is not me being on guard. The interrogation structure, the "cat goes still," the way she "installs" the deserved-it logic and then walks it out as if she meant nothing by it ("as if she'd meant nothing in the world by it. And Vee couldn't tell whether she had") — that last line is the honest one, and I love that the book gives the uncertainty to Vee and, through her, to me. I can't tell either, and the not-telling is the point. What I *don't* know is the endgame, and I'm not going to pretend I do.

The professor's lecture — two kinds of outliers — is the cleanest of the stats-as-symbol beats yet, maybe a touch too clean. "The first kind sits on a single point… you lift it out, and the line goes on like it was never there." "The second kind comes in through the part that carries forward. It's in everything that comes after." Read against Vee reliving the swat, that's obviously about which kind of event the weekend was: not a one-off to be marked and lifted, but something that's now *in* her, carrying forward into everything. I felt the author's thumb a little here — it's the most legible the device has been, and "It's in everything that comes after" is nearly a caption. But it's brief, and the professor's flatness ("a man making sure the road was still there") keeps it from tipping fully into a lecture-to-the-reader, so I'll allow it. Randi's "Mm, which was not agreement and not disagreement, only a thing she set down on the desk between them to pick up later" is the better version of the same move — planted, patient, not underlined.

Erotic charge: high, and it works *because* it's displaced. The heat isn't a sex scene; it's a woman recounting one and her body betraying her in a chair two feet from the person drawing it out. It peaks on "welcome" — "something turned over low in Vee's belly with a soft wet heaviness she had no vote in" — and then again, harder for me, in the bathroom, the hand that knows the way and the flat "she would not say yes to it." That's more erotic than a lot of explicit scenes because the wanting is fighting itself in real time. Nothing went slack. The one place I'd flag isn't slackness but that Randi's over-the-knee monologue is *told* to us fully formed and fluent — it's arguably too well-shaped, too on-the-nose a mirror of the kitchen swat, laid down right when it'll do the most work. But it's in character for her to be that fluent a storyteller, so it reads as her craft rather than the author's.

The title. "The Practice Room" — innocuous on the way in, a place, a detail of setting. On the way out it recolors hard. A practice room is where you *rehearse*, alone, soundproofed, until a thing becomes automatic — and that's exactly what happens: Vee rehearses arousal-on-command in front of Randi, and her body learns the drill. It's also the room "where everybody used to… you know," which she can't say and blushes at, so the sexual charge of the space is named and then made literal. And "practice" ghosts the whole engineered courtship — Vee is being practiced *on*, and being trained. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it detonated after. That's the mechanism I've come to expect from these titles and it's satisfying.

The book's title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the series line *With a Long Spoon* — this is the chapter that made the spoon click hardest for me. "He that sups with the devil must use a long spoon" is the saying under my skin, and here Vee is being fed a story across a very short distance and getting it all over her. The invitation stays "polite": Randi never orders, only asks, only tips her head, only says "Mm" and "did you deserve it," and Vee walks toward every open door herself. The cover tagline I was given — "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — is precisely what the bathroom scene dramatizes: the yes is already in her body, and the only thing between her and it is her own decision not to say it out loud yet. I feel the book steering toward the moment she says it.

What I want and dread next (guesses from here): I dread the discovery — that Vee learns Randi and Pace know each other, that she was chosen, and I fear she learns it at exactly the moment she's most exposed, because that's how this book likes to cut. I half-expect Randi to make the next move a real one — the escalation from goodbye-kisses and this interrogation feels like it's approaching a threshold she'll cross while calling it nothing. And I keep circling Randi's own wound: she just told Vee her truest secret as bait, and I want to know whether telling it moved something in her she didn't plan. Mostly I want to keep reading because I no longer trust anyone to be safe, and I'm not sure I want Vee "rescued" so much as I want to watch her finally look at the thing squarely. The jacket told me not to expect a rescue. I believe it now.
