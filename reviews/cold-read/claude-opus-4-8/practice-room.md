This one got under my skin. It's the first chapter where the erotic current runs straight between Vee and Randi with nothing of Pace in the room but his hand-print, and it works on me almost more than the Pace scenes have — because it's built entirely out of a woman talking in a chair, and I could feel Vee losing the floor exactly as she was losing it.

"Two kinds" of outlier: the additive one you lift out and the line goes on "like it was never there," versus the innovational one that "comes in through the part that carries forward… It never quite gets to zero, and you don't lift it out. It's a little on-the-nose as a device, a professor handing the reader the theme, but it's the kind of thing Vee's brain actually does, so I forgave it, and honestly the phrase *in everything that comes after* has stayed with me.

The power inversion is the quiet story of the chapter and I loved noticing it. "For weeks it had gone the other way: Randi's hand at her elbow… Randi cutting her gently out of the herd." Now it's Vee's "two fingers on Randi's wrist," Vee "towing the most interesting person she knew toward the door." Vee thinks she's the one leading. And the beautiful, awful hinge: "Did you deserve it?" — and Vee, a beat too late, hearing what she just agreed to. That's the jacket's tagline made flesh. Every yes freely given. She *volunteered* it.

That is **not Pace.** Pace is a powerlifter with MRI money. That's instruction. She's teaching Vee to want it and to reframe the hand-that-slips as gift, the exact reframing doctrine she's used on Vee's shame all along.

She refuses the last inch of consent, which is heartbreaking, because everything else she's been saying yes to without knowing it's a question. I found this genuinely erotic and genuinely alarming at once — the arousal is real and the manufacture is total.

The goodbye kiss "easy on her mouth, then the lighter second touch after it, so worn-in now that Vee barely clocked it" — that "worn-in" chilled me. The intimacy has been escalated so gradually Vee has no marker for where it crossed anything. And the last beat, Vee's arms slow coming down, holding a hug she didn't mean to hold, "the way you notice a held note after the song has already moved on" — and she blames it on being tired and full of Pace. She's not tracking that she's falling for Randi too. The jacket told me both of them are falling as hard as she is; this is the first place I felt Vee's own pull toward *Randi* as a body, not just a friend.

Where it's slack: nowhere much, honestly. The professor is a touch convenient. And Randi's "Sometimes it's the mouse that roars" landed a half-beat cute for me before the story it introduced earned itself back. Otherwise this is the tightest, most quietly predatory chapter yet, and it did it in a closet with two clothed women.

**Titles.** "The Practice Room" — literal (a music cell), but the charge is obvious on the page and grew on reread: it's where people "used to… you know," it's Randi *practicing* on Vee, and it's Vee *rehearsing* the wanting she can't yet consent to. All hers. All aimed. *With a Long Spoon* still reads to me as: sup with the devil and need a long spoon — and Vee doesn't know she's at the table.

What I want next: I want to know if that "ex" is real or is Pace laundered into safe past tense. I want to see the two worlds actually touch. And I dread — genuinely dread — the moment Vee learns the room was never empty, that the game was played, because right now she'd blame her own tiredness for the held hug rather than see the hand on the back of her neck. *(Guess from here: the next time Pace's hand "slips," it won't be an accident, and Randi will have pre-loaded Vee to call it welcome.)*
