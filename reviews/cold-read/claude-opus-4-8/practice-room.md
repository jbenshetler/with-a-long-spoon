## Reader reaction

This one got me hot and it also made my skin crawl, at the same time, in the same breath — which I think is exactly what it wanted, and I'm a little unsettled by how well it worked.

The lecture opening is the best sleight-of-hand the book's pulled on me yet. The professor's two kinds of outliers — the one that "sits on a single point," you lift it out and the line goes on like it was never there, versus the one that "comes in through the part that carries forward," that's "in the next value, and the one after that, smaller each time," but "never quite gets to zero" — I copied that down the way Vee did, half-reading it, and then the chapter spent the rest of its length *being* the second kind. The spanking wasn't a one-time shock she can lift out. It's in everything after. It came into the part that carries forward, and here it is carrying forward, two days later, in a piano closet. *Never all the way gone.* The book put its own thesis on the board in a flat cereal-box voice and let me think it was homework. I love that and I distrust it — it's the most naked the machinery's been, and it still landed because it landed *through* Vee not noticing.

And Randi. God, Randi. My trust in her was already at the floor and this chapter drove it through the slab. Watch what she actually does here. She doesn't ask what happened. She *builds* it. "His hand was wet. You said he was doing dishes. So his hand was wet." She reconstructs the exact sensory fact and hands it back to Vee so Vee has to re-enter it — the "recognized method" from last chapter, soaking, let the water do the work, except now Randi's the water. Then "Did you deserve it?" — and the narration says the quiet part *out loud to me* and not to Vee: "She didn't hear what she'd agreed to. Randi did." And "the small private pleasure of a woman who had laid the same soft trap a hundred times." That's the first time the text has this baldly told me Randi is running a trap, in those words, mid-scene. The tagline's word — *trap* — is right there on the page. Every yes freely given: Vee *agreed* she deserved it, laughing, too high, and the agreement is the hook.

Then the mouse-that-roars story. Here's where I split down the middle. On the body: it worked on me, it's a genuinely good dirty story, the escalation of "over his knee, skirt up, panties down," "I had to count," "always, toward the end, his hand would slip," landing on *welcome* — that's built like a proper piece of erotica and Vee's belly turning over on the word is earned. But on the mind: is it even true? Randi produces this at the precise moment Vee is drowning and can't finish a sentence, and it does exactly one job — it tells Vee *this is normal, this is wanted, this is what women like us feel* and it converts Vee's shame into permission and arousal in real time. It's the doctrine again — reframe the shame as agency, "sure, not sorry" — except deployed as pure accelerant. I can't tell if Randi's late-night spanker ever existed or if she invented him on the spot to match Vee's exact wound. And that not-knowing is the scariest thing in the chapter. The narration keeps refusing to tell me what Randi sees: "She didn't say what she herself could see." "She never did settle whether Randi had seen." The withholding is the point.

The bathroom. Oof. This is the chapter's real peak for me and it's not even a sex scene — it's the *refusal* of one. "Her hand knew the way. It moved before she did. She caught it and put it flat against her own knee and held it there. Not here. Not over a story. To do that would be to say yes to it, and she would not say yes to it." She's soaked through "made by nothing but a woman talking in a chair," and she will not give the last inch of consent, and she doesn't know that not-consenting-to-the-orgasm while being aroused to the edge by Randi *is itself* the deeper yes she's already given. That's the trap the tagline promised — she's guarding a door that's already open behind her.

The reversal I clocked hard and the text made sure I did: "Now it was Vee's two fingers on Randi's wrist… towing the most interesting person she knew toward the door." For weeks it was Randi cutting her out of the herd; now Vee does the cutting, "and she didn't notice she'd done it." The apparatus has become the appetite. Vee thinks she's chasing. And "Walk me out?" *To Randi. Only to Randi.* — poor Cassie saying "see you Thursday" to the side of Vee's head, gathering her wrong textbook. Cassie the conscience, skipped again, literally spoken-past. That small cruelty of inattention got me more than anything Randi did.

Where's the friction, the thumb on the scale? Honestly, less than I feared given how mechanical the outlier metaphor is — the professor's speech is *just* on the right side of too-neat because Vee mishears it and because it's genuinely how time-series outliers work, so it plays as a real lecture. The one spot I felt the hand a little was "the small private pleasure of a woman who had laid the same soft trap a hundred times" — that's the narrator confirming Randi's a predator right when I could've been left to suspect it myself. But I'll take it, because the rest of the chapter is so committed to *not* telling me what Randi sees that this one tell reads as a deliberate crack of the curtain, not a slip.

Vee: still my whole heart, and now with a fresh ache, because the wanting she has no name for is being named *for* her, by the wrong person, in the wrong room, as a gift she'll be grateful for. She smelled entirely of him last chapter "and had not minded it"; now she can't finish a single sentence — "Have you ever—", "I just need", "See you soon" — the ellipses are her whole state. She's being talked past her own vocabulary.

Randi's ending move — the goodbye kiss, "the long hug, the kiss easy on her mouth, then the lighter second touch after it, so worn-in now that Vee barely clocked it," and Vee's arms slow coming down, holding on without meaning to, "a held note after the song has already moved on" — that's the chapter telling me the *other* falling is happening too, the one the jacket promised: "Both are falling as hard as she is." Vee put it down to not sleeping. I didn't.

**Titles.** "The Practice Room" — on the surface, a music-building closet, soundproof, where freshmen used to *you know*. Vee even names it and blushes: "People used them to. You know." So the innocent surface is already the charged surface. But read after: it's a *practice* room. Randi is rehearsing Vee — running her through the spanking again, in memory, out loud, so the body re-performs it on cue. And Vee "does it again. Here. Now." It's the room where she practices being the thing Randi's making her. The title gave nothing away going in and turned over completely coming out — my favorite kind so far. *With a Long Spoon* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this is the most literal the tagline has been. The word *trap* is on the page in the narrator's own voice, and the whole scene is a demonstration of consent-as-mechanism: Vee agrees she deserved it, agrees the wanting is normal, guards only the final twitch of her own hand — and every one of those is a yes that tightens the thing. Long spoon: she's supping with the devil across a piano-closet chair and she brought no spoon at all; she brought two fingers to Randi's wrist and towed herself in.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to know if Randi's spanker was real — a *guess from here*: he wasn't, or he's dressed-up, invented to fit. I dread the moment Vee learns Randi and "Peter" are the same secret, because after this chapter that reveal isn't just betrayal, it's retroactive violation of every intimate thing she's confessed in a chair. And I'm newly braced for Vee's own falling *for Randi* to surface — those slow arms are the tell. I want the next chapter badly.
