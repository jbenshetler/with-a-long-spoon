# Reader reaction: "Tannin"

This is a short chapter and it does two things at once — it runs hot and it turns my stomach, and it does both on purpose, and I felt both. The wine kiss is the most erotic thing in the book so far for me. She drinks and comes down and kisses him with it still in her mouth, "the tannin catching at the inside of his lips, the taste of it drawn across his tongue by hers," and he doesn't pour himself a glass because "everything he was going to have of it he was going to have like this." That last line — "He had not had a drop of it. He could taste nothing else." — landed clean. It's the whole book's substitution logic made into a single image: he takes the wine only through her mouth, the way the wanting only ever reaches anyone secondhand. I melted at the craft of it even while the content unsettled me.

But what she's demanding is a *report on Vee*, and that's where the sweetness curdles. "Tell me." "Tell." Hot against his ear, her weight on him, and what she wants fed to her is the froyo date — a girl who "built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop." I read "The Long Way" an hour ago as tender; Vee saying *this is the nicest a person has been to me in a really long time and it's froyo* is one of the most defenseless lines in the book. And now it's being eaten. Randi gets off on hearing that Vee overfilled her cup with no mother-voice to stop her — the exact thing that cracked Randi open on the bench, now being watched for in someone else. That parallel did something cold to me.

My feeling about Randi has moved, and hard. Through the earlier chapters I ached for her — the rationed apple, the face she goes back behind, the vow "not ever." This chapter keeps the ache but adds a predator's outline over it. She engineered the collision, the class, the coffee, and now she's running Pace at Vee and feeding on the returns. And yet the page won't let me simply condemn her: her want is *held* — when Pace says "She's easy to listen to," her thighs "set," the smile arrives "a fraction late." She's the one who begged, and she's poured all that ungovernable want into a project she can own from the outside instead of a feeling she has to sit inside. That's legible and sad and awful at once. I don't know yet whether she wants Vee, or wants to *be* the froyo-girl being chosen, or wants to hand herself away safely by handing over a stand-in. The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar.

Pace: he genuinely seems to enjoy Vee (he protected her froyo-walk confession — "It was hers"), and he reads Randi's held want with real tenderness. But the tenderness is now serving a hunt, and I've stopped finding his patient attentiveness only lovely. The same courtly attention that felt like care in the coffee shop reads, here, like a very good lure being aimed.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** fully suspicious now, and the text earned every bit. Vee is prey. The froyo date I felt as real warmth is inside a trap being sprung in slow motion, and I have the dramatic irony the whole time — I know what Randi asked on the bench, I know what she pointed at on the quad, I know Vee doesn't.

**Erotic charge:** peak. Short as it is, the charge dominates — the "tell," the straddle, his hands closed over her under the wire, the transferred wine. It's the hottest the book has run on me.

**The title.** "Tannin" is the bitterness under the wine — the astringent catch under sweetness, and also the thing that gives wine structure and lets it keep. That's exactly what this chapter tastes of: something dry and dark under the froyo warmth, under the "love you, girl," under the "may I call you." It flavors rather than spoils; a beautiful title.

**The cover board.** "WITH A LONG SPOON · A POLITE INVITATION." *He who sups with the devil should have a long spoon.* That proverb reframes the whole run for me — the politeness *is* the invitation to the devil's table, and someone here is the devil (Pace's "may I," Randi's engineered warmth, or the two of them together as a set) and Vee is the guest who brought no spoon at all. "A Polite Invitation" as Book One suggests the invitation is the whole first movement — and it's nearly complete.

**What I want / dread:** I dread for Vee, plainly. I want to keep reading and I don't want what I think is coming. *Guess from here:* the bench question — "Do you think she'll like the bench?" — is the destination, and Vee's naïveté is the fuel.
