## Reader reaction

**Felt read.**

Short and dense, this one, and it worked on me before I'd decided whether I wanted it to. Randi comes in with the deadbolt already turning, no hello, mouth on his before he's up — and then that flat imperative, *Tell me.* The whole chapter is her feeding on the froyo date. That's the engine, and it's a strange, hot, uneasy engine to be strapped to.

The erotic charge is real and it runs high — her straddling him on the couch the way she straddled the kitchen chair, drinking the wine and kissing it into his mouth, the tannin catching his lip, him taking not a drop himself so that everything he gets of it comes through her. *"Tell."* hot against his ear. It's genuinely charged. But what unsettled me is what the charge is *for* — she's not turned on despite the subject of Vee, she's turned on *by* it, and the two things are fused so completely I couldn't pull them apart. That's the swoon and the dread arriving as one sensation, which is disorienting in a way I think the page earned.

My feeling about Randi has moved. On the quad her want for Vee read as helpless, almost sympathetic — the thing that broke her composure. Here it's grown, and it's grown into something she's *holding level*, and I keep hearing, under it, that decision in the bathroom mirror — the face going back on, *not ever.* So when she says "I already do" — I already like her — bright and easy and then downs the whole glass, I don't trust the brightness. Something is being routed through this Vee project that isn't just generous appetite. I can't name it, and I don't want to name it more definitely than the page does. But I clocked it. When Pace notes the want is "too big for her face, and keeping the face over it anyway," and finds it *moving* — I found it ominous instead.

Because I've read Vee's chapters. I sat inside that glowing, undefended girl at the high table, the borrowed poise, the froyo cone she let get away from her because for once no one told her to stop. And here she's a *report*. "She built a cup like nobody had ever told her when to stop" — Randi's whole body tightens at that line, and I felt the floor tilt, because I know what that detail *is* to Vee and I know it's being consumed here as an appetizer. That's where my suspicion sits now, and it's the text that put it there, not my guarding.

Pace: my read of him is complicating in the direction of unease, but with one genuinely disarming beat — he withholds Vee's confession, the true thing she asked him to pretend she hadn't. *It was hers.* He protects her privacy even here. That's the same closed-door ethic from the Daphne memory, and it's the thing that keeps me from writing him off as simply a predator. And yet he's the one who pointed. He's narrating a courtship of a naive girl to the girlfriend who wants to devour her, reading both women's bodies as their truest yes. The tenderness and the machinery are the same gesture, and I can't get them apart, and I don't think I'm supposed to.

**The title.** *Tannin* — the bitterness under the dark pleasure, the astringency that "catches at the inside of his lips." Wine you let *open.* And the fact that he tastes it only secondhand, through her mouth — he's taking the date, and taking Vee, at one remove, filtered through Randi. That image did a lot. The book-cover line, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*: "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" — that's what surfaced, and after this chapter I can't unsee it, though I couldn't tell you yet who's the devil and who brought the spoon. *A polite invitation* — Vee's *may I call you*, the froyo, the warmth — all of it an invitation into something. The tagline reads darker every chapter.

**Friction.** Almost none — it's lean and controlled. If anything the very compression means the dread does the work and the scene doesn't overstay. I didn't disbelieve a beat of it.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to be back in Vee's head and I'm afraid to be, because she's so happy and so unarmored. *Guess from here:* the "gift" is going to become Vee, and Randi's investment in it is not the sport she's performing — there's something she's doing to herself through Vee that I can feel and can't yet see. I dread that this stays sweet on Vee's side exactly as long as it takes to close.
