## Reader reaction

Oh, this one got me — quietly, and then not quietly at all.

The chapter is almost entirely a table: Vee and Randi at the loft booth again, the same booth, the same shut-the-place-down rhythm as "across." On the surface it's Vee finally getting to spend the weekend she's been hoarding — the truck, the wrist, "like the orange," the wet kiss and her Victorian recoil. But what the chapter is *actually* doing runs underneath every laugh line, and I felt it the way you feel a draft you can't find the source of.

**Vee.** God, I love her and I'm scared for her in equal measure now. The whole chapter is her performing a story — she's *polished* it, she says so, carried it "like a thing you can't wait to give away," built the punchline in the shower. And watching her repeatedly fold the true thing down to "a size she could sit behind" — "It was a lot," "you can fill in the—" — and Randi refusing every single time to let her round it off, that's the engine of the scene and it's merciless. What lands hardest is that Vee keeps getting dropped *back into her own body* by her own telling. "Her mouth went dry, actually dry, and the wine was no help for it." She clenches "right there in the booth" twice. She is arousing herself telling the story to Randi, and — crucially — this time she doesn't get to escape to a bathroom. Tuesday the closing bell saved her. This time there's no relief, just the sentence walking her home.

**The wet-kiss thing.** This is the whole spine and I clocked exactly what the previous chapter planted: the small minor-key stone Vee couldn't get to lie flat. Here she brings it to Randi as *comedy* — "Gross. Wrong. Somebody had to say it" — teed up as a thirty-second bit. And Randi disarms it with four words, flat and mild: "I don't." Then "It was powerful. It turned me on." And then the real scalpel — "How does he act?" — which pulls out of Vee the thing she hasn't let herself hold: *"he's enthusiastic,"* the word "out before any part of her had vetted it." Randi doesn't lecture. She says "Mm," eats the bread, and lets it detonate on a delay. That restraint is *devastating* craft — she doesn't tell Vee anything; she just declines to agree with the mother's-voice verdict, and leaves the door she opened swinging. The chapter ends with Vee walking home with *both* sentences pacing her: her own "he's enthusiastic," and under it, "level, mild, taking up almost no room at all: *I don't.*"

I don't think I'm supposed to know yet what Vee is going to do with that. I don't either. But the page has absolutely earned the unease — this isn't me hunting foreshadowing, it's the text handing Vee a loose thread and Randi giving it one gentle tug.

**Randi.** She's the one I watch hardest and this chapter tightened my chest. There are two moments where the performance-hostess drops and something real and unbraced shows: when Vee says "he opened me like the orange," Randi "held still... and let the quiet sit" — no bawdy handback, nothing. And the fingernail on the wine-glass rim, "singing almost too low to hear," when Vee describes Pace parking his mouth at her wrist. That's the tell I've learned to read on Randi — the glass, the rim, the quarter-turn. She's being turned on *and* stung, both, listening to Vee narrate being loved like that. And knowing what I know — that Randi is in love with Vee both ways and helpless about it — every "don't you dare fade to firelight" reads as a woman who *needs* the whole thing, not for gossip, for herself. The reconnaissance and the wanting are the same act now, and I can't tell anymore where one stops. That's exactly what unsettles me and exactly what makes it hot.

**The neck kiss.** Repeat from "across," and it's escalating. Last time Vee kissed the spot on a memory and Randi flinched-and-laughed it off, "don't weaponize that." This time Randi *tips her head and lays the whole hollow open* — "warm straight out of the cold" — and Vee kisses it again, and "under her mouth Randi's whole body did a small quick thing, a catch and then a rise, and the rise went on a beat too long." "Ticklish," Randi says, taking the spot back. That "a beat too long" is the page telling me, plainly, that this is not ticklishness — and that Vee, "pleased with herself," still isn't looking at what her own mouth keeps doing. The one-sided-as-Vee-knows-it is fraying at exactly this seam.

**Swoon / pull.** The retold bedroom material is genuinely hot even at one remove — "he opened me like the orange" is a beautiful line and it worked on me. But the real charge isn't the remembered sex, it's the *table*: two women getting each other worked up in a public loft under cover of "girl talk," one of them not able to say the true words and the other one refusing to accept the fake ones. That's where the heat actually lives this chapter, and it's the kind I most like — interior, deniable, cresting under a tablecloth.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No new *reason* to doubt anyone's feelings — the tenderness reads real on every side, as always. My unease is structural: Randi's "I don't" is the first crowbar I've seen her set, however softly, under Vee's inherited no. She could be simply, honestly answering. She could be beginning something. The page keeps it ajar and I'll keep it ajar — but I noticed she picked the one loose brick and pressed it.

**The lecture.** Marsh's grooming lecture is doing overt thematic work and I didn't mind it — the baboon spending a fifth of its life with its hands in another's fur, "the fleas were never the point," "what do you spend it on now" — landed against two women who meet twice a week to put their hands in each other's stories. It's a touch on-the-nose ("something in you knew"), but it earned its place; and the wintergreen Lifesaver passed wrist-to-palm, the comb absorbed into a middle row that "couldn't bear to part with it" — those are grooming, plainly. The chapter is *about* the fifth of the day these two spend on each other.

**Friction.** Very little. The one line I felt the author's thumb on was "she'd said the case it closed didn't seem to be staying closed. The folder wouldn't sit flat in the drawer" — the drawer/folder image gets worked a hair too hard. And Marsh's "somebody in this room, by spring break, is going to tell me" is the second lecture built on the identical rhetorical scaffold as Tuesday's Mauss one; the repetition is deliberate but I felt the mold. Small stuff. Mostly this ran clean and close.

**Titles.** "A Clean Plate" — I read it three ways and all three bite. Literal: Vee swims a mile, eats nothing, and cleans the special "without noticing she was doing it," ravenous — the hunger motif, straight through. Then Randi's mushroom joke — "did you swallow all of it and wipe your mouth?" — makes "clean plate" filthy and turns the wet-kiss squeamishness into the exact opposite of clean-plate: the one thing Vee *won't* swallow, the taste she pushes away. So the title quietly indicts her: she clears every plate he sets down but gags at the last taste — and it's *her own*. And there's a third, colder read I can't shake now: the "clean plate club," the child made to finish everything — the mother's voice, the thing installed before she could argue. The title recolors the whole recoil as: she was taught which things you finish and which you call gross. I think it gave a little away, honestly — it points at the swallow before the chapter does, but I only felt the aim on the way out.

**The volume/series titles.** My packet shows the cover board: **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION.** "He that sups with the devil should have a long spoon" — that proverb has been sitting over this whole book like a warning about who's really at the table, and after tonight, with Randi setting down her one soft crowbar, it feels pointed straight at Vee, who thinks she's supping with her best friend. "A Warm Reception" is doing the thing where warmth is the *method* — Pace's too-warm house, this pine roadhouse, the loft, the reception these two give each other. Warmth as the thing that lowers your guard. No tagline in my packet.

**What I want / dread next.** I want Thursday-after-next, honestly — I want to see whether Vee brings the wet-kiss thing back up, or whether "I don't" keeps pacing her until she has to look at it. *Guess from here, marked as a guess:* the chapter is aiming Vee at the question "why does he like it, and why don't I" — and Randi has just quietly modeled the other answer. I dread and half-want the moment Vee lets herself taste something she's been told is wrong, and I suspect Randi's neck is where the page is walking her. I also can't stop hearing Pace's "they share an interest... you" from last chapter next to Randi's rim-singing tonight — two people separately, achingly circling the same girl, and the girl narrating it to one of them across a plate she keeps cleaning.
