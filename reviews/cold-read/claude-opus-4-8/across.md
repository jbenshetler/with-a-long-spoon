# Cold read (grounded) — across

*scene: scenes/across.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · memory: ck-ch050 + raw ch051..ch053 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

The felt read first.

This one just made me happy, and I'm going to let it. After the long dark tunnel of December and the deliciously fraught morning of the forbidden kiss — that minor-key thing still humming under Vee's affection — this is the book handing me a plain, sunlit exhale, and I took it gratefully. A locked dining hall, a bereft Kayla mourning her waffles "like a woman at a deathbed," and Vee doing the thing I love her most for: *feeling the morning organize itself* and reaching for her wallet and her Christmas money and saying "Get in my car." That small motion — the girl who once "couldn't eat that," who cropped her own leg out of photos, now the one who rescues the morning, orders mimosas, and *doesn't do any arithmetic at all* — landed on me warmly and quietly. It's the whole arc of her, cashed in a diner booth without a word of fanfare. She's paying, she's picking the place, she's spending her too-much on purpose, and it's spent on *feeding her friends*, which is the sweetest possible register for it.

Kayla is a joy on the page — "three sentences downstream and going," narrating underwear to a woman on her way to commit a crime — and I loved that she's the engine that lets Vee tell the coat story sideways, all comedy, "I flashed my boyfriend on his front porch," and never once has to touch the drive out or what happened after the door. That's the same trick as with Cassie last chapter (the shrunk-sweater cover) and I clocked it again: Vee tells the *shape* of the truth, the crowd-pleaser version, and keeps the real weight for no one. It's not a lie exactly — "we're better than we were" sits on the table "looking exactly true" — but I notice how practiced she's getting at handing people the size of a thing they can hold. It's charming and it's a tiny bit lonely, and the book knows it's both.

Meg is the one who deepened for me here. The maple-bacon-and-all-the-Matrix-movies speech is doing more than being funny. "Sometimes you have to make it right in their language" — she reads Vee's reconciliation as an apology delivered in Pace's tongue, and she's not wrong, but I felt the small wrongness of the frame too: Meg thinks Vee made it right in *Pace's* language, when really Vee showed up wordless because that's the only language he *permits*, the one where "words are cheap, believe what I do." Meg's Danny brings a heating pad and takes no bait and gets his dozen donuts; it's a whole picture of ordinary reciprocal love held up next to Vee's, and the contrast is quiet but it's there — the "fine" boyfriends of the world, the ones Vee floated above in the last chapter feeling sorry for. I didn't feel superior for Vee this time, though. I felt the faint draft of it.

Swoon and pull: this chapter's warmth isn't erotic at all and I don't want it to be — it's the warmth of *belonging*, worlds about to touch. The real charge is the dinner. Pace wanting to meet her people, "all of them," a table with no ends so nobody gets stuck at one — that landed on me as the most romantic thing in the recent stretch, more than any doorstep sex, because it's him reaching *outward*, toward the public name Vee has been starving for. A man who wouldn't say four words is now assembling her whole floor at one table on purpose. And Vee saying "He's handling it," and *hearing how much she liked saying it* — that's belonging spoken aloud. My chest went warm.

And yet. Here is where the page earned my unease, gently, without my having to reach for it. The whole engine of the dinner started, last chapter, with *"I want to meet your people"* — and it arrived to Vee "as *Randi*." Randi is the one she tells. Randi's name is on the gift. But then Pace, one-handed, without turning around, names Cassie, Meg, Kayla, Theo — everyone *but* Randi — and here in "Across" Vee announces the roster to her friends and Randi is again not in the sentence: "You two, Cassie, Theo maybe." Randi, whose mad gorgeous idea the coat was, who's getting every detail Tuesday — Randi is not on the guest list Vee just recited. Maybe it's nothing; maybe Randi's a given, the one Vee "would tell everything." But I felt the omission with a small cold finger, because I know what Vee doesn't: Pace and Randi already know each other, intimately, and a dinner where Vee's two worlds finally "touch" is a dinner where those two would have to sit across a table and *perform* not knowing. The reader's dread lives exactly in the gap between how lovely this dinner sounds to Vee and how loaded it is under the cloth. I'm not sure the book is walking toward that collision yet — it may be — but the title of the chapter is *Across*, and I can't help reading it as *across a table*, worlds set across from each other, and thinking about who, specifically, would be sitting across from whom.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no *new* suspicion of anyone here — nobody did anything off. My unease is entirely structural, the reader's advance knowledge pressing on an innocent scene, and the page invited it only through that Randi-shaped absence in the roster. Everything else is clean, kind, funny, true.

The titles. *Across* — on the surface, nothing; a diner word, a table word. Read against the chapter it's the sunny south porch, the light that "sat down with them," the three flutes coming together "over the middle of the table." But it points forward, and it points at the dinner: everyone *across* from everyone, worlds laid across each other. If it recolors on reread I suspect it'll be about who was sitting across from whom and pretending. As for the book — *A Polite Invitation*, *With a Long Spoon* — that spoon proverb ("sup with the devil, use a long spoon") keeps its low hum under a chapter this warm; a dinner is being planned, a table is being set, and the series title will not let me forget that somebody at it is supping with something. This morning, though, the invitation was Vee's, and it was genuinely polite and genuinely warm, and I let it be that.

What I want next: I want the dinner, and I'm bracing for it. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the Wednesday dinner is where the Pace/Randi thing risks its first daylight in front of Vee — the two of them in one room having to be strangers — and either it goes off like clockwork (which would chill me) or something slips. I also want to know whether Randi *comes* at all, because her not being on the list is the one thread I'd pull.
