# Cold read — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

Well — there it is. The exact thing I've been dreading since the door slammed: Vee takes her wrecked heart straight up the stairs to the one person who cannot be trusted with it. And I got to watch the whole steering happen with my eyes open, holding a piece of knowledge Vee doesn't have. That's what made this chapter awful in the good way. It's not that Randi says a single false thing — it's that I've read chapter 44. I saw the real Gstaad. So when she "tells the real one," I'm sitting there watching her hand Vee a story I *know* is hollow at the center, and Vee drinks it as the fairytale she'll never have. "The kind you buy to tell your daughter about someday." God.

The chilling thing is how *good* Randi is at this, and how much of it is genuinely kind. That's the knife. She does the wrecked girl first, before the half-unpacked room, before her own bad break — "the wrecked girl in her doorway had come first," and it's true, and it lands on me the way it lands on Vee. The long hug, "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that" — I believe Randi misses her. I believe Randi is falling. So this isn't a con woman working a mark. It's worse: it's a woman who loves the girl steering her back into the bed the woman also shares, because she can't have the thing straight and won't give up the game. Every reframe Randi offers about Pace is *accurate* — the phone in the other room, the heated house, "a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." I actually think she's reading Pace correctly. And she's using true reads to drive Vee back to a door that erased her, without ever once telling her the one fact that would change everything.

The **"ambushed / round two"** reframe is genuinely brilliant and I hated how much it worked on me too. Vee "hadn't once thought of it as his" door, his morning — and Randi turns her toward Pace's interior so deftly that I nodded along before I remembered whose side of the board Randi is playing. She's not wrong that Vee blindsided him. But look at what she does with being right: she converts Vee's legitimate grievance into Vee's fault, softly, lovingly, and reroutes all the momentum toward *appeasement*.

And then the coat. Oh, the coat. The whole second half is a seduction of Vee by Randi — "You've got that good long coat," and then she *stops* and makes Vee find the rest herself so it'll feel like Vee's own idea. "And nothing under it." "Then she heard what she'd said." The shame-then-wanting move I've watched Randi run before, the exact architecture from the spanking story — "That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." "Who's going to see you do it? One man." The mother-voice comes up, the slut-word, and Randi walks her past it a word at a time. It's masterful and it made my skin crawl, because the plan is: go stand at the door that erased you this morning, naked under a coat, wordless, offering everything, no defenses. If Pace opens that door still in stranger-mode, Vee will be more exposed than she has ever been in this book. The dread is enormous.

**And the heels.** "Randi always knows the shoes." Ch.23, 26, 28, 34 — every time Randi's doing the deepest steering, out come the shoes, the one already-decided thing Vee's grateful to be handed. Black heels pressed into her hands "past looking at anything." That's the tell that this is a *production*, dressed as comfort.

The John retelling is the center of the chapter for me because I'm the only one in the room who knows. Randi stops at the bedroom — "and then it got very late, and we went up," and looks at her hands. Vee reads it as discretion. I read it as the one true thing leaking through: Randi has nothing to say about that room because there was nothing there. "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." That line is doing double work — it's the deflection *and* it's the truest confession she makes all chapter, and Vee laughs, and never sees it. The whole exchange is unbearable dramatic irony and I loved it.

**Where I moved on people.** Randi: my distrust hardened into something close to dread-plus-grief, but — annoyingly, honestly — my *liking* of her went up too, which is the trap working on me directly. She is warm, funny, present, right about Pace, tender with a genuinely broken friend. That's exactly why it's poison. Vee: my whole heart, and now real fear for her body and her dignity, because she's about to walk into the coldest possible outcome dressed as an offering. Pace: offstage, but Randi's reads actually *raised* my hope he's not done with Vee — "a man half-starved for the one he's got." I want to believe that. I'm afraid to. Sheri: confirmed again as pure invention on Vee's part, "you gave it a face," and I already know Sheri is not blonde-model-anything.

**Erotic charge.** Low-and-slow but real in the coat sequence — it's not a sex scene, it's the *building* of one, the shame-to-wanting turn: "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it. She was going to do it." That worked on me as anticipation and unease braided together, which is more interesting than heat. Peaks at "nothing under it." Otherwise the chapter is emotional, not carnal.

**Friction.** Very little thumb on the scale — the restraint at "we went up" is exactly right, no author explaining what I already know. If anything I braced for over-explanation of the irony and didn't get it, which I respect. The one spot I felt the machinery is "like a thing being decided for her and by her at once" — that's the chapter naming its own trick a hair too plainly. But it's a small hair.

**Titles.** "My Friend Randi" — from Vee's mouth that's affection; from where I sit it's the whole cruelty in three words. The *friend* is the trap. The chapter is Vee running to her best friend for safety and being steered by her lover's co-conspirator, and the title just says, flatly, in Vee's voice, the thing she believes. It recolors the second I finish: the friendship is real *and* it's the instrument. And it rhymes hard with the book title now — *With a Long Spoon*, sup with the devil. This chapter is the long spoon: Randi feeds Vee exactly enough (comfort, a plan, the shoes, permission), tender the whole time, and the tagline "Every yes was freely given, that was the trap" is *literally the coat scene* — Randi makes Vee say it herself, find it herself, so the yes is unimpeachably Vee's own. I've never seen the tagline operate this nakedly on the page.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the door. I dread the door. Guess from here: the next chapter is Vee showing up bare under the coat, tonight, and the whole book is holding its breath on whether Pace opens it as Peter or as the stranger. If he stays behind the wall, this is the cruelest scene in the book. If he breaks — I still won't know *why* he did the stranger-act, and I need that. Second dread, quieter: that Randi engineered a reunion whose real function is to keep the game running, so *she* keeps her brunches and her access, and Vee will be more bound than ever, thanking her for it.
