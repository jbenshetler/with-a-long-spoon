**Felt read**

God, this chapter is a slow-motion horror show dressed as a comfort scene, and I mean that as praise. Everything on the surface is tenderness — a wrecked girl off a red-eye, a friend who drops her Gstaad sweaters on the floor without a glance because "the wrecked girl in her doorway came first" — and every warm beat of it made my skin crawl *because I know what Randi is*. Vee walks across campus to the one person who will hold her, and that person is the co-architect of the whole thing, quietly steering her right back onto the hook. I spent the chapter with a stone in my stomach.

What guts me is how *good* Randi's counsel actually is. That's the trap. "The fear's a liar, and you haven't slept." "Start over, but slow. That last night." Making Vee re-see the warmed house, the poured wine, the folded arms from *his* side — "You backed him into a corner in his own house. Name it or lose me — the one thing he can't do." It's real emotional intelligence, and it's *accurate*, because Randi is the one person alive who knows exactly what's in Pace and exactly why he went behind his face. She's not lying to comfort Vee. She's using the truth as a tool. That's worse than lying. And the cruelest stroke: dismantling the imagined rival — "this girl, Sheri, blonde, tan, built like a model" — which I know is a phantom Vee built, and Randi knows there's a real brunette, *herself*, standing in the room. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." I actually flinched.

The seduction-coaching is where the chapter runs hottest and most disturbing at once. The coat. "And nothing under it." Vee's mother's voice flooding up — "such a slutty thing to do" — the burn to the ears, and then Randi walking her off the ledge word by word: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." And the line the book has been building toward all along — "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's Vee's whole erotic architecture in one clause, and it *worked* on me; the heat here is genuine, the shame-as-arousal is the most alive thing in the chapter. But I can't take it clean, because the woman lighting that fuse is sending Vee, bare under a coat, to the man they *share*. There's an obscene intimacy in it — Randi dressing Vee for Pace's bed. The black heels pressed into her hands. "Randi always knew the shoes." I wanted to shout at the page.

Then the John story, and this is where I *ache*. Randi telling the fairy tale — naming children on the first lift, the pale metallic-blue dress "the kind you tell your daughter about" — and Vee taking each detail "sideways," because it's the exact easy forward motion she can't have, "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it." That's devastating dramatic irony pointed *at Randi's own life*: she's selling Vee back to the man who won't say the word, while narrating the man who'd say everything and left her cold. And the tell — Randi, who narrates every man's mouth across a dozen tables, going silent at "and we went up," looking at her hands. Vee reads it as discretion. I read it as the one true wound she won't perform. "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." That line is Randi's whole thesis and Vee laughs, not knowing she's the answer to it.

Where's my trust? On the floor, exactly where it was, and no lower — because I already knew. Nothing here surprised my suspicion; it *executed* it. What moved was my dread: I now know precisely how Vee and Pace get back together, and it's Randi's hand on the tiller the whole way. My sympathy for Vee is total and grieving. My pity for Randi actually deepened — that silence about the hotel room, the hands — but so did my sense of her danger, because she used her own real loneliness to build the case that sent Vee to Pace tonight. Pace isn't on the page and I barely missed him; this was a two-hander and it didn't need him.

Friction? Almost none. If anything Randi's dialogue is *so* well-calibrated it occasionally reads a hair like a coach with a script — "Meow" and "gorgeous" and "my love" stacking up — but that's in character for her performed warmth, so I let it ride. The "raccoon who lost a fight" beat earned its small laugh honestly and broke the tension well.

The title — *My Friend Randi* — has never been more of a knife. This is the chapter that makes the possessive ache: "my friend" is exactly what Vee thinks she has and exactly the disguise the trap wears. The friend is the mechanism. The volume line, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*: I finally feel the spoon. "He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon" — and here's Randi, supping close, no spoon at all, arranging the meal. "A polite invitation" reads now like the coat itself: an invitation delivered wordlessly, courteously, to a door. It points me dead ahead — Vee will go tonight, and they'll reconcile, and the reconciliation will be Randi's engineering, which means the thing Vee gets back was never fully hers to begin with. I dread it and I can't stop reading.
