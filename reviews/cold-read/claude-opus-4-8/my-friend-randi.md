## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is the chapter I was dreading, and it played out worse — quieter, warmer, more expertly — than I feared. Vee went to Randi. Of course she did. And I sat there the whole time knowing what Vee doesn't: that the person talking her down off the ledge is the man's other lover, in love with Vee herself, and is right now steering her straight back into the shared bed she doesn't know is shared. Every warm, wise word Randi says is load-bearing in a way Vee can't feel and I can't stop feeling.

What guts me is that the counsel is *good*. That's the horror. If I strip away what I know, Randi is doing exactly what a great friend would do — "the fear's a liar, and you haven't slept," "start over, but slow," making Vee reckon with how *Pace* felt opening that door. "That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." She dismantles the invented Sheri kindly and precisely: "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one." All of it is true, or true enough. And all of it also happens to be exactly what Randi needs Vee to believe to get her back on the road to Pace. I couldn't tell, moment to moment, where genuine love for Vee ended and management began — and I don't think Randi can either. That's the sickening part. She's not lying. She's just *aiming* the truth.

The coat. God, the coat. "You've got that good long coat" — and then she stops and lets Vee build the rest herself, so that Vee says "And nothing under it," so that it's *Vee's* idea. "a thing being decided for her and by her at once." The book's tagline has been sitting on the cover this whole time — *Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.* — and I have never felt it land as hard as in that maneuver. Randi doesn't tell her to go naked under a coat. She arranges the room so Vee arrives there herself and owns it. And then when the shame-voice comes up ("It's such a slutty thing to do"), Randi walks her past it: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." It's the exact inversion of shame-into-chosen-exhibition that's run through the whole book, done here as a *conscription*. I was aroused and horrified in the same breath — the "wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it" is doing its work on me too, and I hate that it works.

And the heels. "she came back with a pair of black heels hooked on two fingers." *Randi always knew the shoes.* This is the fourth or fifth time she's dressed Vee's feet, and every prior time it read as loving attentiveness. Now it reads as the same hand arranging Vee for delivery — to a man Randi shares her with. The motif has fully curdled.

Then the New Year's story, which I already knew was hollow, told again — and this time I watched Randi *edit it live for Vee's benefit*. She told the true fairytale (John, the lift, the named children, the blue dress, the ball) and then hit the bedroom and stopped: "'And then it got very late,' she said, 'and we went up.' And that was all she said about it." Vee reads it as discretion. I read it as the one true thing Randi will not perform: that the perfect man was nothing in bed, that she felt nothing, because the person she wants is sitting on the bed asking about it. "the bedroom's the room I actually live in. What's a girl to do." She made a joke of the exact wound. And the cruelest small beat — Vee, delighted by the story, feeling it "go into her sideways all the same, because it was the exact thing she didn't have," a man who builds a future out loud in the first hour. She's grieving to Randi for the forward-motion Randi is secretly withholding from *her*.

Randi holding the first hug "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that." Vee pulling breath off her neck and not wanting to stop. That's not neutral anymore. I read the longing in it plainly now, on Randi's side, and Vee taking it as pure comfort.

**Trust and suspicion:** No new suspicion — I don't need any; I already know everything, and the chapter is built on my knowing. What moved is that Randi has crossed from *passive* concealment into *active* engineering. Before this she withheld. Here she *drives*. She is now the direct instrument putting Vee back in the bed, and she does it while genuinely loving her, which is the most disturbing possible configuration.

**The house, the sorority:** Brooke's warmth again — "the warmth arrived ahead of her" — gave back "nothing but more warmth," and Vee "felt the house note her and file her, the stats friend, crying in the front hall." That instinct — that the warmth is also a filing system — is the whole book in miniature, and Vee has it about the sorority but not about Randi.

**Friction:** Almost none. If I reach, "a thing being decided for her and by her at once" is the closest the prose comes to naming its own trick — but it's from *inside* Vee's felt experience, not the author leaning in, so I let it stand. The chapter is disciplined; it never winks.

**Titles:** "My Friend Randi" — flat and warm on its face, the way you'd describe the person who saved your worst day. Read against the chapter, it's a small knife: the possessive *my friend* is exactly the identity Randi has chosen to preserve by never telling the truth, and it's the frame Vee uses to trust the one person she most shouldn't. The word "friend" doing the concealing. And *With a Long Spoon* — I still don't have the proverb it's from, but I'm starting to feel it: you sup with the devil with a long spoon, keep your distance from the thing you're eating with. Vee has no spoon at all. She's eating from Randi's hand. The tagline is just the plain description of this chapter.

**What I want / dread:** I want Vee to sleep and I want the tonight-plan to fall apart before she reaches that door. *Guess from here:* she goes to Pace tonight, naked under the coat, and either he takes her back (and the two worlds slam shut around her without her knowing) or Randi has already made a call and I'm about to watch a coordinated recapture. Either way the collision I've dreaded is now scheduled for tonight, and it's Randi who scheduled it. I dread the bench sitting in that house.
