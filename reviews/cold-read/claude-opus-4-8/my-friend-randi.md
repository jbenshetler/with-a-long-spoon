**Felt read**

This chapter made my skin crawl in the exact way the book has been training me to dread, and I mean that as praise. Randi is doing the most tender, competent, loving thing anyone has done for Vee all break — and every word of it is in service of handing Vee back to the man Randi is secretly sleeping with and secretly co-built this whole thing with. I sat there watching her disassemble Vee's fear point by point, *correctly*, kindly, and the kindness was the horror. "That's not a man who's done with you, honey. That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." That's *true* — I read Pace's chapter, I know he's wrecked over her — and Randi delivers the truth as a tool. She is the only person in Vee's life who can confirm Pace's love with authority, because she's the other half of his bed. The reader feels the floor tilt there; Vee feels rescued.

The Sheri bit undid me. Vee has built a blonde model rival out of spite at 3am, and Randi gently dismantles her — "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one." And she's *right*: Sheri is tiny, two-toned, grieving, nothing like the invented woman. But Randi is steering Vee away from the one real adjacent person and toward the doorstep, and I know the actual other woman is Randi herself, sitting knee-to-knee on the bed. "Don't you dare hand him to a woman who doesn't even have a face." The woman with the face is right there, kissing her goodbye.

The coat plan is where my stomach really dropped. Randi walking Vee down off the word "slutty" — "that's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it" — is genuinely beautiful as a piece of shame-undoing. It's the whole arc of Vee's waking appetite, delivered by the person least entitled to deliver it. And the heels pressed into her hands: "Randi always knew the shoes." That line used to read as friendship. Now it reads as handling. I don't think Randi is cold here — that's what's unbearable. She *missed Vee every bit as badly*, the hug held past where a hug stops, the breath off her neck, bitter orange, "nothing sweet." The want is real on both sides. She's routing her own love for Vee straight into Vee's reconciliation with Pace, and I can't tell if that's self-sacrifice or the game or both, and I don't think Randi can either.

The John story is the chapter's quietest knife. Vee begs for the real version, and Randi performs the fairytale — the lift line, naming children on the first day, the ball, being *led*. And Vee aches at exactly the thing the reader knows is hollow: "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it." Vee envies the easy forward motion. I know it left Randi cold. And then Randi stops at the bedroom door — "and then it got very late, and we went up" — and Vee reads it as discretion, the one thing Randi keeps private. The reader knows it's not discretion; it's that there was nothing there. "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." That's the truest thing Randi says all chapter, and Vee laughs it off. Two women, one of them starving in exactly the place the other one just described, neither able to say it.

My feelings moved: toward Randi, pity and dread both deepened — she's never been more lovable or more dangerous in the same breath. Toward Vee, pure ache; she walked in a raccoon-eyed wreck and left with "a plan sitting where the wreckage had been," and the plan is someone else's. I'm not rooting for the coat. I want it to work *for her* — I want Pace to open that door and come apart — and I also dread it, because the reunion she's walking toward is staged on a foundation she can't see. That doubled feeling is the swoon and the sickness arriving together, which is the book at its most characteristic.

Erotic charge: it simmers rather than burns — the heat lives in the *idea* of the coat, in Vee's shame flipping into want ("the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it"), and in the held hugs. Nothing happens on the page, but the bare-under-the-coat plan is charged, and the neck-breath moment has a low current I don't think Vee's admitting to.

The title — "My Friend Randi" — lands like a soft gut-punch *because* it's in Vee's voice. It's how Vee would introduce her: my friend, the one sure thing, the girl who always knows the shoes. The reader hears the gap screaming inside the possessive. "Friend" is the word doing the most lying in the book, and the chapter title just hands it to us flat, in Vee's own trusting mouth. The volume line — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* — the long spoon is for supping with the devil, and this chapter is Vee being fed, warmly, by exactly that spoon. "A warm reception" points dead at tonight: a door, a heated house, a man who won't say no. I'm half-sure the coat scene is coming next and I both want and fear it.

What I want next: Vee at that door. What I dread: that it works, and the working seals her deeper in. Guess from here — the reunion lands, it's tender and hot, and the reader carries the whole weight of what Vee doesn't know into it.
