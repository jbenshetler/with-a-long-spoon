# Cold read — My Friend Randi

*scene: scenes/my-friend-randi.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: the-usual*

## Reader reaction

Oh, this one landed hard, and it landed cold, because I'm sitting on everything Vee doesn't have. She drives in wrecked from the red-eye, cries the whole forty minutes, and the line that got me was the reversal of homecoming — "every mile of it ran toward a town that had Pace in it and not one inch of him for her." That's a beautiful, sad sentence, and I bought all of it. Vee's grief is completely earned; the empty dorm, Cassie's stripped bed, lying on top of the made bed in plane clothes through the gray middle of the day building "the other one, the girl he'd cooked for, alone at Thanksgiving." And then she goes to Randi. Of course she goes to Randi.

And here's where the whole chapter turned into a slow horror for me while staying, on its surface, the warmest thing in the book. **Randi coaches Vivienne back into Pace's bed.** Randi — Pace's secret lover, the woman who picked Vee *with* him — sits knee-to-knee on the bed and talks a frightened girl out of her fear, out of her shame, and straight onto Pace's doorstep naked under a coat. Every single thing Randi says is *true and good counsel*, which is exactly why it's so awful to watch. "Do you actually believe he was using you? Or is that the fear talking?" She's right. "That's not a man who's done with you, honey. That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him." She's right about that too — the reader has literally seen Pace tell Sheri he misses Vee. Randi is *reassuring Vee with the truth she alone possesses*, and using it to steer her.

The most chilling beat: Vee names her rival. "Sheri, she was alone... much more than friends." And Randi — who knows Sheri is real, knows Sheri is not the threat, knows the actual "other woman" is *herself* — dismantles the phantom rival with surgical warmth. "Tell me what she looks like." "Blonde. Tan. Built like a model." "How do you know any of that?" She walks Vee off the imaginary Sheri so cleanly, and she can afford to, because the true answer is standing in the room pushing the wet hair off Vee's face "like she would a child's." I felt genuinely ill watching it, in the way the book keeps earning. The tagline — *every yes was freely given, that was the trap* — I finally feel it fully. This is the yes being freely, tenderly, expertly extracted.

The coat scene is the erotic core, and it worked on me despite the dread — or braided with it. Randi doesn't say it; she lays the pieces down and "let[s] the rest hang in the room, and wait[s] for Vee to find it." "And nothing under it." Then the recoil — the mother's voice, the word already in her mouth, "It's such a slutty thing to do." And Randi's reframe: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do, and there's not a man alive who survives it." The heat here is the by-now-established Vee mechanism — shame lit, then the wanting turning over under the burn "sharper for it." It's charged, simmering, clothed; a 1 pushing toward a 2 in intention but nothing happens on the page. It's genuinely hot precisely because it's shame-managed into consent.

The New Year story — Randi finally telling the *real* Gstaad. And here's what moved my read of Randi: she tells the fairy tale (John, the lift line, naming three children in the first hour, the pale metallic blue dress "you buy to tell your daughter about someday," the ball lit "like the inside of a bell"), and then at the bedroom she *stops.* "And then it got very late, and we went up." And she looks down at her hands. I've already carried, from the New Year chapter, that Randi's Gstaad night was hollow and perfect and a lie she sent Vee. So her later line — "everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in" — landed as the one true thing she gave away all chapter, played off as a quip. Vee "read the quiet as discretion." I read it as the crack. Randi's whole apparatus of warmth, and the one room where it fails her.

What's building under all of it: Vee saw the fairy tale — "a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it" — and felt the exact absence at Pace's core. The whole chapter is Randi handing Vee a future-shaped thing (a plan, a peace sign, the shoes — "Randi always knew the shoes") to replace the future Pace won't say.

**Trust/suspicion:** No new suspicion of Randi's *feelings* — I believe the missed-each-other hugs "past where a hug stops," I believe she loves Vee. That's what makes it worse. The thing I distrust is structural: Randi is using perfect, loving counsel to push Vee toward a door that leads back to the trap she and Pace built. Nothing on the page tips Vee off. The dramatic irony is total and it's the source of all the tension.

**Friction:** Very little. Maybe "that's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" is the closest to the author telling me how to feel about Pace — but it's Randi's mouth, in-character coaching, so it reads as her instrument rather than the author's thumb. The black heels being pressed into her hands ("Just the thing for tonight") — Randi dressing Vee for a night with the man they share — is a small, deniable, perfect knife.

**The titles:** "My Friend Randi" — flat, trusting, the way Vee would title it herself, and that innocence is the whole point. From Vee's side it means the one safe harbor. From mine it's dripping: *my friend* Randi, who is also my lover's lover, coaching me back to bed. The book title *With a Long Spoon* still sits mostly opaque to me — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon," I keep circling that — but this chapter is the first where I feel the *supping* fully: Vee taking spoonfuls of comfort from the one person she should least trust, and it tasting like pure care.

**What I want/dread next:** She's going to do it. Tonight she showers, dresses, and shows up at Pace's door naked under the coat in Randi's heels. *Guess from here:* the next chapter is that doorstep, from one side or both — and I dread it and want it in equal measure, because it's a reconciliation the reader knows is a re-baiting. I also keep waiting for the two worlds to physically touch, for Vee to learn Randi and Pace are connected. Not yet. The collision is still coming and this chapter tightened the spring.
