## Reader reaction

Oh, this one hurt to watch. This is the exact chapter I'd been bracing for — the consolation arriving wearing a lover's face — and it played out worse and more skillfully than I'd feared, because I could see every wire and Vee couldn't see one.

The whole thing is Randi *steering*, and the horror is how good she is at it and how it reads, to Vee, as pure love. Watch the sequence: Vee arrives wrecked, believing the true thing (there's someone else, it was just sex, he's done) — and Randi walks her, step by patient step, back off every accurate instinct and into the trap. "Do you actually believe he was using you? Or is that the fear talking?" She reframes Vee's correct read as sleep-deprived paranoia. She has Vee narrate the last night in a way that turns the *apparatus* — the pre-warmed house, the poured wine, the phone in the other room — into evidence of devotion. Every one of those is a thing I logged as the seduction machinery, and Randi feeds each one back to Vee as proof of love: "He'd warmed the house for you." I know that heat is brought up hours early as *method*, "so she would never once be cold under his roof," and here it's being spent as the closing argument. That's the con eating its own tell.

And the cruelest, cleanest move: Randi dismantles the invented blonde. "Sheri. Tell me what she looks like." Vee describes the spite-blonde she built at 3 a.m., and Randi — correctly, generously, *lovingly* — tells her it's a fear-face, not a real woman. And she's *right*. That's what guts me. Randi is telling Vee the literal truth (the blonde isn't real, that's the fear talking) in order to disarm the one instinct that was pointing, however crookedly, at the actual danger. Vee's fear was correct — there IS another woman, there's a whole apparatus — she just gave it the wrong face. And Randi, who is that other hand, uses the wrongness of the face to erase the correctness of the fear. Masterful and vile.

The coat. God, the coat. Randi doesn't say it — she leaves the sentence hanging and makes Vee finish it herself: "And nothing under it." That's the whole method in one gesture — never take, hand back the question, let the yes come from her mouth so it's freely given. It's Pace's exact playbook ("hand the question back"), and here's Randi running it on Vee, and I've been told they run the same playbook, and watching it in live action was chilling. Then when Vee recoils in her mother's voice — "It's such a slutty thing to do" — Randi reframes shame into bravery: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do." And "gorgeous," the doubled word, dropped twice, each time a caress that I know is also a harvest tag.

The shoes. "Randi always knew the shoes." I've clocked this before — Randi has picked and buckled Vee's heels at every hinge point. And she does it again, presses black heels into her hands "just the thing for tonight," and Vee is grateful to have one thing decided for her. The person dressing her for the doorstep is the co-conspirator staging the delivery. Vee walking out "already being the answer" is Vee being walked back onto the hook by the second operator, and thinking it's the best friend saving her.

The hug. Two hugs, both held "past where a hug stops," Randi "holding on like she'd missed her every bit as badly." I know Randi's in love with her, so the length of that hug isn't instrumental warmth — it's the real thing bleeding through the operation, and that's the part that makes Randi genuinely tragic rather than just monstrous. She loves this girl and is steering her straight back into the machine, and both are completely true in the same embrace.

And the New Year story. This is where the chapter went from painful to devastating for me, because I hold the key Vee doesn't. Randi tells the John story — the lift line, naming three children in the first hour, the pale blue dress "the kind you tell your daughter about," the ball lit "like the inside of a bell" — and Vee drinks it as a fairy tale and envies it: "the exact thing she didn't have, the easy forward motion of it." But I remember what Gstaad *was* for Randi: her proof that the safe world cannot reach her, that "the flaw was hers, carried in the same as the cold." Vee is envying a void, and I got to watch her do it, out loud, delighted, hands over her mouth. And then the tell I'd been half-waiting for: Randi, "who had told Vee across a dozen tables exactly what a man's mouth had done and where," *stops* at the bedroom. "And then it got very late, and we went up." Vee reads it as discretion. I read it as the flaw — the room she "actually lives in" where the safe world went dead. "A masterpiece. Everywhere but the bedroom." That's Randi telling the truth about her own emptiness and having it land as a witty aside. Vee laughs. I didn't.

Erotic charge: low and deliberately so — this is a clothed manipulation scene, and the heat that's present is *anticipatory* and grim (the coat-with-nothing-under-it plan). What charge there is comes wrapped in dread, because I know where that doorstep leads. It works as tension but I wouldn't call it arousing; it's too much like watching someone dress for an ambush they think is a reunion.

Friction: almost none — this is tautly written. The one place I felt the thumb was the smallest bit of over-clarity in "That's not a man who's done with you, honey. That's a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him" — it's *very* clean as persuasion, maybe a hair too quotable, but it's in-character for Randi-as-instrument so I forgive it. And "Meow" landed as a slightly modern jolt but it's the kind of thing that reads as real girl-talk, so fine.

Trust: Randi confirmed at zero and now actively dangerous on the page rather than offstage-loaded. Vee — pure ache, no suspicion; she got everything backwards with total sincerity. The one thing I *don't* have that I keep reaching for: does Randi know she's steering Vee toward Pace *specifically as the other hand*? In Vee's POV I can't see inside Randi here — but the coat plan, the shoes, sending her to "him tonight," all of it lines up too perfectly to be coincidence. Guess from here: Randi is deliberately delivering Vee back to Pace, and this whole comfort session is the consolation-deployment I was primed for, done so lovingly that it doubles as the harvest.

The title — "My Friend Randi." Possessive, from Vee's mouth, and it's the trap in three words. Vee's one sure warmth, the friend she runs to, is the second operator. The title is Vee's love and my dread in the same phrase. Against the book's promises — *A Polite Invitation*, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the tagline made flesh: Vee finishes the sentence herself, chooses the coat herself, is grateful for the shoes, wants him back "more than anything," and every yes is hers, freely given, and every one is Randi's. "With a long spoon" — borrowed warmth across false distance — the whole comfort was borrowed warmth, the hug real and instrumental at once.

What I want/dread next: I dread the doorstep. I want to see whether Pace opens the door and whether he knows Randi sent her. I want to know if Randi told Pace this is coming. And I'm braced for the possibility that the "reunion" is the trap snapping shut — Vee walking through the cold to hand him everything, exactly as designed.
