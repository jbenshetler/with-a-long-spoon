# Cold read (grounded) — Hangover

## Reader reaction

This one caught me off guard. I came in braced for the next round of the game — brunch debriefs, the kiss thread, the slow closing of the trap — and instead the chapter just... sat down beside Pace and let him talk, and by the end I had a lump in my throat I hadn't authorized. It's the tenderest, saddest thing the book has handed me in a long stretch, and it did it without a single erotic beat, which somehow makes it hit harder.

The frame is perfect and I fell for it completely: Vee wrecked from the ciders, the melted ice water with the paper-towel square under it so it wouldn't mark the wood — that detail alone told me everything about how the night's back half must have gone, him undressing her into his own undershirt, the forehead kiss "after he thought she was already gone." That's the Pace I keep being half-seduced by, the one who does all of it quietly and leaves no card. And then the cinnamon-raisin loaf baked *while she slept it off*. The man cannot stop feeding her. I melted, straightforwardly, the way I'm supposed to.

But the gut-punch is the friendship conversation. **"Sheri's my friend. My one friend. There isn't anyone else."** I sat with that the way Vee did — waiting for it to rearrange into something normal, and it wouldn't. What moved my feeling about Pace here is that the withholding I've been distrusting all book got, for once, a floor under it that reads as pure damage rather than control. His whole taxonomy of why — women misunderstand the friendship so it curdles; men make friends by competing at thirteen and fourteen and he could not stand in a single arena, too far ahead to lose to anyone the ordinary way; and the window is on a timer, "men don't start at twenty-two" — landed on me as one of the most quietly devastating self-accounts I've read. And the flat sentence he wouldn't decorate: the men already there "either weren't safe or weren't welcoming." No name. Vee does the arithmetic I did too — the black-window "less than staying," Sheri's "went bad with his daddy, hasn't been home since he was sixteen" — and the book lets us assemble a thing it will never show us directly. I respected that it didn't spell it out.

And then the Daphne re-reckoning, which is where I felt the page earn its unease honestly rather than manufacture it. Vee reaches to hold Daphne as *he* holds her — his best two years, luck — and can't, because the "ugly grown-up word" keeps forming under her tongue and she keeps pressing it down. That's exactly the read I've had since chapter 25, and watching Vee arrive at the edge of it and *refuse the word for his sake* was quietly wrenching. He had one person doing the work of four, in a building of grown men, and that one person only had that much of him "because of the thing about it that was wrong." Then Daphne left "sensibly," and he had no one. That's the line that got me: no Randi to bring it to. Nobody to take the heartbreak to. The book let me feel the cost without letting Pace feel it, and *that gap is the ache* — "It isn't tragic," he says, and Vee (and I) hear the good order as the tell.

My feeling about Pace genuinely moved here — softened, deepened, and got sadder. The devotion-vs-control line I've been walking tilted toward devotion this chapter, not because the game got less troubling but because I saw the shape of the boy under it: handed a whole life years before the growing, left to eat it alone. And Vee is wonderful in this — she doesn't say sorry, because sorry would ask him to hold still and be comforted; she just crosses the counter and holds him the way Sheri did, whole weight committed, and he sets his chin on her head and goes "somewhere else with it," neither happy nor sad. That last image — the wrong bright light off old snow, the bread cooling, neither of them fixing anything — is one of the truest things in the book.

**Swoon / pull:** high, but a grieving kind. The water glass, the loaf, the undershirt, the wordless hug — I ached for them and rooted for her to be enough for him, while a colder voice reminded me she's the second woman he's ever had this with, and the first one cost what it cost.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** here's the thing — this chapter *lowered* my guard on Pace, deliberately and effectively, and I'm aware of that. It's an intimacy that makes his loneliness the reason for everything withheld. I don't distrust anything *on the page*; what stays live in me is dramatic irony, not suspicion: I know Randi exists as exactly the peer-friendship-plus-love he says he can't have, and I know the two of them built Vee. The chapter didn't touch that. It just made me feel his hunger, which is its own kind of unsettling, because a man this starved for a person is a man who might do a great deal to keep one.

**Erotic charge:** near zero, and rightly. The night before is all wreckage and care. This chapter isn't about wanting; it's about being held. A body-before-mind note: my strongest physical response was the throat-tightening at "no one left to take the heartbreak to," not anything sexual.

**Friction:** very little. If anything, the interior arithmetic runs long — Vee lifting Meg, Kayla, Cassie, Randi "out of her life" one at a time to stand in Pace's single slot is a touch schematic, the author's thumb faintly warm on the scale to make sure I count to four. But it mostly earned it. And "It isn't tragic. It's just a door that didn't open, and then you're grown" — that's him, and it's good, so the near-over-explaining is forgivable.

**The title:** "Hangover" — I read it flat at first (three ciders, low bright light pressing the backs of her eyes) and then it recolored on me completely. The chapter is about what *doesn't* wear off: the hangover of a childhood, the residue of Daphne, the thing you can't sleep off with water and two brown pills. What lingers. That's a good title — it points at the body and means the history.

**Book/series titles:** the cover board gives me *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION*, over the chapter title *Hangover*. "With a long spoon" still rings the old proverb for me — *he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon* — and every chapter makes me more sure the spoon is the distance you keep while you eat at a dangerous table, and that Vee has no spoon at all. "A Warm Reception" reads doubled now: the literal warmth of these houses and rooms kept too hot for whoever he loves, and the *reception* of a person — Sheri received on a porch with donuts, Vee received into a warm bed she doesn't remember reaching. Warmth as the thing that disarms. It promises comfort and I keep taking it while quietly waiting for the spoon to matter.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the Saturday dance hall — Sheri, the Brooks & Dunn cover band, the blonde-jealousy thread getting paid off in the flesh. *Guess from here:* the book just spent a whole chapter making me love Pace's loneliness, and it made a point that his *one* possible friendship is with a woman who can't want him — which sits an inch away from Randi, the peer who can and does. I dread the moment those two facts touch. And I still dread, steadily, the day Vee learns the reception was arranged.
