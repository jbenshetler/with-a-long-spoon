## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This is a breather chapter, and I mean that as praise — it's the first time in ages the book has let Vee be alone with herself, no Pace to be dazzled by, no Randi to be drawn open by, just her body doing the one thing it's always done cleanly. The pool as "the one uncomplicated belonging" pays off here in full. I've been braced for so long — waiting for the plan to close on her — that a chapter with no one in the lane but Vee and Cassie's voice landed on me like a held breath let out.

What moved me: Cassie's *"Do you love him"* getting into the lane "off the third wall, whole, in Cassie's voice, flat the way she'd said it." That's the whole chapter, really — Vee interrogating the *maybe* she gave Cassie when the true answer was sitting right there, easy to reach. I found that genuinely tender and a little frightening at once. She loves him and said *maybe*, and she doesn't know why the maybe came out. I do, or I think I do — some old self-protective wire — and the fact that she can't name it while I can is the book's usual ache, running quiet for once instead of hot.

The line that actually got me, the one I'll keep: *"she could not tell, for about four strokes, whether the man she was turning over was Pace or was somebody she had built out of wanting him — whether he was that good or she had made him that good."* That's the truest, most naked thing Vee has thought in the whole book, and it's *hers*, unprompted, un-mined — no Randi coaxing it out one "yeah" at a time. It matters enormously to me that this doubt surfaced in the one place no one is watching her. And the book, to its credit, gives her only four strokes of it before the wall takes it away. It doesn't resolve. She doesn't decide. I love that it stayed ajar.

I have no suspicion to report this chapter, because there's no one here to be suspicious of — and that absence is itself a relief that tells me how tightly wound I've been. What I feel toward Vee is plain, uncomplicated fondness: the green-and-white suit that "had nowhere left to go on her and hadn't for years," and how she "looked at the water instead of at that." The body that "has opinions all day long every day of her life" going silent underwater. After chapters of her body outrunning her consent — going wet, going still, betraying her to Pace and to Randi and to a bathroom stall — here her body finally just *works*, honest, asking nothing, and I felt how rare and good that is for her.

The *"did he mind"* thread — the counter, the drawer he gave her — riding in alongside *"do you love him"* and then dissolving into "nothing at all" by the fourth piece: that's a lovely bit of emotional sorting. The swimming "took the words off things one at a time until what was left was the shape underneath." I buy that completely; it's how the body actually files things. No erotic charge here and the chapter isn't reaching for any — it's the one room in the book with the heat switched off, on purpose, and it earns its quiet.

The title, **"Strokes."** Swim strokes, obviously — the whole chapter is stroke and count and wall. But it also lands, gently, on the other strokes: the hand coming down, the stroking of her hair, "wonderful, wonderful." The word holds both the tender and the disciplinary registers Pace works in, and sets them beside the plain athletic one where nothing is being done *to* her. I like that the innocent meaning gets the chapter and the loaded ones only hum underneath. It didn't give anything away; it just quietly rhymed. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, title *Strokes* — still points where it has: "long spoon" is the sup-with-the-devil proverb, the warning I keep waiting to come due. This chapter is the calm before whatever the invitation turns out to cost.

What I want next: I'm almost afraid of how much this chapter soothed me, because it feels like the book letting me exhale right before it doesn't. *Guess from here:* the *maybe* and the four-stroke doubt are seeds — Vee's first faint unforced question about whether Pace is real or built — and I suspect the plan's machinery closes precisely when she starts, on her own, to wonder. I want more of Vee-alone. I dread how briefly she gets to have it.
