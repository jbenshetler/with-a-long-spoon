# Cold read (grounded) — Rock

## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This one worked on me and frightened me in the same breath, which is becoming the only mode this book has for me. It's a quiet chapter — two people under a quilt by a fire, trading where-they're-from — and it's where I've felt closest to Pace and most afraid of him at once.

The swoon is real and I won't shortchange it. When he opens up about math — the shape you can fill with a cup of paint but never paint the outside of, the coastline that runs to infinity the shorter your ruler, Newton's "three sentences" that govern the ball and the leaf and the stars alike, "the language the world is written in" — I melted exactly as Vee did. "Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some." I've never been handed math as tenderness like that, and the chapter earned the way the stars outside the glass stopped being decoration for a second. And Vee's recognition undid me: "That's how you are with me… It's the lift. It's the same face." That's a genuinely swooning read of being *present*, and it landed.

But the center of this chapter is Daphne, and it dropped a stone in my stomach. He lays it out in perfect order — assigned as his tutor, she nineteen, he *fifteen*, two years, "nothing happened, nobody did anything wrong," she left, "which was only sensible." And the good order is the tell; the narration says so outright: "the careful way a person carries a full glass so it won't go over the rim." Vee does the arithmetic I was doing — the clean ugly grown-up word forming under her tongue — and holds it down because it isn't hers to say. I held it with her. The thing that chilled me most isn't the fact itself; it's that *he named it luck.* "It was the best two years I'd had." He hands her the wound and calls it fortune, and believes it. That reframes the whole courtly machine for me: a boy taken off from home too young and loved by someone who shouldn't have, who now runs a slow patient selection-and-courtship of a younger girl he *chose as a game.* The pattern sitting underneath "May I" got very loud.

Which complicates my memory. I'd carried Daphne as "a door he didn't respect, that cost him." This telling is nothing like that — and I can't tell if that earlier version was his gloss, mine, or a lie he tells differently depending on who's listening. The gap itself unsettles me. I'm not resolving it.

The scar, finally touched: "Not tonight." No wall in it, "only a door with a time on it." That's him keeping his own closed door — the very thing he grants Randi and now grants himself — and I liked that the chapter let it stay shut.

The name reveal is the quiet heart. "My real name is Peter" — and he lifts his *mother* out of the answer, which I noticed and which the prose noticed too. Vee saying "Peter" into the dark "the way you say a word to keep it" is the most intimate beat in the book so far for me, more than any sex. Trust between them has deepened — and that's precisely what scares me, because I'm the one who knows about Randi, who knows this intimacy has a hidden floor.

Heat is low and I don't mind — the hand on her breast "as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm" is a lovely simmering image, but the chapter isn't about wanting, it's about being known. Romance, though, is near its peak for the book.

No new suspicion of anyone *present* — it's just the two of them, and Vee is radiant and safe-feeling. My dread is structural, carried in from what I know. The only friction: "She had stumbled into her… She had stumbled into her" — Vee's closing recap of meeting Randi "by accident" is laid on a touch thick, clearly for *my* benefit, since I know there was no stumble. It's the author nudging the irony, and I felt the thumb. But it's mild.

Titles. "Rock" — Peter means rock; the chapter that gives us *Peter* is called Rock, and he's described squatting a bar that bends steel "like dry spaghetti," the immovable present thing, the one the week bends around. It's the solid-foundation word, and it points me straight at the name. On second thought there's a darker edge — a foundation built on what happened at fifteen is a cracked one. The volume line, "WITH A LONG SPOON · A POLITE INVITATION," keeps ringing the proverb: sup with the devil, use a long spoon. The feeding, the invitations, the courtliness — a *polite invitation* to the table is exactly how you'd dress a trap, and this chapter quietly suggests the devil was himself once invited to someone's table too young.

What I want next: I want to keep sitting in this warmth and I'm terrified of it. I dread the moment Vee learns about Randi, and I now half-dread learning the scar. Guess from here: the scar ties to Daphne or to that early time, and "not tonight" means it's coming.
