# Cold read — Rock

*scene: scenes/rock.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: sorority*

## Reader reaction

This is the chapter I've been quietly waiting for, and it mostly delivered: **his real name is Peter.** After all the load I've been carrying on "Pace vs a hidden true self," it arrives without fanfare — "My real name is Peter" — and the writing knows exactly what it's doing by letting it "sit there between them, plainer than Pace, with no forward motion in it." That plainness is what got me. And then Vee saying it back three times across the scene, the last one "the way you say a word to keep it" — that landed as genuinely intimate, not sentimental. I felt it.

What moved most for me: **the hallway.** Seventh grade, taught himself trig, asked questions the teacher couldn't answer, so she moved his desk into the hall for a year. "They took the thing you loved and made it the punishment." That's the first time I've felt I understood Pace/Peter from the inside rather than admired him from outside. It reframes everything — the self-containment, the maker-of-everything, the rooms-where-people-argue-about-math relief. And crucially it's the first time he read *smaller* to me, more woundable, less like the frictionless operator I've been dreading.

Here's my problem as this particular reader, and it's not the chapter's fault: **I can't fully hand myself over to it.** I know what Vee doesn't. So when she says the thing that should be the emotional peak — "That's how you are with me. It's the lift. It's the same face. You're right here" — I felt two things at once, hard. The tenderness is real; his presence is real. And also she has just handed him, gift-wrapped, the exact read of himself that a person orchestrating her would most want to be believed. The chapter earns the warmth so completely that the cold-underneath is almost unbearable here rather than invisible — because *I* supply it. On a pure first read with no prior knowledge this would just be a beautiful pillow-talk scene. Carrying what I carry, it's the saddest chapter yet.

The math monologue — Gabriel's Horn (finite volume, infinite surface), the coastline/fractal thing, Newton's three laws over ball and leaf and stars — worked on me more than I expected. "Most people walk around inside the poem and never get to read a line of it, and I got to read some." I bought that as the real Peter, the un-instrumental core, the fifteen-year-old in the first rooms that didn't hurt. If anything this is the strongest case the book has made that there's a true self under Pace worth grieving. And it makes the harvest worse.

The **age reveal** — twenty-two, doctorate, SAT at fourteen — recontextualizes him usefully. "Fifteen in a dormitory full of men" as the practical seed of the lifting. That's a clean answer to a question I'd half-formed.

Two of my open threads got touched:
- **The scar.** Her hand finds it on his hip, "old, raised and smooth, longer than her hand," and he covers her hand and says "Not tonight" — deferred again, but now explicitly a door with a time on it. I liked that it wasn't a wall. Still withheld, and I'm content to wait now that I know it's deliberate on his part.
- **The "parents" flinch.** Subtler here. His answer is flat and small — "My mother stays home. My father works in a chemical plant" — set against her warm sprawl, and the text lets the size of it speak: "It was not withheld. It was only the size of it." And the sharp beat: she asks *what his mother calls him* and he answers with his name instead — "He had taken his mother out of the answer. Vee let him." That's the flinch, rendered as behavior, not named. I noticed the swap. I don't know what's behind it and I like not being told.

**Erotic charge:** low and warm rather than hot, and appropriately so — this is aftermath, not a scene. The hand over her breast "holding the soft weight of it without pressing, without asking, as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm," and later his palm flat on her bare hip — those are tender-possessive, not arousing exactly, and I don't think they were meant to spike. The heat here is emotional. It didn't go clinical anywhere.

**Friction:** very little. The prose leans hard on its own cadence — "the yard held its black and the ridge held its darker black" repeats a beat I'd already gotten the first time, and the final paragraph piles up the parallel clauses ("and the fire kept its small business... and the cold stayed on its own side of the glass, and neither of them said anything else") to a degree that started to feel like a held note held a bar too long. The Ohio-food riff ("beige in the ways food could be beige even when it was technically another color," the saffron "old enough to vote") is charming and I laughed, but it's the one place the voice felt like it was performing wit *for me* rather than for the two of them. Minor. The one line I want to flag as almost too on-the-nose: **"So the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other."** Beautiful, and Vee even says "I never put it that way" — but it's Pace doing the thing the book keeps telling me he does (going "to the hand underneath the thing"), and it's so tidy it briefly felt like the author's hand rather than his.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the scar, and I still want to know what his mother calls him, since he so pointedly wouldn't say. I dread — because I can't not — the moment Vee learns Peter and Randi already know each other, and I felt that dread sharpen precisely *because* this chapter made Peter loveable and real. *Guess from here:* the "not tonight" on the scar is a promissory note the book will cash at a moment of maximum vulnerability, and the scar will turn out to connect to whatever the "parents"/chemical-plant flatness is hiding. And I suspect this warm, Randi-free chapter exists to make sure I've fallen as hard as Vee before the trap closes.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** A cold-night intimacy scene at Pace's house, post-sex, under a quilt on the porch. Long pillow talk. Vee gives her whole background: Ohio, casserole food, nurse mother, jet-engine-mechanic father who calls her Vee (Vivienne only in trouble), one distant Army brother. Pace gives almost nothing of his — mother stays home, father in a chemical plant, flat and small — and when she asks what his mother calls him he sidesteps by giving his name instead: **his real name is Peter.** He's revealed to be twenty-two, a prodigy (SAT at fourteen, college at fifteen). The wound underneath: in seventh grade a teacher exiled his desk to the hallway for a year for being too advanced — "they took the thing you loved and made it the punishment." He opens up about the beauty of math (infinite-surface/finite-volume, fractal coastlines, Newton's three laws over ball and leaf and stars) and links his lifting to total presence in the moment. Vee tells him that's how he is with her in bed — fully present — and he answers by putting his hand on her bare hip. Her hand finds the old scar on his hip; he covers it and says "Not tonight" — deferred, but as a door with a time, not a wall. She says "Peter" softly to keep it.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee / Vivienne** — from Ohio, warm, open, gives herself completely; here she's the one drawing Peter out and reading him accurately ("it's the lift, the same face"). Exposure-hungry, but this chapter foregrounds her tenderness over her shame.
- **Pace / Peter** — real name now known: **Peter.** Twenty-two, math-PhD prodigy, powerlifter, maker. Wounded child under the self-containment (the hallway exile). Working-class parents (chemical plant / stay-home mother) rendered flat and withheld; pointedly won't say what his mother calls him. Loves math as "the language the world is written in." Still secretly Randi's co-orchestrator — absent from this chapter but present in my dread. Scar still undisclosed; right-hand-only eating not addressed here.
- **Randi** — not present. Still the secret co-orchestrator/confidante; I carry her as the cold under everything.
- **Cassie** — Vee's roommate/moral floor. Not present.

**How I feel.** The warmest, saddest chapter so far. Peter became real and woundable to me — I love him more and dread for Vee more, at once, because the chapter made me fall as hard as she has. Her naming his presence back to him is beautiful and, given what I know, a knife. Trust in the tenderness (real) coexists with certainty of the harvest (also real). Mood: aching, golden, doom underneath.

**Open questions.** The scar (now an explicit "not tonight" promise). What his mother calls him / the family flatness. Right-hand-only eating still unexplained. When Vee learns Peter and Randi already know each other — dread sharpened by this chapter. Where the Randi touch-staircase and the "same shoe size / we can share" seed go. The induction where the room turns.
