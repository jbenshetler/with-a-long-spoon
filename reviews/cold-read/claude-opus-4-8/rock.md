**Felt read**

This chapter undid me a little, and I want to be honest that it also frightened me in a way the sweeter chapters haven't. It's just the two of them under a quilt by a fire, cold on the far side of the glass, tea gone cold — and it's the most naked Pace has ever been on the page, and none of it is his body. It ran warm and low the whole way, and then it dropped a floor out from under me.

What got to me first was the *quality* of his attention, which the chapter names precisely: he goes to "the hand underneath the thing." She defends her mother's beige casseroles and he says "and affectionate," and she's stopped because no one else would have gone there. The whole first half swooned me clean — the unopened saffron old enough to vote, "the name that means love in one mouth means trouble in the other," the palm laid over her breast "as if it were her skin and not the fire that kept him warm." That's the tenderest image in the book so far, and it's not reaching for sex. I melted. I rooted for them. Against everything I know.

Then the reveals, in ascending order of dread. The hallway — the teacher who took the thing he loved and made it the punishment; Vee names it exactly and he says "that is approximately what happened," and I felt her shoulder come against his arm and felt it in my own chest. Twenty-two, a doctorate, SAT at fourteen, college at fifteen, "less than staying." Fine, aching, sympathetic. And then **Daphne.** His *assigned tutor.* Nineteen to his fifteen. Two years. And he sets it down in that flat, over-ordered voice — "nothing happened, nobody did anything wrong… it would have been stranger if she'd stayed" — and Vee does the arithmetic I did, and the "clean ugly grown-up word" forms under both our tongues and neither of us says it. That sequence is masterful and awful: *he handed her the thing that hurt him and named it luck.* He was groomed. He does not know he was groomed. He is defending his abuser mid-sentence without hearing himself.

And here is where my feeling about Pace *moved*, hard. I've held him as charming-and-sinister — the man engineering a girl's autumn as a game. This chapter doesn't excuse that, but it recolors the machine: the man who thinks a four-year age-and-power gap that "went on two years" was the best thing that ever happened to him is now, at twenty-two, orchestrating a slow, patient, total capture of a twenty-one-year-old who "chooses every step." He is reproducing the exact shape of the thing done to him and calling it patience, calling it letting her get there on her own. The tutor. The catalog joke — "there was nothing I needed tutoring in." I couldn't stop hearing the coffee shop, the calculus, the pencil handed back so the solving stays hers. He learned the whole grammar of it at fifteen from the far side.

Do I trust anyone? I trust Vee's heart completely and fear for her more than ever. Pace — I no longer think he's a cold operator; I think he's a wound that has organized itself into a method, which is somehow scarier. The scar under her hand, "not tonight," a door with a time on it — the text earned my dread there without a single false note. I don't feel the author's thumb; if anything the restraint (letting Vee hold the word down, letting his flat voice be the tell) is the opposite of thumb.

Erotic charge: low and warm, never the point — the palm on her breast, his mouth cleaning nothing here, the hand settling on her bare hip. It simmered. What burned was intimacy, not heat.

The title, **"Rock."** By the end it's the squat bar — steel he bends "like dry spaghetti," the lift where "there can be nothing in my mind but that moment," which Vee recognizes as *how he is with her*: all the way here. Rock as the thing that holds, that carries her weight like the shoe that "stood her up." But it curdled on me second thought — the bar he lifts started as armor: "fifteen in a dormitory full of men." Rock is what you become when you were once soft prey. She calls it presence; I heard survival. The volume line **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** rang louder than ever: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* This chapter is the polite invitation par excellence — a fire, a quilt, a confession — and I'm the one holding the long spoon, watching Vee reach across with her bare hand.

What I want next: I ache to know the scar, and I dread it in equal measure. I want Cassie's sightline back. Guess from here: the scar connects to Daphne, or to the leaving of home — and I fear Randi (who last chapter went *still* hearing Vee described as "the most alive person in the room") is the fracture coming. Vee ends "so fortunate, so happy," which in this book is the sound right before the floor goes.
