## Reader reaction

I did not expect the last word to be *Randi*. The whole chapter she is "she," faceless-named, and I read her as the protagonist — the jacket told me a Vivienne "Vee" Thorne, twenty-one, quietly starving, and this woman is starving too, portioning apple slices thin because she "would eat thin slices and would not eat thick ones," so I spent most of the chapter assuming I was inside Vee's first night. Then "There you are, Randi," and the floor shifted. This is the *friend*. This is the glamorous one who, per the jacket, is going to steer Vee toward Pace. And she is Pace's girlfriend, already — established routine, overnight bag, the safeword she already has, the blanket she brought "after the second week." So the two people the jacket says planned a girl's year are lovers, and I've just watched one of them get taken somewhere she didn't mean to go. That recontextualizes the whole chapter on the last page and I liked being fooled — it didn't feel like a cheat, because the withholding *is* the point: this is a woman who lives behind a "face," and the text withheld her the way she withholds herself.

The erotic charge is real and sustained — a full 3. It works on me. The apple game on his lap, bite-kiss-bite; his hands doing "one unhurried pass and then another, each time turning back a little higher... and each time leaving again before it arrived" — the whole chapter is edging, and the prose enacts it, delay as technique. The bench reveal is genuinely hot precisely because it's *reading* — she deciphers the face cradle, the handholds, "the open middle, where a surface should have been," and her throat bares and she exhales shakily and says *Show me.* That's a yes I believed. The consent architecture is foregrounded — "Tell me your word," "Red," "Tell me anytime, I won't be upset" — and it's exactly the trustworthy heat the blurb promised. Pace asks before he takes. That's on the page, not just claimed.

And yet. My swoon kept curdling into something colder, and I want to be honest about which parts earned it and which came from the jacket priming me. What the *page* earned: Pace is almost unbearably attentive, and the attentiveness has a catalog quality that unsettles me on its own. He sliced the apple thin "because she would eat thin slices," laid the cut sides up "because he had noticed that she ate them faster when the cut sides showed." He clocks that she never comments on the cheap counters. He knows she'll drink more water than she expects. This is a man who has *studied* her, and the studying is presented as tenderness — and mostly it reads as tenderness — but there's a managerial exactness under it that made the back of my neck prickle. He is producing her responses. Even his patience is a method: "He was showing her what the next hour would be." I don't distrust his feeling — he seems genuinely moved by her at the end, "a girl who had been portioning herself... who tonight had finally let someone feed her" — but I distrust how *total* his read of her is while she can't read him at all ("a man, last name unknown to her own sorority sisters").

The payload, though, is Randi's interior at the end, and it's the best thing in the chapter — this is the inward-following I read for. The orgasm "was hers in a way nothing he had done with her had ever been... it had not come from her, and it had not come from anything he had done to her body. It had come from being seen." And her response to being seen is to decide, "quiet as a deadbolt going over — not ever. He could have the face." She's going back behind it and "not coming out again." That's the dark turn I'm allowed to take as meaning, not betrayal. The self-harm details land hard and quietly: the bottle cap pressed under her thumbnail, "she did not seem to know she was doing it"; the plain unpainted nails noticed earlier as "the effort and the cost of her"; the mother who "lived on egg whites and tofu, a thing she said with a knife in it." This is a woman built entirely for being looked at who has just been *seen* and experienced it as unsurvivable. The locked bathroom door — "which was not a thing she had ever done before in his house" — and the smile she reassembles in the mirror to walk back out and lie. It aches. It's not swoon; it's dread, and it's earned.

The Daphne beat is the one place Pace's past cracks open: "Daphne had built a door, once, and he had not respected it... it had cost him more than he had known." First I've heard of her — a separate woman, an ex, the source of his rule about closed doors. It humanizes him and also tells me his gentleness is *learned from a wound*, which is more interesting than innate saintliness.

Trust vs. suspicion: I'm not certain of anyone, and I like that. The page itself gives me a Pace who is loving and scrupulous; the jacket gives me a "plan" and "a game... a secret choice made about her," and I can't yet reconcile the two. My honest position: on the page tonight, nothing cruel happens — the cruelty, such as it is, is Randi's toward herself and toward Pace (the concealment she's choosing). The unease is real but I'll name it as mostly jacket-seeded plus that catalog-of-her quality; the scene itself worked on me as tenderness.

Friction: almost none. Occasionally the interior monologue over-explains its own thesis — "the face was not her, and what was not her could be given away all day and cost nothing... It had worked on everyone" is the author underlining what the bottle-cap and the mirror already showed me. I didn't need "the size of a key turning in a lock" spelled twice (the deadbolt image was already doing it). But these are small; the chapter mostly trusts its images.

Titles. "The Bench" — the object he built "For you. Tonight," a device of exposure disguised as a massage table, "which she had seen without seeing." It's the apparatus that opens her, and the final image is it *waiting for the next time*, top leaning against the wall, "full of what it was." It reads to me as the whole book's machinery in one object: something built for her, that she'll walk past and not look at. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." now make a cold kind of sense — the horror isn't coercion, it's that everything is chosen. And *With a Long Spoon* — I hear the proverb, sup with the devil and use a long spoon. Somebody here is the devil, or the supper is. I can't assign the role yet.

What I want next: I assume the next chapter finally gives me Vee, and I dread and crave watching Randi — who just decided nobody gets to see her — turn around and *offer Vee up to the man who sees.* Guess from here: Randi's wound (being seen is unsurvivable) is the engine of the whole game; she'll give Pace the girl she can't let herself be. I want to know what Pace knows about that plan, and whether his tenderness survives contact with it.
