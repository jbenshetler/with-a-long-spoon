## Reader reaction

# Felt read

Well. That was not what the jacket copy set me up for, and I mean that as praise. I came in expecting the "girl picked out across the quad" — Vivienne — and instead the book opens on the *other* woman entirely, the one the blurb calls Randi, and it opens on her being taken apart. That's a bold move for a first chapter, and it worked on me.

Let me start with the body, because the chapter demands it. The erotic charge is real and sustained — it peaks, for me, not in the spanking or the edging (though those are rendered with genuine heat) but in the slow apple-eating on his lap in the kitchen, "bite, kiss, bite," and then the passage where he runs his hands up her sides and "each time leaving again before it arrived." That withholding is the erotic engine of the whole chapter and the writer knows it. The prose is patient in a way that mirrors what he's doing to her, which is a nice congruence. It went slightly clinical for me only in a couple of the anatomical inventory beats near the bench — "the coral lips, the gloss... the sea in it" — where the cataloguing of her body pulled me a half-step out. But mostly it stayed warm rather than cold, which matters.

Now Pace. I find him magnetic and I do not entirely trust the *book's* relationship to him, which is a different thing. On the page he is almost impossibly attentive — the water bottle left in advance, the apple sliced thin cut-side-up because she eats faster that way, the safe word checked three times, "tell me anytime." The blurb told me a man who "asks before he takes and takes only what he gives," and here it is, delivered. He reads as a craftsman in every sense — he *built the bench* seven months ago and met her three weeks ago, which is a detail that landed like a small cold stone in my stomach. He built the instrument before he had the woman. That's the first thing that made me look at him sideways. He's not responding to her; he had the apparatus ready and was waiting for someone to fit it.

And the ending recontextualizes everything. The whole chapter he "sees" her, "finds" her, congratulates himself — "there you are, Randi," "he had taken her further than she had ever been taken." He's so sure of his reading. And then we cross into *her* head at the mirror and discover the seeing was a catastrophe for her, not a gift. "He could have the face. The one he had found was going back behind it, and she was not coming out again." That gap — his warm certainty that he's given her something beautiful, her private decision that being seen is unbearable and she will spend the rest of this relationship *lying* to him — is the most alive thing in the chapter. The tagline suddenly rings: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." She consented to all of it. And the consent is exactly what sprang shut on her.

Randi herself moved me most. The food policing, the mother who "lived on egg whites and tofu, a thing she said with a knife in it," the plain natural nails as her one refusal, the blue toenail polish as the "one small place she let herself step out of line" — this is a woman built entirely as surface, and the chapter knows precisely how expensive that surface was to maintain. The detail I can't shake is the water bottle cap: she presses the sharp inner edge under her thumbnail "where it would hurt" and "did not seem to know she was doing it." That's a whole interior life smuggled into one gesture, and it's the tell that "the face" costs her constantly.

Trust and suspicion: I have real unease, but it's aimed carefully. Pace has done nothing coercive on the page — he's scrupulous. But the book is quietly showing me that scrupulous attention can be its own kind of trap, that being perfectly *read* by someone can feel like invasion to a person who survives by not being read. The bench built before she existed is the thing that keeps him from being a pure romantic hero for me. He's collecting an experience of her and calling it love, and he doesn't know the woman went into hiding in his own bathroom. I don't think he's malicious. I think he's dangerous the way a very good, very sure person is dangerous.

Friction: very little. The one place the thumb pressed slightly was the mirror monologue, which is gorgeous but does risk *explaining* the thing the water-bottle-cap gesture had already shown me — "Nothing happened to her. That was what the face was for." It's a beautiful paragraph but it tips just past dramatized into thesis-stated. I mostly forgave it because the voice is so controlled.

**The title.** "The Bench" — innocuous going in, just furniture. By the end it's the whole argument of the chapter: the object he made before he knew her, disguised as a table (as she is disguised as "the face"), that reveals a purpose "she hadn't known was there." And the last paragraph literalizes it — top goes back on, it becomes "a low table of odd proportions again," she'll walk past and not look, "full of what it was... waiting for the next time." The bench *is* Randi: a made thing with a hidden purpose, passing as ordinary. The title didn't give anything away going in; it detonated on the way out.

**The book title / tagline.** "With a long spoon" — I know the proverb, *he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon.* So someone here is the devil, or someone thinks they're keeping a safe distance and won't be. Given the blurb's "game" that gets "real for the two who started it," I read the spoon as the doomed illusion of safe distance — you think you can dip in without being consumed. And this chapter just showed me both of them doing exactly that: he thinks he's the one in control who gives; she thinks she can hand over her body and keep her self. Both are wrong. The tagline's "trap" is the freely-given yes, and I now see the mechanism.

**What I want / dread next.** I assume the next movement introduces Vivienne — the actual subject of the blurb, the girl across the quad. And now I dread it a little, because I've just watched what "being chosen" by these two does to a person. *Guess from here:* Randi's decision to go permanently behind the face is going to shape how she recruits Vivienne — she'll do to the girl what she can't let Pace do to her. I want to see Pace with someone who *isn't* already this fortified, and I want to know whether he ever realizes Randi lied to him tonight. I'll keep reading; this hooked me hard.

**How I feel**
Absorbed and slightly uneasy in a good way. Drawn to Randi, ache for her. Pace is compelling and I don't fully trust the book's rosy view of him — the pre-built bench keeps me wary. High erotic charge that never fully divorced from feeling. Carrying the sense that the real drama is the invisible gap between what he thinks happened and what she decided — and that the tagline's "trap" is already sprung on Randi before the girl from the jacket has even appeared.
