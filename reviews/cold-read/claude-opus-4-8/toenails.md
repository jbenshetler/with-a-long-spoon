## Reader reaction

This is a short, quiet morning-after chapter and it lands like a held breath. After the warmth and dread of the sex, we're back in the dorm with Cassie, and the whole thing is about what Vee *edits out* while telling the story.

The image that got me — really got me — is the ladle: "She lifted it out clean as she went, the way a ladle takes soup from the pot and the surface closes without a hole." That's the reframe motif turned inward. She's doing to Cassie exactly what's been done to her: taking the true middle out and letting the surface close seamless. "So it stays," but in reverse — she keeps the good version by removing the part that can't be a story. And the line right after — "she told herself it was a kindness. It was a kindness. She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep" — that triple beat is the closest the book has come to naming the mechanism of self-deception she runs on herself. It's chilling precisely because she's happy.

The zipper moment is the best beat in the chapter and the most physically alive. She goes to shower, draws the zip halfway, and stops — because there's nothing underneath. She gave the panties away, and the next thing off her body "would not be a story she was editing. It would be on the floor, and there would be nothing left to say." Her own bareness has become evidence she can't narrate around. That's the kept-object motif biting from a new angle: what she gave away isn't gone, it's *present as an absence*, and it makes her flinch three feet from her closest friend. She zips back up. She can't even undress in her own room. That's the trap tightening and it's done entirely in body, no commentary.

Cassie breaks my heart a little here. "for half a second something crossed her face and was gone before it found a name" — she reads Vee whole and fast, catches something, and doesn't pull the seam. "He cooked. A man made you dinner." "Mm." And she lets it stand. Cassie is the one person who could see it, and she's being managed by Vee's own editing — and by her own decision not to push. "You need to get your brain back from your boyfriend. Said it kindly, certainly, wrong." That word *wrong* sitting there is the author trusting me to feel it. Cassie's the reader-surrogate who's just close enough and just too kind.

The toenails as the chapter's frame is lovely — Cassie clocks the frosted plum ("In October. I have eyes") as proof there was a man worth painting for, never knowing the plum was already hidden inside the flats before all this. The mother's word for it can come, "she found she didn't much mind it this morning." The shame-metronome is still overrun, still quiet.

Erotic charge: low and that's right — this is afterglow, not heat. What charge there is lives in the zipper stall and the memory running as "a second channel that had not shut off once. His hands. The white sheets. The thing her body had done before she could decide to let it." Clean, not clinical.

Friction: almost none. If I reach, the ladle-and-triple-kindness passage is the one spot where the prose *tells* me the psychology it's usually content to enact — but it's so well-made and it's the thesis of the chapter, so I'll allow it. Everywhere else the thumb is off the scale.

The title, "Toenails" — innocuous surface, exactly the house style. It's Cassie's tell, the visible evidence of a night, the thing you paint for someone. On the reread charge: the plum was hidden proof before, now it's on open display and *she doesn't mind the word for it* — the shame that used to police her is gone, and that's the frightening part dressed as the sweet part. The book title / tagline still hums under this: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is the morning after all the freely-given yeses, and the trap is showing as her inability to say the true thing even to Cassie, her flinch at her own bare skin. The long spoon of the series title is absent again — she's not keeping any distance; she's editing so she can stay close to the happiness.

What I want next: I want Randi back, I want to know if Cassie's half-second face means she smells something, and I dread the moment the editing stops working. Guess from here: the given-away green will resurface the way every made object does, and Vee's happiness is the deepest part of the hook.
