# Cold read — Sheri — First Ride

## Reader reaction

A new voice, a new bike, a new woman — and for once we're back inside Pace's head after chapters away from him, which I was hungry for. This is a small, cold, lovely chapter, and its whole job seems to be to catch Pace out.

**Sheri** — I like her immediately, and I don't quite trust the *ease* of her. She's blunt in a way that's a relief after all of Randi's silk. She "remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather," and the line about Vee — "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that" — landed as genuinely funny and genuinely a little chilling, because it's the same appraising eye the *house* turned on Vee, just worn as friendliness instead of strategy. She's been riding beside Pace for two years, faster than him on the climbs and never letting him forget it, and the physical portrait of her — small frame, bleached ponytail swinging, "the whole small engine of her standing on the pedals" — is affectionate and admiring in a way that reads almost like Pace's marveling at Randi's competence. I clocked "she could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years… and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything." So she's a person who *loves* like a verdict too. There's a family resemblance among the women in Pace's orbit: they all appraise, and their approval is worth everything.

**Pace** — this is the softest I've seen him, and he doesn't know it's showing. That's the hinge of the chapter. He starts telling Sheri about Vee — the walnut chair, the thumb down the grain of the joint he "spent a weekend on," the math book she didn't put back "like it had burned her" — and the tell is: *"He heard himself going and didn't stop."* And then Sheri, sideways, "Listen to you." He genuinely doesn't understand what there's to listen to. That's the jacket's promise arriving from the *other* side — "Both are falling as hard as she is." Here's the falling, and the man doing it can't hear his own voice change. I found that tender and a little alarming at once.

The big cold revelation, delivered so lightly I almost slid past it: **"What happened to the pale sorority chick?" / "That's not finished."** So Sheri knows about Randi ("the pale sorority chick") *and* now Vee, and treats "two of them at once" as arithmetic anybody could run. That confirms from Pace's side that Randi and Vee are both *his*, running concurrently, and that a third party outside the triangle can see the shape of it plainly — "If that's not already complicated, it will be." A flat, true little forecast. Sheri is the second clear-eyed outsider the book's given me (Cassie is the first), and unlike Cassie she isn't fooled and isn't alarmed — she just files it.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — Sheri feels *off* only in the sense that everyone close to Pace turns out to have an angle or a hunger; the text earned a mild wariness with "she could turn it on a person… and keep it burning for years." But she also might just be his one uncomplicated friend, the one relationship that "never once cost him anything." I'm holding both. What the chapter did land hard: Pace is not managing Vee coolly from above anymore. He's *gone* on her and doesn't know it. After 26 chapters of him being the one who reads everyone, being read himself — by Sheri, in one line — is the quiet earthquake here.

**Erotic charge** — none, and rightly. This is a cold-fingered, breath-over-the-shoulder chapter. Heat 0. The only body-heat is Sheri's admiration of her own bike-engine and the casual "hell of a backside," which is appraisal, not arousal.

**Friction** — almost none; it's tight and doesn't overexplain. The one place I felt the thumb, lightly, is "Two of them at once… If that's not already complicated, it will be." It's *close* to the book saying its own theme out loud through a bystander — but Sheri's the kind of person who'd actually say it, so I'll allow it. It's the least earned line only because it's the most on-the-nose.

**This chapter's title — "Sheri — First Ride."** The "Sheri —" prefix is new: this is the first chapter named for someone who isn't Vee/Randi/Pace's interior, tagged like an introduction. "First Ride" is literal (their Saturday rides, and my first time meeting her) and sits innocuous. On reread it'll recolor: *this is the first time we watch Pace be ridden* — caught out, his want visible, going and not stopping. And "ride" carries the sexual ghost the book always keeps in reserve. Nothing gave the chapter away.

**Book title / tagline** — "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter widens the trap to include *Pace*. He gave his own yes — to the game, to Vee — freely, and here's the moment his freely-given interest turns into something he can't hear himself confessing. The falling is mutual and none of the three can see the whole board; Sheri, outside it, sees the arithmetic and shrugs. "With a long spoon" still: someone's supping with the devil, and now I'm less sure who's holding the spoon and who's the meal.

**What I want / dread next** — I want to see the reunion Randi scheduled ("tomorrow night") actually happen, now that I know Pace is arriving to it *already fallen*, not coolly running a play. Guess from here: Sheri will matter later — she's the one person positioned to say the true thing to the wrong person, or to be turned on with that heat she can keep burning for years. And I dread the moment Vee learns "the pale sorority chick" and "Randi" are the same woman.
