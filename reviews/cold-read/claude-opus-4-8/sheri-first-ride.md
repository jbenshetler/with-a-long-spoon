## Reader reaction

This one lands quiet and clean, and I liked it more than I expected to for a chapter that's basically two people on bicycles.

First, the surprise of being inside Pace again — only the second time, and the last time was way back at the start. And what strikes me is how *unguarded* he is here in a way the curated interior of the earlier chapters never let me see. Not because the narration opens him up — it stays as withholding as ever, "He didn't know what there was to listen to. He said nothing" — but because someone who's known him for two years catches him being helpless. That's the whole event of the chapter, and it's a good one: **"Listen to you," she said.** He was past Vee's name "before he'd decided to be," telling Sheri about the thumb down the grain of the joint he'd spent a weekend on, the math book she didn't put back "like it had burned her" — "I heard myself going and didn't stop." For a man whose entire architecture is control and pre-warmed conditions, watching him spill without meaning to is the most naked he's been. And it warms me toward him again, which — per my own running distrust — is precisely the effect I've learned to be wary of. He melts, I melt, I check my pockets.

Sheri. New person, and immediately vivid. She "remarked on women the way other people remarked on the weather," she's faster than him up every climb and never lets it go unmentioned, small on a child-sized frame, bleached ponytail. I like her. She reads as genuinely his friend — not a plant, not part of any machine I can see, just a person he actually has an easy two-year thing with. And that's oddly moving: it's the first relationship of Pace's I've seen that has no apparent *use*. "It had never once cost him anything." After twenty-some chapters of everything-as-instrument, a costless friendship is almost startling.

But she's also the outside eye I've been starving for, and she says the thing. **"Two of them at once… If that's not already complicated, it will be."** That's the reader's own arithmetic, spoken by the one character positioned to see both women as *two women* — because Pace mentions Vee, and Sheri asks "what happened to the pale sorority chick?" and he says "That's not finished." So Sheri holds, for one line, the double picture that Vee herself still doesn't have: Randi (the pale sorority chick) and Vee, both live, both his. It gave me a small cold drop. Not because the text signals doom — it doesn't — but because someone finally named the shape out loud and Pace "didn't answer."

The detail that chilled me most, though, was **"You want me to not like her?"** — and the gloss: "She could turn it on a person inside a breath and keep it burning for years… and seen what it was worth on the other side, which was everything." Pace has, on tap, a friend who will destroy someone socially on his say-so. He declines — "No. She couldn't take your heat" — which is a lovely deflecting compliment, but the fact that the option was *offered and considered as real* tells me something about the world he moves in. That's the second person now (after Brooke's folder) who curates people around Vee.

There's also Sheri's report from Thursday — which I place as the night of the club fight (ch.25). She kept an eye on Vee after Pace left: "All she did was sit by herself, glaring at the floor, nursing the one drink. Danced with nobody. Left alone." So Pace *knows* how the night ended for Vee, secondhand, through a watcher he didn't have to ask. He left wounded and didn't text — but he got a report. That reframes his silence: it wasn't not-caring, and it wasn't not-knowing. He knew, and he waited, and now he's on a bike the next Saturday, spilling her name to a friend. "Hell of a backside on her, though. I'll give her that." — crude, and it's the one beat where the register goes locker-room, but it fits Sheri and Pace lets it pass the way he lets all her weather pass.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** no new suspicion of Sheri — she reads clean, a real friend, the text earned that. My suspicion is all structural and it's the *good* kind here: an outsider almost holds the whole picture. What unsettles me isn't anyone being off, it's that the machine has this much ambient infrastructure (a friend who'll burn a person on command, and did surveillance without being asked) sitting around Pace as ordinary Saturday texture.

**Erotic charge:** basically none, and correctly so — this is a cold-fingered road ride. Heat 0. The one gesture with any charge is Vee's thumb down the walnut joint, which is charged for *him*, retroactively — the seduction he's replaying is furniture and a math book, not skin.

**Friction:** almost none. The "hell of a backside" line is the only spot where I felt a small tonal jolt, and even that is character, not thumb-on-scale. If anything the chapter is so understated I could see a reader wanting more — but I didn't. The restraint is the point.

**What I want / dread next:** I want back to Vee's tomorrow-night reconciliation, which ch.26 loaded and cocked. This vignette is a held breath between the loading and the firing. *Guess from here:* Sheri's "it will be" complicated is a planted stone; she's the one outside eye who could someday see both women and say so to the wrong person — or to Vee. And I still don't know what the bench is for, and now I know Pace has a friend who does surveillance and social murder as favors. I dread the reconciliation and I dread the induction, in that order.
