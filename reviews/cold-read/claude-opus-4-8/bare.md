# Cold read — Bare

*scene: scenes/bare.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: under-the-rug*

## Reader reaction

This one hurt in a low, quiet, sustained way. It's tiny — one girl awake in a bunk in the cold — and it's the loneliest thing the book has given me yet. After the churn of "Under the Rug," where she manufactured "forgiven" and had all his body and none of his name, this is the settling of that. No sex, no Pace on the page, no Randi. Just the residue.

The whole chapter turns on the shirt, and it undoes the shirt for me. All this time the flannel/his shirt has been the tether — the thing that smelled of him, sawdust and salt of the work, the scent-dependence I've been tracking since ch.35. And here she takes it apart. First she wears it over her own shirt, held off her skin, "just a thing she wore, it hid what it was" — that line landed; the shirt as a public alibi, a thing that hides that she's "a girl too far gone in a boy." Then she strips both off and puts his straight onto her skin, "with nothing under it, the way she wore it when no one could see." And then the killing realization: **"it was warm because she was warm. He wasn't in it."** The heat she's been comforted by is her own body heat given back. That's the whole engine of the book compressed into a physical fact — she's been warmed by a thing she heated herself, believing him close, and "there was no one close." That's the provisioning motif turned inside out and handed to *her* to feel, finally, in her own skin. He warms the house; she warms the shirt; neither is him being there.

And the choice at the end wrecked me a little. She takes it off, and the cold is *worse* — "it was so much worse, and she did not put it back on." She chooses the worse cold over the false warmth. She stays bare in it. That's the first thing in a long while that reads to me as her refusing the comfort that lies to her. Not a grand gesture — she's not leaving him, she's not even texting him — but she won't be soothed by her own heat pretending to be his. The dropped shirt "not folded, not set back on the pillow, down in a heap where a dropped thing lands" — from the girl who covers, crops, files, folds everything that hurts, leaving it in a heap is enormous. She didn't file this one.

The phone beat before it is beautifully bleak: thumb over the empty field, "waited for the words / there weren't any." She knows now — has fully metabolized — that no text she sends "came back as the thing she needed," that everything would be "answered small and warm, and would leave her exactly here." That's her naming his precise-warm loophole to herself without naming it. She's stopped reaching for the last half-inch because she's learned the half-inch doesn't close.

Cassie is here, asleep, "slow and easy," the window cracked its two inches. I've been hungry for Cassie and she's present only as a sleeping body and that cracked window — the cold Cassie keeps open is literally the cold Vee finally lies down into, bare. That felt earned and quiet, not cute: the reality Cassie holds ajar is the cold Vee chooses at the end. The "might look and wouldn't have to say anything, the look enough on its own" — Vee's still managing what Cassie sees, still dressing for the roommate's eyes.

Trust/suspicion: nothing new to suspect — Pace and Randi are both offstage. What moved is *Vee*. She's the clearest she's been. No self-deception in this chapter; if anything it's the anti-"forgiven," the chapter where she won't rewrite the fact.

Erotic charge: near zero, and rightly. The undressing is naked but not sexual — it's the opposite, a stripping *away* of the erotic comfort. Heat 0, and the chapter's better for it.

Friction: almost none. It's very repetitive by design — "it always worked / it was warm because she was warm / it was worse / so much worse" — the incantatory loop could tip into overwritten, and "the silence at the center where the word should be" is the closest the chapter comes to saying the quiet part out loud. I felt the thumb there, just slightly — that line explains what the shirt already proved. But it's one line and I forgave it.

What I want next: I want this to *cost* something forward. She's finally felt the false warmth as false — does she act, or does she re-fold it by morning and go back Friday? *Guess from here:* she goes back. This is a low point she absorbs, not a turn. But I also feel the confidante track loading behind exactly this loneliness — Randi's been offstage since ch.38, and "there's no name in her for it" is precisely the wound a naming-machine walks into. I dread that the next warm hand on this specific ache is Randi's.
