# Cold read — Bare

*scene: scenes/bare.md · read after: under-the-rug*

## Reader reaction

This is the shortest, most naked thing the book has given me, and it's the first time the shirt has failed her. That's the whole chapter, and it lands like a stone in water.

I've watched this flannel do its work since chapter 11 — the totem, the smell she rations, the ritual of re-wearing it, the thing she washes when it goes to nothing and makes him refill. It has *always worked*. The book tells me that flat out: "For a while it worked. It always worked." So when it stops working here, I feel it in my body before I understand it, because I've been trained by the whole book to trust that shirt as her one reliable comfort. And the mechanism of the failure is devastatingly simple and I completely buy it: **"it was warm because she was warm. That was the thing she couldn't stop feeling once she'd felt it — the heat in the shirt was her own, given back. He wasn't in it."** That's the whole trap in one image. The devotion is a refillable consumable, yes — but the warmth in it was never his. It's her own heat handed back to her. She's been keeping herself company with her own body temperature and calling it him.

The escalation is small and merciless. First the shirt *over* her sleep shirt — "it was just a thing she wore. It hid what it was." Then she strips both off and puts his back on with nothing under it, cloth straight on skin, the way she wears it when no one can see. And *that* fails too. And then — and this is the line that got me — she takes it off entirely and lies bare in the cold that is *worse* than the shirt, "and she did not put it back on... and stayed in it." She chooses the worse thing. She chooses the cold over the lie of the warmth. That's new. That's a woman stopping herself from being comforted because she's finally caught the comfort in the act of being false.

And it rhymes so hard with Cassie's cracked window — the two inches of cold Cassie keeps every night to sleep in, and Vee up top not sleeping, and now Vee choosing the same cold, bare, on purpose. All this time the draft has been Cassie's thing, the anti-Randi cold, the honest air. Tonight Vee joins her in it. Cassie breathing slow and easy below, asleep, "and the window went grey." I love that Cassie is present just by breathing — the untroubled sleep of the person who wants nothing, right under the one who wants everything and can't name it.

The phone beat is quietly brutal too. His name near the top, "where it stayed." Her thumb over the empty field, *waiting for the words* — and there aren't any. "You couldn't send a message and get out of a person the shape of what you were to them." Every message she can think of is small, and would come back "small and warm, and would leave her exactly here." She knows now that the warmth is the thing keeping her in place. She's naming the mechanism without quite naming it. That's the closest she's come to seeing it clearly, and she does it alone, in the dark, at 3am, and then puts the phone face-down.

The one detail I clocked hardest: at the end the shirt is left "in a heap where a dropped thing lands; not folded, not set back on the pillow." All book she's tended that shirt — worn it, washed it, re-scented it, rationed it. Leaving it in a heap is a small violence against the ritual. Something has shifted in her relationship to the totem. She hasn't decided anything, but her hands have.

**Trust and suspicion:** Nothing new to distrust here — this is pure Vee interior. But the chapter quietly hands me the strongest indictment of Pace yet, and it does it *through her body's own logic*, not through plot. "He was across town in his own bed, gone down easy as he always went, easy as a man with nothing grinding in him." She's finally imagining his side of the asymmetry: she's holding a smell in the dark and he's sleeping fine. Whether that's fair to him I can't know — but that *she's* thinking it is the movement. The not-saying, which I've been wondering whether it ever costs *him* — she now suspects it costs him nothing. That's a real turn in her, even if she buries it by morning like she buries everything.

**Erotic charge:** Almost none, and rightly. There's the nakedness, the cloth straight onto skin, but it's stripped of eros on purpose — the bareness here is exposure, not arousal. The one moment of sensual inventory ("the clean warm of him, and under that... the sawdust and the salt of the work") is the smell-catalog I know well, and it's deployed precisely so it can fail. Heat is not the point of this chapter and I didn't want it to be.

**Friction:** Very little. The prose is doing the incantatory-repetition thing hard — "It wasn't enough," "For a while it worked. It always worked," "the way she wore it when no one could see," "worse... so much worse" — and for the most part the repetition earns its keening quality; this is a 3am brain circling one wound. If I push, the middle paragraph ("You couldn't send a message and get out of a person...") edges close to over-explaining the phone beat, spelling out what the empty field already said. But it's her thought, and 3am thoughts do over-explain themselves, so I let it pass. Nothing here felt like a thumb on the scale; if anything it's the most restrained chapter in a while — no Randi, no scene, no dialogue, just a woman and a shirt and the cold.

**The title.** "Bare." On the page it's literal — she strips to nothing and stays that way. But it recolors immediately into the emotional register: bare of the comfort, bare of the name, bare of the illusion that the warmth was his. And there's a third turn — she's finally *seeing bare*, seeing the mechanism without the shirt's insulation. The chapter titles in this book tend to sit innocuous and then charge on reflection, and this one does exactly that: a plain word for taking your clothes off that turns out to mean stripping the last comfort off the wound. It didn't give anything away — it delivered.

**The book's title and tagline.** *With a Long Spoon* — supping with the devil, the trap you hold shut on yourself — has never felt more exact than tonight. Nobody did anything to her in this chapter. She's alone. And the trap does its work anyway, because the warmth she was fed has become her own warmth given back, and she can feel it and still can't leave it — she can only lie bare in the cold instead. "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." Every yes got her this: a smell in the dark that's just her own heat, a name she can't type, a man sleeping easy across town. The tagline points forward to a reckoning, and this chapter feels like the first night she can't sleep *through* the trap — she's awake inside it now.

**What I want and dread next.** I want to know if this survives the morning. This book's whole engine is that she gets close to the mechanism and then buries the question — the pile, the rug, "forgiven." So I half-expect chapter next to show her sweeping this back under, folding the shirt she left in a heap, and I'll grieve that. But something about leaving it in a heap, and choosing the worse cold on purpose, feels like it might *not* fully re-bury. *Guess from here:* this is the beginning of her stopping being able to un-know it. I dread the moment the game surfaces to her while she's this raw. And I keep waiting for the photo in Randi's hand and Randi's own buried shame to collide with Vee's — none of that is here, but it's all still loaded offstage. Mostly I just want to sit with her in the grey and the cold; this chapter earned that.
