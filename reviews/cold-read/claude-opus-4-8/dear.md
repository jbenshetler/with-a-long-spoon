## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This is the chapter where the irony gets almost unbearable to sit inside, and that's its whole power over me. I *know* — Randi handed Pace to Vee on a lawn, Randi came to his couch the next night asking him to "tell me," Randi wants to know if Vee will like the bench. So every warm thing Randi does here reads double, and the chapter makes me hold both layers at once. When Randi snaps her fingers and pretends she can't remember his name — "Paul?" — I actually went cold. She remembers his name. She tasted the wine of him talking about Vee. That little performed forgetting is the most chilling beat in the chapter precisely because it's so small and so unnecessary, which tells me how total the construction is.

And yet — and this is the ache — the thing Vee is receiving is *real to her*, and some of it may even be real. When Randi says "Vee's my dear friend" and takes her hand, cool and damp, on the white table in front of Brooke, and Vee feels it cost something — I couldn't tell whether it cost something because it was a genuine flare of protectiveness or because claiming a girl like Vee in front of Brooke genuinely does cost social capital and Randi spent it on the mission. The page leaves it ajar and I'm leaving it ajar. That ambiguity is the cruelest and best thing here. I don't think Randi is pure predator. I think she might be a woman who's good at this and is also, somewhere under it, actually warmed by Vee — and that doesn't make it less of a trap.

Vee breaks my heart. The whole opening with Cassie is just *joy* — the clumsy spin that nearly takes out the closet door, "Look at me," "Because of him" coming out instead of the dignified "I made an exception." She's a person being handed, for the first time, the sensation of being chosen, and she's defenseless to it. The detail that got me most: she keeps wanting to *put all of it into Randi's hands*. She hands Randi the kiss, the "may I choose," the going-soft-at-dinner, the no-bra joke — she's narrating the exact interior surrender that is being harvested. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." / "…Yeah." Randi isn't asking questions; she's taking inventory, same as Vee noticed Randi takes inventory. And Vee hands it over gladly because being asked *is* the gift.

Cassie is the one clean instrument in the book and she knows. "Goodbye, Vee" last chapter still rings; here it's "I'll see you at the dorm," easy, folding away whatever was in her eyes when Randi grabbed Vee's wrist. The state-troopers bit — "a man you met at a coffee shop" — is played as tease but it's the only true alarm anyone sounds, and it's about Pace, when the actual hunter is the girl with her arm warm against Vee's. Cassie reaching for the flaw, "How much did he give you to drink," is love. I trust her completely and I'm afraid she's going to lose Vee to this.

Swoon vs. dread: the chapter is engineered so the swoon and the dread are the *same feeling*. I did melt — the froyo recap, "your man," the trail Randi paints with the handing-up-over-rocks — and every time I melted I felt the trap close another notch. "Ask and then ravish," Randi says, low, at Vee's own volume, "half up the trail still, she felt her there in it beside her." That's the tell: Randi climbed into Vee's fantasy *with* her. The seduction of Vee isn't Pace's job alone; Randi is doing it in a smoothie shop with a bruise-colored drink. And Vee's "He'd ask, though. Before he ravished me," hands drifting down her thighs — she's aroused and doesn't clock that the woman beside her is the architect.

Trust/suspicion: I have no suspicion left to earn; I have *dread*, which is different. The text has already paid for it over the last four chapters. What's new and effective here is watching the machinery run on a completely blind target and finding Randi flawless at it — the forgot-his-name, the "catastrophe" date story that positions her as a fellow sufferer so Vee opens all the way, the "you couldn't not, because of him" that rewrites Vee's exception as helplessness. That last one unsettled me badly, because it's the exact move Pace pulled on the bench with Randi: narrate the surrender back to the person so they can't climb out of it.

Erotic charge is low and deliberately so — this is a recruitment chapter, not a bed chapter — but it isn't cold. It simmers under the smoothie table and up the imagined trail. The heat that's actually present is Vee's, unaware, being drawn out.

Friction: almost none. Maybe the Brooke encounter leans a half-step on the "finished women take her measure" beat we've now had twice (the sorority common room, here), but it earns it by giving Vee the eyebrows detail — drugstore tweezer, shared bathroom mirror — which locates her class position with one clean cut. The "wrong size for the room" is doing real work, not padding.

The title — "Dear." Just that word. It lands as *dear friend*, the phrase Randi spends on Vee in front of Brooke, the one that costs something and buys everything. But "Dear" also sits there like the opening of a letter — *Dear ___* — and the volume is called *A Polite Invitation*, and the series *With a Long Spoon*. "He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon." That proverb is the whole book's thesis sitting right on the cover, and reading it after this chapter chills me: somebody here is dining with something dangerous, and the spoon isn't long enough. "A Polite Invitation" — Pace's "May I," Randi's "I've decided," the invitation extended so graciously that saying yes feels like your own idea. The titles aren't oblique anymore. They've told me, politely, what's being done to Vee.

What I want/dread next: I want Cassie to be *right there* when it goes wrong. I dread the hike — "real shoes," the house off 614 past the gravel, the bench waiting under its tabletop. Guess from here: the trail ends at that house, and the thing Randi asked — "do you think she'll like the bench" — starts getting answered. I'm reading on with my stomach in a knot.
