# Cold read — Dear

*scene: scenes/dear.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: off-six-fourteen*

## Reader reaction

Randi's back. Three chapters away and she walks in already reaching for Vee's wrist, and my whole body did a small clench of dread the moment she took it, because I've been braced for exactly this. The chapter is almost unbearably comfortable — that's what scared me. It's warm and funny and I laughed with Vee at the lacrosse story, and the whole time some low part of me was going *no, no, don't.*

The thing that got me hardest, and I want to be honest about it: **the kiss**. In ch.9 with Cassie, Vee's proudest, most load-bearing claim was *I did it on purpose, I made an exception, I authored that.* It's the one thing that's hers. And here Randi takes it. "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." And Vee had "the dignified version sitting right there ready" — *I made an exception* — "and felt it leave her instead as the smaller, truer thing." Randi reframed the one act Vee owned into something that happened *to* her, and Vee handed it over grateful. That's the whole engine of the book doing its work in three lines, and I felt it as a small grief. She's giving away the exact thing she thinks she's keeping.

Randi's technique is right out in the open for me now and invisible to Vee, which is the awful part. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Said "slowly, holding it up to the light." She's not asking, she's *naming* — she takes the froyo/menu/kiss and hands it back to Vee cast as pleasurable surrender, and every time Vee says the smaller truer thing instead of the dignified version. The text told me exactly what she does: Randi did with the retelling what Pace did with "May I choose?" at the actual dinner. Two men, one hand.

And the cup. There it was — I've been tracking Randi's busy wordless hand for nine chapters, and here it's her hand closing over Vee's, "cool and a little damp from the iced cup," held on the white table "in plain view" and "in no hurry to let go." The cold-citrus smell in the goodbye hug. The unmoving finished surface. All the Randi-tells landed and none of them read as sinister to Vee — which is exactly what unsettles me, because they didn't fully read sinister to *me* either in the moment. I wanted the hand-hold to be sweet. It half was.

**Brooke.** God. She's back and she's worse in person than offstage. That slow circuit of the eyes — "her eyes already moving in a slow, easy circuit of the table. Of Vee." — and the smile that "took her measure, top to bottom, and stay[ed] warm the whole way." The predatory-warm I filed in ch.2 is confirmed in the body now. And she drops "We missed you Thursday" and suddenly there's a whole sorority week Vee's not part of, and Vee "went very slightly the wrong size for the room." That detail was devastatingly good. Being made small without a single unkind word.

But here's the move I only half-trust: Randi says **"Vee's my dear friend"** and takes her hand *in front of Brooke*, and the text works hard — maybe too hard — to make me feel it as costly generosity. "She did not know what it had cost Randi to say it... She knew only, without being able to say how, that it had cost something." I don't buy that it cost Randi anything. I think Randi spent nothing and let Vee believe it was priceless. The chapter frames it as Randi rescuing Vee from the wrong-size feeling — but Randi is the one who put her on the high stool "set up on a shelf," in a room full of matched girls where Vee "covered like an apology" in her pilled cardigan. Randi chose the venue that made Vee small and then played savior for making her feel picked. That's the coldest thing in the chapter and it's dressed as the warmest. The title is **Dear** and it detonates: *dear friend* / the *cost* of a thing / what Vee is *dearly* paying without seeing a bill.

**Cassie** in the opening is a quiet knife. Vee catches "the look go past her, to Randi... and by the time Cassie's eyes came back to Vee's, whatever had been in them was folded away." Cassie saw something and swallowed it. And then Vee actively excludes her — "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one... Not her thing" — and rationalizes it as *healthy, everyone has their own people,* while "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the saddest beat in the book so far for me. The anchor is being walked away from, gently, by Vee's own hand, and Vee narrates it as maturity. I dread Cassie's slow subtraction more than anything Pace could do.

Erotic charge: low by design, and that's fine — this is a confidante scene, not a Pace scene. The charge here is social, not sexual: the shelf, the measuring, the hand on the table. The one genuinely charged beat is the hand-hold, and it's charged with *dependency* more than eros — "a warmth that came with weight on it." That's the right kind of hook for this track.

Friction: only one place I felt the thumb. The insistence on the *cost* — "spent it without once looking at the price," "spent it on her without appearing to count it," and again "she had spent it without once looking at the price." Three passes at the same idea in one chapter. It's the book underlining *this is generosity* so heavily that I started to doubt the generosity — which may be intended, but it read as slightly overworked, the one spot where I felt steered rather than shown. Though I'll grant: an unmanaged reader might just feel it as warmth. On me it tipped.

What I want next: the hike. The house off 614. This chapter was the last calm on the confidante side — Randi has now taken the kiss, planted a place ("your place is here, next to Randi"), and started subtracting Cassie. *Guess from here:* the next Pace chapter is the house, and I think the two tracks are going to start bleeding toward each other. I dread it and I can't stop.
