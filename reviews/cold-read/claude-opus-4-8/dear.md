## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This chapter runs entirely on Randi and Vee, and it worked on me more than I expected from what looked like a debrief scene. It's the froyo-date-report-back over smoothies, and it's warm, and it's a slow tightening — I could feel Randi harvesting Vee's kiss out of her hand while making it feel like a gift Vee got to give. The moment I keep returning to: Vee has "the dignified version sitting right there ready" — *I made an exception* — and Randi's soft, near-tender "Because you couldn't not. Because of him" reaches in and pulls out "the smaller, truer thing." That's the whole seduction in miniature. Randi doesn't take; she makes Vee hand it over, exactly like Pace handing back the pencil, exactly like the bench. Watching it from where I sit — knowing they're a couple, knowing this is the plan — it's chilling *and* it's tender, and I can't fully separate the two, which is the ache the book keeps building on.

I feel most for Vee here, and most protective. The cardigan "covered her like an apology"; up on the high window chair she's "a thing the room could turn and look at" — the book keeps putting her body on a shelf for a room's eyes (the pool bleachers, now this) and I feel it in my own skin. But the beautiful, awful thing is how Randi *answers* it: the cool damp hand closing over Vee's on the white table, "Vee's my dear friend," said out loud in front of Brooke. Vee doesn't know what it cost, only that it cost something — and I don't fully know either, which I like. Because from my seat, the "cost" is partly performance, partly maybe real, and the not-knowing is where the whole book lives. Vee walks home "picked." I wanted that for her and dreaded it at once.

Randi I trust less and feel for more every chapter. Her "catastrophe" — the Friday date with the lacrosse bore who talked all night — reads to me as invented on the spot, a mirror-story built to make Pace's listening shine by contrast, and "a man who can't be bothered to listen to you across a dinner table, honey, is not going to bother taking care of you anywhere else" is a line she's steering Vee *toward Pace* with. She's running the froyo report like a woman collecting intelligence for the man she's sleeping with. And yet — the beet-wheatgrass-egg-white bruise-colored drink "the color of a bruise," ordered like a sentence handed down, is her own starving showing through the polish. She's cruel and hungry and boxed in behind the face, and I can't hate her.

The pull that made me sit up: that hand on the table did more erotic-adjacent work than any kiss in the chapter. "A warmth that came with weight on it," Vee thinks, and I felt the book finally letting the seeded Vee→Randi thing breathe a little — not named, but the sun-hot side of her face on the walk, "two small skies where her eyes should have been," Vee glad she'd said the good part "out loud" to *see it land* on Randi. Vee is performing for Randi's smile the way you perform for someone you want to please. The charge here isn't Pace. It's Randi's approval, and Vee doesn't have a word for how much she wants it. Neither, quite, does she want one.

Cassie's exit is the quiet bruise of the chapter. That look going *past* Vee to Randi and getting "folded away," the flat "I'll see you at the dorm," and then Vee doing the small betrayal — "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one… Not her thing" — and reassuring *herself* it's "healthy" while "underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That's the first time I've watched Vee actively edge Cassie out, and the book is honest that she chose not to look at it. It made me a little sick, in the good way. The wedge is Vee's own hand now, not just Randi's.

The unnamed taller one who "had not said a word," drank her coffee, watched, gave Vee "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — I don't know who she is (Brooke's companion, unnamed), and the page keeps her opaque on purpose. She unsettled me: appraisal without warmth, Vee "took her measure, top to bottom." Whether that's just sorority coolness or something that matters later, I can't tell, and I'll leave it ajar.

The friction: almost none, but Randi's monologue setup — "she set it up like a story she had told before and would tell again" — is the author gently tipping me that it's manufactured, and I half-wondered if that nudge was needed; I'd have caught the staging anyway. Minor. Otherwise the prose earns its length; the smoothie shop's "band of hot citrus near the ceiling" and the AC cold are doing real sensory work.

The titles: the chapter is titled **"Dear"** — and it lands on "Vee's my dear friend," the word Randi *spends* out loud. On second thought it recolors: *dear* as in beloved, and *dear* as in costly, what a thing costs — Randi's "dear friend" is expensive, and I don't yet know who's paying. It's also the opening of a letter, an address to someone — which sits oddly, like the book is writing *to* Vee. That flicker unsettles me pleasantly. The volume line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION**, title **Dear** — "long spoon" is the proverb, *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon*, and every chapter of feeding-kisses and handed-over choices makes that promise feel exactly aimed. "A polite invitation" is what this whole courtship *is* — the "May I," the smiling in, the picking-out-loud — politeness as the mechanism of the trap. No tagline was supplied beyond that. It points me toward a supper I already know is the devil's, and toward Vee saying yes to the invitation with her whole open face.

What I want next: the hiking date, Pace's house off 614 — and I dread and crave the moment the froyo intel Randi just gathered gets carried back to his warm living room. *Guess from here:* I think the "dear friend" hand on the table is the surfacing edge of the Vee→Randi pull, and I suspect Randi felt something real doing it. I want Cassie back in frame before Vee edges her out for good.
