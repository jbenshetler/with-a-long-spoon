## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

This one worked on me as pure swoon-with-a-cold-draft-under-it, and the draft kept not quite arriving. Mostly I just sat in Vee's happiness. The walk out into the clean Blacksburg light, "like a person things happened to. Good things" — I was right there with her, warm, rooting. And then the smoothie place pulls the light out of her in the doorway, the pilled cardigan "covered her like an apology" in a room of bare arms, and she gets set up on the high chair, "a thing the room could turn and look at." That image is so exact about her body-shame that I ached. I know this girl's reflexes now and the book keeps rendering them with real tenderness.

Vee telling the date is where the heat lives — not sexual exactly, but the heat of being *drawn out*. Watch what Randi does: she keeps catching the dignified version Vee has ready and lifting the truer, smaller one out of her. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." "Because you couldn't not. Because of him." Each time Vee means to say the composed thing and "felt it leave her instead as the smaller, truer thing." That's the froyo overflow all over again, except now a person is doing the unspooling, gently, and loving every inch she gets. The "ask and then ravish" line "came a beat late and low, the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume" — that landed on me. That's Randi's real want surfacing for a half-second inside the performance, and I felt it slip the leash. The checkpoint told me her want for Vee is real and growing; here I *watched* it flicker.

My feeling toward Randi moved — further into dread, but a tender dread. Because the "dear friend" beat is genuinely moving and I can't pretend it isn't. She takes Vee's hand on the white table in front of Brooke, holds it, "in no hurry to let go," and Vee feels it "cost something" without knowing what. I know what: Randi is spending some of her sorority capital, her face, in public, on Vee. It's the warmest, most generous thing she's done — and it's also exactly how you make someone yours. Both true at once, and the chapter refuses to collapse them, which is why it got me. "No one had chosen her out loud like that in a long time."

Vee: wholly, achingly on her side, more protective than ever. The small wrongness I clocked is her casually editing Cassie out — "it isn't really her place, the smoothie one," then "nobody ever called that anything but healthy," and "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." That turning-over is the book's honesty: Vee knows, a little, that she's choosing the bright matched world over her clear-eyed friend, and she declines to look. That's the first thing all chapter I felt as a genuine loss. Cassie barely appears — "I'll see you at the dorm," easy, already going, the folded-away look — and her absence is the point. The protector is being quietly walked away from.

Brooke and the tall silent woman unsettled me cleanly, and I think the page earned it. Brooke is "only friendly" and still "set down on the table the fact of a Thursday Vee knew nothing about" — the private bright week Vee's no part of. And the tall one never speaks, drinks her coffee, runs "a slow, easy circuit" of Vee, "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over." The page pointedly does not name her, so I won't — but being *appraised* like merchandise, right after a checkpoint full of a pointing-game where women are selected, gave me a genuine chill. I don't know if she's anyone. I'm holding it open.

**Swoon vs. suspicion:** both ran high and that's the chapter's trick. The swoon is real — the hand on the table, "your man," "picked." The suspicion is that every bit of the swoon is also recruitment. I didn't manufacture the shadow; Randi's "I'm going to have to stop taking you out in public" lands as affection *and* as the small print of ownership.

**Erotic charge:** mostly simmer, peaking in the "ask and then ravish" exchange and in Vee's hands "running slow down the tops of her thighs and back" as she lets the trail-and-ravish picture come and stay. That little involuntary self-touch in a loud bright room was the hottest beat — want leaking out past her guard.

**Friction:** almost none. The "two small skies where her eyes should have been" is a touch much for Randi's eyes, slightly purple. Otherwise the thumb stayed off the scale — the chapter trusts me to feel the engineered underside without narrating it.

**The titles.** "Dear" — I love this one. It's "Vee's my *dear* friend," the word Randi spends; but dear also means *costly*, and the text literally says it "cost something." So the chapter title names the beloved and the price in one syllable. It illuminates rather than gives away. The series line, "With a Long Spoon," I read as the proverb — he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon — and with all this food and appetite and supping, that's a flat-out warning to me: Vee is dining with something dangerous. "A Polite Invitation" seals it: the danger arrives courteous, with a "May I." No tagline supplied that I can see. The promise is clear and I believe it.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the hike — "off six-fourteen, past the gravel," and I keep remembering she's never seen his house, and the house is where the bench is. *Guess from here:* the hike, or whatever follows, takes her toward that house and that hidden world, and the "dear friend" hand on the table today is the deposit. I dread the moment Cassie's being-left-behind stops being small. I'm pulled hard to keep reading.
