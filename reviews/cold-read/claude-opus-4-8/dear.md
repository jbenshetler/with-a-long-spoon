## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This is the quietest chapter in a while — no bench, no candlelit dinner, just two girls and smoothies — and it's the one that scared me most, precisely because nothing "happens." It's the seduction of Vee *by Randi*, run in broad daylight over a bruise-colored drink, and I watched it work end to end.

The thing I can't shake: Vee handed the whole dinner over to Randi. Not just told it — *handed* it. The chapter names it for me — "how much she wanted to put all of it into Randi's hands" — and that echo of the dinner ("I just handed it over. I let him choose") landed like a bell. Vee gave Pace the menu; now she gives Randi the story. Same gesture, two hunters, and she narrates it as the best thing that ever happened to her. And Randi does the exact move Pace does: she doesn't push, she *lets her have it and goes no further* — "'Mm,' said Randi, warm, and let her have it... and Vee felt the letting-go as one more kindness." The restraint IS the tool. I've watched Pace do this with the pencil and the "may I"; now Randi does it with a story. They have the same hands.

And the worst little turn — Randi draws out the *true* confession by offering the tender reading. Vee "had the dignified version sitting right there ready" — *I made an exception* — and Randi says "Because you couldn't not. Because of him," soft and near-tender, and the dignified version dissolves and the smaller truer thing comes out instead. The jacket promised someone who "draws out the shames she was raised to bury and hands them back as gifts." I just watched it happen sentence by sentence. It's beautifully done and it made me sick a little.

**Randi.** My dread deepened but so did — annoyingly — my warmth, which is the trap of her. The "dear friend" moment with Brooke watching: Randi takes Vee's cold-damp hand, holds it in plain view, spends *something* Vee can feel cost her, and doesn't count it. Vee reads it as being chosen out loud. And here's what unsettles me most — I *don't know* whether it cost Randi anything. Vee is sure it did. Maybe it did. Maybe Randi's the kind of operator who lets the mark feel a price was paid because that's what closes the deal. My open question — is her warmth real or performed or both — did not resolve; it got *tighter*. That "you little minx," the swat, the "Love you, girl" hug with the flat hand between the shoulder blades — I want it to be real, and wanting it to be real is exactly Vee's position, and that terrifies me.

**Vee.** Total sympathy, total dread. The cardigan "covered her like an apology" again — that image is now load-bearing, and it broke my heart to see it in this specific room full of matched girls. She gets set up on the high shelf *again* ("she had been set up on a shelf... a thing the room could turn and look at"), doesn't like it, "didn't know what to do with not liking it, so she leaned in toward Randi and decided to be happy." That sentence is the whole book to me right now: the discomfort registers, she has no framework for it, so she overrides it and calls the override happiness. She ends "picked." She carried it the whole way home. I believe her joy completely and I'm bracing.

**Cassie.** One small devastating beat at the open. Vee catches Cassie's look "go past her, to Randi... and by the time Cassie's eyes came back to Vee's, whatever had been in them was folded away. 'I'll see you at the dorm.'" Cassie sees Randi, folds something away, leaves. And then Vee — Vee! — is the one who excludes Cassie, saying the smoothie place "isn't really her place... Not her thing," and does the little hand-motion at "the whole bright sorority of it." That's new and it hurt: the wedge is being driven and *Vee's holding the hammer now*. "Underneath it something turned over, small, and she let it lie." She's starting to sort her own people into who fits Randi's world. That's a cost the chapter let me see and Vee didn't.

**Brooke and the silent older woman.** Brooke I remember from the Standards interrogation — all-smiles, meaning-nothing, "we should all get dinner sometime," plainly the end of it. Fine. But the *older one* — unhurried, coffee in hand, "her eyes already moving in a slow, easy circuit of the table. Of Vee," takes Vee's "measure, top to bottom, and stay[s] warm the whole way," says not one word, gives "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over." That's the word — *unhurried* — that's been tagged to the hunt all book ("the long way," Pace's patience). A woman appraising Vee like merchandise and saying nothing. I have no idea who she is and the text gave me no name, but she read like an *assessor*, and the hair on my neck went up. Guess from here: either she's just a sorority elder Vee's projecting onto, or she's someone in on more than Vee knows. The text earned the unease; it didn't earn a conclusion.

**Erotic charge.** Almost none, deliberately — heat's not the point here. The charge is all in the *retelling* of "may I choose," which lands with more weight secondhand than some sex scenes would, and in the hand on the table. Romantic/intimate charge between the two women is high and genuinely seductive. It's working on me the way it's working on Vee, which is the discomfort.

**Friction.** Very little. The prose is doing the thing it does — occasionally I feel the author's palm hovering ("the way you stay aware of deep water for a while after you are back in the boat" is lovely but it's the second water-metaphor for Vee's caution and I clocked it). The clearest near-thumb is how legibly the parallel is drawn between Randi's "let her have it and went no further" and Pace's method — but honestly it's Vee who's blind, not the prose that's shouting, so I'll allow it. The one line I'll flag as slightly too-neat is the closing "She felt picked. She carried it the whole way home," which is the exact beat that ended ch.9 ("insisting SHE chose") — the picked/chose refrain is starting to repeat in shape.

**The title.** "Dear." Innocuous going in — a term of endearment. Coming out: it's the exact word Randi deployed — "Vee's my dear friend" — the coin she "spent without counting." So the title is the transaction. And "dear" has the other meaning riding under it: *costly.* Something dearly bought, sold at a dear price. That's the reread-charge — the title names the affection and prices it in the same breath. Given the book's title, *With a Long Spoon* (you sup with the devil with a long spoon), and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — this chapter is Vee freely, gladly, handing over the story, the truth about the kiss, her place, her people. Every yes was hers. The spoon is Randi's warmth held out across the table, and Vee's leaning right down the length of it.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to know who the silent woman was — that's the sharpest new hook. I dread the hike, and I dread the moment Vee's two worlds touch, which this book keeps almost-doing and pulling back from. Guess from here: the "dinner sometime" Brooke tossed off, or that appraising woman, is a thread that comes back. And I keep waiting for Randi to slip — to have a moment alone where the mask drops the way it did in ch.2 — because the jacket says she's falling too, and this chapter gave me a warmth I half-believed. If Randi is starting to *mean* the "dear," the trap closes on her as hard as on Vee.
