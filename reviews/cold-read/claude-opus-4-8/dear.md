## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

This is the smoothie-shop scene and it works on me exactly the way the froyo and dinner chapters did — I go soft against my own better judgment. Vee glowing on her high chair, feet not reaching the floor, spilling the whole dinner into Randi's hands because she *wants* to put it there — "how much she wanted to put all of it into Randi's hands" — that's the tell of the chapter, and it landed on me before I could get clothes on it, to borrow Vee's own phrase for her laugh. I'm charmed. I'm also quietly sick, because I know who Randi's "catastrophe" date really is, and the reader gets a joke Vee can't: Randi inventing a self-absorbed lacrosse bro to mirror-flatter Pace, then extracting the confession about handing over control at dinner. "You let him choose for you. And you liked it." Randi holding it up to the light, going no further, letting Vee have it — I felt the trap in the gentleness. She's not just gossiping; she's taking inventory of exactly the thing the bench is built for.

The moment that got me most was "*dear friend*" and the cool damp hand on the white table. Because I believe it costs Randi something — the checkpoint tells me her want for Vee is real and growing, and here she spends it in public, in front of Brooke, claiming Vee out loud in a room where Vee has gone "the wrong size." And Vee reads the cost without being able to name it: "no one had chosen her out loud like that in a long time." That's the swoon and the ache at once. I wanted her to have it. I also know it's another thread in the net. The two things don't cancel; they press.

Trust vs. suspicion: nothing *new* went off, but the room did the book's work for it. The Smoothie Bird full of "girls who had all gotten dressed in the same room," the cardigan that "covered her like an apology," Vee set up on a shelf "a thing the room could turn and look at" — that's the body-as-problem motif doing real, unshowy damage, and it made me protective. Brooke's "we should all get dinner sometime," which "was lovely, and meant nothing" — beautifully exact. And the *taller one who never speaks*, who runs "a slow, easy circuit" of Vee and "the look you turn on a thing once you are done turning it over" — she unsettled me more than anyone, precisely because she's unnamed and appraising. I don't know if she's part of anything. The page didn't earn a specific suspicion, but it earned my skin crawling a little.

Erotic charge: low and late, but real when it comes. The "no bra… I'd black my own eye" bit is warm, funny, bodily — the two of them laughing at Vee's chest, easy. Then the actual peak: Vee's hands going to her thighs, "running slow down the tops of them and back," letting the ravished-against-a-tree picture come back and staying in it, her voice dropping — "*Before he ravished me.*" And Randi meeting her there: "Ask and then ravish," "the first thing she had said all day at Vee's own volume." That drop into a shared register, two women half up the same imagined trail together, is the hottest thing in the chapter and it's not even about Pace anymore — it's the seeded Vee→Randi pull surfacing at the edge of the page. That's what I've been waiting for since the jacket flagged it.

Friction: almost none. Occasionally Randi's dialogue is a touch too on-the-nose as instrumentation — "You're going hiking. With your man" is the kind of line where I can feel the author underlining the *your man* she wants to plant. But it's inside Randi's performed-warmth register, so it mostly reads as character, not thumb. The chapter is short and knows its one move.

The titles: this chapter is called **"Dear."** After reading, the whole thing points at "Vee's my *dear* friend" — the word Randi spends in public, the hinge the scene turns on. And it recolors: *dear* as beloved, and *dear* as costly, expensive, a thing that costs. Randi spent something dear. Vee is being made dear — precious and priced at once. That doubling feels deliberate and it sharpened the scene retroactively. The volume/cover board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — the "long spoon" is the proverb: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* Somebody at this table is the devil, and the spoon is the courtesy, the "May I," the polite distance the predators keep. "A polite invitation" is exactly what this chapter is — an invitation extended so warmly you can't see it's an invitation *into* something. It promises the manners will stay immaculate right up to the harm.

What I want next: the hiking date, obviously — Vee alone with Pace on a trail, the "ask and then ravish" she's already rehearsing. But I'm more hungry now for the Vee/Randi line, which just showed its face. *Guess from here:* the "dear friend" hand-holding and the shared-volume moment are the front edge of Randi's own want breaking cover, and I dread the collision of it with the game she's running. I keep waiting for Cassie — folded away and gone up the aisle with her cap down — to be the one who pays or the one who sees.
