## Reader reaction

No Pace, no bed, no woods — a cold lecture hall and a glass study room, and it's the best chapter for Randi as a *person* the book has given me. The setup is quiet: a stats project on PPP loans, the three-since-September trio, Cassie wanting to prove the money never reached workers ("My dad got laid off in 2020," flat, "no handle left out for anyone to pick it up by" — that line gutted me), Randi certain the story's more complicated because she "grew up inside the thing." And then the outlier point, up and to the right, alone in the white, tags *MIRH*, opens to **Miranda Holdings, LLC** — Randi's own family, the worst point in the county, several loans, kept the money, cut the jobs anyway.

The break here reads *real* in a way the last one only might have. "Cheating bastards" — "nothing practiced in them, nothing worn smooth with use... aimed too near to be meant for strangers." The color gone out of her face, the breath "with work in it." Vee, our sharpest reader, reads it as genuine and I believe her. This is the strongest evidence yet that Randi is a wounded person and not only an apparatus — her cultivated privilege is real, and its foundation is something she's ashamed of and maybe didn't fully know. It doesn't cancel the closer-work; it sits underneath it, and I liked being unable to square them.

But the thing that moved me most is **Vee**. Her room-reading — the gift she apologized to Pace for having — comes back online and she uses it as pure kindness: sees Randi leave and return "the same way," gives her "somewhere to put her hands," keeps her in the logistics, doesn't say the name, doesn't tell her then or after. For once Vee holds power in this friendship — she saw something Randi doesn't know she saw — and she spends it entirely on protecting Randi. That's the cleanest, most sovereign thing Vee's done in ages, and it happens *away* from both her handlers, in a room with a door that latched and no arranged music playing. I felt the friendship as something Vee genuinely gives, whatever it is on the other end.

The title lands hard. "The Outlier" already named Vee's condition to her face once; now the outlier is Randi's shame made visible, "invisible if you didn't" know to look. And the class fault line — Cassie's laid-off father on the right, Randi's loans on the left, Vee between them loving both, sensing "the room had stepped somewhere she couldn't follow" — gives the whole trio a real ground it didn't have.

Heat zero, and right to be. This one worked as character, not charge. My wariness about Randi is genuinely complicated in the good direction now.

**How I feel — overall**
Warm, and for once the warmth is on firmer ground. This is the chapter that made me believe Randi is a person with a real wound and not only a device — because the break happened where nothing was staged, unseen by design, over data she couldn't have arranged, and the book's own sharpest reader (Vee) read it as true. And Vee herself came back to life: competent, perceptive, quietly powerful, spending the one advantage she's ever held over Randi entirely on shielding her. I still know what I know about the debt, the nude, the operation, the narrowing — none of that is undone. But this hour ran the other direction: Vee gave, freely and well, in a room no one arranged, and I let myself feel the friendship as something real from her side. My unease didn't vanish; it got more complicated, which is the direction I trust. I ache for both of them now, not just for Vee.
