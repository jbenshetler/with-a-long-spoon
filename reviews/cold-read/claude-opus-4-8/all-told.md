# Cold read — All Told

## Reader reaction

This is a quiet, almost plotless chapter — a stats lecture, three girls in a row of seats — and yet it's one of the more chilling ones I've read, precisely because it doesn't try to be.

**Vee** is at her most beatifically happy: late to everything, "underslept and overfull," the good soreness of the night keeping her body's ledger from eight a.m. on. The image of her own untouched bed as "a tattletale in the cold morning light for anyone who cared to read it" is lovely and exposed at once — she's a woman whose body and whose belongings keep announcing what she won't say. I felt the same tenderness and dread I've been carrying. The detail that she still "hadn't found the word for what she'd seen in the glass" — that's her being genuinely changed, and I ache for how open she is.

**Cassie** owns this chapter for me and I trust her more than ever. She never looks at the professor once. "Cassie did not care about the dress… because the friend was the only part of any of it Cassie had ever been able to see." That's the whole moral floor of the book in one line — Cassie watches *the person*, not the display. Her flat, preamble-free "Your bed's made" and the eyebrow "kept in reserve" are perfect. When something "eased in Cassie's face" at Vee's second, unplanned "Oh, yes" — Cassie relaxing only once she's confirmed Vee is actually happy — I found that genuinely moving. Cassie is love without a plan.

**Randi** — and here the text did its cold work on me without a single overt tell. She's warm, delighted, "the whole warm weight of her attention on, like a lamp finding you." And then the two beats that made my skin prickle, both earned by the page and not telegraphed: first, "already turning the dress over in her hands without a hand on it" — she's *working* even while playing. Second, and this is the one, the shoe date. "That's not a problem, that's a *date.* You and me, Saturday… Don't you dare tell me no." Vee feels "grateful and warmed." I felt Randi converting Vee's one snag of vulnerability into another engineered afternoon alone. And the deadpan counterweight — "Cassie had never once in her life cared what was on anyone's feet" — let me feel the contrast without the author naming it. That's the double register working on me exactly as designed: warm on first pass, cold when I hold it next to Cassie.

**The Simpson's paradox lecture** is the loudest the class-mirror has ever been, and I noticed it — but I don't think it overplayed. "A thing can run one way across every short stretch you measure and the opposite way across the whole of it, and the total will never once tell you that's what happened. The total will hand you the exact reverse of the truth and not lie about a single number." Reading that with Randi's arm on Vee's wrist — I understood it as the book describing itself, and describing Randi. Every warm short stretch; the reverse across the whole; no single number a lie. It's on the edge of too on-the-nose, but it's saved by Vee doing the perfect naive thing: she writes *short stretches can lie against the whole*, underlines it "because it had the ring of an exam question, and gave it no further thought." That gap — she copies down the key to her own life and files it as trivia — is the chapter's real knife.

**Erotic charge:** low and deliberately so, and that's right for the morning-after register. The one live wire is the body-ledger — "a good soreness no gym had ever given her, down low, and up the inside of both thighs, and in her nipples where they sat against her bra… she was sure the whole room could read it off her." "Slut" landing "lower than Randi meant it to." That's Vee's shame/heat still fused even in a lecture hall. Effective, not clinical.

**Friction:** almost none. My only hesitation is the paradox monologue being *twice* stated (the professor lands it, then re-lands it at the reversal). The second pass — "Nobody touched one of them" — risks nudging me. But it's kept in the professor's cereal-box voice and Vee ignores it, so the thumb stayed light.

**What I want / dread next:** The dress is still "shown to not one living soul" — the last line rings like a held breath, and the induction is where the room turns. And now there's a *Saturday* with Randi, alone, buying shoes. I dread that afternoon a little — it's another harvest disguised as friendship. *Guess from here:* the shoe date is going to be another confidante-track escalation, verbal and maybe physical. And I keep waiting for Cassie and Randi to be in enough rooms together that Cassie's clear sight finally catches something off about Randi — this chapter put them shoulder to shoulder for the first time I remember, and Cassie is the one person who watches the friend instead of the show.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Morning after the finished-dress night: Vee leaves Pace's at eight, drifts late through her Friday classes, glowing. In stats class she sits between Cassie and Randi. The professor lectures on Simpson's paradox — how every short stretch can run one way while the whole runs the exact reverse, with no single number lying. Vee half-listens, copies "short stretches can lie against the whole" as exam trivia, and gives it no further thought. Cassie, who never looks at the front, has been reading Vee the whole time (spotted the unslept bed, cares only whether the night was *good*, not about the dress). Randi plays the delighted confidante, calls Vee a lucky slut, and — when Vee admits she has no shoes right for the dress — converts it into a Saturday shoe-shopping "date," just the two of them. The dress stays zipped in a closet, shown to no one yet.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — blissful, underslept, body keeping the night's ledger; shame/heat still fused; so open she copies the key to her own situation down as a throwaway note. Now has a Saturday alone with Randi coming.
- **Pace** — offstage this chapter; the maker of the dress and the night. Still tender-and-instrumental, secretly Randi's co-orchestrator.
- **Randi** — Pace's secret co-orchestrator posing as confidante. Warm as a lamp; "turning the dress over in her hands without a hand on it"; turned Vee's one vulnerability into another engineered afternoon. Text earned my coldness again without a single overt tell.
- **Cassie** — moral floor, the person I trust most. Watches the friend, not the display; doesn't care about the dress or the shoes, only whether Vee is truly happy. First time I've felt her and Randi set side by side as opposites.

**How I feel.** Golden-on-top, cold-underneath, more than ever. Deep affection and mounting dread for Vee; Cassie is the warmth I trust; Randi's warmth reads as harvest. The Simpson's-paradox line ("the total will hand you the exact reverse of the truth and not lie about a single number") is now my frame for the whole book.

**Open questions.** What happens on the Randi shoe date Saturday — another confidante-track escalation? When does Vee learn Pace and Randi know each other? Will Cassie, the one who watches the person, eventually catch something off about Randi? The induction (where the room turns) still looms, dress still shown to no one. Still waiting on "Peter," the scar, the "parents" wound.
