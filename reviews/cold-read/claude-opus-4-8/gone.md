# Cold read — Gone

*scene: scenes/gone.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: sorority*

## Reader reaction

So this is the chapter where the book pulls the curtain all the way back — and instead of feeling triumphant about it, I feel sick and tender at once. We're finally inside Pace's warm house, but the POV isn't Vee's, it's the two of them, Pace and Randi, spent and tangled after sex, Sade going low down the hall. The affair I've been carrying as reader-knowledge is now just *there*, on the page, undeniable: they are lovers, and Vee is what they're talking about in bed.

And here's the thing that gutted me — it doesn't play as gotcha-villainy. It plays as two people who have genuinely fallen for the mark, in their different ways, and it's worse for that.

**Randi** breaks open here more than she has anywhere. "I didn't think I'd like her this much... I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come. She got past me before I could help it." The harvester's own defense breached. And the retold shoe-store story — the bigfoot stomp, the older couple watching, Vee going red — is told with real delight, and then: "That was kind of hot. She was *so* embarrassed." That's the leak I've been holding open, and it just widened. Randi is *attracted to Vee*, and not for the sale — nobody's watching her in Pace's bed.

Then the seam I've been tracking as her armor showed a third and deepest time. When Pace glows — "She comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp... she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her" — Randi "went still... A different one, the kind that costs something to hold... the faintest tightening of her against him." Quiet a beat too long. Then: "Yeah. She is." And she *redirects it into sex.* That's jealousy. Whether she's jealous *of* Vee having Pace's tenderness or jealous of Pace *for getting* Vee — the page holds both, and I think it might be both at once. She's watching the man she's running this with fall for the girl, and it costs her something to sit still under it.

**Pace** — this is the first time I've been inside him, and it complicates my whole reading. He's not the offstage spider I half-built. He's tender and, disturbingly, *insistent on Vee's agency*: "I want her to get there on her own." "You'll want to take your time." And the wound-reading: "She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" — he *sees* the mother-voice, the cardigan, the real thing under it — "And you're showing her she doesn't have to be." That's the con framed, by its author, as liberation. He genuinely believes he's freeing her. That's the most frightening tenderness in the book so far, because it's not cynical. He means it. The instrument and the care are the exact same hand.

The most charged moment isn't the sex — it's when Randi draws her own body down the length of his to *show him* what Vee's got under the cardigan, going hoarse, "It's *criminal*, Pace," and what goes through him "was not the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi, who kept a hand on every ounce of herself out in the world, wanting something out loud in his bed and not able to stop it." That's the marriage-seam again — him reading her past her own performance. The gap between the self she gives him and the body he answers is narrower than she thinks, and I felt it close a little further here.

What's the con actually *for*? "She's not ready, though. She'll get there." Ready for *what*? Between this and last chapter's "practically a marriage" and "we can share," I'm now reading it as: they want to bring Vee *in* — into their bed, their arrangement — engineered so completely that Vee arrives there certain it was her own desire. The debt, the dress, the induction — all the runway toward a "yes" she'll believe she authored. That recolors the whole thing. It may not be about ruining her so much as *having* her, both of them, with her consent manufactured to feel like appetite. Which is, if anything, a deeper violation. But the page keeps the ultimate shape ajar; I'm guessing from here.

**Trust/suspicion:** this is the confirmation chapter — no ambiguity left that it's coordinated. But the warmth is *real* too, and that's the book's whole cruel argument: the affection and the instrument are indistinguishable because they're the same feeling. I can't hate either of them cleanly, and that's the trap sprung on *me* now.

**Title — "Gone":** Vee is "so gone over you... She's got it *bad*." Gone as in lost, caught, fallen. But also Randi went somewhere in that stillness — gone quiet, gone somewhere it costs to hold. And Vee is *literally* gone from her own book this chapter — absent from her POV while the two who own her lie in bed appraising her. Oblique and clean; it didn't give anything away, it just names the fall from the outside.

**What I want/dread next:** I want back to Vee, and I dread it, because now I've heard the room she can't hear. I want to know what "ready" means, and I'm afraid I'm right about it. And I'm watching Randi now as much as Vee — because Randi's the one who "got past" her own defense, and a closer who's genuinely fallen for the mark, and quietly jealous of her partner, is the loose thread that could burn the whole thing down. *(Guess from here.)*

**Cast present (in person):** Pace, Randi. Mentioned-only: Vee.

**Heat:** 2 — post-coital languor into a second round; charged, but shadowed by performance and the appraisal running through it; the real spike is interior, not the sex.

**Romance:** 2 — genuine tenderness between Pace and Randi (his reading her past her armor; her undefended kiss), and Pace's tenderness *toward absent Vee* — but it's tenderness-as-instrument, so it aches sideways.

**Characterization:** Everyone deepened. Pace went from offstage architect to a tender, wound-reading man who believes he's liberating her — the richest and most disturbing turn. Randi cracked open — attracted to Vee, breached in her own boredom-defense, jealous of Pace's regard; the most human she's been. Nobody flattened.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Excellent control; the pillow-talk earns every line, the seam lands mid-scene without a flag, and the slide back into sex is the right evasion for Randi. No drag.

**Pace — chapter to chapter:** A deliberate, powerful pivot — first non-Vee POV, first inside-the-con chapter. Momentum shifts from tightening-the-trap to complicating-the-trappers. Builds hard by widening, not by advancing plot.
