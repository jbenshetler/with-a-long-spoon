# Cold read — Gone

*scene: scenes/gone.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: sorority*

## Reader reaction

So there it is. The room I've been dreading. For chapters I've *known* — as the reader who saw the seams — that Pace and Randi were one hand, that the tutoring was staged, that the friendship was the operation. But knowing it and being put in the low-lamp bedroom with them, Randi still astride him, "loose as she only went afterward," Sade folding warmth under the cool — that's a different thing. My stomach did the drop. This is the two of them, spent, tender, discussing Vee like a shared dish they're taking their time to cook.

And the cruelest, best move: it isn't a villains'-lair scene. It's *warm*. They're genuinely soft with each other. Randi "sets it down at his door," talks less here than anywhere, keeps a hand on every ounce of herself out in the world and loses it in his bed. I found that intimacy real and — against my will — moving, which is exactly the knife. The tooling and the tenderness are the same fabric.

What actually moved my read: **Randi's feeling for Vee is real, and it's cracking her.** "I didn't think I'd like her this much... She got past me before I could help it." I'd guessed the "real laugh that costs her something" meant part of her was genuine — this confirms it and goes further. She finds Vee's embarrassment *hot* ("She went so red I thought she'd go up... That was kind of hot"), and the bigfoot-stomp story is told with helpless delight. This isn't a woman purely running a play. Vee got in.

Then the thing that made me sit up: **the jealousy.** Pace describes Vee — "she comes in and the whole thing goes brighter... the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her" — and Randi goes still. Not the loose after-stillness. "A different one, the kind that costs something to hold," then "the faintest tightening of her against him." She waited "a beat too long" before "Yeah. She is." Randi is jealous — of Pace's feeling for Vee, or of Vee getting that from Pace, or both. And she buries it in sex: "So soon?" The operators have a fault line, and it runs straight through the girl they're farming. That's the most alive thing in the chapter and it's the thing that could break the machine.

The reframe on the shoe trip landed hard too. Last chapter I read that afternoon as Randi lighting Vee's body — the kneel, the thumb on the toenail. Here Randi tells Pace the *cute* version: the bigfoot canoe-shoe, the couple watching, Vee going red. She keeps the toenail moment for herself. Even to her co-conspirator, even in bed, Randi *selects* what she hands over. The harvest flows on — "she talks about you the whole hour, Pace this, Pace that... She's got it *bad*" — but the private charge stays private. That asymmetry is chilling and human at once.

The line that snagged me most: Randi drawing her body down the length of him to *show* him Vee's body — "It's *criminal*, Pace. But you already know." **"You already know."** Has Pace seen Vee's body? He built the dress to her exact shape, took her folded panties, made the fitting happen — so maybe "know" is the maker's knowing, or the harvest. But it reads like he's seen. And it sits against "She's not ready" / "You'll want to take your time" — which says they haven't *taken* her yet. So he knows her body without having had her, and I don't fully know how. Flagging it.

Their program is now explicit and it made me cold: "She's not ready, though. She'll get there... I want her to get there on her own." That's the whole thesis in a whisper. The not-taking is the taking. They will wait for Vee to walk in herself. Every yes freely given.

Erotic charge: it worked on me in one specific place — Randi, "who kept a hand on every ounce of herself out in the world, wanting something out loud in his bed and not able to stop it," and Pace turned on by *that*, not by the picture of Vee. The involuntary want is hotter than the described flesh. The closing paragraph — "she rode him slow in the low lamplight, small and pale above him" — is competent and pretty but went a half-step clinical for me, a soft landing rather than a peak. The peak was the stillness before it.

Friction: almost none. The one thing I clocked — "the kind that costs something to hold" — is now the book's tell for Randi's real feeling breaking surface (I've felt this phrasing before, the laugh that "costs her something"). It's becoming a signature. Earned, but I notice it's the same key struck.

**"Gone"** — Vee is "so gone over you," "that's why she's so gone over you." Gone = fallen, captured, lost to it, already inside the trap. But read against Randi's "she got past me before I could help it," the title floats over both of them: Vee is gone, and something in Randi is going too. And the chapter ends mid-sex, so the last thing "gone" is the quiet — "nothing quiet left in either of them now." Nicely doubled.

**A Polite Invitation / With a Long Spoon** — this is the long-spoon chapter: the two who sup with the devil, in bed together, and I'm no longer sure which of them has the longer spoon, or whether Randi's is long enough. The tagline made flesh: they will not take Vee. They'll wait for her to arrive.

What I want next: I want to be back with Vee, in the dark about all this, and I want to see how unbearable that dramatic irony gets. And I want to watch Randi's fault line — the jealousy, the "got past me" — because *that's* the thing the design didn't plan for. Guess from here: Randi's real feeling for Vee becomes the crack in the machine, and it costs Randi something the play never priced in.
