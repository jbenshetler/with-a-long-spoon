# Cold read (grounded) — Gone

*scene: scenes/gone.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch022 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This is the quietest chapter in a while and somehow the one that scared me most, and I had to sit a second to work out why. On its face it's lovely: Randi *stayed*. After ch20's cold eyes-shut sex and the turned back and the untouched bowl, here she is down on his chest, loose, warm, undefended — the exact opposite of where she left me. She eats the whole plate this time. She asks him to pour her wine and lays her hand on his wrist (in ch20 she poured her own, and it landed like a slap). She asks about his dissertation and actually *follows* it, draws him out, "go back to the part that won't work." After ch20 I'd braced for the game cracking open ugly; instead I got reconciliation, and I believed the warmth of it completely. That's what unsettled me — I don't know what fixed it, and neither does Pace, who just takes the gift.

And woven all through that tenderness is Vee. Randi talking about Vee is the most naked I've seen her. "She got past me before I could help it." "I didn't think I'd like her this much… I get bored of everybody and it doesn't come." The jacket told me Vee's the first woman Randi's let herself want, and here it is, admitted out loud into a dark room, and it's not framed as the plan anymore — it's just want. "That was kind of hot," about Vee going red. "It's romantic," about Vee being gone over Pace, said with something undefended in her face. She's rooting for them and aching over them in the same breath, and I couldn't tell where generosity ended and hunger began. Neither could she.

The moment the floor tipped for me: Pace says Vee comes into a room and it goes brighter, and "she has no idea she does it… she's the most alive person standing there and it never once occurs to her." And Randi goes *still* — "not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding" — then tightens, then after a beat too long says "Yeah. She is." That's the whole thing in a gesture. He's describing the woman he's falling for to the woman who chose her for him, and she has to *hold* herself through it. Then she turns it into sex — shifts her hips down, finds him hard — which is the oldest move she has for making a feeling she can't sit in disappear. I clocked that even as I felt the heat of it.

The heat did land, by the way, especially "God, what she keeps under there. It's *criminal*" — Randi dragging her own body down the length of his to *show* him the thing she's describing, wanting something out loud and not able to stop. Pace's read of it is the sharpest line in the chapter: it went through him "not the picture of Vee she'd handed him, but Randi, who kept a hand on every ounce of herself out in the world, wanting something out loud in his bed and not able to stop it." And then — this is the thing I most trust about Pace — "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." He won't force the door. Daphne taught him that, I think. It's the same restraint as "she chooses it or it isn't anything," and it's genuinely why I can't hate him.

The title. "Gone." Both women are described as *gone* over him — Vee "so gone over you," Randi gone in her own way she won't name. On the page it means infatuated, swept. But the word sat heavy on me, because a chapter called "Gone" that's this warm, this reconciled, this *complete* — Randi stayed, ate, slept, "he held her until she slept" — reads less like arrival and more like a last good night. The cover board calls this volume *A Polite Invitation*, series *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." That proverb has been sitting under everything and I keep not forgetting it: somebody here is supping with something that will cost them, and the long spoon is the distance Pace keeps insisting on. This chapter's sweetness felt like the calm the proverb warns you about.

No new suspicion earned, exactly — I just couldn't enjoy the peace, and I think the book means for me not to. I have zero reason to doubt the warmth is real. That's what frightens me: it's realest right where it's most dangerous. What I want next is Saturday's shoe debt coming due (Vee put a dress-worth of heels on a credit card with nothing under it), and I dread the collision of Randi's two appetites — the plan and the person. And I keep waiting for the eyes-shut coldness of ch20 to be *explained*, and this chapter didn't explain it, it just dissolved it, which somehow made it worse.
