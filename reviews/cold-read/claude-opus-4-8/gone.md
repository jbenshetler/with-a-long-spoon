# Cold read (grounded) — Gone

## Reader reaction

**Felt read:**

This one turned my stomach over — in the good, sick way the book keeps earning. We're back in Pace's warm house, his POV, and it's the mirror of "Portion": last chapter Randi wouldn't eat, wouldn't stay down, rode him with her eyes shut and turned her back. Here she *stays* — down on his chest, loose, and then she does the thing that stopped my breath: she puts on his undershirt, pulls it to her thighs, curls into the chair, and asks *"Is there anything to eat in this house?"* She has never asked him that. And she eats all of it, "the most he had seen her eat at his table." After twenty chapters of Randi as the rationed apple, the woman who feeds Vee and starves herself, watching her *eat* in his kitchen wearing his shirt landed like a confession she didn't know she was making. Something in her has come unlatched.

And what unlatched it is Vee. *"I didn't think I'd like her this much... She got past me before I could help it."* That's the sentence the whole book has been circling — the game becoming real for its maker. I believed it completely, and it moved me, because it's rendered as helplessness, not strategy. The shoe-store story she tells — the yeti stomp, the older couple, Vee going red — is Randi replaying a day she loved, and then the tell: *"She was so embarrassed... That was kind of hot."* There it is, out loud, the desire she's kept underground since ch17. I've been waiting to hear her say it plainly and here it is, offhand, past her guard.

Then the chapter does something quietly devastating. Pace describes Vee — *"she comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp... she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her."* And Randi goes still. Not the loose after-stillness. *"A different one, the kind that takes holding."* She's jealous. Not of Vee having Pace — of *Pace having* that tenderness for Vee, or maybe of Vee being the one seen that way. And her answer to the jealousy is pure Randi: she gets up, takes him in hand, strips off his shirt, and *performs* Vee's body for him — *"What she keeps under there... it's criminal"* — dragging her own breasts across his chest, using the picture of the girl to pull his attention back onto herself. It's the most naked thing she's done, and it's a deflection. She can't hold the still, so she converts it into want.

Pace, God. His restraint reads differently now — I clocked *"He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold."* He *sees* her go still, sees the tightening, and chooses not to touch it. Same door-respecting patience he gave Daphne's door, gives Vee's readiness. It's genuinely tender and it's also how he never has to look straight at what he's doing to either of them. When he says *"She's ashamed. Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body,"* he's exactly right about Vee — and completely blind that he's saying it to a woman who painted the blue on her own toes and won't be seen.

The heat here is real but it's the *cold* underneath doing the work — the second time redirected out of jealousy, eyes I couldn't fully trust. The first coupling (offscreen, "she had stayed down") and the last one (slow, coming apart by degrees, blue toenails "the last composed thing left of her") are tender, and I felt the pull, but I couldn't stop watching Randi manage herself even in coming apart.

**Trust/suspicion:** No new deceit surfaced — this is the two schemers alone, honest with each other as they get anywhere. What unsettles me is subtler and sadder: Randi is falling for Vee and can't say it as love, only as "hot" and "cute" and "pathetic, I love it"; and she's jealous, and she doesn't know what to do with any of it. The trap is getting a third leg, and it's Randi's own heart. That frightens me for all three of them.

**The title — "Gone":** It's Randi's word for Vee (*"She's got it bad... She's so gone over you"*), handed back doubled. Vee is gone over Pace; Randi is *gone* — past her own control, undone, the woman who never gets bored, bored-proofed, caught. It named the chapter cleanly and even pointed a little: someone here is going to be *gone* in the other sense. The volume title, **A Polite Invitation**, and series **With a Long Spoon** — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon." I keep feeling that proverb tighten: everyone at this table is supping with the devil, and I'm no longer sure who brought the spoon. It's pointing me toward a reckoning where the "polite" surface finally tears.

**What I want/dread:** I want Randi to be forced to feel what she feels out loud, and I dread it, because the only way she knows to survive being seen is to hide harder. *Guess from here:* the danger isn't Vee finding out first — it's Randi's jealousy cracking the operation from inside.
