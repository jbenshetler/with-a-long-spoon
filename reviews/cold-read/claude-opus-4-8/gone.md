# Cold read (grounded) — Gone

*scene: scenes/gone.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · memory: ck-ch020 + raw ch021..ch025 · reader-protocol: v3-grounded-checkpoint*

## Reader reaction

Reading this one cold off "Sorority," I came in braced for another Vee chapter and got Pace's bed instead — and it undid me a little, honestly, because the person coming apart here isn't Vee. It's Randi, and she doesn't know it's happening.

The whole chapter runs on things Randi has never done before, and Pace noticing each one the way he notices the lamp. She stays down after. She asks *is there anything to eat in this house* — "She had never asked him that." She gets up and pulls on his undershirt without hunting for the drawer, knows exactly which one, comes to the middle of her thighs, and sits with her feet up and the shirt pulled over her knees "so that all of her was inside it but her face and her hands and the blue of her toes." That image wrecked me a little. This is the woman who built her whole life on the face, who resolved to hide from him forever — and here she is swallowed in his shirt, feet up, *eating* — "the couscous to a line he could have drawn with a ruler," the most he's ever seen her eat at his table, and he has the grace not to say so. The hunger motif finally turning on Randi herself: the one who rations apple slices, fed, unguarded.

And then the thing that actually caught in my throat: "I didn't think I'd like her this much. I keep waiting to get bored of her — you always get bored — and it doesn't come. She got past me before I could help it." She's confessing it as a problem. The game-runner, outflanked by her own mark. I believe it completely and it frightens me, because I don't know yet whether liking Vee this much makes Randi gentler or more dangerous.

The turn is the last page and it's beautifully done. Pace, not even looking, says Vee "comes in and the whole thing goes brighter. Like somebody found the lamp... she's the most alive person standing there, and it never once occurs to her." And behind him "the chair made no sound." Randi has gone still — "Not the loose stillness of after. A different one, the kind that takes holding." Then the shoulders tighten under his shirt, and after a beat too long: "Yeah. She is." And instantly she's up, turning him by the hip, taking the shirt off, dropping her voice — *what she keeps under there, it's criminal, Pace, but you already know* — handing him Vee's body to want so she doesn't have to sit inside what just moved through her. That's jealousy, naked, and she papered it over with sex in about four seconds. Pace clocks it exactly right and doesn't push: "He didn't take up the rest of what she'd set down; that was hers to hold." He always respects the closed door. But I saw what went through it.

What moved for me: Pace, whom I distrust for engineering all this, is almost unbearably tender here and — crucially — *he wants to tell Vee*. He pictures all three of them at his table smiling, "the way they already smiled about each other. He wanted to tell Vee. He could not, not yet." That's the first time his secret reads to me as a weight he's carrying rather than a trap he's springing, and it complicated my dislike. He's not gloating; he's lonely in the middle of two women he cares for. And "Somebody taught her to be ashamed of her body" — he's *read* Vee, the mother's voice and all, without being told. That's real seeing.

Suspicion-wise: I trust Pace's feeling more than I did and trust the *situation* less. Because now there are three people genuinely stuck to each other and a lie underneath all of it, and the person least able to hold her own feeling is the one who built the machine. Randi going "gone" is the scariest development in the book so far — not because she's cold, but because she isn't, and she can't let herself be seen wanting Vee any more than she could let Pace see her on the bench.

Erotic charge: present but — rightly — not the point. The heat here is Randi in the shirt, unguarded; the actual sex at the end is almost a deflection, a thing she does to stop a feeling, and the prose knows it ("there was nothing quiet left in either of them now," but her eyes aren't the tell this time — the *stillness before* was). It ran warm, not peak. The peak charge in my recent memory is still "Peekaboo."

No friction to speak of — the restraint is the craft here, all those "he noticed it and left it" beats. If anything I wanted one more degree of Randi's interior, but withholding it is the whole method of this book, so I won't call it a flaw.

The title. "Gone" — I thought it would mean Vee, gone over Pace, and Randi even says it to her ("that's the best thing there is, being that gone on somebody"). But the chapter spends itself on *Randi* being gone — past her own guard, eating, confessing, caught. It recolored on me as I read: the one who warned Vee about being gone is further gone than her mark, and doesn't get to say so. Lovely, quiet double meaning; it gave nothing away, it reframed.

The volume line — "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION." The long spoon: *he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon.* I keep feeling the point of that tilting. I assumed Vee was the one supping with devils. After this chapter I wonder if Randi is — that she set a table thinking she had the long spoon and is sitting down to it bare-handed. "A polite invitation" sits gentle and ominous over all this courtliness.

What I want next: Vee, and whether any of this reaches her yet. And I dread — genuinely — what Randi does with a feeling she can't admit. Guess from here: Randi's jealousy, not Pace's patience, is the fault line that cracks this open.

**Pace — within the chapter:** Patient and earned; the slow cooking, the noticing, the long build to the chair going silent — every quiet beat paid off in the last page.
