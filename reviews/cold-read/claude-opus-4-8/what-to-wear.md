# Cold read — What to Wear

## Reader reaction

This one goes down easy and warm, and I noticed partway through that I was just *enjoying* it — the gold light, the stolen fries, three girls who won't get up from a table. The retelling-to-friends structure is the same move as the Cassie chapter, but it doesn't feel like a rerun because the register's different: less reverent, more raucous, more comic. Meg following "the Pace situation in installments like her shows" made me laugh out loud, and it also quietly told me how much of Vee's romance is *narrative she performs* — she's not living it so much as serializing it to an audience. That's a small thing that snagged me a little, in a way I can't fully name yet.

**Vee** — I still like her, and the shirt retelling ("I could hear the story getting better in the telling and did not care") is a nice, honest bit of self-awareness. But the thing that struck me hardest is the count. "Twelve." She hadn't even had to count. One kiss in the whole time, and she's "losing my mind." Her own diagnosis — "climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner" — is the seduction's method described *by the victim as charm*, and because I carry the structural dread in, that line landed cold on me even though it's played as girlish delight. She says "He's different. I know how that sounds," and Meg gives her the gentle, devastating "Everybody's different at the start... Sometimes they even are." Vee "let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." That last line is the one that unsettled me most — the one warning that could catch didn't, and the text told me plainly that it slid off. That's Cassie's radar standing down all over again, in miniature.

**Pace** — offstage, rendered only through Vee's admiring installments and one text. The powerlifter reveal is a nice new texture (a math PhD who lifts first thing, writes six hours, stops because "my writing's no good after that" — the same certainty and self-management I already distrust). And then the text: "would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock." Kayla nails what I was already feeling — "That's not a message, that's a ransom note." The *least information a human being has ever sent me*. From inside, it's mysterious and thrilling. From my carry-in seat it's a man who removes decisions extending the pattern to its next riser — and Kayla, of all people, decodes it correctly and unknowingly: "There's no meal at two... He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes." She's right, and she doesn't know how right, and that's the engine working.

**Kayla and Meg** — new to me (or at least newly foregrounded; I'd only had Meg as a floormate name before). They're vivid fast: Kayla the unbroken bright stream, Meg the calm one who lets a sentence finish. I trust them the way you trust a good chorus. Notable: **Cassie isn't here.** Last chapter the roommate was Cassie; this is the athletic dining hall with a different pair. Not alarming, just noted — Vee has a wider social surface than the dorm room suggested.

**Trust vs. suspicion** — nothing new *earned* on the page against Pace here; he's all charm as filtered through Vee. My unease is still mostly carry-in. What the *text* did earn is the pattern-echo: a genuine warning voiced (Meg's "everybody's different") and shown to not-catch. And the content-free invitation to the unseen house — that's the first thing that gave me a small real prickle from the page itself, because it's control dressed as mystery, and even the friends flag the strangeness ("he's not even telling you what to put on").

**Erotic charge** — low and diffuse, deliberately. There's no sex here; the heat is anticipation. It works best in the withholding: one kiss in twelve days, the shirt folded on her pillow "at this exact hour of the night," her heart "doing something quick and light and stupid, and she let it." The title lands as I finish — "What to Wear" is the girls' fizzy question, but under it is the real one, the thing the afternoon's *for* that nobody will name.

**Friction** — very little. The chapter is doing the same retell-to-friends structure a second time and I felt the faint familiarity, but the tonal shift bought it. If anything nagged: Kayla asks "So what's Pace do, then?" and the tag says "This from Kayla" right after Kayla was already the one talking — a tiny attribution stumble that made me reread. Minor.

**What I want / dread next** — I want the house. Finally, the door off 614. I've been holding "what's at the house" as an open question for chapters and now Saturday two o'clock is the appointment. *Guess from here:* the russet dress and the house may be the same beat — that the afternoon is a fitting, or that a dress is waiting for her there, given his provision pattern and Kayla's "bring a change of clothes." I dread the house precisely because everything tender he's done has been prelude, and a man who scripts what she orders for dinner has now built a room she's about to walk into blind. And Randi is *still* absent — the confidante who engineered all of this hasn't surfaced in ages, which is starting to feel like its own held breath.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** At the athletic dorm's dining hall, Vee retold the hike/shirt story again — this time to friends Kayla and Meg — as comic legend: the packed dry shirt, him standing back-turned in the rain, the near-kiss killed by a cop's siren in the fire lane. Tally so far: exactly one kiss ("Twelve" days ago, she didn't have to count), Vee "losing her mind" over how *un*-pushy he is (he asks what she wants and delivers it exactly). Mid-meal a text arrives: Pace invites her to his house — the one she's never seen, off 614 — Saturday, 2:00, no other words, no dress instruction. The girls detonate: "not a message, a ransom note"; Kayla decodes it — no meal at two, "he wants you for the afternoon, bring a change of clothes" — and declares a Saturday hair session. Meg voices the one real warning ("everybody's different at the start... sometimes they even are"); it slides off Vee, "didn't catch on anything."

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — radiant, performing her romance as serialized story to friends; self-aware about embellishing; genuinely undone by his restraint, which she reads as him being "different." Shame/heat still fused; the eating is her peak.
- **Pace** — offstage, all charm through Vee's telling. New: powerlifter, lifts first thing, writes six hours then stops. Sends near-zero-information invitations. House off 614 finally opened to her via Saturday 2:00 text. I distrust him structurally; the page keeps him lovable.
- **Randi** — Pace's secret co-orchestrator posing as Vee's friend; **still conspicuously absent** for many chapters. Her silence is starting to feel loaded.
- **Cassie** — dorm roommate, the moral floor / only real skeptic (converted last chapter). Absent here.
- **Kayla** — new-foregrounded friend; blonde, curls, bright unbroken talker; scandalized decoder of the invitation.
- **Meg** — friend/floormate, calm and level, follows Pace "in installments"; delivered the unheeded warning.
- **Theo** — nice guy Vee turned down in September; waves; comic small note.

**How I feel.** Still tender-sick and half-charmed. The chapter earned little fresh suspicion on the page except two things I'm carrying: (1) a real warning was voiced and *shown* to not-catch — the second time now a skeptic's radar has stood down; (2) the content-free, control-shaped invitation to the unseen house gave me my first prickle *from the text itself*, not just from carry-in dread. Mood: warm, fizzy on top, appointment-dread underneath.

**Open questions.** What's at the house off 614, and what's the afternoon *for* — is it the russet-dress/fitting beat? Does the dress arrive there? When does Vee learn Pace and Randi already know each other? Why the right-hand-only eating? Where has Randi gone, and why so long? Will Meg's or Cassie's warning ever catch loud enough to matter before Saturday?
