# Cold read — What to Wear

*scene: scenes/what-to-wear.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: rye*

## Reader reaction

This one's a party I got let into. New room, new faces — Kayla the bright unbroken stream, Meg the level one who "followed the Pace situation in installments like her shows" — and Vee between them "laughing with her whole body, loud and easy, the kind that made the next table look over and then want in." I liked her here. After all the interiority, this is Vee *social*, magnetic, and I felt the pull of her the way the next table does. Cassie's absent and I noticed — the confidante has rotated to two girls who don't carry the fold, which changes the temperature: this is the version of the story Vee tells to people who'll only cheer.

And they cheer. The shirt retelling lands even harder here than I carried it — "he gets out. Out. Back into the cold rain. So I can change." Kayla's "Stop." Meg gone "quiet in the good way, chin in her hand." I felt the swoon in the room and I felt it on me too, honestly — the standing-in-rain is a genuinely good gesture and it works on everyone at that table, me included. That's the ache of it: I can't call it fake, I only know the frame it sits in.

New information I didn't have: it's been *once*. "Once. All that, and it's been once." She backed him into a tree twelve days ago and that's the whole physical history. And her own read of why she's losing her mind is the tell that isn't a tell to her — "He's not pushing. It's the opposite. He asks me first. The small things. What I want to order, and he waits for the real answer, and then that's exactly what happens, the way I said it." She hears it herself — "It sounds like nothing. I can hear it sounding like nothing" — and can't reach the bottom of why it's undoing her. I've got the bottom she can't reach, and it made me tender and sick in the usual proportion. The restraint-as-seduction motif, named from the inside, by her, as bafflement.

The best line in the room is Meg's, and it's the closest anyone's come to a warning: "Everybody's different at the start. I said it about Danny. Word for word, I think. He's different. Sometimes they even are." That's the real world's ordinary wisdom, gently offered — and "Vee let it go by. It didn't catch on anything." That not-catching is the whole horror in miniature. The one true caution available to her slides off. And Meg's "Sometimes they even are" keeps it fair — the page won't foreclose that he might be. It just declines to protect her.

Then the phone. "His name." And the door I've been dreading opens: *would you come to my house Saturday. Two o'clock.* The house — off six-fourteen past the gravel, the standing dread of this whole book — is finally on the calendar, and the chapter is smart enough to route the alarm through comedy. Kayla: "That's not a message, that's a ransom note." I laughed and my stomach dropped in the same beat. "It's the least information a human being has ever sent me and I have read it a thousand times." The withholding is the seduction again — no fingerprints, no meal named, no *for what*, and she's already read it a thousand times. He hands her the least and she fills the rest, exactly as designed.

Kayla, playing pure delighted friend, says the coldest true thing without knowing it: "There's no meal at two, Vee. He's not asking you over to eat. He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes." That's the reader's dread spoken as girlfriend-hype — and nobody at the table can hear it as dread, because to them it's just *finally*. The gap between how it plays in that gold room and how it plays in my chest is the whole book.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** unchanged in shape, but the invitation to the *house* raises everything. The house has been the withheld interior all along — no fingerprints anywhere — and now there's a door and a Saturday behind it and "not one word about what it was for." I'm not suspicious of any *new* thing; I'm braced. The dread here is fully carried-in, not manufactured by this chapter — the page itself is warm and funny.

**Erotic charge:** simmering, clothed, all anticipation — "My heart was doing something quick and light and stupid, and I let it." The heat is the twelve-days-and-once withholding, and it's working precisely because it's starved. No skin, all appetite.

**Titles:** "What to Wear" — Kayla's outrage that "he's not even telling you what to put on." It sits right on the covered/exposed-body motif and on the invitation itself: the least information, no dress code, so she'll choose how to present the body she manages — she'll dress herself for his door. "Bring a change of clothes" turns the title faintly ominous on a reread: a change of clothes is for after something. *A Polite Invitation* pays off literally this chapter — the polite formula ("would you come to my house") issuing toward her, the door I've been told to dread now opened by a courteous text. *With a Long Spoon* and its "sup with the devil" shadow keeps humming under a scene where the meal is the joke ("He's not asking you over to eat") — no supper at two, and the spoon you'd want is length between you and the thing.

**What I want / dread next:** Saturday, obviously. The house, seen at last. I dread it and I can't not turn the page — which is the trap the chapter is. Guess from here: Saturday is the consummation deferred all book, on his ground, and the russet-dress/flannel object-thread may surface inside those walls. I also want Cassie back — the one person who'd read that text and go still, and she's not in this room.
