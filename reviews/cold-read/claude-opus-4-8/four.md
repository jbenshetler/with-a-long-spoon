## Reader reaction

**Four.** So this is one of the behind-the-curtain chapters — Pace's POV, Randi in his house, the two conspirators alone with the lamp down and the music they've stopped hearing. And it's the one where the mask slips on Randi, not on Vee. I came out of it a little winded, honestly.

The whole chapter is Randi telling Vee's week back to Pace — the stats class, the wet hair, "still full of your come," the brunch, the goodbye kiss — and the longer she talks the clearer it gets that she is not reporting intelligence, she is confessing. The structure is brutal and lovely: every time she gets near the truth of what she feels, she reaches for the cover ("it's not a treat, it's *work*," "I'm doing it for *you*," "for the threesome, when she's ready") and Pace, maddening and exact, keeps declining to accept the cover. "I didn't ask who it was for." "You don't have to do it for me." And it keeps *landing* in her body — she comes on the word, she can't finish her own argument. Then finally, folded over the back of his couch, wrecked: "God, I want her." And she *hears it leave her*, and he feels her hear it. That's the chapter. Randi has been caught by her own mark, and now she's been caught saying so.

I ached for her, which surprised me given how much I distrust the project. The retelling of the goodbye kiss — "she lets me… and then I come off her a little. Not far. And I wait… and she waits" — is the most romantic thing in the chapter and it's one woman describing kissing another woman she's supposed to be *recruiting*, undone by it. And Vee, Saturday, leaning after her, taking the second half-inch: Randi's been carrying that for two days to tell him. The dramatic irony cuts both ways now — Vee doesn't know Randi's a plant; Randi barely admits to herself that the wanting is hers. Two women falling and only the reader (and a little, Pace) can see the whole shape.

Pace. I keep being handed reasons to find him decent inside the thing I can't forgive, and this chapter does it again with the consent beat: "She consents — every time — or I'm done… It isn't you I'm checking on." He holds that line genuinely, and he holds *Randi* with real care at the end — the blanket before she reaches for it, "I'll be careful with you," "You are extraordinary," taking her grip on his arm "for the compliment it was." But the pointing-game retelling is the stone in the shoe. His version: he read *her* the whole walk, pointed only at women he wanted, and had already chosen Vee before the game — the game was "only to find out whether they had chosen the same." And the picture he had on a public lawn, Randi "narrow and sleek and bound against all that sprawl," the two of them and Vee on his pillows. He went hard planning it. So the tenderness and the engineering are the same gesture again, and I still can't separate them, and I've stopped trying to — that fusion *is* the book's temperature.

Heat — it ran high on me, and not because of the mechanics (three, then four times, counted out) but because the sex and the confession are the same event. Every pull of his hands buys another sentence of truth out of her; the kiss she describes to him is hotter than the fucking. The peak for me was "I'm doing it for *you,*" / "I didn't ask who it was for" — manipulation and arousal and self-deception all in one knot. The one cold spot was almost deliberate: Randi "did not look back at him once," face hidden, forgetting the mirror gave him to her anyway. That's "the face" again — she performs even here, and he watches the real one in the glass. Chilling and sad.

Trust/suspicion: nothing *new* to distrust — the chapter confirms rather than complicates. What it adds is the thing I can't shake: Randi's "I have always known what I'm doing," said flat, with the gloss off — and then, a breath later, not knowing what she's doing at all, wanting Vee past the plan. The most ominous note isn't a lie; it's that the architect has lost her footing. That can only go somewhere bad for Vee, who thinks she got lucky twice.

Friction: very little. The one thing I noticed was Randi's "She told me first" when Pace mentions the mouth-asleep line — a flicker of them competing over who owns Vee's confidences, which is doing real work (both of them possessive now). "With the right encouragement" / "I'm inspired by her" is a touch cute but earns it. The prose stays physical and specific; the couch frame "spoke, low, and went on speaking" is the kind of concrete I trust.

The title: **Four.** "That's more than three." The fourth time, worked for, that "crossed over and began to hurt." But four people, too — the threesome keeps being named as the destination, and "Four" sits over the chapter like a countdown. The series title **WITH A LONG SPOON** (sup with the devil, use a long spoon) and Book Two's **A WARM RECEPTION** — now the warm reception reads double: Vee welcomed in, and Vee *received* as the dish. The long spoon is the distance Randi swore she'd keep (the mirror, "the face") and is visibly losing. I don't think it gave anything away; it sharpened the dread.

What I want next: Vee, back in her own POV, carrying a happiness I now know is being handled by two people who are themselves no longer in control of it. And I dread — *guess from here* — that Randi's "I want her" is the crack the whole scheme drains out through.
