So this one belongs entirely to Pace and Randi, alone in the house in the trees, and it's the first time in a long while I've been put back behind the curtain with them — no Vee on the page, only the two people running her. And God, it does exactly what I half-dreaded and half-wanted: it lets me watch Randi come apart over the thing she will not let herself say. The whole chapter is her trying to file her desire for Vee under *work*, under *for the threesome*, under *somebody has to get her used to my mouth* — and the sex keeps betraying her. "The clench came on the word." "I'm doing it for *you.*" "I didn't ask who it was for." Pace keeps lifting the lid off her and she keeps slamming it back, and the slamming is what makes her come. That's the most erotic thing in the chapter and it's not even about Pace's body — it's about Randi being *caught*, and the catching being unbearable and delicious at once.

What moved in me: Randi. I came in fearing her and I leave this chapter aching for her, hard. "I want her." — said down into the wood, after four, with nothing around it, and then the instant walk-back: "For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant." She heard it leave her and he felt her hear it. That's the sharpest the book has cut Randi open yet. She's the one who swore at the mirror she'd never be that seen again (I remember that), and here she is being seen all the way through by the one person she gave "the face" to, and begging him to say something nice and knowing she'll know the difference. "It makes me feel real. And some nights it frightens me." I believe every word, and it undoes me a little.

Pace — I still don't trust the architecture, but this chapter made him quieter and in some ways kinder than I expected. "Is she all right? … Is she happy? … That isn't the same answer." He's the one asking whether the mark is okay, pressing past *radiant* toward *all right*, and "Then I'll be careful with you" lands with real tenderness — toward Randi, not Vee. But I can't unsee the cold center: this entire loving scene is two people luxuriating in how beautifully the engineered girl is responding. "Still full of your come" in the eleven o'clock stats class, the steel cup of his coffee, the twenty minutes about food — they're reading Vee's happiness as a *scoreboard*, and the better she's doing the more pleased they are. That's the horror sitting right under the warmth, and the chapter doesn't flinch from either.

The pointing-game answer chilled me most. "You taught me. Your hand went cold on mine… you gave her a *maybe.*" So the choice of Vee was Randi's tell being read off her body, and Pace confirms: "your face said yes before the rest of you got there. That part was all you." Which quietly reframes the whole plan — Randi didn't just recruit a target, she picked the woman she already wanted, and has been pretending ever since it was strategy. The mouth-kiss ritual I've watched grow for forty chapters is, on this side of the glass, Randi feeding her own starvation under cover of a mission. "She waits. She stands there in the sun with her eyes shut, waiting for me to come back to her." That's not reconnaissance. That's love she's rationing to herself in operational language.

The heat genuinely worked on me — not the mechanics (four times, counted, the wet "unambiguous" in the quiet room) but because the sex is the lie-detector. Every time Pace slows and asks a real question, her body answers before her mouth can. The mirror she forgot, giving him her face the whole time she thought she'd hidden it — lovely, cruel, hot. It peaks at "Oh, but I'm *doing the work,*" and came. The blanket afterward, her pulling his forearm tighter and tighter, him taking her holding on "for the compliment it was" — that's the romantic beat, and it's real, and it's between the two conspirators, which makes it the most quietly disquieting tenderness in the book.

Friction: almost none. Maybe "like a hand laid down on a table" and "like a draft she had thrown out" stack a touch close, but I didn't resent it. The one place I felt the author's hand was "being modest about it, still full of your come" — a little too pleased with its own crudeness as a button. Small.

The title — "Four." I read it first as nothing, then the chapter handed it to me: four times in one day, "that's more than three," his lifter's arithmetic. But by the end it's reaching past the count — *four* people now, maybe, the threesome-that's-really-a-pair Randi keeps invoking; the fourth being Vee, the one who isn't in the room and is the whole subject of it. It lands oblique and then sharpens, which I liked. The cover line — "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION" — the long spoon is the proverb about supping with the devil, and this chapter is two devils at a warm table, so it fits with a chill. "A warm reception" reads newly ironic: Vee's being warmly *received* into something she can't see the shape of.

What I want next: back to Vee, and dread for her now doubled — because I've just watched the two of them agree she'll "get there," and watched Randi's want outrun the plan. *Guess from here:* Randi's the one who breaks this, not the logistics. The feeling's bigger than the scheme now.
