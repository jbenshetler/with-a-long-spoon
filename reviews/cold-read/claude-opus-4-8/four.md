## Reader reaction

**Felt read**

Oh, this one hurt in exactly the way the book has been promising it would. For forty-some chapters I've been living inside Vee's luck, and this chapter pulls me out of her warm kitchen and sets me down in the *other* room — the one she doesn't know exists — on the night after the brunch I just read from her side. It's devastating in its simple structure: everything Vee handed Randi as the most intimate gift of her life ("my mouth had been asleep since I was born," sleeping whole nights, the goodbye kiss she leaned up the last half-inch to keep) comes back here as pillow talk, debriefed between the two people running her. "She told me first." I felt that line land like a cold coin.

And yet — and this is the vise the book keeps tightening — it is not cold. Randi is *wrecked* by Vee. The whole seduction scene over the back of the couch is driven by her retelling the goodbye kiss, and she comes on the word "mouth," comes again when Pace says "you don't have to do it for me." Her insistence that it's "groundwork… it's not a treat, it's *work,* I'm doing the work" is the most naked self-deception in the chapter, and Pace knows it and lances it with kindness, which is crueler and hotter than if he'd pushed. I believe her desire for Vee completely, and I believe she can't afford to believe it herself. That's the interior life I read for, and it's all over this.

Pace — I distrust him the way I always have, but this chapter deepened him rather than flattening him. "I didn't. I pointed. I watched your face. And your face said yes before you did." He's telling Randi the plan was *hers*, that he only read her — which is either the truest thing or the most elegant way to keep his own hands clean, and I can't tell which, and I don't think I'm supposed to. He "checks the gauge," asks "is she all right," "does she want it," "do you like it" — the same catechism of consent he runs on Vee, turned on Randi, and it's doing double work: genuine care and total control at once. When he holds her still and says "you don't have to do it for me" and *feels what it does to her go through her before she had any say* — that's the bench from chapter 1 again, him reaching past the face. He got past hers tonight.

The heat here is real and high, and it runs *on the betrayal*, which is the disquieting part — I was turned on by a scene whose entire erotic fuel is a girl's trust being metabolized into someone else's orgasm. The couch frame "speaking," the three-times-already wet, "it looks good wet / it feels even better," her two fingers shown slow over her shoulder — it's the hottest the book has run in a while, and I noticed I didn't want to flinch from it even knowing what it's made of. That's the book working exactly as designed on me.

The ache underneath: "Twenty-one years old and she found out this autumn that a person can sleep." Randi says it looking at nothing, and it's the one that gets *her* — tenderness and predation indistinguishable. And the ending, "say something nice to me," her hands shut on his forearm, not letting go, taking the compliment as the thing she needed — Randi is lonelier than her mark. I came out of this chapter more frightened *for* Vee and more sorry *for* Randi than before, which is a hard trick to pull in one scene.

No friction worth quoting — the prose stayed in control, the thumb never showed. If anything the restraint (the counted "that's more than three," his flat "I am") is doing the heavy lifting.

**The title — "Four."** It sat opaque until the body count: three times today for Vee's account earlier, and "that's more than three" / "with the right encouragement" here — four. But it widens horribly on second thought: *four* people this whole machine is built around, and the chapter is titled with the number that makes Vee a unit in a tally she can't see. The volume/series line — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION** — "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon," and a "warm reception" is exactly what this house gives: heat turned up at three, the deadbolt at four, a welcome that is also a closing. "Welcoming her in" (the threesome) reads now as the reception the title names. It points dead at the threesome Randi calls "groundwork," and I dread it the way you dread a thing you also want to watch.

What I want next: back to Vee, and the terrible new weight of knowing her Saturday is a debrief. I *guess from here* the threesome is the engine the next stretch builds toward, and that the gap between Randi's "work" and Randi's want is going to crack open. I dread that the PPP secret and this one both land on Vee at once.
