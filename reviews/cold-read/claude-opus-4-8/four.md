**Felt read**

So this is the room I've been kept out of — Pace's house with the door bolted and Vee gone home, the two architects alone. And it guts me in exactly the way I feared it would. For the first time I'm *inside Pace's head*, and the chapter spends its whole length letting Randi come apart in a way she'd never let Vee see. I came out shaken, and not entirely in a bad way.

The thing that landed hardest wasn't the sex (though it ran hot) — it was watching Randi lose her own argument in real time. "I'm doing it for *you.* For the threesome. For when she's ready. Somebody has to get her used to my mouth." And Pace, exact as ever, won't let her have the cover: "I didn't ask who it was for." The way the whole evening is built so that she confesses *into the wood*, face hidden, forgetting the mirror gives him everything — and then it arrives anyway: "God, I want her." The chapter even tells me she heard it leave her, and he felt her hear it. That's the ache of the whole book squeezed into one line. I believe her panic afterward — "For us. When she's ready. That's all I meant" — and I don't believe it for a second, and neither does she. That's the swoon *and* the dread fused, which is this book's whole method.

My feeling about Randi moved — downward in safety, upward in sympathy. She's further gone than Vee is, and she's the one running the machine, and those two facts grinding against each other is unbearable in the right way. The detail about her hair-in-October, being "not the same about it since," the bit where she gives her consent-answer "to the Saturdays" and not to Pace — these are tender and doomed. I feel for her more than I did at the checkpoint, and trust her less, simultaneously.

Pace. God, he's effective and he unsettles me more than ever precisely *because* of the consent beat. "She consents — every time — or I'm done. It isn't you I'm checking on." Read one way it's genuinely load-bearing ethics; read the way the book keeps forcing me to read him, it's a man auditing the structural integrity of a trap he built and calling it conscience. The "pointing game" retold from his side — "the pointing was only to find out whether they had chosen the same," he'd *already* chosen, he went hard on a public lawn imagining Vee's red hair and Randi's black on his pillows — confirms the machinery cold. He never once pointed at a woman he didn't want. He's not discovering Vee; he selected her and let Randi think she helped. That chills me.

The erotic charge is high and it *works*, but it's a complicated working — I'm turned on by the heat and sickened by the content at the same time, because the whole long braced-over-the-couch scene is Randi being fucked into telling the truth about a third person who isn't there and doesn't know she's the subject. The peak is the goodbye-kiss confession ("she *took* it... looking at me like there was nothing better in the world"), and it peaks precisely because it's betrayal rendered as tenderness. Nothing went slack or clinical; the cooking-style prose carries the sex the way it carried the lemon chicken last chapter.

Friction: almost none, honestly. If I reach, the "I'm doing the work" / "Sounds grueling" exchange flirts with being too cute, too aware of its own irony — but it earns it because the joke is the exact shape of her self-deception. The one place the thumb showed a little was "That's the one that got me. Twenty-one years old and she found out this autumn that a person can sleep" — gorgeous, but it's Randi handing me the thesis a touch too cleanly.

**The titles.** This chapter's — "Four" — reads first as the fourth time (she counts it out: "That's more than three"), and then widens under me into the real referent: four people, or the arithmetic of a threesome that keeps not being three because Randi's feelings make a fourth thing in the room. It doesn't give anything away so much as quietly name the geometry. The volume/series line on the cover — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION* — "long spoon" I keep hearing as *who sups with the devil needs a long spoon*, which points right at this: everyone at this table is eating with a tool meant to keep them safe from what they're dining with, and nobody's spoon is long enough. "A warm reception" — the house kept warm two hours ahead of arrival, the welcome being the bait. Sinister and apt. It promises hospitality as the instrument.

What I want next: I dread and want the threesome equally; I want to know what Randi does when the wanting outruns the plan, and whether Vee ever gets this chapter's knowledge. *Guess from here:* Randi cracks before Vee does.
