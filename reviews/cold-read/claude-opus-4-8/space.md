## Reader reaction

**Space.**

This is Pace's chapter, and I came into it braced. After "Gone" — where Randi went still and tight when he called Vee "the most alive person standing there" — I half-expected the crack to widen. Instead it's sealed over, and in a way that unsettles me more than the tightening did. Randi has folded the whole thing into heat. She comes out on a Friday she wasn't summoned to, pleased and letting it show ("she had never once come through that door pleased and let it show like this"), and she rides him while narrating, in detail, undressing Vee this morning — the black lace, walking a slow circle around her, kneeling under the long skirt "so I was under it, all the way up, and she couldn't see a thing," the thumb in the soft back of the knee, "she made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it. I heard it." And she's *getting off on the telling* — "it was coming up in her body while she spoke." That's the thing that landed hardest on me: the seduction isn't cold instrumentation anymore, it's Randi's own live want, and she's feeding it to Pace and taking it back at once.

And it worked on me. Plainly. This ran hot — hotter than I wanted it to, given what I know. The device of it — Randi handing Pace the picture of Vee bare on her rug while she's bare on top of him, both of them lit by a girl who isn't in the room and doesn't know she's being passed between them like a dish — is genuinely charged, and the craft of it (her feet planted on the sheet, the blue lacquer "the only color on the bed") is precise and good. But the arousal came with a cold draft under it, because I'm the only one at this table who knows Vee is the meal. When Randi said "she let me," savoring it, I felt the trap tighten by a full turn. That thumb in the back of the knee — Vee felt that as tenderness from a friend. I read it as a hand going up under a skirt where the girl "couldn't see a thing." The chapter let me feel both and didn't soften either.

Pace I still can't fully hate, which is its own discomfort. His restraint reads as real — he keeps Vee's bar-cruelty to himself ("those were his, and that was Vee's"), he's glad she had somewhere to go, he won't text her not as strategy but because "that was not a thing he had decided, it was only what he was." After the last chapter's fireside confession — the hall, Daphne, the scar he wouldn't open — I understand this man's stillness better, and it makes his part in the game sadder rather than more sinister. But he's lying in bed being fed a girl's body by his girlfriend and *keeping* it, hard for it, and calling none of it what it is.

The friction, small: the "playing with your wood" bit is a groaner, and I think it's meant to be — Randi teasing — so I let it pass. What I noticed more is how completely this chapter re-frames the two prior Vee chapters. The dance ("that's what gets you stepped on — the space you're keeping"), the shoes, the morning at the sorority: I'd read all of it as Vee's genuine thaw. Now I've watched Randi metabolize the shoe-fitting into a sex act. The kneeling under the skirt that felt to me, reading "broken-in," like suspicious tenderness — here's the confirmation, and it's worse than suspicion, because Randi *enjoyed* it as its own thing.

**The title.** "Space" pays off cleanly and a little cruelly. It's the space Vee kept in the dance and got stepped on for — the few inches Pace told her to close. It's the space/room he leaves her now ("he would leave her the room, and wait to see what she walked in wearing"). And it's the space between the three of them that only I can see the true dimensions of. It reads to me as the gap Vee keeps trying to close toward Pace, not knowing the person helping her close it is the one holding the other end. That's a bleak little pun and I felt it. **Volume/series:** the cover board gives me *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION.* "He who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" — that proverb keeps getting truer; the warmth (this warm house, this warm reception) is exactly the bait, and the spoon is the distance nobody at this table is keeping. "A Warm Reception" reads increasingly like a threat dressed as hospitality.

**What I want/dread:** I dread tomorrow night — Vee walking in "sure," in the black dress and the borrowed shoes, to a man who already knows her apology cold because Randi delivered it. *Guess from here:* Randi's want for Vee is no longer only tactical, and I think that's the live wire now — where does a schemer go when she starts actually wanting the mark, and wanting to *watch* her wanted. I want to keep reading and I don't quite trust myself for wanting to.
