## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

So this is the other side of the glass. All through "Rock" and "broken-in" I was inside Vee's warmth — the fire, the scar she wasn't allowed yet, then the panicked, brave march to Randi's door to fix what she'd broken. And now the chapter picks her up and turns her over in someone else's hands: Randi lying on Pace's chest, retelling Vee's worst morning as pillow talk. That did something cold to me. Vee *cried* in that desk chair. She "didn't spare herself one thing." And the tenderness of it is real on Randi's face for a beat — "I wanted to keep her" — but then it goes to bed and becomes fuel. "She made a sound and hoped I didn't hear it. I heard it." That line landed like a small blade. The most private, undefended thing Vee did — tip on one foot and be caught — is now the thing getting Randi off. I believe both women's feeling and I hate what's being done with it, at once.

The big shift for me: Randi *wants Vee.* Not only as intel, not only as a shared game — she's flushed and coming apart narrating the shoe-fitting, "I walked all the way around her. I took my time about it. She let me." That recolors every one of those lingering curb-kisses and the thumb in the hollow of the knee. It isn't cold calculation underneath anymore; it's appetite she's feeding to Pace and feeding on herself. That makes her more dangerous *and* more human — she's not immune to Vee any more than Pace is. "She got past me before I could help it," she said last chapter. Now I've watched it get all the way past her.

Pace I keep circling. He's genuinely relieved Vee had somewhere to go, genuinely won't text her, and I read that as real restraint, not strategy — "That was not a thing he had decided. It was only what he was." But he also *keeps* Vee's cruel words to himself as a courtesy ("those were his, and that was Vee's") in the same breath he's letting Randi narrate Vee's stripped-down morning into his ear. The two of them holding Vee's pieces between them, warm about her, without her — that's the horror dressed as tenderness, and the book knows it. I'm charmed and sickened on the same page, which is exactly where this book keeps putting me.

The heat worked on me, plainly, and I won't pretend otherwise — the telling-as-taking, Randi "giving a thing and taking a thing at the same time," pulling him off her before she'd go over so she could stay in charge of it. That's hot and it's *about* control, which is the most Randi thing there is. But the blue toenails clawing the white sheet at the end kept snagging me back to Vee's frosted-plum toes, to Randi's own sky-blue polish, to how these women keep getting rhymed against each other. The eroticism here is doing double duty: arousing me and implicating me, because I'm being made to enjoy Vee's exposure right alongside them.

No friction to speak of — the prose is controlled and doesn't over-explain; it trusts me to feel the wrongness without narrating it. If anything the restraint is the craft. The one place I felt the thumb, lightly, was "as if the day were his to give, and he set that down beside the rest" — Pace's habit of *noticing* Randi's power-moves and filing them is getting a touch systematic, the same beat as the untouched bowl in "Portion." But it's small.

**The titles.** "Space" — I read it three ways and they stack. The literal empty Friday ("no one was coming"), the space Vee left that Randi walked into and filled. The room Pace leaves Vee ("he leaves you the room and waits"). And — from the dance — the space Vee kept between their bodies that got her stepped on, that closing it was the whole lesson. Here nobody keeps any space; Randi closes every inch, and the three of them collapse into one bed's worth of no-distance. It reads oblique going in and clicks shut on the last page. The volume line — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION*, title "Space" — "long spoon" I still hear as *sup with the devil, use a long spoon*: keep your distance from the thing you're feeding with. This chapter is everyone abandoning the long spoon. "A Warm Reception" — the warm house, the reception Vee got at Randi's door, and dread under both.

What I want: Vee to walk into Pace's on her terms tomorrow — and I dread that Randi will be the one dressing her for it, has already handed her the shoes. I want, guiltily, to see Randi and Vee alone with this want that's no longer only a scheme. And I'm braced for the day the space between what Vee knows and what I know finally closes.
