**Felt read**

Oh, this one *hurt*, and it hurt precisely because it withheld the knife. We've dropped clean out of Pace's and Randi's heads into Vee's, and the whole chapter is her being courted — and I sat there the entire time knowing what the courtship is *for*. That's the engine. Vee experiences the best hour she's had in months; I experienced dread wearing a butter-yellow café. The gap between her felt reality and mine is the whole design, and it's brutally effective.

Vee lands as immediately, achingly real. The girl who reads a room "all at once and in detail, to find her own place in it," who wore the cardigan over the better shirt *on purpose* and lets the room not know it was a choice — I know her in my body. The mother's voice living in her mouth (*floozies*, *that's not the kind of attention a nice girl*), the longer beat of looking at the barely-dressed girls that rides *under* the word before she closes the picture — that's the interior life I read for, the want she won't look at squarely. And the devastating structural joke: she remembers the *feeling* of the hour with perfect totality and the *transcript is blank*. "She had been keeping the feeling... and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed." That's how being love-bombed actually works, and it's rendered from the inside without one word of diagnosis. Gorgeous and sickening at once.

My feeling about Randi has moved — hardened. In "The Pointing Game" I could still hold her as someone in over her own head, fleeing her own unbearable aliveness. Here she's pure instrument: the wattage, "I'm not asking, I've decided," the arm-squeeze deployed twice like a tic, "*Love you, girl*" to a person she's known one day. I've watched her build this exact charm to survive being looked at — and now she's aiming the machinery she uses to hide *herself* at a girl who has no armor. That's what chilled me. The face that begged on the bench is out shopping, on assignment. And Pace isn't even on the page; he's just the absence Vee will never see, the man at the light pole.

The genuinely eerie note — the one the text *earned* — is **Cassie**. Her flat "Cassie," refusing the bit's open door. And then "*Goodbye, Vee.*" Not bye, not text me — the whole word, "for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Vee feels it catch and smooths it, but I didn't smooth it. Cassie reads, without a scene to explain it, as the one person who senses Vee is being carried off somewhere. Her face doing "the thing that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to" — I couldn't name it either, and I'm leaving it ajar, but it sat cold in me. I don't yet know if Cassie *knows* something or just feels it. Either way she's the tuning fork.

Swoon-wise: I did feel the pull, but it's Vee's, and I felt it *with* a wince. The gold Greek sign she'd stood outside of, the high table she'd only seen girls perched at like display — being *let in* under the joke, up in the window. There's real tenderness in how much a right pen and a borrowed hour mean to a girl who lives braced. I rooted for her happiness and dreaded it in the same breath, which is a strange, good ache.

Erotic charge is low and buried — this is a simmer, not heat. But it's *there*: the "flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance," now three feet away; the covert inventory of the bare girls' bodies; "she could wear what they were wearing." Vee's desire is smeared across being-chosen and being-seen and wanting-to-be-Randi and wanting-Randi, undifferentiated, unnamed. That undifferentiation is honest and quietly charged, but the scene isn't about it yet.

Friction: almost none — but "latent variables... the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" is the author's thumb pressing lightly. It's a lovely line and it resonates (I'm inferring the whole predatory apparatus Vee can't measure), but it announced itself as A Theme a half-beat too clearly. I forgave it because it's dropped and left.

**The titles.** "See You Later" — Randi's exit line, and now I read it exactly as Vee does *and* as its opposite: to Vee it's a scheduled promise from a queen; to me it's the casual language of a hunter who's already got the next appointment in a calendar Vee can't see. Set against Cassie's "Goodbye," the chapter is literally two ways of parting — the false-warm *later* and the true-cold *goodbye* — and the title picked the liar's line. That recolors it darkly on the second thought. The volume board — **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION** — now rings loud: sup with the devil, use a long spoon. Vee's just accepted the polite invitation with no spoon at all. The tagline-level phrase "A POLITE INVITATION" reads, after this chapter, as the whole method: the devil doesn't grab, he charms, he *decides there will be a later*.

What I want/dread next: I want Cassie back, badly — she's my anchor and my hope. I dread the threesome premise arriving at Vee's door dressed as friendship. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the "later" is going to be engineered, step by step, and Vee's blank transcript means she'll never be able to point to the moment it turned.
