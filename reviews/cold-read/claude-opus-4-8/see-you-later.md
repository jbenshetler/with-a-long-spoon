# Cold read — See You Later

*scene: scenes/see-you-later.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: the-pointing-game*

## Reader reaction

I finally got Vee's POV, and it does exactly what I was afraid it would: it makes me love her while I watch the door close on her.

The whole chapter is soft and lovely and I kept feeling my own guard drop with hers. That's the horror of it. I *know* what Randi is — I watched her stage the collision one chapter ago, engineer the apology, hunt in daylight — and this chapter shows me the same event from inside, where "one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it" reads to Vee as an accident, as luck, as a brighter life letting her borrow an hour. I have information Vee doesn't and the chapter never once winks at me about it. That restraint is what makes it land. When Vee runs *"we should sit together"* both ways "enough times to wear a groove," I ached, because I know which way it actually was.

**Vee.** Total tenderness, deepened. What moved me most is that she's not naive-dumb — she's socially *brilliant*, reads a room "all at once and in detail," smooths every wrong beat before she's decided to. That fluency is exactly the thing being used against her. And the shame architecture arrived, quiet and devastating: the cardigan worn over the better shirt "on purpose," her mother's voice supplying *floozies* and *that's not the kind of attention a nice girl,* the picture closing itself, "the room did not know it was a choice, and she let it not know." That's a whole psychology in three sentences and it's the first time I've felt the erotic engine of this book pointed at *her* — the body wanting to be seen, the inherited voice shutting it down. She's covered to the collarbone and "the most of what the room was selling" both at once. I believe her completely.

**Randi.** This is the double register working on me. On first read she is *genuinely delightful* — the "not Randy, my parents weren't trying to be funny" bit is charming, the parking-ticket story I can't hear but believe was funny, "I say four hundred words a day and like nine of them land" is a real laugh. If I didn't know what I know, I'd be as gone as Vee. Knowing it, every warm move reads as technique: the forearm squeeze deployed twice, the wattage "turned," "I've decided" / "I'm not asking" as a way of removing Vee's choice while making it feel like being chosen, and the deadly efficiency of "the want had nowhere to put itself except here, this, with her." She produces with words what Pace produces with his body — I was told that, and here it is: Vee leaves with "the leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed... the feeling was total and the transcript was blank." That's a person being emptied of her own experience and calling it the best hour in months. Chilling, and completely un-signaled.

**Cassie.** New and immediately the most important person in the room to me. She's the one who won't take the bit — "she just put her name down flat in the space, one word." And then *"Goodbye, Vee."* The text earned my unease here honestly: it flags the wrongness through Vee's body ("a stair that isn't where your foot expected"), the full finished word "for a longer parting than a Monday," Cassie letting it stand, not fixing it, "something passed over her face, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to." I don't know what Cassie knows. Maybe nothing — maybe she just reads Randi as false and doesn't like it. But that goodbye is doing something a normal goodbye doesn't, and Vee smoothing it away ("It's *coffee,* weirdo") is the saddest beat in the chapter. Cassie is the one person here who isn't charmed, and she's the one who leaves.

**Pace.** Absent, and his absence is loud now — I'm aware this whole warm morning is a move on a board he's playing, though nothing on the page says so.

**Erotic charge.** Not sexual here, and rightly so — but there's a real erotics of *wanting*, and it worked on me. Vee's "flat helpless want" for Randi, the "want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance," the high table as being "lit up in the window... presented at the height the whole room could see" — that's arousal displaced into belonging and being-seen, and it's genuinely charged. The longer beat of looking at the two half-dressed girls, "the sprinter's cut of the leaner one's stomach," under the dismissing word — that's the body wanting before the mind allows it, and it's the most alive thing in her.

**Friction.** Very little. The one place I felt the thumb, faintly, was the professor's lecture landing on *"latent variables. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer... the ones that turn out to be driving the whole thing from underneath."* — that's the book describing itself a little too neatly, and Vee dutifully underlining the word "going nowhere" in her notebook is almost a nudge. I was told the stats-class mirrors are supposed to be subtle; this one I *noticed*. It didn't break the spell, but I saw the hand. Everything else — the Χ ΛΑΤΕ sign resolving, "I'll see you at home in two hours" — is clean.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread the next tightening. I want to know what Cassie sees, and whether that goodbye means she's leaving Vee's life or just marking that something's begun. *Guess from here:* the confidante track is now live — Randi will keep "deciding" there's a later, and the extraction (a guy, the flattering offhand thing that "went in like a key in a lock") will start steering Vee toward Pace. I suspect the "guy" already got seeded in this hour and Vee just can't remember it, which is the point. I'm bracing for the moment Pace enters her world as if by accident.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** In stats class, Vee saved a seat and waited (not-looking at the door) to see if Randi would come; Randi came in late, sat on her other side, and turned complete warmth on her. Vee's quiet friend/roommate **Cassie** met Randi, refused to play along with Randi's charm, and declined coffee — leaving with a flat, wrong-sounding *"Goodbye, Vee"* that unsettled Vee before she smoothed it away. Randi took Vee to Chi Latte (Χ ΛΑΤΕ — the Greek-letter pun Vee had never been "let in on" before), sat her at the see-and-be-seen high window table, talked for an hour Vee found magical and could not afterward remember a single word of — only the feeling of being chosen. Randi hugged her, said *"Love you, girl"* and *"See you later"* (as settled fact, not maybe), and left. Vee floated home warm, the words already gone.

**Who's who.**
- **Vee** — the redhead, now in her own POV. Socially fluent, reads rooms instantly, wears a cardigan over her "better shirt" to hush a body that wants to be seen (her mother's shaming voice lives in her head). Lonely for a brighter life; just been chosen by exactly the person she'd have picked. Warm, generous, wide open.
- **Randi** — flawlessly charming to Vee's face; I've seen her hunt, so every warm move reads as technique. High-status in her (unnamed) house, own room, deploys warmth as a tool that empties Vee of her own experience. First-read delightful, second-read cold — working exactly as designed.
- **Cassie** — Vee's roommate/friend. Terse (four words a day), immune to Randi, has a noon lab. The only unmoved person in the room. Her finished-sounding *"Goodbye, Vee"* rang wrong to Vee and to me — earned unease, unexplained.
- **Pace** — the orchestrator, absent but present as the reason all this is happening. Real name still hidden.
- **Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey** — sorority machine, offstage. **Meg** — Vee's floormate, was with her at the staged collision.

**How I feel.** Deep protective tenderness for Vee, now that I'm inside her and she's completely disarmed. Randi: admiration-as-dread — she's very good, and I trust nothing. Cassie: my one point of hope/alarm — she sees something. The stats "latent variables" line was the one moment I felt the book explaining itself. Mood: warm, sad, and quietly predatory; the trap is comfortable and closing.

**Open questions.** What does Cassie know or sense — is that goodbye a warning, a judgment, or a departure? Who is Pace under the name, and when does he enter Vee's world "by accident"? What was actually *said* in that unrememberable hour — what got planted? How much steering toward a "guy" already happened? When does Vee ever suspect anyone? Still waiting to watch the confidante track do its work now that it's begun.
