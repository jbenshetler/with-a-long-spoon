## Reader reaction

# Felt read

So this is Vee from the inside, finally — and it's a gut-punch precisely because it's so happy. The whole chapter is a girl being made joyful, and I spent it braced, because I know what she doesn't.

The tone shift is the thing. After three chapters of Pace's cold appraisal and Randi's lagging mask, here's a consciousness that just... glows. Vee reads a room "all at once and in detail," she's socially fluent, she covers for Cassie's flat *"Cassie"* by making it "a feature" — she's generous, quick, kind. And she's ravenous for exactly the thing being dangled: "an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter life, and she wanted more of it already." That line landed hard because the jacket promised me Randi "lifts her into a brighter life," and here's Vee naming the hook herself, in her own words, gladly, with no idea a hand is pulling the string.

The "not looking at the door" opening is wonderful and painful — the third minute of not looking, writing the date so her hand has "somewhere to be." That's real longing, the ordinary teenage kind, and it made me like her instantly and dread for her simultaneously. And Randi's entrance, "*There* you are," coming in the back where Vee didn't think to watch — after last chapter I know that entrance was engineered, that the "accidental" collision was staged, that "we should sit together" was never a maybe. Vee "ran it both ways enough times to wear a groove." I know which way it actually runs. That dramatic irony is the whole engine of the chapter and it works on me.

**Cassie.** New character and immediately my favorite thing in the chapter — and my one flicker of hope. She won't take the bit. She puts her name "down flat in the space, one word." And then the goodbye: *"Goodbye, Vee."* The whole word, "flat and finished, the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Vee feels it "the way you feel a stair that isn't where your foot expected" and smooths it over. That chilled me. Cassie sees something. Whether she's read Randi specifically or just clocked that Vee is being lifted away from her — the *something that passed over her face, brief* — Cassie is the one person in the room not enchanted, and the text let me feel her seeing it while Vee explained it away. That's the best kind of dread: planted in a minor character's plain speech.

**Randi.** I now fully distrust her warmth, and watching it operate from the target's side is chilling in a way ch.3 wasn't. "The warmth swung over and landed there, total, undimmed" — swung, landed, the same wattage redirected on command. She turns it on Cassie, gets nothing, and *takes the flat little Cassie as if it were the best thing anyone had handed her* — she metabolizes rejection into more charm. "Love you, girl" and "See you later" — I know from ch.3 she believes she chose this, and I know from ch.3 that Pace let her believe it. So even Randi's sincerity is layered: I think she *does* like Vee, which makes it worse, not better.

**Erotic charge:** none, and rightly — this is a seduction with no sex in it, which is scarier. The "want" here is all Vee's, and it's not even sexual yet, it's belonging-want, being-chosen-want: "a flat helpless want, the want you keep for a thing you've only ever seen from a distance." Though there's one telling beat — Vee clocking the two half-dressed girls, the word "*Floozies*" arriving "in her mother's voice," and then "a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for," the sprinter's stomach, *she could wear what they were wearing.* That's the shame-raised-to-bury-it the jacket named, and the same wanting-she'd-be-ashamed-to-name. First real crack of Vee's own policed appetite, and it's tied straight to her mother, exactly like Randi's portioned appetite is tied to hers. Two girls with mothers in their mouths.

**Friction:** almost none. The chapter earns its length — the stats lecture as texture (she lets the thing she's actually good at "go by like scenery" because Randi's elbow is the better thing — that's the cost of the enchantment shown, not told). If I reach: "the queen having decided there would be a later" is the narration nudging me toward the power dynamic I already felt, slightly on-the-nose, but it's inside Vee's own dazzled register so I'll allow it. And "latent variables. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" — that's the author's thumb, a little wink, the professor handing me the theme. I noticed it as a device. It didn't ruin anything but I saw the hand.

**Titles.** "See You Later" — Randi's parting line, and the chapter earns the recolor: it's set against Cassie's "Goodbye." Two farewells, and the one that *sounds* warm is the trap ("a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see") while the flat one is the true friend saying a truer goodbye than she means to. On reread that pairing is the whole chapter. *A Polite Invitation* — Chi Latte, "I've decided," "I'm not asking" — the invitation that isn't a question, freely accepted. And the tagline: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." I watched Vee's yes "arrive in her chest fully formed" and then *make herself wait one full beat so it would not come out the size it actually was* — a yes so free she has to hide how much she means it. That is the tagline dramatized. *With a Long Spoon* still sits opaque — "sup with the devil, use a long spoon," presumably: whoever dines with this needs distance. Nobody in this book has a long spoon.

**What I want / dread:** I want more Cassie — she's the tripwire. I dread the sweetness continuing, because every good hour Vee banks is leverage. Guess from here: Randi's genuine liking of Vee is the thing the jacket means by "how real it would get" — the game turning real on the hunters. And Cassie's "Goodbye" is a seed I expect to pay off — either she warns Vee or she recedes as Randi displaces her.
