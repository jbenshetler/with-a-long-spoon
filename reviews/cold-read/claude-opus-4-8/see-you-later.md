## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

This chapter did something I should have seen coming and still wasn't braced for: it put me inside the girl. For three chapters I've been with Randi and Pace — watching Randi get taken apart on the bench, watching the two of them cruise the quad playing their "pointing game," watching Randi execute a *collision* on a redhead so clean the redhead apologized for it. And now here's that redhead, Vee, and she's telling herself the collision "had genuinely not been clear, one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it." I know exactly who did it. I watched her train for it on a dance floor. The dramatic irony is total, and it's not fun-ironic, it's sickening in the slow way — because Vee is *lovely*. She's warm and sharp and generous (the way she covers Cassie's flat little *Cassie* and makes it "a feature"), she notices everything, she's lonely in a specific real way, and she is being walked into a trap by people who already own a padded bench with handholds.

So the swoon and the dread are the same sensation here, which is a horrible, effective trick. I *felt* Vee's joy — "she walked back to her dorm not feeling her feet," the right pen landing "like praise she'd worked for," the high table she'd only ever seen girls perched in "like something in a display." Being chosen by the queen. I know the warmth is manufactured, I know "*there* you are" is the exact phrase Pace breathed into Randi at the moment he broke her open on the bench — *there you are, Randi* — and now Randi is spending it on Vee like counterfeit currency, and it still works on me a little, because being wanted that completely is narcotic and the page renders it from the inside. That's the cruelty of the thing. The seduction works on the reader the way it works on the mark.

Cassie is the whole nervous system of this chapter and she barely speaks. She won't take Randi's bit — just lays her name down flat, "one word," and picks her pencil back up. She declines the coffee without sulk. And then: "*Goodbye, Vee.*" The whole word, the word "for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Vee feels it catch and smooths it over — "It's *coffee*, weirdo" — but I didn't smooth it over. Cassie sees something. She can't name it and neither can I in detail, but she's registering a predator in the room, and her goodbye is the only honest line spoken to Vee all chapter. I trust Cassie completely and I'm frightened for Vee *through* her.

My feelings have hardened. Randi I'd watched with real sympathy — the begging, the leaking, the face in the mirror, the vow to go back behind it forever. That sympathy hasn't vanished, but watching her turn that same freshly-learned warmth *outward* as a weapon, "love you, girl" after one hour, the hand flat between the shoulder blades — she's doing to Vee what was done to her, and she's good at it, and it curdled something. Pace isn't even on the page and he's the architect of all of it; his absence makes it worse, like watching a snare someone else set. And the jacket — "WITH A LONG SPOON," *he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon*; "A POLITE INVITATION" — is now fully legible. Vee is the one being politely invited to dine. She has no spoon at all.

Erotic charge is low and I think deliberately so — this isn't a sex scene, it's a grooming scene. But the current is there under it: Vee's "flat helpless want" when she looks at Randi, and that honest beat where she clocks the half-dressed "floozies" and lets herself think *I have more than they have, and better* before her mother's voice slams the picture shut and she keeps the cardigan on. That's the chapter's one hot seam — queer want and body-shame tangled so tight Vee can't look at either straight. It's a simmer, not a burn, and it should be.

Friction: almost none. I did notice the device of Vee not being able to remember any actual *words* Randi said — "the transcript was blank" — which the chapter leans on hard, maybe a half-beat too explicitly ("the leaning-in was the whole content"). But it also rang true to infatuation, so I bought it. The title "See You Later" gives nothing away until Cassie's "Goodbye" recolors it, and then it turns: Randi's line is the hook, "a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see," and Cassie's is the truth. The two partings sitting side by side is the whole book in miniature.

What I want and dread: I want Cassie to get through to her. I dread, hard, the moment the bench and Vee meet — Randi asked "do you think she'll like the bench?" last chapter and it's been sitting in my chest since. I expect the warmth to keep working and Vee to walk in smiling. *Guess from here:* Cassie is the one who'll see it clearly and the one who can't make Vee listen, and that gap is going to hurt.
