# Cold read — See You Later

## Reader reaction

Finally. I've been wanting Vee from the inside for a whole chapter, and this delivers so completely it almost hurts. She's exactly the person I feared for and now I can feel why — the openness isn't naivety exactly, it's *hunger dressed as sunshine*. "She'd carried the morning of it like an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter, better-lit life, and she wanted more of it already." That's the whole trap in one sentence, and the awful thing is I recognize it — being chosen by the person you'd have chosen to be chosen by. The chapter made me like her enormously and dread for her in exactly equal measure.

The reframe of yesterday's collision from Vee's side is quietly devastating because I *watched Randi engineer it* last chapter. Vee: "one of those collisions where both people apologize and neither one did it." I know who did it. That gap — me knowing, her not — is the engine of the whole chapter and it works on me without a single line announcing it. And her running "*we should sit together*" both ways "enough times to wear a groove" — the not-knowing whether warmth is meant or performed is the one true instinct she has, and she overrides it every time, because the wanting is bigger than the doubt.

The star of the chapter for me is **Cassie**, who I've never met before and who I now trust more than anyone in this book. She refuses the bit. Randi runs her charming *not-Randy* routine — "a little bit, Vee saw. A nice one. It had a shape to it, a place at the end where the other person was supposed to laugh and hand their own name back" — and Cassie just says "Cassie" and picks her pencil back up. She doesn't take the door the bit is built to be answered with. That is the first person in three chapters to *not be readable/steerable*, and the text lets her simply decline. Then the goodbye: "**Goodbye, Vee.**" — "the whole word, flat and finished, the word for a longer parting than a Monday." That landed on me exactly as it landed on Vee, as a stair that isn't where your foot expected. I don't know what Cassie knows or senses. Maybe nothing — maybe she's just a plain-spoken person who says goodbye. But something in her face "passed over, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to," and Vee's whole gift is naming rooms, so the *one* thing she won't look at is the one thing I now can't stop looking at. That's the finest bit of craft in the chapter and I can't tell if it earned my dread or manufactured it — I think it earned it, because it's built entirely on Vee's own smoothing-over reflex, not on narration nudging me.

**Randi** from outside is almost unbearable to watch now that I've been inside her cold room. Every gesture I saw her *build* last chapter — the forearm squeeze, the wattage, the "I've decided" — lands on Vee as pure gift. "I'm going to copy off you all semester" — a nothing thing, "and it warmed her like praise she'd worked for." The double register the book keeps promising is fully operational here: on Vee's read, Randi is generous, funny, self-deprecating, includes Cassie completely. On my read, she picked the seat beside Vee, kept "coming back to" Vee, turned warmth on Cassie precisely *because* Cassie is Vee's person and the room has to stay warm. And the swing of that warmth onto Cassie "total, undimmed" is exactly the tell — it's a floodlight, not a feeling; it points wherever the plan needs it. Nothing on the page says sinister. I supplied all of it from memory, which is the trick working.

The erotic charge here isn't sex, it's *want*, and it's strong. The Chi Latte sequence — Vee reading the room, cataloguing the "floozies," and then "behind the word, riding under it where she didn't look, a longer beat of looking than the word accounted for — the sprinter's cut of the leaner one's stomach." That's the shame engine I was told this book runs on, and it's beautifully oblique: her body looks longer than her mother's word allows, and she *lets it come because it was true and a little pleasing and hers.* And then "all Vee could do was look at [Randi] with a flat helpless want." The word "want" does a lot of quiet work — is this a crush, is this desire, does Vee even know? She doesn't, and the not-knowing is the charge. I felt it as genuinely erotic in the way a specific unadmitted longing is erotic.

The "key in a lock" — "Randi said something so flattering and so offhand that Vee had felt it go in like a key in a lock." That stopped me cold, because I've got that exact image from inside *Randi* ("the size of a key turning in a lock," the unnamed thing inside her). Now the same figure appears inside Vee, receiving. The two women locked by the same image, one turning the key she doesn't know she's turning. Gorgeous and grim.

The blank-transcript passage is the thesis made flesh: "the leaning-in was the whole content, and the words it had been leaning toward had not stayed... the feeling was total and the transcript was blank." Randi produces with words what leaves no words behind — Vee can't reconstruct a syllable, only the sensation of being chosen. That's a manipulator's dream and Vee experiences it as the best hour in months. It genuinely frightened me.

Friction: almost none. The one place I felt the author's hand faintly was the professor's send-off — "*latent variables*. The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can, the ones that turn out to be driving the whole thing from underneath." That's the stats-class-mirror the book likes, and it's a hair too on-the-nose landing right as Randi arrives — I clocked it as a wink. But it's brief and the professor is characterized (the laser pointer he over-uses, "a man checking that the road was still there"), so it mostly earns its place. I'd call it the one moment the thumb touched the scale.

What I want next: I want to know what Cassie is. I want to see Vee and Randi again and watch the staircase climb. And I badly want the two tracks to start bending toward each other — Vee now belongs to Randi's plan and, unknowingly, to Pace's selection, and she has met exactly one of the two people aiming her. *Guess from here:* Cassie is the one who sees and won't be believed, and "Goodbye, Vee" is a real goodbye to the Vee who existed before this table. I hope I'm wrong and I don't think I am.
