# Cold read (grounded) — See You Later

## Reader reaction

The floor dropped out from under me on this one, and it did it so gently I almost didn't notice the drop. This is the first time I've been *inside Vee*, and after two chapters of watching the machine assemble itself — the bench, the bathroom mirror where Randi decided to go back behind the face forever, the pointing game where Pace hunted a redhead named Vee across the quad and *sent Randi to collide with her on purpose* — I now get the collision from the target's side, and she is so happy. That's the whole horror and the whole tenderness of this chapter: Vee walks back to her dorm "not feeling her feet," she carries the morning "like an hour she'd been let borrow inside a brighter life," and I know exactly how manufactured that hour was. I watched it get built. She didn't. I have never been so far ahead of a character while liking her this much.

**Vee.** God, she got me. She's not stupid — she's *fluent*, she reads a room "all at once and in detail," she smooths over Cassie's flat beat by turning it into "you'll learn to read them like tea leaves," she folds down her own eagerness one full beat "so it would not come out the size it actually was." That last line ached. This is a girl who has trained herself to portion out her own wanting the way Randi portions apple slices — and here the wanting is bigger than she can meter, and she keeps having to shrink it in real time. The Chi Latte scene did the thing the jacket promised: I fell for it "exactly as she does." The sign resolving — Χ ΛΑΤΕ, the joke she'd "stood outside of," now walking under it with Randi holding the door — that's a lovely, warm, real image of inclusion, and it's poisoned, because I know the door is a trap. The blank transcript is the masterstroke: she hangs on every word and keeps *none* of them, "the leaning-in was the whole content." That's infatuation rendered with real precision, and it's also, coldly, exactly how a mark gets worked — kept in the feeling, not the facts.

**Randi.** After "The Bench" and "Standards" I can't read her warmth as innocent, and the chapter knows it and lets me squirm. Every gesture — the forearm squeeze "once, as she had on the lawn," the "I've decided," the "I'm not asking" — is the exact seated authority Brooke used on *her* in "Standards," now aimed downward at Vee. And yet. The person doing this is a woman who wept on a bench, who begged in a voice that came from below, who decided in a mirror that she'd never come out again. So when she turns the full undimmed wattage on Cassie too and gets *nothing* back, I felt a flicker of something — is any of this warmth real to her, is she performing a role Pace built for her, is she doing to Vee what was done to her? The page holds it open and I'm leaving it open. I don't know if Randi likes Vee. I suspect Randi doesn't fully know either.

**Cassie.** New, and the chapter's tuning fork. She's the one immune to the wattage — one flat "Cassie," won't take the bit's open door, "goodbye, Vee." That *goodbye* is the one cold spot in a warm chapter, and it's earned, not telegraphed: "the whole word, flat and finished, the word for a longer parting than a Wednesday." Vee feels it as "a stair that isn't where your foot expected" and smooths it away. I did not smooth it away. Cassie sees something Vee can't, and something passed over her face "that Vee would not have been able to name and did not try to." I trust Cassie completely and I'm a little frightened for what she can see.

**Swoon / pull.** Real, and complicated. The warmth of the Chi Latte hour genuinely worked on me — the high table, the lavender latte, "love you, girl," the hand flat between the shoulder blades. I wanted Vee to have it. That's the trap the book is building on me: the pull toward Randi is Vee's pull, and it's lovely, and I can't cleanly enjoy it because I know its origin. This isn't a swoon I get to keep. That's the book being merciless exactly where the jacket said it would be.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No ambient dread manufactured here — the suspicion is fully earned by what I already saw. The unease isn't in Randi's *behavior* on the page (which is charming); it's in my knowing what the behavior is *for*. The chapter plays it dead straight and lets the prior two chapters do the poisoning. Only the professor's line — "*latent variables*… the factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can" — felt like the author's thumb pressing, a beat too on-the-nose, Vee even underlining it "going nowhere." I clocked it as a wink. It didn't ruin anything, but I saw the hand.

**Erotic charge.** Low and that's right — this chapter isn't about the body, it's about being chosen. The nearest thing to heat is Vee at the high table, "up in the window… her body presented at the height the whole room could see," and the covered-to-the-collarbone tension with her mother's voice (*floozies*) fighting the true beat of looking at the barely-dressed girls and thinking *she could wear that, she has more than they have.* That's a nice seam of shame-under-want, the interior life I read for. But it simmers; it doesn't burn.

**The titles.** "See You Later" — before reading, a nothing phrase; after, it's the whole hook. It's what Randi tosses off on the sidewalk, "flat fact, a thing already scheduled in some calendar Vee couldn't see," and it's set directly against Cassie's *goodbye*. Two farewells, and the warm one is the ominous one: "see you later" means *you are already in a plan you can't see*, which is precisely the jacket's promise — "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one." The title gives nothing away to a first-timer and turns cold on the reread. That's the mechanism working. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — "he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon." Randi is the long spoon; Pace is doing the supping at one remove, sending someone else to touch the fire. And the invitation is *polite* — warm, gracious, "come to Chi Latte, I've decided" — which is the cruelty of it. This chapter is the polite invitation being extended, in person, and Vee saying yes with her whole chest.

**What I want / dread next.** I want to stay in Vee's head — the switch to her POV is the book's best move so far, because now the dramatic irony has a beating heart in it. I dread, in the good way, watching Pace enter her frame. *Guess from here:* the redhead "Vee" of the pointing game and this Vee are the same girl, and the "threesome, with him" that Randi reframed her own wanting into is the shape the trap will take — Randi delivering Vee to the bed she got broken open on. I dread how much I'll enjoy it before it turns, because so far the book has made me enjoy exactly the things I shouldn't.
