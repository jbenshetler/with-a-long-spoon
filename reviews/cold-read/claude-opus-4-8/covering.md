## Reader reaction

Oh, this one worked on me — and then it turned the knife so quietly I almost missed the hand doing it.

For most of the chapter I'm just happily *in* it. Vee at the classroom door, running a "deficit of hugs," dressing and redressing, choosing the cream wrap because she won't sit across from Randi's glamour in her Tuesday clothes — that's her whole self in a gesture, and I love her for it. The retelling-at-the-table structure is exactly the pleasure I came for: she gives Randi the after-school-special version first, gets the flat "Hmm" that stops being attached to anything, and then Randi coaxes her back to the beginning — "That's not where a night like that starts" — and Vee tells it *properly*, the drawer shut on all the underwear, your-shoes-first, the coat she's worn to meet her mother, and finally turning the coat around on its hook to show the lining. That reveal — "Very. Ready." — is genuinely hot, and it's hot because of Randi's face taking it in: "her eyes were very dark and liquid, staring. Vee could hear her breathing." That's the charge of the chapter. The sex is all reported, secondhand, and it still runs warmer than most first-hand scenes because the wanting is happening *across the table*, right now, in the telling.

The bathroom is where the book quietly told me the truth Vee won't. She goes down to get herself off "about Pace, obviously. All of it. About Pace." And then — "at the top, right where the drop began, Randi's face arrived behind her eyes — the tonight face... *how much could he see*." She comes on Randi's voice and files it under Pace. The "third restroom she'd touched herself in — she counted the practice room; of course she counted the practice room." That precision, and the promise to the flushed, unsorry girl in the mirror to "control herself next time," is the book letting me see exactly what Vee is holding just out of her own sight. I wasn't guarding for it; the page earned it clean.

And then Randi — pleasant as a hostess — "It's a good thing you got relief." *She knows.* Read her through a floor. That landed on me the way it landed on Vee, the loft tilting a degree. It's the handling I keep bracing for, done so warmly it reads as intimacy. She's not embarrassing Vee; she's claiming her.

The heel-in-the-hook beat, the little edge-of-the-glass and fingernail-under-the-rim tics Randi keeps doing — those are new tells for me. Randi *fidgets* under this now. She's not all surface tonight.

**The two stories Randi tells** are the chapter's real weather. The one-knee lawn boy is charming — until Vee asks the wrong small question ("How did they *tell* you they'd found them?") and the whole register drops out of Randi's voice without her deciding it. "They're not mine. They're your color." "She knew my color." The color climbing her neck, the glass forgotten in her hand, the boy who quit that week and found other lawns. That's the most *seen* I've felt Randi since the bench — humiliation as a thing done to her by her own mother, in front of someone who'd knelt for her. It recolors every glamorous, unbothered thing about her: she learned to be untouchable somewhere, and this is a door onto where. I ached for her, and I distrust her, and both got stronger at once, which is exactly her.

**Swoon:** the midnight-pancakes-on-one-knee retelling, "his voice didn't make it all the way through the sentence," and the goodbye on the sidewalk — the mouth-kiss with its little second press, then the hug that doesn't quite end inside both their coats, bitter-orange rising off Randi's collar. And Vee, told about the wet neck from Christmas, *bending without a thought* to kiss the spot — and Randi *flinching*, "don't you dare weaponize that." That flinch is the whole book in miniature: Randi, who orchestrates everyone's undoing, undone for half a second by an innocent kiss she didn't script. I melted and I got scared in the same breath.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** no new deception surfaced — but the wanting has crossed a line the page won't let Vee name, and Randi has just demonstrated she reads Vee completely while Vee reads her not at all. My unease is all structural, all earned by the gap between what Vee tells herself and what her body keeps doing.

**Friction:** almost none. The Mauss/*hau* lecture could have been the author's thumb, but it's too well-placed to resent — a gift carries the spirit of the giver, and "the pull is why a gift can never really be free" lands over a table where Randi handed Vee "the play like a queen lending a sword," where Pace gives blankets with no card so you never have to say thank you. It's doing work, not decorating. The one beat I clocked as slightly convenient was Vee's certainty Randi "always made time," repeated a hair too often — but it's her voice, so I'll allow it.

**The title.** "Covering" — it *gave something away* and then kept giving. The coat over nothing; the scarf covering Randi's throat; the word Randi keeps covered (the checkpoint memory of her choosing to "let the word keep its cover"); the pancake-story cover version vs. the real one; the mother's underwear *laundered and folded* — covered up, then set down as evidence. And the last, worst one: the restaurant closing that "covered" for Vee, got her out of telling the neck-kiss story, and the "little unweighable stone of relief" she can't explain. She's covering something from herself now. The title points straight at the thing she won't look at.

**Book/series titles:** "WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION." *He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon* — that proverb hangs over every warm table in this book; someone here is dining with something dangerous and thinks the meal is just dinner. "A Warm Reception" reads double now — the too-warm house that makes her wear less, and the *reception* of being welcomed in, chosen, folded into a world — warmth as the lure. It promises exactly the thing this chapter delivers: heat as the cover over the trap.

**What I want/dread next:** Wednesday's dinner — Pace and Randi in the same room *in front of Vee*, her worlds colliding by design. Guess from here: I dread Vee catching one wrong glance between them, or one too-easy familiarity. And I'm bracing for the neck-kiss to be the small live wire it clearly is — the thing Vee "would tell Thursday, obviously," which means she won't, or won't be able to.
