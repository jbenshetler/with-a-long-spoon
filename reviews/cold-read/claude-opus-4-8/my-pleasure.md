**The felt read**

Oh, this one turned the lights on in a room I've been standing outside of since page one, and it's colder and hotter in there than I let myself imagine.

We've spent the last four chapters inside Vee's giddy, generous head — the pleasure princess, the coat, the county road, the confessions across the loft table. And now the book does the cruelest, most electric thing it could do: it cuts to the *other* side of that table. Pace and Randi, in his bed, and the woman they're both making love to isn't in the room — she's the subject of it. Everything Vee handed Randi over wine, proud and dying to give it away, comes back here as something Randi and Pace already *have* between them, fluently, as a matter of course.

Start with the wet kiss. In "Missed a Spot," Vee sputtered *"Ugh — gross,"* did the Victorian hand-over-mouth, decided he must have "forgotten to wipe." I already suspected the tidy explanation was a lie she needed. This chapter doesn't just confirm it — it opens on it. Pace lifts his head, *"gave her time to see it,"* and Randi pulls him in and licks herself off his mouth without a heartbeat's hesitation, then makes him eat his own thumb — *"Now it's mutual."* It's their liturgy. He didn't forget to wipe with Vee. He did with Randi what he tried to do with Vee, the exact same offering — and one woman calls it powerful and the other calls it wrong. That parallel gutted me. It recolors the whole warm breakfast morning into something with a trapdoor under the floorboards.

And the wrist. He *"kissed the inside of her wrist"* here — the identical gesture Vee pointed him to and marveled at, *"who stays? He parked."* He does the same things to both of them. That could read as hollow, a technician running his routine. It didn't land that way for me. It landed as a man who loves in one grammar and is spending it on two people, and I couldn't decide whether that's tenderness or a kind of horror, and I think the not-deciding is the point.

Then the talk. God, the talk. *"I miss her during the day. More at night."* This is the thing I keep circling: it isn't cold. There's no villain in this bed. These two aren't gloating over a mark — they're aching, out loud, over a girl they both love, using each other's bodies to say her name they won't say. *"She kisses me first now." "She looks at you, too."* And the neck — **the neck.** Twice in the recent chapters Vee kissed the hollow of Randi's throat, meaning nothing by it, and Randi laughed it off as *ticklish, don't you dare weaponize that.* Here we learn what that flinch actually was: *"I nearly turned around."* *"I wanted to turn around. Put my hand on her."* Vee's innocent, half-drunk affection is a live wire Randi is barely surviving, and she carries it straight into Pace's bed. I felt sick and moved at once. Vee has no idea she's tormenting the one person she trusts most.

And the line that stopped my breath: *"I want her to know exactly what she does to me."* Because she won't tell her. She decided (my memory of ch 46) to keep the word covered. So she pours it into a bed Vee will never see, into a man who also loves Vee, and it goes nowhere Vee can reach. That's the whole tragedy in one sentence.

Then Pace. Seen from outside for once, and he's exactly himself — leading, *"showing off,"* controlling every descent, *"My turn"* — but the chapter cracks him open twice. *"She makes me happy"* — flat, plain, and it's the truest thing he's said in the book, said about Vee to the woman he's inside. And the killer: *"You know she loves you, right?" / "She hasn't said it." / "She doesn't have to." / "She's not the only one in love."* And he *can't tell her she's wrong.* This is the man who will not say four words to Vee across a kitchen — and here his face *"softened before he could do anything about it,"* and Randi *"gave him no smile and no rescue from the sentence."* He can't say it to Vee's face and he can't deny it to Randi's. I've distrusted his withholding for fifty chapters; this is the first time I've seen it cost *him* something, and it made me both softer toward him and more afraid of what happens when this house of unsaid things meets Vee's need to hear it out loud.

**Swoon / pull.** Straightforwardly, as sex, this ran the hottest it's run for me in ages — the reciprocity of it, the taste passed mouth to mouth, the leading and yielding, the cleanup that stops being cleanup. Two people who fit, unperformed, at ease in each other. If I only had the bodies I'd have swooned clean. But I don't only have the bodies, and that's what makes this chapter remarkable rather than just hot: the ache running under it is Vee's name, and every tender beat doubles as dramatic irony so sharp it aches. I was swept up and dread-struck in the same breath, and the book earned both.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** This is past suspicion — it's confirmation, and the confirmation is worse than a scheme would have been. If they were cold, I could root against them cleanly. Instead they're both genuinely, helplessly in love with Vee, and *that's* the danger: the sincerity is the trap, and it's a trap that's closed on the trappers too. What I dread now isn't malice. It's the day Vee learns that the two people who love her most engineered her and hid it — and that the love was real the whole time, which will make it harder, not easier, to survive.

**Erotic charge.** Peak. The wet-kiss open, the neck confession delivered *during* the fucking, the final blowjob-that-isn't-cleanup. Where it went slightly slack was the middle stretch of position changes — *"His hands returned to her waist"* / *"Pace tightened his hands at her waist"* / *"His hands returned to her waist"* — the choreography got a little metronomic, a little same-handed, and I felt the prose treading water between the emotional beats. But those beats carried it.

**Friction.** Only that repetition, and one small thing: a couple of the exchanges are so on-the-nose about the theme (*"You like hearing me want her." / "Yes." / "Why?" / "She makes me happy."*) that I could feel the author making sure I didn't miss it. I wasn't going to miss it. But it's a quibble against a chapter that mostly trusts me.

**The titles.** *"My Pleasure."* Now that I've read it — it detonates on contact. It's Pace's courtly *my pleasure*, the phrase of a man who serves. It's Vee's *pleasure princess* fantasy from the morning before. But sitting in this bed it reads coldest and truest as possession: Vee is *my pleasure* — the shared thing that is ours, the girl they pass between them like the taste passed mouth to mouth. The double meaning of pleasure-as-courtesy and pleasure-as-owned-object is the whole chapter. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it detonated after, which is the best kind.

The volume line — *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION*, title *My Pleasure*. "He who sups with the devil should use a long spoon." Two chapters ago that felt oblique; now it has teeth. Somebody at this table is supping with something, and the long spoon is exactly the distance Randi and Pace keep — loving Vee fully while holding the truth at arm's length. *A WARM RECEPTION* — his house always too warm, the reception of Vee into a world she thinks she wandered into. It all points one way: the warmth is real and the reception was arranged, and I'm braced for the seam to split.

**What I want / dread next.** Guess from here: the neck kiss is going to keep escalating — Vee's body is walking toward Randi as surely as it walked toward Pace, and this chapter tells me Randi *wants* it and is holding the line by making Vee laugh at exactly the wrong moment. I dread the moment the laugh doesn't come in time. And I dread — differently — the moment Vee learns these two are a *they*. Because after this chapter I can't want it cleanly. I want Vee to have what she wants and I know what she wants can't survive the truth intact. Pull to keep reading: total.
