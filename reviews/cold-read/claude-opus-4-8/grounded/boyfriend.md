## Reader reaction

### The felt read

I read this whole chapter with my stomach up in my throat, and I want to be honest that the first thing it did to me was arouse me and the second thing was scare me, in exactly that order — which I think is the chapter's whole cruelty and its whole achievement.

Let me start with the body, because the body is where I lived. That drive out — bare-legged in her father's Corolla, radio off "because she did not want anything in the car with her that might start asking questions" — landed with a plain dread I felt in my own chest. And then the sex. God. The confession-as-currency scene is one of the hottest things I've read in this book, and I say that with my eyes open about what's being done under it. Him going hard *off her shame* — "she'd gone hard off the worst thing she had. But she wasn't annoyed, she was glad" — and then the whole slow economy of it, inches of him traded for inches of truth, "Tell me the real story, and I'll give you what you want" — I was undone by it as prose and as heat. The pacing of the physical against the verbal is masterful: every time she tidies a sentence he stalls her empty, every time she tells the truer, more shameful version he lets her down onto him. It's an interrogation that reads as worship and a seduction that reads as extraction, and I could not always tell which, and neither could she — "she could not have told, even now, which of them she'd been chasing at the end." That last line of the sex flattened me.

But here is where I have to be honest about the unease, because this chapter *earned* it, it isn't me being on guard. The heat is real and so is the thing curdling under it. Watch what Pace actually does across this scene. He isn't jealous. He isn't hurt. A man learns his girlfriend kissed her best friend — a woman — until it "didn't stay a joke," and his response is *appetite* and *curiosity* — "How did this happen?" asked "easy, almost curious." He mines the whole event out of her, orgasm by orgasm, and then, "idle, like a man clearing the last small thing off a table," he asks: "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" And I felt the floor go. Because I know — the reader has known since page one — that Pace and Randi are a couple, that Vee was *chosen*, that this "best year of her life" is a thing the two of them built. So watching him coax her toward wanting the woman he is secretly partnered with, watching him leave her the room to walk into ("He didn't say Randi. He'd stepped around the name like a gentleman stepping around a puddle, and she was so grateful for the room he'd left her that she walked straight into it") — that is not tenderness anymore, or not only. That is a man running a play, and running it while she thanks him for it. I have never distrusted Pace this much, and the chapter did it without a single overt villain-beat. It did it by making his patience feel, for the first time, like *handling*.

And the last knife — his one defense. "You keep saying her name, Vee. Not me." He never defends himself; the checkpoint told me he always leaves her the whole room. So when he *does* explain himself, just this once, Vee reads it as the sweetest thing he's ever done, "a warm blanket." And I read it as the opposite: the one moment he can't afford her aim, so he redirects it — flawlessly — back onto her own wanting. "You keep saying her name" is true, and it's also a maneuver, and the fact that it's both is what left me cold. The blanket, the glass of water put in her hand, the grey blanket tucked up — all of it aftercare that doubles as containment. "She'd gotten away with it," she thinks. She got away with nothing. She walked deeper in and was tucked in for it.

Vee herself — I ache for her past what I can say. The masturbation beat at the top, catching her own hand "like something she'd caught stealing," saying "*I have a boyfriend*" to the dark and reaching for the word "the way you reach for the rail on a stair" — that image gutted me, because it tells me she already knows, at the body level, that she needs a rail. And then the sorting-machine of her own mind at the end, the way she inventories the goodbye kisses and her own hand and the name in her head and files each one as innocent "before it reached her mouth" — "level as a countertop" — that is the most frightened self-deception I've watched her run, and the book knows it's self-deception and lets it stand ajar. "*What is wrong with me,*" she thinks, and has no answer, and "whatever had hold of her was not, even now, anywhere near done." I don't want it to be over. I also dread every inch of what's coming, and those are the same feeling.

The swoon, then, is complicated and total: I was swept up by the sex and horrified by the machinery under it *at the same moment*, and the chapter refused to let me have one without the other. That's the version of "swept up" this book has trained me to. I wasn't rooting for them here the way I rooted at the roadhouse two chapters back — that dinner made me *melt*, all of Pace setting her people alight, Cassie's knighting, "there you are" made flesh. This chapter took that warmth and showed me its underside. The tenderness is still real. That's what's unbearable.

Trust vs. suspicion: this is the first chapter where my suspicion of Pace is fully *textual* and not just structural dread carried from the jacket. He gets hard off her worst secret; he steers her toward the exact appetite that serves him; he defends himself for the first time ever at the precise moment defense is useful. I don't think he's cruel — I think the checkpoint is right that he loves her — and that's what makes it merciless. Randi isn't on the page but she's the third body in the bed, "the woman still in her mouth," and my dread now has a Tuesday attached to it.

Erotic charge: a 3, sustained, the whole engine of the chapter, and it never went clinical because the sex and the confession were the same act. The one place I could feel a slight authorial hand was the density of the inch-by-inch trade — a paragraph or two where I clocked the mechanism ("a reward with a slope to it") a half-beat before I felt it. But it recovered instantly. Almost no friction otherwise; the "I'm not gay" / "I didn't say gay" exchange is the cleanest, coldest little scalpel in the chapter and I didn't see it coming.

**The titles.** "Boyfriend" — I thought at the roadhouse it meant Pace's gift, that plain word said six times to six measuring faces, the credential he finally spent on her. Two chapters later the title recolors hard: here "boyfriend" is the *rail on the stair*, the word she grabs to keep from what she wants, the thing she says to the dark to stop her own hand. It's not the gift anymore; it's the alibi. "*I have a boyfriend,*" she tells herself — and drives twenty minutes to use him to drown out a woman. The word that was warmth is now the walls of the maze. That's a hell of a title turn and it didn't give anything away going in — it sat innocent right up until the chapter walked it into the dark.

*A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* — "he that sups with the devil should have a long spoon." I feel the spoon this chapter. The invitation reached her long before she knew there was one, and here she is stepping through the last door of it thanking the man holding it open. The tagline on my jacket — "*Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.*" — I have never felt it harder than in "You finish when you've finished" and her *choosing* to leave the thong on and not ask herself why. Every yes here is real. That's the horror.

What I want and dread next: Tuesday. Dr. Marsh's room, seven o'clock, side by side, Randi's perfume. The chapter ends aimed straight at it and so am I. *Guess from here:* Pace tells Randi what Vee confessed — or Randi already knows the plan advanced — and the "world she watched from outside" opens by design into the three of them, with Vee walking in convinced every step was hers. I dread it and I'm turning the page.
