## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one got me low in the body and then turned my stomach over, in that order, and I want to be honest that both things happened and neither cancels the other.

First, the heat. The confession mechanic — Pace holding Vee suspended off all but the head of him and trading her inches of himself for truths, "Tell me the real story, and I'll give you what you want" — is one of the most purely erotic things this book has done to me. It's the brunch-edging and the spanking-bet grammar carried into bed and made merciless: he makes her buy back her own pleasure "in the one coin she had," and the toll goes up every time she tidies a sentence. I felt the drag of it. The structural trick where each stall "turned her back to look at the thing she'd smoothed over" — "She moved me around. That wasn't it. That wasn't what had happened" — is doing something genuinely clever, forcing her to un-tidy herself for me as much as for him. And the escalation lands: "You finish when you've finished." I was swept up. I'm not going to pretend I read it at arm's length.

And that's exactly the problem, because I know what Vee doesn't. I have known since page one that Pace and Randi are a couple, that they planned her, that Randi is now helplessly in love with her. So watching Pace lie in the dark and calmly draw out of Vee, orgasm by rationed orgasm, the full erotic account of kissing his own secret girlfriend — and get hard off it, twice — is not a man handling the ordinary greed of "two girls." Vee reads it that way ("the ordinary greed of that, nothing she couldn't handle") and I let her have her read. But mine underneath it is that this is the architect getting the field report, and the field report is that the trap is working. He isn't jealous because he can't be jealous — there's no rival, there's his partner. That's the chill. The hottest thing in the chapter and the most disturbing thing in the chapter are the same thing, and I can't get a knife between them.

The two lines that turned the screw hardest for me were the closers. "Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?" — asked "easy, idle, like a man clearing the last small thing off a table," and pointedly *not* naming Randi, "stepping around the name like a gentleman stepping around a puddle." Vee is grateful for the room he leaves her and "walked straight into it." I read that room he leaves as the next move — the door he and Randi built, held open at exactly the angle that lets her keep saying no while her body says the other thing. And then: "You keep saying her name, Vee. Not me... It's all right. I don't mind." Gentle, no edge. Vee experiences it as the sweetest thing he's ever done, him defending himself when he never defends himself, a warm blanket. I experienced it as the trap purring. He doesn't mind because it's going according to plan. I felt genuinely cold reading "It's all right. I don't mind."

Do I trust him less now? Yes — this is the chapter that tipped me. Up to here I've held the care and the control in balance, given the tenderness its due. Here the tenderness is deployed. The aftercare — the grey blanket, the water put in her hand and made to drink, the mouth in her hair "keeping her gently lit" — is real on the page and I distrust every gesture of it, because it arrives right after he's extracted the thing and confirmed the appetite, and it functions to make her feel she "got away with it." She didn't get away with anything. She reported in.

Where I ache: Vee. God, Vee. "I have a boyfriend, she told the dark... a word for a girl who had one and only one... she reached for it the way you reach for the rail on a stair." That broke my heart a little, because I know the rail is rotten — the boyfriend is one of the two people running her. She grips the safest fact she has and it's the softest ground in her life. And the whole chapter is her using him — driving the county road at 1 a.m., bare-legged, "her hand already down inside his boxers," needing "the plain male fact of him... to crowd the other thing out of her head" — trying to fuck Randi out of herself with the one person who cannot possibly help her do it, and failing. The ending is devastating and quiet: twice, the best sex there is, and "her mind gone straight down the county road and back to campus and into a warm cluttered room with a girl in it, and she could not make it come back. What is wrong with me, she thought. She did not have an answer." Nothing is wrong with her. She wants a woman. She isn't allowed to know it yet, and the two people best positioned to help her know it are the two with the most invested in the not-knowing. The loneliness of that closing image — her lying in her boyfriend's arms unable to bring her mind home — is the truest, saddest thing in the run.

The moment I felt the interior life crack open widest: the confession about Harrison. She gives Pace "a clean little picture with a *him* in the middle of it," and then can't leave the false thing "lodged in his chest, hurting him for no reason," so "the truth tore loose to kill it" — "it was Randi. I kissed Randi... and I kissed her back." That she'd rather hand him the real, dangerous thing than let a lie hurt him — that's the shape of her love for him, and it's the exact tenderness he's exploiting. I believe her love completely. That's what makes it merciless.

Trust/suspicion: this is the least ambivalent I've been about Pace since the book started. I'm not certain of his interior in this scene — the page leaves it ajar whether the arousal is a man's ordinary greed or the architect's satisfaction, and I'll hold that ajar — but the weight has shifted hard toward dread. Randi's not on the page and I distrust her by proxy; the whole confession is, unknowably to Vee, an account of Randi's own mouth. My one clean note: Cassie asleep with her window cracked its two inches, the one cool honest air in the building, the person Vee sneaks out past. I miss her already as the floor under this.

Erotic charge: peaks at 3 and sustains — the rationing-for-truth is the engine and it never goes slack. If anything the arousal is complicated for me by knowledge in a way that made it *more* charged, not less, which is its own uncomfortable thing to report.

Friction: almost none as craft — this is tightly built. The only place I felt the thumb was Vee's over-explaining in the afterglow ("I don't keep things from you," "It didn't mean anything," "I'm not gay" / "I didn't say gay") — but that's clearly *her* thumb on her own scale, the character over-explaining, not the author, so it read as true rather than told. It made me wince for her, correctly.

The title: "Boyfriend." In "Among Friends" the word was a gift — Pace spent it out loud, six times, and it landed in Vee like the first swallow of something hot. Here the same word is a handrail she grips to keep from falling toward Randi, and it doesn't hold. That's a brutal recoloring in the space of a couple of chapters: the word that meant *claimed* now means *the thing I hold onto so I don't have to look at what I want* — and by the last line even sex with the boyfriend twice over can't bring her mind back from the woman. It's the most exact title yet, because it names the one thing Vee is using to not-know herself. And under it, the meaner reading only I can have: the "boyfriend" is the co-architect. The safest word in her mouth is the biggest lie in her life, and she doesn't know it's a lie, and the man who is the lie tucked the blanket up around her.

The book title *A Polite Invitation* and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" — this chapter is that tagline in the flesh. Every "yes" here is hers: she drove there, she reached for him, she told the truth she could have kept, she said no to the woman question of her own free will with "no pause in front of it, no catch — level as a countertop." Nothing was taken. And it's the freeness of it that's the horror — the door held open at just the right angle, the room left for her to walk into. *With a Long Spoon* still sits over it: sup with the devil and use a long spoon; the spoon here is the word "boyfriend," and it's much too short.

What I want / dread next (guesses from here): I dread Tuesday, which the chapter itself circles in red — "she had two nights to find a face for it." She has to sit down beside Randi with all of this in her mouth, and Randi will read her in a heartbeat, and Randi *wants* this. I expect the next move belongs to Randi. My guess from here is that Pace's "Do you want to sleep with a woman" is a door being opened deliberately, and that the two of them, separately or together, will let Vee "arrive on her own" at the thing they've steered her toward — and that the tragedy the book keeps promising is that it will be real for her exactly when she learns it was arranged. I want, painfully, for Cassie to catch her. I don't expect a rescue. I don't need one — but I'm bracing.
