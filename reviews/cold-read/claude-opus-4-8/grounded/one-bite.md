## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one worked on me and then it frightened me, in that order, and the switch between the two is the whole chapter. It's a brunch. It's two women at a sunny table telling each other a sexy story. And by the end I felt like I'd watched a hand close around something warm and soft without a single raised voice.

The first two thirds are pure pleasure to read. Vee walks in flustered from the cold and Randi turns "her whole face came on, warm and sure, as though of all the people and all the mornings this was the one she had been keeping the table for." I love how the chapter lets me feel exactly what Vee feels — that being chosen, that lamp swinging toward you — while at the same time I'm sitting a foot back going, of course she keeps the table warm, that's the job. The mimosa bit is small and perfect and quietly devastating: Vee ordering "the cheapest yes on the card, and no one needed to know that was why," and Randi immediately saying "Two" and never opening the wine list, so it reads to Vee as sisterhood, the same drink, the same easy morning — while I know one of them is counting pennies on a maxed card and the other doesn't glance at a total ever. The class gap sits right there under the sweetness and neither of them names it. That's the book's whole trick in miniature.

The telling of the "We Find Out" story is where the heat is, and it's real heat, because I just read that scene in full and now I get to watch Vee relive it out loud across a tablecloth. That's the erotic engine here and it's shameless about it: every confession lands twice on her body — "the print of his hand arrive again where the seat met her, low and bright and not remotely finished," having to "press her thighs quietly together under the cloth." "He checked." God, the way that lands. The build is genuinely arousing and I felt it — but the thing I clocked hard, the thing that's not innocent, is that Randi is not just a delighted audience. She's a technician. "You just yada-yada'd straight past the good part. How does a girl get from a pantsing to a bare bottom. Walk me." "What was the bet." "And how was it settled?" Quiet, quieter, coming down each time, no less warm — she is steering the confession toward its most exposed point with total control, and the checkpoint already told me she feeds this back to Pace in bed. So the whole time I'm turned on I'm also watching a debrief dressed as girl-talk.

And then the second movement, Randi's Radford story, is where the chapter tips from warm into something I actually distrust — and it earned the distrust, it didn't manufacture it. Randi tells a story that just happens to feature a "young Robert Redford" who "kissed like he meant it. Forceful, but he knew when to ease off and leave you the room to come after him" — which is almost word for word how Pace's whole courtship of Vee has been described to me. And the boy gets a phone call, an emergency, "a friend in some trouble," and has to leave. On Vee's night with Pace. The timing sat me straight up. I can't prove it and the page doesn't let me — but the shape of it is: Randi is telling Vee a fantasy built out of Vee's own romance, and possibly one where the man is Pace himself and the "emergency" was Vee. Whether that's true or I'm seeing ghosts, the *function* is unmistakable: the story exists to walk Vee right up to the edge and over. "He is just starting to find *my* dark corners—" and Vee's body answers the phrase; then "panties to the side, knees wide, doing a private dance" with Randi's "clear eyes right on Vee's." That's not a friend sharing. That's someone placing a picture inside another person on purpose and watching it take.

And it takes. The bathroom scene is the most unsettling and the most accomplished thing in the chapter. Vee flees, gets herself soaked and going, and at the first give of her own hand "Randi's face arrived behind her eyes. Clear, and fond, and near, as though Vee were the best thing in the room." And then the line that actually chilled me: "But it was not the thinking, it was the looking. She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was, and the two would not hold in one body." So she *stops*. She denies herself the peak — "the bright wanting curdled into something low and ashamed and obedient. Frustrated and cowed in the same breath." That's the darkest note the book has hit for me since the bench. Randi's imagined gaze doesn't free Vee's desire, it *governs* it — it reaches all the way into a locked stall and takes the orgasm away. That's not a swoon. That's a leash, and Vee can't see the hand on the other end.

And then "Sticky fingers?" Reader, I gasped a little. The way the chapter plays it — Vee's whole world tilts, named and known and seen — and then Randi tips her head at the pastries and it was, apparently, only about the glaze. "She could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one. She sat down. She did not find out." I love that the chapter refuses to resolve it, and I'm going to honor that and leave it ajar too, because the last image forces me to: Randi took exactly *one neat bite* of a pastry and never touched it again — and at the very end, on Vee's lip, "a thread of sweetness, sugar and butter and something warm under it, the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." Which means the goodbye kiss carried the pastry from Randi's mouth to Vee's. So the sweetness Vee tastes is Randi's. "Sticky fingers" wasn't innocent, or it doesn't matter whether it was — the kiss made it true either way. That is one of the most elegant, quietly predatory closing images I've read in a while, and it's built entirely out of a pastry nobody would look at twice.

How I feel about each of them, moved or not. Vee: I ache for her more, not less. She is so alive and funny and brave in the telling, and so completely undefended in the wanting, that watching her arousal get harvested — by the man in memory and the woman in the room — is genuinely painful even as it's hot. My tenderness toward her went up. Randi: my wariness went up sharply, and cleanly, because the *page* did it this time, not my carry-forward knowledge. The Radford story and "Sticky fingers" and the stolen-sweetness kiss are the text earning the dread. And yet — the checkpoint held open that Randi genuinely loves her, and the "best friend in the whole world" beat is played so warm ("I couldn't bear it if there was something between us") that I can't flatten her into pure operator. That she might mean the love AND be running the extraction, both fully true, is the thing that unsettles me most. Pace isn't on the page but he's all over it — the Redford double, the man whose whole method is leaving a woman "the room to come after him." My trust in him took a small hit by proxy.

Swoon vs. unease: the swoon is real in the first half — the mimosa sisterhood, the delighted shriek, "There's my girl," the luminous "best friend" beat on the cold sidewalk. The unease is real in the second half and it's *earned*, not imported. What impresses me is that the chapter doesn't make me choose: the warmth is the delivery system for the reach. The heat is the reach.

Erotic charge: high and sustained, peaking in the retelling ("He checked") and then displaced, brilliantly, into the bathroom — where the book does something it hasn't done to me before: it takes the release away, on purpose, and makes the *denial* the erotic and psychological event. That's the most sophisticated move in the chapter.

Friction: almost none. If I'm reaching — the Radford-boy parallel to Pace is laid close enough that a slightly warier Vee might have caught it, and there's a faint sense of the author winking at me over her head. But it read as intended dramatic irony, not a thumb on the scale, and I'll allow it.

Titles. "One Bite" — before reading I'd have guessed appetite, the book's whole hunger motif. After reading it's the pastry: Randi's single "neat bite," and the bite of sweetness that ends up on Vee's lip without Vee ever eating. It also rhymes with the bar-fight blonde's "one bite" of a hook Vee took at the Rusty Nail. It's a title about a small, deliberate taste — one bite is all it takes to plant a thing in someone. It illuminates rather than gives away; I didn't understand it until the last paragraph, which is right. The volume title *A Polite Invitation* and "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" sit heavy on this chapter — everything here is Vee volunteering, reaching, confessing, and the leash tightens by consent. *With a Long Spoon* I still read as "he who sups with the devil needs a long spoon" — and this chapter is a brunch table where the spoon is very short and Vee doesn't know who she's supping with.

What I want / dread next (guesses from here): I want to know if that phone-call detail means the Radford night was Pace, or was invented to *be* Pace — and I suspect I'll never get it clean, which is fine. I dread, a little, the moment Vee's disavowed wanting for Randi and the harvested-for-Pace machinery collide — the bathroom scene feels like a threshold, not a peak. I want to keep reading badly.
