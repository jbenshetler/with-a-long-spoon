# Cold read (grounded) — Fed

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This is the second brunch, and I clocked it as a mirror of the first one before Randi opened her mouth — same single heavy card, same reading-the-right-side-first, same mimosa priced against Thursday, same porch light lying on everything "without asking." The book knows it's rhyming, and I let it, because the rhyme is the point: we watched this extraction happen once at the tomato-soup lunch, and here it comes again, softer, and I'm reading it now with the first one loaded in my hand.

What got me, honestly, is how gently it goes this time — and how that gentleness is exactly what should worry me and mostly doesn't, in the moment. At the first lunch Randi hunted the "challenges," cornered Vee into "actually it was really hot," and then, right when Vee's secret was half out of her, shut the door: "You don't have to tell me." That closing was cruel in a way I only understood a beat late. This time Randi does something subtler and, on the page, kinder: she takes the thing Vee is deflecting — "you keep saying he, like you just stood there and let it happen to you" — and turns it around until Vee is the subject of her own desire. "I wanted it. And I took it. For once." And Randi says "Good," and puts her hand over Vee's painted one and leaves it there. As a reader I felt that land as a genuine gift. Vee has spent this whole book apologizing for her own appetite, and here's a woman handing it back to her clean, telling her the wanting is the lucky thing, not the shameful one. If I didn't know what I know, I'd call it the best friend of anyone's life.

But I do know. And the machinery under the warmth is doing exactly what it did last time, only better hidden. Randi opens with her own "sad little story" — the beautiful ex, the boy in a hurry, "at least I got dinner out of it" — and I read that now as a move, not a confidence: she goes small and rueful first so Vee will feel safe going big. "Give me something to live on. Be cruel." "I'm running on fumes here." The whole frame is Randi positioning herself as the hungry one, the one who needs feeding, so that Vee's telling becomes generosity instead of exposure. And the specific thing she's after is the same thing she was after over the soup — the middle, the part that's "only hers." Vee folds it "back into its tissue," waves at the air, *and then, well*. And this time Randi doesn't force that panel open. She takes the toenails instead. "You paint the toes for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands." "Why'd you pick it?" — frosted plum, and Vee can't answer, and it comes up in her face, and Randi reads the face: "That face isn't about a color, is it." That is the same operation as before, run so tenderly it reads as care. She's not prying the secret out. She's teaching Vee to hand it over gladly, to experience the surrender as being seen. Which is precisely, chillingly, what Pace does to her too. The two of them do the same thing to her from two directions, and this chapter let me feel them rhyme.

Where it swooned me: the sheet. Vee's own telling of after — "I took the top sheet off the bed and wrapped up in it and padded out after him... sat at his table in his sheet and finally drank it, and watched him finish making our dinner. The oil going in the pan and starting to tick, the window black behind him, near midnight, neither of us saying a word and nothing missing." That's the warmest thing in the chapter and it's Vee's, not Randi's — her own memory, offered up. "I don't think I've ever felt anything better in my life." I believed it completely, and it ached, because I'm holding it next to the fact that this exact contentment is the product she was chosen to be walked into. Randi's answer — "That's not a story, honey. That's a painting. Keep that one." — is lovely and is also her taking the one piece Vee volunteered and pressing it into a keepsake, curating Vee's own happiness for her.

And the line I keep turning: "You knew how that night was going to end before you ever got in the car. You drove over there sure. So why are you telling me like it's a confession?" That's the whole book's thesis coming out of Randi's mouth as friendship — every yes freely given, the girl was sure, nothing was taken. Randi is teaching Vee to see her own consent, to own it, to stop calling it a confession. On the surface it's the healthiest thing anyone's said to Vee. Underneath, it's the con reassuring the mark that she chose this — which she did, which is the horror of it. The jacket's "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem" was practically spoken aloud at that table, and I felt the floor go a little cold under the warmth.

Trust vs. suspicion: no new evidence against Randi that a naive reader couldn't rationalize — Vee rationalizes all of it, the way she rationalized the goodbye kiss last time ("French thing"). But I'm not naive anymore, the book made sure of that in chapter one, so the suspicion I carry in is confirmed by pattern, not by any tell on this page. What the page itself earns is the sense of a practiced hand: "I have to tell you my sad little story first, to clear the air" is a woman who knows the order the moves go in. The Vee-doesn't-look-down-the-street detail at the end — she's stopped scanning the windows for witnesses — is quietly the most ominous thing here. She's past the reflex of shame that would have protected her. She's being made comfortable.

Erotic charge: low and deliberately so — this is the retelling chapter, the heat is all in the previous night's echo. "Frosted plum," the toes painted "for a man who's going to have your feet bare in his hands," the new underwear as "a campaign" — that's the register, warm-and-clothed, and it worked on me as tenderness more than arousal. The actual heat lives in "Famished," and this chapter is the after-image of it, which is correct. I didn't want it to be hotter.

The one thing I want to name as a reader, not a suspicion but a flavor: this chapter is very close to the shape of the last lunch, and there's a small risk of the beat repeating — brunch, price the drink, Randi's decoy story, the extraction, the goodbye kiss. It didn't bore me, because the gentleness is a real variation and Vee's own contribution (the sheet, "I wanted it and I took it") is new ground. But it's the second time on this exact set piece, and a third would start to feel like a mechanism showing.

TITLES

"Fed" — after reading, it's the hunger motif brought all the way home, and it cuts two ways at once, which I like. Vee is fed: chosen, lit, handed her own appetite back as a gift, told she's lucky to be this gone. And Randi feeds — "give me something to live on," "I'm running on fumes here," she comes to the table hungry for Vee's story and leaves full. The person being fed and the person feeding are the same lunch. Set against "Famished" two chapters back — Vee finally eating without watching herself, the first meal she took with no seam between heat and shame — "Fed" is the sequel state: she's not starving anymore, she's being fed, by both of them, on schedule. It doesn't give anything away before I read it; it colors darker on the second thought, because being fed is a passive thing, something done to you, and that's exactly the register of what's happening to her.

*A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / the tagline — the front carries no tagline (the blurb's closing beat is "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem"), and that line was almost literally dramatized at the table today. "With a long spoon" — you sup with the devil with a long spoon — sits heavy over this exact scene: two lunches now where Randi feeds Vee and feeds on her, all warmth and reach across a small table. The "polite invitation" is every one of these gentle open doors — "you don't have to tell me," "why are you telling me like it's a confession" — each one an invitation Vee walks through gladly. The titles keep promising that the courtesy is the trap, and this chapter is courtesy at its most disarming.

WHAT I WANT / DREAD NEXT: I want the dress finished and worn — the induction is the loaded gun on the mantel, the thing Pace is building toward, the night that's "hers." I dread the moment these two threads meet — the moment Vee learns Randi and Pace are one operation — and I notice the book is deepening her happiness and her openness right before that, which is the cruelest possible place to put it. Guess from here, marked as a guess: the induction, in the burgundy dress he made, is where the walls come down, and Randi will be there.
