## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one swept me up and then, right at the end, put one cold finger on the back of my neck — and I want to be honest that both were real, in that order.

For most of its length "Grace" is the most straightforwardly beautiful chapter I've had from these two. The cabin is gorgeous and unshowy — board-on-board, weathered to pewter, the kind of place she decides "looks like something he might have made," and of course it does. The tikka masala carried up a mountain in a thermos so it'd still be hot for her on a freezing porch, the dry spiced cider that "should not work" and does — I melted at exactly the thing she melted at, that he "sat at home and planned which drink went with dinner," and his answer, flat and total: "I wanted to please you." That undoes me the way it undoes her. He carries her into rooms she isn't even in. That's the warmest thing the book has ever shown me about him, and I felt it clean.

The key got me too. A box he made with his hands, oiled and satiny, and inside a plain brass key on pale silk, and "You're more than a guest in my house. I've never given a key to anyone." I believe it as belonging, and I ached with her at the small precise gap she marks — he says *house*, and "some small exact part of her, the part that had never been quite sure what to call home, marked the word and waited half a beat for the other one, the one with a hearth in it. The other one didn't come." She's learned to let it go. That patience of hers is heartbreaking and I love her for it and I want to shake her out of it at the same time.

Two small things caught in me while I was swooning, though, and I don't think I invented them. First, the silk in the box: "so pale it was nearly not a color, an oyster paleness," and "she had the odd passing sense she had felt that silk against her skin somewhere before, and could not think where, and the not-knowing slid off her." It didn't slide off *me*. I don't have a fixed memory to pin it to, but the déjà-vu is planted on purpose, and in a book where a bench's true purpose hides under an innocent tabletop, a pale silk she can't place makes me lean in, not away. Second — tiny — when she describes him in the mirror ("brown hair, cleft chin, that little birthmark by his ear"), "something crossed his face — a shadow, there and gone." I have no idea what, and the page won't tell me. But this is a man who was once *seen past the face*, and being described by his surface put a flicker in him. I'm holding it open. It's the one moment in an adoring chapter where he had a private weather I couldn't read.

The mirror sequence itself is the erotic center and it is sustained, graphic, and extraordinary. Him cataloguing her blind, inch by inch, in unsparing daylight — the spill of her breast being "the part I want most," the wet being "the most honest thing I've ever stood in front of." He names the exact things her mother's voice taught her to hide, and *wants* them, and I felt the guard come down in her because it came down in me. And the turn near the end — "A beautiful body is just a fact... it's whose this one is. That you meet a thing that frightens you and you walk straight into it — like now. Standing here. Letting me see you." — is the closest thing to a declaration in the whole book, and the flannel over her eyes going damp before she knows why nearly did the same to me.

And then the trapdoor I've been braced for since page one opened a half-inch, quietly, and it's the best and most unsettling thing here. "Do you want relief?" — she nods, hips tipping, sure his hands are coming — and they don't. "Show me." He makes her masturbate, blind, in front of him, in the light. The scene is scorching and I was in it. But afterward, in the dark, sated, half-attending, her hand already reaching for him again, he asks the real thing: "Would you share that pleasure with me? Would you tell me, when you touch yourself thinking of me?" And she says yes "the way you say yes to something small." That's the line the whole chapter was built to earn, and it chilled me precisely because it's dressed as tenderness — because *he* frames it as closeness ("it makes me feel close to you") and I believe he even means it. He has spent the night teaching her body that it is watched, and the last beat converts the last private room she has — the thing she does behind a locked door — into something she'll now report to him. The most careful man she knows asks for the one thing left, at the exact moment she's too melted to weigh it. "Psychologically merciless in its tenderness" is the jacket's phrase and this is the page proving it. I don't feel betrayed by it; I feel the book meaning what it said.

So my trust in Pace hasn't collapsed — the care is real, I keep insisting on that — but this is the first time the *care itself* was the instrument. He didn't take anything against her. He arranged a weekend so perfect that a yes to being watched forever came out of her easy and breathy and "barely attended at all." That's the machine working exactly as advertised, and I'm the reader who's supposed to fall for it as she does, and I did, and I also flinched. Both.

On the "you" climax — her coming apart on the single word *you*, then him past all his own asking, sucking her off his own fingers "like a man who had been waiting all night" — that's heat at full graphic center, and it's also her getting the thing she's wanted forever: proof she can make the careful man lose the thread. She reads it as power. I read it as power too, and as him getting the confession *you, you, you* he was fishing for with "What are you thinking about?" Both true. This book is very good at making the same beat mean two opposite things at once.

TITLES

"Grace" — sits warm on the surface: grace as the unmerited gift (a key, being adored past your shame), maybe grace before a meal (all that feeding). But laid over the chapter it curdles a little on second thought — grace extended by the one who planned you, an "invitation" that reached her before she knew there was one. It's a gift, and it's also the softest possible name for an acquisition. I don't think it gave anything away going in; it recolors gently after.

"A Polite Invitation" / "With a Long Spoon" — the series line finally rang a bell for me here: you sup with the devil with a long spoon, keep your distance from what feeds you. This chapter is all feeding — the thermos, the cider, the naming of nine spices — and the spoon getting shorter, the last inch of privacy handed over across a dark bed. The tagline in my packet, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," is basically annotated by that final "Yes... the way you say yes to something small." It was freely given. That's exactly the problem.

WANT / EXPECT / DREAD NEXT

I want to keep going badly — this was a peak. I dread (a guess from here) that the little reporting-yes she gave so lightly becomes a thread with teeth, that "tell me when you touch yourself thinking of me" is a door, not a moment. I keep waiting for the silk, the key, the whole engineered tenderness to touch the thing she still can't see — Randi, the plan. And I notice Randi wasn't in this chapter at all, which after the roadhouse and the mixer kiss felt almost loud by absence. Some part of me expects her back, and expects that when she comes back, this perfect weekend will look different in her light.
