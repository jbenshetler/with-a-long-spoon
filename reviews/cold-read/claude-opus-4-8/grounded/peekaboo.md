# Cold read (grounded) — Peekaboo

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one swept me clean up, and I'm not going to pretend it didn't. After twenty chapters of bracing for the trap under every tender thing, I let this chapter just be what it was on the page, and what it was on the page is one of the most purely joyful things I've read in the book. The dress reveal got me exactly where it was aimed — "he'd taken all that trouble and then taken the further trouble to hide the trouble, so the only thing left for her to see was the part that looked like no trouble at all." I felt that land in my chest before I could think about it. And the detail that closed it — that he'd been up the night before finishing it "at some hour with no business being awake in it," while she slept across town — is the kind of specific, unglamorous devotion that undoes me. I believe the feeling in this chapter. That's the thing I want to say plainly: whatever the origin, what's on this page reads as real, and it worked on me as real.

Pace: this is the warmest I've felt toward him since I first met him. The kiss at the door that goes deep and then he *breaks it* — "grinning, helpless with it, the way a kid can't keep a surprise in his mouth" — is such a lovely inversion of his usual courtly control. For once he's the one who can't wait, and his impatience is about her seeing the thing he made, not about taking her. That undercuts, just for a chapter, the shadow I carry about him. I know what I know from the checkpoint — the game, Randi, the engineering — but this chapter gave me almost nothing to feed that unease, and I'm not going to manufacture a shadow the page didn't earn. The one small cold spot: the lining. "A soft wall set into the only parts of her the dress had decided not to give away." He's decided, with total precision, exactly how much of her the world gets and how much stays hers. That's tender and it's also *control* — he's curating her display down to the square inch. I noticed it. I didn't recoil from it. But it's the one thread that connects back to the man who plans.

Vee: I love her, and this chapter is her most triumphant. The whole undressing sequence — finding the underwear line cutting the dress's clean line, and then the *choice* to take it away herself — is the book's thesis made physical: "she was the one who'd taken the edge away... the shame of having stripped herself bare on purpose to be looked at, and it crossed into wanting almost before it was shame at all, and she let it come." That's her whole arc in one gesture, and it's her doing it, not being done to. The mother's voice still fires ("*Floozy.*") but it fires and *loses* now — she stays, she looks, "the looking stopped being shame." I rooted for her the whole way.

Swoon: peak is the reveal itself, then the second peak is the walk to him — "she strode, the slit swinging open on her bare leg... the length of her given and taken back and given." The prose earns the strut. The kiss where she hooks her bared leg around him and "there was nothing left for either of them to decide" — that phrase is doing beautiful work, because deciding has been the whole erotic engine of this book and here it just dissolves. Lovely.

Erotic charge: high and warm, though this chapter is more swoon than heat — the actual sex is elided ("nothing left for either of them to decide" / "After"). I didn't miss it. The charge here is in the dress and the self-exposure, and it's genuinely erotic in the way that matters to me: the arousal is inseparable from the interior turn. It never went clinical.

Trust vs. suspicion: honestly, low suspicion this chapter, and I want to be honest that the text didn't hand me new fuel. The daydream at the end — walking into the induction late, on purpose, "in a thing that looked flown in from Milan," letting the room turn — is the closest thing to a flicker, because it's *display*, being looked at as her purpose, and I know that being-looked-at is the exact appetite Randi and Pace selected her for. "It just felt like what she was for." That line has a faint double bottom to it: she means it as liberation, and I can hear, underneath, the shape of a thing designed. But I'm not going to overread it. It sat as a small chill under a warm scene, no more.

Friction: almost none. The prose is doing that run-on, breath-held thing at the door ("and then it wasn't a hug. His face was in her hair, and then it wasn't in her hair") that could tip into mannered, but it read as arousal's own rhythm and I bought it. No thumb on the scale that I felt.

The title — "Peekaboo": before the chapter it meant nothing to me. After, it's doing quiet, unsettling work. On the surface it's the slit falling open and shut — "the length of her given and taken back and given," the leg appearing and vanishing, a game of showing and hiding. Innocent, playful, the froth of the scene. But peekaboo is also a game an adult plays *at* a child, a thing done to someone who delights in it without seeing it's structured. And the chapter is threaded with hiding: he hid the trouble so she'd see no trouble; the lining hides the parts he decided to keep. So the title recolors on a second beat — the whole chapter is a curated reveal, someone deciding what she gets to see. That's oblique and a little cold and I like it. It didn't give anything away before I read it; it deepened after.

Book title / series / tagline: "A Polite Invitation" and "With a Long Spoon" — the spoon proverb (sup with the devil, use a long spoon) keeps promising that someone here is dining with something dangerous at arm's length, and I still can't tell if Vee is the guest or the meal. The tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." sat over this whole chapter like a watermark — because every yes here *was* hers: the undressing, the underwear, the stride. That's what makes the tagline chilling rather than reassuring. This chapter is the tagline's best exhibit: freely given, gladly, and the problem is precisely that it was.

What I want / dread next: I want more of Vee at full height like this — but the checkpoint's cold facts haven't gone anywhere, and a chapter this happy makes me brace. The last line — "she lay there happy, watching the dress go dark on the door, getting away with something" — is the one that tightens my stomach. She thinks she's getting away with something. I know two people arranged for her to feel exactly that. *Guess from here:* the higher and freer she climbs, the closer the discovery — that Randi and Pace know each other, that she was chosen — and I dread it because she's done nothing but bloom.
