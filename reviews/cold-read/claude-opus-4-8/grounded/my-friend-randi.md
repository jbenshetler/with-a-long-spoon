# Cold read (grounded) — My Friend Randi

## Reader reaction

FELT READ

This one worked on me sideways, and it worked hard — because I read "Fairytale" and "Old Acquaintances" right before it, so I walk into this room already knowing everything Randi is not saying, and the chapter uses that against me with a precision that made my stomach turn over while my chest also ached.

Vee first. She comes in wrecked, "like a raccoon who lost a fight," off a red-eye she cried through, and I feel every mile of that drive with her — "every mile of it ran toward a town that had Pace in it and not one inch of him for her." What breaks my heart is that I've just spent two whole chapters inside her New Year's misery, building a blonde-model rival "on the nights I felt stupid for missing him this badly," and here she brings that exact invented ghost into Randi's room and hands it over to be dismantled. And Randi dismantles it beautifully — "fear needs a face to point at, so you gave it one" — and Randi is right, factually right, Sheri is not that, and I know it because I sat in the diner with them. So I'm in the horrible position of watching good, true, tender counsel do its work, and being unable to trust a word of the mouth delivering it. That's the whole book in one scene.

Randi. God. This is the chapter that moved my feeling about her, and it moved it in two directions at once. On the surface she is the best friend anyone could have — she holds the wrecked girl before she looks at a single new thing from Gstaad, sweeps "dresses from Gstaad, a coat in its plastic" onto the floor because "the wrecked girl in her doorway came first." Read cold that's devotion. But I read "Fairytale" first, so I know that coat and those dresses are the props of a night she spent feeling nothing, and I know the sentence she wrote herself in that hotel room: "Let Vee arrive at it on her own. Keep the brunches exactly as they were." And here she is, doing exactly that — steering Vee back to Pace, coaching her toward the coat-and-nothing-under-it, keeping the machine running, keeping her gorgeous close and never spending the word. The counsel is real and it's also handling. Both. The hug held "past where a hug stops, the beat longer and the beat after that" — and I know why now, I know what she decided over a sleeping stranger in Switzerland, and it made the long hug unbearable in the best way.

The gut-punch is the retelling. When Vee begs for the real New Year story, I already have the real one, and it is one of the loneliest things I've read in this book — the cold she "brought in with her and would carry back out," the perfect man who "never found her underneath." And what Randi hands Vee is the fairytale, polished: the lift line, the named children, the pale blue dress, the ball. She's true to every fact and false to the whole thing. And then the tell that gutted me: Randi, "who had told Vee across a dozen tables exactly what a man's mouth had done and where, stopped." "And then it got very late, and we went up." The one time she goes discreet is the one time the night was empty. Vee reads it as "discretion, the one thing even Randi kept behind the door," and I read it as the truth being unspeakable because there was nothing there. "Everywhere but the bedroom, and the bedroom's the room I actually live in." That line is the truest thing Randi says all chapter and Vee laughs at it as a quip.

Swoon and pull — it's not a heat chapter, and I didn't want it to be. The pull here is the ache: Vee wanting "the easy forward motion of it, a man who'd build the whole future out loud in the first hour and not be afraid of one word in it," while she's starving for one word from a man who won't build anything out loud. That contrast is devastating and it's doing real work — she's envying John's fluency out loud to the woman who found John's fluency worthless. Where it landed hottest for me was the shame-turning-to-heat beat at the end, the coat gambit: "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it." That's the engine of this whole book and it's rendered clean here — the floozie voice fires, "there was a word for that girl and it was already in her mouth," and Randi walks her down off it word by word: "That's not a slut, gorgeous. That's the bravest thing a woman can do." I felt the pull of that even while every alarm I have was going off, because Randi is scripting Vee's most intimate gesture and calling it Vee's own courage.

Trust vs. suspicion — this is not a chapter where the text newly earns suspicion so much as one where the suspicion I already carry gets fed a feast. The thing that's genuinely, freshly off — and the page earns it — is the shoes. Randi presses "a pair of black heels" into her hands: "wear these. Just the thing for tonight." Vee's "past looking at anything," "grateful only that here was one piece of tonight already decided for her; Randi always knew the shoes." That "already decided for her" chilled me. The whole scene is Randi deciding for Vee while making Vee feel she's deciding — "a thing being decided for her and by her at once." The book told me that phrase; it's the exact seam of the plan.

Pace, offstage — my read of him warmed, oddly, because Randi's reconstruction of his side ("a boy holding himself still so it won't show how much you got him," "cornered") lines up with what I saw in the diner, where Sheri got him to admit "I want Vee. I miss her" and "I can't" say the word. So Randi is telling Vee a true thing about Pace. He is not with anyone else; he is stuck exactly as described. That the true diagnosis reaches Vee only through the mouth of the woman co-running the game is the sick beauty of it.

Friction — very little. The prose is doing a lot with the double-knowledge and I didn't feel a thumb on the scale; if anything it trusts me to hold both layers without underlining. Brooke's warmth ("Oh, honey") is the one place I paused — it's kind and it's also the sorority's smooth surface, and Vee "shrank inside her coat," which felt right and unforced. Nothing rang false.

The titles. "My Friend Randi" — before I read it I'd have taken it flat; after, it's bitter and precise. It's Vee's phrase, Vee's frame, the innocent name for the one relationship in the book that is least what its name says. "The only girl she could talk to about real things" — and the real thing between them is the one thing that never gets said, on either side. The title is Vee's blindness worn as a banner. The book's title, A Polite Invitation, and With a Long Spoon — the long spoon is the one you sup with the devil with, and this chapter is the spoon: Randi feeding Vee her own comfort at arm's length, close enough to nourish, careful never to touch the thing underneath. The tagline on the blurb page, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," is the whole chapter — Vee is about to walk to Pace's door and hand him everything, and it will be her bravest free choice, and Randi wrote it. The cover carries no front tagline; the blurb's closing line is the one doing the work.

What I want and dread next (guesses from here): I expect the coat-and-nothing gambit to land — Vee goes to Pace tonight, and it "works," because Randi's read of him is accurate. What I dread is that it works, and the reconciliation seals over the unnamed thing again, and Vee never learns that the plan she thinks she's improvising was handed to her on the stairs. I want to know whether Randi's own cost — the long hugs, the stopped sentence, "I'm so jealous I could scream" said "warm and easy and it passed" — keeps rising until it breaks the cover she swore in Gstaad to keep. That's the thread I'm reading for now: not whether Vee gets Pace back, but when Randi's love stops fitting behind the word.

STRUCTURED BLOCK

Cast present (in person): Vee, Randi. Also physically present and speaking: the unknown blonde with wet hair (opens the door), Brooke (comes through the hall). Mentioned only: Pace, Sheri, Cassie, John, Vee's parents, "the girl he cooked for" (Sheri, as Vee's invention).

Heat: 1. No sex on the page; the charge is the shame-into-wanting turn of the coat gambit — "the wanting coming up hand in hand with the mortification and sharper for it" — simmering, clothed, anticipatory.

Romance: 2. Tenderness runs high but crosswise — the deepest intimacy in the room is Vee↔Randi (the long-held hugs, the goodbye kiss "only what they did," "gorgeous," "my love"), while the romance being negotiated is Vee↔Pace at second hand. Real warmth, no declaration; the one true declaration-shaped line ("the room I actually live in") passes as a joke.

Motifs & images: The goodbye kiss on the mouth (repeat, "unremarkable now, only what they did"). Randi's cold-citrus scent — "bitter orange, green at the edge, nothing sweet" (repeat; steadies Vee). Black heels pressed on her, "Randi always knew the shoes" (repeat of Ch 23 shoe-buying — Randi outfitting Vee for a scene). The floozie/"slutty" mother's-voice shame firing at arousal (repeat, central). The warmed house / heat brought up hours ahead so Vee's never cold (repeat, Pace's signature care, re-narrated as evidence). The coat as costume (first sighting in this use). The invented rival built at 3 a.m. (repeat from Old Acquaintances, now named and dismantled). Deciding "for her and by her at once" (repeat of the plan's core seam).

Symbolism: The stopped sentence — Randi narrating everything and going mute exactly at the hollow center of the fairytale ("we went up") — the silence stands for the emptiness the story can't hold. The heels handed down as tonight's one pre-decided piece: agency conferred, styled to look self-chosen. The New Year fairytale itself as counterfeit gold, told true fact by fact and false whole — the whole book's method in a bedtime story.

Characterization: Both deepen. Randi lands as fully coherent with Gstaad — the handler and the woman in love are the same gesture here, and the page lets both be true without resolving which is on top; I held that ajar. Vee deepens in the direction of appetite outrunning shame, and in her heartbreaking fluency-envy (she praises in John exactly what she can't get from Pace, to the woman who found John worthless). No one went thin; Brooke and the blonde are functional but not false.

Pace — within the chapter: Excellent. It earns its length; the slow build from wreck to plan is exactly paced, and the retelling section is placed perfectly — the fairytale lands last, after the counsel, so it recolors everything. No drag.

Pace — chapter to chapter: Building, and cannily so. After two interior lonely-vigil chapters (Vee's, then Randi's Gstaad, then Vee's home) and Pace's diner, this is the convergence that pays them off — the two women finally in a room, each carrying a New Year the reader has seen from inside. Momentum strong; it turns the held silence into forward motion (Vee going to Pace tonight) without rushing the turn.
