FELT READ

This one swept me clean up and only let me down onto the ground at the very end, softly, the way Vee gets set down. It's essentially a single unbroken movement — a woman talking herself into an act of nerve, arming her own body for it, and carrying it out through the cold to a door she isn't sure will open kindly — and I was inside her the whole way. It worked on me almost start to finish, and I want to be honest that it worked as *swoon and heat* far more than as *dread*, even though the machinery I know about never left the room.

The dressing sequence is the best-built erotic passage I've read in this book so far, and it's built out of the smallest domestic truths. The bra drawer is perfect: habit reaching for the black everyday bra, then correcting to "the pretty one, Pace deserves the pretty one," then the little laugh because Pace is getting *neither* — that's a woman's hands and a woman's shame and a woman's humor all in one gesture. And "a girl puts her shoes on last. Always... And here she was putting them on first, and just about only." The whole chapter keeps finding these — the frosted plum toenails she touched up "over Christmas in her old bedroom for a man she'd no longer been sure would ever see them again." That line ached. It reaches back to the worst-nights-of-the-break Vee I just left in "Old Acquaintances," and lands the turn: she kept the polish up in despair, and now he'll see it.

The mirror masturbation and then the *refusal* to finish — "It was not hers to finish... She wanted to carry all of it out into the cold unspent and put it in his hands wound exactly this tight" — that's the erotic idea of the chapter and it's a strong one. Denial as offering. It made the drive charged in a way that paid off in the doorstep scene. The heat peaks for me not at the sex but right there at the glass, hands on her own breasts "which was how he'd put it once, pleased," understanding "a little better why he never seemed to want to leave them." She's learning her own body by borrowing his gaze on it. That's exactly the wound-into-heat the book keeps promising, and here it's hers, done alone, no one steering her hand in the room.

The car scene — touching herself at speed, then the cop lights — is where I felt the author's hand a little. It's a callback (the campus cop who broke up the truck night on the hike), and the fake-out (cruiser blows past, "after something that had nothing to do with her at all") is a beat I saw coming a half-second early. It didn't hurt the momentum much, and the "caught" spiral is true to her shame-engine, but it's the one moment the chapter reached for an event rather than trusting the body. The mark bloomed into the coat lining — "it only told her she meant it" — is the better half of the same idea and I bought that completely.

The doorstep. I was genuinely nervous with her, because I did not know which way it would go, and that suspense is *earned* — I've watched Pace fold his arms and let her walk out, I've watched him hold the wall at cost. When the wall "was simply gone, taken down all at once," and "he looked at her like a starving man, and he looked at her like a man just told he is forgiven, and the two were one look" — that landed on me hard. Heat is a full 3 here and it's the scene's center.

And then the aftercare undoes me more than the sex. "You sure know how to make an entrance," muffled against her breast — "Vee." Her own name, "easy and warm in his mouth," and the laugh coming apart into tears because it's *the same name he said behind her in December as she walked out the door* — that's the chapter's finest stroke, a name that meant absence now meaning arrival. Then the midnight pancakes, "You make that sound like a problem," "Those stay on," and finally him on one knee: "I missed you so much," and his voice cracks and does not come back. That crack is the closest thing to the word she's been starving for, and the book knows it, and gives it to her sideways — through what he does, as always. I felt the whole architecture of the book breathe there: he still didn't say it, and she "tonight there was no room in her for wanting anything he hadn't given." She got everything she drove out for. She did not get the thing she cannot ask for. Both true at once.

Where my trust sits: I am not more suspicious after this chapter, but the thing I already know sat heavier for being *invisible*. This whole beautiful gesture — the coat, nothing under it, "that's the bravest thing a woman can do" — was Randi's script. I read this chapter with the earlier "My Friend Randi" scene loud in my ear: Randi coached the coat, pressed the heels into her hands, walked her past the "slut" ledge word by word. That's the "sick-making by design" thing working exactly as the jacket warned.

Titles: "Nothing Underneath" is the coat, of course — literal, the whole plot of the chapter. But read against the book it recolors: nothing underneath the coat, and also the fear that has haunted her all break, that there's *nothing underneath* what he gives her, no name, no floor, that if he stopped she'd "see this for the smaller, plainer thing it might have been." The chapter answers the coat-meaning triumphantly and leaves the second meaning open. It's a title that promises a striptease and quietly also names her dread. Nicely done — it gives away the surface event (I knew the reveal was coming) but keeps its second charge for the reread. The book title *A Polite Invitation* and series *With a Long Spoon* — the spoon is "sup with the devil, use a long spoon," and this chapter is Vee walking straight up to the table with no spoon at all, handing herself over unspent. No front-cover tagline; the jacket's closing beat, "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem," is the whole chapter in one line — this was the freest, gladdest, most self-authored yes she's given, and it's the one Randi wrote the stage directions for.

What I want next: I want to know whether this reconciliation *costs* Randi something, now that Vee is back inside Pace's care and the brunettes-and-blondes fear is put to bed — and whether Randi keeps steering, or the steering starts to hurt her.
