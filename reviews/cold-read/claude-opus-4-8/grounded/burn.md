## Reader reaction

FELT READ

I came into this chapter still ringing from the party — from the kiss with Randi that was not a bit, that Vee's body has not let go of, that ended with her peeling a soaked thong away from herself alone in the dark. So it lands as a genuine relief, and a genuine trick of scheduling, that "Burn" turns entirely away from Randi and toward Pace. The whole engine of Vee wanting Randi and having nowhere to put it gets, in this chapter, redirected — she says it out loud herself on the drive: "Her wanting had had nowhere to go all night, and it had not gone quiet; it had turned and asked him instead." The book knows exactly what it's doing there, and I felt the seam. The heat Randi lit gets spent, or half-spent, on Pace. That's not a complaint. It's the most honest thing in the chapter and I clocked it.

The gym section is where I fell hardest, and it's not even the erotic part. It's the caretaking-as-devotion that Pace does with weights. "That he took her weight seriously. That the smallness of what she was doing bought her back none of his care." And then the quiet reveal after the bench set — that he kept every rep inside what he could rack alone, "so that she was never once responsible for what she couldn't hold. She hadn't even felt the danger, because he had never let there be any." That undid me a little. It's the flannel-shirt-in-the-rain man, the keeps-the-house-warm man, rendered in iron. I love him in that moment and I trust the care completely. The "everything gets a collar" beat is a lovely small key too — and the book is honest that Vee finds it "a little much, I thought, and not for the first time." That crack of ordinary reservation is what keeps him human instead of a saint.

Then the injury, and here's where the chapter earns its whole second half. The boys, the smack, "you want it in the butt," the plate rolling into her cage, her flinch under a loaded bar — I felt the wrongness go up her neck. And Pace putting himself between her and the cage, voice going "down and quiet and very exact": "You hurt her. Your carelessness hurt her. Apologize to the woman you hurt." I loved it and it also gave me the first small chill of the chapter — not because he was wrong, the boys were careless and she's genuinely hurt, but because of how the boys "looked to Pace, and waited," and only relaxed "when whatever was in his face eased and let them go." That's a man who commands a room by lowering his voice. It's magnetic and it's a little frightening, and the book let both be true without tidying it. That's exactly the register I want.

The long massage-into-Vicks scene is the erotic center and it worked on me, hard, but it's a stranger, more troubling heat than anything Pace has done before, and I want to be honest about why. The setup is impeccable consent-craft: he's tending a real injury, the arm across her breasts is "only where you hold a body to bring it back to rest," he draws his hand back — "Keep still" — before the accident of the balm can "become an act." He makes her say yes. He asks "Do you want me to keep going?" — "all of him waiting on the word and not on her." All of that is the book's whole thesis about choosing, and it's beautiful and I believe it.

And yet what she's choosing is to have a mentholated burn worked into her nipples while she can't move her neck and can't come. That's the part that sat strange in me. It's genuinely her demand — "Her pleasure had demanded steel, and he had heard it under everything else she was, and found it in himself" — and the book is careful to keep the agency hers, palms pressed over the backs of his hands, choosing "second by second, with the fire in full view and coming down." But there's a real edge of the ordeal to it, of her body confused between the caretaker's hand and the lover's, "it could not tell his care from the other thing and did not try." The line that named the unease best is hers: "she could not tell the dread of the fire from the wanting of it." I don't experience that as the book failing consent — I experience it as the book walking right up to the place where tenderness and a kind of controlled cruelty are the same hand, and refusing to blink. That's the "psychologically merciless in its tenderness" the jacket warned me about, and here I actually felt it rather than being told it.

The ending is the most tender and the most withholding at once, and it's where I most trust him and am most uneasy in the same breath. He won't let her come — "Not tonight. We need to wait." And that "We" lands on her "almost worse than the burn." He holds her burning, banks the heat, gives her "nothing to do with any of it," because a real orgasm would arch her neck and cost her. So the cruelty is protection. Which is the whole Pace paradox in one gesture: the man who denies her the thing she's begging for, and denies it out of exact care for her body. I find it swooning and I find it a hair chilling, and I think it's supposed to be both, and I didn't have to manufacture the chill — the balm-burn earned it.

On the checkpoint's larger picture: I notice the reader's dread runs quiet under this whole chapter and never surfaces on the page. Vee's want for Randi got spent on Pace, and neither of them knows the shape of the other. I keep sitting with the fact that the heat she brings to Pace tonight was struck by Randi's mouth two nights ago, and that Pace is the man who conspired with Randi to bring Vee in. The chapter doesn't touch any of that. It just lets me hold it.

TRUST / SUSPICION: No new betrayal-suspicion of Pace — the care reads real. But the gym confrontation gave me a fresh, small, specific unease about how much a room bends to him, and about a man whose tenderness and control are the same instrument. The balm scene made that literal. The text earned it; I'm not importing it.

EROTIC CHARGE: High, and peaking late — the massage-to-burn is the strongest sustained heat, and it works precisely because it's braided with dread and with real injury. It never goes clinical. If anything the danger is that it's so controlled, so exquisitely negotiated, that a colder reader might feel the author's hand in how perfectly Pace threads every consent needle. I felt the perfection but stayed inside it.

FRICTION: Almost none. The one place I felt the thumb faintly was the density of "he had known it before she did" / "she already knew, as surely as she'd once known about the long way she still had to fall, that she was hurt — really hurt — and that he had known it before she did" — the chapter tells me twice that he knew she was hurt first. Once would have landed harder. And the gym section's repeated insistence on his care being taken-seriously ("No one took the small things she did seriously, and he had, with his entire face") is one beat past where I'd have stopped. Small. It didn't break the spell.

TITLES: "Burn" is the plainest title in a while and it pays out completely — the Vicks burn, obviously, but also the whole chapter is about heat lit in one place and made to travel, about a wanting that has nowhere to go and burns because it can't be spent. And burn as in overtrain, injury, the muscle that "shortens." It gave nothing away going in and rewarded me coming out. The book title A Polite Invitation and the tagline "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — this chapter is almost a demonstration piece for that tagline. Every yes here is scrupulously, repeatedly extracted ("Do you want me to keep going?"), and what she's saying yes to is a fire worked into her while she's hurt. The freely-given yes and the thing it consents to sit at a strange angle to each other, and the tagline says that angle is the whole problem. With a Long Spoon — I still read as the proverb about supping with the devil; you eat with him, but you keep your distance. Tonight nobody's keeping any distance, and I don't yet know who the devil in that spoon is meant to be.

WANT / EXPECT / DREAD NEXT: I want to see the Randi kiss come back — it can't just evaporate, and the chapter conspicuously spent its charge elsewhere without touching it. (Guess from here: Vee will have to face Randi with that kiss between them, and I dread and crave that scene.) I want Pace and Randi in a room again now that both have, separately, gotten their hands fully on Vee. I dread, quietly, the moment any of the three things I know and Vee doesn't comes loose. And I want the Brooks-and-Dunn dance Sheri booked — I'm owed a night where Pace dances.
