## Reader reaction

THE FELT READ

This one got me — and then it turned a knife I didn't see coming, so gently I almost thanked it.

The whole first half is pure luxuriant swoon and I let it be. Vee waking to the cold flat side of the bed and lurching back to December for one heartbeat, then the arithmetic — "getting happiness for an answer every time" — I melted. The chapter understands its own device beautifully: the same fact that was terror yesterday (no clothes, buttoned to the collar dying in front of Kayla's laundry) has "changed in the night into something else." The naked-in-a-warm-house-that-smells-of-bread sequence is joy rendered onto the page, and I loved that the story lets her be *delighted* — the shimmy, the giggle, "some kept woman," the distinction she lands on: not kept for a man's pleasure but "kept *in* pleasure." That's Vee finally using the too-much of herself on purpose and enjoying it clean, without the mother's voice. I was rooting for her hard.

Pace stopping dead in the doorway with "Whoa—" and never finishing, then reassembling himself "piece by piece like a man picking up dropped change" and landing on "I got mushrooms" — that's the single funniest, warmest beat in a long while, and it made me trust the reciprocity: she has power here, real power, and he *loses composure* over her. The burnt-toast game, her banking each half-late turn "like earnings," is her wielding exactly the being-looked-at she used to die of. I was fully swept up.

The cooking is food-porn at full tilt and it worked on me without going clinical — the mushrooms "a smell with shoulders on it," the blood orange peeled so "nothing between anybody and the fruit," which she clocks as "the most him thing" — opening the fruit the way he later opens her. That parallel is set up patiently and paid off in the oral scene ("opening her as he had opened the orange, nothing hurried, nothing skipped"). Gorgeous.

The sex itself is a 3 and it earned it. The pointing game — her directing him wrist, nipple, other nipple, lower — is the chapter's cleverest erotic engine: she's the princess, he's the "pleasure slave," and I loved that the fantasy is *hers*, that she's the one composing it, and that her control frays exactly as her body outruns her ("the need had gotten ahead of the performance"). The finger that "was not steady this time, and both of them knew it, and it was not a command now and both of them knew that too" — that's the swoon and the honesty at once. Her shame and heat arriving as "one arrival, indivisible," and him not skipping it, pardoning it, or waiting for it to thin — "He was drinking it" — that's the book's whole thesis about being seen, delivered as heat. I felt it.

And then the turn. The forbidden kiss. He comes up and kisses her and she "kissed him back before she knew what she was kissing," her tongue reaching "toward the taste before any part of her had ruled on it — curious, unforbidden, hers" — and THEN the mother's flat clean voice, "installed in her before she could have argued with it," slams it shut, and out comes "Ugh — gross. That's — no. Wrong," with the back of her hand and the child's gesture. That's devastating and exactly right: the one place the shame still has a locked door. I ached. And the immediate scramble — legs clamping, "not that. Don't you dare stop" — him reading "which no was which," wiping his mouth "unbothered," and gentling toward her "as if she had done something young and dear rather than something unkind." That's the tenderness I trust him for.

But the chapter doesn't let it rest sweet, and this is where it earned real unease. The afterglow reflection is the best writing here and the most quietly ominous. "Because he had liked it." Not tolerated — *liked*, "gone down glad and stayed glad and come up wanting to bring it to her mouth like a good thing." And her noticing he never kissed her mouth again the rest of the way, when he *always* does — "dozens, hundreds" — and her tidy, grateful explanation: he "must have forgotten to wipe. This once." And then her own tongue going out over her lower lip, "looking for something," catching itself, "did not ask herself what it had been looking for." That's Vee's self-deception rendered with surgical patience — the want she will not look at squarely, the small light left on "minor-key, waiting." I didn't flinch from it; I sat in it. This is the interior life under the wanting, and it's why the swoon before it lands harder rather than cheaper.

Where my dread sharpened: the final turn to Randi. "Tuesday there was the anthro class... Randi across a little table wanting every last detail... whose mad gorgeous idea it had been, who had handed her the play like a queen lending a sword." And the strange small thing — the taste, the wanting-to-taste — "she'd just say it anyway. Randi would know what to make of it. Randi always did." I know what the reader knows: the appetite she just discovered on her own tongue, curious and unforbidden, is going to be *handed to Randi* as a puzzle, and Randi is precisely the one steering her toward tasting a woman. So the chapter's warmest close is, to me, its most dangerous. The door the mother's voice slammed on Vee's mouth is the exact door Randi has been working. I don't think that's me on guard — the text put the tongue-reaching *before* the shame-voice, made the curiosity primary and "hers," and then aimed it straight at Randi. That's earned.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no new suspicion of *Pace* here — he's tender, funny, unhumiliating, and the "which no was which" reading is the good kind of attentive. My unease is structural and it's about where Vee's own newly-woken appetite is being routed. And under everything, the bench still colors it: this whole idyll is the reconciliation Randi scripted.

Erotic charge: high and sustained, peaking at the held-breath-over-open-flesh moment and the g-spot scream. Never went slack. The food and the sex share a grammar and it made both hotter.

Friction: almost none. If I reach — the "pleasure slave / gladiator / princess" framing gets laid on a touch thick in her internal narration ("Groomed by her own gladiator — turned pleasure slave by her beauty"), and once or twice the cleverness of the conceit slightly out-talks the feeling. But it's her voice, giddy and self-mythologizing, so I mostly bought it.

Titles: "Forbidden Kiss" — I braced for it to mean the goodbye kiss with Randi, and instead the chapter earns it as the taste of *herself* on his mouth, the one thing her shame still forbids. Read innocently it's a lovers'-swoon title; read after, it names the exact hairline crack the whole book has been widening, and points it — via that closing paragraph — straight at Randi and at a woman's taste. It gave a little away only in retrospect, which is the good kind. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* / "Every yes was freely given. That was the problem." — this chapter is that thesis in miniature: every yes here is real, glad, hers, and the one small no she says is the tell she can't afford to examine. The long spoon (sup with the devil) sat in my mind at that last line: she'll tell Randi "nearly everything," and hand her the one thing she shouldn't.

What I want next: Tuesday, obviously. I dread and crave the little-table dinner where Vee offers Randi the taste-puzzle. Guess from here: Randi will "know what to make of it" all too well, and reframe Vee's shame into permission — the next step toward the taste-another-woman thread that's been latent since the pointing game. I want Vee to keep her joy; I'm afraid the joy is the doorway.
