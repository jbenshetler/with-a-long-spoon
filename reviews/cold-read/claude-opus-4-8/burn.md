# Cold read — Burn

*scene: scenes/burn.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: on-her-floor*

## Reader reaction

This one worked on me hard, and almost entirely on the tender-devotion register — which is a change. For once there was no Randi, no debrief, no reframe waiting in the wings; just Pace and Vee in a private room, and I let myself be swept because the page earned it.

**The gym.** The whole first movement is a slow seduction built entirely out of *seriousness*. He sets her under a bar that weighs nothing "as though it weighed everything," seats her hands and feet and shoulders with the full attention he'd give a man pressing twice his weight, and "that was the thing that got into her first. Not the weight. That he took her weight seriously." I felt that land exactly as she did. It's the truest thing about Pace and the most dangerous: his care doesn't scale down for how small the thing is. And then the detail I loved — that he arranged his own heavy set so she was "never once responsible for what she couldn't hold," kept the whole thing inside what he could rack himself, so she never even *felt* the danger. That's him whole: he removes the floor from under you and also makes sure you never fall. Cherished and managed in the identical gesture, again.

**"Everything gets a collar."** God, that line. He collars a five-pound plate no one would collar, in a corner where no one's looking, and she thinks "a little much" — and then the two careless boys don't collar theirs and it's exactly their carelessness that hurts her. The page let his fastidiousness *pay off* as protection without ever saying so. I didn't feel a thumb on the scale; I felt dread land where he'd already looked.

**The injury and after.** When the plate rolls in and she twists off the bar and tears her neck — and his hands are on her before she can ask, stands her up, walks the bar down into the pins in "one long controlled motion" — I trusted him completely in that beat. And "You hurt her. Your carelessness hurt her" — quiet, exact, the size of him changing without his moving. He makes them apologize *to her*, but they look to *him* for release. That's a real and slightly chilling read of his authority even while he's being purely protective. He didn't ask if she was all right. He *knew* before she did. "He had known it before she did" — the man who reads the room and reads her body.

**The balm.** This is where the chapter turned dark and beautiful at once and where the erotic charge peaked all the way. The camphor-menthol paste starts as pure aftercare — the same warmth she "let her head drop and been grateful for" — and then it turns to burn, and he works pleasure into her breasts while the fire climbs down her from throat to collarbone to nipple. And the terror is that *she demands it.* He keeps one breast plain — "one for the burn and one kept plain," mercy — and "she did not want to be saved." She presses the plain breast into his hand. "Yes," before he's asked. The asking-mechanism came back in full: "Do you want me to keep going?" — "flat and careful, all of him waiting on the word and not on her." He will not take her body's yes; he needs the word. Twice she gives it, "the one word she could get out of herself."

What unsettled me — earned, not on guard — is the sentence "it could not tell his care from the other thing and did not try." Her body has been taught to read tending and taking as one temperature. That's the instrumentation at its deepest yet, and it doesn't need Randi or a scheme: it's just what being loved by Pace *does* to her. And the closing training-note: "She had never known how much of a worked muscle is the muscle fighting back. He had taken that from her, and left her nothing to do but breathe and be worked." I read that both as the most generous thing anyone's done for her body and as something faintly frightening about how completely he can remove her resistance.

**"We need to wait." We.** That single word did what "boyfriend not love" has been failing to do all book. He won't let her spend herself against the injury — "he would not let it cost her" — and he says *we.* After chapters of him never quite saying the thing, "We" went through her "almost worse than the burn," and it went through me too. This is the closest I've felt to him actually naming the bond, and it's smuggled in as a plural pronoun. The whole ending — banking the heat under his palms, giving her "no finish, no service, no way in the world to spend it, nothing but to be held there burning and take it" — is denial as devotion. She hates him a little for the precision of the gentleness. So did I, in the good way.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Almost none this chapter, and I want to mark that honestly rather than manufacture a shadow. There's no scheme visible here, no reframe, no third party. What darkness there is comes from *inside the love itself* — the training of her body, the conflation of care and fire, his total authority over her yeses. That's a more interesting unease than "is he lying to her," because he isn't. He's just remaking her.

**Erotic charge:** high and sustained, peaking in the staggered double-burn where "every roll of his fingers was fire and wanting in the same place at the same moment, and she gave up telling them apart." It never went clinical. The one place I felt the prose reaching a hair too hard was the layered "the neck was what the breast would be. The breast was what the tip would be" — gorgeous, but the chapter leans on that ladder-of-dread structure a couple times and I felt the architecture.

**The title.** "Burn" reads innocent going in — muscle burn, workout burn — and the gym opening lets you keep believing that. Then it's the balm, the literal fire on her skin, and then it's the thing "lit under all of it, low and private," carried home unfinished. It didn't give anything away; it recolored cleanly on arrival. *A Polite Invitation* — "Come to the gym with me," offhand, as if it were nothing, and she says yes before deciding to: that IS the polite invitation, the low-stakes ask that opens onto something she didn't choose the whole of. And *With a Long Spoon* — supping with something that will burn you if you're not careful — sat very close to the surface tonight, with the camphor literally burning her.

**What I want next:** the weekend is still pending — Randi's "sorority thing Saturday" is what freed this whole gym morning, and I'm braced for the two threads to touch. The debrief call is still unmade. And I want to know whether "We" holds or whether it, too, gets sanitized. Right now, though, I mostly just ache for her, and half-trust him, which is exactly the crossbar this book keeps balancing me on.

**How I feel — overall**
Swept up, warm, and only quietly uneasy — and the unease this time comes from *inside the love*, not from a scheme. This was the most tender, most privately erotic chapter yet, and I let it sweep me: his seriousness, his mercy, the "We." What lingers as darkness is subtler and more interesting than any plot-trap — that his care remakes her body, teaches it to stop resisting, teaches it to read fire and tending as one thing. Sheri and the pending weekend keep the larger machine in my peripheral vision, but tonight it was just the two of them, and it ached.
