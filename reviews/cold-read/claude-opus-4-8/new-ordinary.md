## Reader reaction

This one just melted me, and I want to say that plainly before anything else, because the last few chapters had me holding my breath around Randi and this chapter simply set that down and gave me a warm house.

The whole first half is the best comedy of undressing I've read in a while, and it's funny *because* it's desperate. "The jeans had been sensible in the car and were the enemy now." She gets two buttons on his shirt; he gets "all the way down her back with one hand, flat, taking his time about it." That asymmetry is the whole scene — she's frantic, he is not, and his not-hurrying is a kind of power she loves and resents in the same breath. "You're not helping." / "I'm helping." I laughed out loud. And then the bra: he catches it and doesn't pull, "only held it and let her keep going, so she had to come out of it herself, turning, unwinding like something off a spool." That's the most erotic sentence in the chapter and nothing is happening in it. She notes it herself: "She'd lost. She knew it. She put her mouth back on him anyway."

The heat is high and it's *hers* — that's what struck me. Five nights she chose, five nights she counted with her body while her mouth said school. "the exact sound of a woman who had told herself a careful story about needing time to study and was being shown now, by her own throat, just how true the story had been." That's the interior life I read for: not shame exactly, but the humiliation of being contradicted by your own physiology, "her body having just published the number she'd gone to such trouble to keep private." Gorgeous. The sex itself goes fast then slow, first one shameful and quick, second "a deep slow roll," and I felt both.

Pace. He's off-page for two chapters and comes back and I have the same trouble I always have: he is *lovely* here. He didn't fight her for the nights. "He'd said of course. That was all of it." And Vee names the cost of that better than I could: "the plain grace of that had cost her more than a fight would have, because some unreasonable corner of her had wanted him to fight." That's the tell that keeps my carry-forward chord unresolved — a man who never asks for anything creates a woman who supplies everything unprompted. But I want to be honest: the page did not push me there. The page gave me a man who makes a pizza with the same attention he gives her, "He'd have made the pizza exactly like this for an empty house," and Vee finding she doesn't mind sharing his attention with a ball of dough. I believe the warmth. I just also remember what I know.

The palate passage — tasting *every* flavor separately, "cotton candy and a bowl of cut strawberries" — landed on me as pure pleasure and then went cold about two seconds later, because I've been carrying "the remade palate / no going back" as a motif, and here it is again, sweetly, with him watching her face "as if her face had just told him something he'd wanted to know." She doesn't ask what. That's the one moment in the chapter where I felt the shape underneath the tenderness.

And then the flannel. God. I did not expect to be undone by laundry. "It stopped smelling like you... and one day last week it didn't smell like anything at all, so I washed it." The whole ask — wear it all weekend, give it back Sunday — is so naked and so small and she "kept her eyes on the shirt and not on him." His first read is that she's returning it, the flicker of hurt, and he "decided not to ask. He was good at not asking things." (There's my ledger's shut door, in a warm register.) Then he pulls it on over the shirt he's already wearing and stands in his kitchen in two shirts because she told him to. "Smelling like you." / "Smelling like me." That's the swoon of the book so far for me. It's also — and I can't unsee it — a woman engineering a device to keep herself in a state of wanting during the nights she has to be a student.

Trust vs suspicion: this chapter earned no new suspicion of Pace. It earned a *quiet* dread only through things I already carry (the drawer of her set down in his house, the nights she can't stay away from, "Five nights. She'd lasted five."). The friction of school is real and she is losing it, and nobody had to do anything to make that happen. That's the scariest part and it's rendered without a thumb on the scale.

Friction: almost none. The cooking paragraph is long — the half hour of sauce, the buffalo mozzarella, the cornmeal on the stone — and I could feel it slowing, though it's slow on purpose and I let it. "near the only thing he'd said in ten minutes" was a slightly clumsy clause. The "low table of odd proportions" in his bedroom snagged me: it's the second time a piece of his furniture has been described as odd-shaped in a way the page won't explain, and I noticed the thong landing "green on" it. Green keeps appearing — scrunchies, satin, thong — and I don't know yet if that's just her color.

Title: "The New Ordinary" reads straight and then sours slightly. It's the domesticity — drawer, robe, pizza, bed — become baseline. But *new* means there was an old ordinary, and the chapter's real content is that the new one is eating the old one: her studying, her dorm, her four-hours-of-sleep life. The ordinary is what you stop noticing. *A Polite Invitation* and *With a Long Spoon* both still point the same direction for me: politeness as the delivery mechanism, and you sup with a long spoon because of what's at the table. Nothing in this chapter mentions Randi at all, which after last chapter feels like held breath.

What I want next: Randi, and whether Vee's kept secret leaks. And I dread the school thread — she's falling behind, she said so on the page, and she came back after five nights.
