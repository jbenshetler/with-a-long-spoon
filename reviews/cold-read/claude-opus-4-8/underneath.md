## Reader reaction

**The felt read**

Oh, this one hurt in a way I didn't brace for. We've been living in Vee's giddy warmth for three chapters — the froyo, the mountain, the flannel she's still wearing — and then this chapter drops me into Pace's house on the *same night*, and Randi arrives, and the whole engineered thing underneath comes up into the light and it's colder and sadder than I even feared. The title *Underneath* pays out exactly: this is the underneath of everything Vee just told Cassie with her whole face open.

The heat is real and it's doing terrible, precise work. Randi walks him backward to the bedroom, rides him, holds his eyes, "makes" him watch her sink onto him — she's commanding, hungry, present. And then: *"Tell me."* And I knew. I knew before he did what she wanted, and the knowing is the whole engine of the scene. She doesn't want the trail. She wants Vee. "Her hair smells sweet… under it, faint — chlorine. Like she'd been in a pool." And I feel it go through her, the grip tightening, and I'm turned on and sick at once, because the intimate detail Pace is feeding her is *Vee's* — Vee who thinks she's the most interesting girl in the room, Vee weightless and unwatched underwater, and here her chlorine is being used as fuel in a bed she doesn't know exists. "Her stomach is soft." *He did not say how he knew.* That line — that he keeps the truck, keeps what Vee gave him, doesn't hand *that* over — is the strangest tenderness. He portions out Vee to Randi but withholds the most private thing. I don't know if that's loyalty or just another kind of control, and the page leaves it ajar, so I do too.

"I want us," torn out of her — that landed hardest. *Us.* Not "I want her." It's want too big for her, "come down on him with nowhere else to go," and for the first time I really felt the shape of Randi's hunger as its own grief rather than pure scheme. She wants Vee, she wants Pace, she wants the three of them, and she can't say it clean, so she says it while she's coming with her nails in his chest. My sympathy for her, which the checkpoint left divided, tipped further toward ache here — and toward alarm, because that wanting is aimed straight at a girl who has no idea she's the object of it.

And then the detail that gutted me quietly: she doesn't stay. "That was the part of it she kept for herself, after, gone loose on his chest… and tonight she skipped it." The chill takes her, she dresses, "No thanks" to food, bright, gone. He notices — the empty chair by the door (no deadbolt-first ritual tonight? no — she *does* bolt it), her not staying, her never asking again how the hike was. He sets these things beside each other and *leaves them there.* That's Pace all over: he sees, he waits, he doesn't force the door. Daphne's lesson. And the last image — him alone, warm house, eating at the table, still hungry, making more — after a whole book of appetite-as-shame, *he's* the one left plainly, animally hungry and just feeding himself. It reframes the motif: he's the one person here who eats freely without a voice stopping him, and he's eating alone.

My feelings moved. Pace: my wariness didn't lower, but it complicated — he's the still center everyone offloads onto, and there's something lonely in him I hadn't clocked. Randi: more heartbreak, less pure menace, though the menace is still there in what she's building. Vee: unchanged and now unbearable — she's not even *in* this chapter and she's all over it, her hair, her stomach, her chlorine, currency in a room she'll never be shown. That's the cruelty the structure does without a single unkind line.

Trust/suspicion: nothing felt *off* as craft — this is the confirmation of what I already knew, delivered straight. If anything the friction is the opposite of a thumb on the scale: it *underplays*. No one narrates the wrongness. The empty chair, the skipped after, the second helping — the page trusts me to feel it, and I did. The one thing I'll hold open: Pace withholding "how he knew." I can't yet tell if that's him protecting Vee or hoarding her, and I like that I can't.

Erotic charge: high and genuinely two-edged — the physical scene is hot (the slow sinking-down, the audible wetness, the groan he didn't decide on), but the "Tell me / Tell me" refrain braids the arousal into dread so tightly I couldn't have one without the other. That's the peak, and it's the best kind of uncomfortable.

Titles: *Underneath* is perfect and did not give anything away in advance — it only detonates *after*, recoloring Vee's sunny froyo-and-flannel chapters as the surface over this. The book title *WITH A LONG SPOON* — "he who sups with the devil should use a long spoon" — sits right on top of this chapter now: Pace supping with Randi's want, keeping *some* distance (the withheld truck, the long spoon), and Randi supping with something bigger than she can hold. *BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*: the politeness ("May I choose," "See you," warm, easy) is exactly the courtesy laid over the hunt. I feel it taking me toward the moment the invitation gets accepted and the three of them are in one room — and I dread that room for Vee.

What I want/dread next: I want back with Vee, and I dread it, because everything sweet she says now I'll hear doubled. Guess from here: the induction dinner, or the "we should all get dinner sometime," is where the worlds collide — and the russet dress she put back on the shelf feels like a loaded gun. And I dread the bench.
