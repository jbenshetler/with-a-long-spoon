## Reader reaction

This one played me like the dance did her — slow, patient, then it took me before I'd decided anything. It's the least explicitly erotic chapter in a while and one of the most charged, because the whole thing is the D/s architecture translated into a concrete country-western dance lesson and never once named as such. "Give me something to push back against. Otherwise you cannot feel my lead." "Close it — come all the way in — and my feet can't land anywhere but past yours." "Don't think. Just feel the lead." Every instruction is simultaneously literal and about what he does to her in bed and, underneath, about the consent-and-control thing I already dread. It never winks. I loved that the space she keeps "as too close, too fixed, too much rule" turns out to be the thing holding her "in the one place the next step could come from, so there was nothing to do but take it." That's the whole relationship in one sentence and it didn't announce itself.

**Pace/Peter.** I fell further. The details — the pressed crease, the short sleeves in October because he dressed for the dancing not the weather, the granddad's warbling tape, the boom box with electrical tape on the handle — make him tender and slightly out of time, unbearably sincere. He answers her venom straight ("Yes. Sometimes. When it's required.") and doesn't perform hurt, just goes "still and plain" and later sets both untouched beers on the bar and walks out into the cold. That restraint gutted me more than a scene would have. And yet — I carry what I carry. I know this good man is running a harvest, and the bartender's testimonial ("Doesn't take advantage — I'd have heard otherwise") is *true*, which is exactly what makes it terrible: everything good about him is real, and it's all also load. The chapter didn't add a tell. It deepened the ache.

**Vee.** Painful to watch and completely believable. The jealousy misfire — "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" — she hears it going wrong "the way you hear a glass go past the point where you can still catch it," and can't stop it. Her self-punishment is the old fused shame: "she'd spread her legs like a slut," the floozies on the floor, "I'd thought I was special. I was just next." The reversal — that she was punishing him "for being the man she wanted him to be" — is earned, not preached. I ached for her broke-among-the-wealthy detail again: the cab fare "more than she'd guessed, and the money had been meant for other things," the borrowed boots and borrowed shirt, washing her face in cold water in the dark.

**The bartender.** New, and I trust her instantly — the older-woman authority, the unhurried economy, the water set down unasked. "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." That's the load-bearing correction: Vee is the exception precisely because Pace *doesn't* bring dates, and he brought her on a Thursday. It lands as reassurance to Vee and, to me, as one more turn of the screw — she's being told the truth, and the truth is bait.

**The dread.** The last line is the coldest thing in the chapter and nothing violent happens in it: "go to Randi's." She's decided to fix a real wound by carrying it to the one person I know is farming her wounds. "Cool embarrassment was just information." I wanted to reach into the page. This is the harvester's gift arriving gift-wrapped. She specifically *won't* call Cassie — the person I trust — because Cassie would ask questions and Vee "would have to put words to what she'd done." She's routing around her own moral floor toward the confidante. That choice is the whole trap in miniature.

**Friction.** Only the dance mechanics ran a touch long and clinical — "back on the right, back on the right, then back on the left. One-and-two, three-four," and the woman's-part paragraph — I skimmed the counts a little. But it bought the "something gave" payoff, so I forgive it. One tiny snag: "*This is Vee*, he said, each time" — the repetition of the crowd-hug beat is doing good work (establishing the harem-of-the-friendly he moves through) but it flirts with over-making-the-point right before the blonde spells it out. The blonde's "Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to" is nearly a wink; it stayed on the right side because it's the kind of thing a slightly-cruel stranger actually says.

**What I want next.** I want to see Randi receive this. *Guess from here:* Randi reframes Vee's shame into "you were testing whether you're allowed to want him this much," makes the jealousy into evidence of Vee's own depth, and turns the fix into another step toward the induction — producing with words the reassurance the bartender gave, but bent toward the plan. And I dread that the "fix" (an apology to Pace) will be scripted by the very person it serves. I also still want the scar, "not tonight," and the day Vee learns Peter and Randi already know each other.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Pace texted Vee to a Thursday country-western night and, because she can't dance, gave her a private lesson first on a concrete pad under the field lights — teaching her the three-step, frame, and lead ("give me something to push back against," "come all the way in," "don't think, feel the lead") until "something gave" and the dance took her. At the CW bar everyone knew him; he'd taught the class two semesters; women hugged him long. A blonde needled Vee ("he's so good about getting a girl home safe"), and Vee, jealous and ashamed, lashed out — "Does your *service* include getting the girls home safe from here, too?" Pace answered plainly, she told him she didn't need him, and walked off; he set both untouched beers down and left. Vee stayed, spiraled, then an older bartender told her the truth: Pace never takes advantage, never brings dates, and brought her on a Thursday — "Wasn't him they were jealous of, honey." Vee cabbed home (fare she couldn't spare), didn't wake Cassie, lay awake in his laundered-and-ruined shirt in shame — she'd punished a good man for being good — while the body-memory of the lead kept returning underneath. By morning she resolved to go to Randi's to fix it.

**How I feel.** Golden-on-top, cold-underneath, at its saddest. I love Pace more and dread for Vee more with every proof of his goodness — because the goodness is real and it's the bait. The dance-as-D/s worked on me quietly. The ending — Vee choosing Randi over Cassie to heal a real hurt — is the coldest beat precisely because nothing dark visibly happens in it.
