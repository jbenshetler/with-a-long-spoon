## Reader reaction

Quiet, short, and it landed on me like a held breath. After the sealed warmth of the last chapter, this is Cassie again — the cracked window, the last air — and I felt myself relax a half-inch just to be back at a table where someone reads Vee honestly. But the relief is exactly what the chapter uses against me, because this is the first time I watched Vee *sort* the truth out loud, deliberately, into what fits and what doesn't, and admit she's doing it.

The title tells you everything and then the chapter earns it back. "Cropped" — I braced for the induction photo, and there it is: Vee cropped it before sending, "the version they have stops at the waist." Her mother carries the half-picture in her purse and shows it to people who've already seen it. That image alone gutted me a little — the proud mother holding the sanitized half of her daughter, and the daughter having done the cropping herself. But then the chapter widens: the *whole telling* is cropped. "I told Dad the parts that fit." The dress she can't explain, the sewing she'd rather he thought was sex than craft, the owned house, the cooking — all cropped out because "too good is its own kind of suspicious." She's learned her father's own reflex — *look for the catch* — and she's protecting Pace from it by editing him down to something a father can be proud of.

What moved for me: Vee. Not her feeling — she still loves him, still isn't ashamed, "the opposite, if anything." What moved is that she's now doing the covering-move *for herself, about herself,* in a room with the one person who reads her clean. She's been fluent at covering aimed outward (she covered for Randi). Here she covers her own life, and — this is the part that ached — she does it in front of Cassie, who offers her the exact opening: "So what is he. To you." And Vee has nothing. "Not the right word for what he was, not the right story for how it had gone, not the version of herself that stood up cleanly in the telling." That's the closest she's come to turning the reading on herself, and she stops right at the edge of it. She can name that she can't name it. That's new, and it's almost hope, and then it isn't.

Cassie is perfect here and I trust her completely. "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" — that's the whole book in one question, and she asks it without prying, then lets it go when Vee says "both." "She didn't push at it. Vee was grateful. She was also, a little, not." That last line is the best thing in the chapter — Vee *wants* to be pushed, wants someone to pull the rest of the picture out of her, and Cassie, respecting her, doesn't. The one person who could crop back in declines, out of love. The window stays cracked but nobody climbs through it.

There's a line I want to hold up because it's doing quiet, terrible work: "Honestly, my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." Vee frames the made-dress as the *unspeakable* thing — weirder than sex, "weird is worse than wrong." She's right at the surface of the wrongness — the sewing *is* the tell, the made-to-fit that holds-so-it-stays — and she reads it as merely un-explainable to her father, not as a signal about the man. She's touching the exact seam I've been dreading and calling it eccentricity. That's the thumb-free version of the author's thumb: she gets *so close* to the truth and files it under "weird."

Trust/suspicion: nothing new to distrust — this is a two-hander with the trustworthy one. My dread is structural, not fresh. What's sharpened is the loneliness of it: Vee now has no room where the whole of her stands up. Home gets the cropped photo. Cassie gets the cropped story. Pace gets the whole of her but is the reason the rest is cropped. The only place she's uncropped is inside a machine that made her that way.

Erotic charge: essentially zero, and correctly — this is aftermath and confession, clothed, at a cafeteria table. The nearest thing to heat is the *idea* of the dress coming off, invoked precisely as the thing more sayable than the dress being made. That's not arousal, it's diagnosis.

Friction: none, really. It's a small chapter that knows it's small. If anything I could feel it wanting me to notice the cropping metaphor — Vee names "cropped" and then the whole telling performs it — but it's restrained enough that I didn't resent the tidiness. Cassie's un-laugh ("didn't laugh, which was the right call") and the short laugh Vee pulls out of her keep it human.

Series signals: "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." — this chapter is the quietest possible proof. Nobody did anything to Vee here. She freely chose what to say and what to withhold, out of love and pride, and the result is that she's alone with a story that won't stand up. The trap tightens by her own hand again, exactly as last chapter. *A Polite Invitation* / *With a Long Spoon* still point at: you dine with the devil by your own courteous choices.

What I want next: I want Cassie to push, and I dread that she won't. I want to know if the Ohio trip stays this clean or if the debt / the weight / the two-hand rule surfaces to her parents (it didn't — she cropped those too). *Guess from here:* the buried variables (debt, the shrinking body) are being saved to detonate through someone who *does* push — and Cassie declining to push here reads like the book showing me the door that stayed shut. I'm also still braced for Thanksgiving at Pace's table with Sheri, which this chapter didn't touch but sits right behind it.
