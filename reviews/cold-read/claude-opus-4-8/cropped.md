## Reader reaction

# Felt read

This one's quiet — no bench, no bed, just two girls at a cafeteria table after break, and yet it landed on me harder than I expected. It's all Vee narrating her Thanksgiving through what she *didn't* say, and the whole chapter is a catalogue of edits she's making to her own life so it fits inside the frame her parents can hold.

The **cropped photo** is the little gut-punch. "I cropped it before I sent it. The version they have stops at the waist." I've been carrying that detail since the induction chapter — she *cropped it when she sent it home* was in my ledger — and here it opens all the way up. And it's not just the photo she's cropped; it's Pace, it's herself, it's the whole thing. Her dad "kept looking at it. Not at me, at the dress." Her mom touching her own collarbone, not saying anything — "You know the one." "I know the one." That exchange is so economical and so full of a whole childhood of that same silent disapproval. The mother-voice I've watched flare up before is here in its native habitat, and Vee's handling it now with this rueful adult clarity instead of getting swallowed by it.

What moved for me: Vee sounds *older* in this chapter. The passage where she does her father's arithmetic for him — "An expensive gift from a man means his daughter is in deeper than he'd pictured" — and then the turn, "Too good is its own kind of suspicious," and then the killer: "Honestly, my dad would take it better if I told him Pace takes that dress off me than that he made it. Sex he understands. The sewing he never would." That's a genuinely sharp piece of thinking and it made me laugh the way Cassie laughed. She's not wrong, either. The homemade dress — a thing I've read as *provision-as-capture*, care with a hook in it — she now reads as the one part of Pace that's genuinely *illegible* to the world she came from. "Weird is worse than wrong." That's her defending the strangest, tenderest thing about him, and defending her own lack of shame about it.

And then Cassie does the thing that made the chapter for me. "So what is he. Not to your dad. To you." And Vee — who narrates everyone else so fluently, who did all that beautiful arithmetic on her father — comes up empty. "She had nothing that fit in Cassie's frame, not the right word for what he was." Then: "Is it that you can't say, or that you don't want to?" "Both. Probably both." That's the truest and most unsettling beat in the chapter. She can render her father's blind spots perfectly and can't say what Pace *is* to her. Awake, out loud, she still can't. It rhymes with everything — she's whispered "Peter" only to him asleep; she's never said "I love him" awake — and now even to Cassie, her safest person, the word won't come. The book keeps her from being able to name the thing. That withholding is starting to feel like the point.

**Trust vs. suspicion:** nothing new to distrust *in this chapter* — it's just Vee and Cassie, and Cassie remains the one clean instrument I trust completely. But my standing dread got quietly fed. Because the whole chapter is Vee explaining, lovingly and cleverly, why the truth won't fit any frame she's handed. And I'm sitting here holding a frame she doesn't even know exists — that Pace and Randi picked her, together, out of a game. She's an expert at reading the catch, at "too good is its own kind of suspicious," and she's applying that intelligence entirely to her *parents'* suspicion of Pace, never to her own situation. The one catch she can't see is the one I can. That gap felt louder than any bench.

**Erotic charge:** none, and rightly. This is the cooldown/reckoning register, and the closest it comes is the dress-off-versus-sewing line, which is more wit than heat.

**Friction:** almost none. Maybe the very slightest — Vee's arithmetic monologue is so articulate that it edges toward essay for a moment ("Wrong is something he understands and rejects. Weird is something that doesn't fit anywhere in how he sees the world"). But it's in character; she's the girl who prices everything, and this is the same faculty turned on her father. I bought it. Cassie "didn't laugh, which was the right call" and "had a gift for knowing when a door was closed" — that last one is a repeat of a phrase I've carried about Cassie, and it's doing slightly more telling than I'd like, but it's brief.

**The title:** "Cropped." Beautifully plain until you sit with it. On the surface it's the photograph, waist-up, the version safe for Ohio. Underneath it's *everything she does in this chapter* — cropping Pace down to "the parts that fit," cropping herself into a daughter who can stand up cleanly in the telling. And it recolors on the last beat: when Cassie asks what he is to her and Vee can't answer, I realized she's cropped so much away that the center is missing even to her. The title gave nothing away up front and paid off completely by the end. That's the good kind.

**Book title / tagline:** "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap." This chapter is the freely-given yes seen from inside, months in — she's not trapped in any way she'd recognize; she's *happy*, proud, defending it. Which is exactly what makes the tagline hum. The trap isn't a locked door; it's that she can't even name the room she's in.

**What I want / dread next:** I want the collision I've been dreading since Thanksgiving — the two worlds still haven't physically touched, and every chapter that stays golden tightens the wire. *Guess from here:* the Anthro signup and the coming term feel like the mechanism that finally puts Randi and Pace in the same room with Vee present. And I still want — dread — the moment she says the true word out loud, awake. This chapter told me she can't yet. When she can, something will have broken open.
