# Cold read — Water Wings

*scene: scenes/water-wings.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: long-way*

## Reader reaction

What a relief, and what a strange grief, to spend a whole chapter with no Pace and no Randi in it. After all that closing-in, this is the one room in the book so far with no script running in it, and my body felt that immediately — I unclenched.

**Cassie.** I love her here. This is the first real time I've *been* with her instead of hearing about her terse one-liners, and she's exactly the antidote I'd hoped: "Tossers. […] They will never know the love of a woman." She fights her suit like it "insulted her family," she wades to her ribs and stops — "This is as far as Cassie went, and the water knew it. 'Go on,' she said, chin at the deep. 'I'll be right here. Standing. Like a person.'" That's the whole friendship in three lines. And the detail that undid me a little: she *could* have pitched her comeback up into the bleachers, "she had the lungs" — "She'd kept it for Vee." Cassie's love is aimed low and private, never performed. She doesn't ask, because "asking was for people who wanted the sad version to get bigger." She clocks that Vee "came back with her face on wrong" and instead of a question she shoots a low sheet of water across ten feet — outrage-out-of-proportion as care. That's the most trustworthy thing anyone has done in this book. My affection for Cassie went from "the one accurate eye" to genuine warmth. She's the person here who wants nothing from Vee.

**Vee.** The pool cracked her open in a way the frozen yogurt didn't, because here she's alone with herself. The catcalls on the bleacher walk — "You won't need floaties with *those!*", "Legs for *days*" — and how she "had them banked," gives the long blink, the ceiling-aimed eyeroll: she's built armor for exactly this, the body-as-public-property thing. And then underwater it all switches off: "Nobody up there could see her. Nobody down here wanted anything." That line landed hard given everything I know — the whole surface world *does* want something from her right now, engineered and closing, and the only place she's free of it is where no one can reach her. "She had loved this before she had a body to be a problem, and the water was the one part of it that had never been taken." That gutted me. It ties straight back to the mother's shaming voice — "Something in her chest that had stood at attention all afternoon quietly sat down."

And that grief she "didn't let herself name" — I don't know what it is, and I don't think Vee does either, but I felt the reader's version of it clearly: *she is being taken, right now, and the one untaken thing is the water.* Whether that's what she's grieving or something older (sixteen, "less of her to fill it," synchronized swimming, a girlhood before the body was a problem), the text left it open and I'm glad it did.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** Nothing here felt off — which is itself notable, because it's the first chapter where I *wasn't* watching a script tighten. The absence of Pace and Randi is almost load-bearing: this is what Vee looks like when nobody's running an operation on her, and it made me feel the operation more, by contrast. No new suspicion earned; instead a sharpened dread, because I now see clearly what she has to lose and where she keeps it.

**Erotic charge.** None, and rightly none — this isn't a sex scene, it's a body scene of a different kind. The nearest thing to erotic is the *inversion* of it: the bleacher boys sexualizing her ("those," "legs"), and Vee retreating to the one place her body isn't currency. It's the anti-erotic counterweight, and it works as that.

**Friction.** Very little. If I'm honest, "the walk of a girl who had *meant* to arrive" and "put her face on with it… the game face breaking" is a hair self-conscious about its own metaphor, and "the shriek-laugh, the ugly one" edges toward telling me how to feel about a laugh I'd already felt. But these are small; the chapter mostly trusts the water to do the work. The one line I'll flag as maybe a touch on-the-nose: "The body that was such a problem stayed up on the deck with the coiled towel, where it could wait." Lovely image, but it circles back to close the metaphor a little tidily. I didn't mind it much.

**What I want / dread next.** I want more Cassie — she's the only counterweight in the book and I now trust the story to use her. My dread (guess from here): this chapter is the *before* — the last time we see Vee whole and untaken and free in her own body — set here precisely so I'll feel the loss later. And I keep waiting for the moment the pool-truth ("the one part that had never been taken") collides with what Pace and Randi are doing to her. If anyone reaches Vee *there* — into the untaken place — that'll be the real violation.

## Carry-forward state

**What happened.** Interlude at the rec pool, no Pace or Randi. Vee and her roommate Cassie swim. Bleacher boys catcall Vee ("floaties," "legs for days"); she has practiced armor for it, Cassie deflects with dry loyalty ("They will never know the love of a woman"). Cassie wades only to her ribs and won't go deep; Vee swims out and goes under, where the whole demanding world switches off — the only place her body isn't "a problem," a thing "never been taken." She grieves something underwater she won't name. Surfaces with "her face on wrong." Cassie, who never asks, shoots a splash across the gap instead of a question; they play-fight and the grief drowns in it.

**Who's who.**
- **Pace** — absent this chapter. Still: the orchestrator courting Vee, precise, tender-and-cold, distrusted utterly by me.
- **Randi** — absent this chapter. Still: girlfriend/sub co-running the op, removes Vee's brakes with a smile.
- **Vee** — warm, armored against being ogled, carries a mother's shaming body-voice; the pool is her one untaken refuge, loved "before she had a body to be a problem." Wide open, being closed in on. I ache for her more now.
- **Cassie** — now fully drawn: boxy, unbothered by being unlooked-at, fiercely private in her love, never asks, cares by deflection and play. The one person who wants nothing from Vee and reads her true. The only clean relationship in the book. I trust and like her.
- **Brooke, Sloane, Kinsey** — sorority machine, offstage.

**How I feel.** A quiet, aching relief laced with sharpened dread. Seeing Vee free of the script showed me exactly what's being taken and where she hides the untaken part. Cassie is the moral floor of the book so far. Mood: tender, elegiac, a held breath before the water breaks.

**Open questions.** What is the grief Vee won't name underwater? Will anyone reach into the "never been taken" place — and is that the true line the seduction will cross? Who is Pace really? When does Vee learn Pace and Randi already know each other? What does Cassie see and still isn't saying — and will she warn Vee before it's too late? When does the shaming voice come back?
