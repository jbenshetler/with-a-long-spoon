## Reader reaction

I've been bracing for this exact scene for two chapters, and it still got me. This is the one I said I dreaded — Vee confiding ch.30 to Randi, the two tracks closing from the outside while she thinks she's just gossiping over mimosas. And the horror of it is exactly what the design promised: watching the method run doesn't cool the heat, it *is* the heat. I read this half-aroused and half-sick, which I think is the point, and I resent how well it worked.

**Vee.** God, she's so far in now, and the awful thing is how *happy* she is walking in — "she had wanted to talk since she woke, going on a while about how good he was to her." She comes to brunch to brag about being loved. And Randi files that under "vanilla" and Vee spends the rest of the meal escalating to *un*-file it, handing over piece after piece — the pantsing, the bet, "he checked," "*Inside?*", strip to the heels, face-down in the covers, "there was no way he couldn't" see everything. Every confession costs her a degree of body heat she can't hide in the "burning glass" light, and she knows the light will have it "whole, every degree of it." The staircase here is verbal, and it's *her* climbing it, one indignity at a time, thinking she's the one telling the story. That's the cruelest turn: last chapter Pace produced this with his hand; here Vee produces it herself, out loud, and Randi barely has to do anything but say "walk me" and "how was it settled."

The bathroom scene is a near-exact rerun of ch.29 — aroused to the edge across a table by Randi's talk, fleeing to a stall that "clicked" but "in her head it slammed." Same architecture, worse indignity, because this time she's not fleeing the story cold, she's already been made to relive her own spanking out loud first. And the stop is the same devastating beat: "Randi's face arrived behind her eyes... as though Vee were the best thing in the room." She can't come while being looked at like that — the installed mother-governor, only now it wears *Randi's* face. "What would Randi think. But it was not the thinking, it was the looking." She blots herself, aches, comes back cowed.

**Randi.** This is her least deniable chapter and also her most *seamless*, which is the terrifying combination. Watch the choreography: she declines to reach, she just plants "vanilla" and lets Vee spend herself climbing back off it. Then — the second the confession is done and Vee's at the top — Randi picks up her own "story": the Radford boy, "a young Robert Redford," who kisses and then *gets a phone call and has to leave*, stranding her "with the engine running and nobody left to drive it." And she takes care of herself — "Dress up, panties to the side, knees wide, and me singing the whole song of self-pleasure." That's the shove that pushes Vee off the table and into the stall. It arrives right on cue, and its *content* is a mirror of exactly what Vee just confessed being unable to do to herself in ch.29. I can't prove the story's invented, but it's built like a tool. The "young Robert Redford" even rhymes with the Fairytale-prince register — a too-perfect man who reaches nothing / leaves.

And then the two lines that turned my stomach and are the best writing in the chapter:

- **"Sticky fingers?"** — said flat, into Vee's face-on-fire return, and then instantly laundered: "They came while you were up. Go on, have one." Vee "could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." That's the whole book in one exchange. Randi knows. Or Randi means the pastry. It cannot be resolved, and the not-resolving is the trap.
- The **pastry**. Randi takes "one neat bite... and set it down... 'Delicious' in a way that closed the subject, and did not touch it again." And the last line — Vee's tongue finding "a thread of sweetness... the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." That's the taste-thread ghost I've been tracking (ch.29–30) landing as a full chord, and it's *chilling*: the sweetness in Vee's mouth is transferred from Randi, off a kiss, a taste she never earned or chose. Randi ate for her. It reads like a claim being pressed into her body without her knowing she took it. I sat with that for a while.

**Trust/suspicion.** I have no doubt left about Randi's method — the text has earned that over thirty chapters and this one dispenses with plausible cover almost entirely. But I want to be honest: nothing *inside this scene* forces Vee to see it, and the text never announces it either. The "Sticky fingers?" ambiguity is genuinely double — a real friend could say that teasing, or a hunter could say it knowing. I'm holding *both*, which is exactly Vee's position, and I think that's deliberate and it's the most uncomfortable I've been.

**Erotic charge.** High and sustained — a 3 — but it's the *shameful* register, not the tender one. It works on me hardest in the confession itself ("He checked" / "*Inside?*") and peaks at "the whole song of self-pleasure." Then it deliberately curdles in the stall — the arousal is denied, turns "low and ashamed and obedient," and that denial is somehow more charged than a completion would've been. The chapter withholds the orgasm from Vee *and* from me, and I felt the ache of that.

**Friction.** Very little. One small thing: "the way it had Randi's bare honest skin" and later "Randi's bare honest skin" again — "honest" applied to the woman whose whole art is the double register is doing ironic work, and I noticed the seam of it, the author's thumb just barely visible. But it's Vee's word, Vee's blindness, so I let it pass. Nothing here over-explained; the "Sticky fingers?" beat especially is trusted to land without a single line of gloss, which is why it lands.

**What I want / dread next.** I dread — and expect — that "best friend in the whole world" is the hook set deepest. The goodbye kiss escalated again (the beat-past, the drawing-off, the *little more in them*), and it deposited literal sweetness. *Guess from here:* the tracks are now one meal away from touching in Vee's *awareness* — she's confessed Pace's whole scene into Randi's keeping, and Randi's ex-story is built out of Vee's own ch.29 confession. I keep waiting for the moment Vee hears her own installed template come back at her, or recognizes a phrase of Pace's in Randi's mouth (or vice versa). It didn't happen here; it feels one riser away. And I still want Cassie to get one true word before that window shuts — she's been gone too long and this is exactly the meal she'd have read cold.
