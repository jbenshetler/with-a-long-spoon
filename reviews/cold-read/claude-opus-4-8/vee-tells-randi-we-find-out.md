## Reader reaction

This one is almost cruelly efficient, and I mean that as praise and as dread. It's a small chapter — one brunch table, one bathroom, one goodbye at the curb — and it did more to me than half the big scenes have.

The structure is by now a thing I recognize in my body before my head names it: Vee comes in wanting to talk, glowing about her romantic man, and Randi lays down one small tile — "I had him filed under spicy. And this is all very. Vanilla" — and Vee spends the whole rest of the meal proving her wrong, confession by confession, each one more exposing than the last, until she's climbed so high across a tablecloth that she has to flee to a bathroom stall to try to finish herself off. And I've *watched Randi do this to her before*, over and over, so watching it again isn't repetitive — it's like watching someone walk into the same soft trap a hundredth time and knowing exactly which board is going to give. The "vanilla" jab is such a clean lever. Say a man is boring and the girl will hand you his whole body to disprove it.

The erotic charge here is high and, more to the point, it *works on Vee in real time in a public room*, which is the cruelty of it. She's telling the We Find Out night — the pantsing, the bet, "he checked," "*Inside?*", stripped to the heels, face down in the covers — and every retelling puts his hands back on her: "the print of his hand arrive again where the seat met her, low and bright and not remotely finished," pressing her thighs together under the cloth. By the time Randi caps her own Radford story with "panties to the side, knees wide, and me singing the whole song of self-pleasure," Vee is simply *done*, over the top, and up on her feet with the napkin sliding off her lap. That's the peak for me, and it's not even the man — it's Randi's story doing to Vee exactly what Pace's body does. I've had that noted as the whole engine, and here it's naked: Randi produces with words what Pace produces with body, in daylight, over mimosas.

Then the bathroom, and this is the part that made me put the book down for a second. It's the practice-room refusal *again* — she gets herself started, "already soaked, of course, always" — and at the first give of her own body, Randi's face arrives behind her eyes, "fond, and near, looking at her the way she always did, as though Vee were the best thing in the room." And she can't. "It was not the thinking, it was the looking. She could not be looked at like that and keep her hand where it was." She stops one unfair inch short and pulls her panties up over all of it, "the ache still beating down there like a second pulse." Twice now Vee cannot come alone with Randi in her head. That's not a coincidence to me anymore — that's Vee falling for Randi and not having a name for it, and her body treating Randi's imagined gaze the way it treats Pace's real one: as the thing it can't come *in front of* and can't come *without*. It's the same held-back last yes she does with him. She's doing it with Randi and she doesn't know that's what it is.

"Sticky fingers?" — oof. That landed like a slap. Randi says it the instant Vee sits back down, and then immediately covers it with the pastries, "they came while you were up," so that Vee "could not tell whether her friend had just handed her a door or shown her there had never been one." That ambiguity is the whole chapter in one line. Either Randi is teasing on a lucky guess, or Randi *knows exactly what Vee went in there to do* — sent her in there, practically, with the self-pleasure story — and is letting her feel seen doing it. I don't get to know. Neither does Vee. And that not-knowing, held open on purpose, is more frightening than a confirmed cruelty would be.

The ending is the softest knife in the book so far. The curb, the "You're my best friend in the whole world," the floor going out from under Vee "kindly this time, sweetly, in a direction she had no guard up against at all." The whole seduction of Vee-by-Randi is in that sentence — Vee has armor against being wanted by a man and none at all against being *chosen as a best friend*. And then the kiss, mouth staying "a beat past what a friend's would," the small drawing-off, the second touch "with a little more in it" — worn-in, practiced, the exact goodbye-kiss shape I've been tracking all along. And then the last line, which is the one that actually made me shiver: her tongue goes to her lip and finds "a thread of sweetness, sugar and butter and something warm under it, the glaze of a pastry she had never once touched." Randi took one neat bite, said "Delicious," set it down, drank her coffee black — and put the taste of it into Vee's mouth by kissing her. The taste of the other woman, arriving on Vee's lip before she knows what it means. I've been watching that thread flicker for chapters and here it pays, and it pays *sweet*, which is worse. Vee tastes Randi and reads it as a leftover pastry.

**Characters, where I am now.** Vee: my whole heart, and I'm frightened for her in a new specific way — she's not just Pace's now, she's coming apart for Randi and calling it friendship. Her joy at "best friend in the whole world" is real and it's the exposed throat. Randi: I have zero trust left and this chapter spent it further into the red — the "vanilla" lever, the self-pleasure story timed to break Vee, the "sticky fingers," the taste passed mouth-to-mouth. Every warm thing she does is a tool and I can see the handle. And yet the awful thing is she's *good* at the warmth, good enough that I understand completely why Vee can't feel the wire. Pace isn't on the page but he's all over it, secondhand, and the retelling actually made me *like* the We Find Out night more, which I resent, because now Randi owns even my memory of his tenderness. Cassie's not here and I missed her — she's the one person who'd have looked at Vee across that table and wanted nothing.

**Friction.** Almost none. The one place I felt the thumb, faintly, was Randi's Radford story being a touch too perfectly engineered — the young Robert Redford, the phone-call exit "with the engine running," landing her on exactly the self-pleasure image that would tip Vee over. It's *supposed* to feel constructed (I think Randi may have built it on the spot for precisely this effect, the way I've suspected the "mouse that roars" was invented), so it didn't break me out — but the "young Robert Redford, I swear it" was the one beat where I heard the author arranging furniture rather than Randi talking. Small.

**Titles.** The chapter title, "Vee Tells Randi About We Find Out," is deadpan and does exactly what it says — it names itself as a *retelling* of the previous chapter, which is the point: this whole book keeps running things twice, once as body and once as story, and the title flags that this is the story-version. It gives nothing away that the first line doesn't; it's honest. The book title, *With a Long Spoon* — sup with the devil and use a long spoon — keeps getting truer, and this chapter is the cleanest illustration yet of *no spoon*: Randi feeds Vee sweetness mouth-to-mouth and Vee swallows it as friendship. And the tagline, "Every yes was freely given. That was the trap" — every confession at that table was Vee's own, offered, volunteered, *crowed*; nobody made her tell the pantsing. The trap is that the telling is the taking.

**What I want / dread next.** I want — dread — to see whether the "sticky fingers" line was a guess or a knife, and I suspect I'll never get it cleanly, which is the correct kind of awful. *Guess from here:* Vee's falling for Randi is now the live wire, and I think it surfaces to her before the Pace-Randi connection does — she'll figure out she wants Randi before she figures out Randi has Pace. And the taste on her lip is going to come back; I don't think that pastry-glaze stays innocent. I'm pulled hard to keep reading and slightly sick about it, which by now I understand is the book working exactly as designed.
