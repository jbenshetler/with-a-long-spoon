**Felt read**

This one is short and it's mostly sweet on its face, and I spent the whole thing sick with the knowledge Vee doesn't have. That's the engine of the chapter for me: dramatic irony pulled so tight it aches. Vee comes in glowing, and every single thing she offers Cassie as proof that she's safe, that she's *chosen*, that nothing was done to her — "Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it. I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose" — I read as the exact thing Pace engineered. He *built* the not-doing-anything. He left the last inch for her precisely so she'd feel it was hers. So her happiest, most self-possessed line ("*Me*. Look at me.") is the one that curdled worst in my stomach. I wanted to be glad for her and I couldn't, quite, and that gap is the chapter working on me.

The title landed like a trapdoor. "Off Six-Fourteen" — and then Vee, cheerfully assembling her little dossier of safety, *hands over the address*: "He lives out west of town, off six-fourteen, past where it goes to gravel." She says it as *proof* — "See. I know where he lives and everything." And I know what's out there. I've seen the bench. I've watched Randi ask, on the quad, *do you think she'll like the bench?* So the thing Vee brandishes as her security is the coordinates of the room with the thing in it. That's a genuinely chilling use of a title — it gave nothing away going in (it read like nothing, an address), and reading the chapter loaded it completely. Cassie's state-trooper joke — "so I know what to tell the state troopers when you go missing on a mountain with a man you met at a coffee shop" — is played as banter and reads to me as the truest line in the room.

Cassie is who I trust, wholly, and she got dearer here. "How much did he give you to drink?" — she goes straight for the mechanism, the drugging/coercion possibility, because some flat dry floor under her gladness won't quite let this be clean. She doesn't tease Vee about the vulnerable thing; she teases around it and lands on the real questions — *is he rich, what's the whole address, borrow good ones.* "Borrow good ones" is such a small tender thing to end on. And her "Goodbye, Vee" from the coffee-shop chapter still hangs over her for me — Cassie keeps sounding, without knowing it, like someone narrating a disappearance. I keep waiting for her instinct to become knowledge.

My feeling about Pace has hardened since the froyo/dinner chapters, even though he does nothing "wrong" on the page — because I've now seen the two frames side by side. In "Tannin" I watched Randi grind on his lap and demand *tell me* about the girl, watched him withhold Vee's confession ("It's hers") like a man curating. So Vee's whole beautiful evening, replayed here as pure wonder, I read as product. The tenderness is real *and* it's bait, and the book refuses to let me have one without the other. That's the discomfort I can't put down.

Erotic charge: low, and rightly so — this is a debrief, not a scene, the kiss is recounted not enacted. What heat there is lives in Vee's *first-person agency*, the pull-him-back-and-do-it-myself of it, which is genuinely lovely as a beat of desire waking up. But it's swamped for me by dread; I couldn't melt into it. The swoon is there in the prose — the spin that nearly takes out the closet door, "the room too small to hold what she had to do with it" — and I felt the pull of her happiness even as I braced against it.

Friction: honestly very little; it's economical and it earns its brevity. If anything the only faint thumb-on-scale is how *completely* the chapter arranges Vee's every reassurance to double as evidence of the trap — "off six-fourteen" as proof of safety, "nobody did anything to me," the going-missing joke, the never-hiked innocence — it's a lot of irony stacked in one small room, and a hair less would have trusted me more. But it's the kind of over-determination I mostly forgave, because the voice stayed warm and specific.

What I want / dread next (guesses from here): I dread the hike out to that house. I expect the bench is where this is walking — Randi's want, Vee's blithe "I have to wear real shoes." I want Cassie's unease to sharpen into something she acts on; she's the only brake in the machine. And I'm bracing for the moment Vee learns Randi and Pace already know each other — that the collision on the quad was staged. When that lands, this whole giddy chapter is going to feel even worse in retrospect, which I think is the point.
