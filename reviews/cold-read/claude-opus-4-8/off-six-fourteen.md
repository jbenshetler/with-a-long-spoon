**Felt read**

This is the first chapter that made my stomach hurt while I was smiling, and it did it on purpose. It's all warmth on the surface — Vee home in the cold-cracked dorm, spinning a clumsy half-dance that nearly takes out the closet door, flinging a pillow, counting off what she knows about Pace "like a dossier, like plenty." And I couldn't be glad with her, not cleanly, because I know where she got picked. I watched Randi stop dead on the quad and say *yes* in a voice stripped of all her armor, watched her ask *do you think she'll like the bench*, watched Pace "accidentally" cross a coffee shop to a girl whose number Randi already had him primed to get. So Vee's radiant certainty — *I did it because I wanted to and I did it on purpose. Me.* — lands on me as the saddest line in the chapter precisely because she's right about the kiss and catastrophically wrong about the frame. She chose freely inside a room someone else built around her.

That's the engine here, and the chapter is smart enough not to touch it. Nobody winks. Cassie's joke — *how much did he give you to drink?* — is the only shadow the page lets in, and it's the wrong shadow, aimed at date-rape wariness when the real thing is slower and stranger and has Vee's own friend Randi at the center of it. Vee bats it away with the truest thing she's got: *Nobody did anything to me. I wasn't talked into it.* And she's *telling the truth.* That's what undid me. No one is going to be able to point to the moment she was taken, because the whole apparatus is built so she'll walk in on her own feet, grinning.

My feeling about Pace keeps getting more complicated and more uneasy. In "Substitution" and the date he was genuinely, meltingly good — the *may I*, giving back the pencil, the quiet built into the text so she could carry it. I *felt* the swoon; I'm not going to pretend I didn't, because the book earned it on the page. But I've now seen the backstage: Randi reporting to him on the couch, him tasting nothing but the wine off her mouth, the two of them hunting a girl like a shared appetite. So his tenderness reads double — real *and* instrumented, and I genuinely can't tell how much he's lying to himself about which. Randi I ache for in a different direction. Her want is so big it won't fit her face, and she's feeding the one person she can't let herself want straight into her boyfriend's bed to keep from having to own it. That's going to cost somebody, probably Vee, maybe all three.

Cassie is the one clean thing, and I love her for it. *Goodbye, Vee* back in the lecture chapter still rings; here she's the friend who won't tease about the thing that matters, who tells Vee *that's not a boy, that's a man*, who worries about state troopers on a mountain. She's the reader's proxy — close enough to smell something, not close enough to name it — and her plain *borrow good ones* at the end sat on me like a small cold draft.

The erotic charge is low and that's correct; this is the exhale after "May I Choose." No heat to speak of — it's a girl talking to her roommate. What's alive is dread and tenderness braided so tight I can't pull them apart.

**The title.** *Off Six-Fourteen* — and the chapter hands it to me as Vee's proof of safety. *I know where he lives and everything.* Except she doesn't: she hasn't seen the house, doesn't know if he's rich, has only a road that goes to gravel and "a trail he goes to, somewhere." The title names the gap between what she thinks she knows and the almost-nothing she actually has — a vague direction west, past where the pavement ends. And I know what's at the end of that road: a bedroom with a bench in it that becomes a table again by morning. So the title gave me a quiet chill the chapter's sunny surface didn't. The volume line, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION* — the spoon is the "sup with the devil, use a long spoon" proverb, and I feel the full weight of it now: Vee is being politely invited, and she has no spoon at all. *A Polite Invitation* is exactly the mechanism I've watched being built — everything offered, nothing forced, the door held open.

What I want next: I want Vee to stay this happy for one more beat and I know she can't. I dread the hike — the house, the bench, Randi arriving as the "gift." Guess from here: the threesome Randi engineered is going to collide with whatever is actually growing between Pace and Vee, and Randi is going to discover she can't give away the one thing she won't let herself keep.
