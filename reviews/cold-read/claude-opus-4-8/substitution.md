## Reader reaction

**The felt read.**

So here it is: the meeting I've been braced for since the quad. Pace walks across a coffee shop toward Vee, and I'm reading it with my stomach, not my heart, because I know things Vee can't. I know Randi was taken apart on that bench and fled back behind her face, vowing never to come out again. I know the pointing game ended with Randi's helpless, bare *yes* at Vee by the dining hall. And now Randi sits here *texting* — "thumbs quick, the private absorbed focus of a girl writing to someone who had her attention" — and a "cute guy in line" materializes and crosses straight to Vee. I don't think that's a boy. I think that's the summons landing. The whole scene is staged, and Vee is the only one in it who doesn't know she's in a scene.

That's what makes this chapter land on me as dread dressed as a meet-cute. And god, it *is* a good meet-cute — that's the awful part. The math lesson is the most beautiful and the most sinister thing in the book so far. "Turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do." He reads her problem like a stranger meeting it fresh, asks what her professor said instead of flexing, makes a margin note instead of a rewrite, and *gives her back the pencil* at the exact line where she can finish it herself. Vee's econ-eye catches it — "the path of a person who wasn't performing... who had kept himself out of it so the solving stayed hers." That's precisely what he did to Randi on the bench: waited for her to come out from behind the face, kept himself out of it until *she* arrived. The tenderness is real. It's also a *method*. Watching a genuinely gentle thing reveal itself as a repeatable technique is the coldest the book has gone, and it did it with calculus.

And Euler's — the two things that won't give on their own, "put them together, at right angles, and they turn into a single thing that's suddenly plain." He's talking about an integral. He's also describing what he's doing to the three of them. The chapter title did half the work before I read a line: *Substitution.* Vee is the substitute — for Randi, who couldn't survive being seen and is now feeding someone else into the machine. By the time Pace says "May I call you?" and Vee hears the *may* as a word from "another vocabulary," I wanted to reach into the page and pull her out of the chair.

My feeling about Randi has curdled since "The Pointing Game." There she was swept up, helplessly wanting; here she's the *procurer*, and the jealousy leaks everywhere — the coffee cup lifted to her mouth twice with no sip, the hand around it a beat too long while Pace answers Vee longer than he answers her. Vee files it as boredom. I file it as a woman watching the thing she arranged and hating it in real time. Randi's "You should go out with him," delivered flat right after, is chilling — she's closing the trap she built. I don't fully understand her motive and I'm holding it open: is this gift, revenge, self-protection by proxy, or all three? The page leaves it ajar and I'll leave it ajar.

Cassie is my one clear window and my favorite person in the room. "He looked at *her*"—Randi—"the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt." She names the exact thing the reader sees and nobody else will say. Her flat "Goodbye, Vee" from last chapter still hums under this; she sees something coming. I trust Cassie completely and almost no one else.

Pace himself — I can't find the bottom of my unease. He's never cruel on the page. He's courteous, attentive, he forgets his own coffee for her. But I've now watched him deploy the identical attention on two different women, and the "more or less" when Vee asks how far along his PhD is, the unplaceable too-good shirt, the way nothing about him fits any category — it all reads as a man who has effaced himself into a tool. Vee wondering "what he did with his hands besides math" is the only live erotic current in the chapter, and even that's her naivety charging the air, not the scene itself.

**The titles.** The chapter title *Substitution* gave the game away and I'm glad it did — it let me read the math as the thesis and ache the whole time. The cover board, *WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION*, I now read as the proverb about supping with the devil: you'd better have a long spoon. "A polite invitation" is the *may I call you* — the courtesy is the bait. These point me somewhere genuinely dark, and the book keeps earning it rather than promising it.

**What I want / dread.** I dread Vee getting the bench. "Do you think she'll like the bench?" has been sitting in me since the pointing game, and now it has a face and a phone number. *Guess from here:* Randi feeds Vee to Pace and discovers she can't actually bear it — that she wanted to be the one seen and couldn't survive it, and can't survive watching Vee be seen either. I want to keep reading badly, and I feel a little complicit for wanting to.
