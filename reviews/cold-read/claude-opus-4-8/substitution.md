## Felt read

This chapter is the one I've been braced for and wanting since the pointing game, and it delivers on a knife's edge I could feel the whole time: I know exactly what this is, and Vee doesn't, and the pleasure and the dread of that gap are the whole engine. I've watched Randi say *yes* on the lawn, watched her execute the "collision" so cleanly the redhead apologized, watched Pace point and say *her*. So when the bell over the door tinkles and Vee doesn't look, I look. When Randi texts "some boy," I know it's Pace being cued in. When Randi's hand keeps lifting the cup to her mouth without a sip happening, I know that's a woman running an operation and managing her nerves, and Vee's brain filing it as *she's bored*. That last one landed like a small cold stone. It's the cruelest, most economical beat in the chapter — the mark writing the handler's tell down as boredom.

And yet. Here is the thing I have to be honest about: **the meet-cute worked on me anyway.** I did not stay clinical. Pace crossing the floor with his eyes on her the whole way, the room settling "a half-degree" around him, the pencil he "asked for with his eyes first," the *may I call you* — I felt the swoon Vee felt, in real time, even knowing it was aimed. That's the disturbing achievement of the chapter: it puts me inside Vee's want and inside the machinery at once and won't let me pick. The math bit is genuinely lovely — the two curiosities set "at right angles" turning into "a single thing that's suddenly plain," and him handing the pencil back at exactly the line where the solving stays hers. As pure seduction craft it's the best kind: he withholds the answer so she gets to be the one who's good. I melted at "She hadn't known she was allowed." It's tender and it's a technique and I can't unbraid them, which I think is the point.

**Vee.** I ache for her. This is my first long stretch fully inside her, and she's more than the jacket's "quietly starving" tag — she's sharp. The econ eye that reads *how a smart person handles a problem they could handle several ways* is the same eye that clocks his forearms, his too-good shirt, the fact that "he didn't fit anything she had," and then declines to finish the thought. Twice the narration says "the thought did not finish." That's her whole defense mechanism rendered as syntax, and it's devastating because her instrument is good enough to see the shape and she keeps setting the pencil down. The detail of her borrowing Randi's name-bit — "V.T. Yes, like the university" — trying on Randi's poise "to be a little more than herself," is where I felt the trap close softest: she's already imitating the person who set the snare. And the cardigan passage from the previous chapter still hums under this one, her mother's voice, *floozies*, the being-covered-and-being-the-most-of-what-the-room-sells at once.

**Randi.** My feeling about her keeps darkening, and this chapter is why. After the bench — after I watched her beg, break, and then decide *not ever*, going back behind the face — she is now running the con on a girl who is falling for her exactly as she's falling for Pace. Her *"He was cute. You should go out with him"* and the coffee-cup argument (*a man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call*) is her closing the sale, warm, expert. I don't fully hate her, because I've been inside how hollowed-out she is, but I distrust every warm thing she does now, and this chapter earned that distrust on the page, not from the jacket.

**Cassie.** She's the one I trust, and this chapter deepened her into something like the reader's proxy — the only person at the table watching the cups instead of the show. "He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt" and her flat naming of the look "that slid off Cassie every day of her life" — she sees the whole geometry and says the true thing plainly. And that *"Goodbye, Vee"* from the previous chapter, the flat finished word for a longer parting, sits under this whole scene like a warning I can't act on. I don't know what she knows or suspects; the page holds it open and I'm holding it open too. But she's the friend I'd want, and she's the one who wasn't invited to the coffee that mattered.

**Pace.** Harder for me here than in his own POV chapters. Read from inside his head, his courtliness felt real, even principled — the closed door he respected, the *ask before you take*. Read from outside, aimed at a girl who has no idea she was chosen weeks ago, the same courtliness is a lure. "He moved like someone who had never once had to think about where his body was." I still find him compelling — that's the trouble — but this is the first time his gentleness read to me as a *weapon* rather than a virtue, precisely because I watched it get selected off a lawn. The *may I* that undoes Vee undid me a little too, and I resent that it did.

**Trust vs. suspicion.** No suspicion to earn here — I'm past suspicion into knowledge. The chapter isn't asking *is something off*, it's asking me to sit in the fully-loaded irony and enjoy the seduction against my own better information, which is a genuinely uncomfortable, well-earned place to be. The only thing left genuinely ajar is Cassie: what she reads, whether her *goodbye* is grief or foresight or nothing. I won't fix it.

**Erotic charge.** Low and clothed but real — it lives entirely in the forearms, the rolled cuffs, the steady hand on the page, her eye "staying a half-second past where she would have chosen." No heat event; this is a 1, all simmer, and correctly so. The charge is displacement: math as foreplay, the withheld answer as the withheld touch. It works because the previous chapters taught me his register, so a rolled cuff carries the bench behind it.

**Friction.** Very little. If I press: the interior naming of his tutoring method gets a hair over-explicit — "the path of a person who wasn't performing — not showing off, not hiding it either" almost tells me the read it should let me have. But it's saved by being *her* econ-brain doing the analysis, in character, and by refusing to finish the thought. I bought it. Nothing felt like a thumb on the scale; if anything the thumb is invisible, which is scarier.

**Titles.** "Substitution" is a beautiful oblique. On the surface it's the math — u-substitution, "turn the thing in front of you into a thing you already know how to do," which is literally the professor's trick and literally what Pace teaches her. On the reread charge it's colder: Vee is a *substitution*. She was picked to stand in — for the type, the game, whatever Randi and Pace are actually doing to each other; the girl at the table is the answer they turned their problem into. And there's the softer, sadder read: Vee substitutes Randi's borrowed poise for her own, substitutes the *feeling* of being chosen for the transcript she can't keep. The title didn't give anything away before I read it — it opened up *after*, which is the good kind.

The **book title, *A Polite Invitation***, is now fully cashed: *may I call you* is the polite invitation, and the jacket's "the invitation reached her long before she knew there was one" is exactly what I just watched — the invitation was issued on a lawn a chapter ago. **With a Long Spoon** I keep turning over — the proverb is *he who sups with the devil should have a long spoon* — and it reads to me as: someone here is dining with something dangerous and thinks they've got distance from it. After the bench I'd have said that's Randi (supping with Pace and getting burned). Now I wonder if it's all three, and Vee's the one with no spoon at all. The tagline on my jacket — *Every yes was freely given. That was the problem.* — is the whole chapter in one line. Every yes here IS freely given: her *yes, please, thank you*, her number, her nodding before her mouth caught up. And it's a trap. That's the *problem*, and the book is making me feel it as pleasure, which is the discomfort I signed up for.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the phone to light up as badly as Vee does, and I hate that I want it, and the chapter knowing I'd want it is exactly why it ends on the facedown phone she won't turn over. *Guess from here:* it lights up. My real dread is watching Vee tell Randi "everything" over those lunches the jacket promised — handing the choreographer the tape of the choreography — and the slower dread is the thing the bench chapter planted: that this is as real for the two who started it as for the girl who never knew, and nobody has the long spoon. I'm fully pulled in. Read the next one immediately.
