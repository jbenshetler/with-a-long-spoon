# Reader reaction: "Substitution"

Oh, this one made my stomach drop even while it was charming me — and that doubleness is the whole horrible pleasure of it. Because I *know* what Vee doesn't. I sat through the bench, I sat through the pointing game, I heard Randi say *do you think she'll like the bench* over the top of a stranger's copper head. So when Randi "suggests they move to the place on Wilson," and then sits there thumbing at her phone with her hand going to the coffee cup again and again and never quite drinking — the little tell the page keeps ringing like a bell — I didn't read a bored friend. I read a handler. Randi is running this. Pace didn't wander over; he was called across the room. And Vee, brilliant Vee, is being delivered.

What guts me is how *good* the seduction is, because I've seen this exact method before — on Randi, on the bench. He reads the problem like it's new, asks what her professor said instead of showing off, makes a margin note instead of a rewrite, and hands the pencil back *at the exact line where she could solve it herself.* That is precisely how he took Randi apart: patience, restraint, keeping himself out of it so the surrender stays hers. Vee even clocks the technique with her econ eye — "a person who wasn't performing" — and reads tactic as character. That's the tragedy in miniature: she's smart enough to see the method and it only makes her fall harder. The page earned every degree of my dread; none of it is me being on guard.

And still — God help me — the warmth landed. *What's drawing you to it?* is a genuinely good question, and Vee talking for five unbroken minutes about a research project no one ever asked her about, him not touching his phone, the laugh at her accidental joke "over before she had time to be embarrassed" — I felt the lift of being listened to. I rooted for that feeling even knowing whose hands were on the controls. That's the cruelty of the writing: it makes the real thing and the trap the same object. *May I call you.* I heard it the way she heard it, a word from another vocabulary, and I understood exactly why her body nodded before her mouth did.

Vee breaks my heart here. She *borrows Randi's bit* — "Vivienne Thorne, V.T., like the university… no, my parents didn't plan it" — the very swerve she filed away watching Randi in the lecture hall, trying on the poise of the person who is currently hunting her. Then she grabs her own name back — *Vee, people call me Vee* — some instinct reaching for herself. She doesn't know she's a substitution. The title kept detonating for me: Pace literally teaches her *substitution*, turning e^{ix} and the other thing at right angles into "a single thing that's suddenly plain" — and that's what's being done to her, two people put together to make a third thing legible and usable. Substitution as calculus, and Vee as the substitute for what Randi couldn't be on that bench: the one who actually gives herself.

Cassie is the one clean instrument in the room and I clung to her. *"He looked at her the way you'd look at somebody's great-aunt."* She names the whole geometry without a flicker — the look that slides off her every day, so she reads it perfectly on someone else. Randi's closing lines are the coldest in the chapter: *"You should go out with him,"* and then the little forensic flourish — *"a man doesn't forget his coffee over a girl he isn't going to call."* She's teaching Vee to want the trap, coaching the mark. My feeling about Randi hardened all the way through here; after the bench I *ached* for her, and now I watch her weaponize exactly what was done to her, and I can hold both — the wound and the predation in one person.

Where it works on me as heat: the forearms — "not a math tutor's forearms" — the rolled cuffs, the shirt too good for a man who fixes things, Vee's eye snagging half a second past where she'd choose. It's a fully clothed calculus lesson and it ran warm, the erotics of being *chosen* and *attended to.* It never peaks because it isn't meant to; it's a laying of the trap, and the charge is all anticipation braided with dread. My only faint friction is that the econ-eye analysis paragraph runs a beat long — but I forgive it, because her intelligence being useless to save her is the point.

What I want and dread: I want Vee to keep that phone facedown and I know she won't, and the page knows I know — *she let herself want it to light up.* I dread the bench. I dread Randi watching. Guess from here: the threesome Randi "offered" as a gift is the delivery mechanism, and Vee is walking toward the bench Randi already asked about — but I don't know yet whether Randi wants Vee for herself, wants her destroyed, or both, and I'm genuinely unsure, which is why I can't stop reading.
