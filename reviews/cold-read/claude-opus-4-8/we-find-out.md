# Cold read — We Find Out

*scene: scenes/we-find-out.md · model: claude-opus-4-8 · read after: practice-room*

## Reader reaction

This is the chapter the last two have been leaning toward, and it landed on me hard — I read it fast, which is its own verdict. After the sealed-room chill of "The Practice Room," where Randi installed the over-the-knee template with *words*, here Pace does it with his hands, and the collision I've been dreading between the two tracks doesn't happen — instead something worse and more intimate does. Randi's story *rose up in her* mid-scene: "would she have to count out loud? Was that the theatre of it." That's the two tracks touching, but only inside Vee, and she doesn't know it's a touch. She thinks she's bracing for a thing she dreads; she doesn't know she's been *taught* to dread it, primed to expect exactly this. And then the whole point lands: "What came was nothing like the story." Pace is quiet where Randi's ex was theatrical. So the reader gets a cold click — Randi built the expectation, and Pace's tenderness reads as *better* than the template precisely because Vee has a template to measure against — while Vee gets only warmth. That is the most disturbing thing this book has done to me, because it worked *on me too*. I was relieved for her that it wasn't the counting-and-show version. The relief is the trap.

The chase that opens it is genuinely fun, and I want to say that plainly — the shorts-shake-tickle romp, her discovering "she was faster than he was," the flashing, "waved them like a flag over a taken field." It's the lightest, most kid-in-love the book has been, and it *had* to be, because "earn the dark by being light" is exactly what's operating: I have to fall for this afternoon before I can feel the cold under it. And I did fall for it. The flash — "some small astonished part of her stood off and watched her do it and could not believe it was her. The house made her brave" — is Vee at her most freed, and it's lovely.

The heat peaks for me at the bet. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out" — that's the title detonating, and it's a beautiful piece of erotic engineering: he offers her an out that is also a demand, and her body forecloses the out before she can take it. "Some part of her had understood it before the rest, had gone small and quiet and certain a moment ahead." The finger going up the thigh, slow, eyes on hers "so she had nowhere to put her own" — that's the strongest sustained charge in a while. And the shame-doubling is here again, freshly occasioned: not just wet but the *plenty* of it, "the whole length of it coated," held up in lamplight. That's the constant-shame/changing-occasion rule I've watched work over and over, and it still ran heat through me.

But then — and this is the move that got me — he doesn't shame her with it. "He looked at the wet on his finger the very same way" he'd looked at her under the tree that first night. "Like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good." That's Pace's whole tenderness-engine: he takes the thing she's spent her life hiding and *puts it on the record as good*. And her tongue touching her lip before she knows it means to — that's the taste-thread ghost, her body reaching for the wet without permission. I felt the pull of him there almost exactly as hard as she does, which the book keeps telling me is the danger, and I keep not being able to hold the danger and the pull apart. That's the design and it's working.

The **aftercare** is where my suspicion-radar and my heart pull opposite directions. "He hadn't planned any of what came next, she could tell, and that was the thing that undid her. No routine to it, nothing practiced." Vee reads the *juice* as spontaneous, unrehearsed, proof he's as swept-up as she is. But I remember the very first scene — Pace with Randi, the water bottle, "there you are, Randi." And the aftercare ritual: water, "wonderful," "there you are." So when Vee thinks *unpracticed*, I know it's the most practiced thing he does. "He said it like a plain fact he wanted on the record. He kept saying it… wonderful, wonderful, until the word and his hand in her hair were the same thing." The reader gets the cold read (this is his protocol) while Vee gets "for the first time she could remember, treasured." That gap is the whole book in one paragraph. I didn't *want* to notice it. I noticed it anyway, and it hurt.

The **scrunchies** on the counter — deep green with white dots, "far more than a person could go through quickly," no note, "he meant to be looking at her hair a long while yet." That's the pre-warmed-conditions move again: care laundered through provisioning, ownership dressed as thoughtfulness. And it's genuinely tender at the same time — the *plenty* of them is the tell and the gift both. Also I clocked **green** — the folded green panties, and now green scrunchies. His color for her? Or just a color. I have no firm read, but it pinged.

The **scar** got its quietest beat yet: "she just knew it was there, the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep. She didn't ask now either." I've wanted that scar explained for chapters, and this chapter *declined* to explain it in the most loaded way — she's stopped asking. She's converted the withheld interior into intimacy ("a person you've decided to keep"). That's her doing to the mystery what she does to everything: covering the un-given with the chosen. It made the scar feel more ominous, not less, precisely because she's made peace with not knowing.

The bathroom monologue — "Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together," "My Victorian LARPing does me no good" — is Vee at her most self-aware and I *liked* her enormously there, dry and funny at her own reflection. But even the self-awareness serves the capture: "I'm going to start bringing a bag… and meant it." She's furnishing her own residence in him. The antiperspirant — "his smell on her instead of her own" — is her choosing to be marked, and it's sweet, and it's annexation.

**Where it went slack:** almost nowhere, honestly, but the closing curry paragraph, lovely as it is, is the third or fourth time the book has done "he cooks her the abundant meal that looks like her childhood casseroles but tastes like nothing from there." "It looked like a dozen gravy-covered dishes from her childhood. It tasted like none of them." That's a real, earned image — the working-class Ohio girl fed something her mother's kitchen couldn't make — but I've had this beat before and it landed a little softer for the repetition. It's a gentle coda, so I forgive it; I just felt the familiarity.

**Friction / thumb on the scale:** very little. The one place I felt the author's hand was "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no longer any such thing as a witness, anywhere, and for a little while she let her body say the thing her mouth still wouldn't, and was not ashamed." It's the thesis stated almost outright — shame needs a witness, the sealed room removes the witness, so she's freed. It's *beautiful* writing and I felt it, but it's the closest the book came to explaining its own engine to me rather than letting me feel it. It nearly crossed from resonance into exposition. It stayed on the right side, barely.

**What I want / dread next:** Vee has now *lost the bet* — she's crossed a threshold with her body that she narrated denying the whole way ("It's not," "I don't") while rising to meet his hand. The denial-while-yielding is her new steady state and I want to see what it costs. The bench is still unseen. This was over-the-knee-adjacent (kneel at the edge of the bed, fold down), the *domestic* version; the apparatus built seven months before Randi still waits, and after this chapter I'm more sure it's coming and more sure Vee will walk to it as if it's her idea. *Guess from here:* the next Randi scene, Vee tells her about this — and Randi hears her own template come back in Vee's mouth, and I get to watch the two tracks close from the outside while Vee thinks she's just confiding. I dread that and want it. I also keep waiting for the thing Vee can't see — that she was *aimed* — and this chapter made me realize she may never see it until the blindfold, because she's now converting every gap (the scar, the "unplanned" juice) into intimacy instead of question.
