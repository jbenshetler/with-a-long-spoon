## Reader reaction

# Felt read

Well. The border fell.

I came into this chapter braced — she had that one held thing, the stopped hand from The Practice Room, the "I will not say yes to it" — and this chapter is the whole arc of that yes being drawn out of her, inch by inch, through a game she thinks she's winning. And it's devastating, because the whole first half is *play.* Genuine, delighted play. She texts him first for the first time ever, she comes an hour early, she pokes him "Get up. Get up. Get up," she steals his shorts and waves them like "a flag over a taken field." I was grinning through it. It's the lightest the book has been, and the lightness is exactly what makes the turn land so hard.

The thing that got me, physically, before anything else: **she made the wall flinch.** "She had never once seen anything she did land on him... and she had just watched it flinch." That's the first time in this whole book Vee has felt she had power over Pace, and it's a lie — or at least it's a floor she doesn't know is tilted, because the very chase she thinks she's winning ("she was faster than he was") funnels her down the hall into "the bedroom was small and had no floor to lose him on." She runs herself into the trap laughing. The tagline on the cover — *every yes was freely given, that was the trap* — I've been holding it loosely, but here it stopped being marketing. That is *literally the chapter.* He builds an actual **bet**: "If you don't enjoy it, I'll go take my shower. If you do, you let me spank you properly." And then he doesn't wait for her word — he goes and *finds out,* hand up her thigh, two fingers on the gusset, and reads the verdict off her body before her mouth can vote. "Either you admit you lost, or we find out." Her yes is extracted as *evidence,* not asked for. The nod she "didn't decide to."

That's the machinery, and I saw it working, and here's my honest problem as a reader: **it worked on me too.** The erotic charge in this chapter is very high — a real 3 — and it peaks not at the spanking but at the finger held up in the lamplight, "the whole length of it... coated," "the shameful plenty of the wet." That image has been built for so many chapters — the wetness she's policed her whole life — that seeing it displayed back to her, and him looking at it "like it was true. Like it was fine. Like it was hers, and good" — that undid me a little the way it undid her. The book earned that. And then the spanking itself is the *opposite* of what she (and I) were primed for: Randi's story rose up in her, the counting, the theatre, "she braced for that, for loudness, for a show" — and instead "what came was nothing like the story," quiet, gentling, his mouth pressed to the pink of her. The book set up the bench-logic through Randi and then deliberately *didn't* pay it that way. That withholding is smart and it's also — I'll say it plainly — more seductive than the loud version would have been.

**Pace.** My distrust of him has been dissolving for chapters and it kept unnerving me, and this chapter is the sharpest version of that dread yet, because the tenderness is *genuinely* moving and I *know* it's also a win being logged. "You lost. Spanks from a lost bet come on a bare bottom." He invents rules mid-game — "No. Slow. That's part of the bet too. / It hadn't been part of any bet he'd named" — the text flags that he's making it up, that the "bet" is a costume for taking what he wants, and she's "too far down into the warm dim place" to litigate. That's the thumb I feel him pressing, and the book *shows* me the thumb, which is why I both trust the book and distrust him. And then — juice. "He hadn't planned any of what came next, she could tell, and that was the thing that undid her." The unplannedness as the seduction. The green scrunchies with white dots, a whole "form" of them, "more than a person could go through quickly" — the plenty of them saying he means to be looking at her hair a long while. That's the feeding motif again, the warm-house-kept-for-a-coming-woman, and it lands as love and reads as strategy at once, exactly the double vision this book keeps forcing.

**Vee.** I love her and I'm frightened for her. The bathroom monologue is the best character writing in a while — funny and self-aware and doomed in the same breath: "Why do I keep pretending we're not going to sleep together." "My Victorian LARPing does me no good at all when I'm sweaty and sticky." "I'm going to start bringing a bag." She's naming her own shame-costume out loud and shedding it, and it's a relief to watch — except I'm the reader who knows there's a game she can't see, and "no Randi waiting to be told" went through me like ice. She's already narrating her life to Randi in her head. The two worlds still haven't touched, and she's the wire between them, live and unaware.

**Randi** isn't on the page but she's *in* the chapter twice, and pointedly: "Randi's story rose up in her like something she'd swallowed." The Practice Room bedtime story is now operating *inside* Vee's body during sex with Pace, priming her dread, shaping her expectation. And "no Randi waiting to be told" — Vee's default is to hand this to Randi. So the primer is doing its work and the delivery pipeline is armed. That's chilling in exactly the way the book wants, and it's earned, not manufactured — the text put the story in her mouth, so it's fair that it comes back up.

**The scar.** She traces it again, "the one she had found that first night and traced and never been told about," and this time "she didn't ask now either. She just knew it was there, the way you know a thing about a person you've decided to keep." That's a lovely, aching line and it also quietly tells me the scar isn't getting answered yet — it's being *converted* from a question into a kept silence, into intimacy. Which is either sweet or ominous depending on what that scar is, and I still don't know.

**Friction.** Very little. The prose occasionally reaches for the summary that the scene already made me feel — "Shame wants a witness, and in the small lit country of his bed there was no longer any such thing as a witness" is beautiful but it's the author stepping to the front to tell me the theme I'd already got from "the world was the size of the bed." A hair of over-explaining. And "she had agreed to it a long moment before the rest of her caught up" nearly says the quiet part too loud — though given the cover tagline, maybe it's meant to. Minor. It didn't pull me out.

**The title — "We Find Out."** On the page it's his own phrase, the trap's hinge: "Either you admit you lost, or *we find out*." Innocuous as a bet between lovers; sinister once you clock that "we find out" means *he* conducts an examination of her body to convict her against her stated word. The "we" is the tell — it's not her finding out, it's the two of them, him and her body, ganging up on her mouth. It recolored the moment I hit it in the text. It didn't give anything away in advance because I didn't know his line was coming, but it's this chapter's key turned in the lock. **The book's title, *With a Long Spoon*** — still oblique to me; "sup with the devil, use a long spoon," I keep circling it, and this chapter makes me think the long spoon is the *distance* everyone thinks they have — Vee thinks she's playing, the founders think they're steering — and nobody's spoon is long enough. The tagline is now just plainly the book's engine.

**What I want / dread next.** I want the two worlds to touch and I dread it in equal measure — "no Randi waiting to be told" tells me Vee's going to *tell Randi about this,* the spanking, the bet, and Randi's going to receive the story she planted coming back to her, and I don't know how I'll survive that scene. *Guess from here:* Vee narrates the spanking to Randi; Randi performs surprise/delight while privately knowing she authored the script; and the bench — still unseen — is now the only escalation left. I dread the bench appearing with Vee this primed. And I keep waiting for the seam to split: some moment where a detail crosses between Pace's world and Randi's and Vee catches it. Not yet. Golden and tightening, still.
