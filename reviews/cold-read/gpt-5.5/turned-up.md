But I know he and Randi secretly selected Vee, and I remain uneasy about his ability to make chosen surrender feel safe while privately arranging the field. The more beautiful his care is, the more the cover tagline frightens me.

Randi has my sympathy, fascination, and unease. She is luminous and effective with Vee, and in “Dear” her public “my dear friend” felt meaningful and possibly costly. But she also pretended not to remember Pace’s name and reframed Vee’s self-chosen kiss as inevitability “Because of him.” Her hidden role in selecting Vee keeps every kindness double-lit. She has been absent for many chapters now, and that absence feels increasingly charged and almost unstable because Pace and Vee’s private intimacy has escalated enormously: mountain/truck/flannel, house, handmade dress, topless measuring, visible arousal, Pace’s controlled desire, and now Vee’s partial confession to Cassie. I am bracing for Randi’s return.

Vee has my full sympathy and protectiveness. She is hungry for care, respect, chosen attention, bodily freedom, and relief from managing herself. She is increasingly active rather than merely acted upon: she chose the kiss, chose the hike, chose to call Pace into the truck, chose to be seen, chose the house, chose the measurement, and in “A Round” began choosing to affect Pace deliberately. Her shame is old and bodily, especially around her mother’s sexual/body judgments and the loss of swimming after her curves came in. “A Round” gave her a major internal turn: she was seen at her most ashamed and not found wanting, then discovered new erotic power. “Turned Up” shows that this power carries back into ordinary life: she can laugh, tell Cassie, brag a little, and admit she was grinding on him. Her free yeses feel absolutely real; that is exactly why the cover tagline scares me.

Cassie remains one of the safest people in the book. She teases, sees, checks, and grounds. In “Rye,” she received Vee’s erotic/proud report without shaming her, but kept practical caution alive through jokes about vans, blood type, and real boots. Her soft “Vee” felt like care that knows something fragile has happened. In “Turned Up,” she receives the house/dress/fitting story with warmth, humor, and careful attention. She notices connections — the shirt, the sewing, the oddity of Pace’s restraint — and lets Vee pivot when needed. I trust her more than anyone near Vee.

Meg feels like a calm, affectionate witness in Vee’s wider life. She is less protective than Cassie but not foolish; her “Sometimes they even are” keeps both skepticism and hope alive. Absent recently.

Kayla is bright, funny, nosy, and loving in a more chaotic key. She turns the house invitation into a communal event and names the sexual implication directly. Absent recently.

Theo seems genuinely nice and ordinary, a useful contrast to Pace: a boy/man Vee did not want who accepted friendship decently.

Brooke is warm and socially powerful in a way that makes me uneasy. The unnamed older woman with Brooke is also uneasy: silent, appraising, friendly enough but inventorying. Neither appears recently.

Christine remains a small but effective sign that Pace has adult rootedness outside Vee’s campus world.

Overall mood: hot, tender, funny, and ominous. The seduction has moved from social attention to food, from food to touch, from touch to house, from house to craft, from craft to nakedness and visible involuntary desire, and now into afterglow/confession with Cassie. Vee is not being forced; she is glowing, ashamed, aroused, brave, joking, choosing, and beginning to play. The trap promised by the cover feels less like coercion than the terrifying power of true consent inside a hidden game.
