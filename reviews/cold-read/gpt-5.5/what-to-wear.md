## Reader reaction

This chapter feels bright, social, girlish, and dangerous in a much lighter key than the last two. I liked being with Vee among ordinary friends who are not Randi or Pace or Cassie — the athletic dorm dining hall is all gold windows and stolen fries and girls making a crisis out of a text. It gives Vee a public, noisy life around the private seduction, and that makes the private thing feel even more vivid.

Vee is adorable here, and also so far gone. The moment that really lands is not the message itself but her body before she admits it: “her eyes were on it before she chose to look. His name.” That feels exactly right. She is trying to narrate Pace to Meg and Kayla as if she can make him make sense, and she can’t. She knows how ridiculous it sounds that what is undoing her is not pressure but attention: “I’m the one who can’t think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner.” That line is the chapter for me emotionally. It’s sexy because it is restraint producing hunger.

Pace is absent but powerful. He has become a name on a phone, a shirt on a pillow, a house she has not entered yet. I still believe in his care on the page: the dry shirt, the standing in the rain, the fire-lane overhang, the asking first. But the jacket and my memory won’t let me forget that this invitation to his house is not innocent in the larger pattern. “Would you come to my house Saturday. Two o’clock.” It is so bare it feels almost indecent. Not coercive. Not even manipulative on the surface. But the lack of explanation turns into a kind of erotic vacuum Vee immediately fills.

Meg and Kayla both land quickly. Kayla is hilarious — “That’s not a message, that’s a ransom note” — and she brings the chapter its title-question energy: what do you wear, what does it mean, what is he asking without saying? Meg is calmer, dryer, and a little more experienced; I like that she punctures Vee’s “he’s different” without cruelty: “Sometimes they even are.” That’s a good line because it doesn’t sneer at Vee, but it also doesn’t let the glow go entirely unchallenged. Theo is just a small ordinary counterpoint: a nice boy Vee did not want, who took rejection decently. His presence reminds me Vee has options and social existence beyond Pace.

Suspicion-wise: nothing in the chapter itself makes Pace behave badly, because he is barely present. The unease comes from accumulated knowledge: he and Randi chose Vee before she knew there was a game, and now he is inviting her to the house where I know the bench exists. As a reader, I cannot see “his house” without remembering Randi on that hidden-purpose bench. Vee does not know that. That gap is the tension.

The erotic charge works extremely well for me, and it peaks in the absence of sex: the girls revisiting the truck, the one kiss counted exactly — “Twelve” — and then the house invitation. Kayla’s “He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes” is blunt, funny, and hot because it says what the text message refuses to say. The chapter title, “What to Wear,” at first sounds like a practical girl-talk problem, but by the end it means: what self does Vee put on to enter Pace’s private world? What does she wear to a door she doesn’t understand? It doesn’t give away too much; it frames the invitation as anticipation and preparation.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like a warning about supping with danger at a careful distance, though the story keeps making the danger tender. The tagline — “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” — is loud in this chapter, because Vee is about to say yes to the house before she knows “for what.” And the chapter’s great trick is that the uncertainty is part of the pleasure.

What I want next is obvious: I want Saturday at Pace’s house, and I dread it. I want Vee to see the house; I dread her seeing the bench. I want to know whether Randi knows about this invitation, and I especially dread Randi being absent from something that began partly as her game. My guess from here: the house visit is a threshold chapter, and “what to wear” is not going to be the only thing Vee is unprepared for.
