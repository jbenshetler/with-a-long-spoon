## Reader reaction

This chapter feels like Vee in the warm spill of ordinary girlhood, which is a relief after the private intensity of the hike. I liked being with her among Meg and Kayla: fries stolen, overtalking, teasing, the kind of dinner-table attention that makes Pace less like a secret spell and more like something she can narrate in public. The warmth is real, but it also makes the invitation to his house land harder. My stomach jumped when the text came in. Not because Pace has done anything new on the page yet, but because I know from the jacket and from the earlier chapters that his house is not just “his house.” Vee does not know that. So the chapter’s coziness turns into a bright little trapdoor.

Vee feels happy, wanted, socially alive. She is laughing with her whole body, loud enough that nearby tables look over; that is a lovely change from the self-conscious, shamed Vee who worries about taking up too much space. She is proud of Pace and proud of being someone he texts. I also felt how thoroughly hooked she is by his restraint. The line that gets me is: “And I’m the one who can’t think straight, climbing the walls over a man asking me what I want for dinner.” That is exactly the book’s erotic engine right now: not him grabbing, but him asking so precisely that she feels found.

Pace is absent but intensely present. The chapter lets Vee sell him to friends as admirable: doctorate, powerlifting, early rising, disciplined writing, the dry shirt, the fire-lane gallantry. From inside Vee’s telling, he is almost unbearably attractive. But my suspicion stays high because his message is so stripped down: “would you come to my house Saturday. Two o’clock.” It is courteous in shape, but it gives her no frame. The page earns my unease not because he pressures her — he doesn’t — but because he leaves a vacuum that the women immediately fill with fantasy, wardrobe panic, and sexual expectation. He has not told her what she is consenting to yet. That matters.

Meg deepens nicely here. Until now she was mostly the source of the wrong boots; now she has a personality: calm, dark-haired, level, steady with food, someone who listens and then says the dry thing. I like her. She is less protective than Cassie, or at least less sharp-edged about it, but not careless. Her “His house. For what.” is funny and exactly the question.

Kayla is new and instantly legible: bright, blonde, round-faced, all velocity. She makes the scene fizz. She is a little ridiculous in the best way, but she also sees the social/sexual stakes at once: what do you wear, what did he say to wear, two o’clock is not dinner. She turns the invitation into an event. I trust her as comic energy more than as a safety reader; she is excited by the drama, not wary of it.

Theo is only a passing presence, but he helps show that Vee has ordinary campus possibilities too. Someone nice asked her out, accepted friendship, still waves. That makes Pace feel less like the only man in the universe and more like the particular man who has gotten under her skin.

The erotic charge is mostly anticipatory, but it works. The hottest part is not explicit; it is Vee describing the frustration of having kissed Pace only once and being driven mad by all the waiting. Kayla’s “He wants you for the afternoon. Bring a change of clothes” spikes the heat because it says aloud what the invitation refuses to say. The chapter title, “What to Wear,” is funny on the surface — the practical female crisis of a house date — but it points at something deeper: what version of herself Vee is going to put on before crossing Pace’s threshold. It also prickles because clothing and undressing have already been so charged: wet shirt, flannel, wrong boots, russet silk dress, apologetic cardigan. Now the question is not just fashion; it is preparation for an unknown door.

There was very little friction for me. The scene is light, but it does not feel empty because the phone message changes the air. If anything, I missed Cassie’s sharper caution, but that absence seems like part of the texture: Vee is getting a different kind of witness here, more giggly and practical, less investigative. The line “That’s not a message, that’s a ransom note” made me laugh and also agreed with my unease.

The book title and tagline feel louder now. *With a Long Spoon* keeps sounding like appetite plus caution: eat, desire, taste, but from a distance because something is dangerous. “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap” is practically glowing around this invitation. Vee can freely say yes to Saturday, and probably will, but she has not been told the whole game. My guess from here: she will go to Pace’s house, and the question of clothes will become the question of how much she is ready to take off, literally and otherwise. I want to read it immediately; I also dread the bench because I know Pace’s house contains at least one object Vee cannot possibly be prepared to understand.

- **Story so far:**
  The book begins with Randi arriving at Pace’s warm house after chapter dinner, lying to Brooke that she has a paper. Pace has left the door unlocked; Randi locks it. They kiss and begin with apple slices that fit Randi’s careful eating rules, then the apple game turns sexual. Pace reveals that the odd table in his bedroom is a sexual bench he built. Randi is unsettled but says, “Show me.” He undresses her slowly, positions her on the bench, checks her safeword, spanks and edges her, uses imagined sorority judgment to push her into raw begging, and finally lets her come. She cries quietly; Pace says, “There you are, Randi.” Aftercare is careful: bed, cashmere blanket, water, holding. Randi locks herself in the bathroom and sees that her controlled social face has cracked. Pace thinks the night was good and begins planning; Randi feels a low hum and knows she has to figure out what to do.

  The next morning, Randi returns to the sorority house in last night’s clothes and lies smoothly to Brooke, Sloane, and Kinsey about working on a paper and about Pace. In her cold single room, she cannot settle, cannot cry, and lies under her synthetic duvet in last night’s clothes.

  Earlier in the relationship, while Randi is open from sex, Pace asks whether she would like to taste another woman. Her body answers before her mind; later she frames it as only in a threesome, with him. Pace accepts that frame and takes her walking on campus. He points out women until Vee appears: tall, lush, red-haired, laughing. Randi says yes before she can polish it. Pace sends Randi to meet her. Randi engineers a collision, learns Vee’s name and that they share stats, and returns glowing.

  From Vee’s point of view, Randi sits with her in stats after the collision and dazzles her. The professor talks about latent variables, hidden factors beneath what can be measured. Randi takes Vee to Chi Latte after class when Cassie leaves with an oddly formal goodbye. Vee feels admitted into a bright feminine world, drinks lavender oat milk latte, feels displayed and chosen, and goes home warm from Randi’s attention.

  In “Substitution,” Vee struggles with a hard optional econ/calculus problem at the Wilson coffee shop with Randi and Cassie. Pace appears, helps without showing off, gives her the right substitution, returns the pencil so she solves it herself, listens seriously to her interest in econ, asks for her number, and leaves without coffee. Randi encourages Vee to date him; Cassie notes that he looked at Vee like no one else was there.

  In “The Long Way,” Pace texts twenty minutes later and takes Vee for frozen yogurt. Vee takes too much without shame. They walk the long way while he asks questions and waits for answers. She admits it is the nicest someone has been to her in a long time. He lets the truth stand and says her tart is holding up.

  In “Water Wings,” Vee swims with Cassie. Boys heckle Vee’s body on the bleachers; Cassie makes her laugh. Underwater, Vee remembers loving water before her body felt like a problem and briefly grieves. Cassie rescues her through play, splashing instead of asking.

  In “May I Choose,” Pace asks Vee to Saturday dinner. Cassie says he is not a boy but a man because the invitation can keep. Pace takes Vee to a warm small restaurant where Christine knows him. Vee cannot read the menu; Pace asks, “May I choose?” She says yes and feels relief. He orders, she eats unfamiliar food freely, and his voice/food/care let her stop managing herself. Outside he takes her hand. Under a tree shadow, she states her no-kissing rule; he accepts it and starts walking. She stops him and says she is making an exception, kissing him by choice.

  In “Off Six-Fourteen,” Vee tells Cassie everything. Cassie checks whether alcohol or pressure were involved; Vee insists she chose it. Vee is joyful and spinning. She tells Cassie Pace invited her hiking near where he lives, west of town off six-fourteen past gravel. Cassie jokes but warns her to borrow real shoes.

  In “Dear,” Randi catches Vee after stats and takes her to Smoothie Bird. Vee feels underdressed among polished girls. Randi tells a bad-date story, then draws out Vee’s dinner/kiss story about Pace while pretending not to know him well. Vee says she kissed him after only two days; Randi reframes it as because of him. Brooke enters with an older polished woman, asks who Vee is, and Randi publicly calls Vee “my dear friend” while holding her hand. Vee feels claimed and protected at social cost.

  In “Leave No Trace,” Vee goes hiking with Pace. The trail is steeper than she imagined, and Meg’s wrong boots hurt her heel. Pace notices, fixes the hot spot with moleskin, and continues without shaming her. They reach a black-rock summit above a silver lake and burning autumn ridges. Pace feeds her rye/horseradish sandwiches and cherries and teaches her to leave nothing behind. Vee tells him about her upcoming honor-society induction and the russet silk dress she wants but cannot afford. Rain catches them on the descent. At the truck, Pace gives Vee his only dry flannel and stands outside in the rain to let her change. Vee calls him back in and deliberately takes off her wet shirt under his gaze. He looks but does not touch. She realizes his hand is shaking from cold and warms it under the flannel against her belly. He drives her to the dorm and parks under the overhang; a campus cop interrupts before Vee can say something. Pace pulls away followed by the cop, leaving Vee in his flannel with wet clothes and moleskin still on her heel.

  In “Rye,” Vee returns to the dorm, and Cassie immediately sees the oversized flannel. Cassie jokes that she is alive, that there was no windowless van, and that she had questions and Vee’s blood type ready. Vee, sore and exhilarated, retells the hike: the straight-up trail, the wrong boots, Pace fixing the blister, the forearms, the summit lake, her pride in getting up the mountain, the rye bread and horseradish, the shared water, cherries, and leave-nothing rule. Then she tells Cassie about the rain, the flannel, Pace standing outside the truck, and her calling him back in to take off her wet shirt in front of him on purpose. Cassie listens raptly and softly says Vee’s name. Vee says she kept the shirt. Cassie tells her to borrow real boots next time. Vee says she is keeping the shirt. Cassie says, “Keep the shirt.”

  In “What to Wear,” Vee eats dinner with Meg and Kayla in the better athletic dorm dining hall, where Kayla says you can eat if you act as though you belong. The room is warm with westering gold light. Theo waves at Vee; Kayla says he does not wave at everyone and reminds Vee he asked her to a fall event, but Vee says they are friends and he was nice about it. Kayla asks about Pace, and Vee proudly explains his math doctorate, age, morning lifting, powerlifting, and six-hour writing discipline. Meg prompts her to tell “the shirt,” and Vee retells the rain, Pace giving her his dry shirt, getting out into the rain so she could change, the missed truck kiss because he was freezing, and the second missed kiss because the campus cop moved them along from the fire lane. Meg and Kayla are stunned that Vee and Pace have only kissed once, twelve days ago, after dinner when she told him she did not kiss men after two days and then backed him into a tree. Vee admits she is losing her mind because Pace does not push; he asks small questions and waits for the real answer, and somehow that makes her unable to think straight. Then Pace texts, and both friends read her face. He asks her to come to his house Saturday at two. He gives no reason and no clothing instructions. Kayla calls it a ransom note, declares Saturday hair is happening, says two is not dinner or lunch, and concludes he wants Vee for the afternoon and she should bring a change of clothes. Meg starts asking what Vee owns that says “his house” without trying too hard. Vee is flustered, laughing, thrilled, and lets her quick bright heart have the moment.

- **How I feel:**
  - Pace remains intensely attractive: careful, prepared, practical, restrained, adult, disciplined, physically strong, and seductive through attention and waiting. My suspicion remains high because Vee’s choices are real but the larger setup is hidden, and now he has invited her to the house without saying what the visit is for.
  - Randi remains seductive, alarming, and absent in a charged way. Her claim on Vee felt real, but Pace has now invited Vee toward the private space Randi already knows, including the bench, while Vee knows nothing about Randi and Pace’s arrangement.
  - Vee feels more socially alive and more endangered. I love seeing her laugh loudly with friends, steal fries, and be wanted openly. I am proud of her for naming her desire and not pretending indifference. I worry because she is excitedly walking toward a door whose meaning is hidden from her.
  - Cassie still feels deeply trustworthy, though absent here. Her absence makes the scene lighter and less guarded.
