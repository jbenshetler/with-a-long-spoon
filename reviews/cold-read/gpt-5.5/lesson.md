I came into this chapter still warm from Pace and Vee in the dark with the stars, so the opening made me fizz: Vee bursting into Meg’s room over “If you want to, I’ll teach you” is so perfectly her right now, not just excited but thrilled by the permission inside the sentence. The black keyhole shirt is such a small, hot hinge: Vee’s hand starts to cover herself, then doesn’t, and Meg simply says “Jeans.” I loved that.

The dance lesson absolutely worked on me. It’s not coy, even fully clothed; it is all body and trust. Pace’s corrections — “Give me something to push back against,” “Being jelly doesn’t work,” “Close it — come all the way in” — are so erotically charged because they are also plainly instructional. He is not flattering her into it; he is teaching her the real mechanics of moving with him, and that makes the intimacy sharper. The peak for me was when “the pattern came through his body before it arrived in her feet.” That is swoony as hell, and also dangerous in the precise way this book keeps being dangerous: Vee’s body learns him before her mind can protect itself.

Then the Rusty Nail pulls the floor out from under that swoon. I felt the humiliation and jealousy before I wanted to judge her for it. The room is so much bigger than the private Pace she knows: girls touching his arm, women hugging him, people saying “hey, Pace,” and suddenly what made her feel singular looks repeatable. The blonde’s line is perfectly poisoned: “Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to.” It’s mean because it borrows the exact language of the lesson and twists it. I don’t know whether she intends harm, jealousy, warning, or all three, but the text earned my distrust of her in that moment.
