## Reader reaction

This one hit me first in my body as the pleasure of being taught well. Pace on the concrete pad with the old boom box, patient and exact, is deeply attractive: “Give me something to push back against” lands as dancing instruction, but also as the shape of what has made him compelling with Vee all along. He is not asking her to collapse into him. He is asking her to be present enough that they can move together.

The swoon is real before it breaks. Vee learning the lead, fighting it, then suddenly feeling “the pattern came through his body before it arrived in her feet” got me completely. When he tells her the space she is keeping is what gets her stepped on, it feels like dance and also like the whole book pressing gently on her defenses.

Then the chapter hurts. The blonde’s line is nasty because it is half true and half poison: “Knows how to get a girl to do just what he wants her to.” I felt Vee’s humiliation before I judged her. She has been living inside the feeling of exception, and suddenly the room shows her a whole history of women who also know his hands, his patience, his safety. Her jealousy is ugly, but it comes from a real wound: she was visible, new, not sure she belonged, and then she thought she saw herself becoming interchangeable.

I do trust Pace more by the end of this chapter, at least in the immediate moral sense. Randi is absent, but Vee going to Randi’s at the end makes my stomach tighten.
