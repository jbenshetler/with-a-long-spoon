## Reader reaction

This chapter feels like the morning after a detonation, but very quiet: cereal milk, a stuck dorm-room lock, open-window cold, Cassie on the bunk. I liked the smallness of it after “Famished.” Vee comes in almost visibly glowing — “the grin was the entire story” — and I felt happy for her before I felt afraid. That last sentence, “She had never in her life been so happy,” lands with real sweetness and real dread because the cover tagline is sitting right there in my head. The happiness is not fake. That is exactly why it scares me.

Vee breaks my heart a little here because she is so happy and already editing. She tells Cassie “the parts that made a story,” but keeps “the middle.” That felt completely believable to me: not coy, not author-forced, just the way a person protects the most charged part of a night from daylight and from even a trusted friend. But the line that catches me is: “She believed it the way you believe the thing that lets you keep what you want to keep.” That is one of those sentences where I feel the book quietly telling me Vee is not lying only to Cassie; she is arranging the truth so she can continue inside it.

Cassie remains my safety point. She clocks everything instantly: the grin, the toenails, the missing bag, probably more than Vee admits. “Was he worth painting your toenails?” is perfect Cassie — funny, sharp, affectionate, invasive only to the degree friendship allows. I also notice she says “your boyfriend,” “kindly, certainly, wrong.” That wrongness matters. Cassie is placing Pace into a normal category because that is the available category. Vee knows it is wrong, and I know from the jacket and prior chapters that it is much more wrong than Cassie can possibly know.

Pace is absent but all over the room: “His hands. The white sheets. The thing her body had done before she could decide to let it.” My feeling toward him does not really move here because he does not act in person, but the afterglow of him is powerful. The night has lodged in Vee as a bodily current. At the same time, his absence lets the secret arrangement feel louder. Vee is radiant after a yes she truly gave, while Cassie has no idea Pace and Randi chose her first.

Randi is absent too, but the whole chapter makes me anxious about her. Vee tells Cassie a cleaned-up version of sex with Pace; she still has not told Cassie about Randi’s mouth kiss. So the two most dangerous intimacies remain unspoken in the one room where Vee might be safest. I am not suspicious of Cassie at all. I am suspicious of the silence forming around Vee.

Erotically, this chapter is not explicit in the same way “Famished” was, but it is still charged. The peak for me is not Vee recounting dinner; it is the zipper moment. “Nothing under the skirt. She had given them away.” That is hot because it is practical and social at once: the erotic gift now has consequences in the ordinary dorm room. The secret is suddenly not abstract. It is inches from being visible on the floor. The phrase “frosted-plum toenails her mother would have had a word for” also keeps the erotic charge alive through shame, but shame has less authority this morning: “The word could come; she found she didn't much mind it this morning.”

No real boredom here. It is short and earns its length. The only friction I felt was productive discomfort around Vee calling omission “a kindness”: “This was never Cassie's country; you did not march her through it.” I believe Vee would think that, but I also don’t fully trust it. Cassie has earned more truth than Vee gives her. That said, I don’t feel the author’s thumb there; I feel Vee’s self-protective hand.

The chapter title, “Toenails,” seemed almost comic before reading, but it becomes exact. Cassie notices the hidden preparation Vee thought she could keep private. The toenails are the trace of intent: Vee did not pack a bag, could not say the thing plainly, but she painted her toes. They prove she knew something, wanted something, prepared for something, while keeping plausible deniability. The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like a warning about dining with danger at an elegant distance — appetite, manners, care, and harm all tangled. The tagline is almost painfully active now: Vee’s yes was real, her joy is real, and the trap is not that she was forced. It is that she is being drawn in through exactly what she wants.

What I want next: I badly want Vee to tell Cassie more, especially about the panties and Randi’s kiss, but I expect she won’t yet. My guess from here is that Vee will carry this happiness into some encounter with Randi, and that will be electric and dangerous. I dread Randi hearing about the sex secondhand or partially. I also dread Cassie realizing too late that “boyfriend” was the wrong container.
