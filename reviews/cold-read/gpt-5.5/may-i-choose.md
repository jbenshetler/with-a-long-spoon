## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel very soft and very nervous at once. The tenderness works almost embarrassingly well on me: the text, the dinner, the way Pace arrives already showered and shaved, the restaurant where he is known and welcomed, the food chosen for her, the hand taken afterward — it all feels lush and bodily and intimate. And because I carry the jacket and prior chapters, that same softness keeps having teeth. Vee is experiencing this as care, permission, rest, being safely carried. I know enough to feel the danger in exactly that.

Vee is so hungry in this chapter — not just for dinner, though that is vivid, but for being relieved of having to manage everything. The line that hits hardest is that the message leaves “all the rest of the room to her.” That is such a precise deprivation in her: she is used to boys needing an answer immediately, needing to know where they stand, making her responsible for their uncertainty. Pace’s invitation feels adult because it does not demand. Then dinner deepens the same thing: she has no map, he asks “May I choose?”, and her relief at surrender goes straight through the chapter. I find that very erotic, but not in a graphic way — erotic because she is letting go of self-surveillance, letting herself eat, letting his voice carry her, letting the evening decide more than she normally permits.

Pace is almost unbearably attractive here. His care is material: shower, shave, pressed shirt, polished boots; street side; known restaurant; deliberate ordering; listening; no pressure at the kiss. He does not argue when she states her rule. That would make me trust him if this were all I knew. But because I know he and Randi selected Vee, and because the chapter’s title is “May I Choose,” I am very alert to how perfectly his care becomes a structure Vee can surrender into. “May I choose?” is phrased as permission, and she gives it. The yes is real. The choice is his. That is the whole trap in miniature.

Cassie remains a joy and a relief. Her line, “That’s not a boy. That’s a man,” is funny, but it also shows she understands the difference between pressure and space. I still trust her, though I am aware she does not have the hidden context either. She sees the surface accurately: Pace is behaving unlike the boys Vee has known. She cannot see the larger arrangement.

Randi is absent, which matters. The chapter is all Pace and Vee, with no Randi’s hand visibly steering it. That makes the date feel cleaner to Vee than I can let it feel. I kept wondering whether Randi knows about the dinner, whether Pace told her, whether she is waiting somewhere with complicated feelings, whether she is pleased or jealous. Her absence also lets Pace become enormous in Vee’s field of attention.

The erotic charge peaks for me in two places. First, when Vee watches his hands tear bread and then loses the thread after he licks food from his hand. That is intensely physical without becoming explicit: she has been watching the hand, the right hand, the repeated pattern, and then that same hand becomes the one around hers later. Second, the kiss-that-is-not-yet-a-kiss under the tree: her rule, his acceptance, her feet refusing to go, her “But.” The chapter ends right at the voltage peak.

The food sequence is the most sensual part. It does not feel clinical to me; it feels immersive and almost dreamlike. The only place I had a little friction was with how completely Vee softens and loses time — “After that the evening went soft at the edges” — because it is so total that it edges toward enchantment rather than ordinary first-date absorption. But I bought it because the chapter has prepared her hunger, tiredness from swimming, skipped lunch, the warm restaurant, the unknown food, his voice, and her long habit of self-monitoring finally going quiet.

The chapter title is perfectly dangerous. “May I Choose” seems at first to refer to the menu: Pace asks permission to order for her. But of course it points much wider. May he choose the restaurant? The route? The food? The timing? The pace? May he choose Vee, as he already did with Randi in the pointing game? The chapter title recolors the whole date as a series of freely granted permissions. It also brushes against the book title and tagline: every yes is freely given, and here Vee’s yeses feel deliciously free because the offers are so exquisitely shaped.

*With a Long Spoon* still feels like a warning about appetite and distance. Here the spoon is partly displaced by hands and bread — eating without utensils, being fed by example rather than directly — but the title’s caution feels stronger, not weaker. Vee is eating from Pace’s chosen table, Pace’s known place, Pace’s map. She is not being forced. That is precisely what worries me.

What I want next: I want the kiss, and I dread the kiss. I want to know how Pace handles the exception she is making. I also want Randi back in the emotional room, because Vee and Pace are becoming real fast. Guess from here: Vee will experience the kiss as another astonishing confirmation that her body can want without shame, and that will make the eventual withheld truth much more painful.
