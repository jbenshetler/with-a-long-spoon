I came into this chapter still warm from Pace’s house, and what surprised me was how good it felt to be back in the dorm. Not better than his house, not safer exactly, but ordinary in a way Vee needed. The draft from Cassie’s window, the hot-dust radiator smell, the half-unpacked duffel, the “whole chair” turning toward her: it all felt like Vee’s life switching back on outside Pace.

Cassie lands beautifully here. I trust her so much. She is dry and sharp, but the softness is all in what she does not pry open. When Vee comes in bursting with the weekend, Cassie gives her “the entire front of her attention,” and that moved me more than a bigger display would have. She knows there is more under the story, probably a lot more, but she lets Vee have the version she can tell. And the fake laundry story is funny and tender and also a little sad, because Vee is still splitting herself: the naked, worshipped woman at Pace’s house becomes a shrunken sweater and dyed pants in the dorm room. Cassie laughing instead of interrogating her felt like love.

Vee feels radiant but also carefully edited. The weekend is “still on her like sun,” and I felt that. Her happiness is real. The way she retells the mushrooms, the oranges, the salted persimmons, Pace’s “I got mushrooms” like “a man reporting from the front” — she is not just recounting food, she is trying to smuggle the whole feeling of being cared for into a story Cassie can safely hear. The erotic charge is almost entirely displaced into domestic comedy, which somehow keeps it alive: his shirt “comes down to about here,” and “which is why he burned the toast” carries the naked morning inside a joke.

Pace is barely present, but the version of him Vee brings back is warm: bread machine, farmers market, food made with care. The lie that he shrank her sweater does not make him look bad so much as makes the truth impossible to tell. I don’t feel suspicion of him in this chapter. If anything, the chapter lets his care echo from the previous morning without reopening the unsaid words problem. But that problem is still there under the happiness, because Vee is translating a huge reconciliation into harmless roommate currency.

The real little ache for me was Cassie’s mother. “My mother says thank you for the card” hit unexpectedly hard. Vee’s hand going to her robe collar, the small “Oh,” and that warmth “like a hand set flat between her shoulder blades” made me feel how badly Vee wants forms of family and welcome that do not ask her to shrink. Cassie’s mother is offstage, but the invitation matters. Cassie hears that the feeling is bigger than Vee’s answer and closes the subject before it becomes too much. That is Cassie’s tenderness exactly.
