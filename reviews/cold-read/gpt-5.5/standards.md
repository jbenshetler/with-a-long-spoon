## Reader reaction

This chapter lands colder than the last one, and not because the sex has gone away, but because the aftershock has arrived in public daylight. I feel for Randi immediately: the unlocked sorority house should be easy terrain for her, but she enters it like she has to survive being seen. The opening detail hurts: “Randi did not close doors quietly. She closed them with the small unhurried click of a woman who knew the room was hers.” She is still technically in her domain, still Standards, still polished, but she is not inside herself the same way.

Brooke scares me a little, and the chapter earns that without making her a villain. She is warm, controlled, socially exact. “Brooke's voice was always warm. She had a voice the way other people had a perfume” is lovely, but it also makes her warmth feel like an instrument. She never raises her voice, never quite accuses Randi, and still pins her down. Sloane and Kinsey feel like social weather: not cruel exactly, but available to cruelty, available to hierarchy. Kinsey’s “short-cute” makes Pace smaller in the room, and Randi has to laugh at the right pitch before she has time to choose.

My feeling about Pace moves in absence. I still remember the warmth and care of him, but the sentence “He’s hard to say no to” coming back “in a different key” makes my trust catch. I do not think the text has made him predatory; it has not. But it has made Randi’s own account of consent less simple to live with after the fact. The line that worked socially now opens into something she cannot cross. That is the most uneasy part of the chapter for me.

There is no erotic charge in the obvious sense, but there is a charged bodily aftermath: last night’s clothes, shoes on the bed, the cold room, the duvet that is only “a duvet,” inadequate because it is not Pace’s warmth. The heat is negative space here. The peak for me is her lying still and not crying, because it makes the previous chapter’s exposure feel costly instead of merely cathartic.

The chapter title, “Standards,” clicks in several directions. It is Randi’s board role, Brooke’s institutional authority, the sorority’s social code, and Randi’s own impossible internal standard for keeping the face intact. It does not give anything away so much as make the whole chapter feel like an inspection. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, and series title *With a Long Spoon* still feel like promises of social grace with something dangerous under it: invitation, distance, manners, appetite, and the possibility that politeness is how people get close enough to do damage. No cover tagline is present in my carried state, so I cannot use one.

What I want next is to see whether Randi contacts Pace or avoids him, and whether Brooke’s gentle interrogation was ordinary sorority vigilance or the beginning of something more targeted. My guess from here is that Randi will try to restore control socially before she can even decide what she feels privately.
