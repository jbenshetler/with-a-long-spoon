## Reader reaction

This chapter made me tense in a much colder way than the last one. The heat is gone, but the aftereffect is almost worse: Randi walking into the sorority house in last night's clothes feels like walking into a tribunal conducted entirely in smiles. Nothing overtly cruel happens, and that is what makes it sting. Brooke does not accuse her; she just keeps asking warm, perfectly reasonable questions until Randi is pinned.

My feeling about Brooke sharpens a lot here. Before, she was mostly the sorority eye in Randi’s head. Now she is on the page, and she is formidable. “The not-standing was the move” tells me exactly how power works in that room. Her warmth feels real as a social instrument, not necessarily fake, but I do not trust it. The line “Brooke’s voice was always warm. She had a voice the way other people had a perfume” is gorgeous and ominous to me; warmth here has reach, persistence, possession.

Randi makes me ache. The “face” from the previous chapter is back immediately, but this chapter shows the cost of keeping it up in public. Her smile “arranged,” and then “arranged again,” and the fractions of seconds keep widening. I feel her trying to move through a familiar social machine while some private interior part of her has been knocked loose. When she gets to her room and starts walking from vanity to bed to closet to bathroom without being able to complete any action, I believed it completely. That felt like shock.

Pace is absent, but my feeling about him changes anyway because of the sentence Randi hears differently: “He’s hard to say no to.” In the kitchen, that was social flirtation, a line that “landed perfectly.” Alone afterward, it darkens. I am not ready to say Pace has done something wrong on the page, because last chapter was full of asking, waiting, and consent. But the book’s tagline is pressing hard here: every yes was freely given, and now Randi is lying still under a cheap-feeling duvet unable to cry. The suspicion is not that he forced her. The suspicion is that his kind of attention may have opened something she cannot safely metabolize.

Erotically, this chapter has almost no heat in the immediate sense, but it is still charged by residue. Randi’s clothes, her overnight bag, Brooke saying “Pace” like she is filing it, the girls trying to size him socially and sexually — all of that keeps last night present without showing it. The erotic charge peaks for me not in desire but in exposure: “in last night's clothes, on a Saturday.” That phrase makes the whole house feel like it can see through fabric.

The title, “Standards,” lands cleanly and cruelly. It means Randi’s board position, but also the standards of the house, the standards of femininity, taste, sexual disclosure, plausible lies, emotional containment. It did not give anything away, but it made the chapter feel institutional: Randi is not just embarrassed; she is being measured. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels more dangerous now because politeness is everywhere here. Brooke’s questions are polite. Randi’s answers are polite. The trap does not need raised voices. *With a Long Spoon* still means nothing definite to me, but it suggests distance from something tempting or dangerous, and the chapter makes that feel social as much as sexual.

What I want next is Pace and Randi in the same room after this, because I want to know whether he can see what has happened to her. What I dread is that he will see only the face again. I also feel Vivienne’s absence more sharply now: if this is the social world Randi comes from, and if this is how perfectly she can perform inside it, then the jacket’s promised “best friend” role already feels perilous.
