I felt this chapter low and warm more than flashy-hot: a held breath after all the brighter, riskier scenes. It begins in the hush after sex, but what lands hardest on me is not the sex itself, it is Vee lying there and letting herself say the private word: happy, then love. That moved me. Pace is very easy to love here. The spin at the door, the slowness, the cooking, the second taking of her, the scrunchies, the drawer: all of it feels like his language. I trust his physical care again in this chapter. The way he “never went straight at his own finish” and always “came around to her first” keeps the erotic trust alive for me. But the old split is still there: I trust what he does in the room; I do not trust the larger silence around Vee’s life. This chapter almost makes that worse because his care is so persuasive. He is making her a place while still leaving her outside a crucial truth.

Vee is luminous to me here, and painfully young in the exact way that has nothing to do with stupidity. Then the drawer opens, and oh. He has not hidden her away; he has given her the first drawer. That made me melt.

The erotic charge is quieter than the last chapters but still present in the afterglow: the week apart, the door, being lifted, clothes dropped at the threshold, her feeling that a man can return to her again after dinner “like she was something he’d been turning over.” It is hot because it is wanted and bodily and known, not because it is explicit on the page. But the chapter’s true heat turns domestic: the counter, the robe, his arms under hers, his mouth on her neck, the mirror holding both of them. The mirror does not feel like display this time so much as proof.

Randi’s absence is loud. Not in a suspicious, villainous way on the page, but because the last chapter ended with sugar on Vee’s lip that did not come from a pastry she touched, and now Vee is wholly inside Pace, saying love in the dark. I don’t think the chapter itself asks me to suspect this morning tenderness; it earns the tenderness. But as a reader with the memory I have, I cannot unknow that Vee is claiming a place in a house where another woman already has hidden claims. The drawer feels beautiful, and also dangerous because it is so intimate.

The title, “Above Him,” first points simply to the remembered heels and Vee being taller, maybe being physically above him in sex, but after reading it, it points more toward position and perspective: Vee above him in the dark, watching him sleep, saying “Peter” where he cannot hear; Vee above the bathroom drawers, looking down into the place he made for her. The cover board title, **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**, keeps promising welcome with distance built into it. “A Warm Reception” fits Pace’s house almost too well: doors opening, food, drawers, warmth. “With a Long Spoon” still sounds like caution beside intimacy, and this chapter makes that tension ache.

What I want next is for Vee to keep this drawer, this appetite, this happiness. What I dread is exactly how much it will hurt if the drawer becomes evidence against him later, if all this real care has to stand trial beside the secret. My guess from here: Vee is going to start living more openly in his house, and that will make the hidden overlap with Randi harder to keep hidden.
