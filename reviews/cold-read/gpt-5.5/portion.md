## Reader reaction

This chapter hit me with a coldness I was not braced for after the glow of “Fed.” I felt my body tighten almost immediately around the small bowl. Pace has cooked with the same old care: the pot since three, the loaf cooling, the wine opened, the salad waiting. But the detail that lands is “Hers was the small one, the one he sliced her apples into.” Before anyone says the fight, the portion is already sitting there on the table: care as knowledge, care as habit, care that may also be limitation. I do not know yet which it is, but I felt the chapter ask me to look at it.

Randi feels raw here in a way I have wanted from her and also feared. She is not performing her charming competence; “the thing at the corner of her mouth” is gone because “she was not holding anything. It was out.” That moved me. It did not make me trust her more exactly, because she is still talking about Vee as something to be brought “to us,” and that is frightening. But I believed her ache. Her jealousy, impatience, hunger, resentment, whatever mix this is, feels terribly real. The fact that she does not eat the stew matters. She takes the salad, she pours her own wine, she refuses the thing he prepared for her. That felt like a declaration before the sex ever happened.

Pace is harder for me here than he has been, which is interesting because he is also saying the thing I most want to believe: “She chooses it, Randi. Every step of it. Or it isn’t anything.” I do believe he means that. I felt relief hearing it. But the chapter also makes me feel the limits of his self-understanding. “As long as she needs” sounds beautiful until Randi throws back “With you,” and suddenly I can feel how much he benefits from going slowly. He has Vee’s weekends, her wonder, her firsts, her trust. Even if his ethic is real, it may not be clean.

The sex is hot, but not happy-hot. It has charge because it is so angry and withheld. Randi taking him “in one motion” and not saying “tell me” feels like a refusal of the usual intimacy between them, almost a way of using his body while denying him the part he wants most: her admission, her inwardness, the thing he found once and keeps hoping will come back. I did not read it as nonconsensual; he understands enough to “give it to her anyway.” But it is bleak. It peaks for me in the absence: no teasing stop, no eyes, no “tell me,” no staying down after. The chapter makes what is missing feel louder than what is happening.

My suspicion is not new, but it sharpens. The page earns it through language around “us,” “schedule,” and the fact that Vee’s happiness is being discussed over an untouched bowl of food she does not know exists. Pace’s line about Vee choosing every step matters, but Vee cannot choose what she does not know. That is the crack I cannot stop looking at. Randi’s accusation “It’s selfish, Pace” is not simple, because she is selfish too, but it lands somewhere true.

The title, “Portion,” feels exact and a little cruel. It is the small bowl, the food Randi will not eat, the amount of Vee each of them gets, the portion Pace believes Vee can bear, and maybe the portion of truth anyone is allowed. The cover board, “WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION,” keeps feeling darker as the manners deepen. A polite invitation may still be an invitation into danger, and “with a long spoon” now feels like advice nobody is following. I want to keep reading badly, but I dread Vee walking into the “us” before she knows there is an us.
