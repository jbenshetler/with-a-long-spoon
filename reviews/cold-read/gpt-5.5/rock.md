Pace was assigned an older student tutor when he entered college at fifteen; she was nineteen; it went on two years; she left the year she turned twenty-one, when he was seventeen; he says nothing happened and nobody did anything wrong, and that it was the best two years he had had.

Lifting facts: Pace began lifting partly because he was fifteen in a dormitory full of men. He says it was a useful incentive and that he never had to find out whether it worked. Lifting became something else: when he steps up to a squat bar with enough weight to bend one-inch steel like dry spaghetti, there can be nothing in his mind but that lift and heartbeat, which is freeing.

Scar fact: Pace has an old, raised, smooth scar high on the inside of his thigh, longer than Vee’s hand. When Vee touches it and asks “This?”, Pace covers her hand with his, presses it gently down against the scar, and says, “Not tonight.” He does not take her hand away.

Names: Vee’s mother calls her Vivienne; her father calls her Vee except when she is in trouble. Pace’s real name is Peter. Vee says “Peter” once quietly to feel it in her mouth, then later says it softly again with no question in it, “the way you say a word to keep it.”
