## Reader reaction

I felt this one like a bruise being pressed from the other side. After all the chapters of Vee hurting because Pace would not give her the word, here he is alone in the wreckage of that refusal, and the chapter does not let him look noble inside it. He is sick, hungover, cold, eating “everything a body could need and nothing his mouth wanted,” and that plainness hits harder than any melodrama would have. The body comes first here: dry mouth, bleach, shaking hands, food forced down. He is not romanticized in his pain. He is reduced to maintenance.

My feeling about Pace moved. Not all the way into trust, because the secrecy with Randi is still there, still enormous, still untouched by this chapter. But my anger at him softened into grief. The key sentence is devastating: “He had hurt her because he was afraid of how much she could hurt him.” That feels true to him, and it does not excuse him. It finally gives the shape of the locked door inside him. He knew what she asked. He knew what his answer failed to give. He was not oblivious, which is both more painful and more damning.

The Daphne material lands, too. I have been uneasy about Daphne from Vee’s side, because of the age and tutor imbalance, but here I feel how Pace remembers it from inside: she was the first person who made the world navigable. “There had been no one to tell that Daphne was gone. Daphne was the person he would have told.” That line undid me. It explains why love became danger to him, why the word itself got mistaken for the wound. Again, it does not make him right. It makes him legible.

The swoon here is a sad one. It is not sexy-hot, but it is full of wanting. His memory of Vee arriving, her face lighting “into the smile she gave only him,” and his missing “the moment she looked up from the turn and found him waiting” made me ache for them. I wanted the truck to be her even though I knew, from the chapter’s air, that it would not be. When the engine passes and he sobs, it is the first time his restraint truly breaks without sex making a bridge over it. That felt earned.

Trust and suspicion: I trust his love in this chapter. I do. The text earns that. I do not yet trust what he will do with it, and I cannot forget what Vee still does not know. The chapter narrows to the one failure between them, the unsaid “I love you,” but as a reader I still carry the larger failure: Randi, the setup, the hidden arrangement. His suffering is real, but it is not the whole account.

Erotically, this is low heat but not cold. The charge is all in absence and remembered intimacy: Vee barefoot, Vee in his shirt or nothing, Vee’s “backside easy and unguarded,” the first bite of food reaching her. It is desire after the body has left the room. The chapter peaks not in arousal but in hunger: he cannot want the chicken and broccoli because the thing he wants is Vee at the table understanding what he made.

The title, “Not Enough,” is blunt and perfectly cruel. It names his “smaller truth,” the food that sustains but does not satisfy, the actions that are real but not enough without the word, even whiskey as not enough to erase her. It also points back to Vee’s pain: being “the most important person” was not enough. The cover board, WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION, still feels like a promise of dangerous intimacy conducted with manners. The polite invitation has become a trap of withheld names and careful rooms. No tagline was supplied.

What I want next is simple and terrifying: I want him to go to her and say it plainly. I also dread that “I love you” will arrive before the larger truth does, and that the word Vee needs may bind her tighter before she knows what she is inside. My guess from here is that Pace can say the word now, but saying it will not solve the whole book. It may only make the hidden thing more explosive.
