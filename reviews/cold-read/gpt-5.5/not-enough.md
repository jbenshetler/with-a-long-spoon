## Reader reaction

This one hurt in a quieter, cleaner way than the fight chapters. My body was tense almost immediately: the bleach, the second shower, the mouth tasting of whiskey and sickness, the cold counter under his palm. Pace is not romanticized here. He is reduced to function: water, coffee, vacuum-sealed chicken, broccoli, eating because a body has to continue. That made the grief feel more real to me, not less. He is miserable, and the chapter lets him be miserable without making him noble for it.

My feeling about Pace moved. I still feel the shadow of what he and Randi have hidden from Vee, and that does not vanish because he cries. But this chapter finally gives me the thing I needed from inside him: he knows. He knew on the porch. “He had watched the answer reach her and fail.” That line matters enormously. It means Vee was not wrong, not too needy, not inventing the mismatch. He chose the smaller truth, and he knows he chose it because it was safe. I don’t forgive everything, but I trust his pain more now because it is not self-pity pretending innocence.

The swoon here is bleak, but it is there. It peaks for me in his memory of watching Vee arrive: “her face would light into the smile she gave only him.” That lands because it is so specific and ordinary. Not the sex, not the grand meals, not the dress: the car turning in, gravel under tires, her looking up and finding him waiting. I wanted that for them again so badly. And then the passing engine destroys him. The chapter made me want him to run after the word, finally, not because a declaration magically solves anything, but because this man has now admitted to himself that not saying it did not protect either of them.

Erotically, this is not a hot chapter in the usual sense, but it is still charged by absence. His remembering her “wearing one of his shirts or nothing, her backside easy and unguarded” has tenderness and ache more than heat. The food memories carry more sensual force than the body memory, honestly: the first bite reaching her, her face opening, the idea under the food understood. The hunger motif turns painful here. He can make food that wakes her whole self, but alone he eats “everything a body could need and nothing his mouth wanted.”

I have no new suspicion of Pace from this chapter; if anything, the chapter confirms the simplest, saddest thing: he loves her and has built a superstition around silence. I still distrust the larger hidden structure with Randi. Vee still does not know the ground under her feet. But this chapter does not make Pace feel cold or manipulative to me. It makes him afraid, and ashamed of fear, and wrong.

The title “Not Enough” feels brutally exact. His actions were not enough. “You’re the most important person in my life” was not enough. The whiskey was not enough. Food is not enough. Memory is not enough. Even self-knowledge, by the end, is not yet enough unless he does something with it. The cover-board line, “WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK ONE — A POLITE INVITATION,” still feels like a warning dressed as manners: intimacy offered carefully, beautifully, maybe dangerously, with distance built into the utensil. The pull now is sharp. I want the next chapter urgently, and my guess from here is that Pace will have to either spend the word or lose the shape of his life with Vee. What I dread is that the Randi secret may break whatever repair he tries to make.
