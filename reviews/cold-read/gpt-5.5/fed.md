## Reader reaction

This chapter made me feel warm first, then wary after the warmth had already gotten under my skin. The title, “Fed,” lands very plainly and very effectively: Vee is fed twice here, not with food exactly — amusingly, they barely seem to eat, just mimosas — but with recognition. Randi feeds her story back to her in a form Vee can bear. She takes the shame-shape out of it and hands it back as appetite, intention, luck, a “campaign,” a chosen yes. And because I know the cover line — “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” — that feeding feels beautiful and dangerous at the same time.

Vee, right now, feels heartbreakingly happy to me. She is still glowing from Pace, but what moves in this chapter is that she wants to tell. Cassie got the safe version; Randi gets the version with the doors left ajar. I believed every bit of Vee’s desire to sit in that bright porch room and have someone receive the story without flinching. The peak emotional-erotic moment for me is not graphic at all; it is when she says, “I wanted it… And I took it. For once.” That feels enormous for her. It is the whole sexual transformation of the last few chapters becoming a sentence she can own.

Randi is almost unbearably seductive here, and also the chapter makes me more suspicious of her, not less. On the surface, she is perfect: funny, warm, generous, shameless in a way that frees Vee from shame. Her line, “You’re not caught, sweetheart. You’re lucky,” is exactly what Vee needs to hear. I felt the relief of it. But I also flinched at “not caught,” because from the jacket and from what I already know, Vee is in fact caught in something she does not know about. Randi did pick her. Randi and Pace picked her. So when the chapter begins, “Randi had picked it. Randi picked all of them,” I felt the page earn a real prickle. It means brunch places, yes, but it cannot not echo “The Pointing Game.” Randi picks rooms, restaurants, moods, stories, girls.

The chapter is clever about making Randi’s intimacy feel both genuine and managing. I do believe she means “Oh, I love that” when Vee describes the sheet and the midnight kitchen. I believe she is moved by Vee’s happiness. But I also notice how swiftly she directs the confession: “You are not getting away with it,” “You have to tell me,” “Let me ask you something,” “The underwear.” She is not forcing Vee exactly — Vee wants to tell — but Randi knows where to press, and she presses with exquisite social skill. The worry remains the old one: the care is real, but it sits inside a hidden arrangement.

The erotic charge here is a 1 for physical action, but emotionally it is hotter than that. Randi’s mouth is no longer something Vee can file away as a “French thing” so easily, even if she still is not naming it.

The line “Randi picked all of them” primes me to distrust the setup. I don’t know whether the sad boyfriend story is true. It may be, but it lands to me as possibly another prepared offering: she gives Vee a sexual anecdote, then asks for one back. The text earns that suspicion because we have seen her do something similar before with the bondage/tickling story.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like it is about intimacy with something dangerous: the old saying about supping with the devil comes to mind from the title alone, though the book has not spelled it out. Here, that title feels especially present because this is a feeding chapter. Vee is being fed — emotionally, socially, erotically — but perhaps one should eat with a long spoon when Randi is the one setting the table. The cover tagline feels louder than ever. Vee’s yeses are not fake. She wanted Pace. She wanted to tell Randi. She meets Randi’s second kiss. That is exactly why I am tense.

What I want next: I want Randi and Pace in the same room after this. I want to know what Randi tells Pace, if anything, about Vee’s happiness, the underwear, the phrase “I wanted it. And I took it.” I dread Vee being rewarded deeper into trust while still not knowing she was chosen before she ever chose.
