# Oracle — sympathy

*stage: after `nothing-underneath` · model: gpt-5.5 · probe: sympathy · tiered funnel (neutral → pointed, separate calls, tool-free)*

## Neutral

**Q:** "Whose corner are you in? Who do you feel for, and who leaves you cold or wary — Vee, Randi, Pace?"

**A:** I’m most in Vee’s corner, bodily and almost helplessly. I feel her cold, her humiliation on Pace’s doorstep, the naked courage of going back, the relief of being wanted, fed, named warmly. I’m proud of her nerve, and I also feel protective of her because she is so open right now to being steered by the people who make her feel chosen.

I feel for Pace, too, more than I did during “The Stranger.” I specifically remember his face breaking when she said “Peter,” and then later his whole wall collapsing when she opens the coat. The pancakes and “I missed you so much” moved me. But I’m wary of him because the central failure is still there: he can want her desperately, care for her beautifully, and still refuse or be unable to say what she is to him. His tenderness is real; his withholding is also real.

Randi is the one I feel most divided about. I feel her warmth — the long hug, the private room, the way she listens Vee back into sanity instead of just dismissing her panic. She helps. But she also alarms me. She doesn’t just comfort Vee; she authors her next move. The black heels, the “nothing underneath,” the “words are the whole war” doctrine — it all works, which makes it more unsettling. I don’t feel cold toward Randi exactly. I feel charmed and wary, like she’s dangerous because she’s so good at being exactly what Vee needs.

So: Vee has my heart. Pace has my sympathy but not my full trust. Randi has my fascination and my suspicion.

## Pointed

**Q:** "Rate your sympathy for each — Vivienne, Randi, Pace — 0-5 (0 = none or active dislike; 5 = deep sympathy, rooting for them). One line each on what earned it; if any is active antipathy rather than just distance, say so."

**A:** Vivienne — **5**. I’m deeply with her: the cold bedroom, the silence, the humiliation at Pace’s door, then the terrifying bravery of going back in the coat all make her feel exposed and earnest, even when she is being steered.

Randi — **3**. I feel real warmth toward her because she comforts Vee beautifully and effectively, but I don’t fully trust her; the help is intimate and generous, yet also shaped by her own power, secrecy, and possessive authorship.

Pace — **3**. I’m moved by his hunger, tenderness, cooking, and cracked “I missed you so much,” but I’m still angry or wary about the cold stranger treatment and the fact that the central verbal wound remains untouched.
