## Reader reaction

This one landed cold in my body before it landed anywhere else. Vee in that too-small childhood bed, knees bent because she outgrew it years ago, felt almost physically cramped to read. The room loving “Vivienne” and not knowing Vee is such a sad, exact kind of homesickness: not rejected by home, but no longer housed by it. I felt protective of her, and also embarrassed with her, because the chapter lets me sit right inside the old-lady lavender, the Wheel of Fortune, the parental questions she has no answer for, and the phone with nothing from Pace.

My feeling about Vee deepened toward ache. She is not being melodramatic to me here; she is trying to live inside a relationship that has changed her whole scale of hunger but gives her no public shape to stand in. The image that hurt most was the induction photo split in two: the whole image sent to Pace, the waist-up acceptable version sent downstairs. “Same picture, one house, and she was the one who had divided it.” That feels like the whole Vee problem right now: she has learned to be whole somewhere, but she still has to crop herself for home.

Pace is absent, and that absence is loud. I still believe in his care because the memories of him cooking and leaving room are warm, but from Vee’s side his silence over break feels brutal, whether he means it that way or not. “He always left you the room — a thing she'd appreciated once, and hated now.” That moved me. His restraint, which once felt like consent and spaciousness, now feels like abandonment because she needs pursuit or naming, not more room.

Randi is the complicated relief here. Her text makes Vee feel remembered; I felt the lift when it came in. “miss your face most though, gorgeous” works on Vee, and honestly it worked on me too because Vee is so starved for a sure date, a sure face, a sure return. But because I carry Randi’s hidden love and hidden cover, that warmth has a shadow the page has absolutely earned. Vee thinks Randi is “the one sure thing the new year held,” and I’m both glad she has that and afraid of what that certainty is built on.

The erotic charge is mostly remembered rather than present, but it still has heat. Pace at the stove, garlic in oil, the food drawing hunger out of her before she names it, directly braided with what his hands do in bed: that is still charged. The peak for me is when Vee tries to return to “the best night she had” and then the imagined blonde takes her place. That is not sexy anymore; it is desire turning against her. The warmth curdles on the page.

I had no friction in the sense of not buying it. The only place I felt discomfort was earned discomfort: Vee inventing “some pretty girl” and calling her own body “a stripper’s” is ugly because it is Vee’s shame talking, not because the book is pushing me there. The chapter title, “Old Acquaintances,” points straight at New Year’s, but it also feels bitterly personal: Vee is alone with old versions of herself, old rooms, old family rhythms, and old acquaintances who are no longer her life. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, and the series title *With a Long Spoon* still feel like promises of warmth offered with danger held at distance: invitations into rooms, meals, sex, houses, friendships, but always with rules about how near anyone can come. I do not have a cover tagline in the retained packet, so I can’t react to one.

What I want next is simple and not simple: I want Pace to answer with words, and I dread that he will answer with another beautiful act. I want Vee to get back to Randi because I understand why that feels like air, and I dread Randi becoming the place Vee runs because Randi is not cleanly honest with her. My guess from here is that the new term will make Randi feel like safety exactly when Pace feels blank, and that scares me.
