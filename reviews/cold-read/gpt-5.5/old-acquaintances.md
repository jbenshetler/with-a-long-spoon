## Reader reaction

I hurt for Vee all through this. The chapter is so cold in the body first: the too-short bed, the lavender plug-ins, the furnace saving money, her knees bent like her old self is literally too large for the room now. It makes the ache with Pace feel physical instead of abstract. She is not just sad about a man; she has come home to a room that “had no idea who was in the bed,” and that line landed hard. The house loves Vivienne, but Vee is the one lying there freezing.

My feeling about Vee moved toward even more tenderness and fear. She is lucid about so much — the cropped photo, the PPP money, the way she divided herself for her parents — and still completely helpless inside not knowing what she is to Pace. That helplessness feels earned. I don’t think she is being dramatic; the book has made me feel why “the most important person in my life” is not enough if he will not spend the naming word on her. The phone draft, “are you—,” then deleting it letter by letter, is such a small, miserable perfect thing.

Pace is almost unbearable here because he is absent and yet everywhere. The stove, the garlic, the fra diavolo, the arms around her: all the warmth he gave her comes back as a weapon because it can be imagined being given to someone else. I still trust that he loves her, because I remember him sobbing in the last chapter, but Vee does not have that. From inside her knowledge, his silence is a blank big enough to fill with anyone. That is the cruelty of his position, whether he means it or not.

Randi feels warm and dangerous in a different way. Vee’s text to her is so careful, so loving, so protective: “i’m here if you want to talk. or not.” And Randi’s reply gives Vee exactly enough sparkle to ache over. I don’t suspect Randi in this chapter because of anything new she does here; the suspicion is old and structural. What hurts is that Vee calls her “the one sure thing the new year held,” when I know Randi is not simple safety. But I also believe Randi does love her. That makes it worse, not easier.

The erotic charge is mostly grief-charge, but it works on me. The hottest remembered image is the fra diavolo night: Vee wrapped in the sheet, the kitchen “smelling right,” her body “quiet and the evening having used her well,” the sheet slipping and her not fixing it. That memory has real heat because it is so intimate and unashamed. Then the chapter turns it by replacing her with the imagined blonde, and the heat curdles into jealousy and self-erasure. It is painful, but it is not cold writing; it burns.

The title, “Old Acquaintances,” points straight into New Year’s, but bitterly: not cheerful reunion, not “auld lang syne” warmth, but Vee surrounded by old versions of herself, old rooms, old trophies, old safe life, none of them able to know her now. It also makes Pace and Randi feel like old/new acquaintances in the wrong way: people she knows intimately and still cannot fully claim. The cover board says **WITH A LONG SPOON · BOOK TWO — A WARM RECEPTION**, and that title keeps feeling more barbed. This chapter is all reception withheld: no warm welcome from the old room, no message from Pace, only Randi’s glamorous warmth at a distance. “With a long spoon” still sounds like a warning about proximity to something dangerous; here Vee is trying to eat from far away, through photos and memories and texts, and starving.

What I want next is almost painfully simple: I want Pace to break the silence before Vee has to make herself smaller to survive it. My guess from here is that the term starting will pull Randi and Vee back together before Pace and Vee are repaired, and I dread how much Vee may lean on Randi without knowing the trap beneath the tenderness.
