## Reader reaction

This chapter hurts in a quieter, smaller way than “Believe Me” and “Fairytale,” but it hurts a lot. After the grandeur of Randi’s Gstaad chapter, Vee’s Ohio bedroom feels painfully low-ceilinged: “the room kept all of it dusted and had no idea who was in the bed.” That line landed hard for me. The old room loves Vivienne, not Vee. It is affectionate and suffocating at once — trophies, ribbons, clarinet, girls who “hadn’t texted in a year,” the too-short bed, the lavender plugins, the careful furnace. I felt claustrophobic before I interpreted anything.

Vee has my whole sympathy here, and also my worry. She is not being irrational out of nowhere; the text has earned her panic. Pace has not answered, has not followed, has not named anything, and she is at home in a place where none of her real life fits. Her parents’ love is real — the “good meat her mother made because it was Christmas and it was love” — but it is not what she is hungry for, and she knows that now. The house asks ordinary questions: “how’s your young man, when do we get to meet him, is it serious,” and every one of those questions lands on the same closed door. I felt how humiliating and lonely it is that she cannot answer basic love-life questions because the best thing in her life has no public shape.

My feeling about Pace moves darker, though he is not even present. His absence becomes a presence. The fact that he “always left you the room” used to read as respect; here she “hated” it, and I did too, a little. Not because leaving room is bad, but because after that doorway scene, silence is no longer spacious. It is abandonment-shaped. I still don’t know if he thinks he is being principled, wounded, patient, incapable, or done. But Vee’s fear — “Maybe it was over. Maybe he thought it was and she didn’t. Maybe he thought nothing had happened at all” — feels awful because all three are plausible from where she is lying.

The jealousy spiral with Sheri and the imagined blonde is painful and convincing. It is not fair to Pace, maybe, but it is exactly what no-name/no-standing creates: “He could be anyone’s, anywhere, and she had no standing to so much as ask.” That is the central wound for me. The chapter makes “girlfriend” or some equivalent not a silly label but a right to ask where he is, who he is feeding, whether there is a future meeting-place. Without a name, she cannot even claim the dignity of jealousy.

Randi, from Vee’s side, feels like the secure thing — which makes me uneasy because I just read Randi deciding to keep her own word covered. Vee sees Randi’s feed and wants it “every part of it,” even knowing “what had bought the dream.” That is such a morally uncomfortable sentence. Vee knows the Miranda Holdings rot, knows the money is poisoned, and still envies the life because from her cold childhood room, “even a poisoned Gstaad was more life than she had.” I believe that completely. It doesn’t make Vee bad; it makes longing bigger than ethics in the moment. But it also makes Randi’s glitter dangerous. Randi’s reply — “you ANGEL… miss your face most though, gorgeous” — is warm and exactly what Vee needs, and because I know from the last chapter that it is also a cropped truth, it feels double. Vee takes it as proof that Randi is “the only girl she could talk to about real things,” but the realest thing between them is still not being said.

Trust vs. suspicion: I have no new suspicion that Vee is misreporting anything. I trust her pain. I am increasingly suspicious of the whole pattern around Pace’s silence and Randi’s covered language, but not in a cartoon-villain way. The text earns it through absence, not through any sinister gesture. Pace has given Vee much more than most men would, but this chapter shows how “much more” can still leave her with “nothing scheduled, nothing promised, no place she could be sure of him.” Randi gives Vee affection and availability, but she also gives the fairytale version, and Vee does not know it is partial.

Erotic charge is low but not absent. This is mostly ache, not heat. The erotic peak for me is the remembered kitchen scene turning against her: Vee tries to warm herself with “the first one, after,” the fra diavolo, the sheet slipping, “nothing that needed saying,” and then the imagined blonde takes her place in it. That is not arousing exactly; it is erotic memory becoming self-torture. The chapter’s sexiness is in traces — the slit photograph, Pace’s cooking, the sheet, his arms imagined around her — but every trace curdles into lack.

The friction for me is minimal. I bought the spiral because it is emotionally exact. If anything, the repeated bleakness of “cold room / no answer / no name” is heavy, but I think the heaviness is the point of the lived hour. The line “a fashion model’s body where Vee’s was a stripper’s” is ugly in the way Vee is being ugly to herself; I flinched, but I didn’t think the book endorsed it. It felt like midnight self-hatred speaking.

The chapter title, “Old Acquaintances,” points first to New Year’s — “Auld Lang Syne,” old acquaintance, the year turning — but after reading it, it feels crueler and more intimate. Vee is surrounded by old versions of herself: the old bedroom, old trophies, old friends, old family routines, old safe life. They “know” Vivienne and don’t know Vee. The title also makes Pace and Randi strange future-old acquaintances of the new year: one sure, one blank. It didn’t give anything away, but it set a sad New Year’s register before I knew how cold the chapter would be.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, keeps feeling more food-linked and more cautionary. Vee’s hunger is everywhere again: the Christmas food that is love but not what she needs, Pace’s garlic and oil, the plate set down only when she is aching for it, the kitchen memory she cannot keep. The cover tagline — “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” — feels very active here. Vee’s yeses to Pace, to Randi, to secrecy, to cropped versions, to being given room, were real yeses. But now she is alone in the consequence of not being able to name what she said yes to.

What I want next: I want Pace to answer in words, not food, not hands, not a warmed room. I dread that Vee will go to Randi with this wound, because Randi will be loving and brilliant and maybe exactly the wrong safe harbor. My guess from here is that the new term will bring Randi and Vee together first, not Pace and Vee, because Vee has “a day she could circle for the one and nothing at all for the other.” That feels dangerous.
