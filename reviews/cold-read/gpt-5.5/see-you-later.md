## Reader reaction

This chapter made me ache for Vee almost immediately. Being inside her instead of Pace or Randi is a real shift: the “game” suddenly has a human center, and she is so hungry for being chosen that Randi’s attention feels like sunlight and danger at once. I felt the pleasure of it — very strongly — before I felt the dread. The door-watching in stats is painfully recognizable: “now in the third minute of not looking at the door.” I like Vee. I’m drawn to her. I also feel protective of her in a way I didn’t yet in the last chapter, because now I know how much this costs her and how little she knows.

Randi is dazzling here, but she also scares me more. Not because she is cold — she isn’t cold at all on the surface. She is warm, generous, socially gifted, apparently delighted. But because I’ve just seen, from the prior chapter, that the collision and the “oh my god, we should sit together” were not innocent. So when Vee receives Randi’s warmth as miraculous, I’m split: I feel why she falls for it, and I also can’t forget that Randi is carrying Pace’s game into this room. The line “the warmth swung over and landed there, total, undimmed” is exactly what makes Randi powerful. She can make anyone feel like the next wonderful thing. I don’t think the text makes her a villain; I do think it makes her dangerous, partly because the warmth is real enough to work.

Pace is not present in person, and that absence is sinister in its own quiet way. He doesn’t need to be in the room for his framing to continue. Randi is doing the work now. That makes the trap feel less like one man’s scheme and more like a little social weather system Vee has wandered into.

Cassie interests me a lot. She is flat-spoken, maybe blunt, maybe protective, maybe just not easily charmed. Her refusal to take Randi’s bit — “‘Cassie,’ Cassie said. That was all” — made me like her instantly. She is the first person who doesn’t automatically move in Randi’s rhythm. And her “Goodbye, Vee” landed like a tiny bell of warning. The chapter title, “See You Later,” makes that even sharper: Cassie says goodbye, Randi says see you later. Vee hears Randi’s phrase as promise and inclusion, but the title also makes me hear the gap between a casual social line and a hook set in someone’s chest.

The erotic charge is mostly clothed and psychological, but it absolutely works on me. It peaks for me at Chi Latte, not because anything sexual happens, but because Vee is so physically aware: Randi’s cold-citrus smell, the warm latte in both hands, knees nearly touching, being “up in the window” on display. There’s also a charged little flash when Vee looks at the underdressed girls and the inherited word “Floozies” arrives in her mother’s voice — followed immediately by her own appraisal, her own body-pride, and the thought that she “had more than they had, and better than any of them.” That is hot and sad and revealing all at once. Vee has her own buried wanting and her own rules, not just Randi’s.

The chapter’s best unease is that Vee cannot remember what Randi said. “The transcript was blank.” That frightened me. Not because Randi necessarily said anything bad, but because Vee is keeping the feeling instead of the facts. The seduction is not in content; it is in atmosphere, status, attention, access. The spoons ringing, the yellow room, the high table, Randi’s bag touching hers, “Love you, girl,” “See you later” — it all becomes a kind of spell.

I didn’t have much friction. If anything, the only tiny point where I felt the author nudging me was the professor’s “latent variables” definition: “The factors you can't measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can.” It is apt — maybe almost too apt — because the whole chapter is about inferred desire, hidden motives, and Vee trying to read what Randi’s attention means. But I didn’t mind it; it fit the stats-class setting cleanly enough.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like a warning about eating with/deviling-with dangerous company, though the exact phrase hasn’t landed in-story for me. This chapter deepens the “spoon” feeling because of Chi Latte: spoons ringing in cups, sweetness stirred at the bottom, Vee being invited into a room where everyone knows the joke except her until Randi lets her in. The tagline — “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” — is almost unbearably active here. Vee says yes to coffee because she wants to. She sits at the high table because she wants to. She warms under “see you later” because she wants the later more than anything. That freedom is exactly what makes me dread the next steps.

What I want next: I want more Vee and Cassie. I want Cassie to keep seeing clearly. I want Vee to get to be chosen without being used, though the jacket has already told me that’s not the deal. My guess from here is that Randi will keep folding Vee into ordinary friendship — class, coffee, maybe parties or the sorority orbit — before Pace appears directly. I dread the moment Vee realizes there was a game before there was a friendship.
