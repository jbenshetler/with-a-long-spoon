## Reader reaction

This chapter made me ache for Vee almost immediately. Being inside her is such a shift after seeing her from Pace and Randi’s distance: she is not just the lush, laughing redhead now; she is lonely, observant, hungry to be included, and painfully vulnerable to Randi’s kind of shine. The opening with her “third minute of not looking at the door” is funny and tender and excruciating because I know, from the jacket and last chapter, that Randi’s arrival is not innocent in the way Vee thinks it is. But the wanting is innocent on Vee’s side. She has been picked up by a brighter life and she is already carrying it like treasure.

Randi is dazzling here, and I absolutely feel why Vee falls toward her. She is socially brilliant: the “Randi, not Randy” bit, the way she can turn Cassie’s flatness into something delightful, the way she makes Vee’s pen feel like praise, the way she says “Come to Chi Latte with me” as if life will now rearrange around her. But because I’m carrying the last chapter, her warmth also feels dangerous. Not fake exactly — that’s the hardest part. I believe she enjoys Vee. I believe she is charmed by her. I believe “There you are” is sincerely lit. But she also already knows Vee is the chosen girl, and Vee does not. So every touch of the forearm lands twice: as pleasure, and as trap.

Vee won me completely. She notices everything: clothes, rooms, social codes, who belongs where. She is not stupid or blank; she is socially alert. But she is so susceptible to being made special. “The right pen. A nothing thing to say, and it warmed her like praise she’d worked for” is exactly the wound. Randi barely has to press. Vee is ready to bloom. And that makes Cassie’s quiet goodbye feel like a warning bell. Cassie is barely there in terms of words, but I trust her instincts more than anyone else in the room. Her “Goodbye, Vee” is the chapter’s cold finger on the back of my neck. The title, “See You Later,” sets that contrast up beautifully: Cassie says goodbye like an ending; Randi says see you later like a promise. Vee chooses the promise.

Cassie herself is fascinating in a small, dry way. She does not perform. She doesn’t take Randi’s bait, doesn’t return the bit, doesn’t seem charmed into the room’s rhythm. “Cassie,” she says, and that’s all. I don’t know yet whether she sees danger, or simply sees Randi as not her kind of person, but her refusal to be warmed is a relief. When “something passed over her face, brief, that Vee would not have been able to name,” I felt the book deliberately leaving me with the same unreadable thing Vee misses.

The erotic charge here is not explicit, but it’s strong — more romantic/social-erotic than sexual. It peaks for me in Chi Latte, when Vee compares herself to the exposed girls and thinks, “She could wear what they were wearing,” then immediately folds that thought under her cardigan. There is a real bodily charge in the room full of girls, clothes, bare midriffs, fabric, looking, and self-censoring. And then it narrows to Randi: “all Vee could do was look at her with a flat helpless want.” That phrase hit hard. It is desire before Vee has named it as sexual, or maybe before she will allow herself to. The hug at the end also has a jolt: “the cold-citrus smell of her and a hand flat between Vee’s shoulder blades for one second.” Tiny contact, huge effect.

The stats material works better than it has any right to because “latent variables” is too perfect for this chapter without feeling forced in the moment. The professor literally says the hidden factors “turn out to be driving the whole thing from underneath,” and Vee writes it down without understanding that she is inside one. That could have felt like a thumb on the scale, but because it comes as a dry lecture detail and because the whole chapter is about hidden causes — Randi’s purpose, Vee’s hunger, Cassie’s suspicion, Vee’s mother-voice — I bought it.

The only place I felt a little authorial underline was maybe that final lecture sentence itself: “the ones that turn out to be driving the whole thing from underneath.” It is almost too on the nose. But honestly, as a reader, I liked the shiver of it.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like warning more than definition. If the phrase means dining with danger at a distance, this chapter makes me feel Vee has done the opposite: she has stepped close, sat at the high table, taken the drink, accepted the hug. The tagline is screaming now: “Every yes was freely given. That was the trap.” Vee says yes to coffee. She chooses to go. She is happy. No one forces her. And because the yes is real, the trap is more upsetting.

What I want next is Vee with Pace on the page, though I dread it. I also want more Cassie — not because she can save anyone, necessarily, but because she may be the only person near Vee who does not get swept into Randi’s brightness. My guess from here: Randi will keep folding Vee into small, plausible laters until Vee feels chosen by Randi before Pace ever becomes visible. My dread is that by the time the “game” becomes sex, Vee will already be emotionally committed to not seeing the setup.
