### Chapter record

The chapter is titled “See You Later.”

The stats lecture meets on a Wednesday morning in a tiered room on the second floor of the math building, built for a hundred and seating sixty. Seats are bolted in pairs to shared tables running the length of each tier; chairs swing out and back and cannot move elsewhere.

Vee arrives early, as she always does, and sits in her usual spot two-thirds of the way back. She has a notebook and pencil. She writes the date at the top of a clean page. She has decided not to look at the door and keeps failing by waiting for Randi.

Cassie sits beside Vee. She unpacks a granola bar, the wrong textbook, then the right one. She notices Vee watching the door. Cassie says Vee keeps looking at the door. Cassie is terse. After class she puts the wrong textbook back in her bag, pulls a ball cap from a side pocket, sets it on her head with the brim low, and is wearing shorts and yesterday’s shirt. She has a lab at noon. She declines coffee, tells Vee “Have fun,” then says “Goodbye, Vee” plainly and leaves up the aisle toward the doors.

The stats professor is a soft-spoken man. Before class he scrolls his slides forward and back. He uses a microphone clipped to his collar and overuses a laser pointer. He lectures on residuals for fifty minutes. At the end he says next time they will get into latent variables, “the factors you can’t measure directly, that you have to infer from the ones you can.” He tells them to read the chapter and says it is shorter than it looks. The next class is Friday.

Randi enters from the back of the lecture room rather than the front door Vee has been watching. She sidesteps past two annoyed people, raising a hand in small apology. She sits on Vee’s other side, the aisle side, so Vee is between Randi and Cassie. Randi brings a cold-citrus scent. Her clothes are easy, exact, and expensive-looking; her face is done with a light hand that suggests skill, time, and money. She squeezes Vee’s forearm. She jokes that she will copy off Vee all semester because Vee has “the right pen.” She introduces herself to Cassie: “I’m Randi. Yes, like the adjective. No, it’s not a coincidence. Yes, my parents had a sense of humor.”

During lecture, Randi recrosses her legs, writes something, and once leans in to whisper, “wait is this going to be on the thing,” with her breath warm and her shoulder against Vee’s shoulder. Vee whispers back that it will be on the midterm.

The chapter recalls that Vee met Randi yesterday outside the dining hall while walking with Meg from her floor. Meg is dark-haired and easygoing. Vee and Meg were mid-laugh when Randi and Vee collided, with neither clearly at fault. Randi touched Vee’s arm, laughed at something Vee said, asked what she was studying, gave her name, and learned they shared the same stats class. Randi said, “oh my god, we should sit together.” Vee walked back to her dorm not feeling her feet and spent the day unsure whether Randi meant it.

After class, Randi says she is dying for coffee and invites Vee and Cassie to Chi Latte, two blocks away, “the yellow one on Draper.” Randi says she is not asking, she has decided. Vee says yes after holding back her eagerness. Randi invites Cassie too; Cassie declines.

Chi Latte is on Draper. The sign over the door has gold letters on a black field: “Χ ΛΑΤΕ.” Vee understands the Greek Chi plus latte joke as she walks under it with Randi holding the door. The interior is yellow and white, with soft butter-colored walls, white tables and cups, a chalkboard menu in careful hand-lettering with little drawings, coffee and chocolate smells, and many spoons ringing against cups as people stir sweetness at the bottom.

Chi Latte is full of girls. Four sit along the long banquette under the window in leggings and cropped quarter-zips, three sage and one dusty mauve, with big scrunchies and faces done exactly and a shade too loudly. Two girls sit at the bar by the window in revealing clothes: one in a sports bra and little open shirt, another in a cropped tank and low waistband with the brand showing. They have bare midriffs, lots of leg, worked-for bodies, and are deliberately underdressed.

Vee thinks “Floozies” in her mother’s voice while also looking at the sprinter’s cut of one girl’s stomach, the other’s low waistband, and their ease being mostly bare in public. Vee thinks she could wear what they wear and that she has “more than they had, and better than any of them.” Vee is covered to the collarbone and wearing a cardigan over the better shirt, as she does most mornings, by choice. Her mother’s voice in her head says everyone would look and that it is not the kind of attention a nice girl wants.

Randi orders Vee an oat-milk latte, specifically “the lavender thing.” Randi carries both cups and chooses the high table by the window, with two stools. Vee has seen girls sit there before and thought it was where someone sits if they belongs. Vee sits there with Randi, feet off the floor, body visible to the room, knees nearly touching Randi’s, holding the warm latte in both hands. The lavender latte is good.

Vee asks who Randi rooms with. Randi says she does not room with anyone and has her own room. Vee registers that Randi is not only in the house but near the top of it.

Randi and Vee talk for about an hour. Vee later cannot reconstruct the exact words, only the shape: Randi is funny, tells a story about a sister in the house and a parking ticket that makes Vee laugh hard enough to put her cup down, asks Vee things and seems to want the answers, talks at one point about a guy, and says something flattering and offhand that goes into Vee “like a key in a lock.” Vee feels clever, chosen, and lit up at the window in the sweet-smelling yellow room with spoons ringing around them. She keeps the feeling rather than the transcript. It is the best hour she has had in months.

When the cups are empty, Randi looks at her phone and says she has a meeting. Outside on the sidewalk under the gold Greek sign, Randi pulls Vee into a quick, warm, complete hug. Vee smells cold citrus. Randi puts a hand flat between Vee’s shoulder blades for one second, then pulls back holding Vee by the upper arms and looks at her with her lit face. Randi says, “This was the best. Love you, girl.” She walks backward two steps and says, “See you later, okay?”

Vee answers, “Yeah. See you.” Randi goes up Draper into the bright cool day, ponytail not moving. Vee stands under the sign holding an empty white paper cup, with “later” working in her chest. She remembers “love you, girl,” “see you later,” the hand on her back, and that she had been chosen. Vee drops the cup in the bin on the corner and goes home the long way, in no hurry, warm the whole way.
