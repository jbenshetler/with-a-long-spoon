## Reader reaction

This chapter made my body tighten and then go very still. At first I was with Vee in the ordinary embarrassment of the hike: wrong boots, steep trail, borrowed equipment, the fear of being revealed as someone who does not know the rules. Then the chapter opened into beauty — the valley, lake, silver sky, burning ridges — and for a moment the book let Pace become small against the world, which I badly needed. But the erotic charge of the truck scene is the real center: cold, wet fabric, shame, wanting, being looked at, the clean privacy he creates by stepping out into the rain. It is very hot, and also painfully tender, because the heat turns at the exact moment she notices he is freezing.

Vee moves for me here from dazzled girl-on-a-date into someone whose desire is actively, bodily her own. She is still vulnerable to being guided — she said yes to the hike because “There had been him,” not because she understood the trail — but in the truck she calls him back. She chooses the display. She wants him to see her, and the shame-voice does not stop her; it becomes part of the heat. That is both erotic and frightening, because the cover tagline keeps whispering under it: every yes really is hers, and that is exactly why it feels dangerous.

Pace is almost unbearably attractive here. He notices the boot without making her foolish, fixes the hot spot practically, teaches the cherry-pit rule without shaming her, packs food and water, reads the weather, gives her his dry flannel, and gives her privacy to change without asking for credit. The detail of him standing in the rain with his back to the truck is devastatingly seductive. It is care, and it is also an invitation so perfectly shaped that she can discover the next step herself. My suspicion does not go down. If anything, it sharpens, because this is the most persuasive version of him: not predatory on the page, not pressuring, not taking — making conditions in which wanting blooms and feels like freedom. When his hand is shaking and she realizes he is cold, I felt real relief: he becomes human, bodily, vulnerable, not only the man arranging the temperature of everyone else’s experience.

Randi is not present, but I felt her absence. This is the first big Pace/Vee scene since Randi publicly called Vee dear friend, and the triangle is present because I know Randi helped put Vee here. Vee does not. That gap makes the hike feel doubly exposed: Vee is alone with Pace in his terrain, warmed by his care, while the reader carries the knowledge that this intimacy was selected before it was lived.

Cassie is also absent but present in the boots. Meg’s boots are the ones Vee borrowed, but Cassie’s earlier practical warning about real shoes echoes here: the boots are wrong, and Pace’s competence repairs what Vee did not know to prepare for. I want Cassie near this story, because she asks the plain questions no one else does.

The erotic peak is the truck: Vee kneeling on the bench seat, making Pace hold her eyes while she unbuttons the wet shirt, then choosing to look down so his eyes can go with hers. The line “The voice was the heat and the heat was the voice” is exactly the fused shame/desire mechanism in her body, and it worked on me. The moment that goes slack least — really, it doesn’t go slack — is when she puts on the flannel; even the covering is erotic because it is his, dry, warm, and almost too much relief. The scene turns from sexual display into care when she feels his hand shaking. That turn deepened it rather than deflating it.

Friction: I mostly bought the chapter. The only moment that made me slightly aware of shaping was the campus cop arriving exactly as Vee begins to speak — “Pace —” and then the siren takes the sentence. It is effective, but it also feels openly timed to interrupt emotional articulation. I accepted it because the abruptness matched Vee’s startled body, but I noticed the hand of coincidence there.

The chapter title, “Leave No Trace,” first means the literal hiking ethic: no cherry pits, take nothing and leave nothing. But by the end it feels much more charged. Pace teaches Vee how not to leave evidence in the woods; then they have an intensely private, almost-sexual moment in the truck that leaves no public trace except his flannel on her body, the wet shirt in her hand, the moleskin on her heel, and whatever happened inside her. The title points toward secrecy, discipline, and the strange intimacy of knowing how to move through a place without being caught. It also makes me think of the book title, *With a Long Spoon*: care at a distance, appetite mediated by tools, taking without touching directly. The cover tagline feels louder than ever. Vee’s yeses are not being forced. That is precisely the trap.

What I want next: I want to know what happens to Pace with the campus cop, and whether the illegal parking becomes a funny interruption or a real consequence. I want Vee to tell someone — Cassie, especially — about the flannel and the truck, but I dread how much she may soften or romanticize it. I also expect, as a guess from here, that the flannel will matter: as object, scent, proof, and private claim. I dread Randi hearing this story, because I do not know whether it will arouse her, wound her, or make her perform delight while something else happens underneath.
