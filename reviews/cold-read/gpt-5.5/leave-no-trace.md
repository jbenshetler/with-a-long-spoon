## Reader reaction

This chapter worked on me bodily before anything else. I felt the climb in my legs, the wet sock/heel anxiety, the cold rain, the horrible intimate embarrassment of a man handling a sweaty foot, and then the shock of the summit opening. It has a very strong pulse of exposure, care, hunger, weather, and wanting.

Vee is so vivid here. I feel protective of her, but also thrilled for her because so much of this chapter is her finding out she can survive being ignorant, unprepared, hungry, wet, corrected, and seen without being made small. The heel scene is a perfect Vee scene: she is ashamed before she can explain why, her mother’s verdict rising up before Pace has done anything wrong, and then Pace just fixes the problem. “He hadn’t made her small to give it to her” could describe half of what makes him dangerous and beautiful to her.

Pace is extremely attractive here. He is competent without showing off. He sets a pace “at the speed of her not knowing how.” He fixes the boot, feeds her, teaches “leave no trace” gently, gives her privacy to change, and parks illegally so she won’t cross the rain. The chapter makes me trust his practical care more. But the larger book-shape still keeps me uneasy, because his care is so precisely what opens her. His restraint is erotic. His not looking until she calls him to look is erotic. His not touching her while she opens her wet shirt is almost unbearably erotic. And because he and Randi chose her secretly, every real act of tenderness still feels like part of a path she doesn’t know she’s on.

The truck scene is the heat peak by far. It is very charged without becoming explicit sex. The line that turned the whole thing over for me was: “I want him in here with me. / And under it, slower — I want him to see me.” That felt like Vee crossing from being cared for into actively offering herself to be seen. The mother’s voice saying “floozy” and Vee’s thighs pressing hard — that is the erotic engine of the scene, shame turning into heat because she wants the very thing she has been taught to despise. Pace’s response is almost painfully calibrated: he looks because she asks him to look; he does not grab, hurry, or claim. Then the most tender interruption is that his hand is shaking because he is freezing. Her desire turns into care for him: she brings his hand under the flannel to her belly. That moved me. It made the heat human.

The summit itself surprised me by how much it dwarfs him. “His blue shirt was nothing against it. The world swallowed his blue whole.” Up to then his blue shirt is her focal point, almost a lure through the trees, and then the world makes even him small. That gave the chapter air. It was a relief that not everything exists around Pace.

Randi is absent, and I felt that absence. This is Vee and Pace going into his off-campus/outdoor world without her, and it makes me wonder what Randi will feel when she hears about it — especially if she hears the wet-shirt/truck version. Cassie is also absent, but her earlier practical warnings hover over the hike: the vague “off six-fourteen,” the borrowed boots, the state troopers joke. Meg is present only through the wrong boots, and that matters: Vee really did go into new terrain with borrowed equipment that doesn’t quite fit.

Trust vs suspicion: on the page of this chapter, Pace earns trust moment by moment. I don’t feel him violating a boundary here. He gives her the flannel and leaves the cab; she calls him back. He keeps his eyes where she tells him to keep them. The suspicion I carry is not because this chapter makes him seem cruel or predatory; it is because I know from the jacket and earlier chapters that Vee doesn’t know the larger game. So the chapter’s gentleness doesn’t erase the trap — it actually deepens it, because her yes here is so clearly her own.

The title, “Leave No Trace,” lands first as the hiking ethic: cherry pits, wax paper, nothing left behind. But emotionally it’s more complicated. Pace teaches her not to leave a pit on the rock without shaming her; that becomes a miniature of his care. At the same time, the chapter absolutely does leave traces: the moleskin square on her heel, his flannel on her body, her wet shirt in her hand, the thing in the cab “holding still in the warm air.” So the title is almost ironic to me. They take nothing and leave nothing from the mountain, but they leave marks on each other.

The book title, *With a Long Spoon*, still feels like distance from danger: eat with the devil with a long spoon. The cover tagline is louder here because Vee’s agency is unmistakable. She chooses the hike, chooses to keep climbing, chooses to call him back, chooses to show him. “Every yes was freely given” is becoming not an excuse but the trap itself. I dread how impossible it will be to tell Vee later that anything was wrong, because this chapter gives her such good evidence that she wanted it.

Friction: very little. If anything, the mother’s voice is repeated strongly enough that I noticed the mechanism, but I also believed it because Vee’s shame has been built so consistently. The campus cop ending felt abrupt in a good way — not melodramatic exactly, but like reality cutting the sentence off. The siren “took the rest of her sentence with it,” and I felt the stolen moment.

What I want next: I badly want to know what happens with the campus cop and whether Pace gets in trouble. I want to know whether Vee tells Cassie about the truck, whether she returns the flannel, whether Randi hears any of this, and whether Pace tells Randi. I dread Randi’s reaction if she feels Pace and Vee have had a private threshold without her. I also expect the flannel to matter again.
