## Reader reaction

I felt Vee’s hunger in this chapter almost physically, and it is not sexual yet so much as social, aesthetic, belonging-hunger. She is so ready to be chosen that every small thing Randi does lands in her like touch: “The right pen,” the squeeze on her forearm, the high table, the Greek sign finally making sense because she is walking under it with the girl who belongs there. The chapter made me ache for her more than distrust her. She is not stupid; she reads rooms sharply, even cruelly sometimes, and she knows more than she lets herself know. But the want is so enormous that she keeps choosing the warm version of the facts.

Randi is dazzling here, and I felt the pull of her. I understand completely why Vee falls into her orbit. She arrives at the row like a little sunrise: “*There* you are.” That line got me because it is so perfectly calibrated to Vee’s fear that she has imagined the invitation. Randi makes it feel not only real, but inevitable. I also felt the unease because I came in fresh from seeing Randi and Pace make Vee the object of a game. So every warm gesture has a double edge for me. On the page itself, though, Randi’s warmth really works. She is generous with Cassie too, even when Cassie gives her almost nothing back. That made her feel more persuasive, not less.

Cassie is the one I trust most right now. Her refusal to play Randi’s bit is small but bracing. “Cassie,” full stop, is funny and a little protective without becoming dramatic. And her “Goodbye, Vee” landed hard. The chapter itself notices the wrongness: “the whole word, flat and finished.” I don’t know what Cassie sees, but she feels like someone whose plainness cuts through the shine. I liked that she does not sulk or compete. She simply declines, watches, says goodbye too formally, and leaves the door open for my discomfort.

The swoon of the chapter is real. Chi Latte is written like a threshold: the sign, the Greek joke, the yellow room, the spoons ringing, the high table in the window. I absolutely felt Vee being admitted into a world she has only watched. The line “She had stood outside this exact joke. Now she was walking under it” is the whole chapter’s emotional key for me. It’s so simple and so devastating. Randi doesn’t just offer coffee; she offers legibility, a way into the joke, a place to sit.

Erotically, this is a heat-1 chapter for me: no sex, but the charge is there in Vee’s looking. It peaks in the café when she inventories the girls’ bodies and then turns to Randi and “all of it went quiet.” That “flat helpless want” is the most erotic line here because it is still unnamed by Vee. The attraction to Randi is not abstract; it is body-level and status-level and beauty-level all tangled together.

The friction I felt is mostly intentional friction. Vee’s judgment of the girls in Chi Latte as “*Floozies*” in her mother’s voice made me wince, but it also exposed her beautifully: she looks longer than the insult can explain. The one place I felt a little tug of authorial neatness was the professor announcing “latent variables” right before the chapter demonstrates hidden causes through visible behaviors. It is elegant, maybe a touch underlined. Still, I liked it.

The title, “See You Later,” first feels casual and sweet, but by the end it is almost intoxicating for Vee. It means continuity. Randi doesn’t say maybe, doesn’t say let’s sometime; she gives Vee a later as if Vee already belongs in her future. Cassie’s “Goodbye, Vee” darkens it by contrast. The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels very alive here: everything is invitation, and all of it is polite, warm, socially deniable. *With a Long Spoon* still suggests distance from something dangerous or tempting, and the jacket’s closing beat, “Every yes was freely given. That was the problem,” feels painfully relevant already. Vee’s yes to coffee is free. That does not make it uncomplicated.

What I want next is more Vee and Randi, because I am already swept up in how badly Vee wants her. What I dread is Cassie being right before Vee can bear to know what “right” even means. My guess from here is that Randi will keep making Vee feel chosen in ways Vee cannot resist, and Pace will remain offstage for a little while as the hidden pressure behind the warmth.
