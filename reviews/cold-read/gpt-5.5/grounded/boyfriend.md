I came out of this chapter hot, uneasy, and a little heartbroken for Vee, because the whole thing is so bodily honest and emotionally dishonest at once. She is not lying in the simple villainous sense; she is panicking, grabbing the word “boyfriend” like a handrail, trying to make Pace’s body overwrite Randi’s mouth. But the page does not let her get away with calling it simple. The line that caught me early was: “an offering with the wrong name on it.” That is exactly the problem. The waxed bareness was for Pace, but her imagination gives it to Randi before she can stop herself.

Pace lands strangely tender and dangerous here. I still trust his care of Vee in the immediate scene: he wakes, receives her, notices the difference in her urgency, and asks instead of accusing. “That wasn’t you wound up — I know that one. That was angry, Vee.” That feels intimate in a real way. He knows her body well enough to know when she is using sex as escape. But then the interrogation-sex is hot precisely because it is coercive-adjacent without, to me, breaking consent: he makes her “buy it back, inch by inch,” and she wants the rule, wants the control, wants him to force the truth out where she cannot voluntarily put it. It worked on me. It is explicit, sustained, and psychologically filthy in the best way. The peak for me is him holding her at the edge until she says, “I wanted the kiss. I wanted her mouth on me.” That is the chapter’s erotic and emotional center.

Randi is not in the room, but she is everywhere. That is maybe the most powerful thing about the chapter: she is more present in Vee’s mouth, memory, perfume, taste, and dread than Pace is, even while Pace is physically inside Vee. “Salt and lime — she could still taste her, under everything, past the coming; it had not washed out.” That line made the whole sex scene tilt. Vee is trying to use Pace as proof of straightness, as proof of safety, as proof that Randi was just a party accident, and the proof keeps failing because Randi remains in her body.

My feeling about Vee moved from swoony relief in the prior reconciliation chapters into anxious sympathy. She is vivid and brave, but she is also now actively self-deceiving. “I have a boyfriend” is such a sad, ordinary little defense. It should be a warm fact after the roadhouse chapter, where “Vee’s boyfriend” meant everything. Here the same word becomes a barricade. The chapter title gives something away in that sense, but not cheaply: “Boyfriend” points to the word she is using to steady herself, and by the end it feels inadequate, almost too small for the forces moving through her.

The sex absolutely works. It is graphic and sustained, but the charge is not just anatomy; it is confession under pressure. Pace rationing pleasure as truth-payment could have gone clinical or cold, but for me it stayed hot because Vee’s own response is so clearly present: “Something in her put its whole weight on the rule and leaned.” The heat slackens only a little after the climax, when Vee starts explaining too much, but that slackness feels intentional. The erotic pressure drains and fear comes in.

Trust versus suspicion: I have no new suspicion that Pace is angry or punishing her here. I do feel the old larger unease, because he knows more than she knows, and his calm around “Do you want to sleep with a woman, Vee?” may not be as innocent as it feels to her. The text earns that unease because I know from memory that Pace and Randi already have history and plans around Vee’s desire. Vee reads his “I don’t mind” as pure generosity; I cannot receive it that cleanly. It is tender, yes, but it is also sitting on top of a secret.

The biggest friction for me is not disbelief, but dread at Vee’s “No. Nothing.” She has just insisted she does not keep things from him, and then immediately sorts away the goodbye kisses and the moment in her room. That felt painfully true, not forced. The one line that maybe felt slightly explanatory was “That was all this was,” because the prose immediately knows it is not all this is. But even that is Vee’s own desperate narration, so I bought it.

The book title, *A Polite Invitation*, feels sharper now. This chapter is full of invitations that pretend not to be invitations: Randi’s kiss as a “show,” Pace’s question about women without saying Randi, Vee’s own body inviting thoughts she refuses to name. The series title *With a Long Spoon* keeps feeling like a warning about intimacy with something dangerous, something you may feed or be fed by but should not hold too close. Vee says yes to Pace, yes to telling, yes to the pleasure, but her yes is moving through rooms she does not understand.
